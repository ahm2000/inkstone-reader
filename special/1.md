# Special Chapter 1: The Night the Sky Stopped

A thin white bandage rested just below Qin Yuhan's left cheekbone. She was finally asleep under the warm quilt at Emerald Riverside Villa, her breathing slow after the shock of the evening. Two hours earlier, an unmarked black van hired by an overseas syndicate had rammed her sedan on the Pudong riverside road, shattering the rear window with a steel pipe while a masked driver shouted a threat meant for her husband.

Chen Xuan tucked the blanket gently around her shoulder, switched off the bedside lamp, and stepped out into the quiet winter courtyard. The mild, easygoing smile that never left his face was completely gone. His eyes were dark and still, cold enough to freeze the mist rising off the river.

He lifted his smartphone and sent a single line to Number 1.

"They touched my wife."

Inside a hidden command room in Shanghai, Number 1 froze as the message lit up his screen at 9:15 PM. For fifteen years, he had watched Young Master laugh off insults, ignore billion-yuan traps, and let corrupt officials dig their own graves with patience. He had never seen a message like this.

Number 7 leaned over the console and went pale. "Number 1, Young Master is furious. Really furious. What do we do?"

Beside him, Number 11 and Number 12 stared at the live street feed from the Pudong road, their hands visibly shaking over their keyboards. "We already locked the local thugs," Number 11 stammered, "but the order came from an offshore syndicate backed by three foreign banking houses. If Young Master acts on his own, we cannot hold the fallout."

Number 1 stood motionless for three seconds, sweat beading at his temple. The White Guard was built for quiet sweeps and clean ledgers, not for containing the wrath of Young Master. With no other choice left, Number 1 walked into the inner steel vault and unlocked a matte-black cipher console that had not been powered on since the autumn crisis.

He placed three encrypted calls in rapid succession to the three surviving Founding Elders who had built the guard order decades ago, starting with Elder Song in Beijing.

"Elders," Number 1 said, his voice tight and urgent. "Young Master's wife was attacked tonight. He is truly angry. The White Guard cannot contain what is coming. I need the founding words."

Dead silence answered on the conference line. Then Elder Song drew a slow, heavy breath. "Read the oath," the ninety-year-old elder said quietly. "Iron sleeps beneath the pine."

A second raspy voice spoke from the southern coast. "Ash remembers the flame."

The third elder followed from the northern mountains. "No crown above the quiet hand."

Number 1 typed each founding phrase into the matte-black device as the voiceprints verified in real time. Three amber lights turned solid crimson. A low hum vibrated through the vault floor, and across the country, the unnumbered Black Order woke from sleep.

"Black Order active," Number 1 briefed into the open channel, his voice steady again though his palms were soaked. "Foreign syndicate struck the Pudong riverside road. Young Master is furious. Contain every offshore link and seal every border before he moves."

Within sixty seconds, the activation pulse of the Black Order rippled across hidden channels that only the world's highest circles could detect. Across Beijing, Handong, and foreign embassies from Tokyo (+9:00 GMT) to Zurich (+1:00 GMT), a wave of cold dread hit countless powerful people who had no idea how many others were trembling at the exact same moment. In Jingzhou, Li Dakang dropped his teacup onto his desk as his private red phone buzzed with a silent security alert. In the provincial courtyard, Sha Ruijin stepped to his window and stared into the dark sky, his jaw clenched tight. In Zurich (+1:00 GMT), two bank directors threw up in their private washrooms the instant their secure terminals flashed a single black seal.

Back in the courtyard of Emerald Riverside Villa, Chen Xuan's smartphone buzzed. Number 1's voice came through, tense and respectful. "Young Master, the Founding Elders authorized the Black Order. They are already moving to handle the syndicate by the book."

Chen Xuan looked out across the dark river, his voice flat and quiet. "I do not want to play by the book tonight."

Before Number 1 could speak another word, Chen Xuan tapped his screen, entered a seven-digit override known only to himself, and directly activated the Zero Guard at full national authority.

In the Shanghai command vault, every monitor flashed pure white and went dead. Number 1 sank into his chair, whispering in shock. "He called the Zero Guard directly."

Outside, the real storm began.

Within five minutes, the entire country and its surrounding air, water, and land corridors came to a dead stop. High above Shanghai, unmarked dark tilt-rotors and sleek interceptors dropped out of the winter clouds, their engines rolling like distant thunder as every commercial flight in the eastern airspace was ordered into immediate holding patterns or grounded on the tarmac. Along the Huangpu River, the Yangtze mouth, and the coastal shipping lanes, long gray patrol cutters and high-speed hydrofoils cut cleanly across the dark water, their floodlights locking onto every outgoing freighter and private yacht until not a single hull moved.

On the ground, matte-black armored carriers and heavy signal trucks rolled smoothly onto expressway ramps, cross-sea bridges, and rail hubs. Traffic lights across major arteries locked solid red. Port cranes froze mid-swing with steel containers hanging motionless in the night air. Even stock exchanges in neighboring hubs saw cross-border settlement lines freeze solid.

Everyone felt it. On the Bund, late-night commuters stopped mid-step and stared up at the dark silhouettes hovering silently above the skyline. Far away in Yunxi County, a noodle seller on Old Street paused with his ladle in hand as the distant highway fell eerily quiet, every truck parked along the shoulder with hazard lights blinking in a long amber line. A ferryman on the southern coast shut off his engine and whispered that the wind itself seemed afraid to blow.

Inside Beijing's top command hall, Premier Gu Chengshan stood rigid before a wall-sized national map where every air corridor, sea lane, and expressway glowed solid crimson. Beside him, Elder Song leaned heavily on his cane, his eyes closed in quiet sorrow.

"Every route in and out of the country just locked down," Premier Gu said softly, his voice carrying a chill that silenced the entire room of generals. "Tell me who was foolish enough to make him angry."

Elder Song opened his eyes and looked at the frozen map. "Whoever it was," the old man murmured, "they will not see sunrise."
