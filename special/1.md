# Special Chapter 1: Broken Glass on the Riverside Road

The evening rain had turned into a cold winter mist along the Pudong riverside road when the unmarked black van pulled up beside Qin Yuhan's sedan.

Her driver spotted the sudden swerve too late. The van clipped the rear fender with a harsh metallic screech, forcing the sedan onto the wet shoulder beneath a flickering streetlamp. Before the driver could shift into reverse, the van's sliding door flew open. A tall man in a dark raincoat swung a heavy steel pipe squarely into the rear passenger window.

Safety glass exploded inward in a white spray. Qin Yuhan raised her forearm to shield her eyes, feeling a sharp, stinging heat slice just below her left cheekbone.

"Tell your quiet little husband to stop blocking the coastal shipping permits," the man barked in accented Mandarin, leaning close to the shattered frame. "Next time we will not aim for the glass. We will visit his parents on Old Street in Yunxi."

Then the van's tires screamed against the wet asphalt, and it vanished around the river bend toward the tunnel ramp.

Twenty minutes later, at Emerald Riverside Villa, Chen Xuan sat on the edge of the sofa with a small bottle of antiseptic and a clean cotton swab. His hands were completely steady as he dabbed the thin cut beneath Qin Yuhan's left eye.

"Does it still sting?" he asked softly, his voice warm and unhurried.

Qin Yuhan tried to force a calm smile, though her fingers still trembled slightly around her mug of ginger tea. "I am fine, Xuan. Truly. It is just a scratch from the glass. The driver already called the police and filed the report."

"Good," Chen Xuan said gently, pressing a neat white bandage over her cheek. "They were probably just desperate rivals from the port bid trying to scare you. Do not let them ruin your sleep."

"I am not scared for myself," she whispered, her brow furrowing as she looked up at him. "The man mentioned your parents in Yunxi. How did he even know about Old Street? Should we call Mom and Dad right now?"

Chen Xuan brushed a loose strand of hair behind her ear and smiled easily. "Old Street is full of nosy neighbors and bright lamps. Nothing happens there without the whole block waking up. I already texted my cousin to check the gate, and everyone is fast asleep. Finish your tea and go upstairs. I will lock up."

She studied his calm face for a moment, letting out a slow breath as the tension finally eased from her shoulders. "You always know how to calm me down. Do not stay up too late."

"I will be right up," he promised.

He walked her to the bedroom, waited until the steady rhythm of her breathing told him she was deeply asleep, and pulled the quilt neatly over her shoulder. Only after he clicked the bedroom door shut and walked downstairs into the cold winter courtyard did his expression change.

The gentle, easygoing warmth vanished from his face all at once.

Standing beneath the bare willow tree beside the stone pond, Chen Xuan looked out toward the dark Huangpu River. The air was chilly, yet he felt no cold at all. His eyes were dark, quiet, and utterly merciless.

He took his single smartphone from his jacket pocket. The screen held an encrypted message that had arrived ten minutes earlier from an untraceable offshore relay. He tapped it open and pressed play at low volume.

"A small reminder from Zurich," a smooth, mocking English voice said through the speaker. "Three of our freighters were seized last week because someone in Shanghai refused to step aside. Your wife got a tiny scratch tonight. If the holds are not lifted by 8:00 AM tomorrow, we will burn a certain hardware shop in Yunxi County to the ground."

The audio ended with a soft, arrogant chuckle.

Chen Xuan stared at the dark screen for five full seconds. For years, he had chosen to live quietly. He clocked out at 4:55 PM, carried his green thermos to his desk at Hengxin Logistics, cooked braised pork ribs for his wife, and let greedy fools hang themselves with their own ropes while the courts and police cleaned up the mess. He had tolerated insults, office schemes, and even billionaire traps with an amused smile.

He had only one line that nobody in the world was allowed to cross.

His wife had a white bandage on her cheek tonight. His parents' quiet street had been named as the next target.

Chen Xuan's thumb moved across the glass. He did not call the police. He did not open a financial terminal. He opened a single plain chat thread with Number 1 and typed four words.

"They touched my wife."

He hit send, slipped the phone back into his coat pocket, and stood alone in the freezing mist while the dark water rolled past the garden wall.
