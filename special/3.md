# Special Chapter 3: The Three Founding Words

The inner vault smelled faintly of dry cedar and cold steel. Number 1 lifted the glass cover off the matte-black cipher console and flipped the brass power switch on its side. Three dim amber indicator lamps flickered to life across the dark slate.

Beside the console sat a plain corded handset wired directly into three dedicated lines that bypassed every public tower and satellite network in the country. Those three lines had not rung together in more than twenty years.

Number 1 pressed the three red keys in sequence and waited.

The first line clicked open after two rings. In a quiet courtyard in Beijing's Western Hills, ninety-year-old Elder Song set down his folding fan beside a cold chessboard. "Number 1," Elder Song said softly, his voice immediately losing its evening warmth. "You only dial this line when the sky is falling."

Three seconds later, the second line connected from a misty seaside study in the south. "Speak quickly," Elder Shen rasped, his voice weathered like salt-cured oak. "I was already asleep."

The third line opened from a snowbound timber house in the northern mountains. "All three lines at once?" Elder Lu asked sharply. "Who died?"

"Nobody died, Elders," Number 1 said, standing at rigid attention even though none of them could see him. "Not yet. That is why I am calling."

He forced himself to speak clearly, without a single wasted word. "At 8:50 PM tonight on the Pudong riverside road, hired thugs backed by three offshore European banking houses rammed Young Master's wife's car, smashed her window with a steel pipe, and cut her cheek with flying glass. Ten minutes ago, they sent a voice threat to Young Master's smartphone saying they will burn his parents' shop on Old Street in Yunxi County if port holds are not lifted by 8:00 AM."

A heavy, suffocating silence dropped across all three lines.

"How is the girl?" Elder Song asked first, his tone tight.

"Safe and asleep at home, Elder," Number 1 replied. "A minor cut on her cheek. The local thugs are already in our hands."

"Then let the White Guard clean up the offshore trash," Elder Shen growled from the southern coast. "Why are you waking three old men in the middle of the night?"

"Because Young Master sent me four words five minutes ago," Number 1 said, his voice trembling slightly for the first time. "'They touched my wife.' Nothing else. No plan, no patience, no smile. He is standing alone in his winter courtyard right now. Elders, in fifteen years I have never seen him genuinely angry. Truly, coldly furious. The White Guard cannot clear three foreign banking houses and a maritime syndicate before sunrise. If we do not crush them in the next hour, Young Master will not stay quiet."

In Beijing, Elder Song's fingers tightened slowly around the head of his wooden cane. He still remembered the afternoon by the Western Hills lake when he had sat across from that calm young man and felt the weight of the entire country resting lightly on a pair of relaxed shoulders.

"If he loses his temper," Elder Lu murmured from the northern mountains, "neither Beijing nor Zurich (+1:00 GMT) will sleep for a month."

"Then you know what you are asking for," Elder Shen said darkly. "If we wake the Black Order, every intelligence desk from Tokyo (+9:00 GMT) to London (+0:00 GMT) will feel the tremor. Dozens of powerful people inside our own borders will panic."

"Let them panic," Elder Song said calmly, his voice carrying the iron of a man who had signed founding treaties sixty years ago. "A little fear in the capital is a cheap price compared to letting him move personally. Number 1, is the console powered?"

"Yes, Elder. All three amber lights are steady."

"Then take the key," Elder Song commanded. "Listen carefully and enter my founding word."

Number 1 placed his fingers over the matte-black mechanical keys.

"Iron sleeps beneath the pine," Elder Song spoke, each syllable slow, resonant, and unmistakable.

Number 1 typed the exact words into the slate. A faint acoustic sensor inside the device matched Elder Song's voiceprint against the original vault recording from forty years ago. The first amber lamp snapped into solid, glowing crimson.

"My turn," Elder Shen said from the southern coast, his gravelly voice turning solemn. "Ash remembers the flame."

Number 1 entered the second phrase. The console hummed softly, and the second amber lamp shifted to crimson.

From the northern mountains, Elder Lu let out a long breath that sounded like winter wind across frozen pines. "No crown above the quiet hand."

Number 1 typed the final founding phrase and pressed the recessed steel bar at the bottom of the slate.

For two seconds, nothing moved. Then the third lamp flared crimson. All three lights pulsed once in unison, and a deep, low vibration trembled through the concrete floor of the vault. Across the dark glass screen, a single line of plain silver text appeared.

BLACK ORDER: ACTIVE.

"They are awake," Elder Song said quietly on the line. "Brief them cleanly, Number 1. Keep them fast, keep them silent, and pray we are not already too late."
