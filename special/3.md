# Special Chapter 3: The Three Founding Words

The inner vault smelled faintly of dry cedar and cold steel. Number 1 lifted the glass cover off the matte-black cipher console and flipped the brass power switch on its side. Three dim amber indicator lamps flickered to life across the dark slate.

Beside the console sat a plain corded handset wired directly into three dedicated lines that bypassed every public tower and satellite network in the country. Those three lines had not rung together in more than twenty years.

Number 1 pressed the three red keys in sequence and waited, his own pulse hammering against his ribs.

The first line clicked open after two rings. In a quiet courtyard in Beijing's Western Hills, ninety-year-old Elder Song set down his folding fan beside a cold chessboard. "Number 1," Elder Song said softly, his voice tightening at once. "You only dial this line when the sky is falling."

Three seconds later, the second line connected from a misty seaside study in the south. "Speak quickly," Elder Shen rasped, his voice weathered like salt-cured oak. "I was already asleep."

The third line opened from a snowbound timber house in the northern mountains. "All three lines at once?" Elder Lu asked sharply. "Who died?"

"Nobody died, Elders," Number 1 said, standing at rigid attention even though none of them could see him. "Not yet. That is why I am begging for your keys."

He forced himself to speak clearly, without a single wasted word. "At 8:50 PM tonight on the Pudong riverside road, hired thugs backed by three offshore European banking houses rammed Young Master's wife's car, smashed her window with a steel pipe, and cut her cheek with flying glass. Ten minutes ago, they sent a voice threat to Young Master's smartphone saying they will burn his parents' shop on Old Street in Yunxi County if port holds are not lifted by 8:00 AM."

A heavy, suffocating silence dropped across all three lines.

Then, through the Beijing line, came the sharp, hollow clatter of a heavy wooden cane hitting a marble floor.

"What did you just say?" Elder Song whispered. His voice was no longer steady. It shook with a raw, bone-deep terror that Number 1 had never heard from a man who had survived six decades of wars and political storms. "They drew blood on his wife? And they named his parents' street?"

From the southern coast, a porcelain teacup shattered loudly against tile. "Fools! Suicidal, blind fools!" Elder Shen gasped, his breathing suddenly ragged and shallow, as if someone had squeezed his chest in an iron vice. "How is Young Master reacting right now? Tell me he is smiling! Tell me he is letting the police handle it!"

"He is not smiling, Elder," Number 1 said, his own throat dry. "Five minutes ago, Young Master sent me four words. 'They touched my wife.' Nothing else. No joke, no patience, no order to wait for the courts. He is standing alone in his winter courtyard right now. In fifteen years, I have never felt him truly furious. Elders, my White Guard cannot clear three European banks before sunrise. If we do not crush them in the next hour, I do not know what he will unleash."

On the northern mountain line, Elder Lu let out a trembling, horrified breath. "He is truly angry," the old man stammered, his voice cracking like thin ice. "Do you hear me, Song? Shen? If he actually loses his temper, we will not even know how many borders or governments will stop breathing before morning! Even we do not fully know everything buried behind his final authority!"

In Beijing, Elder Song sank slowly into a wooden chair, cold sweat drenching the collar of his wool shirt. His hands shook so hard he had to brace his wrist against the edge of the table just to hold the receiver. He still remembered the afternoon by the Western Hills lake when he had sat across from that calm young man and felt an abyss of power so deep it had left him unable to sleep for three nights.

"We cannot let him move himself," Elder Song breathed, his voice urgent and nearly breaking with fear. "If we wake the Black Order, every retired elder and hidden desk from Tokyo (+9:00 GMT) to Washington (-5:00 GMT) will panic. Let them panic! A global panic is nothing compared to him standing up in anger! Number 1, is the console powered?"

"Yes, Elder! All three amber lights are steady!"

"Then enter the keys before we are already dead men!" Elder Shen shouted, his voice shaking violently.

Number 1 placed his fingers over the matte-black mechanical keys.

"Iron sleeps beneath the pine," Elder Song spoke, his voice trembling yet forcing every syllable clear for the voiceprint sensor.

Number 1 typed the exact words into the slate. The acoustic lock matched Elder Song's voice against the forty-year-old vault record. The first amber lamp snapped into solid crimson.

"Ash remembers the flame," Elder Shen gasped from the southern coast, his breath hitching in terror.

Number 1 entered the second phrase. The second amber lamp shifted to crimson.

From the northern mountains, Elder Lu whispered as if praying to a storm. "No crown above the quiet hand."

Number 1 typed the final founding phrase and slammed the recessed steel bar at the bottom of the slate.

For two seconds, nothing moved. Then all three crimson lights pulsed once in unison, and a deep, cold vibration shook the concrete floor of the vault. Across the dark glass screen, plain silver text flared to life.

BLACK ORDER: ACTIVE.

"Brief them right now, Number 1!" Elder Song pleaded, his voice hoarse with dread. "Tell them to move like their lives depend on it, and pray to heaven he has not already decided to bypass us all!"
