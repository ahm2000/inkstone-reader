# Special Chapter 7: Iron Across Sky, Sea, and Road

When the Zero Guard moved in peace, they were invisible, blending into crowds as cheerful neighbors, delivery riders, and quiet aunties.

When Chen Xuan gave them full national authority in anger, the entire country and every surrounding corridor across East Asia turned into a cage of silent iron within five minutes.

At 9:32 PM, every major air traffic control tower from Beijing Capital and Shanghai Pudong to Guangzhou Baiyun, Chengdu Shuangliu, Xi'an, Harbin, and Urumqi flashed solid amber at the exact same instant.

"What the hell just happened?" a young controller at Pudong shouted, grabbing his headset as forty-two commercial flight strips locked in place. "Who just overrode the entire national airspace? Even Harbin and Chengdu just went solid amber!"

Senior Section Chief Gao stepped up behind him, his face white with dread as a single blank zero code scrolled across the master dispatch board. He could not even tell which branch of government owned that code.

"Ground every departure across every runway immediately!" Section Chief Gao barked, his voice shaking. "Put every inbound plane into outer holding rings! Clear every altitude band below ten thousand meters!"

"Sir, it is not just us!" another controller yelled in shock, pointing at the international relay monitor. "Incheon in Seoul (+9:00 GMT), Fukuoka in Japan (+9:00 GMT), and Hanoi (+7:00 GMT) just declared emergency airspace holds because every flight corridor touching our borders went completely opaque!"

Through the low winter clouds above Shanghai, Beijing, Shenzhen, and coastal harbors from Dalian to Sanya, unmarked matte-black tilt-rotor aircraft and sleek twin-engine high-altitude interceptors dropped cleanly into view without a single navigation strobe or military insignia. Regional air defense radar operators stared at their scopes in sheer disbelief. Nobody could identify the aircraft designs, their launch bases, or their command frequency. They simply formed an unbroken silent dome over the entire country and its surrounding air corridors.

Below the clouds, the water stopped next.

Across the Bohai Sea, the Yellow Sea, the Huangpu River, the Yangtze mouth, the East China Sea, the South China Sea, and out toward the international approaches of the Malacca Strait near Singapore (+8:00 GMT), dozens of sleek gray patrol cutters and low-slung high-speed hydrofoils surged out of hidden coastal slips and dark ocean swells. Their twin bow floodlights snapped on in unison, turning black water into blinding white walls.

Aboard a forty-thousand-ton container ship nearing the outer buoy, the captain froze as two unmarked gray hydrofoils sliced effortlessly across his bow. His bridge radio crackled once with a flat, unyielding voice.

"All vessels in national and adjacent transit lanes, cut engines and drop anchor immediately. Sea corridors are sealed by direct authority. Any hull maintaining speed will be boarded."

"Who are you?!" the captain screamed into his handset. "Even the Coast Guard and the Singapore (+8:00 GMT) harbor desk do not know who you are!"

No reply came. Overhead, a dark tilt-rotor dropped smoothly just off his starboard bridge, its rotor wash flattening the sea into foaming rings. Across the surrounding seas, hundreds of freighters, tankers, and private yachts cut engines one after another until the busiest shipping lanes on earth lay completely frozen.

On land, the total lockdown struck every province and border at once.

At 9:35 PM, heavy matte-black armored carriers and tall mobile signal trucks rolled smoothly onto expressway ramps, cross-sea bridges, high-speed rail switches, and international land border crossings from the northern steppe of Inner Mongolia and Heilongjiang down to the mountain passes of Yunnan and Guangxi. They carried no license plates, no sirens, and no flags. They unfolded twin signal arrays on their roofs, and every highway overhead board from coast to desert flipped solid red.

STOP. ALL ROUTES SEALED. REMAIN IN VEHICLES.

Across the country, miles of freight trucks and late evening sedans slowed smoothly to a halt behind the unmarked barriers, their hazard lights blinking in long amber ribbons across the winter asphalt. Along the northern frontier crossings toward Siberia and the southern mountain highways leading into Southeast Asia, customs gates slammed down in unison as dark armored carriers slid neatly into the lanes, leaving border guards staring in bewilderment at their blank dispatch terminals.

At Yangshan, Ningbo, Shenzhen, and Qingdao ports, towering gantry cranes froze mid-swing with massive steel containers hanging motionless in the night air. Gleaming white bullet trains slowed smoothly to silent stops inside stations from Suzhou and Wuhan to Xi'an and Changsha, while train dispatchers traded frantic, unanswered calls across every regional control room.

Even financial clearing hubs in Hong Kong, Singapore (+8:00 GMT), and Tokyo (+9:00 GMT) felt the shockwave as cross-border settlement bridges froze solid mid-transaction. Foreign military satellites sweeping across the entire rim of East Asia hit an impenetrable wall of white-noise signal interference stretching hundreds of kilometers beyond the borders.

Air, water, ground, and signal. Not a single province, not a single city, not even the surrounding countries' shipping and flight lanes remained untouched. Everything simply stopped.
