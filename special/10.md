# Special Chapter 10: Silence in the Capital Command Hall

Before Elder Lu's frightened voice even faded from the conference screen, the bank of red diplomatic telephones along the eastern wall began ringing in an overlapping, frantic chorus.

The Foreign Minister hurried back to the table, his tie twisted sideways and sweat shining across his forehead. "Premier! Elders! We cannot hold the lines anymore!"

"Speak!" Premier Gu Chengshan ordered, his own voice tight with strain.

"Not just ambassadors," the Foreign Minister gasped, flipping through shaking handwritten notes. "Retired elders from other countries are breaking twenty-year silences to call our emergency channels! The ninety-one-year-old retired American intelligence patriarch in Virginia (-5:00 GMT) is screaming on line two, asking if we just launched a continental blackout! The retired Japanese defense elder in Kyoto (+9:00 GMT) called line four in tears, saying every ship in their southern lane just froze! Moscow (+3:00 GMT) wants to know why our northern frontier slammed shut with unmarked armored carriers! Seoul (+9:00 GMT) and Singapore (+8:00 GMT) say their skies and straits are completely locked!"

"And the Swiss?" Elder Song asked weakly from his chair, his eyes squeezed shut.

"The retired European banking patriarch in Zurich (+1:00 GMT) is begging on line six," the Foreign Minister whispered, his hand shaking so badly the paper rustled loudly. "He says three major banks just collapsed in ten minutes, and he is pleading to know whose family was touched in Shanghai!"

"Shanghai?!" Elder Qin Zhengguo spun around, his eyes snapping wide open as he stared straight at Elder Song. "What happened in Shanghai?! Why is a Swiss banking patriarch begging about a family in Shanghai?!"

"Shut up and let the Air Force finish their report first!" Elder Ma barked, though his own lips were trembling uncontrollably. "General! Tell us what is in the air right now!"

The silver-haired Air Force general swallowed hard, his throat dry. "Elders, at 9:32 PM, our central radar network was completely bypassed. Unmarked matte-black tilt-rotors and interceptors launched from coordinates that do not exist on any active military map. Every commercial airport from Beijing and Shanghai to Chengdu, Harbin, and Urumqi is grounded. Neighboring airspaces in Korea and Japan are holding flights outside our rim. We hailed them on every military frequency. They did not answer once."

"And the sea?" Elder Zhong demanded, leaning forward with white knuckles pressed against the oak table.

The Naval Commander's face was gray. "Unmarked gray cutters and hydrofoils sealed every river mouth, coastal port, and outer strait from the Bohai Sea down to the Malacca approaches. Our own destroyers received a single white zero code telling them to shut off active radar and hold position. Elders, I have worn this uniform for forty-two years. I do not even know who crews those vessels or where they were built!"

"Because they were never built for you!" Elder Shen shouted from the southern screen, his face drenched in cold sweat. "They do not answer to the Defense Ministry! They do not answer to the Premier! They do not even answer to us!"

"Then who DO they answer to?!" a younger two-star general burst out, his voice cracking with sheer, helpless fear as he pointed at the solid crimson map of the entire country. "We are standing inside the highest command hall in the nation, and our entire country plus half of Asia just stopped breathing! Are you telling me nobody here can stop them?!"

"Nobody!" Elder Song cried, slamming his trembling palm down onto the oak table so hard his tea glass tipped over. "Do you hear me?! Nobody on earth can order them to stand down except the one man who woke them!"

Premier Gu Chengshan stepped slowly beside Elder Song's chair, his gaze steady even though cold sweat had darkened the collar of his shirt. "Elder Song," Premier Gu said softly. "You and I know who holds the quiet shield. Tell the elders and the generals what happened tonight in Shanghai. Without naming him."

Elder Song wiped his shaking hand across his face, taking two ragged breaths before he could speak.

"At 8:50 PM tonight," Elder Song whispered, his voice raw with dread, "an overseas maritime syndicate backed by three European banks sent hired thugs to ram a young woman's sedan on a quiet Shanghai road. They smashed her window with a steel pipe and cut her cheek. Then they called her husband and threatened to burn his parents' shop on a quiet county street before 8:00 AM."

Elder Qin Zhengguo froze for half a second, a faint, strange unease brushing the back of his mind, before dismissing it immediately. His granddaughter's husband was a relaxed logistics clerk who cooked braised pork ribs and laughed over chess; what could he possibly have to do with a global lockdown?

"Wait," Elder Qin frowned sharply. "A street attack in Shanghai? Song, you are telling me the man who saved our national reserve last autumn got so angry over a street attack that he bypassed the Black Order and froze the whole country?!"

"Yes!" Elder Lu screamed from the northern monitor, his voice breaking completely in terror. "We woke the Black Order to crush those fools cleanly by the book! Number 1 begged him for two hours! And he told Number 1 he did not want to play by the book tonight!"

"He is truly angry," Elder Wei muttered, sinking heavily into a chair as his face turned the color of ash. "God help us all. He never gets angry. Never. And the one night he does, he wakes the Zero Guard."

"Stop saying that name like it is a ghost story!" the younger two-star general shouted, his eyes wide with panic. "What even ARE the Zero Guards?! We have heard rumors of the Black Order, fine! Who are these Zero Guards that even the Founding Elders are shaking in their boots?!"

Every old man in the room went dead silent. Even the two elders on the wall monitors stopped breathing.

Slowly, Elder Ma lifted his pale, wrinkled face and looked at the younger general with eyes full of ancient, haunted terror.

"You want to know why we are shaking, boy?" Elder Ma whispered. "Because thirty-five years ago, five of us watched them move once. Just once. And none of us have slept with the lights off since."
