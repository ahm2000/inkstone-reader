# Special Chapter 2: Panic in the White Command Room

At 9:15 PM, inside a soundproof basement operations room in Shanghai, a soft chime sounded on the central console.

Number 1 was reviewing port clearance reports with a cup of plain green tea beside his elbow. He glanced at the small encrypted window on his monitor, expecting another casual reminder from Young Master about a family dinner or a neighbor's delivery route.

Instead, four words sat on the screen in plain black text.

"They touched my wife."

Number 1's teacup slipped an inch across the steel desk, spilling pale tea over the edge of his folder. He did not even look down to wipe it. His face drained of color in a single heartbeat.

"Number 7!" Number 1 barked, his chair scraping loudly against the concrete floor. "Pull every traffic camera along the Pudong riverside road for the last two hours. Now!"

Number 7, who had been leaning back near the wall monitors, bolted upright at the tone in Number 1's voice. In nine years of service, he had never heard Number 1 shout.

"On it," Number 7 said quickly, his fingers flying across the console. Across the room, Number 11 and Number 12 spun around in their seats, instantly catching the sudden shift in the air.

Three seconds later, the main screen split into four grainy night feeds. Frame three showed an unmarked black van swerving sharply beside Qin Yuhan's sedan beneath a yellow streetlamp. A figure in a dark raincoat swung a steel pipe straight through the rear glass. The next frame showed Qin Yuhan clutching her cheek as the van tore away toward the tunnel.

The operations room went dead silent.

"God," Number 12 whispered, his knuckles turning white on the edge of his desk. "She is bleeding. Someone actually drew blood on Young Master's wife."

"Where is the van now?" Number 1 demanded, his voice razor-sharp.

"Number 44's street team already intercepted the driver and the hitter near the outer ring road twelve minutes ago," Number 11 reported, sweat breaking out along his forehead as he scanned the live audio log. "They are hired street trash paid through three shell wallets. They did not even know whose car they hit. Wait, there is a second trace. An offshore voice relay pinged Young Master's smartphone ten minutes ago from Zurich (+1:00 GMT)."

Number 7 swallowed hard and turned slowly toward Number 1. His hands were visibly trembling. "Number 1, I traced the three shell wallets. They lead to three private banking houses tied to a European maritime syndicate whose smugglers we flagged last week. They have no idea who Young Master is. They think he is some rival shipping broker."

"It does not matter what they think," Number 12 muttered, his face pale. "Look at the message again. Four words. No instructions. No joke. No mention of leaving it to the police."

Number 11 looked up in genuine panic. "What do we do? In fifteen years, Young Master has never once been truly angry. When his uncle was framed on Old Street, he stayed calm and let the provincial sweep handle it. When the Shen family threatened him in Shenzhen, he smiled over tea. If Young Master is truly angry right now, he is not going to wait for a court warrant."

"We have to stop this before it blows up," Number 7 said urgently, stepping up beside Number 1. "Give me the word and I will put forty White Guard men on flights to Zurich (+1:00 GMT) tonight. We will grab their directors before sunrise and bring them back."

"Too slow," Number 1 cut him off, his chest rising and falling sharply. "Even with forty men, it takes twelve hours to fly to Europe and crack three state-backed private banks cleanly. Look at the clock. Young Master is standing alone in his courtyard right now. If we tell him to wait twelve hours, he will tear the entire system open himself."

Number 7 froze. "You mean he might call the others?"

"I do not know what he will do," Number 1 said grimly, wiping cold sweat from his temple. "And that is the problem. The White Guard was built to protect his quiet life and clean up dirt behind the scenes. We do not have the authority to seal international borders, freeze foreign clearing houses in sixty seconds, or lock down diplomatic channels. We cannot contain Young Master's anger."

The room fell heavy with dread. Number 11 stared at the blinking cursor beneath the four words on the screen. Number 12 paced three steps and stopped, helpless. None of them had ever felt so small.

"There is only one group that can move fast enough to settle this before Young Master takes over," Number 1 said quietly.

Number 7's eyes widened. "You cannot mean the Black Order. They were stood down in autumn. You do not even have the sole key to wake them."

"I know I do not," Number 1 replied, his jaw locking hard. "Which is why I have no choice left."

He turned sharply on his heel and walked toward the heavy steel door at the back of the command room. Placing his palm against the biometric scanner, he unlocked the inner vault where the old matte-black cipher console sat beneath a thick glass cover.

"Lock the outer doors," Number 1 ordered over his shoulder. "Nobody speaks a word outside this room. I am calling the Founding Elders."
