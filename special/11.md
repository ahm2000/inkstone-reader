# Special Chapter 11: The Ghosts Nobody Survived

The subterranean command hall went so quiet that the faint hum of the air vents sounded like distant wind across a graveyard.

"Thirty-five years ago?" the silver-haired Air Force general whispered, stepping closer to the oak table. "I was a young squadron captain thirty-five years ago. There was no war in the archives."

"Of course it is not in the archives!" Elder Shen snapped from the southern conference screen, his voice trembling with raw, lingering horror. "Every sheet of paper from that night was burned before sunrise! If it had stayed in the archives, half the admirals in the Pacific would have resigned in shame!"

"Shut up, Shen, my chest hurts just remembering it," Elder Ma wheezed, pressing a shaking hand over his heart while his doctor tried to hand him a water cup. Elder Ma waved it away. "It was the winter of the grain blockade. Four foreign battle groups parked just outside our southern strait, backed by three offshore intelligence rings trying to force an emergency treaty before dawn."

"I remember that winter," Premier Gu Chengshan said softly, his eyes narrowing. "The official history says the four foreign fleets suffered a sudden storm and withdrew before morning."

"A storm?!" Elder Lu let out a bitter, terrified laugh on the northern monitor, his fingers digging into the arms of his chair. "There was not a single cloud in the sky that night, Chengshan! The sea was flat as glass! Our regular navy was outnumbered three to one, and the Black Order was still two hours away from cutting their fuel contracts!"

"And then?" the younger two-star general asked, his voice barely above a whisper.

"Then the previous holder of the quiet hand lost his patience," Elder Song said quietly, his voice cracking as cold memories washed over his pale face. "At 11:10 PM, a single white zero flashed on the old southern coastal relay. Just like tonight. Nobody in Beijing knew what it meant. Not even us."

"Eleven minutes," Elder Wei broke in, his voice dropping to a horrified croak as he stared blankly at the tabletop. "It only took eleven minutes. I was sitting in the coastal radar bunker with Old Qin and Old Zhong. Tell them what happened to the screens, Old Zhong!"

Elder Zhong closed his eyes, a visible shudder running through his shoulders. "Every radar dish along four thousand kilometers of coastline went snow-white at once. Not jammed like normal warfare. Erased. We thought our power grid had blown. Then the foreign fleet's emergency voice channel leaked straight onto our speakers."

"What did you hear?" the Naval Commander asked, his palms slick with sweat.

"Screaming," Elder Qin Zhengguo answered grimly, his own proud soldier's face finally losing all its color as the memory dragged him back. "Four-star foreign admirals screaming into dead radios. Their flagship turbines shut off mid-ocean. Their satellite uplinks vanished. Unmarked dark aircraft that looked like shadows dropped out of a clear sky and hovered straight over their flight decks without making a sound loud enough to wake a gull."

"And that was not even the terrifying part," Elder Song interrupted softly, his hands shaking visibly on his cane. "Tell them about the bridges, Qin."

Elder Qin swallowed hard. "At 11:18 PM, the lead foreign admiral's voice suddenly cut off mid-sentence. When his deputy grabbed the microphone three seconds later, he was crying. Literally sobbing like a child. Because six men in plain dark weather coats were already standing inside his locked, armored command bridge with zero alarms tripped, zero hatches forced, and zero shots fired."

A collective gasp rippled around the briefing table.

"Six men boarded an active carrier flagship in open ocean without tripping a single alarm?!" the Air Force general stammered in pure disbelief. "How?! Who trained them?!"

"Nobody knows!" Elder Shen shouted back in frantic terror from the screen. "That is why we are terrified out of our minds tonight! By 11:21 PM, all four foreign battle groups turned around and fled into international waters at full steam, and the three offshore intelligence heads who planned the blockade turned themselves in to their own courts before noon, babbling about ghosts in plain coats!"

"Wait," the younger general whispered, his face white. "When it ended, where did those Zero Guards go? Which base did they return to?"

"Nowhere!" Elder Lu cried, his voice shaking violently. "We sent our best Black Order trackers after them at dawn! We wanted to know who they were, how many of them existed, and where they hid those ships and aircraft! Every single trail vanished into thin air! One of our trackers swore he followed a man from a gray cutter straight to a coastal village, and ten minutes later the man was frying dough sticks at a breakfast stall with forty years of clean household records!"

"You mean," Premier Gu Chengshan stopped himself, his breath catching sharply, "they are everywhere. And even when they are standing right next to us, we cannot tell who they are."

"We cannot tell!" Elder Song whispered, tears of sheer, exhausted dread shining in his old eyes as he looked back up at the solid crimson map of the nation. "We do not know how many thousands of them exist. We do not know where those aircraft sleep, or where those cutters hide. All we know is that they live quietly among regular people until the one man they answer to gets truly angry."

Elder Ma leaned back in his chair, his voice barely a dry rasp. "And tonight, some blind fools in Shanghai drew blood on his wife and threatened his parents. So pray, Chengshan. Pray to heaven that they surrender before sunrise, because if he decides eleven minutes is too gentle, none of us will recognize the map tomorrow morning."
