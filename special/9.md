# Special Chapter 9: Silence in the Capital Command Hall

At 9:45 PM in Beijing, the subterranean national command hall beneath the Western compound was bright enough to pass for midday, yet every man inside felt a chill sinking straight into his bones.

Around the curved oak briefing table stood four senior generals, the Deputy Director of National Security, the Foreign Minister, and Premier Gu Chengshan. Nobody sat down. Every pair of eyes remained locked on the massive curved wall screen displaying the entire country and its surrounding neighbors.

From Harbin to Sanya, from Urumqi to Shanghai, and out across the air and sea approaches of Korea, Japan, and the southern straits, every single transit corridor glowed solid crimson.

"Report," Premier Gu Chengshan said, his voice taut as a drawn bowstring. "Tell me who owns those aircraft and vessels."

The silver-haired Air Force general swallowed hard, his hands visibly shaking as he gripped his blue folder. "Premier, we cannot identify them. At 9:32 PM, a Zero-level override bypassed our central air defense network completely. Unmarked tilt-rotors and high-altitude interceptors launched from sites that do not even appear on our active military registry. Every airport nationwide is grounded, and every neighboring country's air control is holding flights outside our rim."

"And the navy?" Premier Gu demanded, his gaze snapping to the Naval Commander.

"The exact same thing, Premier," the admiral whispered, his face pale with dread. "Unmarked gray cutters and hydrofoils sealed every river mouth, coastal port, and outer strait from the Bohai Sea to the Malacca approaches. Our own fleet radars received a single white zero code ordering them to hold position. Premier, we do not even know how many of these vessels exist or who crews them!"

Before the Premier could answer, the Foreign Minister hurried over from the bank of glowing red hotlines, sweat shining on his forehead.

"Premier, the entire international hotline wall is melting down!" the Foreign Minister said urgently, his voice trembling. "Not just sitting ambassadors, even retired founding elders from other countries are calling our senior channels in sheer panic! Washington (-5:00 GMT) is terrified our satellite blackout is the opening move of a global strike. Moscow (+3:00 GMT) called asking why our northern land borders slammed shut without notice. Tokyo (+9:00 GMT), Seoul (+9:00 GMT), and Singapore (+8:00 GMT) are begging to know what just froze the entire East Asian sky and shipping lanes! And the retired banking patriarch in Zurich (+1:00 GMT) called our envoy in tears, pleading to know whose family was touched in Shanghai!"

A stunned, horrified murmur swept through the generals around the table.

"Who sits above the central military command?!" one general burst out in genuine fear. "Twenty minutes ago, the three Founding Elders woke the Black Order, and even that terrified half the world. Now the Black Order itself has been wiped off the grid by a single white zero! Who are these people?!"

Every head in the hall turned slowly toward the oak pillar near the entrance.

Ninety-year-old Elder Song stood leaning heavily on his wooden cane, his face whiter than winter snow. His hands trembled so visibly against the polished wood that his aide stood half a step behind him, terrified the old man might collapse.

Premier Gu Chengshan walked quickly across the stone floor and stopped in front of him. "Elder Song," Premier Gu said softly, his own voice carrying a rare, unmistakable tremor. "The whole country and half of Asia just stopped moving. Foreign elders are calling us in panic. Tell this room who those Zero forces are, and tell me if we are about to watch a catastrophe."

Elder Song drew a shaky, shallow breath. For the first time in Premier Gu's memory, the ninety-year-old founding elder looked genuinely, deeply afraid.

"Chengshan," Elder Song whispered, his voice barely steady enough to carry across the silent hall. "Even I cannot tell you who all of them are. Neither can Elder Shen nor Elder Lu. We helped build the White Guard and the Black Order decades ago, so we thought we understood the depth of the water. Tonight, I realized we were only standing on the shore."

Dead silence crushed the room. Even the silver-haired Air Force general took an involuntary half-step back.

"Tonight at 8:50 PM in Shanghai," Elder Song continued quietly, careful never to speak a single personal name, "an overseas maritime syndicate backed by three European banks sent hired thugs to ram a young woman's car, shatter her window with a steel pipe, and cut her face. Then they called her husband and threatened to burn his parents' home on a quiet county street before morning."

"And the husband," Premier Gu murmured, his breath catching in his throat.

"Is the man who placed our national reserve back on solid ground last autumn without asking for a title, a seat, or a word of thanks," Elder Song whispered, cold sweat beading at his temple. "When Number 1 told us he was truly furious, all three of us elders were terrified out of our minds. We turned the founding keys to wake the Black Order so they could crush those fools cleanly by the book before he stood up. He refused to play by the book tonight. He bypassed the Black Order, bypassed us, and unleashed the Zero Guard himself."

The entire command hall went so quiet you could hear the faint hum of the ceiling lights.

"Will he stop?" Premier Gu asked softly, his eyes fixed on the solid crimson map of the nation.

Elder Song looked up at the glowing red borders, his fingers trembling on his cane. "Only when the last person who threatened his wife and parents is in chains, Chengshan. Until then, tell Washington (-5:00 GMT), Moscow (+3:00 GMT), Tokyo (+9:00 GMT), and every retired elder calling from abroad to keep their ships and planes still and wait for sunrise."
