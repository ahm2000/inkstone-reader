# Special Chapter 9: The Old Men Who Woke in the Dark

At 9:34 PM, two minutes after the blinding white zero erased the Black Order from the national grid, the quiet courtyards of Beijing's Western Hills compound erupted into pure panic.

Inside his dark bedroom, ninety-year-old Elder Song was already dressed in a heavy wool coat over his sleep shirt, struggling to button his collar with fingers that shook so hard he kept missing the holes. His senior aide rushed through the door holding a flashing red handset.

"Elder Song, the Black Order relay just went completely dead!" the aide gasped, his face as pale as chalk. "Every radar screen in the capital just turned solid crimson! The Prime Minister's office is calling every retired elder in Beijing!"

"Drop the phone," Elder Song wheezed, grabbing his wooden cane with both hands just to keep his knees from buckling. "Get the car to the gate! And wake Elder Qin, Elder Zhong, Elder Ma, and Elder Wei! Tell them if they can still breathe, they need to be inside the subterranean command hall in ten minutes!"

"Elder Song, Elder Ma is ninety-three and his doctor forbade night travel!"

"Tell his doctor if he stays in bed, there may not be a morning to treat him in!" Elder Song snapped, his voice cracking with a terror his aide had never witnessed in thirty years of service. "Move!"

Three courtyards away, retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo was already storming down his front steps with his winter overcoat thrown crookedly over his cotton pajamas. His pager was no longer blinking black. It glowed pure, blinding white with a single zero in the center.

"Where is Old Zhong?!" Elder Qin barked at the panicked security officer holding open his car door.

"Already in the second sedan behind you, General!" the officer stammered. "Elder Ma and Elder Wei are coming straight from the military hospital wing! Even the guard posts at the outer gate have their radios dead!"

"Dead?!" Elder Qin stared up at the dark Beijing sky as two unmarked matte-black interceptors slid silently across the clouds without a single wing light. A cold shiver ran straight down the old general's spine. "Who flies over the Western Hills without transponders?! Drive! Faster!"

Eight minutes later, the heavy steel blast doors of Beijing's subterranean national command hall hissed open.

Inside, Premier Gu Chengshan, the Foreign Minister, and four senior generals spun around in stunned disbelief. They were used to seeing retired elders walk slowly at National Day parades with calm, dignified smiles.

Instead, five of the country's oldest living legends stumbled through the blast doors looking like men fleeing a collapsing mountain.

Elder Song came first, his face bloodless, leaning so heavily on his cane that his knuckles looked like bare bone. Right behind him came Elder Qin Zhengguo in slippers and a crooked overcoat, Elder Zhong clutching his chest as he gasped for air, and ninety-three-year-old Elder Ma supported under both elbows by two pale doctors while eighty-nine-year-old Elder Wei followed with trembling lips.

"Elder Song! Elder Qin! Elder Ma!" Premier Gu Chengshan hurried forward in genuine alarm. "What are you all doing out of bed at this hour? Elder Ma, sit down before your heart gives out!"

"Do not tell me to sit down, Chengshan!" Elder Ma wheezed, pushing his doctor's hand away as he stared up at the wall-sized national map glowing solid crimson from Harbin to Sanya and out across the surrounding countries. His eyes widened in sheer, paralyzed horror. "It is true. The zero code is live."

"You mean you did not authorize it?!" the silver-haired Air Force general blurted out, his voice pitching high with panic. "We thought the Founding Elders escalated from the Black Order to a full national freeze!"

"Escalated?!" Elder Wei laughed once, a dry, terrified sound that made every general in the room go cold. "Boy, we do not have the authority to turn that white key! Nobody in this room does!"

"Then who just locked down the entire country and half of East Asia?!" Elder Qin Zhengguo demanded, his loud soldier's voice echoing sharply off the concrete walls, though even his own fingers were shaking at his sides. "Song! You and Shen and Lu woke the Black Order twenty minutes ago! Why did my pager turn pure white?! Why are unmarked aircraft flying over Beijing?!"

On the side wall, the encrypted conference monitor suddenly flared to life on a backup fiber line, showing the pale, terrified faces of Elder Shen from the southern coast and Elder Lu from the northern mountains.

"Song!" Elder Shen's voice burst through the speakers, shrill with panic. "Tell me you are in the command hall! Tell me Premier Gu is right there!"

"We are all here, Shen!" Elder Song cried back, bracing himself against the edge of the oak briefing table as his legs finally gave out into a chair. "And it is too late! He did not wait for the Black Order!"

"He did not wait?!" Elder Lu gasped on the screen, his hand clutching his throat in sheer terror. "He turned the Zero key himself?! Oh heaven, we are finished! Everyone stop talking and tell me who is on the international hotlines right now!"

Across the huge command hall, not a single general dared to breathe. Watching five men who had survived wars, revolutions, and half a century of global crises shake like frightened children made the air inside the bunker feel ten degrees colder.
