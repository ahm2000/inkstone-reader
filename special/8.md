# Special Chapter 8: The Breathless Country

You did not need a high security clearance or a red desk telephone to feel what had just happened. From the glowing skyline of Shanghai to the misty streets of Chengdu, from snowy northern highways in Harbin to crowded airport terminals in neighboring countries, everyday people stopped in their tracks and felt the world hold its breath.

Along the Bund in Shanghai, hundreds of late-night walkers and office workers froze along the stone railing beside the Huangpu River.

"Look at the river," a young delivery rider muttered, raising his helmet visor in awe. "Not a single barge is moving. Even the sightseeing ferries shut off their lights."

Beside him, a taxi driver stepped out of his cab beneath a row of solid red traffic lights, tilting his head up toward the silent black shapes hovering steadily against the low winter clouds. "My radio died. My navigation map froze. Even my phone lost international roaming," he said softly, his voice tight with unease. "What is happening tonight? Is it a war?"

"No," an old retired sailor whispered beside the railing, his eyes fixed on the unmarked gray cutters blocking the river mouth. "If it were a war, sirens would be screaming. Look at how dead quiet it is. Somebody unimaginable just got angry."

Two thousand kilometers west in Chengdu, a steaming night-market alley fell eerily quiet as every passerby looked up at two dark tilt-rotors sweeping silently over the ring road while highway ramps outside the city blinked solid red. Inside Xi'an North Railway Station, thousands of travelers stood stunned along the bright platforms as every departure board flashed red at the exact same second, holding twelve high-speed trains motionless on their tracks without a single announcement explaining why.

In Yunxi County, Old Wu paused with his bamboo ladle over his noodle pot on Old Street, listening to the sudden, uncanny silence from the distant provincial expressway. Outside Mingde Hardware, Wang Lei stepped down from his silver delivery van and looked toward the overpass where a glowing amber ribbon of truck hazard lights stretched for kilometers.

"Director Wei!" Wang Lei called to Acting Station Director Wei Kun, who stood outside Old Street Police Station staring grimly at his dead police radio. "My freight dispatcher says every expressway from Handong to Jiangsu and Zhejiang is sealed by unmarked black trucks! Nobody knows who they belong to!"

"Keep your van parked and go home, Wang Lei," Wei Kun said quietly, glancing toward the peaceful, darkened courtyard of Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang two doors down, where Chen Xuan's parents slept safely while calm neighborhood regulars strolled the lane with warm tea flasks. "Nobody across the whole country is moving tonight."

Even across the borders in surrounding countries, normal people felt the exact same chilling halt.

Inside the bright departure hall of Incheon International Airport in Seoul (+9:00 GMT), hundreds of passengers crowded beneath the giant flight boards in bewildered murmurs as every single westbound and southbound flight shifted simultaneously to HOLD UNTIL FURTHER NOTICE. A Korean businessman gripped his boarding pass and turned to a nervous gate agent. "My flight to Hong Kong and my colleague's flight to Singapore (+8:00 GMT) both froze at the exact same minute! What happened over the sea?"

The gate agent stared helplessly at her blank terminal, her face pale. "Sir, even regional air control does not know. They just told us the entire sky west of the peninsula is locked shut."

On a foggy fishing dock near the southern strait, a weathered ferryman shut off his diesel engine as sleek gray cutters lit up the mist like ghosts. Over his crackly handheld radio, the harbor master's voice came back trembling. "Tie your rope and stay off the water, Old Chen. Even the foreign cargo lanes outside the strait stopped dead. Even the wind feels afraid to blow tonight."

And inside provincial emergency halls across the entire country, from Guangdong and Sichuan to Heilongjiang and Handong, top local leaders stood frozen in equal, helpless shock.

In Handong's provincial operations hall, Party Secretary Sha Ruijin, the Provincial Governor, and Municipal Secretary Li Dakang stared side by side at a giant national transit map where not just Handong, but every single province from the northern tundra to the southern sea glowed solid crimson. All political rivalry vanished from the room.

"Secretary Sha," the Provincial Governor whispered, his voice hollow with dread. "I just spoke to the governors of Guangdong, Jiangsu, and Sichuan. None of us received a single order from Beijing. Not one ministry knows who owns those black carriers or those aircraft."

Li Dakang's handkerchief trembled in his fingers. "First the black seal froze our customs. Now the whole country and the surrounding seas just stopped breathing without a single official signature. Secretary Sha, who in this world can stop an entire nation and leave even our own governors completely blind?"

Sha Ruijin stared at the solid crimson map, his heart pounding slowly against his ribs, unable to speak a single word.
