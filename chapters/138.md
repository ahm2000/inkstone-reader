# Chapter 138: A Quiet Call from the Twelfth Floor

The 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics smelled faintly of printer ink, damp wool coats, and hot jasmine tea.

Three clerks tapped lazily at old desktop keyboards while the office radiator hissed softly beneath the frost-rimmed window. Outside, golden plane leaves drifted down along the quiet Pudong street in the chilly November breeze.

"Have you seen the market news, Chen Xuan?" Sun Haoran called out around 2:15 PM, leaning casually against the edge of Chen Xuan's wooden desk. "Haiyue Group's stock just fell off a cliff! Down 10% before noon, straight to the floor!"

Chen Xuan set down his black ballpoint pen beside a neat stack of freight receipts.

"I saw the headline at lunch," Chen Xuan replied calmly.

"Ten percent in two hours!" Sun Haoran whistled, scrolling down his phone screen with a worried frown. "And look at these wild posts online. Some overseas account claims customs officers raided Haiyue's third dock for smuggled cargo! Now people are saying four banks in Shanghai are ready to call in 6 billion yuan in loans!"

A younger clerk named Xiao Lin looked up nervously from the desk across the aisle. "If Haiyue Group goes under, half the container trucks on the east ring road will have nothing to haul. Even our small warehouse routes might slow down."

Chen Xuan reached over, unscrewed the lid of his dented green metal thermos, and took a slow, unhurried sip of warm green tea.

"Do not get worked up over anonymous forum posts," Chen Xuan said lightly. "Haiyue owns real ships, real cranes, and clean docks. A company like that does not vanish overnight just because someone yells fire on the internet."

"Easy for you to say," Sun Haoran laughed, shaking his head as he wandered back to his own cubicle. "You never buy stocks anyway. Give you 12,000 yuan a month and a warm thermos, and you could sleep through a typhoon."

Chen Xuan smiled faintly and went back to stamping his delivery slips.

Two minutes later, once the office returned to its quiet afternoon hum, he picked up his single everyday smartphone from beside his keyboard. With a quick thumb swipe, he opened a market app and pulled up Haiyue Group's trading chart.

His easygoing smile faded slightly as his eyes traced the volume bars.

It took him less than ten seconds to read the entire playbook. The sell orders had hit in synchronized blocks of 500 million yuan, routed through offshore channels right as the fake customs rumor flooded retail boards. Someone was trying to pin his wife's company to the floor and choke off her bank credit before Wednesday.

Chen Xuan locked his screen, pushed his chair back, and stood up slowly.

"Heading to the washroom," he murmured to nobody in particular, strolling out past the water cooler.

Instead of turning left toward the restroom, he pushed open the heavy metal door to the quiet concrete stairwell beside the 12th-floor fire exit. The stairwell was empty and cool, lit by a single dusty bulb overhead.

Chen Xuan leaned one shoulder against the painted iron railing, pulled his regular smartphone from his pocket, and tapped a private number with no saved label.

The line connected on the very first ring.

"Young Master," Number 1's steady, respectful voice came through immediately.

"Who is shorting Haiyue Group today?" Chen Xuan asked softly, his tone relaxed yet unmistakably cold beneath the surface.

"Zurich's Aegis Meridian hired an aggressive short-selling fund called Aegis Meridian, led by Julian Ash," Number 1 answered without a second's pause. "They lost their foothold in Handong after Li Weiming's arrest, so they borrowed 8 billion yuan in Haiyue shares through nine offshore shell accounts and dumped them this morning. They also paid media brokers to circulate the forged customs memo. Our intelligence confirms they have another 4 billion yuan in short orders staged for tomorrow's opening bell."

Chen Xuan let out a quiet, amused breath.

"Using 12 billion yuan to bully my wife and buy her docks at a discount," Chen Xuan said calmly. "They really think Shanghai's market is their backyard."

"Shall I have the relevant departments freeze their offshore channels immediately, Young Master?" Number 1 asked.

"No," Chen Xuan replied smoothly. "If you freeze them today, they walk away with a small fine and try again next month. Since they want to play in the open market, let them play by market rules until they have nothing left."

He tapped the iron railing lightly with his fingers and gave two clear instructions.

"First, tell the relevant people in Handong and Shanghai to check Haiyue's actual customs logs right away. Since their cargo is completely clean, make sure the official clearance notice is published before 8:00 AM tomorrow. Clear my wife's name properly."

"Understood, Young Master. That will be handled cleanly," Number 1 confirmed.

"Second, call Gu Shenzhi at the private trust office in Lujiazui," Chen Xuan continued, his voice calm and firm. "Tell him to authorize 20 billion yuan from the mainland reserve. Tomorrow morning, the moment Aegis Meridian drops its short orders at 9:30 AM, buy every single share they dump. Sweep the entire sell wall clean, push the price straight to the upper limit, and lock the float. I do not want Zurich to find a single cheap share to cover their bets."

"Understood, Young Master," Number 1 said crisply. "I will contact Master Gu right now and confirm the staging before evening."

"Good. Do not leave any trace pointing back to me, and do not bother Yuhan at her office."

"Yes, Young Master."

Chen Xuan ended the call and slipped his smartphone back into his jacket pocket. The whole conversation had lasted less than two minutes.

He pushed open the stairwell door, strolled back to his wooden desk on the 12th floor, and neatly finished stamping his last batch of shipping receipts. At 4:55 PM sharp, he packed his dented green metal thermos, clocked out at the punch machine, and headed downstairs to stop by the neighborhood market for fresh lotus root and pork ribs.
