# Chapter 138: A Quiet Call from the Twelfth Floor

At 2:15 on Monday afternoon, the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics smelled faintly of printer ink and hot jasmine tea.

Three clerks tapped lazily at old keyboards while a ceiling fan clicked near the back wall.

"Have you seen the news, Chen Xuan?" Sun Haoran called out, leaning against Chen Xuan's wooden desk. "Haiyue Group's stock just fell off a cliff! Down 10% before noon, right to the floor!"

Chen Xuan set down his pen beside a stack of freight receipts.

"I saw the headline at lunch," Chen Xuan said calmly.

"Ten percent in two hours!" Sun Haoran whistled, scrolling his phone with a worried frown. "And look at these wild rumors online. Some overseas page claims customs officers raided Haiyue's third dock for smuggled goods! Now four banks in Shanghai are threatening to call in 6 billion yuan in loans!"

A younger clerk looked up nervously. "General Manager Sun, we have three trucks picking up containers at Haiyue's second dock Wednesday. Will the docks stay open?"

"Of course they will, keep typing," Sun Haoran waved him back to work, though his own forehead stayed creased. He lowered his voice and leaned closer to Chen Xuan.

"Between you and me, Chen Xuan, I feel awful for Chairwoman Qin Yuhan," Sun Haoran muttered, rubbing his neck. "We saw her Friday at the Sha Provincial Forum. When everyone else was too scared to speak, she stood up and backed your three questions in front of all those cameras. A decent company like Haiyue Group, and now foreign wolves are trying to chew them alive."

"Who are the reports blaming?" Chen Xuan asked mildly.

Sun Haoran squinted at his screen. "Some big overseas hedge fund called Aegis Meridian. Finance chats say they dumped over 8 billion yuan of borrowed shares this morning, and everyone expects them to hit Haiyue even harder tomorrow at the opening bell."

He sighed and patted Chen Xuan's shoulder. "That is big money for you. People like us can only drink our tea and hope Chairwoman Qin gets through the week. I am going downstairs to check Driver Liu's tire repair. Watch the office for twenty minutes."

"Sure thing, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said with a quiet nod.

The glass door swung shut behind Sun Haoran.

Chen Xuan picked up his plain black phone and opened the local financial news.

The top headline showed a sharp red arrow pointing down beside Haiyue Group's name.

Chen Xuan looked at the photograph for three quiet seconds.

He remembered the bright stage lights of the Sha Provincial Forum. She had stood up straight in her navy suit and put Haiyue Group's whole name on the line beside an ordinary clerk she barely knew.

Now Zurich was trying to crush her for it.

Chen Xuan locked his phone, screwed the cap onto his green thermos, and stood up slowly.

"I am stepping out to take a quick call," he told the clerk near the door.

He walked down the hall and pushed open the heavy fire door into the concrete stairwell between the 12th and 13th floors. The stairwell was cool and empty. Afternoon sunlight slanted through a small wired window onto the gray steps.

Chen Xuan walked up four steps to the landing, leaned lightly against the railing, and dialed a private number.

The line connected on the first ring.

"Good afternoon, sir," Number 1's steady voice came through.

"Have you seen the news on Haiyue Group?" Chen Xuan asked simply.

"Yes, sir. I pulled the report at eleven thirty," Number 1 replied immediately. "Northern Ledger in Zurich is behind the attack. After Li Weiming was arrested in Sha City, their senior partner Henrik Lindholm met with Julian Ash, head of an overseas hedge fund called Aegis Meridian, late Sunday night."

"Go on," Chen Xuan said evenly, gazing out the dusty window at the traffic below.

"Northern Ledger backed Aegis Meridian with 10 billion yuan in fresh credit," Number 1 continued crisply. "They planted fake port-smuggling rumors across four foreign wires before the bell, then dumped borrowed shares to knock the price down 10%. Four local banks panicked and threatened to call in 6 billion yuan in loans by Wednesday. Our people in Hong Kong confirmed Aegis Meridian is lining up another 4 billion yuan in short orders for tomorrow morning at nine thirty."

"And Haiyue Group itself?" Chen Xuan asked. "Are their docks clean?"

"Clean from top to bottom, sir," Number 1 answered. "Not a single unlawful container in ten years. Qin Yuhan refused her board's demand to sell two docks this morning, and she is using 500 million yuan of Haiyue's cash to buy back shares this afternoon. Against Aegis Meridian's 4 billion yuan wave tomorrow, Haiyue cannot hold past ten o'clock alone."

Chen Xuan watched a yellow city bus pull away from the stop across the street.

"Qin Yuhan stood up when she did not have to," Chen Xuan said softly. "A person who keeps her word when the wind turns cold should not be left standing in the rain alone."

"Understood, sir," Number 1 said calmly. "How would you like to handle Aegis Meridian?"

Chen Xuan's voice stayed as mild as if he were ordering fresh tea leaves.

"Haiyue Group is clean," Chen Xuan said. "Tell our Shanghai trustee to buy every share Aegis Meridian sells tomorrow morning."

On the other end, there was a brief, appreciative pause.

"Every single share, sir?" Number 1 asked softly. "Not just enough to steady the price?"

"No," Chen Xuan replied plainly. "If they want to sell 4 billion yuan of borrowed shares, let them sell. Buy every last share until the price hits the daily limit and stays locked. Since Northern Ledger wanted to teach Shanghai a lesson, let them pay the tuition."

"Yes, sir," Number 1 said, faint warmth touching his voice. "I will call Master Gu in Shanghai on the verified line right now. He can line up clean domestic and overseas cash across twelve trading desks before midnight. No borrowed money, no trail back to Shanghai, and nothing Aegis Meridian will see coming until nine thirty."

"Good," Chen Xuan said. "Have the provincial office remind the customs bureau to finish their clearance statement on Haiyue's docks quickly. Clean businesses should not wait three days for a stamp."

"It will be on the morning news before the market opens, sir," Number 1 promised.

Chen Xuan ended the call and slipped his phone into his pocket.

Down on the street, a sweeper pushed a bamboo broom along the curb. Fifty meters away beside a newspaper kiosk, a quiet figure in a dark jacket folded a paper and stood watching the traffic in silence.

Chen Xuan pushed open the fire door and walked back to his desk on the 12th floor. He poured another capful of hot jasmine tea from his green thermos and picked up his pen to finish the afternoon receipts.
