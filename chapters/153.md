# Chapter 153: Persimmons and Village Laughter

By midday, the brick courtyard of Grandma Zhou Guilan's house looked like a small autumn market. Rows of freshly dug sweet potatoes dried on straw mats in the pale sunshine, while three overflowing baskets of bright orange persimmons sat neatly beneath the wooden eaves.

Inside the warm kitchen, dry pine branches crackled softly inside the brick stove. Steam rose from a wide iron pot where free range chicken simmered slowly with peeled mountain chestnuts, filling the whole house with a rich, savory aroma.

Grandma tried twice to shoo Qin Yuhan out to the courtyard bench to rest, but Qin Yuhan simply smiled, tied a faded cotton apron over her cream sweater, and stepped up to the wooden chopping board.

"Grandma, let me wash the greens and slice the ginger," Qin Yuhan said calmly, rinsing a crisp bundle of late autumn bok choy in the enamel basin. "At home in Shanghai, Chen Xuan never lets me near the stove. If I do not practice here, I will forget how to hold a knife."

Grandma chuckled warmly, glancing toward the doorway where Chen Xuan stood leaning lazily against the doorframe with his dented green metal thermos in hand.

"Look at him," Grandma teased, shaking her wooden spatula. "All morning he talks big, and the moment the pot starts boiling he stands by the door like a stray cat waiting for a piece of meat. When he was ten years old, he used to climb the persimmon tree behind the wall just to dodge sweeping the yard. Yuhan, you are far too gentle with him."

"I am supervising the fire, Grandma," Chen Xuan replied innocently. "Somebody has to make sure the pine wood does not burn too fast."

When Grandma turned to lift the heavy wooden lid on the steamer, he quietly plucked a warm roasted chestnut from a bowl by the hearth and tucked it straight into Qin Yuhan's palm. Qin Yuhan bit back a smile, peeled the warm nut while Grandma was not looking, and slipped half of it back toward his lips.

Twenty minutes later, Second Uncle Chen Mingde carried the round wooden table out into the sunlit courtyard. Dish after dish appeared on the tabletop: golden braised chicken with chestnuts, crisp stir fried greens with garlic, steamed sweet potatoes split open to show their honey colored centers, pickled radish strips, and a steaming clay pot of tofu soup.

The whole family gathered around the table on wooden stools, laughing and passing bowls back and forth. Grandma kept piling tender pieces of chicken into Qin Yuhan's bowl until a small mountain formed over her rice.

"Eat more, Yuhan," Grandma urged fondly. "Running a big company in Shanghai takes so much energy. You are much too slender."

"Thank you, Grandma. Your cooking tastes ten times better than any hotel restaurant," Qin Yuhan said sincerely, taking a bite of the sweet, tender chestnut chicken. "I do not think I have eaten this well in months."

Halfway through the lively meal, cousin Chen Hao set his chopsticks down neatly across his bowl. He glanced at his younger sister Chen Yuan, took a small breath, and looked earnestly across the table at Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan.

"Brother Xuan, Sister in law," Chen Hao began, his ears turning slightly red with excitement. "I wanted to ask you something. Our school has a short break next weekend after the midterms. Could Chen Yuan and I take the high speed train to Shanghai to visit you for two days?"

Chen Xuan smiled immediately. "Of course you can. You never need to ask so formally. What made you decide to come next weekend?"

Chen Hao's eyes lit up. "I want to visit Fudan University! Ever since middle school, Fudan has been my number one goal for the college entrance exam. Our teacher said walking through the campus and seeing the old lecture halls and physics labs in person really helps keep your spirits up during senior year."

"And I want to see the big library and walk along the river at night!" Chen Yuan chimed in eagerly, holding up her spoon. "We already saved up our own train ticket money from helping Uncle pack walnuts last month!"

Second Uncle Chen Mingde immediately waved a hand, looking mildly embarrassed. "Now hold on, you two. Your brother and sister in law work hard all week. Yuhan has a whole company to manage. We cannot just send two noisy kids to crowd their home."

"Uncle, please do not say that," Qin Yuhan spoke up warmly before Chen Xuan could even answer. "Emerald Riverside has plenty of spare bedrooms, and we are honestly a little too quiet in that big house by ourselves. Next Saturday, I can have my assistant Lin Wei arrange a proper campus tour guide at Fudan so Chen Hao can visit the history hall and the science library without getting turned away at the gate."

Chen Hao's jaw dropped slightly in delight. "Really? A real campus tour? Thank you, Sister in law!"

"And leave the food to me," Chen Xuan added with a grin, topping off Second Uncle's cup of warm tea. "I will make a huge pot of slow braised beef brisket and stir fried river shrimp on Saturday evening, then take you both around the Bund after dinner so Yuhan can put her feet up."

"That settles it," Grandma laughed happily, patting Qin Yuhan's arm. "Look how lucky our Xuan is to have married someone so kind and thoughtful. Hao, when you get to Shanghai, study hard and listen to your brother and sister in law."

"I promise, Grandma!" Chen Hao said, grinning from ear to ear.

The afternoon sun slanted warmly across the brick courtyard as laughter echoed over clinking bowls. Watching his wife chat happily with his grandmother and cousins, Chen Xuan felt a quiet, deep warmth settle in his chest. This simple table, bathed in late autumn light, was worth more than all the vaults in the world.
