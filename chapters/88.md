# Chapter 88: The Trustee's Signature

Midnight rain tapped gently against the tall windows on the fifty-second floor of an understated granite tower in Shanghai's Lujiazui financial district.

Unlike the glittering neon skyscrapers around it, this private floor carried no corporate logo on its directory and employed no receptionist at its bronze doors. Inside the wide, oak-paneled study, the only light came from a green-shaded banker's lamp on a massive rosewood desk.

Behind that desk sat a slender, gray-haired man in his late sixties wearing a dark charcoal wool suit.

He held no public title on any board of directors, yet financial ministers in Beijing and central bankers in London spoke of him only in hushed tones as the Trustee. Three years ago, it was this very gray-haired man who had traveled quietly to Qingyan City in the rain, walked into the private vault of Yunhai Grand Hotel, and placed the matte-black Card 01 into safe custody on behalf of its silent owner.

Since that night three years ago, the Sovereign's vast holding trusts had remained completely undisturbed, compounding silently across energy grids, shipping ports, and national treasury instruments without a single withdrawal order.

At twelve seventeen in the morning, a small silver chime sounded from the recessed encrypted console beside the Trustee's left hand.

The gray-haired Trustee set down his porcelain teacup without haste and leaned forward.

Across the dark glass scrolled Number One's verified authentication seal, followed by Chen Xuan's single calm instruction and a forty-page forensic attachment detailing every Cayman, Singapore, and Zurich shell layer beneath Aegis Meridian Capital.

The Trustee read the single line from Qingyan City twice, his weathered eyes lingering on the quiet restraint of the wording.

Slowly, for the first time in three long years, a faint, genuine smile curved the corners of the gray-haired Trustee's mouth.

"Three years of quiet living, a bus pass, and a clerical desk at a freight company," the Trustee murmured softly. "And when he finally asks us to open the vault, he simply wants an old friend's family left in peace."

He stood gracefully, walked to the recessed steel vault built into the oak wall behind his desk, pressed his palm to the biometric plate, and turned two brass keys in sequence.

With a low click, the steel door swung open. Inside lay a single leather-bound vault ledger resting atop a felt tray.

The Trustee carried the ledger back beneath the banker's lamp, opened it to a fresh ivory page, and drew a black fountain pen from his pocket.

He pressed a discrete brass buzzer beneath the desk. Ten seconds later, the oak doors opened silently and his chief operations deputy, Director Gu Mingchen, stepped inside wearing a crisp dark coat.

"Trustee," Gu Mingchen said quietly, inclining his head in deep respect. "Did the relay come from Qingyan City?"

"It did," the Trustee replied warmly, signing two sovereign-trust authorization sheets with fluid strokes before pressing a square jade seal beside his signature. "Zurich's Northern Ledger sent its offshore hedge fund, Aegis Meridian Capital, to short Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group into a hostile takeover."

Gu Mingchen's eyebrows rose slightly as he scanned the holding tree projected onto the desk terminal.

"Naked shorting through forty-two northbound shells, paired with fabricated debt memos to freeze local credit lines," Gu Mingchen noted with cool disdain. "They are bold."

"They chose the wrong Tuesday," the Trustee said smoothly, sliding both signed authorizations across the rosewood desk.

"Activate four of our Tier-One domestic institutional holding funds before the opening bell," the Trustee instructed clearly. "Route twelve billion yuan in fully cleared, unleveraged cash liquidity through standard exchange custody accounts. Do not break a single market rule, do not use a yuan of margin, and do not reveal the Sovereign's name."

Gu Mingchen took the signed sheets with steady hands. "What is our timing, Trustee?"

"Let Aegis Meridian push Haiyue Group down through Tuesday morning and early afternoon," the Trustee replied, his pale eyes gleaming with quiet steel. "Let their envoy walk into Haiyue Tower thinking he has already won. Let them commit every last borrowed share and naked short contract they can scrape together. Then, at two forty-four in the afternoon, buy everything they threw at the wall in twelve minutes."

Gu Mingchen allowed himself a thin smile. "A textbook institutional short squeeze. Every penny lawful, every share settled in clean cash. When Haiyue locks at limit-up, Aegis Meridian's Asia desk will face instant multi-billion-dollar margin liquidation."

"And send a quiet courtesy memo from our institutional desk to the headquarters of Huadong Commercial Bank, Jincheng Trust, and Donghai Development Bank before two o'clock," the Trustee added mildly. "Let them know four national-grade institutional trusts are taking a long-term strategic stake in Haiyue Group. I imagine their credit freezes will thaw rather quickly."

"It will be done before sunrise," Gu Mingchen promised, bowing briefly before turning swiftly out of the study.

* * *

Six hundred miles north in Beijing, the Emergency Market Surveillance Center of the Central Financial Commission blazed with white fluorescent light despite the two-in-the-morning hour.

At the center of the operations floor, Vice Chairman Zhou Guowei stood behind curved monitors, his expression carved from granite as he reviewed the forensic dossier delivered thirty minutes earlier by Number One's relay channel.

Beside him stood Chief Enforcement Officer Inspector Lu Wenbin, dressed in a pressed dark uniform jacket with a sealed silver briefcase at his feet.

"Look at these clearing logs," Zhou Guowei said coldly, tapping the screen where forty-two Cayman and Singapore brokerage accounts converged into a Zurich master server. "Every single northbound short block was executed without underlying share locates. Pure illegal naked shorting, combined with cross-border market manipulation and fabricated credit rumors."

"The evidence chain is airtight, Vice Chairman," Lu Wenbin reported briskly. "Every server timestamp and wire paying those Hong Kong rumor portals is cryptographically verified."

Zhou Guowei picked up the crimson official stamp from his desk and brought it down firmly onto the Emergency Freeze and Detention Order.

"Effective two forty-five tomorrow afternoon, the Central Financial Commission freezes every mainland clearing account, northbound trading quota, and collateral pool linked to Aegis Meridian Capital," Zhou Guowei ordered sharply. "No capital leaves the border. Every short contract must settle in full."

He turned his stern gaze toward Lu Wenbin.

"Take a four-man joint enforcement team on the six-o'clock flight to Qingyan City," Zhou Guowei commanded. "Coordinate with the Qingyan Financial Crimes Bureau, maintain radio silence until the freeze locks at two forty-five, and then walk straight into Haiyue Tower and arrest Shen Kaiyuan in person."

"Yes, Vice Chairman," Lu Wenbin replied crisply, saluting once before turning toward the elevator.

Back in Shanghai, the gray-haired Trustee closed the leather-bound vault ledger, locked the steel door softly, and stood by the rain-washed window overlooking the dark Huangpu River.

Across the ocean in Zurich, Henrik Lindqvist slept peacefully in his alpine penthouse, completely unaware that the guillotine had already been released.
