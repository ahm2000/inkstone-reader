# Chapter 88: Number 1's Private Call

Late afternoon sun slanted low across Old Street in Yunxi County, turning the worn brick pavement warm gold. Fine glass dust still sparkled along the threshold of Mingde Hardware, while the savory smell of slow-simmered beef broth drifted out from Gong's Noodles next door.

Chen Xuan sat comfortably on an upturned wooden crate beside the open doorframe, resting his green metal thermos on one knee. Two paces away, his uncle Chen Mingde swept the last slivers of shattered glass from the metal door track with a bamboo broom. His left shoulder still moved a little stiffly, though his back stood straighter than it had in days.

Beside the window frame, Chen Xuan's father Chen Jianguo muttered under his breath while tugging a bent aluminum strip loose with a pair of rusted pliers.

"Four hundred and eighty yuan for one sheet of thick glass," Chen Jianguo grumbled, squinting at the receipt tucked into his shirt pocket. "Old Zhou at the glass shop saw us coming from half a street away. He knows Mingde Hardware cannot stay open with cardboard taped across the front."

"Let him earn his fifty yuan profit, Jianguo," Chen Mingde said with an easy laugh, pausing to wipe his brow with the back of his sleeve. "The county offices stamped our reopen permit without a single fee, and the street is quiet again. That is worth ten panes of glass."

From inside the dim, tidy shop, Chen Xuan's mother Liu Meifang stepped out carrying a round tin tray with four chipped porcelain cups and a steaming pot of jasmine tea. "Stop complaining about glass money, both of you. Sit down and drink before it goes lukewarm. Xuan, put that thermos down and have fresh tea."

"Coming, Mom," Chen Xuan said, smiling lightly.

Just as he reached for a cup, the phone inside his jacket pocket vibrated twice against his ribs.

Chen Xuan paused, set his green thermos neatly beside the crate, and stood up. "I am going to take a quick call around the corner, Mom. Save me a cup."

"Do not stand out in the draft too long," Liu Meifang called after him, already pouring golden tea for her husband and brother-in-law.

Chen Xuan strolled ten steps past the hardware store and turned into the narrow brick side alley where laundry lines hung high between two quiet rooftops. A fat tabby cat lay curled on a sun-warmed brick wall, blinking lazily at him. No one else was in sight.

He pulled out the phone and pressed answer.

"Young Master," Number 1's steady, respectful voice came through the receiver.

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said softly, leaning one shoulder against the warm brick wall.

"I am reporting on Yunxi County and Handong Province, Young Master," Number 1 said concisely. "The Yunxi local case file is completely sealed and closed. Every county official and local thug who touched Mingde Hardware has been removed and transferred into provincial custody. Your parents' shop records are clean, and no local office will ever bother Old Street again."

"Good," Chen Xuan replied. "How is Jingzhou City?"

"Everything is moving smoothly, Young Master," Number 1 reported. "Handong Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin and Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang have secured the provincial government. Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping has taken full custody of Deputy Provincial Secretary Gao Yuliang and Shanshui Group head Zhao Ruilong, along with the full evidence ledgers. The White Guard's nationwide stand-down lifted right on schedule, and our teams have pulled back to quiet outer observation."

Chen Xuan watched a dry maple leaf drift down onto the cobblestones beside his sneakers. "And Beijing?"

"Premier Gu Chengshan has convened the top council in the Great Hall for an evening vote," Number 1 answered. "They are launching a national clean-up order tonight to strip the remaining Handa Faction protectors in the central ministries. None of them know your name, Young Master. They are handling the rest on their own."

A brief pause followed on the line.

"Young Master," Number 1 asked carefully, "should I station a discreet White Guard ring two blocks out from Old Street, or press harder on any remaining Shanshui Group accounts?"

"No," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Do not put extra people around Yunxi County. Keep the White Guard invisible, and let Beijing and Handong finish sweeping their own floor. My parents just want to open their hardware store in peace and complain about the price of window glass."

"Understood, Young Master," Number 1 replied immediately, warmth and quiet respect threading his steady tone. "No extra units near Old Street. Every channel stays sealed, and nothing from Handong will ever reach your family's ears."

"That is all," Chen Xuan said. "Get some rest yourself."

"Thank you, Young Master. Good evening."

The line clicked off. Chen Xuan slipped his phone back into his jacket pocket, reached out to scratch the tabby cat once behind its ears, and walked calmly back around the corner into the fading sunlight of Old Street.

Around the small folding table inside the shop doorway, Chen Jianguo was already arguing cheerfully with Chen Mingde over which brand of hinges sold fastest before winter. Liu Meifang slid a steaming porcelain cup across the wood the moment Chen Xuan stepped back inside.

"Who was on the phone so long?" she asked, pushing a plate of warm sesame cakes toward him. "Your supervisor from the office?"

"Just an old friend checking in to see if the shop window was fixed," Chen Xuan said easily.

He sat back down on his wooden crate, wrapped both hands around the warm cup of jasmine tea, and listened to his father laugh as evening lamps began to flicker awake along Old Street.
