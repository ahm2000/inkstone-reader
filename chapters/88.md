# Chapter 88: Number 1's Private Call

Late afternoon sun slanted across Old Street, shining off the last tiny bits of broken glass along the doorstep of Mingde Hardware. From next door, the warm smell of beef broth drifted out of Gong's Noodles.

Chen Xuan crouched beside the doorframe with a metal dustpan and a short straw broom, sweeping fine glass grit from the track. Beside him, Chen Mingde leaned on a long broom. His left shoulder was still stiff, though he stood straighter than he had in days. Two steps over, Chen Jianguo muttered while pulling a bent metal strip off the window frame with pliers.

"Four hundred and eighty yuan for one thick glass pane," Chen Jianguo grumbled, squinting at the metal. "The glass shop owner saw us coming a mile away. He knows Mingde Hardware cannot open with cardboard taped over the front."

"Let him earn his fifty yuan profit, Chen Jianguo," Chen Mingde said, wiping his brow with his sleeve. Easy relief filled his voice. "The provincial inspection team brought me straight home this morning with the red-stamped cleared-name paper in my hand. Nobody on Old Street is whispering behind their curtains anymore. Four hundred and eighty yuan is cheap."

Wu Lanying stepped to the doorway carrying a tray with three chipped mugs of jasmine tea. She glared straight at Chen Jianguo's bare fingers on the sharp metal edge.

"Put gloves on, Chen Jianguo!" Wu Lanying scolded, setting the tray on an upturned crate. "You want to bleed over the clean doorstep right after Chen Xuan swept it? And Chen Mingde, stop leaning on that broom like you are twenty-five. Drink your tea."

"I am fine, Wu Lanying," Chen Mingde said with a grin, though he immediately leaned the broom against the doorframe and took a mug. "Chen Xuan did ninety percent of the sweeping anyway."

"Because Chen Xuan actually listens," Wu Lanying said, handing Chen Xuan a mug with a warm look. "Take a drink before you empty that dustpan. Liu Meifang called ten minutes ago to ask if you are coming home for braised pork ribs."

"Tell her I will be back before seven," Chen Xuan said easily, setting the dustpan beside the doorframe.

Before he could take a sip, the second black phone in his left pants pocket buzzed twice. Short, crisp pulses.

Chen Xuan's face did not change at all. He set his mug beside Wu Lanying's tray and dusted his hands together.

"Work call," Chen Xuan told Chen Jianguo and Chen Mingde casually. "Probably the office clerk asking about Monday's forms. I will take it in the alley where it is quieter."

"Go ahead," Chen Jianguo waved him off with the pliers. "Tell your supervisor you took the afternoon to help your uncle sweep glass. If he complains, tell him to call me."

Chen Xuan nodded slightly and walked ten steps down the sidewalk, turning into the narrow alley beside Gong's Noodles. Empty soy-sauce crates sat stacked against the brick wall, and an orange stray cat blinked lazily on the top ledge.

Chen Xuan pulled out the black phone, pressed answer, and raised it to his ear.

"Speak," Chen Xuan said quietly.

"Chen Xuan," Number 1's voice came through the private line, crisp and with zero wasted words. "Black Pine Guard's nationwide stand-down lifted at five-oh-four. Both guards at Lanshui and all guards across the other thirteen provincial stations left at the same time without trouble."

"Any friction?" Chen Xuan asked, watching the stray cat flick an ear.

"None," Number 1 replied. "Every agent followed the lockdown rules cleanly. All lines are green, and all hallway video at our safehouses has been wiped."

Chen Xuan hummed softly. "And Yunxi?"

"Cleanly closed," Number 1 said, his tone sharp and exact. "The Provincial Joint Inspection Group filed the full cleared-name papers for Chen Mingde before noon. Local police, district officials, and city lawyers have all signed off. Zero trouble touches Old Street, Mingde Hardware, or anyone in your family. The thugs under Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu are locked up waiting for formal charges, and Zhao Dehai's provincial gang is completely broken."

"Good," Chen Xuan said, his voice as calm as if he were checking a store receipt.

A brief silence followed. For half a second, Chen Xuan heard the faint tap of Number 1's fingers on a desk. Even the head of Black Pine Guard was still taking in the weight of the last twenty-four hours.

"In the capital," Number 1 went on, lowering his voice, "forty-two sealed evidence cases were delivered straight to the central office and the military commission. Every money channel, foreign shell firm, and top backer tied to Deng Yongchang over the last twenty years is exposed beyond appeal. Forty national departments are meeting in the Great Hall at eight-thirty tonight to sign a joint clean-up and money recovery order. Full legal charges start before midnight."

"Then the state has everything it needs," Chen Xuan remarked calmly.

"More than they ever dared ask for," Number 1 said, a guarded hint of awe slipping under his steady tone. "The central cyber office checked the cases and satellite records all afternoon. They found nothing. Not a fingerprint, not a flight path, not a single trail pointing toward Yunxi or toward you."

"As it should be," Chen Xuan said.

Another quiet beat stretched across the line.

"Chen Xuan," Number 1 said carefully, weighing every word. "My people in Lanshui and the capital are shaken. They saw what happened in those four hours. They know Black Pine Guard was locked in its own rooms while something far above our reach tore apart a twenty-year national web. Do you have any further orders for Black Pine Guard, or for the local lines around Yunxi?"

Chen Xuan glanced toward the bright mouth of the alley, where Chen Mingde was arguing cheerfully with Chen Jianguo over how straight the new trim should sit. The orange cat hopped down from the crates and trotted toward the noodle shop's back door.

"Good," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Keep everyone quiet and go back to normal routines."

On the other end, Number 1 let out a quiet, steady breath. "Understood. No unplanned moves, no chatter, no questions about last night. Every station goes back to normal daily cover at once."

"And tell Number 7 not to overthink things," Chen Xuan added lightly. "A quiet year is better for everyone."

"I will pass that straight to him," Number 1 answered crisply. "Have a peaceful evening, Chen Xuan."

The line clicked dead.

Chen Xuan slipped the black phone back into his pocket, walked out of the alley, and picked up his mug of jasmine tea. Still warm.

"Office sorted?" Chen Mingde asked over the rim of his own mug.

"All sorted," Chen Xuan said. He took a slow sip before bending to lift a heavy cardboard box of brass hinges off the sidewalk. "Nothing urgent. Just routine paperwork."

"Good," Wu Lanying said briskly from the doorway. "Carry those hinges inside before the glass arrives, and wash your hands. Chen Jianguo, drop that jagged strip right now!"

Chen Xuan carried the box past the clean doorstep into the familiar smell of wood and machine oil, while two thousand kilometers away, forty ministries braced for the longest night in twenty years.
