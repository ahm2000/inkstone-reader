# Chapter 88: Number One's Private Call

Late afternoon sun slanted across Old Street, glinting off the last splinters of shattered glass along the threshold of Mingde Hardware. From next door, the warm smell of beef broth drifted out of Gong's Noodles.

Chen Xuan crouched beside the doorframe with a metal dustpan and a short straw broom, sweeping fine glass grit from the track. Beside him, Chen Mingde leaned on a long broom, his left shoulder still stiff though his posture stood straighter than it had in days. Two steps over, Chen Jianguo muttered while prying a bent aluminum strip off the window frame with pliers.

"Four hundred and eighty yuan for one tempered pane," Chen Jianguo grumbled, squinting at the metal. "The glass depot owner saw us coming a mile away. He knows Mingde Hardware cannot open with cardboard taped across the front."

"Let him earn his fifty yuan margin, Chen Jianguo," Chen Mingde said, wiping his brow with his sleeve. Calm relief sat easy in his voice. "The provincial inspection group brought me straight home this morning with the red-stamped exoneration certificate in my hand. Nobody on Old Street is whispering behind their curtains anymore. Four hundred and eighty yuan is cheap."

Wu Lanying stepped to the doorway carrying an enamel tray with three chipped mugs of jasmine tea. She glared straight at Chen Jianguo's bare fingers on the sharp aluminum edge.

"Put gloves on, Chen Jianguo!" Wu Lanying scolded, setting the tray on an upturned crate. "You want to bleed over the clean threshold right after Chen Xuan swept it? And Chen Mingde, stop leaning on that broom like you are twenty-five. Drink your tea."

"I am fine, Wu Lanying," Chen Mingde protested with a grin, though he immediately leaned the broom against the doorframe and took a mug. "Chen Xuan did ninety percent of the sweeping anyway."

"Because Chen Xuan actually listens," Wu Lanying said, handing Chen Xuan a mug with a softening look. "Drink a mouthful before you empty that dustpan. Liu Meifang called ten minutes ago to ask if you are coming home for braised pork ribs."

"Tell her I will be back before seven," Chen Xuan said easily, setting the dustpan beside the doorframe.

Before he could sip, the secondary matte-black phone in his left trouser pocket vibrated twice. Short, crisp pulses.

Chen Xuan's expression did not shift by a millimeter. He set his mug beside Wu Lanying's tray and dusted his palms together.

"Work call," Chen Xuan told Chen Jianguo and Chen Mingde casually. "Probably the department clerk asking about Monday's forms. I will take it in the alley where it is quieter."

"Go ahead," Chen Jianguo waved him off with the pliers. "Tell your supervisor you took the afternoon to help your uncle sweep glass. If he complains, tell him to call me."

Chen Xuan nodded slightly and walked ten paces down the pavement, stepping into the narrow alley beside Gong's Noodles. Empty soy-sauce crates sat stacked against the brick wall, and an orange stray cat blinked lazily on the top ledge.

Chen Xuan pulled out the black phone, pressed answer, and raised it to his ear.

"Speak," Chen Xuan said quietly.

"Chen Xuan," Number One's voice came through the encrypted channel, crisp and stripped of wasted words. "Black Pine Guard's nationwide stand-down lifted at five-oh-four. Both monitors at Lanshui and all monitors across the other thirteen provincial nodes withdrew simultaneously without incident."

"Any friction?" Chen Xuan asked, watching the stray cat flick an ear.

"None," Number One replied. "Every operative followed the freeze protocol cleanly. Relays are green, and all local corridor footage at our safehouses has been purged."

Chen Xuan hummed softly. "And Yunxi?"

"Cleanly closed," Number One said, tone tightening with precision. "The Provincial Joint Inspection Group filed the full dismissal and exoneration record for Chen Mingde before noon. Local police, district cadres, and municipal prosecutors have all signed off. Zero blowback touches Old Street, Mingde Hardware, or anyone in your family. The thugs under Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu are in formal custody awaiting indictment, and Zhao Dehai's provincial faction is completely neutralized."

"Good," Chen Xuan said, his voice as level as if he were checking a grocery receipt.

A brief silence followed. For half a second, Chen Xuan heard the faint tap of Number One's fingers against a desk, a rare sign that even the head of Black Pine Guard was still absorbing the weight of the last twenty-four hours.

"In the capital," Number One continued, lowering his voice, "forty-two sealed evidence cases were delivered straight to the central secretariat and the military commission. Every financial conduit, offshore shell, and senior patron tied to Deng Yongchang over the last twenty years is exposed beyond appeal. Forty national departments are convening in the Great Hall at eight-thirty tonight to sign a joint clean-up and asset recovery order. Full lawful prosecution starts before midnight."

"Then the state has everything it needs," Chen Xuan remarked calmly.

"More than they ever dared ask for," Number One said, a guarded tremor of awe slipping beneath his discipline. "The central cyber bureau audited the cases and orbital telemetry all afternoon. They found nothing. Not a fingerprint, not a flight path, not a single trail pointing toward Yunxi or toward you."

"As it should be," Chen Xuan said.

Another quiet beat stretched across the line.

"Chen Xuan," Number One said carefully, weighing every word. "My people in Lanshui and the capital are rattled. They saw what happened in those four hours. They know Black Pine Guard was locked in its own rooms while something far above our clearance dismantled a twenty-year national web. Do you have any further instructions for Black Pine Guard, or for the provincial lines around Yunxi?"

Chen Xuan glanced toward the bright mouth of the alley, where Chen Mingde was arguing cheerfully with Chen Jianguo over how straight the new trim should sit. The orange cat hopped down from the crates and trotted toward the noodle shop's back door.

"Good," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Keep everyone quiet and return to normal routines."

On the other end, Number One let out a quiet, steady breath. "Understood. No unauthorized movement, no chatter, no inquiries into last night. Every node returns to standard cover posture immediately."

"And tell Number Seven not to overthink things," Chen Xuan added lightly. "A quiet year is better for everyone."

"I will pass that directly to him," Number One answered crisply. "Have a peaceful evening, Chen Xuan."

The line clicked dead.

Chen Xuan slipped the black phone back into his pocket, walked out of the alley, and picked up his mug of jasmine tea. Still warm.

"Office sorted?" Chen Mingde asked over the rim of his own mug.

"All sorted," Chen Xuan said, taking a slow sip before bending to lift a heavy cardboard box of brass hinges off the pavement. "Nothing urgent. Just routine paperwork."

"Good," Wu Lanying said briskly from the doorway. "Carry those hinges inside before the glass arrives, and wash your hands. Chen Jianguo, drop that jagged strip right now!"

Chen Xuan carried the box past the clean threshold into the familiar smell of timber and machine grease, while two thousand kilometers away, forty ministries braced for the longest night in twenty years.
