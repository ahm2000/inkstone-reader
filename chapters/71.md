# Chapter 71: The 8:30 AM Roll Call

At twenty-five minutes past eight on Monday morning, the third-floor meeting room of the Yunxi County Government Compound smelled of cheap cigarettes and old tea. Forty county department heads sat stiffly around the long tables. Their eyes kept jumping between the wall clock and their dark phone screens.

At the front table, County Magistrate Sun Weiming tapped ash into a glass tray. His fingers were shaking. Beside him, Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong leaned closer. His collar pinched tight against his sweaty neck as he lowered his voice.

"Did your city phone line come back up?" Sun Weiming asked without turning his head.

Liang Guodong forced a dry laugh. "Still dead. My duty clerk checked the phone center at seven. The workers swore a main cable snapped near the river bridge during the storm last night. It is just a cut line, Sun Weiming. Stop looking like you saw a ghost."

"There was no storm last night, Liang Guodong," Sun Weiming hissed. He took a hard drag on his cigarette until the paper crackled. "The sky was clear at midnight. One minute I was drinking tea with Chairman Deng Yongchang's secretary. The next minute every phone tower in Yunxi County dropped to zero for three hours. Even my brother-in-law in the city office has not called me back."

"City officials sleep late on Mondays," Liang Guodong muttered. He pressed the side button on his silent phone again. "By ten o'clock the lines will work again. By noon, Old Street will be half cleared."

Sun Weiming blew out a thin stream of smoke. "What about that hardware-store owner, Chen Mingde?"

"Locked tight in Holding Cell Two at Old Street Station," Liang Guodong said with a thin smile. "Cheng Biao handled the papers. Forty-eight hours on a cold bench, and Chen Mingde will beg to sign over his shop."

Near the back of the room, Land Resources Director Ma Zhenyu leaned toward Urban Planning Chief Xu Wenbin. "Did you see the highway overpass at eight?" Ma Zhenyu whispered, tapping his fingers on the table. "Black provincial license plates. Over twenty vans came off the ramp."

Sun Weiming's jaw snapped shut. He crushed his cigarette into the glass tray and tapped the microphone hard.

"Quiet!" Sun Weiming barked. Forty heads snapped up at once. "This is the Monday morning county roll call, not a vegetable market! Ma Zhenyu, tell me why the Old Street land permits are still sitting unsigned on your desk!"

Ma Zhenyu's face turned red. "County Magistrate Sun Weiming, the fire-safety check is not finished yet. Also, the people living there turned in signed complaints yesterday."

"There are no complaints!" Sun Weiming snapped, glaring down the aisle. "The Hongrui Real Estate building plan is a top county project! Anyone who slows it down will answer to the Discipline Office personally! Now open your binders to page four."

The heavy double doors at the back of the room slammed open. The brass handles cracked loud against the plaster wall.

Every voice stopped dead. Forty officials spun around in their chairs.

A tall man in a dark gray suit walked straight down the aisle. Behind him marched twelve officers in dark windbreakers with the gold badges of the Provincial Discipline Office and the Supreme Prosecutor's Office. Four uniformed court officers stepped up and blocked both doors.

Sun Weiming's hand froze on his teacup. All the color drained out of Liang Guodong's face in two heartbeats, leaving his skin pale and gray.

The tall man stopped at the front table. He did not offer to shake hands. "I am Shen Wenchao, Deputy Team Leader of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group," he said. His sharp voice cut right through the smoky air.

He slapped two red-stamped arrest warrants flat onto the wooden table. Sun Weiming's teacup rattled, spilling warm tea across his binder and coat sleeve.

"Sun Weiming," Shen Wenchao said, his eyes locked on the magistrate's shaking face. "Liang Guodong. Stand up."

For three long seconds, neither man moved. Ma Zhenyu's pen rolled off his desk and hit the tile floor with a loud clatter. Nobody reached down to pick it up.

"Wait a minute!" Sun Weiming blurted out. He shoved his chair back with a loud scrape and forced a sweaty smile. "Comrade Shen Wenchao, there has to be a huge mistake! Nobody told the Yunxi County Party Committee you were coming today! Even a normal check needs notice from the city first."

"This is not a normal check," Shen Wenchao cut in coldly. "By joint warrant of the Provincial Discipline Office and the Supreme Prosecutor's Office, you are both under immediate arrest. You are charged with taking bribes, abusing your power, working with a crime group, and faking evidence."

Liang Guodong jumped to his feet. His jacket was wrinkled, and his face twisted with panic and anger. "Faking evidence? That is a lie! I have worn this badge for twenty-two years! You cannot march in here without telling City Secretary Li Weiming! I demand to call the city office right now!"

"City Secretary Li Weiming is already answering questions of his own," Shen Wenchao replied without blinking. "As for your twenty-two years in uniform, Liang Guodong, maybe you remember last Thursday night. You were in the private bamboo room at Jade Spring Teahouse."

Liang Guodong's mouth froze wide open.

Shen Wenchao flipped open the top folder and read in a flat, clear voice. "At nine forty-two in the evening, Sun Weiming told Chairman Deng Yongchang, and I quote: Sign the sixty-percent discount price on the Old Street land tomorrow, and I will push the land stamp through before Monday. Send the 3 million fee to my nephew's Shenzhen bank account by Friday."

A sharp gasp swept through the forty seated officials.

"That is a lie!" Sun Weiming shouted. His voice cracked thin as his fingers gripped the edge of the table. "Fake recording! Somebody is setting me up! I never stepped foot inside Jade Spring Teahouse!"

Shen Wenchao turned the page and fixed his cold stare on Liang Guodong. "Four minutes later, at nine forty-six, Liang Guodong raised his teacup to Deng Yongchang and said, and I quote: Give Cheng Biao the green light on Old Street. Lock up one stubborn hardware-store owner for seventy-two hours on an assault charge, and the whole street will give up before noon."

Heavy silence crushed the room. The only sound was Liang Guodong's ragged, wheezing breath.

Sun Weiming's knees gave out. He had to catch himself with both palms on his tea-soaked binder. His forgotten cigarette slipped from his shaking fingers onto his pants, burning a small black hole he did not even feel.

"How did you get that?" Liang Guodong whispered, his eyes wide with terror. "There were only three of us in that room. All the windows were shut tight."

"Your bank transfers, your nephew's Shenzhen shell company, the fake hospital file, and Hongrui Real Estate's account books are already in provincial hands," Shen Wenchao said. He snapped the folder shut. "Take them away."

Four officers stepped behind the front table.

"Do not touch me!" Sun Weiming shrieked. He kicked and thrashed as strong hands twisted his arms behind his back. "I am the County Magistrate! Call Deng Yongchang!"

Steel handcuffs clicked shut around Sun Weiming's wrists. Beside him, Liang Guodong slumped forward without fighting back. His police cap fell off the front table and rolled away as a second pair of cuffs locked over his shaking hands.

Officers marched both gray-faced men down the aisle past forty stunned officials. Not a single person in the room dared to meet their panicked eyes.

Once the heavy doors closed, Shen Wenchao rested both hands on the front table and looked out over the quiet room.

"Starting at eight thirty this morning, the Provincial Joint Inspection Group is taking full control of Yunxi County," Shen Wenchao said calmly. "Every Hongrui Real Estate demolition order is stopped right now. Those of you with clean hands stay in your seats and get your account books ready. Those who took cash envelopes from Deng Yongchang have thirty minutes to walk into the side office and confess. After nine o'clock, there will be no mercy."

Nobody in the room dared to breathe.
