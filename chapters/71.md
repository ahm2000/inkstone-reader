# Chapter 71: The 8:30 AM Roll Call

At twenty-five minutes past eight on Monday morning, the third-floor conference room of the Yunxi County Government Compound smelled of cheap tobacco and stale tea. Forty county department directors sat stiffly along the tables, eyes flicking between the wall clock and their dark phone screens.

At the front dais, County Magistrate Sun Weiming tapped ash into a glass tray with unsteady fingers. Beside him, Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong leaned in, collar pinched tight against a damp neck, and lowered his voice.

"Did your municipal line come back up?" Sun Weiming asked without turning his head.

Liang Guodong forced a dry chuckle. "Still dead. My duty clerk checked the telecom hub at seven, and the engineers swore a trunk cable snapped near the river bridge during the storm last night. Just a fiber outage, Sun Weiming. Stop looking like someone walked over your grave."

"There was no storm last night, Liang Guodong," Sun Weiming hissed, dragging hard on his cigarette until the paper crackled. "The sky was clear at midnight. One minute I was drinking tea with Chairman Deng Yongchang's secretary, the next minute every signal tower in Yunxi County dropped to zero for three hours. Even my brother-in-law in the municipal office has not returned a call."

"Municipal officials sleep late on Mondays," Liang Guodong muttered, thumb pressing the side button of his silent phone. "By ten o'clock the lines will hum again, and by noon Old Street will be half cleared."

Sun Weiming exhaled thin smoke. "What about that hardware-store owner, Chen Mingde?"

"Locked tight in Holding Cell Two at Old Street Station," Liang Guodong said, smiling thinly. "Cheng Biao handled the paperwork. Forty-eight hours on a cold bench, and Chen Mingde will beg to sign the waiver."

Near the back, Land Resources Director Ma Zhenyu leaned toward Urban Planning Chief Xu Wenbin. "Did you see the highway overpass at eight?" Ma Zhenyu whispered, fingers drumming. "Provincial black plates. Over twenty vans coming off the ramp."

Sun Weiming's jaw snapped shut. He crushed his cigarette into the tray and rapped the microphone.

"Quiet!" Sun Weiming barked. Forty heads jerked up. "This is the Monday morning county roll call, not a vegetable market! Ma Zhenyu, explain why the Old Street commercial rezoning permits are still unsigned on your desk!"

Ma Zhenyu flushed red. "County Magistrate Sun Weiming, the fire-safety review is incomplete, and residents filed formal petitions yesterday."

"There is no petition!" Sun Weiming snapped, glaring down the aisle. "The Hongrui Real Estate redevelopment plan is a priority county project! Anyone who drags their feet answers to Discipline Inspection personally! Open your binders to page four."

The heavy double doors at the rear slammed open, brass handles cracking against the plaster.

Every voice died. Forty cadres spun in their chairs.

A tall man in a charcoal suit strode down the aisle. Behind him marched twelve officers in dark windbreakers bearing the gold emblems of the Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection and the Supreme People's Procuratorate. Four uniformed judicial officers sealed both exits.

Sun Weiming's hand froze on his teacup. Blood drained from Liang Guodong's cheeks in two heartbeats, leaving his skin gray as cold lard.

The tall man halted at the dais without offering a handshake. "I am Shen Wenchao, Deputy Team Leader of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group," he announced, voice slicing through the cigarette haze.

He slammed two crimson-stamped warrants flat onto the wood. Sun Weiming's cup rattled, splashing lukewarm tea across his binder and sleeve.

"Sun Weiming," Shen Wenchao said, eyes locked onto the magistrate's trembling face. "Liang Guodong. Stand up."

For three seconds, neither man moved. Ma Zhenyu's pen rolled off his desk and clattered across the tile. Nobody picked it up.

"Wait a minute!" Sun Weiming blurted out, shoving his chair back with a harsh screech and forcing a sweating smile. "Comrade Shen Wenchao, there is a terrible misunderstanding! Nobody notified the Yunxi County Party Committee today! Even a routine audit requires prior municipal notice."

"This is not a routine audit," Shen Wenchao cut in coldly. "By joint warrant of the Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection and the Supreme People's Procuratorate, you are both under immediate criminal detention for bribery, abuse of authority, collusion with an organized-crime syndicate, and fabrication of criminal evidence."

Liang Guodong shot to his feet, jacket rumpled, face twisting with panicked fury. "Fabrication of evidence? That is slander! I have worn this badge for twenty-two years! You cannot march in here without notifying Municipal Secretary Li Weiming! I demand to call the municipal bureau now!"

"Municipal Secretary Li Weiming is currently answering questions of his own," Shen Wenchao replied without blinking. "As for your twenty-two years in uniform, Liang Guodong, perhaps you recall last Thursday night in the private bamboo room of Jade Spring Teahouse."

Liang Guodong's mouth froze open.

Shen Wenchao flipped open the top folder and read flatly. "At nine forty-two in the evening, Sun Weiming told Chairman Deng Yongchang, and I quote: Sign the sixty-percent discount appraisal on the Old Street parcel tomorrow, and I will push the zoning stamp through before Monday. Deposit the 3 million consulting fee into my nephew's Shenzhen account by Friday."

A sharp collective gasp swept the forty seated cadres.

"That is a lie!" Sun Weiming shouted, voice cracking thin as his fingers clawed the table edge. "Forged tape! Political framing! I never set foot in Jade Spring Teahouse!"

Shen Wenchao turned the page and fixed his cold gaze on Liang Guodong. "Four minutes later, at nine forty-six, Liang Guodong raised his cup to Deng Yongchang and said, and I quote: Give Cheng Biao the green light on Old Street. Lock up one stubborn hardware-store owner for seventy-two hours on an assault charge, and the whole street's spine will snap before noon."

Dead silence crushed the room, broken only by Liang Guodong's wheezing breath.

Sun Weiming's knees buckled, forcing him to brace both palms on the tea-soaked binder. His forgotten cigarette slipped from his trembling hand onto his trousers, scorching a tiny black hole he did not feel.

"How did you get that?" Liang Guodong whispered, eyes glassy with terror. "There were only three of us in that room. The windows were shut."

"Your bank transfers, your nephew's Shenzhen shell company, the backdated hospital file, and Hongrui Real Estate's ledgers are already in provincial custody," Shen Wenchao said, snapping the folder shut. "Take them."

Four officers stepped behind the dais.

"Do not touch me!" Sun Weiming shrieked, thrashing as strong hands twisted his arms behind his back. "I am the County Magistrate! Call Deng Yongchang!"

Steel clicked sharply around Sun Weiming's wrists. Beside him, Liang Guodong collapsed inward without resistance, his police cap sliding off the dais as a second pair of cuffs locked over his shaking hands.

As officers marched both gray-faced men down the aisle past forty stunned subordinates, not one cadre met their frantic eyes.

Shen Wenchao rested both palms on the podium once the doors shut.

"From eight thirty this morning, the Provincial Joint Inspection Group assumes full oversight of Yunxi County," Shen Wenchao stated evenly. "Every Hongrui Real Estate demolition order is suspended immediately. Those with clean hands stay seated and prepare your ledgers. Those who accepted envelopes from Deng Yongchang have thirty minutes to step into the side office and confess. After nine o'clock, leniency expires."

Nobody in the room dared to breathe.
