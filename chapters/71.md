# Chapter 71: The Hidden Server Clause

Night settled softly over Lanshui City, painting the wide river below in ribbons of gold and amber reflection.

Downstairs in the hotel dining hall, the evening buffet had been lavish, with carved roast duck, steamed river bass, and rows of warm pastries. Yet at Table Twelve, General Manager Sun Haoran had barely touched his bowl of noodles, staring silently at his fork, while Manager Hu Dewei had sighed heavily through two small plates of stir-fried greens.

"If Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang signs that pilot decree tomorrow morning, we might as well sell half our trucks before New Year," Hu Dewei had muttered gloomily.

"Eat something warm first, Manager Hu Dewei," Chen Xuan had replied gently, passing him a plate of steamed dumplings. "Worrying on an empty stomach never solved a single contract problem. Tomorrow morning is still twelve hours away."

After making sure Sun Haoran drank a full cup of hot barley tea and went up to rest, Chen Xuan returned quietly to his own standard room on the twelfth floor.

First, he rinsed his scratched green thermos in the bathroom sink, set it neatly on the desk, and placed a quick five-minute phone call back to Room 601 in Qingyan City.

"Yes, Mom, the hotel room is very clean and quiet," Chen Xuan said warmly, smiling as he heard Liu Meifang's lively voice on the line and Chen Jianguo chuckling in the background. "The buffet had roast duck and steamed river bass tonight. Tell Tang Xiaoyu I already spotted the old walnut pastry bakery two blocks from the train station, so I will buy two boxes before we head back on Wednesday."

"Make sure you sleep early and do not catch a chill from the air conditioning," Liu Meifang reminded him fondly. "And tell General Manager Sun Haoran we send our regards."

"I will, Mom. Goodnight, Dad," Chen Xuan replied gently.

After hanging up, Chen Xuan turned off the overhead chandelier and switched on the single warm reading lamp on the wooden writing desk.

Quietness filled Room 1208.

Chen Xuan sat down, pulled the glossy blue Wanfeng Freight Group contract booklet toward him, and opened it cleanly to page forty-eight. From his shirt pocket he drew a plain red ballpoint pen that he normally used for checking Tang Xiaoyu's math homework and marking discrepancies on county dispatch sheets.

Under the yellow pool of lamplight, his calm eyes moved steadily across Article 14, Section 3.

With slow, unhurried strokes, the red pen drew neat, straight lines beneath three buried technical phrases.

*Designated Cross-Border Analytics Partner: Helvetia Transit Analytics AG, Zurich.*

*Mandatory Unfiltered API Mirroring: Real-time port bills of lading, customs weight certifications, and rare-earth mineral corridor schedules.*

*Exemption Clause: Encrypted telemetry packets bypass municipal staging gateways to prevent latency.*

Beside the third line, Chen Xuan drew a single neat red circle around the Zurich holding name. He set the red pen down cleanly along the spine of the booklet and leaned back slightly in his chair, his expression utterly peaceful yet clear as cold autumn water.

Everything from the past week now connected without a single loose thread.

Li Weiming's smooth questioning beside the pastry trays had not been casual small talk, and Hengxin Logistics' sudden invitation to a provincial summit had not been an administrative fluke. Back in Yunxi County, the Northern Ledger syndicate had watched its underground financial clearing channels, its local banking shells, and thirty-two protected intermediaries collapse in a single night when the Zero Guard severed their network.

Stripped of their old provincial conduits, the overseas syndicate was desperate to rebuild a permanent pair of eyes inside the coastal corridor.

Rather than smuggling bribes through local banks again, Li Weiming had poured offshore capital into Song Baisheng's Wanfeng Freight Group and bought Deputy Provincial Governor Wu Zhenbang's administrative signature. By forcing every regional logistics company in Lanshui Province onto one mandatory smart-terminal platform, the Northern Ledger would not merely monopolize local trucking fees.

They would rebuild an automated overseas intelligence pipeline right under the province's nose, siphoning live shipping data on coastal ports, industrial supply chains, and strategic rare-earth transit straight to a cloud server in Zurich.

And by inviting Chen Xuan to Lanshui City, Li Weiming had hoped to kill two birds with one stone: inspect the mysterious clerk from Qingyan City up close, and swallow Hengxin Logistics whole before Friday.

Chen Xuan shook his head slightly, a faint, quiet sigh escaping his lips.

"Really," Chen Xuan murmured softly to the empty room. "All I wanted was to sort manifests quietly, collect my salary, and bring home walnut pastries for Grandpa Chen Fugen and Grandma Zhou Guilan. They really never know when to stop."

* * *

Chen Xuan stood up and walked unhurriedly over to the tall glass window overlooking the glowing skyline of Lanshui City.

Sixteen floors above him, on the twenty-eighth floor, the luxury suites occupied by Li Weiming and the visiting European directors burned bright against the dark sky. Down along the riverfront boulevard, headlights moved like tiny streams of sparks across the bridges.

Somewhere within fifty meters of Room 1208, perhaps in the quiet room directly across the corridor or along the service stairwell, the permanent Zero Guard team remained completely silent, motionless, and unseen. Chen Xuan did not need to look for them to know the perimeter was airtight.

He did not feel anger, nor did he feel any urge to storm into a banquet hall and confront Song Baisheng or Wu Zhenbang in person. Raising one's voice in public only drew crowds, cameras, and tiresome explanations back at the office.

Besides, Chen Xuan had already worked a full day attending panels and listening to speeches. Handling international intelligence leaks and corrupt deputy governors was not in an administrative clerk's job description.

Standing peacefully by the window, Chen Xuan unlocked his plain black phone.

He opened the bare, unlabelled encrypted messaging channel that held only one contact: Number One.

His thumbs moved slowly and evenly across the screen, typing one short, calm line with zero exclamation marks and zero wasted words.

*Wanfeng Freight's smart-terminal contract routes port data to Zurich. Check Song Baisheng, Li Weiming, and Wu Zhenbang.*

Chen Xuan glanced over the line once to make sure every name was spelled cleanly, then pressed send.

A single muted gray checkmark appeared beside the text.

Chen Xuan locked the phone, turned away from the window, and set the device face down beside his scratched green thermos on the bedside nightstand. He closed the glossy blue contract booklet neatly, tucked his red ballpoint pen back into his jacket pocket, and pulled back the crisp hotel duvet.

Tomorrow morning, the hotel breakfast buffet opened at seven o'clock, and Sun Haoran had mentioned they served fresh steamed buns. Chen Xuan switched off the bedside lamp and went peacefully to sleep.
