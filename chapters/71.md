# Chapter 71: The 8:30 AM Roll Call

Inside the third-floor meeting room of the Yunxi County Government Compound, harsh fluorescent lights hummed overhead. Cold morning air leaked through the tall aluminum window frames, and the polished terrazzo floor reflected the pale faces of forty county department heads.

Nobody spoke above a whisper. Every official sat stiffly around the long rectangular tables, eyes darting between the round wall clock and their silent, dark phone screens.

At the head table, County Magistrate Sun Weiming tapped his fountain pen rapidly against a leather notebook. Beside him, County Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong tugged at his tight uniform collar, wiping a sheen of sweat from the back of his neck.

"Did your office line come back up?" Sun Weiming asked under his breath.

Liang Guodong forced a dry chuckle that sounded hollow in the quiet room. "Still dead. My duty clerk sent a technician to the exchange at dawn. They said a main fiber line snapped near the river bridge. Relax, Magistrate Sun. You look like you saw a ghost."

"There was no storm last night, Liang Guodong," Sun Weiming hissed, leaning closer so the front row could not hear. "The sky was clear at midnight. How does a buried fiber trunk snap by itself on the exact same morning every mobile phone in this building loses signal?"

"Coincidence," Liang Guodong muttered, though his own fingers twitched against his teacup. "Even if the city is running a routine inspection, our backers in Jingzhou City and the province are solid. Public Security Director Qi Tongwei promised last month that Yunxi County is safe under his wing. Nobody dares move against Shanshui Group's flagship project."

Sun Weiming's stomach twisted. Two nights ago, he had heard a whispered rumor from an old classmate in the provincial capital that Shanshui Group head Zhao Ruilong had vanished from public view. Worse still, Sun Weiming had signed the emergency land clearance order for Old Street just to please Hongrui Real Estate, and Liang Guodong's men had locked up a stubborn hardware shopkeeper named Chen Mingde overnight to force his family to sign.

"We should have waited on Old Street," Sun Weiming muttered. "If the inspection team asks about that hardware shop, we have no clean paperwork."

"Stop panicking," Liang Guodong snapped softly. "That old shopkeeper has no backing and no money. By tonight he will sign the transfer papers, and Hongrui Plaza will expand straight to the riverfront. Now start the roll call before the clerks start gossiping."

The minute hand on the wall clock clicked onto 8:30 AM.

Sun Weiming straightened his jacket, cleared his throat, and reached for the tabletop microphone. "Quiet down, everyone. Let us begin the Monday roll call. First item on the agenda is the Old Street redevelopment plan."

His voice cut off.

Down in the courtyard, the deep thrum of heavy engines echoed off the concrete walls. A second later, rapid, synchronized footsteps thundered up the main stairwell, shaking the hallway floorboards.

Before anyone could rise, the double wooden doors at the back of the meeting room swung wide open.

A dozen uniformed officers from the provincial procuratorate and special police stepped inside, swiftly taking positions along the walls and sealing both exits. Behind them walked two men in crisp dark coats.

Hou Liangping, Chief of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau, led the way with a calm, razor-sharp gaze. Beside him stood Chief Procurator Ji Changming, holding a stack of red-stamped warrants.

"Stay in your seats," Hou Liangping said, his voice carrying clearly across the dead-silent room. "Place your hands flat on the tables where my officers can see them."

A chair scraped loudly as Liang Guodong jumped to his feet, his face flushing dark red. "Who gave you permission to barge into a county government meeting? I am the Public Security Bureau Chief of Yunxi County! Do you know who I report to in the provincial department? Wait until Director Qi Tongwei hears about this!"

Hou Liangping stopped directly in front of the head table and looked Liang Guodong straight in the eye.

"I am Hou Liangping, Chief of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau," he announced. "Beside me is Chief Procurator Ji Changming. We are here under the direct orders of Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin."

A collective gasp rippled through the forty department heads. Several faces went completely white, and two bureau chiefs nearly knocked over their water glasses.

Sun Weiming slowly sank back into his chair, his knees turning to water. "Secretary Sha?"

"As for your provincial backers," Hou Liangping continued coldly, "Deputy Provincial Secretary Gao Yuliang and Shanshui Group chairman Zhao Ruilong are already under arrest. Qi Tongwei cannot save himself, let alone you."

Liang Guodong staggered back half a step, the color draining from his cheeks. His mouth opened, yet not a single word came out.

Ji Changming stepped forward and opened the top folder. "Sun Weiming, County Magistrate of Yunxi County. Liang Guodong, County Public Security Bureau Chief. Plus Deputy Magistrate Tang, Land Bureau Director Ma, and Urban Management Chief Liu. You are hereby detained on charges of taking bribes from Hongrui Real Estate, abusing public power, and fabricating criminal evidence to unlawfully detain an innocent citizen on Old Street."

"No! Wait!" Sun Weiming grabbed the edge of the table as two special police officers stepped behind him. "I only signed the planning review! Liang Guodong handled the police arrest! I did not touch that shopkeeper!"

"Save your explanations for the interrogation room," Hou Liangping said flatly. "Take them out."

Cold steel handcuffs snapped shut with sharp metallic clicks. Liang Guodong went limp, his shiny leather shoes dragging across the floor as officers hauled him toward the doorway. Sun Weiming stumbled behind him, weeping quietly while forty silent officials stared straight down at their notebooks, terrified that their own names would be called next.

Hou Liangping turned to Ji Changming. "Leave half the team here to freeze the county archives and take statements from the remaining clerks. Come with me. We have an innocent man waiting at Old Street Police Station."

"Right behind you," Ji Changming replied.

As the doors swung shut behind them, the 8:30 AM roll call in Yunxi County ended before a single name had been read.
