# Chapter 19: Monday, Leave on Time

On Monday morning, Chen Xuan stepped out of the Hengxin Logistics elevator early, with his green thermos in his hand and a sleepy smile on his face.

He had ridden in on the Sunday-night bus, and halfway there his mother had sent him a message.

*Your Second Uncle slept fourteen hours! He ate two bowls of soup and went back to sleep. Your Second Aunt has not let go of his sleeve all day.*

Chen Xuan read it three times before he put the phone away.

A tired man was asleep in a warm room, and to Chen Xuan that was enough for any Monday.

"Supervisor Chen!"

Zhang Pengfei stood beside his desk with a tray of coffee cups in both hands, and his eyes said that he had not slept all weekend.

"I brought coffee for everyone, a fresh start for the new week," Zhang Pengfei said brightly. "No hard feelings about last Friday, so latte or americano?"

"Oh, nice," Chen Xuan said. "Latte, thank you."

Zhang Pengfei smiled even wider, and inside he felt very proud of his clever plan.

He had thought it over all Sunday night, and his logic was simple: a rival who sneers in front of everyone looks small, but a rival who hands out coffee looks big.

So he had spent his own money on a cup for the whole floor!

To be honest, he did not feel friendly at all, because for three weeks he had waited for one careless mistake, and this man had found fourteen thousand yuan before lunch.

Where was the justice in that, he asked himself?

"Pengfei paid for the whole tray himself," Sister Pan whispered across the partition. "That is half a day of a clerk's pay, just to say sorry!"

"Sister Pan, how do you know that?" a young clerk whispered back.

"Everybody tells me everything," Sister Pan said with great pride. "I never ask. It just comes."

At half past nine, General Manager Sun Haoran walked into the meeting room, and everybody stood up at once.

"Good morning, General Manager!"

Sun was the man who signed every bonus in the company, and when he walked in, even the air conditioner seemed to sit up straight.

"First, good news," Sun said. "Port Agent Hailong, the shipping agent that billed us twice for storage, paid back our fourteen thousand yuan on Saturday."

"Supervisor Chen caught that error on Friday morning, before lunch," he added.

Silence.

Everyone in the room turned to look at Chen Xuan, who was busy peeling the lid off his latte.

"I would like to make this a habit," Sun went on. "Starting this month, we will run an Employee of the Quarter ranking, with a board in the hallway, and colleagues will vote at the end of the month."

"And Supervisor Chen, your name is already on the list."

A name on that board would be read by everyone who walked past it, including the man who signed the bonuses.

A murmur ran around the table.

Zhang Pengfei's cup stopped halfway to his mouth.

Already on the list, before any vote had even been held, and this was not the script at all!

He had just handed out coffee, so today everyone should have been looking at him, and somebody had put that name there on purpose. Did Chen Xuan have someone behind him?

His fingers tightened on the cup, and he swore that this month his own name would be on that board.

Beside him, Hu Dewei tugged his tie so hard that it nearly choked him.

"Didn't know Operations had anyone worth watching," a man from Sales murmured to the colleague next to him.

"Mm," Chen Xuan said politely. "Thank you, General Manager. Is lunch still at twelve?"

The whole room burst out laughing!

Chen Xuan had meant it as a plain question, but the table believed the quiet man had just brushed off a ranking contest in one sentence.

Sister Pan was already counting lunches for her betting pool.

"He asked the General Manager about lunch," someone whispered. "In the middle of a ranking!"

"Cool as ice," another voice said.

---

At lunchtime, a quiet voice spoke beside his desk.

"Here, child."

A warm tea egg appeared on his desk before he looked up, and the hand that put it there belonged to Auntie Lu.

Her full name was Lu Qinghe, and she was the cleaning auntie of the Operations floor, in a blue cap over short gray hair, with a gray cart and a mop bucket.

She called everyone under fifty "child" and everyone over fifty "old friend," and nobody had ever corrected her.

"Thank you, Auntie Lu."

"Tomato and egg over rice, no cilantro," she said, wiping the corner of his desk. "Eat before it gets cold."

Chen Xuan stopped with the egg in his hand, because he ordered lunch from his phone every noon, and today he had not even opened the app.

"Hmm? How did you know?"

"You have ordered the same thing since spring," Auntie Lu said dryly. "A woman who cleans the floor sees what goes in the trash."

That made sense, and he nodded, and she rolled her cart away, and only then did he notice, as he had many times before, a small old radio tied to her cart handle with a red cord.

In all these months, he had never once heard it make a sound.

"Does that radio work, Auntie Lu?"

"It's for company," she said over her shoulder. "A quiet kind."

---

After lunch, Zhang Pengfei had a new plan, and the plan was this: a man who wants to look useful should fix something nobody asked him to fix.

So he opened the department's shared folder, the one holding every shipping sheet, every bill and every link the floor relied on, and decided it was untidy!

Soon, he was busy renaming folders, moving them under new headings and dragging old files into a new order.

After forty minutes, he leaned back and admired his work, with every file sitting in a tidy group under a clear heading.

Mid-afternoon, Sister Pan screamed.

"My whole month is gone! The links are all red!"

"Mine too!" "Where did the Hailong sheet go?" "Who touched the folder?!"

Zhang Pengfei's face went the color of paper, because every spreadsheet in the department still pointed to the old places, and now every one of them pointed at nothing.

"It's, uh, only moved," he said weakly. "It will sort itself out."

At the end of the row, Chen Xuan set down his tea, opened the folder's history, chose yesterday's version and clicked restore.

Click.

It took two minutes, and when it was done, he simply picked up his tea again.

"Oh! It's back!" Sister Pan cried. "All green!"

Zhang Pengfei stared at the screen with his mouth open, and a run of questions went through his head.

Had the system fixed itself, or had the tech team done it?

He looked around the room, but nobody met his eyes, least of all the quiet man peeling a second tea egg.

Passing the row, Hu Dewei stopped, and when Chen Xuan looked up, his back straightened and his heels came together like a man being checked by an officer.

Then he scurried away, red in the face.

"Why do I always do that?" he muttered.

---

Near the end of the workday, Hu Dewei crept up to Chen Xuan's desk, tugging his tie.

"Supervisor Chen, General Manager Sun wants to see you on Wednesday at nine sharp, and it's about something from Haiyue Hospitality, a hotel and property group that wants a logistics partner."

"Oh, nice. Is it a long meeting?"

"I don't know," Hu Dewei said, swallowing. "He did not say what it was about, but he wasn't smiling when he said it."

"Mm," Chen Xuan said. "Then I'll bring my thermos."

He walked out at five to five on the dot.

Behind him, Hu Dewei stood frozen with his hand on his tie, sure that something big had just changed.

So why had nobody told him?
