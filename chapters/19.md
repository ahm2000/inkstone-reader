# Chapter 19: The River Barge at Pier Four

By nine o'clock on Saturday evening, dense white mist rolled down the Yunxi River valley, swallowing the far bank and turning the dark water into a slow, oily mirror. Three hundred meters east of the shuttered cement plant, Pier Four jutted into the current like a broken concrete tooth. Its chain-link gate hung padlocked beneath a faded county notice declaring the wharf decommissioned and unsafe.

Half a kilometer upriver and forty meters above the waterline, perched inside the rusted observation gallery of an abandoned grain silo, two motionless figures watched the fog through stabilized matte-black optics. Rain beads slid silently down their charcoal ponchos, leaving no glint against the wet concrete.

Number Nine adjusted the focal ring of the long-range optical-thermal scope, sweeping across the shadowed concrete overhang beneath Pier Four's crumbling crane tower. Beside Number Nine, Number Nineteen sat hunched over a ruggedized field terminal shielded by a light-baffled hood, monitoring passive river-traffic feeds and an encrypted regional freight database.

"Movement under the south sluice," Number Nine said calmly, voice barely carrying three feet. "Running lights cut to minimum red. AIS transponder dark. Forty-eight-meter flat-bottomed steel freight barge."

Number Nineteen synced the optical crosshairs to the terminal's telemetry suite. "No vessel is registered to berth within four kilometers of Yunxi County tonight. Wait. Look at the Lanshui river-patrol log. A sand-dredging permit was stamped at six o'clock for an unnamed work barge hauling construction gravel downriver to the Lanshui bridge project."

Through the drifting mist, the heavy black hull glided out from beneath the concrete shadow of Pier Four, where the old floodgate sluice met the dark current. Its diesel engine ran through a custom underwater exhaust baffle, reducing the usual hammering throb of a river barge to a low, muted thrum carried beneath the ripples. Four men in dark rain slickers moved silently along the wet gunwale, casting off thick hemp mooring lines without shouting a single command or flashing a single deck torch.

From the upper rim of the abandoned grain silo, Number Nine tracked the dark vessel as it cleared the slack water and eased steadily into the main shipping channel. Even through forty meters of damp river fog, the stabilized night optic resolved every weld seam along the steel hull and the faint white numerals painted near the bow's draft scale.

Number Nine zoomed tightly onto the waterline marks as the barge settled into the current.

"That is not river sand," Number Nine stated immediately.

"Confirm draft," Number Nineteen prompted, fingers flying across the keypad.

"Freeboard is down to fifty-five centimeters," Number Nine read off the laser rangefinder. "The cargo hold is only two-thirds full, heaped with wet gray gravel. Yet the hull is displacing nearly nine hundred metric tons. Wet river sand at that volume cannot exceed four hundred and twenty tons."

"Double the expected mass," Number Nineteen murmured. "Bringing up the thermal-differential array."

A narrow, silent lens beside Number Nine's scope swept the open cargo bay. Because river gravel cooled evenly in the autumn fog while dense metallic containers held warehouse temperature far longer, the thermal display bloomed into stark contrast.

Beneath a thin, carefully raked layer of damp gravel barely forty centimeters deep sat forty-two rectangular blocks arranged in six symmetrical rows. Their edges were razor-straight, their thermal cores uniformly insulated, and each crate showed the dense absorption profile of heavy reinforced steel and lead shielding.

Number Nineteen ran an automated dimensional match against the customs and industrial logistics catalogs in Black Pine's regional archive. The progress bar crossed the screen in four seconds and flashed crimson.

"Crate footprint matches to the millimeter," Number Nineteen said, voice dropping into cold focus. "Standardized heavy-density transport casks, Type Seven-B. Used for high-purity separated rare-earth oxides and precision dual-use alloy housings."

Number Nine kept the optical reticle locked on the barge's stern as it slid toward the eastern bend. "Trace the upstream origin. Yunxi County has no rare-earth refinery and no alloy foundry."

"Pulling highway weigh-station logs and toll-gate captures from the past seventy-two hours," Number Nineteen replied swiftly. "Three covered heavy-axle trucks bearing provincial agricultural permits exited the mountain expressway at Yunxi West between midnight and three o'clock Friday morning. Registered weight at the mountain checkpoint: thirty-eight tons each. By dawn, when they exited north, they were empty."

Number Nineteen linked the plate histories three hundred kilometers inland across provincial dispatch ledgers and railway siding manifests no county official could touch. One line locked into place with brutal clarity.

"Source confirmed," Number Nineteen reported. "The cargo originated from a subcontracted processing depot attached to the Seventh Inland Military-Industrial Materials Plant. Official records classify two batches of refined dysprosium-terbium concentrate and forty-eight machined precision alloy casings as scrap-grade metallurgical waste slated for local reclamation."

For a long moment, only mist condensing on the rusted silo roof broke the silence between them.

"Military-grade rare-earth concentrates and dual-use precision alloy casings," Number Nine repeated softly. "Siphoned off as scrap inland, trucked into a fourth-tier county under agricultural permits, staged through a forgotten 1970s civil-defense tunnel beneath Old Street, and floated out of a condemned pier beneath river gravel."

"It gets worse," Number Nineteen said, pointing to the maritime clearance manifest populating the right screen. "Matching the barge's engine-acoustic signature against river-lock recordings, this exact hull has made eleven nighttime runs down the Yunxi tributary in fourteen months. Each time, it offloads at an unregistered slip outside Lanshui into sealed refrigerated seafood containers bound for Pier Twelve at Qingyan Port."

"Bonded international transshipment berth," Number Nine said quietly. "Once a container clears Pier Twelve under a pre-approved customs seal, it loads onto an outbound freighter with zero physical inspection."

"Which means," Number Nineteen finished evenly, "whoever backs Hongrui Real Estate is not building riverside apartments. They are building a permanent subterranean smuggling vault to move strategic defense materials overseas."

Down on the dark river, the barge rounded the wooded headland and vanished into the white wall of fog. Number Nine confirmed the passive acoustic-wake tag had handed tracking off to the downriver relay outside Lanshui.

"Intercept now?" Number Nineteen asked, hands poised over the transmitter.

"No," Number Nine answered without hesitation. "Grabbing four boatmen on a foggy river only warns Qingyan Port. Observation and evidence until Number One calls the net."

Number Nineteen compressed the thermal scans, weight calculations, weigh-station logs, and Qingyan Port manifests into a triple-encrypted packet stamped with a level-one red escalation flag.

"Transmitted to Lanshui safehouse," Number Nineteen said. "Number One will have it in three seconds."

Far across the misty county, in the warm yellow light of a sixth-floor kitchen at Fuxing New Village, Chen Xuan was still washing dinner bowls beside his mother, unaware that the quiet stone he dropped to clear his uncle's name had cracked open a fault line reaching all the way to the sea.
