# Chapter 19: The Hotel Belongs to the Company

On Friday evening, thirty-eight stories of glass stood over the river. Twenty clerks from Hengxin Logistics stood under them, taking selfies and trying not to look poor.

A black Audi slid up to the curb, and the window came down on Zhang Pengfei in a new red tie and needless sunglasses.

"Manager Hu! Sister Pan! Wait, I'll park!" He waved a silver card out of the window. "Young Master Fang lent me his Silver VIP card, and his car! Ten percent off and a free fruit plate!"

The clerks gasped, and Zhang felt three pounds lighter.

Across the street, a city bus sighed to a stop, and Chen Xuan stepped off with his canvas bag over one shoulder.

"You came by bus?" Zhang laughed so loudly that a doorman turned. "You could have squeezed into my back seat!"

"Evening, Manager. Evening, Sister Pan," said Chen Xuan. "The stop is right there. No traffic."

Manager Hu handed the reservation slip to the front-desk clerk, a young woman with a neat bun, and watched her face change as she read her screen.

"I'm so sorry, Manager Hu. Your Peony Room on the third floor was released ten minutes ago. A Diamond guest needed it for clients. We can offer you two side tables in the second-floor buffet hall."

"Released?" Hu's face turned the color of a ripe tomato in front of his whole staff.

"Leave it to me!" Zhang surged forward and slapped the silver card on the marble. "Miss, whose card is this? Young Master Fang Zihao's! Give us our room back!"

The clerk looked at the card. Her smile stayed polite.

"Sir, Silver is our entry-level membership. The guest in the Peony Room holds Diamond, and he has spent three million yuan with us this quarter."

Lin Dong did the sum under his breath: two hundred fifty months of Chen Xuan's pay, more than twenty years.

"A Silver card can't move a Diamond guest," the clerk finished.

Entry-level, again. Zhang's face went from white to purple under the whole department's stare.

"It's fine, Manager Hu," said Chen Xuan, stepping up. "The buffet hall will do. Or I can make one call about a quiet room."

"Call who?" Zhang snapped, because he had to snap at somebody. "The bus driver?"

Chen Xuan reached into his canvas bag for his phone. Something slid out of the side pocket and rolled across the marble.

A small float of dark yellow boxwood, with a tiny square seal cut into its base.

Then a plain matte-black card, no words on it, with a number engraved in one corner: 01.

It landed on the counter with a soft click.

The young clerk looked at it, and her whole body went still.

Her training binder held one page she had never expected to use. *If a black card engraved 01 is ever placed on this desk, press the red button under the counter before you speak to anyone.*

Her finger shook, and she pressed it.

* * *

Then General Manager Dai Wenbin ran.

He had not run in twenty years. Guests stopped to stare at a fat man in a black suit sprinting across the marble.

He reached the counter with his gold badge swinging and looked at the card. Then he looked at the young man in the gray jacket, who looked mildly sleepy.

Three years ago, in the week the hotel opened, a quiet gray-haired trustee of the holding company had sat in Dai's office and said only this. *One day a young man will put this card on your counter. Serve him, ask nothing, tell nobody.*

Dai had waited three years, picturing a prince or a general. He never pictured a bus ticket sticking out of a canvas bag.

He bowed to the waist, a full ninety degrees, with his hands flat at his sides.

"Sir. Welcome. I'm Dai Wenbin, General Manager. Please forgive us for making you stand at a counter." He lowered his voice. "Your pass is honored here, sir. Please come up."

Twenty clerks stopped breathing.

"General Manager Dai?" Hu squeaked. "You know Supervisor Chen?"

Dai did not know his name, and he did not want to.

His orders said ask nothing, and he had never obeyed anything so hard in his life.

"Tonight the Peony Room is nothing," he told the whole lobby. "I'm opening the thirty-eighth-floor Cloud Sea Hall for your department, free of charge."

"The Cloud Sea Hall?" a clerk whispered. "My cousin says a table up there costs over a hundred thousand. That's eight months of Chen's pay! And you can't buy it at any price!"

"Evening, Manager Dai. Thanks," said Chen Xuan, dropping the card and the float in his bag. "Home-style dishes are enough. Don't waste food."

Dai held the golden elevator doors with both hands, and Sister Pan stepped in behind Chen Xuan and breathed, "Oh my."

"Dai!"

A bell rang from the other elevator bank. A young man in an open silk shirt stepped out with two friends trailing behind him, sunglasses pushed up on his hair, his phone held like a weapon.

Fang Zihao, Silver member, eighty thousand yuan a year, who called the Yunhai Grand Hotel "my place" at every table.

"A message just told me my card was refused at your front desk," he said, loud enough for the whole lobby. "In front of Zhang's whole company! And you gave the Cloud Sea Hall, which you told me last month can't be sold for any money, to a man off a bus?"

"Who's sitting in my seat, Dai? Do you know what one call from my father does to a hotel license?" He swung his phone toward the counter. "And you at the counter. Your name and your village, by midnight."

The clerk's hand went flat on the marble to keep from shaking.

From inside the elevator, Chen Xuan looked out at him, flat and unhurried.

"It's a nice seat," he said. "You shouldn't have asked for it that way."

Fang's mouth opened.

"Threaten her again, and your father will learn what one call can do to him."

He did not raise his voice. The lobby went so still that the fountain outside sounded loud through the glass.

Zhang slipped into the elevator at the last second, as far from Fang as he could get. His voice shook.

"Supervisor Chen. How do you have a Zero-One pass at the Yunhai Grand Hotel?"

Chen Xuan blinked and answered flatly.

"It belongs to the holding company. Someone gave it to me for dinner."

Then Zhang let out a long, shaky breath, and the color came back to his face.

"A company card!" He laughed, almost sobbing with relief. "You borrowed a client's card! You nearly stopped my heart, Supervisor Chen!"

"Borrowed is a good word," said Chen Xuan gravely. "Keep it between us, Pengfei. Don't tell Young Master Fang."

Zhang nodded hard, proud to be trusted with a secret.

"Maybe he saved the owner's life," Sister Pan said.

"Maybe he's the owner's secret godson!"

Chen Xuan put two fingers to his forehead. He had been given a card for dinner, and he had never thought anyone would ask why.

* * *

In the lobby, the golden doors closed on the last of the clerks, and Dai stood straight for the first time in seven minutes. Then he mopped his neck with a handkerchief and found his hand shaking.

"General Manager," the front-desk clerk whispered, white to the lips. "He told them the card wasn't his. He said he borrowed it. Was he only borrowing a company card?"

"Borrowing?" Dai whispered back, watching the floor numbers climb. "That pass has never been lent to anyone in three years. So who is the someone?"

Behind them, Fang Zihao had not moved, and his ears had gone white.

"Dai," he said at last, far more quietly than before. "Who is that man?"

Dai looked at the Silver card in Fang's hand. He had no answer for a Silver member about a man whose name he was forbidden to learn.

* * *

At eleven that night, Fang Zihao's father hung up on twenty minutes of his son's shouting. Then he called an old friend in the Organization Department.

"Check a man for me. Chen Xuan, a clerk at Hengxin Logistics. He took my son's seat tonight."

Keys clicked. "Simple, honest, nothing," said the friend. "A boring file, Jiancheng."

"Too boring. Open the rest."

A long silence. When the friend spoke again, his voice was thin.

"The screen just went red. PERMISSION DENIED. And under it: *This attempt has been reported.*"

"Reported to whom?"

"Do not ask," the friend whispered. "Do not ever ask."

The line went dead. Fang Jiancheng stood in his dark study with the phone in his hand, and cold sweat ran down his back.

Then the phone rang again, from a number he had never seen.
