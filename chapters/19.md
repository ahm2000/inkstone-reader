# Chapter 19: The True Hour

At 7:50 the next morning, Cheng Biao stood over Wei Kun's desk with a steamed bun in one hand and a blank detention notice in the other.

He had not slept. His eyes were red, and every minute or so he glanced at his phone. The county freeze was still on. Not one patrol car had left the yard since the night before.

"Friday, two-thirty p.m., right after the arrest. Write it down."

Cheng Biao needed that hour on the paper. It would show that the order to hold Chen Mingde was signed the moment he was brought in, and the long night in the cell would look legal. The truth was uglier. Nobody had signed anything until this morning. An old man had sat on a concrete bench for eighteen hours with no order at all.

Lei Bo hovered at his elbow with the red stamp ready. He looked as if he had slept in his chair. When a door banged down the hall, he jumped.

Wei Kun picked up the pen. His fingers were numb. Everything came at once: the paper cup of hot water, Su Min's voice on the phone, two hundred thousand yuan, the sting of the slap.

*Don't play the hero,* she had said.

He was not a hero. He was only a man whose hand would not write a lie for an old man's family to read.

He did not write Friday. He wrote the true time.

*Time of Decision: Saturday, 7:50 a.m.*

The ink was still wet when Cheng Biao's phone rang.

He read the name on the screen, and his shoulders sank. He turned to the window and spoke in a low voice. "Dong Haisheng. No. No word. Nobody has heard a thing. Director Mai's freeze is still on, and I cannot send one car out of the yard." He listened. "The nephew comes in today. I will have the old man's signature this morning. Yes, I know the date. And do not call me on this phone again."

"Director," Wei Kun began, ready to tell him what he had written.

"Fine, fine." Cheng Biao waved him off without turning around.

Lei Bo snatched the sheet and brought the red stamp down with a thump. He did not read a word. His eyes stayed on Cheng Biao's back.

At the next desk, Zhong Hai looked up from his lunch box. He and Wei Kun had shared a pot of tea every shift for ten years. He read the timestamp upside down, and then he stared at his shoes. His phone lay face down beside his rice. It had buzzed twice since Lei Bo walked in, and he had not dared to touch it.

Lei Bo carried the stamped page down to the records room, where every notice was filed.

Number Thirty-four sat behind his desk with a stapler in his hand and the kind of face nobody ever remembers.

He looked at the notice, and the stapler stopped in the air. A true hour in black ink proved that Chen Mingde had been locked up for eighteen hours with no order. For one quiet breath, Number Thirty-four smiled.

The Black Pine Guard could not act while the Zero Guard watched. But a clerk could still log a paper. He filed it, then typed one line into a phone no bigger than a matchbox. *A man wrote the true hour.*

Upstairs, Wei Kun's palms were slick with sweat. He had done the smallest thing in the world, and he was sure it would cost him everything.

In a building like this one, who would ever read one small line?

* * *

At 8:40, in the lobby of Old Street Police Station, the ticket machine spat out a slip of paper. The number was thirty-seven.

Chen Jianguo read it twice. Then he looked up at the red screen above the Case Inquiries window. It showed number twelve.

"Thirty-seven?" he snapped. "By the time they call us, your uncle will have a long white beard."

He strode to the window. Wei Kun sat behind the glass.

"We want the detention notice for my brother, Chen Mingde. In writing."

"Take a number and wait your turn." Wei Kun did not look up. The notice he had written less than an hour ago lay in a tray behind him. He could not hand it over without Cheng Biao's word, and Cheng Biao had not given it.

Chen Jianguo marched back, fuming. "Chen Xuan, go sit down. I'll stand here and glare at them."

"Okay, Dad." Chen Xuan kept his voice gentle. "Does your cheek still hurt?"

"It's nothing."

It was not nothing. The bruise had turned yellow and purple overnight. Chen Xuan's eyes stayed on it a moment too long before he looked away.

He sat on the cold steel bench with his green thermos on his knee and unwrapped the red-bean bun his mother had packed.

Behind the glass, a door opened a hand's width. Lei Bo looked out at the lobby. He looked at the young man with the bun, the clerk who had slapped Yan Tiehu on Friday night. Then he looked at the glass front doors, the way a man looks who has heard that his friend vanished from a road.

A clerk, he told himself. Clerks did not make vans disappear.

He shut the door.

Beside Chen Xuan, a delivery rider in a headset turned and looked him over.

"Brother, your dad looks ready to tear the place down. You look like you're waiting for a bus."

"My uncle spent the night in a cell," Chen Xuan said. "I'm worried too."

"Don't you feel like shouting? Everybody else does."

"Does it help?"

"No."

"Then I'd rather eat my bun and wait."

On the wall hung a sign that read Legal Aid Desk. A paper arrow underneath pointed to an empty table with a dusty chair.

Chen Jianguo's phone buzzed. It was his wife, Liu Meifang. Her voice could fill a room by itself, so he put her on speaker.

"Chen Jianguo! Have you seen him? Did they let you in?"

"We're in the lobby, Liu Meifang. Chen Xuan is sitting on the bench."

"Sitting?" Her voice rose so sharply that two people nearby flinched. "Your brother slept on concrete last night, and he's sitting? Put Chen Xuan on!"

"Mom," Chen Xuan said. "They haven't let us in yet. How is Second Aunt? Did she eat anything?"

For a breath, Liu Meifang went quiet.

"She drank some water." Her voice softened. "What about you? Are you eating?"

"Yes, I'm eating right now. Don't worry, Mom. We'll get the paperwork and sort this out."

"I told your father to raise a fuss. Why do I have one son who never raises his voice and another who sleeps through everything? Finish the bun I packed for you!"

She hung up.

Chen Xuan smiled faintly at the silent phone. "Yes, Mom."

An old woman with a sack of onions on her knees leaned toward the delivery rider.

"Why is the son so calm? Lazy, if you ask me." She did not bother to lower her voice. She pointed at Chen Jianguo. "Look at the father. He's the only one here with some fire. A father ought to look angry."

Chen Jianguo puffed out his chest for half a second before he realized she was praising him by putting down his son.

He muttered, "Well, he does take things too slow."

"I'm not lazy, Auntie. Just hungry." Chen Xuan held out half his bun. She waved it away with a proud sniff, so he smiled and took another bite.
