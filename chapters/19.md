# Chapter 19: The River Barge at Pier Four

By nine o'clock on Saturday evening, mist hung low over the Yunxi River, hiding the far bank. Three hundred meters east of the shuttered cement plant, Pier Four jutted into the current behind a padlocked gate marked decommissioned and unsafe.

Half a kilometer upriver, inside the observation gallery of an abandoned grain silo, two motionless figures in dark ponchos watched the wharf through matte-black optics. Number Nine adjusted the focal ring of the long-range optical-thermal scope while Number Nineteen worked a ruggedized field terminal beneath a light-baffled hood, monitoring river-traffic feeds and regional freight logs.

"Movement under the south sluice," Number Nine said quietly, voice barely carrying three feet. "Running lights cut to minimum red. AIS transponder dark. Forty-eight-meter flat-bottomed steel freight barge."

Number Nineteen synced the optical crosshairs to the terminal telemetry. "No vessel is registered to berth within four kilometers of Yunxi County tonight. Wait. Checking the Lanshui river-patrol log. A sand-dredging permit was stamped at six o'clock for an unnamed work barge hauling construction gravel downriver to the Lanshui bridge project."

"Listen to the hull," Number Nine murmured, shifting the acoustic pickup slightly. "Underwater exhaust baffle. No normal gravel crew pays to muffle a diesel engine below the waterline."

"And no normal gravel crew loads at a condemned pier in the dark," Number Nineteen muttered, zooming the telemetry feed. "How many on deck?"

"Four men in dark rain slickers," Number Nine answered, watching them cast off thick mooring lines without a single shouted order or deck torch. "Disciplined movement. Not local river hands."

Through the mist, the heavy black hull glided out from beneath Pier Four's concrete overhang where the old floodgate sluice met the current. Number Nine tracked the vessel as it eased into the main shipping channel, zooming tightly onto the white draft numerals painted near the bow.

Number Nine's hand froze on the focus ring. "That is not river sand."

"Confirm draft," Number Nineteen prompted immediately, fingers poised over the keypad.

"Freeboard is down to fifty-five centimeters," Number Nine read off the laser rangefinder. "The cargo hold is only two-thirds full, heaped with wet gray gravel. Yet the hull is displacing nearly nine hundred metric tons. Wet river sand at that volume cannot exceed four hundred and twenty tons."

Number Nineteen's posture snapped rigid. "Double the expected mass. Bringing up the thermal-differential array now."

A narrow lens beside Number Nine's scope swept the open cargo bay. While river gravel cooled evenly in the evening fog, dense metallic containers held warehouse warmth far longer. Across the terminal hood, the thermal image bloomed into stark contrast.

Beneath a thin, raked layer of damp gravel barely forty centimeters deep sat forty-two rectangular blocks arranged in six symmetrical rows. Their edges were razor-straight, their cores uniformly insulated, and each crate showed the dense absorption profile of reinforced steel and lead shielding.

"Run dimensional match," Number Nine ordered evenly.

Number Nineteen fed the measurements against the customs and industrial logistics catalogs in Black Pine's regional archive. Four seconds later, the screen flashed crimson.

Number Nineteen's voice dropped into cold focus. "Crate footprint matches to the millimeter. Standardized heavy-density transport casks, Type Seven-B. Used for high-purity separated rare-earth oxides and precision dual-use alloy housings."

Number Nine kept the reticle locked on the barge's stern as it moved toward the eastern bend. "Yunxi County has no rare-earth refinery and no alloy foundry. Where did those casks come from?"

"Pulling highway weigh-station logs and toll-gate captures from the last seventy-two hours," Number Nineteen replied, typing rapidly. "Here. Three covered heavy-axle trucks bearing provincial agricultural permits exited the mountain expressway at Yunxi West between midnight and three o'clock Friday morning. Registered weight at the mountain checkpoint: thirty-eight tons each. By dawn, when they exited north, they were empty."

"Trace the plates upstream," Number Nine instructed. "Ignore the county registry."

Number Nineteen cross-linked the plate histories three hundred kilometers inland across provincial dispatch ledgers and railway siding manifests no county official could touch. One line locked into place. Number Nineteen stopped typing and stared at the screen for two full seconds.

"Speak," Number Nine said quietly.

"Source confirmed," Number Nineteen reported, every trace of routine calm replaced by sharp tension. "The cargo originated from a subcontracted processing depot attached to the Seventh Inland Military-Industrial Materials Plant. Official records classify two batches of refined dysprosium-terbium concentrate and forty-eight machined precision alloy casings as scrap-grade metallurgical waste slated for local reclamation."

For a long beat, neither operative spoke.

"Military-grade rare-earth concentrates and dual-use precision alloy casings," Number Nine repeated softly. "Written off as scrap inland, trucked into a fourth-tier county under agricultural permits, staged through a forgotten 1970s civil-defense tunnel beneath Old Street, and floated out of a condemned pier under forty centimeters of river gravel."

"It gets worse," Number Nineteen said, tapping the right display where maritime clearance manifests were populating. "I matched the barge's engine-acoustic signature against river-lock recordings. This exact hull has made eleven nighttime runs down the Yunxi tributary in fourteen months. Every single time, it offloads at an unregistered slip outside Lanshui into sealed refrigerated seafood containers bound for Pier Twelve at Qingyan Port."

Number Nine's eyes narrowed behind the optic. "Pier Twelve is a bonded international transshipment berth. Once a container clears that gate under a pre-approved customs seal, it loads straight onto an outbound freighter with zero physical inspection."

"Which means," Number Nineteen finished evenly, "whoever backs Hongrui Real Estate is not building riverside apartments. They are building a permanent subterranean smuggling vault to move strategic defense materials overseas."

Down on the river, the barge rounded the wooded bend and slipped out of sight. Number Nine confirmed the passive acoustic-wake tag had handed tracking off to the downriver relay outside Lanshui.

Number Nineteen hovered a gloved hand over the transmitter. "Do we intercept before the Lanshui lock? Four men on an unarmed barge. Containment could board them inside twenty minutes."

"No," Number Nine answered without a fraction of hesitation. "Grabbing four boatmen on a foggy river only warns Qingyan Port and gives whoever runs Pier Twelve time to burn the manifests and cut the inland trail. Observation and evidence until Number One calls the net."

"Understood," Number Nineteen said, already compressing the thermal scans, draft calculations, weigh-station logs, and Qingyan Port manifests into a triple-encrypted packet stamped with a level-one red escalation flag. "Packet away to the Lanshui safehouse. Number One will have it in three seconds."

"Stay on the river feed until relief checks in," Number Nine said, eyes returning to the dark water below. "Tonight just stopped being a county case."

Far across the misty county, in the warm light of a sixth-floor kitchen at Fuxing New Village, Chen Xuan was still washing dinner bowls beside his mother, unaware that the quiet stone he dropped to clear his uncle's name had cracked open a fault line reaching all the way to the sea.
