# Chapter 19: Monday, Leave on Time

Qingyan City, Hengxin Logistics Group, Operations Department. Monday, 8:55 a.m.

Chen Xuan stepped out of the elevator five minutes early with his green thermos in his hand and a sleepy smile on his face.

He had come in on the Sunday-night bus. On the way, his phone had buzzed with a message from his mother: *Your Second Uncle slept fourteen hours. He ate two bowls of soup and fell asleep again with the blanket on his knees. Your Second Aunt hasn't let go of his sleeve all day.*

Chen Xuan had read it three times before he put the phone away. A tired man was sleeping safely in a warm room, and that was enough for any Monday.

"Supervisor Chen!"

Zhang Pengfei was waiting beside his desk, holding a cardboard tray of coffee cups with both hands. His suit was crisp, his smile was wide, and his eyes looked like they hadn't closed all weekend.

"I brought coffee for everyone, a fresh start for the new week," Zhang Pengfei said brightly. "No hard feelings about last Friday. Latte or americano?"

Chen Xuan blinked. "Oh, nice. Latte, thanks."

Last week Zhang Pengfei had checked his spreadsheets four times. This week he was checking his manners as well. He had thought it over all Sunday evening: a rival who sneers in front of everyone looks small, and a rival who hands out coffee looks big. So he had bought a cup for the whole floor!

"One of those cups costs part of a clerk's day," Sister Pan whispered across the partition, counting on her fingers. "Pengfei is spending a whole afternoon of our pay to say sorry. Did you hear Manager Hu's wife bought him a smaller mug, and he asked for the big one back? He said the small one made him feel like a visitor!"

"Sister Pan, how do you know that?" a young clerk whispered.

"Everybody tells me everything," Sister Pan said with great pride. "I never ask. It just comes."

At 9:30, General Manager Sun Haoran stepped into the meeting room. He was fifty-two, the man who signed every bonus in the company, and when he walked in, even the air conditioner seemed to sit up straight.

"First, good news," Sun said. "Port Agent Hailong, the shipping agent who had billed us twice for storage, paid back the 14,000 yuan on Saturday. The double storage charge, caught on Friday morning by Supervisor Chen, before lunch."

Everyone turned to look at Chen Xuan. He was peeling the lid off his latte.

"I'd like to make this a habit," Sun went on. "Starting this month, we'll run an Employee of the Quarter ranking, with a board in the hallway. Colleagues vote at the end of the month. And Supervisor Chen, your name is already on the list."

A murmur ran around the table. Zhang Pengfei's cup stopped halfway to his mouth, and Hu Dewei tugged his tie so hard that it nearly choked him.

Someone from Sales, visiting for the meeting, leaned toward a colleague and murmured, "Didn't know Operations had anyone worth watching," as if the whole floor were furniture until today.

"Mm," Chen Xuan said politely. "Thank you, General Manager. Is lunch still at twelve?"

The room burst out laughing!

Chen Xuan really didn't mean it as a joke. He meant it as a question. But the whole table thought the quiet man had just shrugged off a ranking contest in one sentence, and Sister Pan was already counting lunches for her betting pool.

Operations floor. 12:05 p.m.

"Here, child."

A warm tea egg appeared on his desk before he'd looked up. The hand that put it there belonged to Auntie Lu.

Her full name was Lu Qinghe, and she was sixty-one, the cleaning auntie of the Operations floor. She wore a blue cap over short gray hair and pushed a gray cart with a mop bucket. She called every person under fifty "child," and every person over fifty "old friend," and nobody had ever corrected her.

"Thank you, Auntie Lu."

"Tomato and egg over rice, no cilantro," she said, wiping the corner of his desk. "Eat before it gets cold."

Chen Xuan stopped with the egg in his hand. He ordered lunch from his phone every noon, and today he hadn't even opened the app.

"Hmm? How did you know?"

"You've ordered the same thing since spring," Auntie Lu said dryly. "A woman who cleans the floor sees what goes in the trash."

That made sense! He nodded, and she rolled her cart away. Only then did he notice, as he had many times before, the small old radio tied to her cart handle with a red cord. In all these months he had never once heard it play a sound.

"Does that radio work, Auntie Lu?"

"It's for company," she said over her shoulder. "A quiet kind."

Operations floor. 2:30 p.m.

Zhang Pengfei had a plan. A man who wants to look useful should fix something nobody asked him to fix.

So he opened the department's shared folder, the one with every shipping sheet, every bill, and every link the whole floor relied on, and he decided it was untidy. For forty minutes he renamed folders, moved them into five new headings, and dragged old files into a new order. At the end he leaned back and admired his work: every file sat in a tidy group under a clear heading!

At 3:10, Sister Pan screamed.

"My whole month is gone! The links! They're all red!"

"Mine too!" "Where did the Hailong sheet go?" "Who touched the folder?!"

Zhang Pengfei's face went the color of paper. He had moved the files, and every spreadsheet in the department still pointed to the old places. Which meant that all of them were now pointing at nothing.

"It's, uh, it's only moved," he said weakly. "It'll sort itself out."

At the end of the row, Chen Xuan set down his tea. He opened the folder's history, chose yesterday's version, and clicked restore. It took two minutes. Then he picked his tea up again.

"Oh! It's back!" Sister Pan cried. "All green!"

Zhang Pengfei stared at the screen with his mouth open. Had the system fixed itself? Had the tech team done it? He looked around the room, but nobody met his eyes, least of all the quiet man peeling a second tea egg.

Passing the row, Hu Dewei stopped. He didn't know why, but when Chen Xuan looked up, Hu Dewei's back straightened and his heels came together, like a man being checked by an officer. Then he scurried away, red in the face.

"Why do I always do that?" he muttered.

Operations floor. 4:50 p.m.

Chen Xuan was packing his thermos when Hu Dewei crept up to his desk, tugging his tie.

"Supervisor Chen. General Manager Sun wants to see you on Wednesday, nine o'clock sharp. It's about something from Haiyue Hospitality."

"Oh, nice. Is it a long meeting?"

"I don't know." Hu Dewei swallowed. "Sun didn't say what it's about. But he wasn't smiling when he said it."

"Mm," Chen Xuan said. "Then I'll bring my thermos."

He walked out at 4:55 sharp. Behind him, Hu Dewei stood frozen with his hand on his tie, wondering why he had a feeling that something big had just changed and nobody had told him.
