# Chapter 19: The Hotel Belongs to the Company

On Friday evening, thirty-eight stories of glass stood over the river. Twenty clerks from Hengxin Logistics stood under them, taking selfies and trying not to look poor.

A black Audi pulled up to the curb. The window came down on Zhang Pengfei, in a new red tie and sunglasses at night.

"Manager Hu! Sister Pan! Wait, I'll park!" He waved a silver card out of the window. "Young Master Qi lent me his Silver VIP card and his car! Ten percent off and a free fruit plate!"

The clerks gasped. Zhang Pengfei felt much lighter.

Across the street, a city bus sighed to a stop. Chen Xuan stepped off with his canvas bag over one shoulder.

"You came by bus?" Zhang Pengfei laughed so loudly that a doorman turned. "You could have squeezed into my back seat!"

Chen Xuan nodded to the group. "Evening, Manager. Evening, Sister Pan. The stop is right there. No traffic, and I got a seat."

Hu Dewei, the department manager, gave the reservation slip to the front-desk clerk, a young woman with a neat bun. Her face changed.

"I'm so sorry, Manager Hu. Your Peony Room on the third floor was released ten minutes ago. A Diamond guest needed it for clients. We can offer you two side tables in the second-floor buffet hall."

"Leave it to me!" Zhang Pengfei slapped the silver card on the marble. "Miss, whose card is this? Young Master Qi Zihao's! Give us our room back!"

The clerk looked at the card. Her smile stayed polite.

"Sir, Silver is our lowest membership. The guest in the Peony Room holds Diamond. He has spent three million yuan with us this year. A Silver card can't move a Diamond guest."

Lowest, again. Zhang Pengfei's face went purple under the stare of the whole department.

"It's fine, Manager Hu." Chen Xuan stepped up. "The buffet hall will do. Or I can try the card a friend gave me for dinner."

"What card?" Zhang Pengfei snapped, because he had to snap at somebody. "A bus card?"

Chen Xuan dug in his canvas bag. Something slid out of the side pocket and rolled across the marble.

It was a small float of dark yellow wood, with a tiny square seal cut into its base.

Then came the card. It was plain and matte black, with no words on it. A number was engraved in one corner: 01. It was the hotel's top pass.

It landed on the counter with a soft click.

The young clerk looked at it, and her whole body went still.

Her training binder had one page she had never expected to use. *If a black card engraved 01 is ever placed on this desk, press the red button under the counter before you speak to anyone.*

Her finger shook. She pressed it.

* * *

Then Dai Wenbin, General Manager of the Yunhai Grand Hotel, ran.

He had not run in twenty years.

He reached the counter and looked at the card. Then he looked at the young man in the gray jacket, who looked a little sleepy.

Three years ago, in the week the hotel opened, a gray-haired trustee of the holding company had sat in Dai Wenbin's office and said only this. *One day a young man will put this card on your counter. Serve him, ask nothing, tell nobody.*

Dai Wenbin had waited three years, picturing a prince or a general. He had not pictured a bun wrapper sticking out of a canvas bag.

He bowed to the waist, a full ninety degrees, with his hands flat at his sides.

"Sir. Welcome. I'm Dai Wenbin, General Manager. Please forgive us for making you stand at a counter." He lowered his voice. "Your pass is honored here, sir. Please come up."

Twenty clerks stopped breathing.

"General Manager Dai?" Hu Dewei squeaked. "You know Supervisor Chen?"

Dai Wenbin did not know the young man's name. His orders said ask nothing. He had never obeyed anything so hard in his life.

"Tonight the Peony Room is nothing," he told the whole lobby. "I'm opening the thirty-eighth-floor Cloud Sea Hall for your department, free of charge."

"The Cloud Sea Hall?" a clerk whispered. "My cousin says a table up there costs over twenty thousand. That's almost two months of Chen's pay!"

"Evening, Manager Dai. Thanks." Chen Xuan dropped the card and the float into his bag. "Home-style dishes are enough, please. Nothing fancy. Don't waste food. My mom would never forgive me."

Dai Wenbin held the golden elevator doors with both hands. Pan Xiulan stepped in behind Chen Xuan.

"Dai!"

A bell rang from the other elevator bank. A young man in an open silk shirt stepped out with two friends, holding his phone like a weapon.

It was Qi Zihao, a Silver member at twenty thousand yuan a year. He called the Yunhai Grand Hotel "my place" at every table.

"A message just told me my card was refused at your front desk," he said, loud enough for the whole lobby. "In front of Zhang's whole company! And you gave the Cloud Sea Hall to a man off a bus? You told me last month it can't be bought for any money!"

He swung his phone toward the counter. "Who's sitting in my seat, Dai? My father's company books four hundred room nights here every year. One call and that stops. And you, at the counter. Give me your name and the village on your ID card by midnight. By Monday no hotel in Qingyan will hire you, and your family at home will hear why."

The clerk's hand went flat on the marble to keep from shaking.

From inside the elevator, Chen Xuan did not look at Qi Zihao. He looked at the clerk.

"Miss. What is your name?"

"Xia Mengyao, sir," she whispered.

"Manager Dai, write that down. Xia Mengyao keeps her job, her pay and her shifts. If any of the three changes, I will ask you why."

Dai Wenbin pulled out a pen and wrote the name on the back of his hand.

Chen Xuan had not raised his voice. The lobby went so still that the fountain outside sounded loud through the glass. Qi Zihao's two friends stepped back from him.

Zhang Pengfei slipped into the elevator at the last second, as far from Qi Zihao as he could get. His voice shook.

"Supervisor Chen. How do you have a Zero-One pass at the Yunhai Grand Hotel?"

Chen Xuan scratched his ear, as if the question was a little silly.

"It belongs to the holding company. A friend of the company gave it to me for dinner."

Zhang Pengfei let out a shaky breath. The color came back to his face.

"A company card!" He laughed, almost sobbing with relief. "You borrowed a client's card! You nearly stopped my heart, Supervisor Chen!"

"Borrowed is a good word." Chen Xuan said it gravely. "Keep it between us, Pengfei. Don't tell Young Master Qi."

"Maybe he's the owner's secret godson!" Pan Xiulan whispered.

Chen Xuan put two fingers to his forehead and sighed.

* * *

In the lobby, the golden doors closed on the last of the clerks. Dai Wenbin stood straight for the first time in seven minutes. Then he wiped his neck with a handkerchief and found his hand was shaking.

The front-desk clerk whispered, white to the lips. "General Manager. He said he borrowed it. Was he only borrowing a company card?"

"Borrowing?" Dai Wenbin watched the floor numbers climb. "That pass has never been used in three years. So who is the friend?"

Behind them, Qi Zihao had not moved. His ears had gone white.

"Dai." He spoke far more quietly than before. "Who is that man?"

Dai Wenbin looked at the Silver card in Qi Zihao's hand. He had no answer for a Silver member about a man whose name he was forbidden to learn.

* * *

At eleven that night, Qi Jiancheng, Qi Zihao's father, hung up on twenty minutes of his son's shouting. Then he called an old friend in the Organization Department, the office that picks officials.

"Check a man for me. Chen Xuan, a clerk at Hengxin Logistics. He took my son's seat tonight."

Keys clicked. "Simple, honest, nothing," said the friend. "A boring file, Jiancheng."

"Too boring. Open the rest."

There was a long silence. When the friend spoke again, his voice was thin.

"The screen just went red. PERMISSION DENIED. And under it: *This attempt has been reported.*"

"Reported to whom?"

"Do not ask. And Jiancheng, tell your son to stay away from that hotel."

The line went dead. Qi Jiancheng stood in his dark study with the phone in his hand. Cold sweat ran down his back.

Then the phone rang again, from a number he had never seen.
