# Chapter 89: The Joint National Clean-Up Order

Thick cigarette smoke filled the crisis hall in Beijing's Great Hall. Around the massive wooden table sat the heads of more than forty central departments: the Military, State Security, Public Security, the Discipline Office, the Supreme Court, the Supreme Prosecutor's Office, the National People's Congress, the Advisory Conference, the Organization Department, the Development Commission, Finance, and Customs.

Behind the main table sat an inner ring of mahogany chairs that had not been filled all at once in twenty years.

In the center sat four white-haired Founding Elders: ninety-year-old Elder Song, leaning both thin hands on a dark cane; Elder Qin, his posture straight as a bayonet; Elder Ye, his jaw set like granite; and Elder Lin, a cigarette burning calmly between his fingers. Flanking them sat the heads of Beijing's second-tier and third-tier political and military families, including Zhao Mingyuan, Xu Chengwen, Du Jianhua, and Zheng Haoran.

Red-stamped files lay stacked before every seat. Cold tea sat untouched.

At the lower end, Commerce Minister Ruan Defu wiped his glasses with a shaking handkerchief. "Premier Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, I do not doubt the forty-two cases. My worry is local trade. Deng Yongchang's web touches three port firms, two regional banks, and four shell companies. Even with the new reserves, freezing every account tonight could rattle southern business."

Development Commission Director Tong Zhaoxing nodded fast. "Spread the freezes over sixty days so provinces can breathe."

General Qin Zhengguo slammed a red folder flat onto the wood. The crack echoed like a gunshot. Ruan Defu flinched so hard his glasses clattered beside his cup.

"Sixty days?" Qin Zhengguo barked, eyes blazing. "Give them sixty hours and those traitors move half their stolen wealth into foreign vaults we can never touch! Did you read Case Seven, Tong Zhaoxing? Deng Yongchang sold military port leases through those companies! That is treason!"

"General Qin Zhengguo is right," Discipline Office Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi said coldly, tapping ash into a brass tray. "Supreme Prosecutor Kuang Shouyi already has forty-seven warrants signed across six provinces, and Supreme Court Vice President Mu Delin has special judges ready for closed trials. Every wasted hour lets a guilty man swallow a pill or board a private plane."

From the mahogany chairs behind the table came a careful cough. Du Jianhua, head of a second-tier military family, clasped his hands smoothly while sweat shone above his collar.

"Premier Gu Chengshan, Founding Elders, nobody excuses treason," Du Jianhua said softly. "Still, several lower names are foolish nephews and cousins from old Beijing houses. If forty departments drag them out in public handcuffs tomorrow, outsiders may think our founding circle is tearing itself apart. Could we handle minor relatives through internal family discipline first?"

Zheng Haoran and Xu Chengwen stayed quiet, yet both looked up with hopeful eyes.

The hall turned icy cold.

Elder Qin did not even glance at Du Jianhua. He sipped his tea and set the cup down with a sharp clink.

"Internal family discipline?" Elder Qin's voice cut like winter wind. "Du Jianhua, your nephew took 300 million yuan in kickbacks from a coastal radar contractor. Since when does a beating at home pay for selling out our coastline?"

Du Jianhua's face went white. "Elder Qin, I did not know the figure! I swear on my father's grave!"

"Then shut your mouth and read your folder," Elder Ye snapped across the smoke, pointing sharply at the second-tier row. "For twenty years, half of you hid behind your grandfathers' medals while your cousins drank Maotai with smugglers. Now you want to sweep the blood under the rug to save face?"

Elder Lin exhaled gray smoke and fixed his eyes on Xu Chengwen and Zhao Mingyuan. "And drop any dream of using the banks as leverage. Every man here knows 300 trillion yuan in clean, legal reserves now backs China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank. Nobody is too big to touch, and no family name in Beijing stands above the law."

Elder Song tapped his dark cane once against the stone floor.

Thock.

Every voice died. Even Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua turned in quiet respect.

"Seventy years ago, we did not bleed in the snow so our grandsons could sit above the law," Elder Song said in his raspy voice, sweeping every family head in the ring. "Tonight, the Song family signs first. The Qin, Ye, and Lin families sign right behind me. Every second-tier and third-tier family in Beijing puts its name on the witness bond beside the forty departments. If my own grandson breaks the law, Guo Zhengming arrests him at the front gate. Clean house strictly by rank and law."

Silence pressed down like a mountain. Du Jianhua slumped in defeat. Zhao Mingyuan and Xu Chengwen stared grimly at their knees. The old game had ended forever.

Premier Gu Chengshan lit a cigarette with a worn steel lighter and blew a thin stream of smoke.

"At eight-thirty tonight, the state stops reacting and starts ruling again," Gu Chengshan declared, setting the lighter down with a click. "Before the clerk passes the Joint National Clean-Up and State Asset Recovery Order, every department head and family elder hears three iron rules."

He raised one finger.

"First: full legal action. From the lowest street fixer in Yunxi County to the highest backer in Beijing, every proven name faces arrest, trial, and sentence under the law. No quiet medical retirements. No family shields."

State Security Minister Ding Guoping uncapped his fountain pen. Beside him, Jin Rongzhi rested one calm hand on blank red folders.

Gu Chengshan raised a second finger.

"Second: full recovery of stolen state wealth. Ruan Defu, Tong Zhaoxing, Finance, and Customs freeze every linked domestic and overseas account tonight. Backed by our clean reserve shield across the Four Major National Banks, not one saver loses a fen while every stolen yuan returns to the treasury."

Ruan Defu swallowed and nodded. "Commerce starts the freeze at nine-oh-five, Premier Gu Chengshan."

"And third," Gu Chengshan said, his voice dropping until the air felt tight. "Complete secrecy about Black Guard and the source of the reserve."

Even a clerk's pencil froze in midair.

"Zero official mention of unlisted numbers, black cases, or outside guards in any court file or record," Gu Chengshan ordered, his eyes cutting down the table. "To the courts, press, and history, this clean-up began with an internal check and ended with the power of the law. Any official or family member who breaks this seal faces a military court as a traitor."

He crushed his cigarette and pulled the leather signing book forward. "Sign it."

His pen scratched across thick paper. Zhou Zhenhua and Qin Zhengguo signed next, followed in silence by more than forty department heads: Gao Zhengyi, Ding Guoping, Jin Rongzhi, Guo Zhengming, Mu Delin, Kuang Shouyi, Ruan Defu, Tong Zhaoxing, and the legislative deputies.

Then the clerk carried the book to the inner ring.

Elder Song wrote his name cleanly with steady spotted fingers. Elder Qin, Elder Ye, and Elder Lin signed beneath him. One by one, Zhao Mingyuan, Xu Chengwen, Du Jianhua, Zheng Haoran, and every remaining family head put ink to paper, binding every house in Beijing to the law.

Two minutes before nine, the last signature dried. Nobody cheered. Every powerful man in the hall felt cold steel at his spine as the clean-up began.
