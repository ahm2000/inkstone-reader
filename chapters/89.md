# Chapter 89: The Joint National Clean-Up Order

Evening settled deeply over Beijing as the heavy bronze doors of the Great Hall's main crisis chamber swung shut with a dull, final thud. Bright chandeliers cast clear, steady light over the polished walnut ring table and tall marble pillars. Rows of crimson-tabbed binders lay neatly aligned in front of every seat, the air crisp with the scent of fresh paper and cold tea.

At the head of the inner ring sat Premier Gu Chengshan, his dark jacket buttoned straight, his face carved with quiet resolve. Around the inner ring sat all 24 core voting members of the Capital Top Council, every one holding half-premier or sub-national rank above provincial secretaries and cabinet ministers, including Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua.

Directly behind the head of the table stood four high-backed wooden chairs, occupied together for the first time in twenty years by four senior founding and retired leaders, including ninety-year-old Elder Song and retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo. None of the four held a voting binder, yet their silent presence turned the chamber into the highest court of political accountability in the country.

Premier Gu Chengshan opened the top crimson binder and looked slowly around the walnut ring.

"Every member here has reviewed the final joint investigation dossier," Premier Gu began, his voice carrying cleanly across the chamber. "Over the past five days, the Central Discipline Inspection teams, the Anti-Corruption Bureau, and the Supreme Procuratorate have verified every paper trail, bank ledger, and witness record from Handong Province and three coastal hubs."

He turned to the first tabbed page. "The facts are plain and beyond dispute. Shanshui Group ran a decade-long scheme of illegal land seizures, rigged municipal tenders, and hollow regional bank loans. To protect those profits, senior officials fabricated criminal cases at the county police level, silenced honest witnesses, and moved dirty assets across borders. Zhao Dehan, Zhao Ruilong, and Gao Yuliang are already in custody, and their signed confessions match the bank audits line for line."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua leaned forward slightly. "More importantly, the evidence shows this was not the work of three isolated men. A wider protective network inside provincial offices, municipal bureaus, and judicial departments cleared their paperwork and buried citizen complaints for years."

"Tonight, we do not issue vague warnings," Premier Gu said firmly. "We vote on the Joint National Clean-Up Order. Every decision will be recorded by show of hands and executed across all ministries and provinces starting at 8:00 AM tomorrow."

He raised his gaze to the 24 core members. "Resolution One: Direct arrests and full criminal prosecution. This covers seventeen provincial and municipal officials, nine senior police and judicial officers tied to Qi Tongwei's remaining network, and twenty-one bank executives who knowingly approved forged collateral. All will be stripped of office immediately and transferred to the Procuratorate for public trial."

Premier Gu raised his right hand. "All in favor of Resolution One."

Without a single second of hesitation, twenty-four right hands rose around the walnut ring. The chamber clerk logged the unanimous tally with a crisp stroke of his pen.

"Resolution One passes unanimously," Premier Gu announced. He turned to the second crimson tab. "Resolution Two: Public demotions and party expulsions. This covers thirty-four mid-to-high-level officials who did not take direct cash bribes, yet knowingly turned a blind eye, signed irregular land transfers, or pressured subordinates to drop valid investigations. They are dismissed from leadership posts, stripped of rank, and permanently barred from public office."

A faint rustle of paper moved around the table as several members glanced at familiar names in their binders. Some of those officials had built careers over thirty years, only to trade their integrity for quiet convenience.

"All in favor of Resolution Two," Premier Gu called out.

Again, all twenty-four hands rose steadily into the chandelier light. Not one member lowered his eyes or hesitated.

"Resolution Two passes unanimously," Premier Gu said. He turned to the third and final tab. "Resolution Three: Hollow exile transfers. Our investigators identified nineteen senior faction allies and overly cautious department heads against whom there is no criminal proof, yet whose long-standing ties to Gao Yuliang and coastal networks make them unfit to hold economic, personnel, or security authority."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua added calmly, "Leaving them in operational posts would slow down every reform we pass next month. Transferring them out cleanly avoids baseless charges while removing their ability to obstruct."

"Under Resolution Three," Premier Gu stated, "all nineteen are reassigned immediately to non-voting historical archives, policy research offices, and advisory committees far from public budgets, land approvals, and appointment powers. All in favor."

For the third time, twenty-four hands rose in complete unison.

Behind the head of the table, Elder Song gave a single, slow nod. Beside him, Elder Qin Zhengguo sat straight as a pine, his weathered face calm and approving.

Premier Gu closed his binder and folded his hands over the red seal on its cover. "Finally, this Council formally affirms its full backing for the upright officials leading the clean-up on the ground. Provincial Secretary Sha Ruijin, Jingzhou Secretary Li Dakang, Anti-Corruption Chief Hou Liangping, and Chief Procurator Ji Changming are granted full authority to rebuild regional governance, clear every fabricated county case in Yunxi and Jingzhou, and restore strict rule of law without interference from any quarter."

He picked up the heavy red state stamp beside his blotter and pressed it firmly onto the final page of the Joint National Clean-Up Order. The sharp click echoed off the marble pillars.

"This session is adjourned," Premier Gu said. "Transmit the sealed orders to every provincial committee and ministry tonight."

As the heavy bronze doors swung open, couriers in dark suits stepped forward to collect the sealed packets. Outside the Great Hall, a crisp autumn wind swept across the wide stone square, carrying away the last warmth of the season as a stricter, cleaner chapter began across the country.
