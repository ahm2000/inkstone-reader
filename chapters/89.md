# Chapter 89: The Joint National Clean-Up Order

Evening settled deeply over Beijing as the heavy bronze doors of the Great Hall's main crisis chamber swung shut with a dull, final thud. Bright chandeliers cast clear, steady light over the polished walnut ring table and tall marble pillars. Rows of crimson-tabbed binders lay neatly aligned in front of every seat, the air crisp with the scent of fresh paper and cold tea.

At the head of the inner ring sat Premier Gu Chengshan, his dark jacket buttoned straight, his face carved with quiet resolve. Around the inner ring sat all 24 core voting members of the Capital Top Council, every one holding half-premier or sub-national rank above provincial secretaries and cabinet ministers, including Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua.

Directly behind the head of the table stood four high-backed wooden chairs, occupied together for the first time in twenty years. Ninety-year-old Elder Song sat with his thin hands resting calmly atop his dark cane. Beside him sat retired General Elder Qin Zhengguo, spine straight as a bayonet, followed by Elder Chen Yanshi with his jaw set like granite, and Elder Lin with his fingers folded neatly over his sleeve.

In the wide outer ring sat every non-standing cabinet minister and central agency head: Military, State Security, Public Security, Discipline Inspection, Supreme Court, Supreme Procuratorate, Finance, Commerce, Customs, and Organization. At the archive desk beside the eastern marble pillar waited two senior clerks with heavy brass state seals and crimson wax boxes.

Premier Gu Chengshan opened the crimson binder in front of him and looked slowly around the quiet ring.

"Every member here has read the forty-two evidence cases verified this afternoon," Gu Chengshan began, his voice clear and unhurried. "In Handong Province alone, Deputy Provincial Secretary Gao Yuliang, Shanshui Group head Zhao Ruilong, former Ministry Division Chief Zhao Dehan, and former Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen built a private kingdom. Direct cash bribes exceed 200 million, and tangled provincial debt and stolen state land tied to Shanshui Group exceed 1.2 trillion."

No one in the outer ring dared shuffle a single sheet of paper.

"Worse still," Gu Chengshan continued, "those ledgers trace straight up through three central ministries and two financial boards. Public Security Director Qi Tongwei's provincial protection ring did not grow on its own. It was watered from right here in Beijing."

Near the lower curve of the inner ring, a silver-haired council member raised two fingers slightly. "Premier, the evidence is undeniable. Still, removing dozens of senior bureau chiefs and provincial figures in a single sweep may rattle regional markets and stir resistance inside old ministerial factions. Should we phase the discipline hearings over two quarters?"

Before Premier Gu Chengshan could reply, a single sharp tap echoed across the marble floor.

Elder Song had lifted his dark cane one inch and set it back down. The entire chamber froze in absolute silence.

"A house with rotten beams does not stay standing because you paint over the cracks," Elder Song said softly, his voice dry yet carrying effortlessly to every corner of the hall. "Wait two quarters, and the roof falls on the heads of ordinary citizens."

Elder Song let his steady gaze sweep across the 24 voting members.

"Furthermore, none of you should forget who placed those forty-two cases at our doorstep," Elder Song added calmly. "The holder of the Black Order asked for nothing for himself. He merely reminded this hall of the founding pledge we signed in harder years. If we hesitate tonight to protect comfortable chairs, we dishonor the republic itself."

Beside him, Elder Qin Zhengguo spoke one crisp sentence. "All five military theater commands are already on clean-order readiness. No faction in any province will move a single finger."

The silver-haired council member lowered his eyes immediately and nodded once. "I withdraw my reservation, Premier."

Premier Gu Chengshan closed the binder and stood straight behind the walnut table.

"Then I place the Joint National Clean-Up Order before the Capital Top Council for formal vote," Gu Chengshan announced. "First, immediate removal from office and full prosecution for Gao Yuliang, Zhao Ruilong, Qi Tongwei, and every central ministry protector named in the forty-two ledgers. Second, complete asset freezes across every Shanshui Group holding and offshore shell."

He turned one page on the decree sheet.

"Third, full joint authority granted directly to Handong Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin, Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang, Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping, and Chief Procurator Ji Changming to finish clearing the province without outside interference. Fourth, immediate hollow exile transfers to remote archival desks and frontier posts for every borderline official who looked the other way."

Gu Chengshan looked around the inner ring. "All 24 core voting members, vote by show of hands."

Not a chair creaked. In complete, heavy silence, all 24 right hands rose together into the bright chandelier light.

"Twenty-four in favor," Premier Gu Chengshan stated firmly. "Zero opposed. Zero abstentions. The Joint National Clean-Up Order passes unanimously."

He signed the bottom line with a steady stroke and passed the thick white parchment across to the eastern pillar desk. The two senior archive clerks pressed the heavy brass state seal down into the warm crimson wax, stamping the order into law.

As the tall bronze doors swung open to dispatch sealed couriers into the Beijing night, Elder Song remained seated quietly behind the table, watching the ministers hurry out with pale, urgent faces. Tomorrow morning, the storm would break over every corrupt office in the country, while far away in a quiet county, an ordinary shop would simply open its doors to the sun.
