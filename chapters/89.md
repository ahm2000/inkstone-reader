# Chapter 89: The Joint National Clean-Up Order

Gray cigarette smoke drifted toward the tall ceiling of the crisis hall in Beijing's Great Hall.

Around the inner ring sat all 24 core voting members of the Capital Top Council, every one at half-premier or sub-national rank above provincial secretaries and ministers.

Behind the head of the table sat four senior chairs filled together for the first time in twenty years: ninety-year-old Elder Song, thin hands on his dark cane; Elder Qin, straight as a bayonet; Elder Chen Yanshi, jaw set like stone; and Elder Lin, hands folded on the table.

In the wide outer ring sat all non-standing cabinet ministers and central agency heads: Military, State Security, Public Security, Discipline Office, Supreme Court, Supreme Prosecutor's Office, Finance, Commerce, Customs, and Organization. At the archive desk beside the pillar sat two senior clerks with fountain pens poised over thick linen ledgers for the sealed minutes.

Premier Gu Chengshan nodded toward the lectern. "Present the final clean-up report."

Discipline Office Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi stepped up with a red binder and spoke crisply.

"Premier Gu Chengshan, Founding Elders, and core voting members," Gao Zhengyi reported. "All branches of the joint clean-up are ready. All 318 core traitors, from county fixers in Yunxi County and Handong Province up to senior ministry backers in Beijing, sit in custody with verified bank, shipping, and voice proof. Across China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank, private citizen Chen Xuan's 300 billion yuan clean, legal gift has held the Beijing and Shanghai markets steady, with 500 billion yuan on standby. Every linked domestic shell firm and overseas vault is mapped for full recovery. Report complete."

Gao Zhengyi closed his binder, bowed once, and returned to his outer-ring seat.

Heavy silence filled the hall.

Across the 24 core seats, polite masks held firm, yet a cold chill slid down every spine. Now the truth lay open, backed by a 300 billion yuan shield from an unseen citizen who asked for neither title nor favor. An unspoken debt pressed on every chest.

Premier Gu Chengshan tapped ash into his saucer and spoke first, his voice calm as bronze.

"Tonight, the state stops reacting and starts ruling again," Premier Gu Chengshan declared, sweeping his gaze across all 24 core seats. "Before the final vote on the Joint National Clean-Up Order, three iron laws stand above debate. First, every proven traitor faces full arrest, trial, and sentence strictly by law, with zero medical retirements and zero family shields. Second, every stolen yuan returns to the treasury while ordinary savers and honest factories stay safe behind our financial firebreak. Third, absolute secrecy protects the Black Guard and the citizen behind Number 1. Not one word about outside guards or the reserve's source enters any court file, newspaper, or private room. Anyone who breaks that seal faces a military court as a traitor."

In the outer ring, forty silent ministers lowered their heads, pens moving swiftly.

Premier Gu Chengshan turned slightly. "Zhou Zhenhua, state the full personnel order."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua lifted a crimson folder, every word landing like cold steel.

"Cleaning house requires more than prison gates," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said evenly. "This order uses every personnel tool in the state's hand so clean blood rises and dirty roots never regrow. First, awards and rapid promotions: frontline discipline officers, honest county cadres in Handong Province, and clean bank auditors who refused bribes receive national commendations and immediate double-step promotions to fill the emptied chairs."

He turned a page smoothly.

"Second, iron punishments: all 318 traitors face full prosecution under civil and military law, with total seizure of illegal wealth. Third, withheld ranks: ninety-four negligent senior officials across central bureaus and provinces who looked away or stalled audits have their promotions canceled and ranks frozen permanently."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua paused one beat, sweeping several stiff faces in the inner ring before reading the fourth line.

"Fourth, clean dismantling of faction webs: forty-six faction-tainted senior cadres nationwide whose hands lack enough criminal proof for prison, yet whose patronage ties poison every office they hold, transfer tomorrow morning. Each receives a higher-sounding, powerless honorary or advisory title in historical study commissions and senior culture committees. Every budget, seal, staff roster, and signature leaves their hands overnight."

Quiet dread rippled beneath the calm surface of the 24-seat ring.

Two half-premier members stared at their folded hands. Every veteran in Beijing understood exile-by-promotion. Handing a power-hungry faction head a grand honorary title with zero staff and zero budget was the coldest, cleanest political burial in the capital.

From behind the head of the table, Elder Song tapped his dark cane once against the stone floor.

Thock.

Even the scratch of pens froze. Every back straightened.

"Seventy years ago, we did not bleed in the snow so our descendants or old factions could sit above the law," Elder Song said in his raspy, quiet voice, cloudy eyes sweeping both rings. "Tonight, the Song house signs the witness bond first. Elder Qin, Elder Chen Yanshi, and Elder Lin sign right behind me. No grandfather's medal will ever shield a crooked relative again. An unseen citizen trusted this nation with 300 billion yuan and asked for nothing. If the 24 leaders at this table cannot match that spine, we do not deserve these chairs."

Silence pressed like a mountain.

Premier Gu Chengshan squared his folder cleanly.

"We now call the final show-of-hands vote on the Joint National Clean-Up Order and the full personnel decree," Premier Gu Chengshan announced. "All 24 core voting members in favor, raise your right hand."

Hands rose crisply around the mahogany ring. Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua raised theirs first, followed by twenty-one other half-premier members.

One seat stayed frozen.

Vice Chairman Liao Guohua sat stiff as dry wood, pale face tight, fingers gripping his red folder. Three of his oldest protégés sat on that list of forty-six faction cadres marked for powerless honorary exile. His wrist twitched between old loyalty and survival.

For two agonizing seconds, the hall held its breath.

Nobody spoke. Yet ninety-year-old Elder Song turned slowly and locked his quiet, piercing eyes directly onto Liao Guohua. That silent gaze felt like winter iron pressed against the back of Liao Guohua's neck.

At the end of the second agonizing second, Liao Guohua swallowed hard. His trembling right hand rose straight into the air.

"Abstentions?" Premier Gu Chengshan asked quietly. Zero hands moved.

"Against?" Zero hands moved.

At the archive desk, the chief clerk finished writing in black ink and read the entry aloud.

"Formal vote recorded in the sealed state minutes: twenty-four in favor, zero abstentions, zero against. Unanimous. All 24 core voting members logged by full name. Order passed and sealed permanently in the state archives."

A heavy red seal pressed onto the thick linen ledger. In the outer ring, forty cabinet ministers closed their notebooks in quiet awe. Nobody cheered. Every leader in Beijing felt the cold weight of the law settle permanently over his shoulders.
