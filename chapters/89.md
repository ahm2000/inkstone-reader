# Chapter 89: The Joint National Clean-Up Order

At eight-thirty on Monday night, cigarette smoke hung so thick inside the Great Hall crisis room that the ceiling lamps glowed through haze. Around the long mahogany table sat the heads and senior deputies of more than forty central organs: Military, State Security, Public Security, CCDI, the Supreme Court, the Supreme Procuratorate, the NPC Standing Committee, CPPCC, Organization, NDRC, Finance, and Customs.

Every chair was filled. Red-stamped dossiers lay stacked before every nameplate, and cold tea sat untouched.

At the lower end, Ma Zhenyu of Finance wiped his glasses with a trembling handkerchief. "Premier Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, I do not question the forty-two cases. My concern is macroeconomic. Deng Yongchang's holding web touches three port conglomerates, two regional banks, and four listed infrastructure shells. If Customs and Finance freeze every account before sunrise, southern markets will bleed by ten tomorrow."

Beside him, Li Weiming of the NDRC nodded fast, face grey with exhaustion. "Ma Zhenyu is right. Stage the seizures over ninety days so we can ring-fence provincial bonds and avoid alarming foreign clearinghouses."

Before Premier Gu Chengshan could speak, General Qin Zhengguo slammed a crimson folder flat onto the wood. The crack echoed like a rifle shot. Ma Zhenyu flinched so hard his glasses clattered beside his cup.

"Ninety days?" General Qin Zhengguo barked, eyes blazing under grey brows. "Give them ninety hours and half those offshore shells vanish into foreign trusts no treaty can touch! Did you read Case Seven, Li Weiming? Did you see the military port leasing clauses Deng Yongchang brokered through those conglomerates? That is not market friction. That is high treason!"

"General Qin Zhengguo is right," CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi said coldly, tapping ash into a crowded brass tray. "We verified every routing number with the Supreme Procuratorate. Xu Wenbin has forty-seven arrest warrants queued for six provinces the second this directive carries the joint seal. Every delayed hour buys a guilty official time to swallow a pill or catch a charter."

Xu Wenbin of the Supreme Procuratorate gave a curt nod, jaw set. "And Lin Zhengde at the Supreme Court has already cleared special panels for closed national security trials. Legally, the chain of custody is airtight."

"Airtight," Guo Zhengming of Public Security muttered, rubbing bloodshot eyes. "Except nobody in this room can sign a subpoena for how those forty-two cases landed on our desks."

A suffocating hush clamped down on the room. Several ministers dropped their eyes to their folders. Ding Guoping of the Organization Department shifted stiffly, his cigarette burning down toward his knuckles unnoticed.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua leaned forward, forearms flat on the table, his gaze sweeping from Ma Zhenyu to Li Weiming and across every tense face.

"Let me remind everyone," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said softly, forcing the room to lean in, "that the forensic and telemetry audit finished this afternoon and is sealed at the highest level. Every document inside those cases has already been independently corroborated against our own banking switches, customs logs, and personnel files. Deng Yongchang's network is real. The treason is real. The stolen sovereign wealth is real."

He held Ma Zhenyu's eyes until the Finance official looked down.

"If any ministry tries to soften a clause tonight to shield a former protege or a quarterly index," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua added, "CCDI and State Security will open a fresh file before you reach the garage."

Han Zhiyuan of State Security did not smile, resting one calm hand on a stack of blank red folders beside his elbow.

"Customs already has port holds drafted for all twelve coastal terminals," a grey-haired Customs deputy spoke up fast, his palms damp on his dossier. "Give us the signed directive and no container linked to Deng Yongchang leaves territorial waters tonight."

"And the NPC Standing Committee and CPPCC liaisons have cleared the emergency suspension notices for three sitting provincial deputies named in Cases Twelve and Thirty-One," the NPC representative added stiffly, avoiding everyone's eyes. "No procedural immunity will delay Guo Zhengming's arrest teams."

Guo Zhengming let out a short, grim breath through his nose. "Good. My officers have been sitting in unmarked vans outside six residences since seven o'clock waiting for the green light."

At the head of the table, Premier Gu Chengshan lit a cigarette with a battered steel lighter and drew a slow breath of smoke. Deep lines scored the sides of his mouth, yet his eyes were winter steel.

"Enough," Premier Gu Chengshan said.

Every murmur died.

"At eight-thirty tonight, the state stops reacting and starts governing again," Premier Gu Chengshan said, setting the lighter down with a sharp click. "Before the clerk circulates the Joint National Clean-Up and Sovereign Asset Recovery Directive, every head in this room will hear three iron rules. No amendments. No private interpretations."

He raised one finger through the smoke.

"First: total lawful prosecution. From the lowest district fixer in Yunxi to the highest patron in the capital, every corroborated name will be indicted, tried, and sentenced strictly under law. No quiet medical retirements. No internal slaps on the wrist. No protective umbrellas."

Ding Guoping exhaled slowly and uncapped his fountain pen.

Premier Gu Chengshan raised a second finger.

"Second: total sovereign asset recovery. Ma Zhenyu, Li Weiming, and Customs will freeze every linked domestic and offshore conduit tonight. Every yuan stolen from public land, military procurement, and state infrastructure returns to the national treasury. If the southern index drops three points tomorrow, let it drop. A clean foundation beats a painted crack."

Ma Zhenyu swallowed hard and nodded once, his pale face steadying. "Finance executes the freeze at nine-oh-five, Premier Gu Chengshan."

"And third," Premier Gu Chengshan said, his voice dropping until the room felt airless, "absolute secrecy regarding Zero Guard."

Nobody moved. Even a clerk's pencil at the back wall froze.

"Zero official mention of unrecorded numbers, matte-black cases, or perimeter sentries in any court transcript, memo, or archive," Premier Gu Chengshan stated, eyes sweeping like a blade down the table. "No intelligence branch opens an inquiry. No military office runs a trace. To the courts, the press, and history, this clean-up began with an internal joint inspection and ended with the weight of the state. Any official who breaches this seal faces martial law as a traitor."

He crushed his half-smoked cigarette into the ashtray and pulled the leather signature ledger forward.

"Sign it," Premier Gu Chengshan ordered.

His pen scratched firmly across thick cotton paper.

He slid the ledger to Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, who signed without hesitation and passed it to General Qin Zhengguo. Around the table, forty-plus hands pressed ink into history: Gao Zhengyi, Han Zhiyuan, Guo Zhengming, Lin Zhengde, Xu Wenbin, Ding Guoping, Ma Zhenyu, Li Weiming, and the liaisons of the NPC and CPPCC.

When the final signature dried, the brass wall clock stood at two minutes to nine.

Nobody cheered. As exhausted hands gathered red folders, every official felt the same unspoken weight at the back of his neck: outside the granite walls, the silent watchers still stood in the dark.
