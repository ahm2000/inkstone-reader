# Chapter 182: Seeing the Cousins Off

By early afternoon, the wide departure hall of Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station hummed with the steady rhythm of rolling suitcases and platform announcements. Sunlight poured through the high glass roof, catching the dust motes above the rows of boarding gates. Chen Xuan carried two bulging canvas tote bags in one hand while Qin Yuhan walked beside Chen Yuan, adjusting the collar of the girl's jacket against the draft from the automatic doors.

Inside the canvas bags sat half a bakery's worth of Shanghai treats that Qin Yuhan had insisted on buying the evening before. There were flaky butterfly pastries from the International Hotel, tins of walnut crisps, boxes of soft green bean cakes, salted egg yolk pastries, and two neatly wrapped cashmere scarves for the elders back home.

"Sister-in-law, this is way too much," Chen Hao protested as they stopped near Gate 14, though his eyes kept drifting happily toward the pastry boxes. "My mom is going to scold us for emptying your pantry."

"Tell Auntie these are from both of us," Qin Yuhan replied with a gentle smile, handing the lighter tote to Chen Yuan. "The walnut crisps stay fresh for two weeks, and the scarves will be useful once December cold hits. Make sure you share the butterfly pastries on the train before they get crushed."

Chen Xuan set the heavier bag down by Chen Hao's feet and reached into his coat pocket. He slipped a neat red envelope into Chen Yuan's jacket pocket before she could dodge away. "Keep this for snacks and books this semester," he said lightly. "Do not let your brother talk you into spending it all on arcade games."

"Hey, I have not been to an arcade since middle school!" Chen Hao complained, puffing out his cheeks. Then his voice softened a little as the digital board above Gate 14 flickered to show their 2:30 PM high-speed train preparing to board. "Thanks, Brother Xuan. Thanks, Sister-in-law. This was the best weekend we have had all year."

Chen Yuan stepped forward and gave Qin Yuhan a quick, tight hug. "I am going to work really hard on my grades," she promised quietly. "Next summer, I want to come back and help you cook dinner at the villa."

"Deal," Qin Yuhan said, patting the girl's back warmly. "Message our family chat the moment your train pulls into the station."

They stood behind the glass barrier as the line moved forward. Chen Hao slung the heavy canvas bag over his shoulder to look grown-up, turning back twice to wave enthusiastically until both teenagers vanished down the escalator toward the platform.

The ride back across the city to Pudong felt noticeably quieter. Outside the car windows, the late November sun was already sinking toward the western skyline, brushing the surface of the Huangpu River with warm copper light. Chen Xuan kept one hand lightly on the steering wheel, cruising at an unhurried pace in the middle lane.

Qin Yuhan leaned her head against the headrest and let out a contented sigh. "The house is going to feel a little too quiet tonight," she murmured, glancing out at the passing trees along the riverside road. "Having them around made the whole weekend feel livelier."

"We can always invite them back during winter break," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile. "Though next time, I am making Chen Hao peel the garlic and wash the vegetables before he gets any braised ribs."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly and reached over to rest her hand lightly on his forearm. "You pretend to be lazy, yet you stood in the kitchen for two hours last night making four different dishes just because Chen Yuan mentioned she liked sweet and sour spare ribs."

"That is called strategic household management," Chen Xuan replied without missing a beat. "Keep the younger generation well fed, and they will always take my side when you complain that I slack off at Hengxin Logistics."

When they pulled into the courtyard of Emerald Riverside Villa, the garden lights were just flickering on against the gathering dusk. A cool autumn wind rustled the bamboo along the low stone wall. Inside, the living room was warm and orderly, carrying the faint scent of cedar and clean linen.

Chen Xuan set his dented green thermos on the kitchen counter, washed his hands, and pulled a fresh white radish and a pack of pork ribs from the refrigerator. He liked Sunday evenings like this: no frantic schedules, no noisy banquets, just the soft hiss of a simmering soup pot and the unhurried pace of home.

Upstairs, Qin Yuhan changed into a soft gray knit sweater and comfortable lounge pants. She came back down with a thin tablet and a folder of Haiyue Group's November port summaries, curling up on the corner of the living room sofa beneath a woven wool blanket.

Ten minutes later, Chen Xuan walked over and set a steaming mug of oolong tea on the wooden coaster beside her. "Take it easy on the spreadsheets tonight," he advised, dropping onto the other end of the sofa for a moment. "You already impressed Professor Shen this morning. Give the rest of Shanghai's executives a chance to catch up."

Qin Yuhan took a careful sip of the warm tea and smiled over the rim of the mug. "Only if your radish soup tastes as good as it smells."

"Give it forty minutes," Chen Xuan said, rising lazily to head back toward the kitchen. "I will even let you have the best pieces."
