# Chapter 182: Tea at Tomson Riviera

By one o'clock in the afternoon, the huge departure hall of Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station buzzed with travelers, rolling suitcases, and bright boarding announcements echoing down the glass ceiling.

Chen Xuan stood beside Gate 14B, carrying two boxes of warm Shanghai butterfly pastries and green bean cakes in his left hand while steadying the three cream garment bags from Plaza 66 over his right arm.

Chen Yuan hurried over from the security check lane, carefully zipping her small backpack. She took the garment bags gently as if holding fine porcelain, then reached up to straighten the collar of Chen Xuan's dark-blue windbreaker.

"Do not skip dinner when you get busy at work, Ge," Chen Yuan scolded softly, her dark eyes filled with familiar sisterly worry. "You always eat cold noodles at your desk when a shipping order runs late. Promise me you will eat hot rice at least once a day."

"I promise," Chen Xuan smiled warmly, handing her the two pastry boxes. "Give the green bean cakes to Mom tonight. Tell her the burgundy coat was on a huge seasonal discount from my company's partner, so she does not lock it inside the closet and refuse to wear it."

"I know how to handle Mom," Chen Yuan laughed lightly. "Wait until she sees how soft the cashmere is. She is going to show it off to every auntie on our street."

Beside her, Chen Hao slung his backpack over one shoulder and gripped the handle of his small suitcase. His face was still flushed with excitement from the morning walk through Fudan University and Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

"Ge, do not forget our bet," Chen Hao said seriously, sticking out his fist. "Next summer, when my Fudan University letter arrives, Lujiazui window table, biggest crab on the menu."

Chen Xuan bumped his younger brother's fist lightly. "I never break a deal. Study steady, sleep on time, and call me if you need anything for school."

"Got it, Ge! See you at Spring Festival!" Chen Hao grinned wide.

The boarding chime rang over the loudspeakers. Chen Yuan gave Chen Xuan one quick, tight hug, grabbed her brother's sleeve, and walked through the ticket gate with the stream of passengers. Halfway down the escalator toward the platform, both of them turned back and waved until the crowd carried them out of sight.

Chen Xuan stood by the glass railing for a few extra seconds, watching until the silver high-speed train doors closed on the display board. Only then did the relaxed smile on his face settle into his usual quiet calm.

Fifty meters back near a coffee counter, two silent Black Guard members in plain matte-black jackets lowered their paper cups without a word and fell into quiet step behind him at a steady distance as he walked out toward the west pickup lane.

Outside the terminal, a plain pearl-white sedan waited cleanly at the curb with its hazards off. The rear door opened before Chen Xuan even reached the handle. He climbed inside, and the car glided smoothly onto the elevated highway heading east toward Pudong.

Thirty minutes later, the pearl-white sedan crossed the Nanpu Bridge and turned onto quiet, tree-lined Huayuanshiqiao Road in the heart of Lujiazui.

Tall bronze gates swung open silently without a single honk. The car rolled down into the private underground garage of Tomson Riviera, known across Shanghai as Tangchen Yipin. Security guards in dark suits straightened sharply as the vehicle passed into the private elevator bay, though none of them stepped close or asked a single question.

Chen Xuan rode the private keycard elevator straight up to the thirty-sixth floor of Building A.

When the brushed-steel doors slid open, he stepped directly into a vast, sunlit living room he had bought years ago and barely visited twice a year. Soft afternoon sunlight poured through ten-meter floor-to-ceiling glass walls, bathing the pale marble floors in warm gold.

Directly outside the wide balcony flowed the broad curve of the Huangpu River. Across the water stood the historic stone buildings of the Bund, their clock towers gleaming in the sun. Just to the right, close enough that it felt like you could reach out and touch its silver-and-rose spheres, rose the Oriental Pearl Tower. Behind the building, the towering spires of Jin Mao Tower and Shanghai Tower climbed smoothly into the clear blue sky.

Inside the quiet living room, three figures stood neatly beside the long pale-stone tea table.

Number 1 stood at the head of the room in a crisp dark coat with subtle white trim along the cuffs, his posture straight and composed. Beside him stood Number 11 in a tailored charcoal suit with a clean white lapel pin, holding a thick white-sealed dossier. To his left stood Number 34, a sharp-eyed young woman in a neat white-lined trench coat, resting her hand beside a closed matte-white laptop and three matte-white hard drives lined up on the marble.

The moment Chen Xuan stepped across the threshold, all three inclined their heads together in crisp, silent respect.

"Sir," Number 1 greeted evenly.

"Sit," Chen Xuan said calmly, slipping off his faded dark-blue windbreaker and draping it casually over the back of a pale leather sofa. "No need to stand on ceremony. How is the tea?"

"Fresh Suzhou jasmine, steeped two minutes ago," Number 1 replied smoothly. He lifted the pale porcelain teapot and poured a clear golden cup, setting it neatly before Chen Xuan as the three of them took their seats across the table.

Chen Xuan picked up the warm porcelain cup, took a slow sip, and looked out briefly at the sunlit river barges gliding below the Oriental Pearl Tower.

"My brother and sister are safely on the train back to Sha Province," Chen Xuan said quietly. "Now tell me what Koo Tianming was hiding inside those Shenzhen drives."

Number 34 sat straight and opened the matte-white laptop, tapping a short unlock sequence across the keys.

"Sir, our technical team finished opening every locked file seized from Koo Tianming's villa at Shenzhen Bay No. 1 and his private safe house in Hong Kong at four o'clock this morning," Number 34 reported cleanly, her voice brisk and clear. "He used triple-layer bank codes from Zurich and Singapore to hide three separate sets of ledgers."

"Good work," Chen Xuan said mildly. "Were they mostly Shen Wanshan's shipping bribes?"

"Only the first layer, Sir," Number 34 replied, her eyes sharpening as she slid one of the matte-white drives forward. "The first layer listed Shenhai Shipping's 140 billion yuan network, the Yantian Port smuggling logs, and the 4 million yuan paid to corrupt dock inspectors. That part is already sitting on the desks of Shenzhen Public Security and the Four National Banks."

She paused and glanced at Number 11.

"The second and third layers were far bigger than Shen Wanshan," Number 11 spoke up, his calm voice dropping half a tone as he broke the white wax seal on the thick folder in front of him. "Shen Wanshan was never the owner of the Seven-Harbor Consortium. He was only their southern doorkeeper."

Chen Xuan's fingers paused slightly against the warm porcelain teacup. His gaze shifted smoothly from the sunlit river outside back to Number 11's face.

"Show me," Chen Xuan said softly.

Number 11 opened the white-sealed folder and spread three crisp printed sheets and a detailed geographic map across the pale marble table.

"For the last twelve years, Koo Tianming did not send the Seven-Harbor Consortium's real profits overseas to Hong Kong," Number 11 said quietly, tapping the northern corner of the map. "Every month, the biggest money and the highest-grade smuggled cargo went north. Straight into the snow-covered forests of Changbai Mountain."
