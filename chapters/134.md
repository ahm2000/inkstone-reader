# Chapter 134: Playing Hooky on Jingzhou Lake

By eleven o'clock that morning, the Handong shipping forum had wrapped up a full day ahead of schedule.

With Ding Yizhen and Cai Chenggong sitting in the provincial detention center, Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang personally stamped all four of Haiyue Group's coastal warehouse permits and handed them straight to Qin Yuhan and her father Qin Zhengming before noon.

At twelve thirty, Qin Zhengming headed off to the provincial compound for an afternoon tea meeting with Old Leader Sha Ruijin, leaving the young couple outside the sunny front porch of Shanshui Villa with six free hours before their six o'clock high-speed train back to Shanghai.

Qin Yuhan stood on the stone steps in the warm autumn sunshine, holding her black leather folder under one arm while her right thumb already hovered over the email icon on her phone.

"Since the afternoon sessions are canceled," Qin Yuhan said briskly, switching back into her Lujiazui executive voice, "we can go back up to Suite 1408 for three hours. If I review the Q4 Jin Mao Tower freight budgets now and call Vice President Liu before two, we can get a full day's work done before the train."

Beside her, Chen Xuan reached over, gently plucked the slim smartphone right out of her fingers, pressed the side button for three seconds, and slid the power-off bar across the screen.

Qin Yuhan blinked in surprise. "Hey! What are you doing, Mr. Chen?"

"Confiscating contraband," Chen Xuan said solemnly, dropping her powered-off phone into his hoodie pocket beside his green thermos. "Look at the sky, President Qin. Not a single cloud over Jingzhou Lake, warm breeze, and you just won an 80 billion yuan harbor battle before breakfast. Working in a hotel room on an afternoon like this is against marriage law."

"Marriage law?" Qin Yuhan laughed, reaching playfully for his hoodie pocket. "Give me back my phone! Which article of marriage law says the logistics clerk gets to turn off the company president's phone?"

"Article 14, Husband Edition," Chen Xuan grinned, catching both of her soft hands in one of his own and leading her down the stone path toward the willow-lined shore of Jingzhou Lake. "It says whenever the wife does a great job on a business trip, the husband takes her out to play hooky for the afternoon."

Qin Yuhan looked down at his warm hand holding hers, felt the gentle autumn breeze across her cheeks, and realized she had not taken an unplanned afternoon off in nearly two years.

Her fingers relaxed inside his grip.

"Fine," Qin Yuhan said, lifting her chin with a playful pretend sigh, though her eyes curved into bright crescents. "Four hours. If Haiyue Group loses ten thousand yuan because I missed an email today, I am docking it straight from your twelve-thousand-yuan monthly clerk salary."

"Deal," Chen Xuan chuckled. "Even if you leave me with two thousand yuan, I will still have enough to buy you street snacks."

Five minutes down the brick lakeside walk, an old vendor stood beside a red clay barrel stove floating with the rich, sweet scent of charcoal-roasted sweet potatoes.

Chen Xuan bought two fat, steaming sweet potatoes wrapped in brown paper for twelve yuan, paid another thirty yuan at the wooden pier, and untied a small cedar rowboat painted sky blue.

"Take off those high heels before you step in, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan smiled, steadying the wooden boat with one sneaker while holding out his free hand. "I do not want to fish a luxury president out of the reeds."

Qin Yuhan slipped off her beige heels, held them in one hand, and stepped barefoot onto the warm wooden planks while gripping Chen Xuan's steady forearm.

With four easy pulls on the wooden oars, Chen Xuan guided the little blue rowboat out into the middle of Jingzhou Lake.

The water sparkled like crushed gold under the afternoon sun. Along the shore, green weeping willows trailed their leaves in the ripples, and not a single city horn or office phone could be heard.

Chen Xuan rested the oars across the locks, pulled one steaming sweet potato from the paper bag, broke it neatly in half, and blew on the bright orange middle until it was cool enough to hold.

"Here," Chen Xuan said, passing the sweeter top half across the bench. "Better than any five-star hotel pastry."

Qin Yuhan took it with both hands. When she took a small bite, the honey-sweet, smoky flavor melted straight on her tongue.

"It is so sweet!" Qin Yuhan said, her eyes lighting up like a twenty-year-old Fudan University student sneaking out of an afternoon lecture. She took a second big bite without caring a bit about her executive image. "Where did you learn how to pick the best one from the barrel?"

"Years of thorough scientific research outside the company gate at four fifty-five," Chen Xuan replied proudly, leaning back against the bow and eating the other half. "You have to look for the ones with dark sugar bubbling out near the stem."

Qin Yuhan giggled, leaning back against the stern cushion with her bare feet tucked onto the sunny plank.

"Wait," Chen Xuan said softly, leaning forward with a small smile. "Do not move."

"What?" Qin Yuhan blinked.

Chen Xuan reached out and brushed his thumb gently across the corner of her left lip, wiping away a tiny speck of sweet potato ash.

Qin Yuhan's ears turned pink in the sunlight. Instead of pulling back, she leaned her cheek lightly into his palm for a second and looked at him with soft, bright eyes.

"Chen Xuan," she asked quietly as the wooden boat rocked gently on the clear water. "How come whenever I am with you, even a big scary mess like yesterday turns into a vacation?"

"Because you do all the hard thinking, and I am responsible for the rowing and snacks," Chen Xuan said easily, pulling out a clean tissue for her fingers. "Division of labor, President Qin."

"Nonsense," Qin Yuhan laughed, splashing two drops of cool lake water at his sneakers. "Row me over to that lotus pavilion by the stone bridge, Boatman Chen! If you row smoothly, I might let you keep your full twelve thousand yuan this month!"

"Yes, ma'am," Chen Xuan grinned, picking up the oars.

Out on the water, their laughter drifted across the quiet lake while fifty meters away along the shaded willow footpath, two unremarkable joggers in plain black tracksuits kept a quiet pace until it was time to catch the evening train home to Shanghai.
