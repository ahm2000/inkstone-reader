# Chapter 134: Playing Hooky on Jingzhou Lake

True to Chen Xuan's words, the paperwork moved faster than anyone expected, and the Handong shipping forum wrapped up a full day ahead of schedule.

With Ding Yizhen and Cai Chenggong sitting inside the provincial detention center, the remaining harbor delegates cleared their agreements without delay. By noon, Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang had personally stamped all four of Haiyue Group's coastal warehouse permits and placed the thick red-sealed folder into Qin Yuhan and Qin Zhengming's hands.

After a quick lunch, Qin Zhengming headed to the provincial compound for an afternoon tea meeting with Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin. That left the young couple outside the sunlit front porch of Shanshui Villa with several free hours before their 6:00 PM high-speed train back to Shanghai.

Qin Yuhan stood on the stone steps in the crisp late-autumn sunshine, holding the permit folder under one arm while her right thumb already hovered over the email icon on her smartphone.

"Since the afternoon turned into closing speeches, I should head back to the room and answer those supply-chain emails from Pudong," she said briskly, her executive habits kicking right back in. "If I clear the budget approvals now, Monday morning will be lighter."

Chen Xuan reached over casually, plucked the phone gently from her fingers, and slipped it straight into her cream leather handbag.

"Hey!" Qin Yuhan blinked at him, half startled and half amused. "What are you doing?"

"Declaring an official afternoon of playing hooky," Chen Xuan said smoothly, zipping her handbag shut and taking the permit folder into his own hand. "You just won four coastal permits and watched a corrupt Vice Mayor walk away in handcuffs before breakfast. Even a steel engine needs to cool down."

"I am not a steel engine," Qin Yuhan protested, though a smile tugged at her mouth. "What do you want to do instead?"

Chen Xuan pointed down the stone path lined with fiery red maples and golden ginkgo trees toward the shimmering blue expanse of Jingzhou Lake.

"Rent a wooden boat," he said simply. "Float around in the sun, eat some lotus seeds, and pretend zero emails are waiting in Shanghai."

Qin Yuhan hesitated for two whole seconds, looking at the sparkling water and then at her husband's relaxed grin. All the crisp tension in her shoulders melted away.

"Fine," she laughed softly, hooking her arm through his. "If my secretary calls asking where I went, I am blaming you."

"Gladly," Chen Xuan grinned. "Blame everything on the lazy clerk."

They strolled down the leaf-strewn path to a quiet wooden pier tucked away from the hotel crowd, where traditional wooden rowboats bobbed gently against mossy pilings.

Sitting on a low bamboo stool near the pier was a cheerful older boatman in a faded blue quilted vest and straw hat, whistling a tune as he coiled a hemp rope.

"Afternoon, young folks!" the old boatman called out with a crinkled, warm smile, looking every bit like a lifelong Jingzhou fisherman. "Want to take a boat out before the evening chill rolls in? The water is smooth as glass today."

"Yes please, Uncle," Chen Xuan replied warmly, catching the easy, natural friendliness of the Zero Guard elder without showing a flicker of anything unusual. "One wooden boat for two hours."

"Coming right up," the elder chuckled, steadying the narrow hull so Qin Yuhan could step onto the cushioned bench. Before pushing them off, he tossed a small brown paper bag onto Chen Xuan's lap. "Fresh roasted lotus seeds from my neighbor's stall. Eat them while they are crisp, on the house!"

"Thank you, Uncle!" Qin Yuhan called back as the boat glided smoothly away from the pier.

Chen Xuan sat across from her and dipped the wooden oars into the clear autumn water. His strokes were slow, steady, and completely unhurried. Ripples spread outward in soft silver arcs, carrying them past tall clusters of golden reeds and drifting maple leaves.

The bustle of the forum faded completely behind them. Out on the open lake, there was only the gentle lap of water against wood, the call of a distant waterbird, and the crisp scent of late-autumn pine.

Qin Yuhan leaned back against the wooden bench, letting her fingertips trail lightly through the cool water.

"It is so quiet out here," she murmured, watching sunlight dance across the lake. "Before I married you, every business trip felt like a marathon. Airports, meetings, late dinners, and straight back to the office. I never once stopped to look at a lake."

"Then it is a good thing you brought a professional slacker along," Chen Xuan teased, resting the oars across his knees so the boat drifted lazily. He unscrewed his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos, poured warm chrysanthemum tea into the lid, and handed it over with a handful of shelled lotus seeds.

Qin Yuhan took the warm lid with both hands, sipping slowly while her dark eyes studied him.

In the late-autumn sunlight, wearing a simple gray hoodie with his sleeves pushed up, Chen Xuan looked completely at peace.

"Next weekend, let us visit Grandpa Qin together," she smiled warmly. "He has been bragging to his old army buddies all week that his grandson-in-law makes the best braised pork ribs in Shanghai. Though he still insists you only beat him at chess because he lets you."

"I will buy two extra racks of ribs on Friday after clocking out at 4:55 PM," Chen Xuan promised readily. "And I will let him win one game out of three."

Qin Yuhan laughed, a clear sound skimming across the calm water. She popped a sweet lotus seed into her mouth and looked at him with quiet affection.

"You know," she said softly, "even if you stay a 12,000-yuan clerk on the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics for the next fifty years, I would not trade you for anyone in the world."

Chen Xuan paused, a warm smile spreading across his face. He leaned over, tucked a stray lock of hair behind her ear, and picked up the oars.

"That sounds like the best deal I have ever made, President Qin," he said softly. "Let us row around that red maple island before heading for the train."
