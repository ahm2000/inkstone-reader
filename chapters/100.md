# Chapter 100: Mingde Hardware Reopens

By eleven o'clock on Tuesday morning, Old Street in Yunxi County looked brighter than it had all spring.

Outside number 18, the iron shutter of Mingde Hardware was rolled all the way up. The bent metal frame that Hongrui Real Estate's men had kicked loose on Saturday night was straightened and oiled. Overhead, a new dark blue canvas awning shaded the front step, and the freshly painted wooden signboard reading Mingde Hardware gleamed in the sun.

Inside, shelves of steel wrenches, brass hinges, boxes of screws, and coils of wire stood lined up cleanly.

Chen Mingde stood in the doorway wearing a clean pale blue shirt, healthy color back in his cheeks. Beside him, Wu Lanying set a thermos of hot jasmine tea and a stack of paper cups on a small folding table.

"Easy on that crate, Jianguo," Chen Mingde called as Chen Jianguo slid a heavy box of locks behind the counter. "Chen Xuan told you this morning not to hurt your back."

"One box of locks will not break me," Chen Jianguo laughed, dusting his palms. "If I do not help lift it, Liu Meifang will say I only came to drink your tea."

Liu Meifang stepped out from the back room smiling. "You did only come to drink tea. Now step aside, Yu Changshun is crossing the street."

From his small sewing shop across the lane, old tailor Yu Changshun walked over with his tape measure draped around his neck, carrying a green potted lucky bamboo tied with a red ribbon.

"Look at this place!" Yu Changshun grinned as he stepped onto the clean pavement. "Fresh paint, new awning, clean glass. Chen Mingde, are you opening a hardware store or a bank?"

"Come on in, Old Yu," Chen Mingde chuckled, setting the lucky bamboo proudly on the corner of his counter. "You helped me hang that awning at seven this morning. Why did you buy a plant too?"

"A reopening needs green leaves and red ribbon," Yu Changshun said firmly, taking a cup of tea from Wu Lanying. "Otherwise last week's bad luck does not know it is supposed to leave. Honestly, Mingde, seeing your shutter open again makes the whole street feel right."

Before Chen Mingde could reply, the warm smell of fresh dough drifted down the lane.

Number 23 walked over from Gong's Noodles three doors down, wearing his clean white apron over a plain dark shirt. He carried a bamboo tray piled high with warm pork buns and a small jar of vinegar.

"Congratulations on reopening, Brother Mingde," Number 23 said with an easy smile, setting the tray beside Wu Lanying's teapot. "I steamed an extra batch just now. Everyone grab one while they are hot."

"Oh, you went to too much trouble!" Wu Lanying exclaimed warmly, handing a bun to Yu Changshun. "You are already busy from dawn till night at Gong's Noodles."

"Neighbors look after neighbors," Number 23 replied simply, taking a cup of tea and nodding politely to Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang. "Now that Old Street is quiet again, business at the noodle shop has already doubled since yesterday morning. Nobody is scared to sit outside and eat anymore."

"That is the truth," Yu Changshun agreed, nodding happily as he chewed his bun. "Two old ladies from the east alley came in this morning to get trousers hemmed. Last week they would not even walk past the corner because Hongrui's thugs kept parking their black vans across the lane."

"Speaking of quiet streets," Chen Jianguo said, looking toward the mouth of Old Street, "look who just walked in."

Everyone turned. Walking down Old Street on foot, with no flashy escort or official car blocking the lane, was Acting Director Wei Kun. He wore a plain dark navy jacket over a white shirt, carrying a coiled string of small red firecrackers in one hand and a bag of tangerines in the other.

Chen Mingde stepped forward quickly, surprised. "Director Wei! What brings you down to Old Street?"

Wei Kun smiled warmly and shook Chen Mingde's hand with both of his own. "Do not be formal, Old Chen. I am off duty for an hour, and I wanted to check on Old Street myself. Congratulations on reopening Mingde Hardware."

He handed the tangerines to Wu Lanying and held up the string of small red firecrackers. "I bought a short string at the corner store. Nothing too loud for the old folks upstairs, just enough to celebrate a clean new start."

"You are too kind, Director Wei," Wu Lanying said gratefully, pouring him a fresh cup of tea. "Please, have a bun."

Wei Kun took the warm cup and looked around at the smiling shopkeepers and neighbors who had gathered outside.

"I also wanted to bring everyone on Old Street good news in person," Wei Kun said clearly. "First, the county street office officially cancelled every single one of those fake renovation and eviction notices this morning. Nobody is tearing down Old Street, and nobody is forcing any shopkeeper out."

Warm applause and relieved laughter broke out among the neighbors standing nearby.

"Second," Wei Kun added steadily, "every member of Hongrui Real Estate's crew who threatened people on this lane is locked up and facing real charges. Their protection inside the county offices is gone for good. From today on, if anyone tries to bully an honest shopkeeper here, you call my desk directly."

Yu Changshun raised his paper cup in a cheerful toast. "Best words I have heard in five years! To clean streets and honest work!"

"To honest work!" Chen Mingde echoed, his eyes shining slightly as he looked at his wife, his brother, and his neighbors. "Two days ago, sitting in that cold room, I thought I was going to lose this little shop my father left me. Now look at us. Hot tea, warm buns, good neighbors, and nobody banging on the shutter."

Number 23 stood quietly beside the doorway, sipping his tea with a calm smile. He glanced once toward the bright midday sky in the direction of Shanghai, then handed a warm bun to a curious child peeking around Yu Changshun's coat.

"All right, Mingde," Chen Jianguo grinned, pointing at the red coil in Wei Kun's hand. "It is eleven-eighteen, a lucky minute. Light those firecrackers before the buns get cold!"

Chen Mingde laughed, took the string of small red firecrackers, and stretched them neatly across the clean stone pavement out front. Everyone stepped back under the shade of the new canvas awning, covering their ears.

Chen Mingde struck a match, touched the flame to the paper fuse, and jogged back beside Wu Lanying.

Pop! Pop! Crackle, crackle, crackle!

The bright burst echoed down Old Street. Tiny scraps of red paper fluttered into the sunlit air and drifted down onto the stone tiles like spring petals. A thin puff of white smoke faded in the breeze.

When the last firecracker popped, everyone clapped and laughed together as shopkeepers down the lane waved from their doorways.

Old Street was noisy again, not with shouting thugs, but with the warm, ordinary sound of people living in peace.
