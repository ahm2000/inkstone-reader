# Chapter 100: Leave on Time

On the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics, the air conditioner hummed with a steady, gentle warmth. The whole floor smelled of roasted jasmine tea and peeled winter oranges.

Chen Xuan watched the clock on his screen and felt happy.

He had only fifteen minutes left.

At 4:55 he would stand up. At 6:10 the express bus to Yunxi County would pull out of the south terminal. By half past seven he would climb six flights of stairs, push open the door of Room 601, and sit at his mother's table. Liu Meifang would tell him he was too thin and ladle tender ribs into his bowl of hot pork-rib soup.

That was his whole weekend plan, and it was still perfect.

His job title had not changed. He was still an operations supervisor on the twelfth floor, earning a quiet, regular paycheck. He had turned down two offers to transfer to headquarters and politely ignored every hint from General Manager Sun Haoran about a corner office.

His method had not changed either. He finished every bill of lading, port reconciliation, and customs schedule before lunch. Then he drank hot tea all afternoon while the winter sun warmed his sleeves.

"Chen Xuan! Stop staring at the clock!" Pan Xiulan leaned over the partition from the next desk, grinning as she held up a peeled tangerine. "Every Friday at half past four, your body sits here while your soul is already halfway to Yunxi County!"

Chen Xuan took a calm sip from his scratched green thermos. "My soul commutes back every Monday morning, Sister Pan. It just needs a weekend of soup first."

Pan Xiulan laughed and tossed two sweet segments of tangerine onto his desk napkin. "Fair enough. At least none of us have to work unpaid Friday overtime anymore. Ever since you fixed the port templates, the whole department sees daylight before six."

Across the aisle, Zhang Pengfei looked up from a neat stack of customs invoices.

He no longer wore an overly stiff suit or watched his coworkers with calculating eyes. His sleeves were rolled neatly to his forearms, his desk was spotless, and every column on his monitor balanced to the last cent.

When his gaze met Chen Xuan's, Zhang Pengfei paused his typing and gave a quiet, sincere nod of real respect.

"Container two cleared early, Chen Xuan," Zhang Pengfei said evenly. "I double-checked the port storage receipts myself. Zero duplicate charges this week."

"Good work, Zhang Pengfei," Chen Xuan replied with a mild smile. "Have a good rest this weekend."

"You too, Chen Xuan. Safe trip home."

Just then, the glass door at the end of the floor clicked open quietly.

Nobody jumped. Thirty backs stayed relaxed.

Hu Dewei walked out of his office carrying neither a red folder nor an angry frown. His hair looked less gray than it had six months ago, and his shoulders no longer carried the frantic weight of a man waiting to be shouted at.

He strolled down the aisle with a stainless-steel pitcher in his hand. Stopping beside Chen Xuan's desk, Hu Dewei quietly refilled the small porcelain cup next to Chen Xuan's keyboard with fresh hot water.

"Weekly port summary is already in the shared folder, Manager Hu," Chen Xuan said politely. "Uploaded at 11:15."

"I saw it before lunch," Hu Dewei chuckled, setting the pitcher down. "Not a single decimal out of place. General Manager Sun Haoran told me that if anyone asks you to stay past five on a Friday, I am personally required to dock my own bonus."

Pan Xiulan snorted softly behind her hand. "That is the smartest rule General Manager Sun Haoran has ever written."

Hu Dewei grinned and tapped Chen Xuan's partition. "Pack up, Chen Xuan. The winter wind is sharp tonight. Do not miss your bus."

"Thank you, Manager Hu. Have a good weekend."

The digital numbers on the monitor flicked cleanly from 4:54 to 4:55.

At 4:55 sharp, Chen Xuan shut down his computer, slipped his soft red-bean bun into his worn canvas bag, and picked up his scratched green thermos.

He stood up, tucked his chair under the desk, and waved once to Pan Xiulan, Zhang Pengfei, and Hu Dewei.

"See you Monday, everyone."

"Safe trip, Chen Xuan!"

He walked calmly into the elevator lobby. At the end of the marble hall, the brass arrow chimed softly, and the stainless-steel doors slid open to reveal an empty elevator already waiting on the twelfth floor.

Chen Xuan smiled faintly to himself.

"Lucky day."

He stepped inside, pressed the ground-floor button, and let the doors glide shut.

* * *

A thousand kilometers to the north, inside a quiet courtyard framed by tall red walls, evening snow drifted lightly over stone eaves.

Elder Li Weiming and Elder Song Baisheng stood beside a wide map table beneath a single desk lamp. Across the southern coastal corridor, every alert marker that had once flickered around Qingyan had long since faded into unbroken green.

"No disturbances," Elder Song Baisheng observed softly, setting his teacup beside the map. "The trade routes are clean, the local courts are steady, and the northern border reports calm skies."

Elder Li Weiming looked down at the small county dot near the river bend of Qingyan, his eyes peaceful. "Keep it that way. Let him have his quiet winters."

Hundreds of kilometers away, inside a bright military command hall lined with brass emblems, four-star General Qin Zhengguo closed the weekly readiness binder with a satisfied thud.

A young adjutant stepped forward. "Any further directives for the southern garrison, General Qin?"

General Qin Zhengguo glanced out at the winter stars and smiled faintly. "None. Tell my granddaughter Qin Yuhan to bring me a jar of pickled radish from Yunxi County next time she visits."

Down in Qingyan City, inside an unmarked courtyard beneath an ancient pine tree, Number One closed the active roster of the two hundred and fourteen operatives of the Black Pine Guard.

Down in the marble lobby of Hengxin Logistics, Number Fourteen stepped politely aside with his maintenance cart as Chen Xuan walked out through the revolving glass doors into the evening air.

Sixty kilometers away on Old Street in Yunxi County, Number Twenty-three ladled rich beef broth beneath glowing red lanterns, while Chen Mingde locked his gleaming hardware counter and Liu Meifang lifted the lid off a steaming clay pot in Room 601.

And at the edge of the Qingyan South Express Bus Terminal, exactly fifty meters from Platform Four, three motionless figures in featureless matte-black gear stood silently in the winter shadows.

Through their encrypted channel, a flat, neutral, genderless chilled voice spoke once.

"Target boarding the 6:10 express bus to Yunxi County. Perimeter clear. Weather clear. Road clear."

"Maintain silent watch."

Inside the warm coach of the 6:10 express bus, Chen Xuan took his favorite window seat in the fifth row. He set his canvas bag on his lap, unwrapped his red-bean bun, and leaned comfortably against the glass as the engine rumbled to life.

The bus rolled out of the station and turned south toward the hills.

Across the vast country, elders behind red walls, a four-star general in his command hall, two hundred and fourteen operatives of the Black Pine Guard, and the silent masks of the permanent Zero Guard team all kept the sky calm.

All of it, so that one ordinary office worker with a scratched green thermos could leave work on time and catch the 6:10 express bus home in time for his mother's pork-rib soup.
