# Chapter 100: Moving into Emerald Riverside Villa

By five-thirty that afternoon, two fresh red marriage booklets sat neatly inside Qin Yuhan's leather handbag.

After stopping by Chen Xuan's small rented flat so he could pack his things, Qin Yuhan drove her sleek black sedan through the green tree-lined gates of Emerald Riverside Villa in Pudong. Through the tall windshield, the glowing spheres of the Oriental Pearl Tower and the glass towers of Tomson Riviera right next door shone under the warm evening sky.

As Qin Yuhan parked in her private courtyard, she glanced over at Chen Xuan in the passenger seat with a slightly nervous smile. "This is Emerald Riverside Villa. I bought it two years ago when Haiyue Group expanded its Lujiazui headquarters. It is a bit big for one person, and it sits right next to Tomson Riviera, so the view of the river is quite nice. Do not feel pressured or uncomfortable living here."

Chen Xuan looked out the window at the tall penthouses of Tomson Riviera right next door, where Number 7 kept four empty top-floor units cleaned every week in case Chen Xuan ever wanted to take a nap near the river.

"It looks very nice and quiet," Chen Xuan said warmly, unbuckling his seatbelt and picking up his green thermos. "As long as the kitchen stove has good gas pressure and the bed mattress is not too soft, I can sleep anywhere."

Qin Yuhan burst out laughing as she popped the trunk. "Every time I think you are going to say something normal, you surprise me. Come on, let us carry your luggage inside."

Except "luggage" turned out to be a very generous word.

When Chen Xuan lifted his belongings out of the trunk, all he had were two worn dark blue canvas bags, a bamboo chessboard, and a thick glass jar of Liu Meifang's homemade pickled chilies wrapped in newspaper.

Inside the bright marble foyer, Qin Yuhan showed him up to the spacious master suite on the second floor, which had a huge walk-in closet and a separate study lounge.

"Wait," Chen Xuan paused by the closet doorway, holding his two canvas bags. "Did you not say we are living like roommates? Should I not take the guest room down the hall?"

"My mother Lin Shuyin already threatened on the phone to fly down from Beijing next month to check on us," Qin Yuhan said, her cheeks warming slightly as she opened a wide section of the custom walnut wardrobe for him. "If she walks upstairs and finds all your clothes in the guest room, she will know we signed a quick agreement right away. You can sleep on the wide sofa-bed in the inner study lounge next to the bedroom, and we will share the walk-in closet."

"Fair enough," Chen Xuan said cheerfully.

In less than three minutes, he unzipped both canvas bags. He hung up four plain white shirts, three pairs of dark trousers, and two light jackets on the polished wooden hangers right next to Qin Yuhan's rows of custom Milan designer suits. Then he set his newspaper-wrapped jar of pickled chilies prominently on the center marble island of the closet, right beside her display case of diamond watches.

Qin Yuhan stared at the fat glass jar of red chili oil sitting among her luxury watches and laughed until her shoulders shook. "Chen Xuan, why did you put the pickled chilies in my jewelry closet?"

"Wait, is that not the pantry counter?" Chen Xuan blinked innocently, picking up the jar again. "Everything in this house is white marble. Your closet looks wider than the supermarket on Nanjing Road."

"Take that downstairs to the kitchen right now!" Qin Yuhan scolded playfully, following him down the grand staircase with a light smile on her face that had not faded all afternoon.

In the sleek open kitchen on the first floor, Qin Yuhan opened the double-door refrigerator. Inside sat only six bottles of imported sparkling water, two boxes of low-fat yogurt, and three fresh tomatoes together with a carton of eggs that her housekeeper had left on Monday.

"I usually eat late at Haiyue Group or order a light salad," Qin Yuhan admitted a little sheepishly, loosening her blazer collar. "I was going to call a hotel restaurant near Plaza 66 to deliver dinner for us."

"Hotel delivery takes forty minutes and gets lukewarm in the box," Chen Xuan said, rolling up his white shirtsleeves to his elbows. "Go sit on the sofa and rest your feet. You have been answering company messages on your phone since we left the civil affairs bureau. Give me twelve minutes."

Qin Yuhan sat on the tall barstool by the kitchen counter, resting her chin in her hand as she watched her new husband move around the stove.

Chen Xuan did not look like a clumsy bachelor at all. His hands moved with an easy, rhythmic grace as he washed the three ripe tomatoes, scored their tops, blanched them in boiling water to peel the skins, and diced them with crisp, even taps of the chef's knife. Within minutes, three golden eggs sizzled fluffily in the pan before meeting the rich red tomato broth.

When he tossed two handfuls of fine wheat noodles into the boiling pot and topped the bowls with chopped green onions and a small spoonful of his mother's fragrant pickled chilies, a warm, mouthwatering aroma filled the entire villa.

"Here you go, President Qin," Chen Xuan said, setting a steaming ceramic bowl and a pair of chopsticks in front of her. "One bowl of home-cooked tomato-and-egg noodles with Yunxi County chili oil."

Qin Yuhan picked up her chopsticks and took one bite. Her eyes lit up at once. The broth was rich, savory, and slightly tangy, the eggs were tender and soaked in flavor, and the tiny hint of homemade pickled chili warmed her stomach all the way down.

Without saying a word, the refined president of Haiyue Group finished the entire bowl, drank the warm soup, and held her empty bowl out toward the pot with slightly pink cheeks.

"Is there a little more left in the pot?" Qin Yuhan asked softly.

Chen Xuan chuckled, took her bowl, and ladled out a full second serving with two big pieces of golden egg on top.

"Eat as much as you want," Chen Xuan said warmly, leaning against the counter with his green thermos in hand. "Starting tomorrow, as long as I clock out at four fifty-five, dinner is on me."
