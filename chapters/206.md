# Chapter 206: The New Coastal Route

By Tuesday morning, the ripple effects of the Seven-Harbor Consortium's sudden collapse had turned the maritime shipping circles of eastern China completely upside down.

Across the major coastal ports stretching from Tianjin and Qingdao down to Ningbo and Shanghai, hundreds of commercial cargo berths that had been locked under exclusive cartel contracts for more than a decade were abruptly opened for public bidding. Freight tariffs that had been artificially inflated by the Hong Kong syndicate dropped by over twenty percent in a single trading session, releasing millions of tons of trapped domestic freight.

At the top of the 58-story Haiyue Financial Center in Pudong, the executive boardroom was alive with intense commercial discussion.

Qin Yuhan sat at the head of the conference table, wearing a crisp charcoal-gray pantsuit, her dark hair pinned up in an elegant chignon. Before her lay a thick stack of newly authorized maritime transport permits issued directly by the Ministry of Transport and the State Council in Beijing.

"President Qin," her chief commercial director said, using a laser pointer to trace the projected coastal shipping routes across the high-definition wall display. "The federal maritime bureau has formally awarded Haiyue Group the exclusive operating licenses for six major northern container lanes. With Seven-Harbor's accounts frozen and their fleet immobilized, we now command over forty percent of the domestic coastal feeder routes between Bohai Bay and the Yangtze River Delta."

Qin Yuhan examined the audit figures with a calm, discerning gaze. A quiet and confident smile touched her lips as she reviewed the projected balance sheets.

"This is an extraordinary expansion opportunity for Haiyue," Qin Yuhan said, her clear voice commanding the complete attention of every director in the room. "However, our domestic feeder network cannot handle this sudden surge in volume alone. We need reliable secondary logistics partners who can manage regional warehousing, cold-chain transfers, and dockside distribution without cutting corners or missing dispatch windows."

"The major logistics firms across Shanghai have already submitted dozens of emergency proposals," her commercial director noted, adjusting his glasses. "Three state-affiliated firms are requesting exclusive allocation."

"We will not give exclusive allocation to any single entity," Qin Yuhan stated firmly. "Monopolies breed laziness and poor service. We will divide the feeder lanes among dependable regional operators with proven dispatch records."

Her executive assistant, Lin Wei, stepped quietly forward from behind her chair and leaned down to whisper, "President Qin, General Manager Sun Haoran from Hengxin Logistics has been waiting in the visitor lounge since eight-thirty this morning. He brought their entire regional operations portfolio, vehicle inspection records, and past audit reports. He asked for just ten minutes of your time to pitch for the northern feeder distribution."

Qin Yuhan's eyes softened with a private, warm amusement that none of the executives at the table could decipher.

Hengxin Logistics was the very company where Chen Xuan worked as a quiet operations clerk for 12,000 yuan a month. Just two days ago at Sunday dinner, her mother had mentioned how Sun Haoran was trying to promote Chen Xuan to deputy general manager with a 50,000-yuan salary, only for Chen Xuan to turn it down because he preferred his afternoon tea and leaving the office at four-fifty-five.

"Let General Manager Sun wait in the VIP conference room on the fifty-fifth floor," Qin Yuhan said calmly, closing her leather folio with a soft click. "Have our operations analysts review their past dispatch logs first. If their delivery reliability meets our standards, we can allocate twenty percent of the feeder volume to Hengxin."

Meanwhile, three floors below, General Manager Sun Haoran was pacing nervously back and forth across the thick wool carpeting of the VIP waiting room.

Sun Haoran wore his most expensive navy wool suit, but his silk tie was already pulled half an inch loose from anxious perspiration. Beside him sat Department Manager Hu Dewei, clutching two heavy leather briefcases stuffed with company brochures, driver certifications, and past financial audits.

"Old Hu," Sun Haoran whispered tensely, wiping his brow with a monogrammed handkerchief. "Did you double-check the cold-chain dispatch reliability figures? Haiyue Group is now the undisputed queen of eastern maritime freight. If we can secure even a minor share of their new coastal routes, Hengxin's yearly revenue will easily break 300 million yuan!"

Hu Dewei nodded vigorously, though his hands were trembling slightly against his briefcase handle. "General Manager Sun, our dispatch numbers are completely verified. But President Qin Yuhan is famously strict in the shipping sector. Last year, when Rongda Logistics had a two-hour delivery delay in Qingdao, she terminated their contract on the spot without blinking!"

"That is precisely why we must display absolute professionalism," Sun Haoran said, straightening his cuffs and checking his gold wristwatch for the tenth time. "No mistakes, no sloppy numbers, and no excuses. When we step into that boardroom, we must show her that Hengxin never leaves cargo unverified!"

The heavy frosted glass doors of the waiting room slid open, and Assistant Secretary Lin Wei walked in with a polite, measured smile.

"General Manager Sun, Manager Hu," Lin Wei said smoothly. "President Qin has concluded her executive briefing. However, her schedule is very tight this morning. She can grant you a fifteen-minute preliminary review at eleven-forty-five."

Sun Haoran exhaled a massive sigh of relief, bowing slightly from the waist. "Thank you, Secretary Lin! Fifteen minutes is more than enough. We will be completely ready."

As Secretary Lin walked back toward the executive elevators, Sun Haoran collapsed into an armchair, patting his chest with both hands. "Did you hear that, Old Hu? Eleven-forty-five! This is the most critical fifteen minutes in the history of Hengxin Logistics!"

Hu Dewei swallowed hard, wiping his palms against his trousers. "General Manager Sun, what should our opening angle be?"

"Focus on operational discipline," Sun Haoran instructed firmly. "Mention our dedicated tracking teams on the twelfth floor. Show her that Hengxin treats every container manifest with personal care!"

Neither man had the faintest inkling that the most important person on Hengxin's twelfth floor was currently walking out of a nearby subway exit, carrying a warm stainless steel thermal lunchbox in one hand and an old green metal thermos in the other.
