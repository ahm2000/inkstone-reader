# Chapter 206: The New Coastal Route

The next morning, the 38th floor executive boardroom of Haiyue Tower was already in full swing. Beyond the tall glass curtain walls stretched the wide curve of the Huangpu River, while inside, a hushed anticipation hung over the pale oak conference table.

Qin Yuhan sat at the head of the table in a tailored charcoal wool suit, a thin silk scarf resting lightly at her collar. Her expression was calm and focused as she turned the final page of the Port Authority allocation notice. Across from her, Maritime Operations Director Zhou and the head of legal counsel waited with their folders open.

"The northern terminals confirmed berth clearance at 8:30 AM," Director Zhou reported, tapping his tablet with quiet satisfaction. "With the Seven-Harbor Consortium assets frozen and their holding licenses revoked by the ministry, three major northern container lanes have officially transferred to Haiyue Group. That connects our Yangshan berths directly to Bohai Bay and two winter ice-free ports further north."

"Good," Qin Yuhan said, setting down her fountain pen. "December shipping demand peaks in two weeks. We cannot afford a single stalled vessel or missed customs window. How are our Shanghai feeder and bonded warehouse schedules looking?"

"That is our one tight spot, President Qin," Director Zhou admitted. "Our own fleet is already running at full winter capacity. We need a reliable local feeder logistics partner in Shanghai to handle container transfers, customs manifests, and short-haul scheduling between Yangshan and the inland depots. Operations shortlisted four local firms this morning."

Qin Yuhan's assistant, Lin Wei, stepped forward and placed a slim blue summary folder beside her teacup. Qin Yuhan opened it, scanning the performance tables with a practiced eye. Three of the companies were well-known mid-sized carriers with solid fleets but high error rates during peak winter storms. The fourth name near the bottom of the page made her eyebrows lift faintly.

Hengxin Logistics.

Her lips curved into a small, private smile that vanished before anyone at the table could catch it. Hengxin was the modest logistics firm where her husband, Chen Xuan, worked at a 12,000 yuan dispatch desk on the 12th floor. She had never once pulled strings for his company, and she had no intention of starting now unless the numbers earned it.

"Look at Hengxin's third-quarter and November dispatch logs," Qin Yuhan said evenly, pointing to the fourth column. "Their container turnaround accuracy is ninety-nine point four percent. That beats the other three candidates by nearly three points."

Director Zhou blinked and leaned over his own printout. "You are right, President Qin. Honestly, their regional management is nothing special, and their fleet is smaller than the others. Yet somehow their 12th-floor dispatch office cleared every bonded manifest ahead of schedule for four straight months. Not a single misrouted winter crate."

Qin Yuhan suppressed another warm smile. She knew very well how meticulous Chen Xuan was whenever he organized anything, even when he pretended to be the laziest man in Shanghai.

"Have the Hengxin executives arrived yet?" she asked Lin Wei.

"Yes, President Qin," Lin Wei replied. "General Manager Sun Haoran and their dispatch department manager, Hu Dewei, checked in at 11:15 AM. They are waiting in the 35th-floor visitor lounge."

Qin Yuhan glanced at the slim silver clock on the wall. It was approaching 11:45 AM. "Leave them comfortable with hot tea for now. I want to review the feeder contract terms over lunch before I see them. Keep my schedule clear until 12:45 PM."

"Understood, President Qin."

Three floors below, inside the quiet, sunlit 35th-floor visitor lounge, the atmosphere was far less relaxed.

Hu Dewei sat on the edge of a leather armchair, dabbing his forehead with a folded handkerchief despite the cool December air. He clutched a thick leather portfolio against his knees like a life preserver and stared nervously at the frosted glass doors.

"General Manager Sun, do you think Haiyue really put us on the final list?" Hu Dewei whispered, his voice tight. "An annual coastal feeder contract from Haiyue Group is worth at least 50 million, maybe 80 million yuan. If we land this, Hengxin's revenue triples overnight."

Sun Haoran sat calmly beside him, nursing a cup of jasmine tea. Unlike his sweating subordinate, Sun Haoran's eyes were sharp and reflective.

"If President Qin had no interest in us, we would not be sitting on the 35th floor of Haiyue Tower," Sun Haoran said mildly. "We would have been turned away at the lobby."

Hu Dewei let out a shaky laugh and straightened his silk tie. "True, true! And it is all thanks to how strictly I run the 12th floor. Last month alone, I pushed through 300 overdue route archives so our audit score would look spotless. Of course, some of my clerks still test my patience."

Sun Haoran paused with his teacup halfway to his lips. "Who?"

"That Chen Xuan," Hu Dewei muttered, frowning with familiar irritation. "The fellow sits by the window with his dented green metal thermos, never volunteers for overtime, and packs up at 4:55 PM sharp every afternoon. I dumped 300 historical files on his desk last month just to wake him up. He finished them, I will give him that, though he still acts like the whole office moves at his personal pace."

Sun Haoran slowly set his teacup down on the glass table. The soft clink made Hu Dewei jump.

Ever since the autumn business forum at Shanshui Villa, when Sun Haoran had glimpsed Chen Xuan near the top VIP section and seen seasoned provincial figures treat the quiet young man with unmistakable deference, a thrilling suspicion had taken root in Sun Haoran's mind. Hu Dewei had been stuck in the outer banquet hall that night and had seen nothing.

"Hu Dewei," Sun Haoran said in a low, warning tone, "listen to me carefully. When we walk into President Qin's office later, keep your mouth shut about your staff. And from today on, stop picking on Chen Xuan."

Hu Dewei stared at him, completely baffled. "General Manager Sun, why? He is just a regular clerk making 12,000 yuan a month."

Sun Haoran looked out the 35th-floor window at the bright winter sky over Pudong and shook his head slowly. "Because some people keep their heads down by choice, Old Hu. Do not be the fool who kicks a mountain thinking it is a pebble."

Hu Dewei frowned, convinced his general manager had caught pre-meeting nerves, and checked his watch at 11:50 AM. He had no idea what important guest could be holding up President Qin Yuhan on the 38th floor during lunch.
