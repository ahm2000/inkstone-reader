# Chapter 79: Wang Lei's Delivery Tricycle

At noon, the familiar rattle of a loose chain and squeaking back brake echoed down the lane of Fuxing New Village.

Chen Xuan had just stepped outside Building 2 to buy a bottle of dark vinegar for Wu Lanying's braised fish when a beat-up blue delivery tricycle turned past the bike shed. Pedaling hard up the slight slope was Wang Lei, his thin cotton shirt damp at the collar from his morning route.

Wang Lei spotted Chen Xuan and squeezed both brakes at once. The tricycle stopped with a loud metal screech.

"Chen Xuan! Good thing I caught you downstairs!" Wang Lei called out. He swung one leg over the bar and lifted two heavy red plastic bags full of crisp apples and yellow pears from the back bed. "I just dropped off bottled water on Old Street and heard from the noodle shop auntie that Second Uncle Chen Mingde came home cleared this morning! Did provincial officers really haul Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu away?"

"They did, Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, real warmth softening his eyes as he took one of the bags. "Second Uncle is upstairs eating hot soup. Come up for lunch. My second aunt is making braised fish."

"No, no, I cannot break into your family's meal!" Wang Lei waved both hands quickly and stepped back as sweat ran down his face. "My shirt smells like cardboard and grease! Just take these apples and pears upstairs for Second Uncle and Second Aunt. Tell them Wang Lei is glad the sky finally fell on Hongrui."

Chen Xuan looked at the heavy bags of fruit, then at his old classmate's sunburned face. Fresh apples and pears like these cost half a morning's pay, yet Wang Lei pressed them into his hands without a second thought.

"Stay five minutes," Chen Xuan said quietly, setting the bags on the low brick ledge by the stairs. "Catch your breath. You have been pedaling since six."

Wang Lei paused, then grinned sheepishly and wiped his forehead with his sleeve. "Alright, five minutes. My next hardware crate does not leave the shipping yard until one."

He leaned against the tricycle frame, still shaking his head in half-disbelief. "You should have seen Old Street ten minutes ago, Chen Xuan. Half the shopkeepers were setting off firecrackers behind their shutters! Nobody could believe Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming and Chairman Deng Yongchang went down in one morning. For two years, Yan Tiehu's thugs parked outside the shipping yard collecting gate fees from every tricycle driver. Today the gate was wide open and not a single thug was in sight."

"They were squeezing your shipping yard too?" Chen Xuan asked. His tone stayed mild, though his eyes sharpened for a moment.

"Three hundred yuan a month per tricycle, cash only, no receipt," Wang Lei muttered, kicking a small stone under his sneaker. "Complain, and your tires went flat overnight. I was scared to death last night when I heard Cheng Biao dragged Second Uncle away. I kept checking my phone all morning wondering if I should ride over to Old Street Station."

Chen Xuan looked steadily at Wang Lei. "Second Uncle told me what you did last week."

Wang Lei blinked, truly confused. "What I did? I did not do anything."

"Last Thursday afternoon," Chen Xuan said evenly. "When Yan Tiehu's men blocked Mingde Hardware and chased every customer off the street. You parked this tricycle right in front of their bumper, walked past six men holding iron pipes, and stood at Second Uncle's counter to buy one brass hinge."

A sudden flush crept up Wang Lei's neck. He rubbed the back of his head awkwardly and let out an embarrassed laugh. "Come on, Chen Xuan, do not make a big deal out of something that small! My cupboard door really was sagging! Besides, Second Uncle gave me free bolts whenever my bike broke in high school. What was Yan Tiehu going to do, beat me up in broad daylight over a three-yuan hinge?"

"He would have," Chen Xuan replied simply. "And you knew it when you walked through the door."

Wang Lei's grin faded a little. He shrugged and looked down at his scuffed sneakers. "We grew up on the same streets. You do not walk past an old neighbor getting cornered by dogs and pretend you are blind. That is all it was. Really, it was nothing."

Chen Xuan said nothing for two quiet seconds, looking at Wang Lei's worn, grease-stained sleeves.

He remembered far more than one brass hinge. He remembered cold winters at Yunxi High School when Chen Xuan's pockets were empty after buying his mother's medicine, and Wang Lei casually pushed half an eight-yuan plate of pork and rice across the lunch table, claiming he was not hungry. He remembered every rainstorm when Wang Lei waited outside the school gate with a broken umbrella and never once kept score.

In a world where ministers, generals, and massive business empires bent their knees for power, Wang Lei walked past six thugs for a three-yuan hinge and brought two bags of fruit on a beat-up tricycle without expecting a single word of thanks.

"Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, his voice dropping into a calm, serious tone that made Wang Lei look up. "How much are you clearing a month running this tricycle for the yard?"

Wang Lei scratched his chin, caught off guard. "On a good month, maybe four thousand five hundred, after battery charging and repairs. Why? Moving back to Yunxi County for work?"

"Not me," Chen Xuan replied, a faint smile touching his mouth. "With Hongrui Real Estate's demolition gang gone, Yunxi County's shipping, building-supply routes, and provincial rebuilding jobs are going to be rebuilt clean. The old yard bullies are finished."

Wang Lei's eyes widened. "Sure, maybe the gate fees stop, which saves me three hundred yuan a month. Big jobs go to people with trucks and connections, not a guy on a three-wheel cart."

"Keep your phone charged and turned on later this week," Chen Xuan told him quietly, meeting his eyes with total certainty. "A clean regional supply and shipping group will be looking for an honest local manager in Yunxi County. Someone who knows every alley, every shipping yard, and never cheats a customer. When they call, do not hang up thinking it is a joke."

Wang Lei stared at Chen Xuan for three full seconds, squinting to see if his old classmate was teasing him. There was no laughter in Chen Xuan's face, only that strange, calm weight Wang Lei had noticed ever since Chen Xuan came back from the city.

"A local manager?" Wang Lei chuckled nervously and shook his head. "Chen Xuan, I barely passed high school math! You talk like you know the provincial transport office!"

"You do not need hard math to run an honest fleet and keep clean books," Chen Xuan said mildly, patting the tricycle rail once. "Just keep your phone on, Wang Lei. Eat a proper lunch before your one o'clock run."

Wang Lei hesitated, felt a sudden warmth at how seriously Chen Xuan meant it, and finally nodded with an easy grin. "Alright, my phone stays at full battery! Tell Second Uncle I will come drink tea when Mingde Hardware reopens!"

He swung onto the seat, twisted the handle, and waved over his shoulder as the beat-up blue tricycle rattled back toward the gate.

Chen Xuan picked up the two bags of apples and pears and stood watching until the squeak of the brake faded into the noon traffic.
