# Chapter 79: Wang Lei's Delivery Tricycle

At noon, the familiar rattle of a loose chain and squeaking rear brake echoed down the lane of Fuxing New Village.

Chen Xuan had just stepped outside Building 2 to buy a bottle of dark vinegar for Wu Lanying's braised fish when a battered blue delivery tricycle turned past the bicycle shed. Pedaling hard against the slight slope was Wang Lei, his thin cotton shirt damp at the collar from his morning route.

Wang Lei spotted Chen Xuan and squeezed both brakes at once. The tricycle stopped with a sharp metallic screech.

"Chen Xuan! Good thing I caught you downstairs!" Wang Lei called out, swinging one leg over the crossbar and hauling two heavy red plastic bags bulging with crisp apples and yellow pears from the cargo bed. "I just dropped bottled water on Old Street and heard from the noodle shop auntie that Second Uncle Chen Mingde came home cleared this morning! Did provincial officers really haul Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu away?"

"They did, Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, genuine warmth softening his eyes as he took one of the bags. "Second Uncle is upstairs eating hot soup. Come up for lunch. My second aunt is making braised fish."

"No, no, I cannot interrupt your family's meal!" Wang Lei waved both hands quickly, backing half a step as sweat ran down his temple. "My shirt smells like cardboard and axle grease! Just take these apples and pears upstairs for Second Uncle and Second Aunt. Tell them Wang Lei is glad the sky finally fell on Hongrui."

Chen Xuan looked at the heavy bags of fruit, then at his old classmate's sunburned face. Fresh apples and pears like these cost half a morning's commissions, yet Wang Lei pressed them into his hands without hesitation.

"Stay five minutes," Chen Xuan said quietly, setting the bags on the low brick ledge by the stairwell. "Catch your breath. You have been pedaling since six."

Wang Lei hesitated, then grinned sheepishly and wiped his forehead with his sleeve. "Alright, five minutes. My next hardware crate does not leave the depot until one."

He leaned against the tricycle frame, still shaking his head in half-disbelief. "You should have seen Old Street ten minutes ago, Chen Xuan. Half the shopkeepers were setting off firecrackers behind their shutters! Nobody could believe Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming and Chairman Deng Yongchang went down in one morning. For two years, Yan Tiehu's thugs parked outside the freight depot collecting gate fees from every tricycle driver. Today the gate was wide open and not a single shaved head was in sight."

"They were squeezing your freight depot too?" Chen Xuan asked, his tone mild though his gaze sharpened briefly.

"Three hundred yuan a month per tricycle, cash only, no receipt," Wang Lei muttered, kicking a pebble under his sneaker. "Complain, and your tires went flat overnight. I was scared to death last night when I heard Cheng Biao dragged Second Uncle away. I kept checking my phone all morning wondering if I should ride over to Old Street Station."

Chen Xuan looked at Wang Lei steadily. "Second Uncle told me what you did last week."

Wang Lei blinked, genuinely confused. "What I did? I did not do anything."

"Last Thursday afternoon," Chen Xuan said evenly. "When Yan Tiehu's men blocked Mingde Hardware and chased every customer off the block. You parked this tricycle right in front of their bumper, walked past six men with iron pipes, and stood at Second Uncle's counter to buy one brass hinge."

A sudden flush crept up Wang Lei's neck. He rubbed the back of his head awkwardly and let out an embarrassed laugh. "Come on, Chen Xuan, do not make a speech out of something that small! My pantry cabinet door really was sagging! Besides, Second Uncle gave me free bolts whenever my bicycle broke in high school. What was Yan Tiehu going to do, beat me up in broad daylight over a three-yuan hinge?"

"He would have," Chen Xuan replied simply. "And you knew it when you walked through the door."

Wang Lei's grin faded a fraction. He shrugged, looking down at his scuffed sneakers. "We grew up on the same streets. You do not walk past an old neighbor getting cornered by dogs and pretend you are blind. That is all it was. Really, it was nothing."

Chen Xuan said nothing for two quiet seconds, looking at Wang Lei's worn, grease-stained sleeves.

He remembered far more than one brass hinge. He remembered eighteen-year-old winters at Yunxi High School when Chen Xuan's pockets were empty after buying his mother's medicine, and Wang Lei casually shoved half an eight-yuan plate of stir-fried pork and rice across the canteen table, claiming he had lost his appetite. He remembered every rainstorm when Wang Lei waited outside the school gate with a broken umbrella and never once kept score.

In a world where ministers, generals, and trillion-yuan syndicates bent their knees for power, Wang Lei walked past six thugs for a three-yuan hinge and brought two bags of fruit on a battered tricycle without expecting thanks.

"Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, his voice dropping into a calm, serious register that made Wang Lei look up. "How much are you clearing a month running this tricycle for the depot?"

Wang Lei scratched his chin, caught off guard. "On a good month, maybe four thousand five hundred, after battery charging and repairs. Why? Switching jobs back to Yunxi County?"

"Not me," Chen Xuan replied, a faint smile touching his mouth. "With Hongrui Real Estate's demolition ring dismantled, Yunxi County's logistics, building-supply routes, and provincial reconstruction contracts are going to be rebuilt clean. The old depot monopolies are finished."

Wang Lei's eyes widened. "Sure, maybe the gate fees stop, which saves me three hundred yuan a month. Big contracts go to people with trucks and connections, not a guy on a three-wheel cart."

"Keep your phone charged and on later this week," Chen Xuan told him quietly, meeting his eyes with total certainty. "A clean regional supply and logistics group will be looking for an honest local operations lead in Yunxi County. Someone who knows every alley, every depot, and never cheats a customer. When they call, do not hang up thinking it is a prank."

Wang Lei stared at Chen Xuan for three full seconds, squinting to see if his classmate was joking. There was zero laughter in Chen Xuan's face, only that strange, calm weight Wang Lei had noticed since Chen Xuan returned from the city.

"An operations lead?" Wang Lei chuckled nervously, shaking his head. "Chen Xuan, I barely passed high school math! You talk like you know the provincial transport bureau!"

"You do not need advanced math to run an honest fleet and keep clean books," Chen Xuan said mildly, patting the tricycle rail once. "Just keep your phone on, Wang Lei. Eat properly before your one o'clock run."

Wang Lei hesitated, felt a sudden warmth at how seriously Chen Xuan meant it, and finally nodded with an easy grin. "Alright, my phone stays at full battery! Tell Second Uncle I will come drink tea when Mingde Hardware reopens!"

He swung onto the seat, twisted the throttle, and waved over his shoulder as the battered blue tricycle rattled back toward the gate.

Chen Xuan lifted the two bags of apples and pears and stood watching until the squeak of the brake faded into noon traffic.
