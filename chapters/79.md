# Chapter 79: Wang Lei's Delivery Tricycle

At noon, the rattle of a loose chain and squeaking brakes echoed down the lane of Fuxing New Village.

Chen Xuan had just stepped outside Building 2 to buy dark vinegar for Wu Lanying's braised fish when a beat-up blue delivery tricycle turned past the bike shed. Pedaling hard up the slope was Wang Lei, his thin shirt damp at the collar from his morning route.

Wang Lei spotted Chen Xuan and squeezed both brakes, stopping with a loud metal screech.

"Chen Xuan! Good thing I caught you!" Wang Lei called out, swinging off the seat and lifting two heavy red bags of crisp apples and yellow pears from the back bed. "I just dropped off water on Old Street and heard from the noodle shop auntie that Second Uncle Chen Mingde came home cleared this morning! Did provincial officers really haul Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu away?"

"They did, Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, real warmth in his eyes as he took one bag. "Second Uncle is upstairs eating hot soup. Come up for lunch. My second aunt is making braised fish."

"No, no, I cannot break into your family's meal!" Wang Lei waved both hands and stepped back, sweat running down his face. "My shirt smells like cardboard and grease! Just take these apples and pears upstairs for Second Uncle and Second Aunt. Tell them Wang Lei is glad the sky finally fell on Hongrui."

Chen Xuan looked at the heavy fruit bags, then at his old classmate's sunburned face. Fresh apples and pears like these cost half a morning's pay, yet Wang Lei pressed them over without a second thought.

"Stay five minutes," Chen Xuan said quietly, setting the bags on the brick ledge. "Catch your breath. You have been pedaling since six."

Wang Lei grinned sheepishly and wiped his brow with his sleeve. "Alright, five minutes. My next hardware crate does not leave the shipping yard until one."

He leaned against the tricycle, still shaking his head in disbelief. "You should have seen Old Street ten minutes ago, Chen Xuan. Half the shopkeepers were setting off firecrackers behind their shutters! Nobody believed County Magistrate Sun Weiming and Chairman Deng Yongchang went down in one morning. For two years, Yan Tiehu's thugs parked outside the shipping yard taking gate fees from every tricycle driver. Today the gate was wide open and not one thug was in sight."

"They were squeezing your shipping yard too?" Chen Xuan asked mildly, his eyes sharpening for a moment.

"Three hundred yuan a month per tricycle, cash only, no receipt," Wang Lei muttered, kicking a stone. "Complain, and your tires went flat overnight. I was scared to death last night when I heard Cheng Biao dragged Second Uncle away. I kept checking my phone all morning wondering if I should ride to Old Street Station."

Chen Xuan looked steadily at Wang Lei. "Second Uncle told me what you did last week."

Wang Lei blinked, confused. "What I did? I did not do anything."

"Last Thursday afternoon," Chen Xuan said evenly. "When Yan Tiehu's men blocked Mingde Hardware and chased every customer away. You parked this tricycle right in front of their bumper, walked past six men holding iron pipes, and stood at Second Uncle's counter to buy one brass hinge."

A flush crept up Wang Lei's neck. He rubbed the back of his head with an embarrassed laugh. "Come on, Chen Xuan, do not make a big deal out of something that small! My cupboard door really was sagging! Besides, Second Uncle gave me free bolts whenever my bike broke in high school. What was Yan Tiehu going to do, beat me up in broad daylight over a three-yuan hinge?"

"He would have," Chen Xuan replied simply. "And you knew it when you walked through the door."

Wang Lei's grin faded a little. He shrugged, looking down at his scuffed sneakers. "We grew up on the same streets. You do not walk past an old neighbor cornered by dogs and pretend you are blind. That is all it was. Really, it was nothing."

Chen Xuan stayed silent for two seconds, looking at Wang Lei's worn, grease-stained sleeves.

He remembered far more than one brass hinge. He remembered cold winters at Yunxi High School when Chen Xuan's pockets were empty after buying his mother's medicine, and Wang Lei casually pushed half an eight-yuan plate of pork and rice across the lunch table, claiming he was not hungry. He remembered rainstorms when Wang Lei waited outside the school gate with a broken umbrella and never once kept score.

In a world where ministers, generals, and business empires bent their knees for power, Wang Lei walked past six thugs for a three-yuan hinge and brought two bags of fruit on a beat-up tricycle without expecting a single word of thanks.

"Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, his voice dropping into a calm, serious tone. "How much are you clearing a month running this tricycle?"

Wang Lei scratched his chin, caught off guard. "On a good month, maybe four thousand five hundred after battery charging and repairs. Why? Moving back to Yunxi County for work?"

"Not me," Chen Xuan replied with a faint smile. "With Hongrui Real Estate's gang gone, Yunxi County's shipping, building-supply routes, and provincial rebuilding jobs are going to be rebuilt clean. The old yard bullies are finished."

Wang Lei's eyes widened. "Sure, maybe the gate fees stop, which saves me three hundred yuan a month. Big jobs go to people with trucks and connections, not a guy on a three-wheel cart."

"Keep your phone charged and turned on later this week," Chen Xuan told him quietly, meeting his eyes with certainty. "A clean regional supply and shipping group will look for an honest local manager in Yunxi County. Someone who knows every alley, every shipping yard, and never cheats a customer. When they call, do not hang up thinking it is a joke."

Wang Lei stared for three seconds, squinting to see if his old classmate was teasing him. There was no laughter in Chen Xuan's face, only that quiet, steady weight Wang Lei had noticed since Chen Xuan came back from the city.

"A local manager?" Wang Lei chuckled nervously. "Chen Xuan, I barely passed high school math! You talk like you know the provincial transport office!"

"You do not need hard math to run an honest fleet and keep clean books," Chen Xuan said mildly, patting the tricycle rail. "Just keep your phone on, Wang Lei. Eat a proper lunch before your one o'clock run."

Wang Lei hesitated, felt a sudden warmth at how seriously Chen Xuan meant it, and nodded with an easy grin. "Alright, my phone stays at full battery! Tell Second Uncle I will come drink tea when Mingde Hardware reopens!"

He swung onto the seat, twisted the handle, and waved as the blue tricycle rattled back toward the gate.

Chen Xuan picked up the two bags of apples and pears and stood watching until the squeak of the brake faded into noon traffic.
