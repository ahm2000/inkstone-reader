# Chapter 79: The Hero Who Only Wanted Soup

The afternoon high-speed train hummed smoothly across the northern plains, carrying autumn sunlight along the glass windows of Carriage Six.

Inside the second-class cabin, Chen Xuan sat quietly by the window in his washed gray jacket. Two paper-wrapped boxes of walnut and sesame pastries from the old bakery near Lanshui Station rested neatly on his knees, tied with red cotton twine. He had promised Grandfather Chen Fugen, Grandmother Zhou Guilan, and Tang Xiaoyu that he would bring them back before the weekend.

In the seat beside him, General Manager Sun Haoran could not sit still for ten seconds.

For the fifth time since leaving the provincial capital, Sun Haoran opened the embossed red commendation folder on his tray table. Inside lay an official provincial recognition certificate praising Hengxin Logistics for exemplary regional transport standards, clipped to a formal transfer notice granting the company two prime coastal freight routes freed from Wanfeng Freight.

"Chen Xuan, pinch me if I am dreaming," Sun Haoran murmured, his voice shaking with disbelief. "We rode up to Lanshui hoping not to embarrass ourselves. We are riding home with a clean provincial commendation and two coastal freight routes that could triple our revenue."

Chen Xuan turned from the passing sorghum fields and smiled mildly. "You kept our safety records spotless for five years, General Manager Sun Haoran. Once Wanfeng Freight collapsed under its own violations, the federation naturally looked for a clean carrier."

Sun Haoran closed the folder carefully, pressing both palms against the red cover as if afraid it might vanish.

"Clean records get you a seat in the back row," Sun Haoran said softly, looking at Chen Xuan with deep gratitude. "Keeping your head when half the hall was watching, speaking plainly without flinching, and never losing your temper made those provincial leaders remember Hengxin Logistics."

Chen Xuan opened a small paper bag from his coat pocket and offered Sun Haoran a warm sesame biscuit.

"The conference chairs were comfortable, and the tea was decent," Chen Xuan replied gently. "Honestly, General Manager Sun Haoran, by the second evening I mostly missed my mother's lotus-root and pork-rib soup."

Sun Haoran let out a hearty laugh that drew a polite glance from across the aisle. He took the biscuit, shook his head in wonder, and leaned back against the blue cushion with the easiest smile he had worn in a decade.

When the train glided into Qingyan Railway Station forty minutes later, the platform buzzed with ordinary commuters dragging suitcases toward the escalators. Chen Xuan walked beside Sun Haoran through the ticket gate with his cloth bag over one shoulder and his pastry boxes tucked under his arm.

Forty meters back near a shadowed concrete pillar on the north concourse, two figures in featureless matte-black gear blended seamlessly into the blind spot of the corridor. No passenger noticed them, and no camera caught their reflection. The permanent Zero Guard team shifted silently as Chen Xuan stepped into the autumn air, maintaining their unbroken fifty-meter perimeter without disturbing a single leaf.

* * *

By four in the afternoon, the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics smelled of roasted barley tea and fresh paper.

The moment the elevator doors parted and Chen Xuan stepped into the hallway, a burst of genuine applause rolled across the office.

Manager Hu Dewei hurried out from the glass partition so fast his glasses slid down his nose. Behind him came Pan Xiulan, Lin Dong, and half the dispatch clerks from the east row, all grinning warmly.

"Our provincial representative is back!" Manager Hu Dewei declared, clapping Chen Xuan on the arm. "General Manager Sun Haoran already called from the station. Two coastal freight routes and a provincial commendation! Chen Xuan, you turned our twelfth-floor office into a quiet legend across Qingyan City!"

"It was a team effort, Manager Hu Dewei," Chen Xuan said calmly, lifting his pastry boxes. "I brought walnut pastries from the old station bakery in Lanshui. Everyone should take a piece before they cool."

Pan Xiulan took one box with a fond laugh and pressed a freshly washed tangerine into Chen Xuan's hand.

"Coming back from a provincial banquet hall and still bringing snacks for the dispatch desk," Pan Xiulan teased warmly. "The whole logistics park heard about Wanfeng Freight folding this morning. Even the warehouse supervisors downstairs were asking about you."

Lin Dong sidled up with a grin, nudging Chen Xuan's elbow. "Never mind the main stage, Brother Chen Xuan. Tell the truth. Was the free hotel buffet in Lanshui actually worth the train ride, or did they skimp on the braised pork ribs?"

"The steamed buns were good," Chen Xuan answered solemnly, his eyes crinkling. "The pork ribs were nowhere near as good as my mother's."

Laughter rippled through the office. Manager Hu Dewei waved everyone back to work while insisting Chen Xuan take the final hour slowly and turn in his travel receipts whenever he felt ready.

* * *

Chen Xuan walked back to his quiet cubicle near the western window.

His desk had been wiped spotless in his absence. His blue dispatch binder sat aligned beside his monitor, and the small potted succulent on the windowsill had been freshly watered.

He hung his washed gray jacket over his chair and turned on his computer.

Before the desktop finished loading, quiet footsteps stopped beside the low gray divider.

Chen Xuan looked up and saw Zhang Pengfei standing there.

Gone was the sharp, restless pride that had once made Zhang Pengfei pace the aisles looking for shortcuts. His white shirt was pressed neatly, his expression was steady, and in both hands he carried a clean glass mug filled with steaming hot water.

Zhang Pengfei set the mug gently on the cork coaster beside Chen Xuan's keyboard.

"Welcome back, Chen Xuan," Zhang Pengfei said quietly, his voice clear and completely free of bitterness. "The water just boiled, so give it a minute."

Chen Xuan looked at the steaming mug, then met Zhang Pengfei's eyes with a gentle, approving nod.

"Thank you, Zhang Pengfei," Chen Xuan replied warmly. "And thank you for watering the plant while I was away."

Zhang Pengfei rubbed the back of his neck with honest humility. "It was nothing. Manager Hu Dewei announced the two new coastal routes before lunch. Because of those routes, our department kept its full quarterly bonus, and the two junior clerks worried about layoffs just had their contracts renewed."

He paused, straightening his shoulders as he spoke from the heart.

"I used to think getting ahead meant shouting louder and cutting corners," Zhang Pengfei said softly. "Watching how you work taught me I had everything backward. Thank you, Chen Xuan."

Chen Xuan picked a golden walnut pastry from the open box on his desk and handed it over.

"You handled the eastern route schedules cleanly all week, Zhang Pengfei," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Everyone pulls their weight here. Eat this while it is crisp."

Zhang Pengfei accepted the pastry with both hands, nodded with quiet respect, and returned to his desk.

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of the warm water, opened his dispatch ledger, and smiled peacefully as late-afternoon sunlight spilled across his keyboard. In two hours, he would catch the bus home and finally have a hot bowl of his mother's soup.
