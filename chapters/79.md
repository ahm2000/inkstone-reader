# Chapter 79: Wang Lei's Delivery Tricycle

Around midday, the cheerful rattle of a loose chain and the high squeak of worn brakes echoed down the courtyard lane of Fuxing New Village.

Chen Xuan had just stepped out of Building 2 to buy a bottle of dark vinegar for Aunt Wu Lanying's braised fish. Sunlight warmed the cracked concrete yard, and the savory scent of garlic, ginger, and simmering broth drifted down from open kitchen windows on every floor.

A beat-up blue delivery tricycle turned sharply past the bicycle shed, stacked with empty water jugs in the back bed. Pedaling hard up the slight slope was neighbor Wang Lei, his thin cotton shirt damp at the collar from his morning route across town.

Wang Lei spotted Chen Xuan by the archway and squeezed both hand brakes at once, skidding to a halt with a loud metallic screech.

"Chen Xuan! Good thing I caught you before you went back inside!" Wang Lei called out, swinging one leg over the worn seat. He reached into the front corner of the tricycle bed and lifted two heavy red plastic bags bulging with crisp red apples and bright yellow pears. "Here, take these upstairs for Second Uncle Mingde!"

"You did not need to spend your money on fruit, Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said with a warm smile, stepping over to help steady the handlebars.

"Nonsense! Second Uncle fixed the axle on my tricycle last month and refused to charge me a dime," Wang Lei insisted, pressing the heavy bags into Chen Xuan's hands. He wiped the sweat from his forehead with the back of his wrist, his eyes shining with excitement. "I just delivered bottled water to the noodle shop on Old Street, and Auntie Zhang told me Second Uncle came home completely cleared this morning! You should have seen the street, Xuan! It looked like Spring Festival!"

"I was there earlier," Chen Xuan said easily. "The county crew washed the paint off the shutters."

"That is not even the half of it!" Wang Lei leaned against the handlebars, eager to share every detail he had witnessed on his route. "I rode straight past Hongrui Plaza an hour ago. The whole building is wrapped in blue and white provincial seals! All the cranes at the east construction site stopped dead in midair, and a huge crowd stood outside cheering while provincial officers marched Cheng Biao, demolition foreman Yan Tiehu, and Hongrui's managers into police vans!"

Chen Xuan listened quietly, a faint glint of amusement in his eyes. "People must be relieved."

"Relieved? The fruit vendor by the bus stop gave away free tangerines!" Wang Lei laughed, slapping his thigh. "Everyone on the street is talking about how Shanshui Group head Zhao Ruilong got dragged down in Jingzhou City, and how Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang personally ordered a full review of Yunxi County's land deals. Even the old bullies who used to hang around the market have vanished without a trace."

Wang Lei lowered his voice slightly, grinning from ear to ear. "Oh, and get this. While I was unloading water on Old Street, a new Acting Director from Old Street Station walked right down the middle of the lane on foot. Officer Wei Kun! Remember him? The quiet deputy who always helped old folks carry heavy sacks across the intersection? He shook hands with old tailor Yu Changshun and promised that anyone bothering the shopkeepers would go straight into handcuffs."

"Sounds like Old Street is in good hands now," Chen Xuan remarked calmly.

Wang Lei shook his head in genuine wonder, squinting up at the clear blue sky. "Honestly, Xuan, it feels like a story out of a television drama. Yesterday everyone thought Hongrui Real Estate could swallow half the county without blinking, and overnight their whole empire crumbled like dry mud. Old tailor Yu kept joking that Mingde Hardware must have unbelievable feng shui, or some hidden benefactor in Jingzhou must be watching over Old Street."

Wang Lei nudged Chen Xuan's arm with a grin. "Tell me the truth, Xuan. You work in an office in the big city. Is anything at your company half as wild as what happened in our little county this morning?"

Chen Xuan shifted the red bags of apples and pears to his left hand and smiled peacefully. "Not at all. I am just an ordinary office worker. Most days I sit by the window, drink tea, and stay out of trouble. Quiet days are much better."

"True enough," Wang Lei chuckled, hopping back onto the saddle of his tricycle. "Still, it feels mighty good to see crooks get what they deserve."

"Stay for lunch," Chen Xuan offered warmly. "Second Aunt is making braised fish and pork ribs upstairs. There is plenty for everyone."

"I wish I could, Xuan!" Wang Lei said, patting his empty stomach with a regretful grin. "My mouth is already watering just smelling the courtyard, but I have twelve more jugs of water to deliver to the middle school before afternoon classes start. Tell Second Uncle I will drop by his hardware shop tomorrow to drink a cup of tea with him!"

"I will pass the message along," Chen Xuan said. "Ride carefully."

"Always do!" Wang Lei waved cheerfully, pushed down hard on the pedals, and rattled back down the lane whistling an old folk tune.

Chen Xuan stood for a moment watching the blue tricycle disappear past the iron gate. Across the street, the corner grocery shop still had its red awning pulled low against the noon sun. He walked over, picked up a glass bottle of dark vinegar, and strolled back toward Building 2.

As he climbed the familiar concrete stairs carrying fresh fruit and vinegar, the sound of Chen Mingde's hearty laugh drifted down from the sixth floor. Chen Xuan smiled softly to himself and stepped upward into the warmth of home.
