# Chapter 95: Packing the Green Thermos

By eleven fifteen on Monday night, the small apartment in Fuxing New Village had settled back into its familiar, unhurried rhythm.

Down on Old Street, Mingde Hardware's rolling shutter was locked tight for the night, Chen Mingde was already asleep in his own bed for the first time in days, and Wu Lanying had walked them to the alley mouth with a genuine smile softening the lines around her eyes.

Inside the warm kitchen, Liu Meifang twisted the metal lid onto a thick glass jar packed with bright red homemade pickled chilies, garlic cloves, and ginger slices. She pressed her thumb against the center of the lid to check the seal, then wrapped the jar tightly in two sheets of folded newspaper and a plastic grocery bag.

"Hold the mouth of the canvas bag open, Chen Xuan," Liu Meifang instructed, nudging his elbow with her forearm. "Put it flat at the bottom so the oil does not leak onto your clean shirts."

Chen Xuan leaned over the dining table and held open his worn dark blue canvas travel bag. "Mom, that is already the third jar. If I add any more pickled chilies and braised pork, the strap is going to snap before I reach the county bus station."

"That canvas strap is double-stitched," Liu Meifang replied briskly, tucking a second newspaper-wrapped jar beside the first and wedging a folded cotton towel between them. "The canteen food near Hengxin Logistics is all grease and no flavor. Last time you came home, your cheekbones looked thinner. Eating plain rice with a spoonful of my pickled chilies is ten times better than ordering greasy takeout boxes."

From the wooden chair across the table, Chen Jianguo lowered the county newspaper he had been folding and peered over the rim of his reading glasses. "Let her pack it, Chen Xuan. If you argue with Liu Meifang about food, she will stay awake until one in the morning frying peanuts to stuff into your pockets too."

"I am not staying awake for peanuts," Liu Meifang said, though the corner of her mouth twitched upward. She wiped her hands on her apron and glanced at the wall clock above the cupboard. "Eleven twenty already. Chen Xuan, zip that bag up and wash your face. Your father is right, you need to sleep."

Chen Jianguo set his folded newspaper down neatly beside his teacup and looked steadily at his son. "The first express bus to Qingyan leaves at six forty. If you miss it, the seven thirty bus hits the ring road traffic and you will walk into Hengxin Logistics late."

"I set my alarm for five fifty, Dad," Chen Xuan said easily, zipping the canvas bag shut and setting it upright beside the chair leg. "I will not be late."

Chen Jianguo paused, his rough fingers resting lightly on the edge of the wooden table. The worry that had kept his shoulders hunched all weekend had finally melted away, leaving only a father's quiet, habitual concern.

"Chen Xuan," Chen Jianguo asked softly, "how are things really going at Hengxin Logistics? Your supervisor does not give you a hard time for taking two days off to come home?"

"Not at all," Chen Xuan answered with an easy, grounded smile. "I finished my monthly dispatch manifests on Friday afternoon before I left. Twelve thousand yuan a month, regular shifts, quiet office, and nobody bothers me as long as the freight sheets balance."

Chen Jianguo nodded slowly, his eyes crinkling with relief. "Good. Do not envy those young men who brag about chasing quick fortunes or trading favors with big developers. Look at what happened to Deng Yongchang in Qingyan on the evening news tonight. Yesterday everyone thought he owned half the city, and today he is in handcuffs. A clean conscience and a steady twelve-thousand-yuan paycheck will let you sleep soundly every night of your life."

"Your father is right," Liu Meifang chimed in, stepping back from the kitchen sink carrying Chen Xuan's scratched green thermos. She had rinsed it thoroughly with boiling water and dried the dented stainless-steel rim with a clean dishcloth. "Keep your head down, do your job properly, and stay away from noisy crowds."

She handed the green thermos across the table. "Here. I checked the rubber ring inside the cap. It still seals tight, no leaks. Fill it with hot tea before you get on the bus tomorrow morning."

Chen Xuan took the green thermos in both hands. The chipped dark green paint along its lower curve felt smooth and familiar beneath his palms. He twisted the cap open a quarter turn, checked the clean interior, and screwed it shut again with a soft click.

"Thank you, Mom," Chen Xuan said warmly. "And Dad, do not carry heavy crates for Uncle tomorrow morning. Wait until the delivery boys unload them."

"Listen to him lecturing me," Chen Jianguo grumbled affectionately, standing up and stretching his back. "Your uncle Chen Mingde already has Yu Changshun coming over at seven to help hang that new canvas awning. We are not helpless old men."

"Still, take it easy," Chen Xuan replied, picking up his canvas bag and his green thermos. "Good night, Dad. Good night, Mom."

"Sleep early," Liu Meifang called softly as she turned off the kitchen light. "I will have boiled eggs and millet porridge ready at six."

Chen Xuan stepped into his small bedroom and clicked the latch shut. The room was narrow and tidy: a single wooden bed with a freshly aired cotton quilt, a plain desk by the window, and a worn plastic chair.

He set his canvas bag beside the desk and placed the scratched green thermos right beside his bus ticket and his ordinary silver wristwatch.

Before turning off the desk lamp, Chen Xuan glanced once at the matte-black secondary phone resting face-down on the desk. The screen remained completely dark. No perimeter alerts, no emergency pings. Beyond the quiet walls of Fuxing New Village, the nationwide clean-up was already rolling forward under its own momentum, provincial bureaus were tightening their ledgers, and Old Street would wake up at dawn to its first true morning of peace.

Forty meters down the dark lane beneath the locust tree, two motionless shadows stood watch in silence without asking for a word of thanks.

Chen Xuan did not pick up the secondary phone. Instead, he checked the alarm on his ordinary personal phone, confirmed it was set for five fifty in the morning, and switched off the desk lamp.

In the quiet darkness of the bedroom, he rested his head on the cotton pillow and smiled faintly.

Tomorrow morning, he would catch the six forty bus back to Qingyan, carry his green thermos and his mother's jars of pickled chilies up to his rented apartment, and clock in at Hengxin Logistics as an ordinary twelve-thousand-yuan clerk.
