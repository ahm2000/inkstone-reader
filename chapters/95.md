# Chapter 95: Packing the Green Thermos

Before dawn on Tuesday morning, soft blue light pressed against the frosted kitchen window of the small apartment in Fuxing New Village. Outside, the neighborhood still slept beneath a thin veil of morning mist, broken only by the distant rattle of a delivery tricycle rolling down the main lane.

Inside, the aluminum kettle on the gas stove gave a gentle, steady whistle. The cozy kitchen smelled of freshly boiled eggs, warm ginger, and toasted sesame.

Liu Meifang had been awake since before sunrise in her faded quilted vest. At the wooden table, she twisted the metal lid onto a thick glass jar filled with homemade pickled chilies and garlic slices, pressed her thumb against the center to test the seal, and wrapped the jar snugly in two layers of folded newspaper and a clean plastic bag.

"Hold the canvas bag open, Chen Xuan," Liu Meifang told him, nudging his elbow. "Set the jar flat at the bottom so it cannot tip over in the overhead bin."

Chen Xuan held the straps of his worn khaki canvas bag steady while she tucked the bundled jar carefully between two rolled sweaters. Next went six still-warm boiled eggs wrapped in a clean cloth napkin, a small paper sack of roasted peanuts, a neatly folded wool scarf, and two pairs of thick cotton socks.

"Mom, I am only flying back to Shanghai," Chen Xuan said with a quiet laugh. "They have grocery stores there. I am not crossing a desert."

"Store-bought chilies are all salt and no flavor," Liu Meifang countered briskly, zipping the side pocket shut. "And airport food costs three times what it is worth. Your flight is at 7:30 AM. If you skip breakfast on the road, your stomach will ache before noon."

From the doorway, Chen Jianguo stepped in wearing his dark cotton coat. He checked the main zipper of the canvas bag with rough, careful fingers, then slipped a neat folded paper envelope into the inner pocket beside Chen Xuan's plain plastic office badge.

"There is some spare cash inside," Chen Jianguo said quietly, avoiding his son's eyes as he adjusted the shoulder strap. "Phone payments fail sometimes when the signal drops. Keep it for taxis and hot meals."

"Dad, I still have plenty from my salary," Chen Xuan said gently.

"Keep it anyway," Chen Jianguo replied firmly. "Call your mother the minute the plane touches down in Shanghai. Do not wait until evening."

Before Chen Xuan could answer, a soft, familiar knock sounded at the front door. Chen Xuan walked over and pulled it open to find Chen Mingde and Wu Lanying standing in the cool hallway, their cheeks pink from the pre-dawn walk over from Old Street.

"Uncle, Aunt, why did you get up so early?" Chen Xuan asked, stepping aside to let them in. "Uncle's shoulder still needs rest."

"My shoulder is fine," Chen Mingde said with a warm, bright smile, pressing a small brown paper parcel into Chen Xuan's hands. It radiated gentle heat through the paper. "Old Lin opened his bakery oven early today. Fresh toasted sesame cakes. Eat two on the way to the airport."

Wu Lanying patted Chen Xuan's arm affectionately. "The hardware shop is already bright and steady again, Xuan. Do not worry about home for a single minute. Just focus on your ordinary office work in Shanghai and take care of yourself."

Chen Mingde exchanged a quiet, grateful glance with Chen Jianguo. "After everything this past week, waking up to an ordinary morning feels like the greatest luck in the world," he said softly. "You go back to your desk in Shanghai with a light heart, Xuan. Your father and I will look after things here."

"I will, Uncle," Chen Xuan said, his voice warm and steady. "Thank you both."

He walked back into the kitchen, turned off the gas burner, and lifted his old scratched green thermos from the drying rack. The paint along the base was chipped from years of daily commutes, and the stainless lid carried a faint dent near the rim, yet it still held heat better than any new bottle.

Chen Xuan dropped a pinch of dried jasmine tea leaves inside, poured steaming water smoothly from the kettle, and screwed the metal cap down tight. He checked his pockets for his ID card and boarding pass, tucked the warm green thermos familiarly under his left arm, and slung the worn canvas bag over his right shoulder.

Downstairs at the entrance to Fuxing New Village, an early taxi waited by the curb with its amber headlights glowing softly through the pale dawn mist. The driver popped the trunk, though Chen Xuan kept both his light canvas bag and his green thermos with him in the back seat.

Under the concrete awning of the apartment building, Liu Meifang, Chen Jianguo, Chen Mingde, and Wu Lanying stood close together in the cool morning air, waving warmly as he climbed inside.

"Eat the eggs while they are warm!" Liu Meifang called out, pulling her vest closer around her shoulders.

"Do not stay up too late!" Chen Jianguo added with a brisk wave.

Chen Xuan rolled down the window half an inch and smiled back at them until the taxi turned the corner onto the quiet main road toward the airport highway.

As the car picked up speed, the morning sky ahead brightened from deep indigo to pale silver. Chen Xuan leaned back against the worn vinyl seat and rested one hand lightly on the warm, scratched green thermos beside his knee.

Behind him, Yunxi County woke to a calm, ordinary Tuesday morning. Ahead of him lay the 7:30 AM flight to Shanghai, and whatever storm waited beyond the clouds.
