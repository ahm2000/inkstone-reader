# Chapter 83: The Second Cross-Department War Room: Financial Firebreak

By two o'clock on Monday afternoon, the second cross-department war room in the capital's central compound was so thick with cigarette smoke that the overhead lamps looked like pale lanterns in fog. Seven ceramic ashtrays lined the long mahogany table, every single one heaped with crushed butts.

Premier Gu Chengshan sat at the head of the table, his jacket off, sleeves rolled once at the wrists, listening in heavy silence as the morning's interrogation transcripts from Gao Zhengyi and Guo Zhengming were distributed in red-bordered folders. Beside him, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua rolled a heavy brass lighter between his thumb and forefinger, his eyes fixed on the projection board at the far end of the room.

On the board stood a single red figure that had drained the blood from every regulator's face: 100 trillion yuan in compounded twenty-year shadow-asset trails, layered across domestic trust products, cross-border letters of credit, off-book smelter equities, and offshore bearer custody.

People's Bank of China Governor Shen Wenchao was the first to break the silence, his voice tight and strained. "Premier Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, I have to speak plainly. If we freeze every linked domestic clearing account tonight under an open enforcement order, the interbank repo market will seize up before the Shanghai opening bell tomorrow morning."

Finance Minister Han Zhiyuan nodded sharply, his fingers tapping a frantic rhythm against his folder. "Shen Wenchao is right. Those three retired bank presidents He Guoliang named did not park their money in isolated shoeboxes. Cao Wenhui, Zheng Rongkui, and Pan Desheng wove those shadow liabilities directly into municipal infrastructure bonds, rural credit cooperatives, and commercial wealth-management pools. If we pull the plug publicly, twenty-two provincial financing vehicles will default by Friday. Ordinary depositors will line up outside bank branches before noon."

China Securities Regulatory Commission Chairman Xu Wenbin leaned forward, sweat shining on his forehead. "My desks ran a stress model an hour ago. Even a leaked rumor of 100-trillion-yuan shadow-asset freeze will wipe twelve percent off the benchmark index in forty-eight hours. Foreign short-sellers in Hong Kong and Singapore will pile in the second they smell a liquidity squeeze."

Customs Director Wu Yongqiang scoffed loudly, stubbing out his cigarette so hard the filter split. "So what are the financial ministries proposing? We let Fang Qiming's bonded export wharves keep moving stolen military alloys because the stock market might catch a cold?"

"Nobody said let them move a single gram of alloy, Wu Yongqiang!" Xu Wenbin snapped back, his face flushing red. "Customs can lock the wharves quietly! I am talking about publicly freezing three hundred domestic trust companies and forty-seven listed shell corporations at once!"

Commerce Minister Ma Zhenyu raised a hand, his voice tense. "Xu Wenbin is not exaggerating. Twelve of those offshore trading houses tied to Geneva hold active letters of credit for grain and liquefied natural gas imports. If we slap public sanctions on them without a replacement clearing channel, port terminals from Ningbo to Qingdao will halt unloading by Wednesday."

National Development and Reform Commission Director Li Weiming exhaled a long stream of smoke, his shoulders hunched. "And six provinces are sitting on county demolition projects tied to Shengyuan Capital. If municipal contractors stop getting paid overnight, migrant construction crews will block county government gates before the week is out."

Across the table, State Security Minister Ding Guoping had not touched his teacup. He spoke quietly, his voice cutting straight through the bickering. "Every hour we hesitate to lock those domestic shells, Lukas Weber's handlers in Zurich can burn the remaining electronic mirrors and liquidate the liquid tranches through Tokyo and London. We already have the custody strings from Zero Guard's archive. Waiting for a polite market window is handing them an escape hatch."

The room erupted into overlapping voices. Shen Wenchao argued with Ding Guoping over settlement cutoffs; Han Zhiyuan and Wu Yongqiang traded sharp words over bonded-zone jurisdiction; Xu Wenbin muttered furiously while flipping through liquidity tables.

Crack.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua slammed his heavy brass lighter onto the mahogany table. The sharp metallic report echoed off the paneled walls, cutting every voice dead in a single heartbeat.

Every minister froze. Cigarette smoke curled lazily upward from forgotten cigarettes resting in trembling fingers.

Zhou Zhenhua looked slowly down the length of the table, his expression hard as granite. "Are you finished panicking?"

Nobody answered. Xu Wenbin swallowed and looked down at his notepad.

"Listen to yourselves," Zhou Zhenhua said, his voice low and lethal. "Half of you are acting like we have to choose between letting traitors bleed the country dry or blowing up our own banking system in front of the television cameras. Who told you this has to be announced on the evening news?"

Premier Gu Chengshan nodded once, his dark eyes steady. "Speak, Zhou Zhenhua."

"We build a silent firebreak," Zhou Zhenhua said, standing up and walking to the projection board. "Starting at four o'clock this afternoon, no public asset-freeze notices, no press releases, and no sudden halts on retail wealth-management redemptions. Instead, Shen Wenchao and Han Zhiyuan activate the sovereign stabilization trust under emergency reserve code seven."

Shen Wenchao blinked, sitting up straighter. "Emergency reserve code seven? Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, that transfers beneficial ownership of distressed clearing assets straight into central-bank custody behind the settlement layer."

"Exactly," Zhou Zhenhua said sharply. "Every single one of the tainted trust pools, off-book credit cooperatives, and listed shell equities gets absorbed quietly into the sovereign trust in four phased tranches over thirty days. On the surface, the interbank clearing lines stay green. Depositors withdraw their pensions on time. County construction workers receive their Friday wages from replacement state-owned engineering bureaus assigned by Li Weiming. Ordinary citizens across the country do not feel a single tremor."

Han Zhiyuan's eyes narrowed as he began calculating rapidly on his pad. "And the actual equity holders behind those shells? The domestic proxies for Cao Wenhui, Fang Qiming, and the offshore Geneva channel?"

"Their beneficial claim is zeroed out silently at the clearinghouse level," Zhou Zhenhua replied coldly. "They do not get a court summons in the newspaper. They wake up tomorrow morning, try to wire a single yuan out of their shadow accounts, and discover the account is legally owned by the state treasury while Ding Guoping's officers are already knocking on their front doors."

Ma Zhenyu exhaled slowly, the tension easing from his jaw. "If Commerce swaps the grain and gas letters of credit onto state-bank balance sheets overnight before Wu Yongqiang seals the bonded wharves, port operations will not lose an hour."

"Customs can lock every wharf tied to Shengyuan Capital at midnight," Wu Yongqiang agreed, his voice grimly satisfied. "Quietly. Under routine quarantine and hazardous-material inspection orders."

Premier Gu Chengshan picked up his fountain pen, uncapped it, and looked around the smoke-filled room. Every department head straightened in his chair.

"Hear this clearly," Gu Chengshan said, his voice calm yet carrying the full weight of the state. "We are dismantling a twenty-year cancer worth 100 trillion yuan in compounded shadow claims. Not one bank run. Not one panicked depositor. Not one ordinary family losing its savings because parasites hid behind their accounts. Anyone who leaks a word of this room's protocol to a market desk or a former colleague will join He Guoliang in the basement before midnight."

He signed the sovereign-trust authorization with a single firm stroke and slid it to Zhou Zhenhua.

"Move," Gu Chengshan ordered. "Before the London desk opens."
