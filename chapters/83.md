# Chapter 83: The Second Cross-Department War Room: Financial Firebreak

Velvet curtains stayed drawn over the tall windows of Beijing's second cross-department war room. Warm tungsten lamps cast steady pools of light across the long mahogany table, reflecting off white porcelain teacups and neat stacks of red-bordered financial briefs. Along the north wall, three wide digital displays traced real-time clearing flows across the country's Four National Banks.

Around the inner ring sat the 24 core voting members of the Capital Top Council, every one holding senior state rank across finance, central banking, regulatory oversight, and national audit bureaus. At the head sat Premier Gu Chengshan with his sleeves rolled neatly to his forearms, beside Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, who tapped a silver fountain pen against his open ledger.

Flanking the head in high-backed chairs sat ninety-year-old founding elder Elder Song and two fellow senior elders, listening with quiet focus as the morning's emergency numbers scrolled across the screens.

"Let us keep this sharp and factual," Premier Gu Chengshan said, his voice calm and decisive. "Following the arrests of Zhao Dehan, Zhao Ruilong, and Gao Yuliang in Handong over the past forty-eight hours, forensic auditors uncovered far more than a provincial land syndicate. Vice Premier Zhou, walk the council through the cross-border exposure."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua stood and pointed to the central display, where red settlement lines branched outward from regional clearing hubs toward offshore shell jurisdictions.

"At 2:15 AM, our automated anti-money-laundering monitors flagged a coordinated outbound capital surge," Zhou Zhenhua reported. "Seventeen offshore shell syndicates tied to foreign financial groups attempted to exploit the Handong anti-corruption sweep to dump 1.2 trillion yuan in dirty cross-border assets and trigger a short-term liquidity squeeze across regional interbank clearing houses. Their goal was straightforward: spook domestic bondholders, force regional commercial banks into overnight defaults, and buy up distressed state and private industrial shares at steep discounts."

A low murmur rippled around the mahogany table. The Governor of the Central Bank leaned forward, his brow furrowed. "What is the current status of the 1.2 trillion yuan outflow?"

"Contained," Zhou Zhenhua answered firmly. "Working under emergency financial security statutes, the Central Bank and the Four National Banks executed synchronized settlement freezes across all seventeen conduit networks before the morning markets opened. Every single frozen account is backed by audited transaction logs, shell ownership traces, and criminal referrals from the Handong investigation. Not one yuan of that 1.2 trillion left the domestic clearing floor."

"Freezing dirty capital stops the immediate hemorrhage," the Minister of Finance spoke up, tapping his red-bordered brief. "However, our interbank market is still absorbing the shock of freezing 1.2 trillion yuan in a single night. Foreign rating agencies and short-selling desks are already watching the Shanghai and Shenzhen opens. If commercial banks tighten credit out of caution, legitimate manufacturers and exporters will feel the pinch before Friday."

Premier Gu Chengshan nodded. "That brings us to the second item on the agenda. Turn to page four of your sealed briefs."

Twenty-four folders opened in near unison. A heavy, focused silence fell across the room as senior ministers and bank governors read the verified sovereign trust settlement sheet.

"Early this morning," Premier Gu Chengshan said evenly, "the sovereign reserve linked to the holder of the Black Order cleared full verification through the Central Bank's master custody ledger. Total verified liquidity available to stabilize national clearing and sovereign credit stands at 300 trillion yuan."

Even seasoned central bankers who managed multi-trillion budgets daily exhaled slowly as they verified the audit stamps at the bottom of the page.

"300 trillion yuan," the Chairman of the Banking Regulatory Commission murmured, adjusting his spectacles. "With an audited liquidity floor of that scale behind the Four National Banks, no offshore short-selling consortium on earth can force a run on our currency or bond market."

"Precisely," Premier Gu Chengshan said. "Yet sheer scale is not a policy. We must deploy this financial firebreak with surgical discipline. I want zero market panic, zero inflationary spillover into consumer prices, and zero excuse for poorly managed local financing platforms to treat a national reserve as a bailout fund."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua flipped to the execution schedule. "Here is our proposed three-step framework. First, we allocate a targeted stabilization window through Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China to guarantee overnight interbank liquidity for verified trade, manufacturing, and payroll accounts. Second, we ring-fence the frozen 1.2 trillion yuan in illicit cross-border funds inside a court-supervised escrow account until full asset forfeiture completes. Third, we pair the liquidity backstop with mandatory quarterly audits across every provincial investment platform, starting with Handong."

Elder Song, who had remained silent while the ministers examined the figures, set his porcelain teacup gently onto its saucer. Every eye at the table turned toward the ninety-year-old elder.

"Premier Gu and Vice Premier Zhou have framed this properly," Elder Song said in a clear, unhurried voice. "A 300 trillion yuan sovereign reserve is an immense shield, not a license for loose discipline. Whoever holds the Black Order placed this firebreak at the state's disposal because foreign predators and domestic corruption nearly converged into a systemic crisis. If we use this breathing room merely to paper over broken provincial supervision, we will waste the greatest strategic margin this country has ever been handed."

"Elder Song is right," the Minister of Finance said firmly. "Stabilize the clearing floor today, prosecute every corrupt channel behind the 1.2 trillion yuan capital flight tomorrow, and lock permanent audit transparency into the banking rules by the end of the quarter."

Premier Gu Chengshan looked around the inner ring, meeting the steady gaze of every voting member. "Are there any remaining technical objections to the financial firebreak protocol?"

None came.

"Then we call the vote," Premier Gu said, raising his right hand. "All in favor of activating the interbank stabilization window, locking the 1.2 trillion yuan forfeiture escrow, and authorizing the nationwide provincial audit mandate."

All 24 core voting members raised their hands without hesitation.

"Unanimous," Premier Gu announced, signing the execution order in crisp black ink. "Transmit the clearance codes to the Four National Banks. The markets open in twenty minutes, and they will open steady."
