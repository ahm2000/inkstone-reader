# Chapter 83: The Second Cross-Department War Room: Financial Firebreak

Pale blue cigarette smoke hung beneath the lamps of Beijing's second war room. Heavy velvet curtains stayed drawn, and cigarettes burned slowly down in glass ashtrays along the dark walnut table.

Around the inner ring sat the 24 core voting members of the Capital Top Council, every one at half-premier or sub-national rank, standing above provincial party secretaries and cabinet ministers. At the head sat Premier Gu Chengshan with sleeves rolled neatly to his forearms, beside Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, who rested one steady hand near a brass lighter. Flanking the head in three senior mahogany chairs sat the white-haired Founding Elders: Elder Lin, architect of the state's financial backbone; Elder Ye, eyes carrying thirty years of border frost; and ninety-year-old Elder Song, thin hands resting calmly on his dark cane.

Behind them in the outer ring sat the non-standing observers with open notebooks and silent pens: Fang Zhengming of China Construction Bank, Han Guoliang of ICBC, Tang Wenhao of Bank of China, and Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank, beside People's Bank of China Governor Qu Shilin, Finance Minister Fei Yaoting, Commerce Minister Ruan Defu, Customs Director Ou Guanghui, and State Security Minister Ding Guoping. Not one observer spoke out of turn.

At the corner desk, two archive clerks in gray uniforms recorded every word and vote into linen ledgers for the permanent sealed minutes.

Premier Gu Chengshan nodded toward the lectern. "Deliver the financial verification report."

Shen Wenbo, deputy director of the Central Financial Clearing Office, stepped forward with a blue binder and spoke crisply.

"Premier Gu Chengshan, Founding Elders, and core members," Shen Wenbo reported. "Our audit confirms 100 trillion yuan in dirty funds woven through twenty years of provincial trusts, shell firms, and overseas vaults in Geneva and Zurich. The full counter-shield is locked in place. Private citizen Chen Xuan's 300 trillion yuan clean, legal gift has passed every check, with no loans, no interest, and no conditions. Another 500 trillion yuan stands ready on standby if the state needs a single extra fen."

He turned one page cleanly.

"The 300 trillion yuan sits evenly across the Four National Banks. 75 trillion yuan is verified in China Construction Bank for city bonds and county payrolls. 75 trillion yuan sits on ICBC's books to cover any cash run. 75 trillion yuan guards Bank of China's foreign desk against attacks on the yuan. The final 75 trillion yuan backs Huaxia Bank's port and grain credit lines. Report complete."

Shen Wenbo closed his binder, bowed once, and returned silently to the back row.

Dead silence pressed down on the hall.

A long ash broke off a core member's cigarette onto his sleeve, untouched. A cold chill slid down almost every spine. These veteran leaders had spent decades bargaining over 10 billion yuan budgets behind closed doors. Now an unseen citizen had placed 300 trillion yuan on the state's table for free, with 500 trillion yuan behind it, asking for neither a title nor a favor.

Deep awe mingled with a heavy, unspoken debt in every chest. In the outer ring, the four bank presidents kept their heads bowed, hiding the slight tremor in their fingers as their pens scratched quietly.

Premier Gu Chengshan spoke first, his voice flat as iron.

"The firebreak is built," Premier Gu Chengshan said, sweeping all 24 core seats. "The old threat that tearing out dirty roots will break the banks is dead. Tonight, we cut out 100 trillion yuan of twenty-year rot behind a 300 trillion yuan steel wall. Not one bank run. Not one scared saver in Beijing, Shanghai, or Sha Province. Zhou Zhenhua, state the execution and personnel plan."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua clicked his lighter shut and opened a red-tabbed folder.

"Four clean steps overnight," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said evenly. "No public freeze notices or press leaks. Under emergency rule seven, the Four National Banks absorb healthy payrolls and port credits while State Security and the courts seize every tainted trust. By morning, Geneva's front men find the state owns their ledgers and hear officers at their doors."

He turned to the second sheet, eyes hardening.

"This firebreak is also a broom," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua added. "Clean bank auditors and local inspectors who refused bribes receive immediate commendations and rapid promotions. Negligent regional directors who looked away while dirty trusts grew fat have their ranks frozen and withheld. As for senior faction figures inside the financial system who cannot yet be jailed, we strip their real power cleanly. They move tomorrow to higher-sounding, completely powerless honorary advisory posts, far from every vault and credit line."

The air in the inner ring turned colder. Polite faces stiffened around the table.

Then State Councilor Kong Weimin, one of the 24 core voting members, cleared his throat softly. Wearing a mild, grandfatherly smile, he folded his right hand over his folder while his left thumb rubbed nervously against his trouser seam beneath the table.

"Premier Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, and Founding Elders," Kong Weimin said smoothly. "This firebreak is a blessing. None of us defends foreign traitors. My only concern is the southern and Sha Province regional state-bank group. Those branches hold forty years of ties to honest county factories. Freezing their leadership in one night may shake local morale. Could we grant that regional bank group a thirty-day grace period to sort its own books?"

Subtle glances darted across the 24 seats. Every veteran knew Kong Weimin's brother-in-law and two former secretaries ran that regional bank network. Two seats away, Vice Chairman Liao Guohua lowered his eyes to his teacup, fingers gripping the lid, waiting to see if old Beijing mercy still lived.

Elder Lin did not raise his voice. He tapped neat gray ash into his saucer and locked his cold eyes onto Kong Weimin.

"Kong Weimin, an unseen citizen just handed this nation 300 trillion yuan for free so ordinary savers would not lose a fen, and you are sitting here begging for thirty days to let your old faction carry their dirty ledgers out the back door?"

The single sentence cut through the smoke like a winter blade.

A cold chill shot down Kong Weimin's spine. Sweat shone along his hairline. His polite smile crumbled. At the corner desk, the two clerks kept their heads down while their pens flew across the linen ledger, logging every word. Kong Weimin lowered his eyes and dared not speak again.

"No grace periods," Elder Lin said softly as Elder Ye nodded once in grim approval. "Call the vote, Gu Chengshan. Let the sealed minutes remember where every man stood tonight."

Premier Gu Chengshan read the motion cleanly.

"Motion before the 24 core voting members: immediate overnight activation of the 300 trillion yuan firebreak across China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank; zero grace periods for any regional state-bank faction; full seizure of tainted trusts; and immediate execution of the promotions, withheld ranks, and honorary advisory transfers. All in favor, raise your right hand."

Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua raised their hands first, followed cleanly by twenty more half-premier members.

Two seats stayed frozen. Kong Weimin pressed his pale hand flat against his folder. Beside him, Vice Chairman Liao Guohua glanced sideways for half a second, jaw tight, keeping his hand down.

"Abstentions, raise your hands," Premier Gu Chengshan said evenly.

Stiffly, Kong Weimin and Liao Guohua lifted their hands halfway.

"Against?" Zero hands moved.

The chief clerk rose slightly and read the permanent entry in a dry, clear voice.

"Formal vote recorded in the sealed state minutes: twenty-two in favor, two abstentions, zero against. Motion passed. Abstaining members logged verbatim by full name: Kong Weimin and Liao Guohua."

A shiver ran through Kong Weimin's shoulders. Having his name inked as an abstainer in the sealed archive was a slow political death sentence.

Premier Gu Chengshan signed the order with one heavy stroke and slid it for the red seal. "Move," he ordered quietly. "Not one word leaves this hall."

In the outer ring, the four bank presidents and five ministers closed their notebooks in silent unison as the state moved its blade behind a wall of 300 trillion yuan.
