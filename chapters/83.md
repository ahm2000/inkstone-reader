# Chapter 83: The Second Cross-Department War Room: Financial Firebreak

Velvet curtains stayed drawn over the tall windows of Beijing's second cross-department war room. Warm tungsten lamps cast steady pools of light across the long mahogany table, reflecting off white porcelain teacups and neat stacks of red-bordered financial briefs. Along the north wall, three wide digital displays traced real-time clearing flows across the country's Four National Banks.

Around the inner ring sat the 24 core voting members of the Capital Top Council, every one holding senior state rank across finance, central banking, regulatory oversight, and national audit bureaus. At the head sat Premier Gu Chengshan with his sleeves rolled neatly to his forearms, beside Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, who tapped a silver fountain pen against his open ledger.

Flanking the head in high-backed chairs sat ninety-year-old founding elder Elder Song and retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo. Neither spoke as the financial briefing began, their presence alone keeping the room sharp and disciplined.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua stood and turned toward the central display. With a tap of his remote, red and amber columns lit up across the screen, mapping the fallout from the weekend arrests of Zhao Dehan, Zhao Ruilong, and Gao Yuliang, along with the freeze placed on Shanshui Group and its coastal shell networks.

"Let us look at the exact numbers first," Zhou Zhenhua said, his voice flat and steady. "When the Anti-Corruption Bureau froze those accounts and seized the forged collateral in Handong and three coastal ports, we exposed two immediate holes before Monday's market open."

He pointed to the left column. "First, 1.2 trillion yuan in direct bad loans sitting on the books of regional banks tied to Shanshui Group and its linked borrowers. The underlying land and project assets were pledged three or four times over. Without fresh capital, those regional branches cannot clear Monday morning withdrawals."

Several banking regulators tightened their jaws, staring at the red ledger lines.

Zhou Zhenhua moved to the center column. "Second, 3.8 trillion yuan in exposed municipal bonds and short-term interbank credit lines. Word of the provincial arrests leaked to overseas trading desks last night. Foreign funds and leveraged short-sellers have already lined up to dump regional debt and pressure our clearing rates at 9:30 AM. Together, 1.2 trillion yuan in bad loans and 3.8 trillion yuan in exposed credit create a total liquidity gap of 5 trillion yuan."

A heavy quiet settled over the mahogany table. A 5 trillion yuan gap was not a minor accounting snag. If clearing froze at the regional level, honest factories would miss payroll by Wednesday, suppliers would halt shipments, and worried families would form lines outside bank branches before noon.

"Can the Four National Banks absorb 5 trillion yuan on their own before the opening bell?" asked the Chairman of the Banking Regulatory Commission. "Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China have strong reserves, yet moving that volume in six hours without triggering market panic will strain interbank liquidity."

"They will not absorb it alone," Premier Gu Chengshan said. He folded his hands on the red-bordered brief in front of him and looked around the ring. "At 3:00 AM, the emergency sovereign firebreak channel authorized by the holder of the Black Order completed settlement into our segregated state clearing accounts. The total sum deployed is 8 trillion yuan."

Every pen in the room stopped moving. The Central Bank Governor immediately checked the encrypted terminal beside his elbow. Three seconds later, he looked up and nodded firmly.

"Confirmed, Premier," the Governor said. "Full clearing codes verified across all four national settlement hubs. The funds are liquid, clean, and already under central bank custody."

"Here is how we deploy all 8 trillion yuan," Premier Gu said, tapping the table once. "First, 1.2 trillion yuan goes straight into the affected regional banks before 8:00 AM. Every depositor and every honest factory account stays 100 percent whole. Not a single citizen loses one cent because of Zhao Ruilong's fraud."

He looked to Vice Premier Zhou. "Second, 3.8 trillion yuan backs every exposed municipal bond and provincial credit line through the Four National Banks. Any legitimate city project or industrial supplier needing rollover credit gets it on schedule, audited line by line."

"And the remaining 3 trillion yuan?" asked the Finance Minister, already leaning forward.

"That 3 trillion yuan forms an active market-suppression wall," Premier Gu replied, his eyes turning cool. "Position it across the domestic bond exchange and offshore currency clearing desks before 9:00 AM. When foreign speculators try to short our regional debt and drive panic selling at the open, we absorb every sell order and push the price straight back up. Make their short positions bleed until they are forced to cover."

A low ripple of approval passed through the 24 members. The math was airtight, proportional, and built for real-world execution. Every single yuan had a clear destination and a hard operational purpose.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua noted the final routing orders in his ledger. "Audit teams from the National Audit Office will sit inside every regional branch receiving the 1.2 trillion yuan injection. Any bank officer who tries to hide private losses inside the rescue package goes straight to the Procuratorate."

"Agreed," Premier Gu said. He turned slightly toward the high-backed chair on his right. "Elder Song, do you have anything to add before we issue the clearing order?"

Elder Song lifted his teacup, took one unhurried sip, and set it softly back on the saucer. "The numbers are sound, and the plan is clear. Keep the rescue funds clean, protect the people's savings first, and hold every corrupt borrower legally accountable after the storm passes. Do not let a single yuan slip back into dirty hands."

"Understood," Premier Gu said. He looked around the inner ring. "All 24 members, confirm readiness for the 8 trillion yuan firebreak deployment."

Around the mahogany table, twenty-four senior officials raised their right hands in unison. On the north wall, the amber warning bars shifted to steady green as the Four National Banks began routing the emergency liquidity across the nation's clearing lines.

Outside the curtained windows, the crisp autumn dawn was breaking over Beijing. By the time the first commuters stepped onto the streets and the markets opened for the week, the financial firebreak would already be locked in place.
