# Chapter 83: The Second Cross-Department War Room: Financial Firebreak

Pale cigarette smoke hung beneath the lamps of Beijing's second war room. Velvet curtains stayed drawn.

Around the inner ring sat the 24 core voting members of the Capital Top Council, every one at half premier or sub national rank, above provincial party secretaries and cabinet ministers. At the head sat Premier Gu Chengshan with sleeves rolled to his forearms, beside Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, resting one hand near a brass lighter. Flanking the head in mahogany chairs sat the white haired Founding Elders: Elder Lin, architect of the state's financial backbone; Elder Ye, eyes carrying thirty years of border frost; and ninety year old Elder Song, hands resting on his cane.

In the outer ring sat the non standing observers with open notebooks: Fang Zhengming of China Construction Bank, Han Guoliang of ICBC, Tang Wenhao of Bank of China, and Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank, beside People's Bank of China Governor Qu Shilin, Finance Minister Fei Yaoting, Commerce Minister Ruan Defu, Customs Director Ou Guanghui, and State Security Minister Ding Guoping. Not one observer spoke out of turn, while two archive clerks at the corner desk recorded every word and vote into linen ledgers for the sealed minutes.

Premier Gu Chengshan nodded toward the lectern. "Deliver the financial verification report."

Shen Wenbo, deputy director of the Central Financial Clearing Office, stepped forward with a blue binder.

"Premier Gu Chengshan, Founding Elders, and core members," Shen Wenbo reported. "Our audit confirms 1.2 trillion yuan in dirty funds woven through twenty years of provincial trusts, shell firms, and overseas vaults in Geneva and Zurich. The counter shield is locked in place. Private citizen Chen Xuan's 300 billion yuan clean, legal gift has passed every check, with no loans, no interest, and no conditions. Another 500 billion yuan stands ready on standby if the state needs a single extra fen."

He turned one page cleanly.

"The 300 billion yuan sits evenly across the Four National Banks. 75 billion yuan is verified in China Construction Bank for city bonds and county payrolls. 75 billion yuan sits on ICBC's books to cover any cash run. 75 billion yuan guards Bank of China's foreign desk against attacks on the yuan. The final 75 billion yuan backs Huaxia Bank's port and grain credit lines. Report complete."

Shen Wenbo closed his binder, bowed, and returned to the back row.

Dead silence pressed down on the hall. Ash broke off a member's cigarette onto his sleeve, untouched. These veteran leaders had spent decades bargaining over 10,000,000,000 yuan budgets. Now an unseen citizen had placed 300 billion yuan on the state's table for free, with 500 billion yuan behind it, asking for neither title nor favor. Deep awe mingled with an unspoken debt in every chest, while the four bank presidents bowed their heads to hide their trembling fingers.

Premier Gu Chengshan spoke first, his voice flat as iron.

"The firebreak is built," Premier Gu Chengshan said, sweeping all 24 seats. "The old threat that tearing out dirty roots will break the banks is dead. Tonight, we cut out 1.2 trillion yuan of twenty year rot behind a 300 billion yuan steel wall. Not one bank run or scared saver in Beijing, Shanghai, or Sha Province. Zhou Zhenhua, state the execution and personnel plan."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua clicked his lighter shut and opened a red folder.

"Four clean steps overnight," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said evenly. "No public freeze notices or leaks. Under a State Council emergency decree, with a written notice to each owner, the Four National Banks absorb healthy payrolls and port credits while State Security and the courts seize every tainted trust. By morning, Geneva's front men find the state owns their ledgers and hear officers at their doors."

He turned to the second sheet.

"This firebreak is also a broom," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua added. "Clean bank auditors and inspectors who refused bribes receive immediate commendations and rapid promotions. Negligent regional directors have their ranks frozen and withheld. Senior faction figures inside the financial system who cannot yet be jailed are stripped of real power and moved tomorrow to powerless honorary advisory posts."

Faces stiffened around the table. State Councilor Kong Weimin cleared his throat, wearing a mild smile while his thumb rubbed his trouser seam beneath the table.

"Premier Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, and Founding Elders," Kong Weimin said smoothly. "This firebreak is a blessing. None of us defends traitors. My only concern is the Sha Province regional state bank group, which holds forty years of ties to county factories. Freezing their leadership overnight may shake local morale. Could we grant that group a thirty day grace period to sort its books?"

Glances darted across the table. Every veteran knew Kong Weimin's brother in law and two former secretaries ran that network. Two seats away, Vice Chairman Liao Guohua lowered his eyes, waiting to see if old Beijing mercy still lived.

Elder Lin tapped ash into his saucer and locked cold eyes onto Kong Weimin.

"Kong Weimin, an unseen citizen just handed this nation 300 billion yuan for free so ordinary savers would not lose a fen, and you are sitting here begging for thirty days to let your old faction carry their dirty ledgers out the back door?"

The sentence cut through the smoke like a winter blade. Sweat shone along Kong Weimin's hairline as the clerks logged every word. He lowered his eyes and dared not speak again.

"No grace periods," Elder Lin said softly as Elder Ye nodded. "Call the vote, Gu Chengshan. Let the sealed minutes remember where every man stood tonight."

"Motion before the 24 core voting members," Premier Gu Chengshan read. "Immediate overnight activation of the 300 billion yuan firebreak across the Four National Banks, zero grace periods, full seizure of tainted trusts, and immediate execution of the promotions, withheld ranks, and honorary transfers. All in favor, raise your right hand."

Twenty two hands rose cleanly. Kong Weimin and Vice Chairman Liao Guohua kept their hands down, then stiffly lifted them halfway when Premier Gu Chengshan called for abstentions. Zero voted against.

"Formal vote recorded in the sealed state minutes: twenty two in favor, two abstentions, zero against," the chief clerk read aloud. "Abstaining members logged verbatim by full name: Kong Weimin and Liao Guohua."

Kong Weimin shivered, knowing that entry was a slow political death sentence.

Premier Gu Chengshan signed the order for the red seal. "Move. Not one word leaves this hall."

In the outer ring, the bank presidents and ministers closed their notebooks as the state moved its blade behind a wall of 300 billion yuan.
