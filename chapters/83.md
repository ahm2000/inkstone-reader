# Chapter 83: The Second Cross-Department War Room: Financial Firebreak

Cigarette smoke hung thick inside Beijing's second war room, turning the ceiling lamps into pale lanterns. Seven glass ashtrays lined the long dark table, every one piled high with crushed butts.

Premier Gu Chengshan sat at the head with his sleeves rolled up. Beside him sat Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, rolling a heavy brass lighter between his fingers. Flanking them in the senior chairs sat two white-haired Founding Elders: Elder Lin, who had built the state's financial backbone, and Elder Ye, whose sharp eyes carried thirty years of frost from the northern border.

Across from them sat the heads of the Four Major National Banks: Fang Zhengming of China Construction Bank, Han Guoliang of ICBC, Tang Wenhao of Bank of China, and Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank. Beside them sat People's Bank of China Governor Qu Shilin, Finance Minister Fei Yaoting, Customs Director Ou Guanghui, Commerce Minister Ruan Defu, and State Security Minister Ding Guoping. At the lower end sat two second-tier family representatives from the national business council, Zhao Mingyuan and Xu Chengwen.

On the screen glowed a single red figure: 100 trillion yuan in twenty years of hidden money trails.

"Premier Gu Chengshan, Elder Lin, Elder Ye, let us be practical," Zhao Mingyuan spoke first, adjusting his glasses with a dry cough. "Nobody defends Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang or those three retired bank heads, Cao Wenhui, Zheng Rongkui, and Pan Desheng. My only worry is timing."

Xu Chengwen nodded fast, tapping his pen on a red folder. "Those dirty funds are woven straight into city bonds, local credit banks, and forty-seven listed firms. Freeze 100 trillion yuan overnight and twenty-two provincial loan groups snap before Friday. Savers will line up outside banks by noon."

Commerce Minister Ruan Defu wiped his brow. "Twelve trading firms tied to Geneva hold active bank papers for grain and gas ships. Pull the plug too fast and ports from Ningbo to Qingdao stop unloading."

"So what do you suggest?" Customs Director Ou Guanghui scoffed, crushing his cigarette. "Let Shengyuan Capital Chairman Fang Qiming keep shipping stolen army metals because your friends' shares might catch a cold?"

"Watch your mouth, Ou Guanghui!" Xu Chengwen snapped, his neck flushing red. "Some of those provincial trusts are simply too big to fail! Break them in one night and the whole financial roof drops on our heads!"

Elder Ye let out a short, cold laugh. Every man at the lower end stiffened.

"Too big to fail," Elder Ye repeated, blowing gray smoke across his teacup. "Xu Chengwen, your cousin sits on two of those trusts in Sha Province. Zhao Mingyuan, your family's shipping firm shares three port leases with Deng Yongchang's shell companies. Are you worried about factory workers' savings, or your own cousins' golden bowls?"

Zhao Mingyuan paled. "Elder Ye, that is unfair! I am asking a plain question: where will the state find enough clean cash to plug a 100 trillion yuan hole overnight?"

Silence dropped like an iron lid.

Elder Lin slowly tapped his cigarette ash into a saucer, his eyes amused as they swept from Zhao Mingyuan to Xu Chengwen.

"Who told you the state is short of cash?" Elder Lin asked softly.

Zhao Mingyuan blinked. "Even the central bank's emergency reserves cannot absorb a hundred-trillion shock in two days without shaking the yuan."

"We are not touching the old reserves," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said flatly, setting his lighter down with a crisp click. "Open the blue folder."

Paper rustled through the haze. The moment Zhao Mingyuan and Xu Chengwen read the first page, both men froze as if drenched in ice water.

"300 trillion yuan?" Xu Chengwen whispered, his voice cracking. "Clean, legal, debt-free? Handed straight to the state for free?"

"With up to 500 trillion yuan standing ready if the banks need a single extra fen," Elder Lin said calmly. "No loans. No foreign strings. No interest. No favors asked. Every yuan passed full legal checks at noon."

Zhou Zhenhua looked at the four bank presidents. "Confirm it for the room."

Fang Zhengming of China Construction Bank opened his binder steadily. "75 trillion yuan in clean liquid reserves cleared into our main Beijing vault two hours ago. That covers every city bond, county credit union, and state payroll account under our network five times over."

Han Guoliang of ICBC spoke next with quiet pride. "Another 75 trillion yuan sits verified on ICBC's national clearing books. If every saver in twelve provinces demanded cash tomorrow morning, we could pay every last one before lunch without blinking."

Tang Wenhao of Bank of China smiled coldly. "75 trillion yuan in clean domestic and foreign settlement reserves sits locked at our international desk. If foreign short-sellers in London or Hong Kong attack the yuan when we freeze Geneva's dirty accounts, Bank of China will bury them before the afternoon bell."

Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank closed his folder neatly. "The final 75 trillion yuan is active across Huaxia Bank's trade and port credit lines. Minister Ruan Defu, move every grain and gas ship onto our books tonight. Not one dock loses a single minute."

Dead silence filled the smoky room.

Zhao Mingyuan stared down at the blue folder, his fingers trembling. Beside him, Xu Chengwen's face turned the color of cold ash. The oldest shield in Beijing politics, the threat that tearing out dirty roots would break the banks, had just been smashed to dust.

"No corrupt faction can hide behind too big to fail anymore," Elder Lin said quietly, his eyes hard as jade. "That 300 trillion yuan shield is thicker than steel. Anyone who thought the state would blink can start packing his winter coat for prison."

State Security Minister Ding Guoping rested a hand on his black-tabbed file. "With the banks covered, my officers do not wait an hour. We have the vault codes from Black Guard's files. Lock the shell firms tonight before Zurich middleman Lukas Weber burns the rest."

"Good," Zhou Zhenhua said, walking to the screen. "At four o'clock sharp, no public freeze notices and no press statements. Qu Shilin and Fei Yaoting activate emergency rule seven behind the scenes, backed by the 300 trillion yuan free reserve across China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank. Every tainted trust and shell share gets taken over quietly in four clean steps."

Finance Minister Fei Yaoting wrote fast. "And the front men behind Cao Wenhui, Fang Qiming, and Geneva?"

"Their claim disappears inside the bank system overnight," Zhou Zhenhua replied coldly. "Tomorrow morning they try to wire one yuan, find the state treasury owns it, and hear Ding Guoping's officers knocking at their doors."

Premier Gu Chengshan uncapped his pen, letting his heavy gaze linger on Zhao Mingyuan and Xu Chengwen.

"We are cutting out 100 trillion yuan of twenty-year rot behind a 300 trillion yuan shield," Gu Chengshan declared. "Not one bank run. Not one scared saver. Anyone who leaks a word to a market desk or a relative joins He Guoliang in the basement before midnight."

He signed the order with one firm stroke and slid it across the wood.

"Move," Gu Chengshan ordered. "Before London opens."
