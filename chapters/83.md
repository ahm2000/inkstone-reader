# Chapter 83: The Second Cross-Department War Room: Financial Firebreak

By two o'clock on Monday afternoon, the second war room in the capital's central compound was thick with cigarette smoke. The ceiling lamps looked like pale lanterns in fog. Seven ashtrays lined the long dark table, every one piled high with crushed butts.

Premier Gu Chengshan sat at the head of the table with his jacket off and his sleeves rolled up. He listened in heavy silence as the morning's questioning reports from Gao Zhengyi and Guo Zhengming were handed out in red folders. Beside him, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua rolled a heavy brass lighter between his fingers, his eyes fixed on the screen at the far end of the room.

On the screen stood a single red number that had drained the blood from every official's face: 100 trillion yuan in twenty years of hidden money trails. It stretched across local savings trusts, foreign bank papers, secret factory shares, and hidden Swiss accounts.

People's Bank of China Governor Qu Shilin was the first to speak, his voice tight and strained. "Premier Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, I have to be honest. If we freeze every linked bank account tonight under a public order, the bank-to-bank loan system will lock up before the Shanghai stock market opens tomorrow morning."

Finance Minister Fei Yaoting nodded sharply, tapping his fingers fast against his folder. "Qu Shilin is right. Those three retired bank presidents He Guoliang named did not hide their money in empty boxes. Cao Wenhui, Zheng Rongkui, and Pan Desheng wove those hidden debts straight into city building bonds, local credit banks, and public savings funds. If we pull the plug in public, twenty-two provincial loan groups will fail by Friday. Ordinary people will line up outside bank doors before noon."

China Securities Regulatory Commission Chairman Shu Yanting leaned forward, sweat shining on his forehead. "My team ran a test run an hour ago. Even a leaked rumor of a 100 trillion yuan freeze will wipe twelve percent off the main stock market in two days. Foreign sellers in Hong Kong and Singapore will pile in the second they smell a cash crunch."

Customs Director Ou Guanghui scoffed loudly and crushed his cigarette so hard the filter split. "So what are the finance offices suggesting? We let Fang Qiming's tax-free export docks keep shipping stolen army metals because the stock market might catch a cold?"

"Nobody said let them move a single gram of metal, Ou Guanghui!" Shu Yanting snapped back, his face turning red. "Customs can lock the docks quietly! I am talking about publicly freezing three hundred local trust firms and forty-seven listed shell companies all at once!"

Commerce Minister Ruan Defu raised a hand, his voice tense. "Shu Yanting is not overstating it. Twelve of those foreign trading firms tied to Geneva hold active bank papers for grain and natural gas imports. If we hit them with public bans without a backup payment channel, ports from Ningbo to Qingdao will stop unloading ships by Wednesday."

National Development and Reform Commission Director Tong Zhaoxing blew out a long cloud of smoke, his shoulders hunched. "And six provinces have county wrecking jobs tied to Shengyuan Capital. If local builders stop getting paid overnight, migrant work crews will block county government gates before the week ends."

Across the table, State Security Minister Ding Guoping had not touched his teacup. He spoke quietly, his voice cutting straight through the argument. "Every hour we wait to lock those local shell firms, Lukas Weber's leaders in Zurich can burn the remaining computer files and pull the cash out through Tokyo and London. We already have the vault codes from Zero Guard's files. Waiting for a polite market window is handing them an open door to run."

The room broke into loud, overlapping voices. Qu Shilin argued with Ding Guoping over bank cutoffs. Fei Yaoting and Ou Guanghui traded sharp words over port authority. Shu Yanting muttered angrily while flipping through his charts.

Crack.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua slammed his heavy brass lighter onto the wood table. The sharp metal bang echoed off the walls, cutting every voice dead in a single heartbeat.

Every minister froze. Smoke curled slowly up from forgotten cigarettes held in shaking fingers.

Zhou Zhenhua looked slowly down the length of the table, his face hard as stone. "Are you finished panicking?"

Nobody answered. Shu Yanting swallowed and looked down at his notepad.

"Listen to yourselves," Zhou Zhenhua said, his voice low and sharp. "Half of you are acting like we have to choose between letting traitors bleed the country dry or blowing up our own banks on the evening news. Who told you this has to be announced on television?"

Premier Gu Chengshan nodded once, his dark eyes steady. "Speak, Zhou Zhenhua."

"We build a silent firewall," Zhou Zhenhua said, standing up and walking to the screen. "Starting at four o'clock this afternoon, no public freeze notices, no press statements, and no sudden stops on people pulling out their savings. Instead, Qu Shilin and Fei Yaoting turn on the national backup fund under emergency rule seven."

Qu Shilin blinked and sat up straighter. "Emergency rule seven? Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, that moves true ownership of those troubled accounts straight to the central bank behind the scenes."

"Exactly," Zhou Zhenhua said sharply. "Every single tainted trust fund, off-book credit bank, and shell company share gets taken over quietly by the national fund in four quiet steps over thirty days. On the surface, bank transfers stay green. Retired workers get their pensions on time. County builders get their Friday pay from replacement state building crews sent in by Tong Zhaoxing. Ordinary people across the country will not feel a single bump."

Fei Yaoting's eyes narrowed as he began writing fast on his pad. "And the real owners behind those shell firms? The local front men for Cao Wenhui, Fang Qiming, and the Geneva channel?"

"Their claim to the money is wiped out silently inside the main bank system," Zhou Zhenhua replied coldly. "They do not get a court notice in the paper. They wake up tomorrow morning, try to wire a single yuan out of their hidden accounts, and find out the state treasury owns it while Ding Guoping's officers are already knocking on their front doors."

Ruan Defu let out a slow breath, the tight knot leaving his jaw. "If Commerce moves the grain and gas bank papers onto state bank books overnight before Ou Guanghui seals the export docks, the ports will not lose a single hour."

"Customs can lock every dock tied to Shengyuan Capital at midnight," Ou Guanghui agreed, his voice grimly pleased. "Quietly. Under routine health and safety checks."

Premier Gu Chengshan picked up his fountain pen, took off the cap, and looked around the smoky room. Every department head sat up straight.

"Hear this clearly," Gu Chengshan said, his voice calm yet carrying the full weight of the state. "We are cutting out a twenty-year rot worth 100 trillion yuan in hidden claims. Not one bank run. Not one scared saver. Not one ordinary family losing its savings because parasites hid behind their accounts. Anyone who leaks a single word from this room to a market desk or an old friend will join He Guoliang in the basement before midnight."

He signed the national fund order with one firm stroke and slid it to Zhou Zhenhua.

"Move," Gu Chengshan ordered. "Before the London market opens."
