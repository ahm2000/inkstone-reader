# Chapter 45: Miss Qin Next Week

On Wednesday, Zhang Pengfei came to work early, and nobody could tell what was wrong with him.

Then Pan Xiulan worked it out. "He is not wearing the watch. He is not talking. Is he sick?"

"It is his wife's uncle," Lin Dong whispered. "The Port auditors are still there. Deng Weidong has not been home in three days."

At ten, Chen Xuan went to the pantry to fill his thermos. Zhang Pengfei was standing beside the water machine as if he had been planted there.

"Supervisor Chen."

"Morning, Zhang Pengfei. The water is hot, so be careful."

Zhang Pengfei bowed. It was a real bow, from the waist, and his hands shook.

"Please. My wife cried all night. Her uncle is a good man. He just stamped what was put in front of him. If you know someone, a friend who knows forms, please."

Chen Xuan screwed the lid on his thermos. He took his time, and when he looked up, his face was kind and a little tired.

"I only checked a form, Zhang Pengfei. That is all I did."

"I know you say that."

"It is also true." Chen Xuan shrugged. "If your wife's uncle tells the auditors everything, the law will look at that. It looks at that more than people think. That's the only advice I have. I wish it was a better one."

Zhang Pengfei stood with his bowed head for a moment longer. He did not believe it. Nobody in the building would have believed it. But for the first time in a month, he did not argue.

"Thank you," he said. He meant it, and it was strange to him how much.

When he left, Chen Xuan stood alone with the water machine and rubbed the back of his neck.

"Zhang Pengfei bowed to me," he told the machine. "Why does everybody bow this week?"

The machine hissed and said nothing.

* * *

At three, Hu Dewei called the whole floor together. He had a new tie on, and it was crooked.

"Listen, everyone. On Monday at noon, a guest will visit us. She is Miss Qin Yuhan, from the Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group."

Nobody breathed. Haiyue owned hotels in three provinces. Everyone knew that the Yunhai Grand Hotel was part of its family.

"A partner lunch," Hu Dewei went on. "Sun Haoran has not slept well since Tuesday. Shoes polished, tie straight, no jokes about the cafeteria. And one more thing."

He took a very deep breath.

"This quarter's prize lunch goes to the first-place supervisor. This time, the guest asked to sit with the winner. That is Chen Xuan."

Thirty heads turned. Chen Xuan was peeling an orange.

"Do I need a tie?" he asked.

"A tie, a pressed shirt and a calm face," Hu Dewei said. His voice cracked a little. "Please be calm."

"I only have gray shirts."

"Then wear the gray one politely."

Pan Xiulan was already circling his desk like a tailor. "The collar is soft, and the cuff has a thread. I will bring an iron. Do you have shoes?"

"I have shoes."

"Show me the shoes."

Chen Xuan lifted one foot. It was a clean, flat, brown shoe with a patch of glue near the toe. Pan Xiulan closed her eyes for a moment, like a woman praying.

Pan Xiulan clapped both hands over her mouth. "Chen Xuan is having lunch with Haiyue."

"It is just lunch," Chen Xuan said.

"Haiyue pays eight hundred thousand a year for their top chef," Lin Dong whispered.

Chen Xuan ate a slice of orange and nodded. Somebody had told him that the lunch would be free, and he had no further questions.

* * *

On the fortieth floor of the Haiyue tower, Qin Yuhan took off her glasses and rubbed her eyes.

She was twenty-eight. She was calm, quick to laugh, and very tired of suitors. Three had phoned that week. One had sent a basket of fruit with a poem in it.

Her assistant, Jin Ke, set a thin file on her desk. "You asked about Hengxin Logistics. It is a small firm. Its top supervisor this quarter is Chen Xuan."

Qin Yuhan put her glasses back on and read. *Chen Xuan, 29. Operations supervisor. 12,000 yuan a month. Born in Yunxi County. Honest, quiet, no debts, no property, no connections. Simple.*

She read it twice and laughed out loud.

"No connections," she said. "And yet the General Manager of the Yunhai Grand Hotel bowed to him in front of twenty people."

"That is the story, Miss Qin."

"I do not believe in files that clean." She tapped the page. "Nobody is that boring."

Her phone buzzed. The name on it was Grandpa. She picked up.

"Yuhan." Her grandfather's voice was dry and warm. "I hear a clerk made Dai Wenbin bow. Go and see him."

"Grandpa, it is only a partner lunch."

"Then it is a lunch. Go and see the clerk."

He hung up before she could argue. Qin Yuhan looked at the file for a long time. In the photo, a young man in a gray jacket was holding a steamed bun and looking slightly to the left of the camera, like somebody who had just remembered an errand.

She smiled, and was surprised that she had.

"Miss Qin," Jin Ke said carefully, "he is a clerk. A kind of man who does not wear a watch."

"Good," Qin Yuhan said. "My last three suitors wore four between them."

"Jin Ke. Monday noon, a small table, no suitors. And no tea ceremony. I want to see what he does with a bun."

* * *

In the courtyard house in Qingyan, Number Fourteen's report lay on Number One's desk. It was three lines long.

*Haiyue's guest asked for the Boss by title. Miss Qin Yuhan. She read his file and laughed.*

Number Seven looked up. "Do we tell the Boss?"

"Nothing," Number One said. "Let it be ordinary."

"Number One, the Boss has never had a lunch like this one."

"That is why." Number One closed the folder. "He eats best when nobody is watching how he eats. Let it be ordinary."

* * *

On Thursday, when Chen Xuan sat down at his desk, his chair was warm.

A small heat pad lay on the seat, plugged into a thin cord that ran down to the floor. A square of paper sat beside it. It had one word, in careful, round handwriting.

*Eat.*

Ge Qinghe was mopping near the pillar. She did not look up.

"Auntie Ge." Chen Xuan held up the note. "This is the kindest thing anybody wrote to me this week."

"You are too thin," Ge Qinghe said to the mop. "My own son was like you. Always forgot lunch. Eat and shut up."

He laughed and sat down. The pad was warm all through his back, and for a second he felt about eight years old.

Then he reached for his thermos and found something under the lid. It was a cream-colored card. The writing was neat and fast.

*Lunch Monday, noon. Q.*

He turned it over. The back was blank.

"Hmm?" Chen Xuan said, to nobody at all.
