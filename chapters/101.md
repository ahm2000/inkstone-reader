# Chapter 101: The Husband Who Rides the Subway

The next morning, the kitchen at Emerald Riverside Villa smelled of toasted sesame and hot jasmine tea. Through the slightly cracked window above the sink drifted the quiet hush of the riverside courtyard.

Chen Xuan stood by the stove in a soft gray cotton sweater, flipping two golden scallion pancakes in a flat pan. Beside the kettle sat his dented green metal thermos, its lid unscrewed so the rising steam warmed the rim before he poured his tea.

Behind him, crisp heels clicked down the oak staircase.

Qin Yuhan walked into the kitchen wearing a sharp ivory blazer and tailored navy trousers. Her long dark hair was pinned up neatly, giving her every bit of the cool, decisive grace of the president of Haiyue Group. In her right hand, however, two leather key fobs jingled softly.

She stopped beside the kitchen island, placed both keys on the polished marble, and slid them across toward Chen Xuan.

"Pick one," Qin Yuhan said, trying to sound strictly businesslike even as her eyes drifted toward the sizzling pan. "The white Porsche is smoother in city traffic, and the black sedan has heated seats for chilly autumn mornings. Both are sitting idle in the garage anyway."

Chen Xuan turned off the burner, slid the crispy pancakes onto two warm ceramic plates, and carried them to the island. He poured two cups of hot soy milk, set one beside her, and glanced down at the shiny silver emblems on the marble.

"Neither," Chen Xuan said easily, sitting down and breaking off a corner of pancake. "Eat while it is hot. The edges go soft if you wait."

Qin Yuhan blinked, resting her chin in one palm as she stared at him. "Chen Xuan, Hengxin Logistics is twenty minutes down the avenue through rush-hour traffic. You ride Metro Line 2 every morning during the rush. Why squeeze into a train car when two cars are parked ten steps from our front door?"

"Simple math, President Qin," Chen Xuan replied, sipping his soy milk. "Driving into Lujiazui during morning traffic means forty minutes of staring at red brake lights and listening to angry horns. Then the underground lot near my office charges 60 yuan a day for parking. That is more than a good plate of braised pork ribs."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft laugh despite herself. "I own a shipping and logistics group worth tens of billions, and my husband is calculating a 60-yuan parking fee."

"Metro Line 2 takes twenty-two minutes from door to door," Chen Xuan continued calmly, ticking points off on his fingers. "The ticket costs 4 yuan. The air conditioning works, nobody cuts me off at an intersection, and I can close my eyes and rest for twenty minutes while holding my thermos. That is what smart living looks like."

Qin Yuhan shook her head, her cool corporate expression melting into helpless amusement. She took a bite of the scallion pancake. The outer crust crackled lightly, salty with green onions and sesame oil, and she immediately forgot her lecture.

"Fine," she murmured, chewing happily. "Ride your subway. Just promise me you will take a taxi if it rains."

"Deal," Chen Xuan smiled. "As long as you promise not to skip lunch again when your afternoon meetings run long."

"I already told Lin Wei to order hot soup for my office," Qin Yuhan said. She finished her soy milk, wiped her lips with a napkin, and stepped around the island before heading to the foyer. Reaching up, she straightened the collar of his gray sweater with gentle fingers. "See you tonight."

"See you tonight," Chen Xuan said softly.

A few minutes later, Qin Yuhan's black company sedan glided smoothly out of the villa gates toward Haiyue Tower. Chen Xuan rinsed both plates, placed them neatly in the drying rack, screwed the cap tight onto his green metal thermos, and slung his plain canvas bag over his shoulder.

He strolled down the quiet tree-lined lane outside Emerald Riverside Villa. Golden ginkgo leaves rustled along the stone curb beneath his sneakers. Ahead, the glass canopy of the subway station gleamed in the clear autumn sunlight.

Two blocks back, parked unobtrusively beneath a row of tall maples, sat a spotless pearl-white sedan with tinted windows.

Inside the cabin, Number 1 sat in the front passenger seat, his posture straight and calm as he watched Chen Xuan's relaxed figure disappear down the subway stairs. Behind the steering wheel, Number 7 glanced at a white tablet displaying real-time traffic routes across Pudong.

"The White Order convoy is cleared on three alternate avenues," Number 7 said quietly, his eyes steady on the screen. "If Young Master ever decides to ride instead of taking the train, we can pull up to the curb within ninety seconds."

Number 1 let a faint, respectful smile touch his lips. "Keep the convoy out of sight, Number 7. Young Master enjoys his quiet morning walk and his simple desk. Our duty is to make sure nothing in this city ever interrupts his peace."

"Understood," Number 7 replied softly.

Down inside the bright subway station, Chen Xuan tapped his transit card through the turnstile and stepped onto the platform as the train rolled in with a gentle rush of air. Nearby in the morning crowd, a college student wearing headphones and a friendly uncle holding a folded newspaper stepped smoothly into the same car, blending effortlessly into the Shanghai commuters.

Chen Xuan found a spot near the glass partition, hooked one hand around an overhead strap, and tucked his warm green thermos against his chest. As the train glided smoothly toward Lujiazui and his corner desk at Hengxin Logistics, he closed his eyes and smiled.

A warm breakfast, a gentle wife, and a 4-yuan ride to work. In his book, no throne on earth beat this.
