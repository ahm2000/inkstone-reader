# Chapter 101: The First Sealed Trial in the Capital

Heavy gray curtains covered every window of the closed courtroom on the third floor of the Supreme Court building. No reporters sat on the benches, no cameras lined the aisle, and no crowd waited outside. Only armed guards stood along the back wall.

Behind the wooden rail stood five men who, two weeks ago, could move billions of yuan with one call. Four were former state-bank chiefs: Sun Jianming, Luo Wenbin, Fang Peilin, and Qian Zhigang. They wore plain gray cotton jackets without ties, shoulders slumped and faces pale under the ceiling lights.

At the center stood former Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang. Unlike the four bankers, He Guoliang kept his chin raised, graying hair combed neatly, fingers gripping the rail as if behind a podium at a national meeting.

On the raised bench sat Supreme Court Chief Judge Xu Zhengping with two associate judges. Across the aisle, Lead CCDI Prosecutor Gao Mingyuan sat behind a tall stack of thick blue folders.

"He Guoliang," Chief Judge Xu Zhengping said flatly. "You have heard the charges. Over nine years, you approved nineteen overseas energy loans and coastal power-grid deals that stripped more than 14 billion yuan from state companies. Do you admit these facts?"

He Guoliang let out a slow breath, as if explaining a small mistake to a junior clerk. "Chief Judge Xu Zhengping, I do not deny my signature is on those sheets. I was Vice Minister of Energy. Signing large energy deals was my duty. When a state project loses money overseas, that is a business risk, not a crime."

Gao Mingyuan did not look up from his folder, turning one page. "A business risk does not send 14 billion yuan into four empty shell companies in Zurich, He Guoliang."

"I did not know who owned those foreign firms," He Guoliang replied sharply, eyes narrowing. "I am an energy official, not a bank auditor. The bank chiefs beside me checked the foreign partners. More than that, every major loan on that list was pushed through on direct verbal orders from my former superior, retired Deputy Director Ma Rongsheng."

A quiet stir moved through the room. Beside He Guoliang, Sun Jianming flinched and stared at his shoes.

Gao Mingyuan raised his eyes, calm and steady. "You are telling this court that retired Deputy Director Ma Rongsheng forced you to sign all nineteen approvals, and that you never took a single yuan for yourself?"

"That is the exact truth," He Guoliang said with practiced confidence. "Ma Rongsheng had thirty years of seniority over me. When he called and said a foreign partner had backing from the old leadership, I had to sign. Check my apartment in the capital. Check my wife's bank card. You will find nothing except my salary."

"We already checked your apartment," Gao Mingyuan said softly. "You kept only 40,000 yuan in cash there, neatly wrapped, in case investigators ever knocked."

He Guoliang's jaw tightened, though he held his composure. "Then you have your proof. I lived cleanly."

Gao Mingyuan stood up, picked up a thin red-bordered folder from the bottom of his stack, and stepped closer to the rail.

"Deputy Director Ma Rongsheng retired on March twelfth, four years ago," Gao Mingyuan said clearly. "That afternoon, he handed back his seal and lost all authority over energy approvals. Yet here in my hand are three transfer records from the Zurich Northern Ledger."

Color drained from He Guoliang's cheeks. His knuckles turned pale on the wooden rail.

"I do not know what a Zurich Northern Ledger is," He Guoliang said quickly. "Anyone can type my name onto a foreign sheet of paper."

"Let me read the dates aloud so you remember," Gao Mingyuan said, opening the red folder. "June fourth, three years ago: 180 million yuan moved from the Southern Coastal Grid fund through a Hong Kong account into Zurich Account Number 7741. Fifteen months after Ma Rongsheng retired."

He Guoliang opened his mouth, but Gao Mingyuan kept reading.

"November nineteenth, two years ago: 420 million yuan shifted out of a state coal-shipping loan into that same Zurich account. And February eighth of this year: 1.2 billion yuan paid in one afternoon as a fake equipment penalty to a Swiss firm controlled by that exact account."

"That proves nothing!" He Guoliang barked, his smooth manner cracking. "Show me where my name is on Account Number 7741!"

"Your name is not on it," Gao Mingyuan answered calmly. "Your thumbprint is."

The courtroom fell completely silent.

Gao Mingyuan held up a clear plastic sheet showing a sharp photograph of a dark leather ledger page. "Four nights ago, investigators opened a hidden wall safe inside a lakeside villa in Hangzhou registered to your brother-in-law. Inside were a Swiss bank key and a thumbprint-locked verification card. Your right thumb opened it in two seconds. That account holds 1.8 billion yuan in gold certificates and foreign currency, waiting for your son in Geneva."

He Guoliang stared at the photograph. For three long seconds no sound came out. Sweat rolled down his temple.

"That villa," He Guoliang whispered hoarsely, "my brother-in-law bought it on his own. I never set foot in it."

Beside him, Sun Jianming suddenly lifted his head, face gray with fear, voice trembling as he spoke to the bench.

"Chief Judge Xu Zhengping, he is lying!" Sun Jianming blurted out, pointing a shaking hand at He Guoliang. "On February sixth of this year, He Guoliang called me into his private tea room behind the ministry. He said if my bank did not approve a 650 million yuan emergency credit line before noon on the eighth, he would remove me by Friday. He handed me the Zurich account slip written in his own hand!"

"Sun Jianming, shut your mouth!" He Guoliang snapped, whipping his head around.

"He did the same to me!" Luo Wenbin cried from the end of the row, voice cracking. "He promised the foreign partners were untouchable! He said nobody in Beijing would ever dare touch the Zurich accounts!"

"Silence in the dock," Chief Judge Xu Zhengping ordered, cold as iron.

He Guoliang stopped looking at the other bankers and stared at the red folder in Gao Mingyuan's hand. His shoulders caved inward all at once, as if the last beam holding him upright had snapped. His knees buckled against the rail, catching himself with both palms.

"I," He Guoliang began, voice thin and broken, wiping sweat from his chin with a trembling hand. "I want to talk. I will tell you everything. Every date, every middleman, every foreign contact who came to my office. Just put it on the record."

Gao Mingyuan closed the red folder and set it on his desk. "You will have plenty of time to put every name on the record, He Guoliang. Starting tonight."

Chief Judge Xu Zhengping brought the gavel down with a sharp crack that echoed through the sealed hall.

"Record the defendant's change of plea," Xu Zhengping ordered. "This trial continues behind closed doors until every last account is accounted for."
