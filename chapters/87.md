# Chapter 87: The White Guard Stand-Down Lifts

Inside the windowless basement command room of White Guard's Jingzhou City safehouse, the red digits on the wall clock crept toward 5:00 PM. For sixteen straight hours, every wide monitor along the steel desk had displayed nothing but a flat grey lock screen. The only sound in the concrete room was the steady hum of cooling fans.

Number 7 sat upright in a steel folding chair with both palms resting flat on his knees. Beside him, Number 11 stared at a cold paper cup of water he had not touched since dawn. His shoulders ached from holding the same stiff posture, yet he did not dare stretch his neck. Neither man looked straight at the reinforced steel door across the room.

Beside that door stood two figures in plain black gear, dark masks hiding their faces, compact rifles hanging quiet against their chests. Neither figure had spoken a single word since walking through the safehouse's triple-locked front hall the night before. They had not shifted their boots, checked a watch, or glanced at the monitors. Even their breathing was lost under the soft hum of the computers.

The red digits on the wall clock flipped cleanly to 5:00 PM.

With no chime, no radio click, and no hand signal between them, both black-clad figures turned in perfect unison. One pulled the heavy steel latch open. They stepped out into the dim corridor, pulled the door shut behind them, and vanished without a footstep echoing back through the concrete.

Five seconds later, the bank of monitors flickered awake. The grey lock bars disappeared, replaced by live maps of Handong Province, secure comms feeds, and encrypted status logs.

Number 11 let out a long, ragged breath and slumped forward, rubbing his stiff wrists. "Sixteen hours," he muttered under his breath. "They walked past three biometric locks as if the doors were propped open with bricks. Not even a single word."

Before Number 7 could answer, the plain white landline bolted to the center steel desk rang once.

Number 7 was on his feet instantly. He pressed the speaker button and stood rigid at attention, his spine straight as a rifle barrel.

"Number 7 reporting," he said clearly.

A calm, heavy voice came through the speaker, carrying unmistakable authority. "The nationwide stand-down has lifted. Confirm perimeter status in Jingzhou City."

It was Number 1.

"Safehouse systems are back online," Number 7 answered at once. "All twelve local units in Handong Province are checking in clean. No casualties, no leaks, and zero unauthorized movement during the lockdown."

Number 11 stepped closer to the desk, still glancing uneasily toward the closed steel door. "Number 1, the two men in plain black gear just left at 5:00 PM sharp. Should I pull the alley cameras outside the east exit to log their plates and track which division they belong to?"

The line went dead silent for two full seconds. When Number 1 spoke again, his voice had dropped ten degrees colder.

"Number 11," Number 1 said, every word sharp as broken glass. "Listen to me very carefully. Delete the outside camera cache from the last twenty-four hours right now. Do not log them. Do not trace them. Do not even whisper about them on an encrypted channel."

Number 11 stiffened, color draining from his face. "Understood, Number 1. Deleting the cache now."

"Do not mistake our rank in the shadows for the top of the mountain," Number 1 warned evenly. "The Black Guard answers solely to Young Master. When the Black Order moves, even I step aside and keep my hands visible. You will remember that for the rest of your service."

"Yes, Number 1," both men answered in unison.

Number 7 lowered his gaze to the live province map glowing across the center monitor. "How is the cleanup moving in Handong Province?"

"Smoothly," Number 1 replied. "Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin and Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang have locked down the provincial apparatus. Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping and Chief Procurator Ji Changming already have Deputy Provincial Secretary Gao Yuliang and Shanshui Group head Zhao Ruilong in full custody. Public Security Director Qi Tongwei's remaining network is being dismantled precinct by precinct."

Number 11 typed swiftly at the side terminal, wiping the alley camera drives clean, then glanced back at the speaker. "What about the remaining Handa Faction assets around Jingzhou City? Some of Zhao Ruilong's private enforcers are still trying to move cash out of Shanshui Group shell accounts."

"Leave them to Hou Liangping's prosecutors and Beijing's discipline teams," Number 1 ordered. "Young Master does not want the White Guard stepping into the spotlight. Pull every field team back to quiet perimeter watch. Keep every trace of the Shanshui Group mess completely away from Yunxi County."

"Clear, Number 1," Number 7 said. "We will hold the outer ring and wait for Young Master's next word."

"Good. Keep your people disciplined. Beijing is voting on a national clean-up decree tonight, and every eye in the provinces will be twitching at shadows. We stay invisible until Young Master calls."

The secure line clicked dead.

The basement command room fell quiet again, lit only by the cool blue glow of the active monitors. Number 11 let out another slow breath and leaned against the edge of the steel desk.

"I used to think the White Guard stood at the ceiling of the underworld," Number 11 said quietly, staring at the locked steel door. "Sixteen hours in a chair cured me of that."

Number 7 adjusted his collar and turned back toward the glowing map of Handong Province. "Keep your head down and do your job, Number 11. We are only the outer shield. Young Master sits in a world none of us will ever fully see."
