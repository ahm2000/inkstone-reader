# Chapter 87: The Black Pine Stand-Down Lifts

In the windowless basement command room of Black Pine Guard's Lanshui safehouse, the wall clock ticked past five in the afternoon. For sixteen hours, every monitor along the steel desk had shown nothing but a flat grey lock screen.

Number 7 sat rigid in a folding chair, hands flat on his knees. Beside him, Number 11 stared at a cold paper cup, shoulders knotted from holding the same posture since dawn. Neither man looked directly at the steel door, where two figures in featureless matte-black gear stood completely motionless.

Neither monitor had spoken a word since walking through the safehouse's triple-locked vestibule the night before. They had not shifted weight or checked a watch. Even their breathing was inaudible over the server hum.

At five-oh-four, with no chime, radio click, or gesture between them, both matte-black figures turned in exact unison.

One slid back the heavy steel latch. The two sentries stepped into the concrete corridor one after the other and pulled the door shut without a sound.

For three full seconds, nobody breathed.

Then Number 11 sprang up, crossed the room in three silent strides, and pressed his eye to the peephole. He froze, then stepped back slowly, his face pale under the strip light.

"Empty," Number 11 whispered. "Thirty meters of straight corridor and a weighted stairwell door. They did not even trip the latch."

Before Number 7 could speak, the entire wall of encrypted screens flashed white and roared back to life.

Cooling fans spun up. Green status lights cascaded down the server rack, and the national relay map snapped back onto the main display. Dormant Black Pine nodes across fourteen provinces blinked online together.

Number 11 exhaled shakily, pulled a crushed pack of cigarettes from his jacket, and jammed one between his lips. His lighter clicked twice before his thumb stopped trembling enough to catch the flame.

"Stand-down lifted," Number 7 said, voice rough from hours of silence. He flexed his stiff fingers and tapped the red handset as its green lamp began pulsing. "Number 1 is on the bridge."

He switched the receiver to speaker.

"Report," Number 1's voice came through the encrypted line. Steady on the surface, yet carrying a tight, dry edge beneath.

"Lanshui node is online," Number 7 answered crisply. "Both monitors walked out at five-oh-four without a word. All relays green. Zero hardware tampering."

"Same across the capital, coastal ports, and northern grid," Number 1 said. A faint lighter click carried over the channel. "Every station cleared at the exact same millisecond."

Number 11 drew hard on his cigarette and leaned over the console, eyes widening as live national wire digests began scrolling down the side monitor. "Number 1, are you seeing the central alerts?"

"Reading them now," Number 1 replied quietly.

"Forty-two sealed dossiers dropped straight onto the central secretariat," Number 11 read aloud, his voice dropping in sheer disbelief. "Deng Yongchang's offshore ledgers, twenty years of provincial graft, customs smuggling corridors, military procurement kickbacks, and three deputy backers in the capital. All dismantled. Four hours."

Number 7 stared at the scrolling text, jaw tightening. "Four hours," he echoed softly. "Black Pine Guard spent six years mapping the outer shell of Deng Yongchang's southern chain. We could not touch the core without triggering a bureaucratic war across three provinces. They stripped the whole web to bare bone between midnight and four."

"Without one registered shot," Number 11 muttered. Ash fell from his cigarette onto the keyboard unnoticed. "Without our relays, our safehouses, or our informants. They locked us in our own rooms like children so we would stay out of the way."

"Watch your tone, Number 11," Number 1 said, no anger in his voice, only cold weight. "They locked us down because we would have gotten in the way. Do you know what happened when Zhao Dehai's people tried to mobilize in Lanshui and Yunxi last night?"

Number 7's eyes narrowed. "We caught fragments before the screens went grey."

"Every car lost ignition before clearing its garage," Number 1 said. "Every fixer who reached for a satellite phone found a matte-black figure inside his locked study. Nobody died unless they drew steel, and nobody drew steel twice. By sunrise, the provincial inspection group had handed Chen Mingde his exoneration certificate and brought him straight home to Old Street."

Number 11 let out a short, unsteady laugh that died in his throat. "Number 1, I have carried a Black Pine number for eleven years. I thought we were the sharpest blade in the dark. Who are they? How many Zero numbers even exist?"

Silence hummed across the encrypted line.

"I do not know," Number 1 said finally.

Number 11 froze with his cigarette halfway to his lips, staring at the speaker box. "You do not know? You coordinate the entire Black Pine network."

"And I have zero authority over them," Number 1 said flatly. "I have never seen their roster. I do not know where they stage, how they move, or who answers their line. Burn two rules into your skull right now, Number 11."

Number 7 straightened automatically. "Listening."

"First," Number 1 said, dropping his voice an octave, "they answer to nobody in the state, nobody in the military, and nobody in Black Pine Guard. They move only for Chen Xuan, and they do not wait for our permission to clear his doorstep."

Number 11 swallowed hard, smoke curling past his cheek. "And the second?"

"Never look for them," Number 1 said. "Do not pull street cameras outside the safehouse. Do not trace the lock-screen code they dropped on our consoles. Do not ask another operative what he saw. If a Zero Guard sentry stands in front of you, lower your eyes, keep your hands visible, and wait until he leaves."

"Understood," Number 7 said immediately. "Lanshui node is purging all local corridor feeds from the last twenty-four hours and logging nothing."

"Good," Number 1 replied. "Keep the provincial lines quiet. No chatter on the mesh, no unauthorized movement near Yunxi. Forty ministries are signing a joint clean-up directive in the capital tonight. Let the state chew through what Zero Guard left on their plate."

Number 11 crushed his half-smoked cigarette into the tin tray, fingers still stiff. "Does Chen Xuan know the stand-down has lifted?"

"I am calling him now," Number 1 said. "Stay at your desks and wait for my next ping."

The speaker clicked dead.

In the sudden quiet, Number 7 glanced at the closed steel door, then back at the glowing national map. "Twenty years of rot," he murmured, shaking his head. "Gone in four hours, and he is probably sweeping his uncle's threshold right now."

Number 11 did not laugh. He sat back down slowly, staring at the empty doorway where two unrecorded shadows had stood all day, and felt a cold chill settle deep in his spine.
