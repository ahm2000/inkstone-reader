# Chapter 87: The White Guard Stand-Down Lifts

In the windowless basement command room of White Guard's Lanshui safehouse, the wall clock ticked past five in the afternoon. For sixteen hours, every screen along the steel desk had shown nothing but a flat grey lock screen.

Number 7 sat stiffly in a folding chair with his hands flat on his knees. Beside him, Number 11 stared at a cold paper cup, his shoulders tight from holding the same pose since dawn. Neither man looked straight at the steel door, where two figures in plain black gear stood completely still.

Neither guard had spoken a single word since walking through the safehouse's triple-locked front hall the night before. They had not shifted their weight or checked a watch. Even their breathing could not be heard over the hum of the computers.

At five-oh-four, with no chime, radio click, or hand sign between them, both black-clad figures turned at the exact same instant.

One slid back the heavy steel latch. The two guards stepped into the concrete hall one after the other and pulled the door shut without a sound.

For three full seconds, nobody breathed.

Then Number 11 jumped up, crossed the room in three quiet steps, and pressed his eye to the peephole. He froze, then stepped back slowly, his face pale under the bright light.

"Empty," Number 11 whispered. "Thirty meters of straight hall and a heavy stair door. They did not even rattle the latch."

Before Number 7 could speak, the whole wall of screens flashed white and roared back to life.

Cooling fans spun up. Green lights raced down the computer rack, and the national map snapped back onto the main screen. Quiet White Guard stations across fourteen provinces blinked online together.

Number 11 let out a shaky breath. He pulled a crushed pack of cigarettes from his jacket and jammed one between his lips. His lighter clicked twice before his thumb stopped shaking enough to catch a flame.

"Stand-down is lifted," Number 7 said, his voice rough from hours of silence. He flexed his stiff fingers and tapped the red phone as its green light began to blink. "Number 1 is on the line."

He switched the phone to speaker.

"Report," Number 1's voice came through the private line. He sounded steady on the surface, yet a tight, dry edge sat underneath.

"Lanshui station is online," Number 7 answered crisply. "Both guards walked out at five-oh-four without a word. All lines are green. Zero damage to the gear."

"Same across the capital, coastal ports, and northern grid," Number 1 said. A faint lighter click came over the line. "Every station unlocked at the exact same split second."

Number 11 took a deep pull on his cigarette and leaned over the desk. His eyes went wide as live national alerts began scrolling down the side screen. "Number 1, are you seeing the capital alerts?"

"Reading them now," Number 1 replied quietly.

"Forty-two sealed cases dropped straight onto the central office," Number 11 read aloud, his voice dropping in pure shock. "Deng Yongchang's foreign bank books, twenty years of local bribes, customs smuggling routes, army supply kickbacks, and three top backers in the capital. All torn apart. Four hours."

Number 7 stared at the scrolling text, his jaw tightening. "Four hours," he repeated softly. "White Guard spent six years mapping the outer ring of Deng Yongchang's southern web. We could not touch the core without starting a huge official war across three provinces. They stripped the whole thing to bare bone between midnight and four."

"Without one registered shot," Number 11 muttered. Ash fell from his cigarette onto the keyboard unnoticed. "Without our lines, our safehouses, or our tipsters. They locked us in our own rooms like children just to keep us out of the way."

"Watch your tone, Number 11," Number 1 said. There was no anger in his voice, only cold weight. "They locked us down because we would have gotten in the way. Do you know what happened when Mo Tianyu's men tried to move in Lanshui and Yunxi last night?"

Number 7's eyes narrowed. "We caught bits of it before the screens went grey."

"Every car failed to start before leaving its garage," Number 1 said. "Every fixer who reached for a satellite phone found a black-clad figure standing inside his locked study. Nobody died unless they pulled a weapon, and nobody pulled a weapon twice. By sunrise, the provincial inspection team had handed Chen Mingde his cleared-name paper and brought him straight home to Old Street."

Number 11 let out a short, shaky laugh that died in his throat. "Number 1, I have carried a White Guard number for eleven years. I thought we were the sharpest blade in the dark. Who are they? How many Black numbers even exist?"

Silence hummed across the private line.

"I do not know," Number 1 said at last.

Number 11 froze with his cigarette halfway to his lips, staring at the speaker box. "You do not know? You run the whole White Guard network."

"And I have zero power over them," Number 1 said flatly. "I have never seen their list. I do not know where they wait, how they travel, or who answers their phone. Burn two rules into your head right now, Number 11."

Number 7 sat up straight at once. "Listening."

"First," Number 1 said, dropping his voice lower, "they answer to nobody in the government, nobody in the army, and nobody in White Guard. They move only for Chen Xuan, and they do not wait for our okay to clear his doorstep."

Number 11 swallowed hard, smoke curling past his cheek. "And the second?"

"Never look for them," Number 1 said. "Do not pull street cameras outside the safehouse. Do not trace the lock code they dropped on our screens. Do not ask another agent what he saw. If a Black Guard stands in front of you, lower your eyes, keep your hands where he can see them, and wait until he leaves."

"Understood," Number 7 said at once. "Lanshui station is wiping all hallway camera footage from the last twenty-four hours and logging nothing."

"Good," Number 1 replied. "Keep the local lines quiet. No talk on the network, no unplanned moves near Yunxi. Forty ministries are signing a joint clean-up order in the capital tonight. Let the state chew through what Black Guard left on their plate."

Number 11 crushed his half-smoked cigarette into the tin tray, his fingers still stiff. "Does Chen Xuan know the stand-down is over?"

"I am calling him now," Number 1 said. "Stay at your desks and wait for my next call."

The speaker clicked dead.

In the sudden quiet, Number 7 looked at the closed steel door, then back at the glowing national map. "Twenty years of rot," he murmured, shaking his head. "Gone in four hours, and he is probably sweeping his uncle's doorstep right now."

Number 11 did not laugh. He sat back down slowly, staring at the empty doorway where two unrecorded shadows had stood all day, and felt a cold chill settle deep in his spine.
