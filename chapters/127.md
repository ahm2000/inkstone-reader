# Chapter 127: The Reception Hall

By six thirty, the Grand Ballroom on the third floor of the Shanshui Villa glowed with light and the buzz of two hundred voices. Round linen tables stretched across the carpet toward a blue backdrop for the Handong Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum.

Sun Haoran stepped through the double doors in his neat navy suit. Beside him, Chen Xuan wore a white shirt beneath his plain dark windbreaker, his scratched green thermos in his left hand.

"Look at the turnout," Sun Haoran murmured. "That tall fellow near the front is the chairman of Donghai Shipping, and by the flowers is Director Lin from the provincial transport office."

"Try the braised beef at the buffet before the crowd finishes it," Chen Xuan suggested mildly. "The speeches usually run long."

Loud laughter rose from the center of the hall, where freight executives and reporters circled two men beneath the main chandelier: Cai Chenggong, Chairman of Wanfeng Freight, wearing a dark gray suit and a gold watch, and Deputy Mayor Ding Yizhen, a thick set man in his fifties with silver streaked hair.

"Gentlemen, the days of scattered shipping ledgers and handwritten dock slips are finished," Cai Chenggong declared loudly. "Starting tomorrow morning, the Provincial Freight Data Alliance will link every major harbor, bonded warehouse, and trucking fleet from Jingzhou City to Shanghai onto one shared cloud platform."

Applause rippled through the circle. Deputy Mayor Ding Yizhen lifted his wine glass smoothly.

"The provincial committee fully supports Chairman Song's initiative," Ding Yizhen said. "Our coastal ports handle over 80,000,000,000 yuan in annual trade. Putting every ship schedule, container weight, and customs manifest into one unified system will save time and raise our standing nationwide. Any company that wants provincial freight contracts next year will naturally join the alliance."

"Hear, hear!" several directors chimed in.

Sun Haoran adjusted his badge ribbon and nudged Chen Xuan. "Come on. It would be rude not to say hello to Chairman Song and the Deputy Governor."

During a pause at the edge of the circle, Sun Haoran stepped forward politely, holding out his business card with both hands.

"Deputy Mayor Ding, Chairman Song, good evening," Sun Haoran said respectfully, back straight. "I am Sun Haoran, General Manager of Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai. It is an honor to attend the forum and hear about the new alliance."

Cai Chenggong turned slowly, his eyes sliding from Sun Haoran's suit down to Chen Xuan's windbreaker and scratched green thermos. Smirking, he pinched the card between two fingers and pocketed it without looking.

"Hengxin Logistics," Cai Chenggong repeated loudly. "Ah, yes. That little sixty truck outfit in Shanghai. I wondered why the staff let regional clerks into the welcome dinner."

Several executives chuckled. Sun Haoran's ears warmed, though his voice stayed even. "We run sixty two trucks now, Chairman Song, and we just signed two coastal routes with Haiyue Group."

"Sixty two trucks," Cai Chenggong laughed, tapping Ding Yizhen's arm. "Deputy Governor, sixty two trucks, and he wants to talk about a provincial data platform! My Wanfeng Freight loses more tires in a month than his whole yard owns."

Ding Yizhen gave Sun Haoran a dismissive look through his glasses without offering his hand. "Small companies belong on local roads, General Manager Sun. Tomorrow, do not waste time asking technical questions. Just sign the member sheet at the back door so your trucks keep their port passes."

"Right," Cai Chenggong added, waving a hand. "And tell your clerk to leave his rusty water bottle in his room. This is the Shanshui Villa, not a construction site."

Sun Haoran's jaw tightened, ready to answer, when Chen Xuan rested a calm hand on his shoulder.

"General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said pleasantly, completely unbothered. "The braised beef at the side table is still steaming. Let us eat while it is warm. Chairman Song and Deputy Mayor Ding look very busy."

Cai Chenggong blinked at the clerk's flat expression, scoffed, and turned back to his crowd. Sun Haoran walked with Chen Xuan toward the buffet.

"Did you hear him?" Sun Haoran muttered low. "Sign at the back door! Rusty water bottle! Who does Cai Chenggong think he is?"

"A man who talks loudest when he wants nobody looking closely at his paperwork," Chen Xuan said calmly, plating roast beef and a warm roll. "Eat first, General Manager Sun. Anger ruins digestion."

Forty paces away by a cream pillar near the VIP lounge, a man in a charcoal suit and silver rimmed glasses held a glass of red wine, wearing a badge that read Marcus Vance, European Logistics Advisory.

Behind his glasses, Li Weiming had been watching Cai Chenggong with cold satisfaction, expecting twenty chairmen to sign by noon tomorrow so Zurich's backup servers in the bonded warehouse could come online before sunset. Then his gaze drifted toward the buffet table and saw a tall young man in a dark windbreaker holding a scratched green thermos.

Li Weiming's breath stopped. His wine glass tilted, and an icy chill ran down his spine.

He knew that face. Three weeks ago, before his two Shanghai port brokers were arrested and his three tracking crews vanished into custody overnight without a warning call, a scout had sent a street photo from Shanghai Harbor showing that same young man walking out of a tea shop near Hengxin Logistics right before the coastal network collapsed in forty eight hours.

"No," Li Weiming whispered behind clenched teeth. "He is just a clerk from a tiny Shanghai trucking firm. Why is he here?"

Just then, Chen Xuan sipped from his green thermos and glanced toward the pillar. His eyes were completely calm, without anger or curiosity, passing over Li Weiming like a moth on a windowpane.

Li Weiming's heart hammered. Setting his glass down, he stepped into the pillar's shadow and beckoned to Cai Chenggong's aide.

"You," Li Weiming said in a tight voice. "That small Shanghai company that just spoke to Chairman Song. Bring me the attendee sheet for Hengxin Logistics and check their room number right now, without letting them see you asking."
