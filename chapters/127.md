# Chapter 127: The Reception Hall

By six thirty, the Grand Ballroom on the third floor of the Grand River Hotel glowed with warm light and the steady buzz of two hundred voices.

Rows of round banquet tables covered in white linen stretched across the patterned carpet. Waiters in black vests moved smoothly through the crowd carrying trays of wine glasses and warm appetizers. At the front of the hall stood a wide blue backdrop displaying the title of the Sha Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum.

Sun Haoran stepped through the wide double doors with his shoulders squared and his navy suit buttoned neatly. Beside him, Chen Xuan wore a crisp white shirt beneath his plain dark windbreaker, his scratched green thermos resting comfortably in his left hand.

"Look at the turnout," Sun Haoran murmured, his eyes darting across the crowded room. "That tall fellow near the front table is the chairman of Donghai Shipping. And over by the flower stand, that is Director Lin from the provincial transport office."

"Try the braised beef near the buffet before the crowd finishes it," Chen Xuan suggested mildly, glancing toward the long food tables along the side wall. "The speeches usually run long."

Before Sun Haoran could reply, a burst of loud laughter rose from the center of the hall.

A dense circle of freight executives and local reporters had gathered around two men standing beneath the brightest chandelier. One was Song Baisheng, Chairman of Wanfeng Freight, dressed in a tailored dark gray suit with a gold watch flashing on his wrist as he gestured with a glass of white wine. Beside him stood Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang, a thick-set man in his fifties with silver-streaked hair and a practiced, heavy smile.

"Gentlemen, the days of scattered shipping ledgers and handwritten dock slips are finished," Song Baisheng declared loudly, his voice carrying easily over the music. "Starting tomorrow morning, the Provincial Freight Data Alliance will link every major harbor, bonded warehouse, and trucking fleet from Sha City down to Shanghai onto one shared cloud platform."

A chorus of polite applause and flattering nods rippled through the circle.

Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang lifted his glass slightly, his tone smooth and full of authority. "The provincial committee fully supports Chairman Song's initiative. Our coastal ports handle over 80 billion yuan in annual cargo. Putting every ship schedule, container weight, and customs manifest into one unified system will save time and raise our standing across the whole country. Any company that wants provincial freight contracts next year will naturally join the alliance."

"Hear, hear!" several directors chimed in at once.

Sun Haoran took a steady breath, adjusted his blue badge ribbon, and nudged Chen Xuan lightly. "Come on. Since we are here, it would be rude not to say hello to Chairman Song and the Deputy Governor."

They walked over to the edge of the circle. When a brief pause opened in the conversation, Sun Haoran stepped forward politely, holding out his business card with both hands.

"Deputy Governor Wu, Chairman Song, good evening," Sun Haoran said respectfully, keeping his back straight just as Chen Xuan had told him. "I am Sun Haoran, General Manager of Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai. It is an honor to attend the forum and hear about the new alliance."

Song Baisheng turned his head slowly. He did not reach for the card right away. Instead, his eyes slid from Sun Haoran's ordinary navy suit down to Chen Xuan's plain dark windbreaker and scratched green thermos.

A dry, amused smirk tugged at the corner of Song Baisheng's mouth. He pinched the card between two fingers, gave it a half-second glance, and slipped it carelessly into his side pocket without looking.

"Hengxin Logistics," Song Baisheng repeated, drawing out the name so the men around him could hear. "Ah, yes. That little forty-truck outfit down in Shanghai. I wondered why the forum staff let regional clerks into the main welcome dinner."

Several executives in the circle let out soft, mocking chuckles.

Sun Haoran's ears turned warm, though he kept his voice even. "We run sixty-two trucks now, Chairman Song, and we just signed two steady coastal routes with Haiyue Group."

"Sixty-two trucks," Song Baisheng laughed loudly, tapping Wu Zhenbang's arm. "Deputy Governor, did you hear that? Sixty-two trucks, and he wants to talk about a provincial data platform! My Wanfeng Freight loses more tires in a month than his whole yard owns."

Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang did not even bother to offer his hand. He gave Sun Haoran a brief, dismissive look from behind his wire-rimmed glasses.

"Small companies have their place on short local roads, General Manager Sun," Wu Zhenbang said coolly. "When the alliance launches tomorrow, do not waste time asking technical questions during the session. Just sign the standard member sheet at the back door so your trucks do not lose their port passes."

"Right," Song Baisheng added, waving a hand as if brushing away a fly. "And next time, tell your clerk to leave his rusty water bottle in the hotel room. This is the Grand River Hotel, not a railway construction site."

Sun Haoran's jaw tightened. His fingers curled slightly at his sides, ready to speak back, when a calm hand rested lightly on his shoulder.

"General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said in an unhurried, pleasant voice, his face completely unbothered. "The braised beef at the side table is still steaming. Let us eat while it is warm. Chairman Song and Deputy Governor Wu look very busy."

Song Baisheng blinked once at the young clerk's utterly flat, unimpressed expression, frowned slightly, and then scoffed as he turned back to his crowd of flatterers.

Sun Haoran let out a sharp breath through his nose and walked away with Chen Xuan toward the quieter edge of the ballroom.

"Did you hear how he talked?" Sun Haoran muttered low under his breath once they reached the buffet line. "Sign at the back door! Rusty water bottle! Who does Song Baisheng think he is?"

"A man who talks loudest when he wants nobody looking closely at his paperwork," Chen Xuan said calmly, placing two slices of roast beef and a warm roll onto a white plate. "Eat first, General Manager Sun. Anger ruins digestion."

Forty paces across the ballroom, near a tall cream-colored pillar beside the VIP lounge, a man in a sharp charcoal suit and silver-rimmed glasses stood quietly holding a glass of red wine.

Pinned to his lapel was a white visitor badge printed in neat English and Chinese letters: *Marcus Vance, European Logistics Advisory*.

Behind the silver-rimmed glasses, Li Weiming had been watching Song Baisheng work the room with cold, quiet satisfaction. Everything was moving on schedule. By noon tomorrow, twenty provincial freight chairmen would put their signatures on the alliance agreement, and Zurich's backup servers in the bonded warehouse would come online before sunset.

Then Li Weiming's gaze drifted past the laughing circle toward the buffet table along the side wall.

He saw a tall, ordinary-looking young man in a plain dark windbreaker holding a scratched green thermos.

Li Weiming's breath stopped dead in his chest.

The wine glass tilted slightly in his fingers, sending a dark red ripple up the inside of the crystal rim.

Every hair along the back of Li Weiming's neck stood straight up. A sudden, icy chill ran straight down his spine, turning his palms damp inside the warm ballroom.

He knew that face.

Three weeks ago, before his two Shanghai port brokers were dragged out of their offices in handcuffs, before his three tracking crews vanished into provincial custody overnight without leaving a single warning call behind, one of his scouts had sent a grainy street photograph from Shanghai Harbor. It showed a calm young man in a plain dark jacket walking out of a tea shop near Hengxin Logistics, right before the entire coastal network collapsed in forty-eight hours.

"No," Li Weiming whispered silently behind his clenched teeth, forcing his fingers not to shake. "That is impossible. He is just a clerk from a tiny Shanghai trucking firm. Why would he be here?"

Yet as Li Weiming stared across the room, Chen Xuan happened to lift his scratched green thermos, take a slow sip of tea, and turn his head half an inch in the direction of the cream-colored pillar.

Chen Xuan's eyes were completely calm. There was no anger in them, no threat, not even curiosity. They passed over Li Weiming as lightly as a man glancing at a stray moth on a windowpane.

Li Weiming's heart hammered twice against his ribs.

He set his untouched wine glass down sharply on a passing waiter's tray and stepped half a pace back into the shadow of the pillar. He beckoned briskly to one of Song Baisheng's personal aides standing nearby.

"You," Li Weiming said in a low, tight voice, keeping his eyes locked on the buffet table. "That small company from Shanghai that just spoke to Chairman Song. Bring me the full attendee sheet for Hengxin Logistics, and check which hotel room they are staying in. Do it right now, and do not let them see you asking."
