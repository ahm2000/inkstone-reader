# Chapter 127: The Forum Welcome Dinner

As evening settled over Shanshui Villa, the crystal chandeliers of the Grand Lake Banquet Hall glowed warmly over forty round tables draped in crisp white silk. Outside the tall glass windows, strings of amber lanterns reflected across the dark late-autumn lake, while inside, the air buzzed with polite conversation and the soft clink of porcelain.

Tonight's welcome banquet was hosted directly by Handong Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin and Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang. With three hundred visiting executives and bank directors gathered in one room, the seating chart reflected every inch of corporate weight.

Way back at Table 36, tucked near the decorative potted ferns, Sun Haoran sat with his spine straight and his eyes glued to the front of the hall. Beside him, Hu Dewei had barely touched his cold appetizers.

"Manager Sun," Hu Dewei whispered, gripping his chopsticks so tightly his knuckles turned white. "Look at Table 1. He is actually sitting at Table 1."

Sun Haoran swallowed hard and nodded. Right at the center front table, dressed in a clean dark sweater and a simple navy blazer that Qin Yuhan had insisted he wear, Chen Xuan sat calmly beside his wife. Just two seats to their left sat Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin, and across the crystal centerpiece sat Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang.

While every other executive at the front table sat stiffly, measuring every word they spoke, Chen Xuan looked entirely at ease. He poured warm jasmine tea for Qin Yuhan, placed a small dish of peeled roasted chestnuts beside her plate, and listened with a pleasant, unhurried smile.

Sha Ruijin rose briefly to open the dinner, his voice steady and clear without any wasted flourishes. He welcomed the visiting business leaders to Handong Province, emphasizing that the upcoming Jingzhou River Port expansion would be built on clean governance, fair rules, and open competition.

Warm applause rippled across the banquet hall. When Sha Ruijin sat back down, Li Dakang leaned over with his trademark crisp energy to speak with Qin Yuhan.

"President Qin, Haiyue Group's cold-chain logistics network in the Yangtze Delta is impressive," Li Dakang said, smiling appreciatively. "Jingzhou needs partners who bring real efficiency to the river port, not empty promises on paper."

"Thank you, Secretary Li," Qin Yuhan answered gracefully, her poise effortless. "We have been studying Handong's river freight corridors for months. As long as the bidding rules are fair and transparent, Haiyue Group is ready to commit serious long-term investment."

Li Dakang nodded in approval, then turned a friendly gaze toward the quiet young man sitting at her elbow. "And it is good to see President Qin brought family along to enjoy Jingzhou in late autumn. Working nonstop is hard on the nerves."

"He keeps me from skipping meals, Secretary Li," Qin Yuhan said with a soft smile, glancing warmly at Chen Xuan.

Chen Xuan raised his teacup politely toward both Sha Ruijin and Li Dakang. "The lake view here is wonderful, and the food is even better. Thank you both for the hospitality."

Sha Ruijin smiled kindly and inclined his head, returning the simple toast with his own teacup. Neither the Provincial Party Secretary nor the Municipal Party Secretary felt anything unusual about the young man beside Qin Yuhan. To them, he was simply a polite, warm-hearted husband accompanying a brilliant young CEO. Neither of them had the faintest idea that the relaxed man quietly peeling chestnuts at Table 1 was the unseen holder of the Black Order whose quiet interventions had steadied the nation's financial tides.

Halfway through the main course, while Sha Ruijin and Li Dakang stepped over to greet the regional directors of China Construction Bank and Huaxia Bank at Table 2, two men approached Qin Yuhan's side with practiced, oily smiles.

The first was Jingzhou Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen, his hair slicked back neatly and a gold watch flashing under his cuff. Behind him stood Cai Chenggong, a portly local shipping contractor whose smile never quite reached his eyes.

"President Qin, what an honor to finally meet you in person," Ding Yizhen said smoothly, extending a hand that Qin Yuhan shook briefly. "I heard you expressed strong interest in the Jingzhou River Port logistics concession. To save you time before tomorrow morning's keynote session, my office prepared an advance copy of the preliminary bidding rules."

Cai Chenggong promptly stepped forward and placed a thick, gold-stamped forty-page folder beside Qin Yuhan's assistant.

"Every detail is laid out cleanly inside, President Qin," Cai Chenggong added, lowering his voice with fake helpfulness. "We worked closely with Vice Mayor Ding's committee to make sure local port safety and customs dispatch stay smooth. If Haiyue Group signs the letter of intent tomorrow morning, we can fast-track your berth allocation right away."

Qin Yuhan thanked them politely and signaled her assistant to keep the folder safe. "I will read every page carefully tonight, Vice Mayor Ding."

"Excellent, excellent," Ding Yizhen chuckled, patting the back of his chair as he glanced briefly past Qin Yuhan at Chen Xuan. Seeing Chen Xuan's plain sweater and quiet posture, Ding Yizhen barely spared him half a second before turning away with a satisfied glint in his eyes.

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of jasmine tea. He did not say a word, yet his eyes caught the quick, smug glance Ding Yizhen exchanged with Cai Chenggong as they walked back toward Table 5.

"Something smells off about those two," Chen Xuan murmured softly, leaning close enough that only Qin Yuhan could hear.

Qin Yuhan hid a slight smile behind her napkin. "You noticed too? Vice Mayor Ding is far too eager for us to sign before the open floor discussion tomorrow."

"Good thing we have a quiet suite and a full night," Chen Xuan said calmly, placing a piece of steamed perch onto her plate. "Eat first. We can read their forty-page storybook after dinner."

Across the hall at Table 36, Hu Dewei watched Chen Xuan calmly serve fish to the President of Haiyue Group while provincial leaders laughed ten feet away, and quietly decided he would never complain about the 12th floor again.
