# Chapter 133: Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang's Anger

Outside the main auditorium, the wide marble lobby hummed with angry voices as freight chairmen gathered around the coffee tables, still arguing over Article 14.

Sun Haoran hurried Chen Xuan away from the crowd and into the quieter east hallway near the side lounges. As soon as they turned past the potted palms, Sun Haoran stopped, pressed both hands over his chest, and let out a shaky breath.

"Chen Xuan, my heart is still hammering against my ribs," Sun Haoran gasped, dabbing his forehead with a tissue. "Did you see Song Baisheng's face? And President Qin of Haiyue Group backed you up right in front of the television cameras! Should we slip out the back door and drive straight back to Shanghai?"

"There is no need to rush, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said calmly, holding his green thermos loosely in one hand. "The best part of the trip has not even started yet."

Before Sun Haoran could answer, heavy, rapid footsteps echoed down the marble hallway.

"Stop right there!"

A sharp, furious bark cut through the quiet corridor.

Sun Haoran stiffened and spun around.

Wu Zhenbang stopped three steps from Sun Haoran and Chen Xuan, breathing hard through his nose.

"Hengxin Logistics!" Wu Zhenbang snapped, stabbing a trembling finger at Sun Haoran's chest. "You run that little Shanghai trucking firm, do you not?"

Sun Haoran went paper-white and bowed low. "Yes, Deputy Governor Wu! I am Sun Haoran. Please listen, my young clerk did not mean any disrespect to the provincial government."

"No disrespect?" Wu Zhenbang barked, stepping closer. "You brought a loud-mouthed nobody into a provincial forum! You embarrassed our transport office in front of three hundred chairmen and live television!"

"Deputy Governor, I am truly sorry!" Sun Haoran stammered. "Chen Xuan is just an honest office clerk who read the back page by habit. Please forgive us!"

"Forgive you?" Wu Zhenbang sneered coldly, eyeing Chen Xuan's plain white shirt. "A tiny backwater firm with sixty trucks dares to ruin a provincial signing? Listen to me, Sun Haoran. By Monday morning, every Sha port pass your trucks hold will be cancelled. I will bury Hengxin Logistics so deep that you will not run a single bicycle across Sha Province!"

The brutal threat hit Sun Haoran like a bucket of ice water.

"Deputy Governor, please," Sun Haoran pleaded weakly, his eyes wide with terror. "Forty families depend on Hengxin for their wages. Punish me if you must, do not ruin the whole company."

Wu Zhenbang folded his arms across his chest, his eyes gleaming with petty triumph. "Too late for begging. Pack your things and get out of my sight before I call security to throw you out."

Beside the trembling Sun Haoran, Chen Xuan had not flinched an inch.

Chen Xuan opened his mouth, about to speak.

Before a single word left his lips, calm, measured footsteps sounded around the marble corner ten paces away.

"Deputy Governor Wu," a deep, unhurried voice spoke. "Since when does our provincial government threaten private business owners in a public hallway?"

Every person in the corridor froze.

Around the corner walked Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai, wearing a simple dark jacket with his hands folded behind his back. Walking half a step beside him was Haiyue Group chairman Qin Zhengming, his face serious and composed, followed by two quiet aides.

Wu Zhenbang's arrogant sneer vanished in half a heartbeat. His face drained from dark red to ghostly white. He dropped his arms to his sides, straightened his jacket, and hurried two steps forward with a stiff bow.

"Secretary Zhao! Chairman Qin!" Wu Zhenbang stammered, forcing a tight smile. "You are here! I was just speaking strictly to these two men from a small Shanghai firm. They stood up without approval and disrupted our provincial forum, causing confusion among the port directors. I was only reminding them to respect discipline."

Zhao Dehai stopped three feet in front of Wu Zhenbang. The mild look usually seen in newspapers was gone completely. His eyes turned ice-cold.

"Disrupted the forum?" Zhao Dehai asked, his voice dropping low and razor-sharp. Every bystander at the far end of the hall went dead silent. "I just watched the live feed upstairs with Chairman Qin. That young man asked three clear questions about a Zurich mailbox company and a 15 million yuan setup fee buried on page forty-eight. Asking why our coastal port schedules are sent to Zurich is not disruption, Wu Zhenbang. It is basic loyalty to this country."

Wu Zhenbang's throat worked dryly. Sweat broke out across his forehead. "Secretary Zhao, I did not read page forty-eight myself. Song Baisheng assured the transport office that everything followed international rules."

"You are a Deputy Governor of Sha Province, not Song Baisheng's errand boy!" Zhao Dehai snapped coldly, every quiet word hitting like a hammer. "Threatening to bury a law-abiding Shanghai firm because they caught your mistake? Disgraceful. Cancel the afternoon signing right now. Report to the provincial discipline office at once. Write a full account of every meeting you held with Song Baisheng and Li Weiming, and they will take your statement this afternoon."

Wu Zhenbang's shoulders slumped, his face burning with total humiliation in front of his own aides and the watching corridor. "Yes, Secretary Zhao. I will go at once."

Zhao Dehai did not spare Wu Zhenbang another glance.

Instead, the Provincial Party Secretary turned toward the plain young man standing quietly beside Sun Haoran.

In an instant, the ice melted from Zhao Dehai's eyes, replaced by deep, quiet respect. He did not draw a scene or shake hands loudly. He simply looked at Chen Xuan and gave him one slow, deeply respectful nod.

"Well asked today, Mr. Chen," Zhao Dehai said softly, his tone carrying unmistakable warmth. "Good tea keeps a clear head. Enjoy the rest of your stay in Sha City."

Beside him, Qin Zhengming also gave Chen Xuan a warm, knowing smile and a courteous nod.

"Thank you, Secretary Zhao. Thank you, Chairman Qin," Chen Xuan replied politely, returning a calm nod.

Without another word, Zhao Dehai turned and walked smoothly down the corridor with Qin Zhengming at his side.

Wu Zhenbang stood rooted to the marble floor, staring first at Zhao Dehai's back and then slowly swinging his stunned gaze back to Chen Xuan. A cold chill ran down Wu Zhenbang's spine. He spun on his heel and practically fled down the side passage, his pale aides scrambling after him.

Only then did Sun Haoran let out the breath he had been holding. His legs gave out, and he leaned his back heavily against the marble wall.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran whispered, staring blankly at the ceiling. "Did Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai just call you Mr. Chen, and nod to you like that?"

Chen Xuan unscrewed his green thermos and set the cap beside Sun Haoran.

"Pour yourself a little warm tea, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said gently. "Your hands are shaking."
