# Chapter 133: Congee and Handcuffs

The next morning proved him right. Bright late-autumn sunshine poured through the tall glass windows of the Shanshui Villa breakfast hall, painting wide golden squares across the white tablecloths.

At a quiet corner table overlooking the garden pond, the mood was downright peaceful.

Two steaming bowls of yellow millet congee, crisp fried dough sticks, pickled radish cubes, and a warm bamboo steamer of pork soup dumplings sat neatly between Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan.

Qin Yuhan wore a clean light-blue silk blouse under a cream jacket, looking refreshed after a deep night of sleep. Across from her, Chen Xuan wore his favorite gray cotton hoodie, unhurriedly tapping a warm boiled egg against the rim of his saucer.

"You slept like a little cat last night, President Qin," Chen Xuan teased softly, peeling the shell away in neat white strips. "Even when the garden sprinklers hissed on at dawn, you just pulled my pillow over your head and kept snoozing."

Qin Yuhan blushed faintly and nudged his shin lightly under the tablecloth.

"That is because someone insisted on warming my hands until I fell asleep," she murmured, smiling as he dropped the peeled egg smoothly into her bowl. "Still, I kept thinking about what Chief Hou said in the hallway last night. Do you think Ding Yizhen really got caught that fast?"

"When someone digs a pit deep enough, they only need one small push," Chen Xuan replied easily, breaking a crisp fried dough stick in half and dunking it into his hot congee. "Try the soup dumplings before they cool down."

Qin Yuhan lifted a plump dumpling with her chopsticks, blew on it gently, and took a small bite. Her dark eyes brightened at the rich, savory broth.

Just then, the cheerful clatter of porcelain spoons and quiet morning chatter across the breakfast hall dropped sharply into a stunned hush.

Dozens of heads turned at once toward the wide glass-walled corridor running outside the dining hall toward the main courtyard.

Walking briskly down the sunlit passage was Hou Liangping in a crisp dark jacket, flanked by four stern Anti-Corruption Bureau investigators.

Locked between two officers, wearing yesterday's crumpled suit with his tie missing and gleaming steel handcuffs around his wrists, was Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen. After an all-night preliminary inquiry in the villa's east wing, he was finally being escorted out.

Five paces behind him walked Cai Chenggong, his face gray as ash, also in steel cuffs.

Every trace of Ding Yizhen's oily poise was gone. His hair hung messy over his forehead, his shoulders slumped inward, and dark rings circled his bloodshot eyes. As they passed the tall glass windows of the breakfast hall, Ding Yizhen glanced sideways and spotted the sunny corner table.

Qin Yuhan sat calmly in her cream jacket, resting her chopsticks on the ceramic holder. Beside her, Chen Xuan lifted a spoonful of steaming millet congee to his mouth without a ripple of surprise.

Ding Yizhen's steps faltered. Disbelief and bitter shock twisted his pale face. Yesterday afternoon he had threatened to freeze their permits and ruin their trip. Less than twenty hours later, he was walking out of Shanshui Villa in steel cuffs while the quiet logistics clerk ate breakfast in the morning sun.

Ding Yizhen opened his mouth as if to shout through the glass, yet Hou Liangping gripped his elbow firmly and steered him straight forward.

"Save your energy for the interrogation room, Ding Yizhen," Hou Liangping's steady voice carried clearly through the open courtyard doors. "The Zurich account ledgers, the harbor rebates, and the confessions from the four thugs you hired last night are all logged in our case files."

Ding Yizhen's shoulders sagged completely. He lowered his head in defeat as the officers led him out into a waiting black sedan.

Inside the breakfast hall, astonished whispers swept quickly from table to table. Several harbor directors who had hesitated at yesterday's forum looked toward Qin Yuhan with open admiration and quiet gratitude.

Qin Yuhan set her spoon down, watching the black sedan roll smoothly down the maple-lined driveway.

"He really fell," she whispered, exhaling a long breath of genuine relief. "And Cai Chenggong went down right beside him."

"See?" Chen Xuan said mildly, dabbing the corner of his mouth with a napkin. "No need to lose sleep over people like that. Eat your boiled egg before it gets cold."

Before she could pick up her spoon again, brisk, energetic footsteps approached their corner table.

Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang strode over in a neat dark wool coat, his expression candid and full of drive.

"President Qin," Li Dakang greeted her warmly, shaking her hand as she rose politely. "On behalf of Jingzhou City, I owe Haiyue Group a sincere apology. We let a rotten apple sit at the bidding table and cause trouble for our guests. That ends today."

"Secretary Li, you are too kind," Qin Yuhan replied gracefully. "We appreciate how swiftly the province and the city protected fair business."

"Fair business is the backbone of Jingzhou's port reform," Li Dakang said decisively. "If investors cannot trust our contracts, nobody will bring ships to our docks. I have instructed the municipal office to review Haiyue Group's four coastal warehouse permits on merit this morning. Provided your blueprints meet safety codes, I will sign them myself before noon."

"Thank you very much, Secretary Li," Qin Yuhan smiled brightly. "Haiyue Group looks forward to investing in Handong."

Li Dakang turned briefly toward Chen Xuan, who stood politely beside his wife holding his folded napkin.

"And you must be President Qin's husband," Li Dakang nodded appreciatively, completely unaware of Chen Xuan's true standing. "I heard you kept a steady head when those thugs showed up in the hallway last night. Good man. Enjoy the rest of your stay in Jingzhou."

"Thank you, Secretary Li," Chen Xuan replied politely with a relaxed smile. "The millet congee here is wonderful."

Li Dakang chuckled heartily, patting Chen Xuan's arm once before striding back out to address the waiting shipping delegates.

Qin Yuhan sat back down, her cheeks glowing with quiet joy as she looked across the sunlit table.

"Four coastal warehouse permits signed before noon," she murmured happily. "My father is going to smile so wide his cheeks will hurt."

"Then finish your soup dumplings," Chen Xuan said warmly, sliding the bamboo steamer closer to her bowl. "You will need plenty of energy to sign all those papers."
