# Chapter 133: Deputy Mayor Ding Yizhen's Anger

Outside the main auditorium, the wide marble lobby hummed with angry voices as freight chairmen gathered around the coffee tables, still arguing over Article 14.

Sun Haoran hurried Chen Xuan away from the crowd and into the quieter east hallway near the side lounges. As soon as they turned past the potted palms, Sun Haoran stopped, pressed both hands over his chest, and let out a shaky breath.

"Chen Xuan, my heart is still hammering against my ribs," Sun Haoran gasped, dabbing his forehead with a tissue. "Did you see Cai Chenggong's face? And President Qin of Haiyue Group backed you up right in front of the television cameras! Should we slip out the back door and drive straight back to Shanghai?"

"There is no need to rush, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said calmly, holding his green thermos loosely in one hand. "The best part of the trip has not even started yet."

Before Sun Haoran could answer, heavy, rapid footsteps echoed down the marble hallway.

"Stop right there!"

A sharp, furious bark cut through the quiet corridor.

Sun Haoran stiffened and spun around.

Ding Yizhen stopped three steps from Sun Haoran and Chen Xuan, breathing hard through his nose.

"Hengxin Logistics!" Ding Yizhen snapped, stabbing a trembling finger at Sun Haoran's chest. "You run that little Shanghai trucking firm, do you not?"

Sun Haoran went paper-white and bowed low. "Yes, Deputy Mayor Ding! I am Sun Haoran. Please listen, my young clerk did not mean any disrespect to the provincial government."

"No disrespect?" Ding Yizhen barked, stepping closer. "You brought a loud-mouthed nobody into a provincial forum! You embarrassed our transport office in front of three hundred chairmen and live television!"

"Deputy Governor, I am truly sorry!" Sun Haoran stammered. "Chen Xuan is just an honest office clerk who read the back page by habit. Please forgive us!"

"Forgive you?" Ding Yizhen sneered coldly, eyeing Chen Xuan's plain white shirt. "A tiny backwater firm with sixty trucks dares to ruin a provincial signing? Listen to me, Sun Haoran. By Monday morning, every Sha port pass your trucks hold will be cancelled. I will bury Hengxin Logistics so deep that you will not run a single bicycle across Handong Province!"

The brutal threat hit Sun Haoran like a bucket of ice water.

"Deputy Governor, please," Sun Haoran pleaded weakly, his eyes wide with terror. "Forty families depend on Hengxin for their wages. Punish me if you must, do not ruin the whole company."

Ding Yizhen folded his arms across his chest, his eyes gleaming with petty triumph. "Too late for begging. Pack your things and get out of my sight before I call security to throw you out."

Beside the trembling Sun Haoran, Chen Xuan had not flinched an inch.

Chen Xuan opened his mouth, about to speak.

Before a single word left his lips, calm, measured footsteps sounded around the marble corner ten paces away.

"Deputy Mayor Ding," a deep, unhurried voice spoke. "Since when does our provincial government threaten private business owners in a public hallway?"

Every person in the corridor froze.

Around the corner walked Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin, wearing a simple dark jacket with his hands folded behind his back. Walking half a step beside him was Haiyue Group chairman Qin Zhengming, his face serious and composed, followed by two quiet aides.

Ding Yizhen's arrogant sneer vanished in half a heartbeat. His face drained from dark red to ghostly white. He dropped his arms to his sides, straightened his jacket, and hurried two steps forward with a stiff bow.

"Secretary Sha! Chairman Qin!" Ding Yizhen stammered, forcing a tight smile. "You are here! I was just speaking strictly to these two men from a small Shanghai firm. They stood up without approval and disrupted our provincial forum, causing confusion among the port directors. I was only reminding them to respect discipline."

Sha Ruijin stopped three feet in front of Ding Yizhen. The mild look usually seen in newspapers was gone completely. His eyes turned ice-cold.

"Disrupted the forum?" Sha Ruijin asked, his voice dropping low and razor-sharp. Every bystander at the far end of the hall went dead silent. "I just watched the live feed upstairs with Chairman Qin. That young man asked three clear questions about a Zurich mailbox company and a 15 million yuan setup fee buried on page forty-eight. Asking why our coastal port schedules are sent to Zurich is not disruption, Ding Yizhen. It is basic loyalty to this country."

Ding Yizhen's throat worked dryly. Sweat broke out across his forehead. "Secretary Sha, I did not read page forty-eight myself. Cai Chenggong assured the transport office that everything followed international rules."

"You are a Deputy Governor of Handong Province, not Cai Chenggong's errand boy!" Sha Ruijin snapped coldly, every quiet word hitting like a hammer. "Threatening to bury a law-abiding Shanghai firm because they caught your mistake? Disgraceful. Cancel the afternoon signing right now. Report to the provincial discipline office at once. Write a full account of every meeting you held with Cai Chenggong and Li Weiming, and they will take your statement this afternoon."

Ding Yizhen's shoulders slumped, his face burning with total humiliation in front of his own aides and the watching corridor. "Yes, Secretary Sha. I will go at once."

Sha Ruijin did not spare Ding Yizhen another glance.

Instead, the Provincial Party Secretary turned toward the plain young man standing quietly beside Sun Haoran.

In an instant, the ice melted from Sha Ruijin's eyes, replaced by deep, quiet respect. He did not draw a scene or shake hands loudly. He simply looked at Chen Xuan and gave him one slow, deeply respectful nod.

"Well asked today, Mr. Chen," Sha Ruijin said softly, his tone carrying unmistakable warmth. "Good tea keeps a clear head. Enjoy the rest of your stay in Jingzhou City."

Beside him, Qin Zhengming also gave Chen Xuan a warm, knowing smile and a courteous nod.

"Thank you, Secretary Sha. Thank you, Chairman Qin," Chen Xuan replied politely, returning a calm nod.

Without another word, Sha Ruijin turned and walked smoothly down the corridor with Qin Zhengming at his side.

Ding Yizhen stood rooted to the marble floor, staring first at Sha Ruijin's back and then slowly swinging his stunned gaze back to Chen Xuan. A cold chill ran down Ding Yizhen's spine. He spun on his heel and practically fled down the side passage, his pale aides scrambling after him.

Only then did Sun Haoran let out the breath he had been holding. His legs gave out, and he leaned his back heavily against the marble wall.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran whispered, staring blankly at the ceiling. "Did Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin just call you Mr. Chen, and nod to you like that?"

Chen Xuan unscrewed his green thermos and set the cap beside Sun Haoran.

"Pour yourself a little warm tea, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said gently. "Your hands are shaking."
