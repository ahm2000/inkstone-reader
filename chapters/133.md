# Chapter 133: Congee and Handcuffs

Bright late-autumn sunshine poured through the tall glass windows of the Shanshui Villa breakfast hall on Sunday morning, painting golden squares across the white tablecloths.

At a quiet corner table overlooking the garden pond, the mood was downright peaceful.

Two steaming bowls of yellow millet congee, crisp fried dough sticks, pickled radish cubes, and a warm bamboo steamer of pork soup dumplings sat between Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan.

Qin Yuhan wore a clean light-blue silk blouse under a cream jacket, looking refreshed after a deep night of sleep. Across from her, Chen Xuan wore his favorite gray cotton hoodie, unhurriedly tapping a warm boiled egg against his saucer.

"You slept like a little cat last night, President Qin," Chen Xuan teased softly, peeling the shell in neat strips. "Even when the sprinklers hissed on outside, you just pulled my pillow over your head and kept snoozing."

Qin Yuhan blushed and nudged his shin lightly under the table.

"That is because someone insisted on warming my hands until I fell asleep," she murmured, smiling as he dropped the peeled egg into her bowl. "Still, I kept thinking about what Chief Hou said last night. Do you think Ding Yizhen really got caught that fast?"

"When someone digs a pit deep enough, they only need one small push," Chen Xuan replied easily, dunking a fried dough stick into his hot congee. "Try the soup dumplings before they cool down."

Qin Yuhan picked up a dumpling with her chopsticks, blew on it gently, and took a bite. Her eyes brightened at the savory broth.

Just then, the cheerful clatter of spoons and chatter across the breakfast hall dropped sharply into a stunned hush.

Heads turned toward the wide glass corridor running outside the dining hall toward the main courtyard.

Walking briskly down the sunlit passage was Hou Liangping in a crisp dark jacket, flanked by four Anti-Corruption Bureau investigators.

Locked between two officers, wearing yesterday's crumpled suit with his tie missing and gleaming steel handcuffs around his wrists, was Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen.

Five paces behind him walked Cai Chenggong, gray as ash, also in handcuffs.

Every trace of Ding Yizhen's oily poise was gone. His hair hung messy over his forehead, his shoulders slumped, and dark rings circled his eyes. As they passed the tall glass windows of the breakfast hall, Ding Yizhen glanced sideways and spotted the sunny corner table.

Qin Yuhan sat calmly in her cream jacket. Beside her, Chen Xuan lifted a spoonful of steaming millet congee to his mouth without a ripple of surprise.

Ding Yizhen's steps faltered. Disbelief and bitter shock twisted his pale face. Yesterday afternoon he had threatened to freeze their permits and ruin their trip. Less than twenty hours later, he was walking out of Shanshui Villa in steel cuffs while the quiet logistics clerk ate breakfast in the sun.

Ding Yizhen opened his mouth as if to shout through the glass, but Hou Liangping gripped his elbow firmly and steered him forward.

"Save your energy for the interrogation room, Ding Yizhen," Hou Liangping's steady voice carried through the open doors. "The Zurich account ledgers, the harbor rebates, and the confessions from the four thugs you hired last night are all logged in our case files."

Ding Yizhen's shoulders sagged completely. He lowered his head as the officers led him out into a waiting black sedan.

Inside the breakfast hall, astonished whispers swept from table to table. Several harbor directors who had hesitated yesterday looked at Qin Yuhan with open admiration.

Qin Yuhan set her chopsticks down, watching the black sedan roll down the maple-lined driveway.

"He really fell," she whispered, exhaling a long breath of relief. "And Cai Chenggong went down right beside him."

"See?" Chen Xuan said mildly, dabbing his mouth with a napkin. "No need to lose sleep over people like that. Eat your egg before it gets cold."

Before she could pick up her spoon, brisk footsteps approached their corner table.

Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang strode over in a neat dark coat, his expression candid and energetic.

"President Qin," Li Dakang greeted her warmly, shaking her hand as she stood. "On behalf of Jingzhou City, I owe Haiyue Group a sincere apology. We let a rotten apple sit at the bidding table and cause trouble for our guests. That ends today."

"Secretary Li, you are too kind," Qin Yuhan said gracefully. "We appreciate how swiftly the province and the city handled it."

"Fair business is the backbone of Jingzhou," Li Dakang said decisively. "I have instructed the municipal office to review Haiyue Group's four coastal warehouse permits on merit this morning. Provided your blueprints meet safety codes, I will sign them myself before noon."

"Thank you very much, Secretary Li," Qin Yuhan smiled brightly.

Li Dakang turned briefly toward Chen Xuan, who stood politely beside his wife holding his napkin.

"And you must be President Qin's husband," Li Dakang nodded appreciatively, completely unaware of Chen Xuan's true standing. "I heard you kept a steady head when those thugs showed up last night. Good man. Enjoy the rest of your stay in Jingzhou."

"Thank you, Secretary Li," Chen Xuan replied politely with a relaxed smile. "The millet congee here is wonderful."

Li Dakang chuckled heartily and strode back out to address the shipping delegates.

Qin Yuhan sat back down, her cheeks glowing with joy as she looked across the table.

"Four coastal warehouse permits signed before noon," she murmured happily. "My father is going to smile so wide his cheeks will hurt."

"Then finish your soup dumplings," Chen Xuan said warmly, sliding the steamer closer. "You will need the energy to sign all those papers."
