# Chapter 198: The Mountain Vault

The concrete staircase descended sixty meters into the granite bedrock beneath Changbai Mountain, illuminated by rows of recessed fluorescent strips behind wire cages.

Unlike the rough timber mill above, the lower level opened into a spotless, climate-controlled bunker lined with polished gray epoxy flooring and heavy industrial air scrubbers that hummed with a quiet, steady vibration.

Inside the central control office, thick bulletproof glass looked out over a vast underground cavern. Across the cavern floor, fifty wooden pallets stood stacked to the ceiling with shrink-wrapped cardboard crates, each stenciled with the emblem of the Seven-Harbor Consortium. Beyond the pallets, behind a reinforced steel grille, three electric smelting furnaces glowed with dull orange pilot lights, waiting to melt down foreign bullion into unmarked bars.

Kou Zhenshan, a stout fifty-five-year-old man wearing an expensive cashmere sweater and gold-rimmed spectacles, sat behind a broad mahogany desk in the control office, rapidly stamping export manifests with red ink.

Across from him stood Viktor Orlov. The Russian mercenary commander was a massive, square-jawed man of forty-five, wearing a gray tactical turtleneck and a drop-leg holster holding a customized automatic pistol. A thick scar ran from his right ear down to his collarbone, a souvenir from an African border skirmish ten years ago.

"How much longer for the third container shipment, Kou?" Orlov demanded, his voice thick and harsh in the quiet room. "The heavy transport trucks should have arrived at the logging road two hours ago. My men are getting restless in this cold."

Kou Zhenshan adjusted his glasses nervously, wiping a bead of sweat from his forehead despite the cool room temperature. "Mr. Victor Leung called from Hong Kong at nine. He said the authorities in Shanghai and Shenzhen have started cross-checking coastal port manifests. He wants every bar of gold and every physical account book loaded onto the trucks by midnight so we can cross the northern border into Russia before Monday morning."

"The border is closed to unauthorized heavy freight," Orlov growled, folding his thick arms across his chest. "We will have to grease the border posts with another 2 million yuan."

"Money is not an issue," Kou Zhenshan said hastily. "Victor Leung has authorized an emergency fund of 50 million yuan from the Swiss accounts. Just get the convoy moving."

Orlov frowned and reached for the radio handset on the desk console. He pressed the transmit button. "Post One, report status. How does the north road look?"

Only static hissed from the speaker.

Orlov's brow furrowed deeper. He clicked the channel switch. "Post Two. Post Three. Acknowledge."

Silence. Not a click, not a breath, not a word.

"What is wrong?" Kou Zhenshan asked, freezing with his stamp suspended an inch above the paper.

"The radio repeater is dead," Orlov said, his voice dropping into cold alertness. He stepped to the surveillance console and tapped the keyboard. Every screen displayed an identical view of snowy pines with the timestamp frozen at ten-fifteen.

"Cameras are locked on a loop," Orlov snapped, drawing his automatic pistol in a single smooth motion. "Someone is inside the compound."

Kou Zhenshan jumped out of his chair so fast his mahogany seat tumbled backward against the wall. "What? Inside? How could anyone get past your twenty-eight sentries without a single alarm?"

"Shut up," Orlov commanded, walking toward the heavy reinforced steel door of the office. He pulled a palm-sized black remote control from his vest pocket, flipped open the safety guard, and held his thumb over the glowing red toggle. "The demolition detonators are set. If it is federal police, I blow the main tunnel and seal them in the outer canyon."

He reached for the heavy steel latch of the office door.

Before his fingers could touch the handle, the reinforced lock mechanism let out a soft electronic click. The heavy steel door swung inward on oiled hinges, moving with eerie smoothness.

Orlov raised his pistol toward the doorway, his muscles tensing to fire.

A young man in his late twenties walked calmly into the control office. He wore an ordinary dark winter coat over a simple sweater, comfortable canvas shoes on his feet, and carried a dented fifteen-yuan green metal thermos in his left hand.

Orlov stared at him in utter disbelief. He had prepared for a squad of helmeted counter-terrorism commandos with ballistic shields and submachine guns. Instead, an unassuming young clerk stood before him, looking around the underground bunker with the mild curiosity of someone inspecting a new warehouse floor.

Behind the young man, two tall figures in clean white gear entered and took positions on either side of the doorway, their hands folded neatly behind their backs.

"Who the hell are you?" Orlov barked, pointing the gun straight between Chen Xuan's eyes. "Take one more step and I blow your brains across that wall!"

Chen Xuan did not even blink at the black barrel pointed at his forehead. He strolled toward the mahogany desk, pulled the rolling chair back upright, and sat down comfortably. He placed his green thermos on the desk blotter, right beside Kou Zhenshan's abandoned ink pad.

"Mr. Orlov," Chen Xuan said in an easy, pleasant voice, speaking in fluent English. "Point your weapon somewhere else. If an accidental discharge scratches my thermos, my wife will be very upset. It took me a long time to find one with this exact shade of green."

Kou Zhenshan stared at Chen Xuan with his mouth wide open. "You, who sent you? Are you from the Ministry of State Security? From the Central Commission?"

Chen Xuan chuckled softly, unscrewing the green lid. "Neither. I am just a logistics supervisor from Hengxin in Shanghai. I handle freight manifests and warehouse records. Speaking of manifests, Mr. Kou, your export forms here have completely incorrect clearance codes. You really should be more thorough with your paperwork."

Orlov's face twisted in savage fury. "You think this is a joke, boy? Look at what I have in my left hand!" He shoved the glowing red detonator forward, his thumb resting firmly on the trigger. "This switch is wired to four thousand kilograms of military explosives packed into the tunnel ceiling! One press, and this entire mountain comes down on our heads!"

Chen Xuan took a calm sip of hot jasmine tea, savored the roasted flavor for three quiet seconds, and looked at Orlov with gentle amusement.

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said softly. "Press it."
