# Chapter 198: The Mountain Vault

Inside the mountain vault, the air was dry, warm, and faintly scented with machine oil and old paper. Rows of bright fluorescent tubes hung from the arched concrete ceiling, casting crisp white light down a long underground hall carved deep into the granite.

Along both sides of the hall stood heavy steel industrial racks, bolted straight into the concrete floor. Stacked neatly across those racks were thousands of gleaming gold bars, each stamped and weighed, catching the overhead light in a dull, heavy sheen. Three hundred tons of solid gold sat quietly inside the mountain, hidden from tax inspectors, customs scanners, and banking regulators for years.

At the center of the main chamber sat a heavy steel desk. Behind it, Kou Zhenshan wore a thick cashmere sweater beneath an unbuttoned fur-lined coat. His fingers moved rapidly as he checked the serial numbers on a row of matte black encrypted hard drives, placing each one carefully beside a stack of thick cloth-bound ledgers.

Kou Zhenshan glanced down at the gold watch on his wrist. It was 10:18 PM Beijing (+8:00 GMT).

"Victor Leung's legal team in Zurich (+1:00 GMT) lost the emergency injunction an hour ago," Kou Zhenshan muttered, his voice tight with strain. "Every Aegis Meridian account tied to Seven-Harbor has been locked down tight. We cannot move a single dollar through Europe anymore."

Standing beside a bank of six security monitors a few paces away, Viktor Orlov did not look worried. The tall, scarred former mercenary wore a heavy wool sweater and kept a matte black pistol holstered at his hip. He tapped the rugged satellite radio resting on the console.

"Paper numbers in Zurich can be frozen," Orlov said gruffly. "Three hundred tons of physical gold cannot be frozen by a keyboard. Once this snowstorm clears at dawn, our transport helicopters will lift the first batch across the border, and those ledgers will buy us safe passage anywhere we choose."

Kou Zhenshan wiped a thin film of sweat from his forehead. "Make sure your men on the ridge stay awake. I do not like how quiet tonight feels."

Before Orlov could answer, the top-left security monitor flickered sharply and went black. One second later, the second monitor died. Then the third, fourth, fifth, and sixth screens snapped dark in clean sequence, leaving only empty glass reflecting the bright vault lights.

At the exact same instant, the satellite radio on the desk spat a short hiss of white static and went completely silent.

Kou Zhenshan shot to his feet, knocking his chair back against the steel rack. "What happened? Did the generator trip?"

"The lights are still on," Orlov snapped, his expression hardening instantly. He grabbed his short-range radio and pressed the talk button. "Ridge One, report. Ridge Two, report! Entrance team, respond!"

Only dead silence answered him.

Orlov dropped the radio, drew his pistol, and barked a sharp order in Russian. The four armed mercenaries stationed along the gold racks raised their rifles immediately, aiming straight down the central aisle toward the massive steel blast door at the end of the hall.

Kou Zhenshan's face turned chalk-white. He scrambled to sweep the black hard drives into a reinforced aluminum case. "Who is out there? State Security could not have found this tunnel! Nobody outside our inner circle knows the coordinates!"

"Stay behind the desk," Orlov growled, stepping swiftly over to a concrete pillar near the control console. Mounted on the pillar was a heavy red steel box with a large round detonator button under a flipped-up plastic cover. A small green indicator light glowed steadily beside the switch.

Orlov's jaw tightened with grim relief. The green light meant the circuit was intact. Even if an entire regiment was outside that door, one press of his palm would collapse the entrance tunnel and seal the mountain before anyone could take a single bar of gold.

Then came a low, smooth hiss of hydraulics.

Slowly and steadily, the thick steel blast door slid open along its heavy tracks. A rolling wave of freezing white mist poured in from the snowy Changbai night, swirling across the warm concrete floor.

Orlov and his four mercenaries tensed, fingers hovering over triggers.

Out of the white mist walked a young man in a plain dark winter down jacket and knit scarf. His hands were not raised, and he carried no weapon at all. Instead, he held a green metal thermos loosely in his left hand. Walking calmly a few steps behind him were three men in simple dark winter parkas.

"Hold your fire until I see who they are!" Orlov barked, startled by the sight of a single unarmed man strolling into an underground fortress.

That single second of hesitation was all it took.

Number 12 and Number 44 broke forward from the mist with blinding speed. Before the four interior guards could squeeze a single trigger, Number 12 swept the two rifles on the left aside, striking both men clean in the ribs and jaw, sending them crashing to the concrete. On the right, Number 44 closed the gap in two strides, wrenching the rifles free and pinning both guards flat against the floor with sharp strikes to the backs of their necks.

Within four seconds, all four interior mercenaries were disarmed and out of the fight.

Chen Xuan did not even quicken his pace. He walked smoothly down the central aisle between the gleaming walls of gold, stopped a few paces in front of the metal desk, and casually unscrewed the cap of his green thermos. A thin ribbon of hot steam curled upward into the bright fluorescent light.

Kou Zhenshan gripped the edge of the desk with trembling hands, staring at the calm young man in sheer disbelief.

"Who are you?" Kou Zhenshan gasped. "How did you find this vault?"
