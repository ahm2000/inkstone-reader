# Chapter 36: Eight Sentries in the Courtyard

The sharp rapping on the bedroom door broke the quiet of the capital at ten minutes past one on Monday morning. Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua was awake before the third knock finished. Men who carried half the country's ministries on their shoulders rarely slept deep enough to miss an urgent footstep in the hall.

He swung his legs off the bed, reached for his dark wool coat, and opened the door himself.

His secretary, Tang Weimin, stood in the corridor with his spectacles crooked on his nose. The younger man's face had drained to the color of wet chalk. His left hand gripped the doorframe so tightly that his knuckles shone white, and his chest rose and fell in shallow, ragged pulls.

"Vice Premier," Tang Weimin whispered, his voice cracking. "We have armed men in the courtyard."

Zhou Zhenhua did not flinch. His gaze held Tang Weimin's panicked eyes for two steady seconds while he slid his arms into his coat. "Slow down, Tang Weimin. Breathe. Who is in the courtyard?"

"I do not know," Tang Weimin stammered, glancing back over his shoulder. "They are not our guards. They wear matte-black tactical gear, full masks, no insignia, no patches. They did not climb the wall. One minute the courtyard was empty, and the next minute they were simply standing there."

Zhou Zhenhua's jaw tightened by a fraction of an inch. He picked up his pack of cigarettes and brass lighter from the nightstand and stepped into the corridor. "What did the perimeter detachment say?"

"That is the worst part," Tang Weimin said, his voice shaking so badly he had to swallow twice. "I ran down to the duty room three minutes ago. Captain Liu Yong and his six men are sitting on the wooden bench with their holsters buttoned and their hands flat on their knees. Captain Liu Yong would not even look at me. He told me to wake you and tell you not to touch a single weapon. Vice Premier, the guard detachment may be compromised. We need to move to the basement safe room right now, or I should call the Central Guard Bureau on my personal phone."

"Put your phone back in your pocket," Zhou Zhenhua said flatly.

Tang Weimin froze, his fingers halfway into his jacket. "Vice Premier, if this is a coup, waiting here means walking straight into a cage."

"If this were a coup, Tang Weimin, Captain Liu Yong would be lying dead in the duty room, and you would never have reached these stairs." Zhou Zhenhua flicked the brass lighter open, lit his cigarette, and blew a thin stream of smoke toward the ceiling as he started downstairs. "Come with me. Keep your hands out of your pockets and stop trembling."

Tang Weimin hesitated for a heartbeat, then hurried after him. The house felt unnaturally silent.

Zhou Zhenhua unlatched the heavy front door and stepped onto the porch. He stood beneath the eaves, took another slow drag, and swept his eyes across the dark courtyard from left to right.

One stood beside the iron gate. Two flanked the gravel driveway. Two held the corners beneath the old cypress trees. Two stood along the east walkway near the duty room, and the eighth stood directly at the foot of the stone steps, thirty paces away, facing the porch.

Eight figures in featureless matte-black gear. Their masks had no visors, no visible microphones, no rank tabs, and no unit markings. None of them raised a rifle, yet every angle of the residence was sealed with cold precision. Not one of them shifted his weight or turned his head when the door opened.

Behind Zhou Zhenhua, Tang Weimin let out a choked breath and instinctively stepped back against the doorframe.

"Eight of them," Tang Weimin whispered, his eyes wide behind his spectacles. "Vice Premier, they are not even blinking. Should I tell the inner bodyguards to draw their pistols and hold the foyer?"

"If a single man inside this house draws a pistol tonight, I will personally send him to a military tribunal if he survives the next three seconds," Zhou Zhenhua said. His tone stayed low, yet the cold weight behind it made Tang Weimin stiffen immediately.

"Vice Premier," Tang Weimin pressed, desperation breaking through his discipline, "who are they? Military? State Security? Nobody has authority to surround a Vice Premier's residence without a warrant from the top."

Zhou Zhenhua tapped ash into the night air, his gaze fixed on the silent figure thirty paces away. "Look at their posture, Tang Weimin. Military special forces shift their weight every forty seconds to keep their knees loose. State Security operatives watch the windows and check their comms. These eight have not moved a millimeter since we stepped outside. They do not need comms, and they do not care whether I am standing on this porch or hiding in a cellar."

Tang Weimin swallowed hard. "Then who could possibly command them?"

"Nobody you have ever seen on a broadcast," Zhou Zhenhua replied quietly. He took a long draw on his cigarette, the orange tip flaring in the dark. "In thirty years of service, I have only heard their name spoken twice in closed rooms, and both times the men who spoke it lowered their voices even though the walls were soundproofed."

A cold shiver ran through Tang Weimin's shoulders. "Are they here for you, Vice Premier? Did someone fabricate a dossier?"

Zhou Zhenhua let out a short, humorless breath through his nose. "If they were here for me, Tang Weimin, they would not be standing politely in the courtyard waiting for morning. They would already be inside my study, and I would already be sitting in an unmarked car."

"Then why are they outside our door at one in the morning?" Tang Weimin asked, his voice rising before he forced it back down. "Why seal off your residence if you are not the target?"

"Because tonight, nobody in this capital is allowed to make a phone call, send a courier, or whisper a warning to an old friend," Zhou Zhenhua said. He glanced sideways at his pale secretary. "Go down to the duty room. Tell Captain Liu Yong he made the right choice. Order every guard on this property to keep his sidearm holstered, sit at the table, drink hot water, and keep both hands visible. No shouting, no sudden movements, no heroism."

Tang Weimin stared at him, sudden dread dawning across his face. "Vice Premier, if they have surrounded your courtyard, what about the other residences on this lane? What about the ministries?"

Zhou Zhenhua looked out across the silent courtyard one last time. The eight matte-black sentries stood like iron statues in the dark, indifferent to his rank and indifferent to the trembling secretary behind him.

"Go give Captain Liu Yong my order," Zhou Zhenhua said, stubbing his dying cigarette into the brass tray beside the door. "Then meet me in my study. We are about to find out whether the red phones still ring."
