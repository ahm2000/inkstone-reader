# Chapter 36: Eight Sentries in the Courtyard

Sharp knocking on the bedroom door broke the capital's quiet at ten past one on Monday morning. Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua woke before the third knock ended. Men carrying half the country's ministries rarely slept deep enough to miss hurried footsteps.

He swung his legs off the bed, grabbed his dark wool coat, and opened the door himself.

His secretary, Tang Weimin, stood in the hall with crooked glasses and a face like wet chalk. His left hand gripped the doorframe until his knuckles shone white, his breaths short and shaky.

"Vice Premier," Tang Weimin whispered, voice cracking. "There are armed men in the yard."

Zhou Zhenhua did not flinch. He held Tang Weimin's panicked eyes while slipping into his coat. "Slow down, Tang Weimin. Breathe. Who is in the yard?"

"I do not know," Tang Weimin stammered, glancing back down the hall. "They are not our guards. Plain black gear, full masks, no badges or patches. They did not climb the wall. One minute the yard was empty, and the next they were just standing there."

Zhou Zhenhua's jaw tightened slightly. He picked up his cigarettes and brass lighter from the nightstand and stepped out. "What did the outside guard team say?"

"That is the worst part," Tang Weimin said, swallowing twice. "I ran down to the duty room three minutes ago. Captain Liu Yong and his six men are sitting on the bench with guns buttoned inside their holsters and hands flat on their knees. Captain Liu Yong would not even look up. He told me to wake you and tell you not to touch a weapon. Vice Premier, the guard team may be bought off. We need the basement safe room right now, or I should call the Central Guard Bureau on my personal phone."

"Put your phone back in your pocket," Zhou Zhenhua said flatly.

Tang Weimin froze, fingers halfway inside his jacket. "Vice Premier, if this is a coup, waiting here walks straight into a cage."

"If this were a coup, Tang Weimin, Captain Liu Yong would already be dead in the duty room, and you would never have made it upstairs." Zhou Zhenhua lit a cigarette and blew smoke toward the ceiling as he headed down. "Come with me. Keep your hands out of your pockets and stop shaking."

Tang Weimin hurried after him through the unnaturally still house.

Zhou Zhenhua unlocked the heavy front door and stepped onto the porch. Under the eaves, he took a slow drag and swept the dark yard from left to right.

One figure stood beside the iron gate. Two flanked the gravel drive. Two held the dark corners beneath the cypress trees. Two stood along the east walk near the duty room, and the eighth stood at the foot of the stone steps, thirty paces away, facing the porch.

Eight figures in plain black gear. Their masks had no clear glass, visible microphones, rank strips, or unit marks. None raised a rifle, yet every exit was sealed with cold precision. Not one shifted a foot or turned a head when the door opened.

Behind Zhou Zhenhua, Tang Weimin choked back a breath and pressed against the doorframe.

"Eight of them," Tang Weimin whispered, eyes huge. "Vice Premier, they are not even moving. Should I tell the inside guards to draw their pistols and hold the front hall?"

"If a single man inside this house pulls a gun tonight, I will personally send him to a military court if he lives through the next three seconds," Zhou Zhenhua said. His voice stayed low, yet its cold weight stiffened Tang Weimin at once.

"Vice Premier," Tang Weimin pressed, panic pushing past training, "who are they? Army? State Security? Nobody has authority to surround a Vice Premier's home without a direct warrant from the very top."

Zhou Zhenhua tapped ash into the night air, watching the silent figure thirty paces away. "Look at how they stand, Tang Weimin. Army special forces shift their weight every forty seconds to keep their knees loose. State Security officers watch windows and check radios. These eight have not moved an inch since we walked outside. They do not need radios, and they do not care whether I stand here or hide in a cellar."

Tang Weimin swallowed hard. "Then who could possibly give them orders?"

"Nobody you have ever seen on television," Zhou Zhenhua replied quietly, taking a long drag. "In thirty years of service, I have only heard their name spoken twice in closed rooms. Both times, the men who said it lowered their voices even though the walls were soundproof."

A cold shiver crossed Tang Weimin's shoulders. "Are they here for you, Vice Premier? Did someone make up a fake file against you?"

Zhou Zhenhua let out a short, dry breath. "If they were here for me, Tang Weimin, they would not be waiting politely in the yard for sunrise. They would already be inside my study, and I would already be in an unmarked car."

"Then why are they outside our door at one in the morning?" Tang Weimin asked, lowering his voice. "Why seal off your home if you are not the target?"

"Because tonight, nobody in this capital is allowed to make a phone call, send a messenger, or whisper a warning to an old friend," Zhou Zhenhua said, glancing at his pale secretary. "Go to the duty room. Tell Captain Liu Yong he made the right call. Order every guard to keep his gun holstered, sit at the table, drink hot water, and keep both hands in plain sight. No shouting, no sudden moves, and no trying to be a hero."

Cold new fear dawned across Tang Weimin's face. "Vice Premier, if they surrounded your yard, what about the other homes on this lane? What about all the ministries?"

Zhou Zhenhua looked across the silent yard one last time. The eight black-clad guards stood like iron statues, indifferent to his rank.

"Go give Captain Liu Yong my order," Zhou Zhenhua said, crushing his cigarette into the brass tray beside the door. "Then meet me in my study. We are about to find out whether the red phones still ring."
