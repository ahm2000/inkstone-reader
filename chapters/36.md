# Chapter 36: The Hotel Belongs to the Company

Seconds later, Dai Wenbin, General Manager of the Yunhai Grand Hotel, broke into a run.

He had not run in twenty years.

He skidded to a halt at the counter and stared at the black card, then lifted his eyes to the young man in the plain gray jacket, who looked mildly sleepy.

Three years earlier, during the week the hotel first opened, a gray-haired trustee of the holding company had sat in Dai Wenbin's office and delivered a single command. *One day a young man will place this card on your counter. Serve him, ask nothing, tell nobody.*

Dai Wenbin had waited three years, picturing a prince or a decorated general. He had never once pictured a steamed-bun wrapper peeking out of a canvas tote bag.

He bowed from the waist, a full ninety degrees, palms pressed flat against his trousers.

"Sir. Welcome. I am Dai Wenbin, the General Manager. Please forgive us for keeping you waiting at the counter." He lowered his voice. "Your pass is honored here, sir. Please allow me to escort you upstairs."

Twenty clerks stopped breathing.

"General Manager Dai?" Hu Dewei squeaked. "You know Supervisor Chen?"

Dai Wenbin did not know the young man's name. His orders were to ask nothing, and he had never obeyed an order with such fierce discipline in his life.

"Forget the Peony Room tonight," he announced clearly to the lobby. "I am opening the thirty-eighth-floor Cloud Sea Hall for your department, entirely complimentary."

"The Cloud Sea Hall?" a clerk whispered in awe. "My cousin says a single table up there costs over twenty thousand. That's nearly two months of Chen Xuan's pay!"

"Evening, Manager Dai. Thanks." Chen Xuan slipped the black card and the wooden float back into his bag. "Simple home-style dishes are plenty. Nothing fancy. Don't waste food, or my mom will never forgive me."

Dai Wenbin held the golden elevator doors open with both hands. Pan Xiulan hurried inside right behind Chen Xuan.

"Dai Wenbin."

A chime echoed from the opposite elevator bank. A young man in an unbuttoned silk shirt strode out with two friends at his heels, brandishing his phone like a weapon.

It was Qi Zihao, a Silver member who paid twenty thousand yuan a year and boasted of the Yunhai Grand Hotel as "my place" at every banquet.

"I just got a message saying my card was turned away at your front desk," he barked, loud enough for the whole lobby to hear. "In front of Zhang Pengfei's whole company. And you give the Cloud Sea Hall to some guy who got off a city bus? Last month you told me that hall couldn't be booked at any price."

He jabbed his phone toward the reception desk. "Who's taking my seat, Dai Wenbin? My father's company books four hundred room nights here every year. One phone call and that stops. And you behind the counter, give me your name and the hometown on your ID by midnight. By Monday, no hotel in Qingyan will touch you, and your whole family back home will hear why."

The young clerk pressed her palms flat on the marble to hide her shaking.

From inside the elevator, Chen Xuan did not even glance at Qi Zihao. He looked straight at the clerk.

"Miss, what is your name?"

"Xia Mengyao, sir," she whispered.

"Manager Dai, write down her name. Xia Mengyao keeps her job, her pay and her shifts. If any of them change, I will ask you why."

Dai Wenbin immediately uncapped a pen and wrote her name across the back of his own hand.

Chen Xuan had not raised his voice a fraction. Yet the lobby fell so quiet that the courtyard fountain outside could be heard through the glass. Qi Zihao's two friends quietly took a step back from him.

Zhang Pengfei darted into the elevator at the last second, putting as much distance between himself and Qi Zihao as the cabin allowed. His voice shook.

"Supervisor Chen. How on earth do you have a Zero-One pass to the Yunhai Grand Hotel?"

Chen Xuan scratched behind his ear, as if the question were a bit silly.

"It belongs to the holding company. A friend from the company lent it to me for dinner."

Zhang Pengfei let out a long, ragged breath as color rushed back into his cheeks.

"A company card." He laughed, sagging with relief. "You borrowed a client's card. You nearly gave me a heart attack, Supervisor Chen!"

"Borrowed is a good word for it," Chen Xuan said gravely. "Keep it between us, Zhang Pengfei. Don't tell Qi Zihao."

"Maybe he's the owner's secret godson," Pan Xiulan whispered, thrilled.

Chen Xuan pressed two fingers to his forehead and sighed.

* * *

Down in the lobby, the golden elevator doors slid shut on the last of the clerks. Dai Wenbin straightened his back for the first time in seven minutes. When he wiped his neck with a handkerchief, he noticed his hand was still trembling.

The front-desk clerk spoke in a hushed voice, her lips pale. "General Manager. He said he borrowed it. Was he really just borrowing a company card?"

"Borrowing?" Dai Wenbin watched the floor indicator climb toward thirty-eight. "That pass hasn't been used once in three years. So tell me, who exactly is this friend?"

Behind them, Qi Zihao stood rooted to the marble floor. His ears had gone chalk-white.

"Dai Wenbin," he said, far more quietly than before. "Who is that man?"

Dai Wenbin glanced at the Silver card clutched in Qi Zihao's fingers. He had no answer to give a Silver member about a man whose name he was strictly forbidden to learn.

* * *

At eleven that night, Qi Jiancheng finally hung up after twenty minutes of his son's ranting. He then dialed an old friend in the Organization Department, the office that oversaw official appointments.

"Do me a favor and check someone. Chen Xuan, a clerk at Hengxin Logistics. He bumped my son's reservation tonight."

Keyboard keys clicked over the line. "Plain, honest, nothing remarkable," his friend read out. "A completely boring file, Qi Jiancheng."

"Too boring. Open the deeper record."

Silence stretched across the line. When his friend spoke again, his voice had gone paper-thin.

"My screen just went red. PERMISSION DENIED. And right under it: *This access attempt has been logged and reported.*"

"Reported to whom?"

"To the review team. The one above the province. Rumor says that on Monday a man in the provincial office touched that same file, and by evening he was taken to a closed room. Nobody has heard from him since. Don't ask me more. And Qi Jiancheng, tell your son to stay far away from that hotel."

The line went dead. Qi Jiancheng stood alone in his dark study with the phone still pressed to his ear. Cold sweat trickled down his spine.

A second later, his phone began to ring again, displaying a number he had never seen before.
