# Chapter 36: Eight Sentries in the Courtyard

Sharp knocking on the bedroom door broke the quiet of the capital at ten minutes past one on Monday morning. Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua was awake before the third knock ended. Men who carried half the country's ministries on their shoulders rarely slept deep enough to miss hurried footsteps in the hall.

He swung his legs off the bed, reached for his dark wool coat, and opened the door himself.

His secretary, Tang Weimin, stood in the hallway with his glasses sitting crooked on his nose. The younger man's face had turned the color of wet chalk. His left hand gripped the doorframe so tightly that his knuckles shone white, and his chest rose and fell in short, shaky breaths.

"Vice Premier," Tang Weimin whispered, his voice cracking. "There are armed men in the yard."

Zhou Zhenhua did not flinch. He held Tang Weimin's panicked eyes for two steady seconds while he slipped his arms into his coat. "Slow down, Tang Weimin. Breathe. Who is in the yard?"

"I do not know," Tang Weimin stammered, glancing nervously back down the hall. "They are not our guards. They wear plain black gear, full masks, no badges, and no patches. They did not climb the wall. One minute the yard was empty, and the next minute they were just standing there."

Zhou Zhenhua's jaw tightened a tiny fraction. He picked up his pack of cigarettes and brass lighter from the nightstand and stepped into the hall. "What did the outside guard team say?"

"That is the worst part," Tang Weimin said, his voice shaking so badly he had to swallow twice. "I ran down to the duty room three minutes ago. Captain Liu Yong and his six men are sitting on the wooden bench with their guns buttoned inside their holsters and their hands flat on their knees. Captain Liu Yong would not even look up at me. He told me to wake you and tell you not to touch a single weapon. Vice Premier, the guard team may have been bought off. We need to move to the basement safe room right now, or I should call the Central Guard Bureau on my personal phone."

"Put your phone back in your pocket," Zhou Zhenhua said flatly.

Tang Weimin froze with his fingers halfway inside his jacket. "Vice Premier, if this is a coup, waiting here means walking straight into a cage."

"If this were a coup, Tang Weimin, Captain Liu Yong would already be dead in the duty room, and you would never have made it up these stairs." Zhou Zhenhua flicked his brass lighter open, lit a cigarette, and blew a thin stream of smoke toward the ceiling as he started downstairs. "Come with me. Keep your hands out of your pockets and stop shaking."

Tang Weimin paused for one heartbeat, then hurried after him. The whole house felt unnaturally still.

Zhou Zhenhua unlocked the heavy front door and stepped out onto the porch. He stood under the roof eaves, took another slow drag, and swept his eyes across the dark yard from left to right.

One figure stood beside the iron gate. Two stood on either side of the gravel drive. Two held the dark corners beneath the old cypress trees. Two stood along the east walk near the duty room, and the eighth stood right at the foot of the stone steps, thirty paces away, facing the porch.

Eight figures in plain black gear. Their masks had no clear glass, no visible microphones, no rank strips, and no unit marks. None of them raised a rifle, yet every path out of the house was sealed with cold precision. Not one of them shifted his feet or turned his head when the front door opened.

Behind Zhou Zhenhua, Tang Weimin let out a choked breath and stepped back against the doorframe.

"Eight of them," Tang Weimin whispered, his eyes huge behind his glasses. "Vice Premier, they are not even moving. Should I tell the inside guards to draw their pistols and hold the front hall?"

"If a single man inside this house pulls a gun tonight, I will personally send him to a military court if he even lives through the next three seconds," Zhou Zhenhua said. His voice stayed low, yet the cold weight behind it made Tang Weimin stiffen at once.

"Vice Premier," Tang Weimin pressed, panic pushing past his training, "who are they? Army? State Security? Nobody has the authority to surround a Vice Premier's home without a direct warrant from the very top."

Zhou Zhenhua tapped ash into the cold night air, keeping his eyes on the silent figure thirty paces away. "Look at how they stand, Tang Weimin. Army special forces shift their weight every forty seconds to keep their knees loose. State Security officers watch the windows and check their radios. These eight have not moved an inch since we walked outside. They do not need radios, and they do not care whether I stand on this porch or hide in a cellar."

Tang Weimin swallowed hard. "Then who could possibly give them orders?"

"Nobody you have ever seen on television," Zhou Zhenhua replied quietly. He took a long drag on his cigarette, the orange tip glowing bright in the dark. "In thirty years of service, I have only heard their name spoken twice in closed rooms. Both times, the men who said it lowered their voices even though the walls were soundproof."

A cold shiver ran across Tang Weimin's shoulders. "Are they here for you, Vice Premier? Did someone make up a fake file against you?"

Zhou Zhenhua let out a short, dry breath through his nose. "If they were here for me, Tang Weimin, they would not be standing politely in the yard waiting for sunrise. They would already be inside my study, and I would already be sitting in the back of an unmarked car."

"Then why are they outside our door at one in the morning?" Tang Weimin asked, his voice rising before he forced it back down. "Why seal off your home if you are not the target?"

"Because tonight, nobody in this capital is allowed to make a phone call, send a messenger, or whisper a warning to an old friend," Zhou Zhenhua said. He glanced sideways at his pale secretary. "Go down to the duty room. Tell Captain Liu Yong he made the right call. Order every guard on this property to keep his gun in his holster, sit at the table, drink hot water, and keep both hands in plain sight. No shouting, no sudden moves, and no trying to be a hero."

Tang Weimin stared at him as a cold new fear dawned across his face. "Vice Premier, if they have surrounded your yard, what about the other homes on this lane? What about all the ministries?"

Zhou Zhenhua looked out across the silent yard one last time. The eight black-clad guards stood like iron statues in the dark, completely indifferent to his rank and to the trembling secretary behind him.

"Go give Captain Liu Yong my order," Zhou Zhenhua said, pressing his dying cigarette into the brass tray beside the door. "Then meet me in my study. We are about to find out whether the red phones still ring."
