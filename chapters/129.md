# Chapter 129: Slippers on the Balcony

After finishing every last drop of the midnight shrimp broth, Qin Yuhan felt wide awake and full of energy. She slipped her feet into a pair of fluffy cream house slippers, pulled a thick knit cardigan over her silk pajamas, and stepped out onto the wooden lakeside balcony with her tablet and the marked-up bidding draft.

Inside the living room, Chen Xuan washed both porcelain bowls in two minutes flat, wiped the counter dry, and filled his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos with fresh hot jasmine tea. Seeing the cool mist curling off the lake outside, he grabbed a soft cashmere blanket from the sofa and pushed open the sliding glass door.

"You are going to catch a cold out here in slippers, President Qin," Chen Xuan said softly, draping the warm blanket neatly over her shoulders.

Qin Yuhan leaned back against the cushioned wicker chair and pulled the blanket snug around herself. "The cool air keeps my head sharp. Besides, look how quiet the lake is."

She tapped her stylus lightly against the tablet screen, where a clean five-minute presentation outline was already taking shape. Then she tilted her head, peering up at Chen Xuan with a mischievous glint in her eyes.

"Wait a minute," she teased, poking his sleeve with the back of her stylus. "Let us be honest, Chen Xuan. You spotted Article 14 faster than my senior legal counsel, you stayed completely calm sitting next to two provincial secretaries, and you cook better shrimp noodles than a hotel chef. Are you sure you are really just a lazy clerk from the 12th floor?"

Chen Xuan set his green thermos on the small bamboo table and gave her a completely serious look. "Of course I am. Do you know how hard it is to survive on 12,000 yuan a month in Shanghai without developing sharp eyes for sneaky extra fees?"

Qin Yuhan burst out laughing, her shoulders shaking under the cashmere blanket. "Fine, fine. Then how about a formal job offer right now? Come work on the 38th floor of Haiyue Tower as my special advisor. I will double your salary on day one, give you your own corner office, and let you bring your green thermos to every board meeting."

Chen Xuan shook his head without a second of hesitation. "No deal. Terrible working conditions."

"Excuse me?" Qin Yuhan arched an eyebrow, trying hard not to grin. "Half the business graduates in Shanghai would fight for a spot on the 38th floor!"

"Look at the facts," Chen Xuan counted off on his fingers. "First, your executives work until 9:00 PM. At Hengxin Logistics, I clock out cleanly at 4:55 PM. Second, if I work for you, I will not have time to walk through the wet market and pick out the freshest pork ribs before dinner. Third, if my wife is my supervisor, who is going to warm your slippers and rub your shoulders when you get home?"

Qin Yuhan stared at him for a moment, her cheeks turning faintly pink in the warm balcony lantern light. She swatted his arm lightly with her notebook. "Smooth talker. Fine, keep your 12th-floor desk. Just remember you promised to stay on standby as my unpaid midnight chef."

"Lifetime contract," Chen Xuan promised, sitting down in the wicker chair beside her and pouring her a small cup of hot jasmine tea. "Now let me hear your speech for tomorrow morning. Pretend I am Vice Mayor Ding sitting in the front row."

Qin Yuhan immediately sat up straighter, her playful expression shifting into crisp executive focus. She walked through her five-minute breakdown of Article 14, laying out the 200 million yuan bond, the 300-yuan-per-container surcharge, and the narrow criteria tailored for Cai Chenggong's shell operator. Her logic was clean, polite on the surface, and devastating underneath.

When she paused, Chen Xuan cleared his throat pompously and puffed out his chest in an uncanny impression of Ding Yizhen.

"Ahem, President Qin," Chen Xuan drawled with fake bureaucratic gravity. "You misunderstand our good intentions! Article 14 is merely a technical safety clause designed to prevent inexperienced outside carriers from jamming up our precious Jingzhou river channels. Are you saying Haiyue Group does not care about port safety?"

Qin Yuhan giggled at his imitation, then snapped right into her rebuttal without missing a beat.

"Not at all, Vice Mayor Ding," she replied smoothly, her voice crisp and clear. "In fact, national maritime safety standards already require licensed harbor pilots under the Ministry of Transport, which costs less than forty yuan per container. Locking every carrier into a single newly registered private firm at seven times the national rate does not protect port safety. It creates a private tollbooth."

Chen Xuan clapped quietly. "Clean. No personal attacks, just hard numbers and public rules. Once Secretary Sha and Secretary Li hear the phrase private tollbooth, Ding Yizhen will not have a single excuse left to hide behind."

"What if Cai Chenggong claims the draft was only a preliminary discussion copy?" she asked thoughtfully.

"Even better," Chen Xuan smiled. "Then you simply ask Secretary Li and Director Hou to review who printed forty pages of gold-stamped copies and pushed investors to sign letters of intent before the forum even opened."

Qin Yuhan's eyes lit up as she jotted that exact sentence onto her final slide.

Down below along the dimly lit lakeside boardwalk, an older resort night watchman in a thick padded vest strolled past carrying a small flashlight. Spotting the couple on the balcony, he slowed down and smiled up warmly like a friendly neighborhood uncle.

"Still up late, young folks?" the watchman called out in a gentle, unhurried voice. "The late-autumn dew is heavy tonight. Do not stay out in the wind too long, or you will be sneezing at tomorrow's meeting."

"Thank you, Uncle! We are heading inside right now," Chen Xuan called back cheerfully, waving down to him.

The watchman nodded with a kindly smile and continued his unhurried round along the maples.

Qin Yuhan closed her tablet, tucked her cold toes briefly against Chen Xuan's ankle under the table, and leaned her head onto his shoulder with a contented sigh. "Come on, Mr. Unpaid Advisor. Let us get some sleep so I can put on a good show tomorrow."
