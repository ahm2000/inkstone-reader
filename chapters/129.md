# Chapter 129: The Hidden Clause

Qin Yuhan held page 28 closer to the warm terrace lamp and read the third sentence of Article 14 under her breath.

Then she stopped, frowned hard, and read it a second time from start to finish.

"To ensure smooth international clearance and independent safety storage," she whispered, her eyes tracking the tiny print, "all member port cargo lists, vessel weights, fuel records, container serial numbers, and customs codes shall pass through the designated European clearing partner, Northern Ledger Limited of Zurich, prior to local harbor confirmation, with full record copies held by the partner for ten years under Swiss commercial rules."

Silence settled over the stone bench.

Qin Yuhan's fingers tightened until the white paper edge bent under her thumb. Her face went pale, and her polite dinner smile vanished completely.

"Zurich," she breathed, looking up at Chen Xuan in shock. "They buried it right inside a dull maintenance paragraph! Not a single word about Zurich appears on the cover, in the slides, or in Song Baisheng's speech tonight."

"If he mentioned Zurich in the ballroom, half the harbor directors would walk out," Chen Xuan said calmly, taking a quiet sip of jasmine tea. "Put it in tiny print on page 28, call it routine backup storage, and let Deputy Governor Wu pressure everyone to sign before lunch. Nine out of ten companies will never read past the fee chart."

Qin Yuhan's chest rose and fell once as the full scale of the clause hit her.

"This is not a business tool," she said, her voice tight and sharp. "Every port cargo list, ship weight, and customs code across the whole province! Steel shipments for the shipyards, grain reserves, coal barges, chemical drums, all 80 billion yuan of annual coastal trade routed through an overseas company in Zurich before our own docks even confirm the load!"

"And kept on their shelves for ten years," Chen Xuan added quietly. "With live shipping data like that, someone in Europe does not need spies on the docks. They can see every factory's supply chain and every port's weak spots in real time."

Qin Yuhan stared at him for a long moment.

"How did you find this in ten seconds?" she asked softly. "Haiyue Group brought three senior lawyers up to Sha City today. They spent two hours reading this exact booklet in the hotel lounge, and every one of them missed Article 14."

"Lawyers look at the money first," Chen Xuan replied simply, setting his green thermos on the stone railing. "They check the membership fees and exit fines. When someone shouts loudly about a free upgrade on page one, the real price is always hiding in the dullest paragraph near the back."

Qin Yuhan let out a dry, humorless laugh. She closed the booklet sharply against her knee, her mind racing.

"We cannot let them sign this tomorrow morning," she said firmly, reaching for the phone in her blazer pocket. "I have to call my father right now. Haiyue Group will refuse to sign, and we can warn the chairman of Donghai Shipping tonight before the nine o'clock session opens."

"And what will happen at nine o'clock?" Chen Xuan asked evenly.

Qin Yuhan paused with her hand on her pocket. Her brow knitted tightly.

"If I stand up in the main hall and read Article 14 aloud," she said slowly, "Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang will take the microphone back. He will call it a standard international trade link approved by the provincial office. Song Baisheng has already paid or pressured half the freight chairmen in this hotel. They will clap, vote the charter through, and anyone who refuses to sign will lose their port passes by Friday."

She looked down at the dark river water, her jaw set hard.

"Even Haiyue Group cannot win a direct fight against a sitting Deputy Governor inside his own forum before nine in the morning," she admitted bitterly. "By the time my father sends a report higher up, the contracts will be signed and Wanfeng Freight's servers will already be running."

"Then do not argue with them tomorrow morning," Chen Xuan said calmly.

Qin Yuhan turned to him in surprise. "Do not argue? Chen Xuan, we cannot sit there and watch them hand our ports to Zurich."

"I did not say they would succeed," Chen Xuan replied lightly, a faint, steady calm in his eyes. "I only said you do not need to waste your voice shouting across a crowded hall. Go back inside, greet your father's partners, and get a good night's sleep tonight, Miss Qin."

Qin Yuhan studied his quiet face under the terrace lamp. For a second, she remembered Shanghai Harbor, the overnight collapse of Wei Kun's underworld syndicate, and the way Zhou Zhenhua had looked at this plain young clerk as if standing before a mountain.

Her breathing slowed, and the tightness in her shoulders eased.

"You really think someone will stop them before morning?" she asked softly.

"Some doors are heavy to push from the outside," Chen Xuan said mildly. "It is faster when the right offices unlock them before breakfast."

Before Qin Yuhan could speak again, her phone buzzed twice in her pocket. She glanced at the screen and sighed quietly.

"My assistant," she said. "Two port directors from Ninghai are looking for me inside. I should get back before people start asking questions."

She stood up, tucking the white handbook tightly into her black leather folder, and gave Chen Xuan a warm, grateful look.

"Thank you for the tea, Chen Xuan," she said sincerely. "And thank you for page 28. Whatever happens tomorrow, Haiyue Group owes you another big debt."

"Just make sure General Manager Sun gets his coastal routes paid on time," Chen Xuan joked mildly.

Qin Yuhan smiled genuinely, nodded once, and walked back through the glass doors into the bright ballroom.

Once the door slid shut, the stone terrace fell silent again, save for the quiet lap of the river below.

Chen Xuan stood up and walked over to the wide stone railing. Setting his scratched green thermos beside his elbow, he looked out across the dark water toward the city lights of Sha City and reached into the inner pocket of his plain dark windbreaker.

He drew out a thin black private phone with no logo on its back.

His thumb moved steadily across the screen as he typed one short message to **Number 1**:

*Sha City Grand River Hotel. Wanfeng Freight, Song Baisheng, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang, and Marcus Vance. Article 14 sends provincial port cargo data to Zurich. Clear it before sunrise.*

He pressed send, slipped the phone back into his inner pocket, and took an unhurried sip of warm jasmine tea.

Two seconds later, the phone vibrated once against his chest.

Chen Xuan did not even need to look, though he already knew the two-word reply from Number 1:

*Already moving.*

Thirty paces back inside the ballroom, behind a cream-colored pillar, Song Baisheng's aide hurried up to Marcus Vance with a folded hotel slip showing the room numbers for Sun Haoran and Chen Xuan. Li Weiming smiled thinly behind his silver-rimmed glasses, completely unaware that the clock on his entire Sha City plan had just run out.
