# Chapter 157: Number 1's Southern Dossier

The phone rang only once before the line connected.

"Sir," Number 1's steady, respectful voice came through the speaker. Even near midnight, there was no trace of sleep in his tone, only quiet readiness.

Chen Xuan leaned lightly against the cool concrete rail of his fourth-floor balcony. A soft breeze stirred the leaves of the plane tree down by the lane gate, where the plain dark sedan sat silent in the shadows fifty meters away.

"Tell me what you have on Nanhai City," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Nanhai Pier Nine and the Shen Family."

On the other end of the line, there was the brief, crisp sound of a paper folder opening on a desk.

"We already have a complete file ready, sir," Number 1 replied without delay. "Number 19 and Number 88 run our southern branch out of Nanhai City. They have been watching the Shen Family and Nanhai Port for fourteen months."

Chen Xuan looked down at the empty street. "Fourteen months is a long time to watch one local shipping family. What caught their eye?"

"The money did not match the ships," Number 1 answered plainly. "On paper, Shenhai Shipping is a mid-sized coastal firm with twelve cargo boats and a family grip on Pier Nine. Their real yearly profit from normal freight should be around 60 million yuan. Yet over the past three years, more than 2 billion yuan in clean bank transfers has flowed through three small warehouses owned by the Shen Family right behind Pier Nine."

Chen Xuan tapped a finger once against the rail. "Where is that money coming from?"

"The Seven-Harbor Consortium," Number 1 said, his voice dropping half a shade lower. "They are a greedy alliance of seven port families and dirty freight brokers spread across the southern coast. They run night boats, hide untaxed luxury cargo inside normal shipping containers, and wash dirty overseas money through fake port fees and empty container routes."

"And the Shen Family is their gatekeeper in Nanhai," Chen Xuan murmured.

"More than a gatekeeper," Number 1 said. "Last quarter alone, Pier Nine's night shift moved nearly 400 million yuan worth of hidden cash and unmarked crates for the Seven-Harbor Consortium. Because the Shen Family has relatives and old classmates inside the Nanhai Port Inspection Office, nobody checks their night dock slips. Every paper gets stamped and buried before sunrise."

Chen Xuan let out a quiet breath. Now the fake safety hold on Haiyue Group's flagship ship made complete sense.

"Haiyue Group runs clean books," Chen Xuan said slowly. "Their ships carry full customs records, and Qin Yuhan refuses to pay under-the-table pier fees."

"Exactly, sir," Number 1 confirmed. "Ever since Haiyue Group won two southern supply contracts last autumn, their ships have docked at Pier Nine twice a week. Two months ago, a Haiyue cargo clerk nearly walked into Warehouse Four after midnight and saw unsealed crates being loaded onto unmarked trucks. The Shen Family scared the clerk off, paid him 200k yuan to quit, and decided Haiyue Group had to go."

"So the ballast valve excuse is just a club," Chen Xuan said evenly.

"Yes, sir. Number 19 confirmed it this evening," Number 1 replied. "The chief inspector who locked the gate is named Lu Guobin. Three years ago, his son lost 3 million yuan at an underground gambling table in Nanhai. The Shen Family paid the debt in cash the next morning. Since then, Lu Guobin signs whatever paper the Shen Family slides across his desk."

Chen Xuan's face remained calm in the pale streetlamp glow, though a cold glint settled in his eyes. "What about Shen Jingzhou?"

"The eldest grandson of the Shen Family," Number 1 said, turning a page. "Twenty-nine years old. Loud, proud, and hungry to sit at the main table of the Seven-Harbor Consortium. His grandfather is stepping back from daily port work. Shen Jingzhou promised the consortium elders that he would force Qin Yuhan to hand over 30% of Haiyue's southern routes before the end of the week. If Haiyue bleeds 8 million yuan a day at the dock, he thinks she will break by Thursday."

"He picked the wrong person to squeeze," Chen Xuan said mildly.

A brief pause followed on the line. Even over a cheap phone speaker, Number 1 seemed to sense the quiet shift in the air.

"Do you want Number 19 and Number 88 to freeze the Shen Family's accounts tonight, sir?" Number 1 asked. "With the paper trail we hold, we can hand the whole file to the southern coastal customs office by eight o'clock tomorrow morning. Shenhai Shipping would be shut down before lunch."

"No," Chen Xuan said gently. "Not tonight."

"Understood, sir. May I ask what you have in mind?"

"Qin Yuhan's uncle, General Qin Zhengguo, told her to fight this on business ground," Chen Xuan replied, looking up at the dark sky over Qingyan. "He is right. If Nanhai Port gets raided at dawn out of nowhere, the Seven-Harbor Consortium will panic, burn their side books, and blame the Qin Family for pulling strings from the top. I want the Shen Family to show their whole hand in daylight first."

"You are going to Nanhai City yourself?" Number 1 asked softly.

"Yes. Tomorrow's noon flight," Chen Xuan said. "Hengxin Logistics has six containers stuck on that same ship. It is only right for an ordinary office worker to go on a business trip and sort out his company's cargo."

A trace of warmth softened Number 1's steady voice. "A very reasonable business trip, sir. What are your orders?"

"Send Number 7 down to Nanhai City on the first morning flight," Chen Xuan instructed clearly. "Have him meet Number 19 and Number 88 before noon. Tell them to stage two ordinary cars outside the airport arrival lane. Do not approach me at the terminal unless I signal."

"Clear, sir," Number 1 said.

"Second," Chen Xuan continued, "tell Number 19 to print out clean copies of the Shen Family's hidden night ledgers, the 3 million yuan gambling receipt for Lu Guobin's son, and the port records linking Shenhai Shipping to the Seven-Harbor Consortium. Have the southern customs and banking regulators ready to receive a formal tip at five o'clock sharp tomorrow afternoon."

"Five o'clock sharp," Number 1 repeated. "Not a minute earlier?"

"Shen Jingzhou wants to meet Qin Yuhan at Pier Nine at four o'clock," Chen Xuan said calmly. "I will give him one full hour to be polite and open that gate on his own. If he still wants to play tough at five o'clock, then we will let the rules of Nanhai Port bury him."

"It will be done, sir," Number 1 answered. "Number 7, Number 19, and Number 88 will have everything waiting for your word."

"Keep an eye on the rest of the Seven-Harbor Consortium," Chen Xuan added. "If any other family tries to move ships or hide cash once Pier Nine opens, note their names. We will deal with them one by one."

"Understood, sir. Have a safe flight tomorrow."

The call clicked off.

Chen Xuan stood on the balcony for another quiet minute. Down below, fifty meters past the iron gate, the dark sedan under the plane tree did not move an inch. Inside, a team of Black Guard members sat in total silence, keeping their own unexplained watch over the lane.

Chen Xuan slid the glass door shut and walked back inside. From the bottom of his closet, he pulled out a faded gray canvas bag and packed two cotton shirts, dark trousers, a thin windbreaker, and a small tin of jasmine tea leaves. He set the bag beside his shoes, turned off the lamp, and lay down to sleep.
