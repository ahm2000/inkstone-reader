# Chapter 93: Sha Ruijin and Li Dakang's Midnight Reflection

Around midnight in Jingzhou City, light autumn rain tapped steadily against the tall windows of the Handong Provincial Party Committee building. On the sixth floor, only the corner office remained lit. Inside, the air was cool and quiet, carrying the clean aroma of freshly steeped green tea from a white porcelain pot on the mahogany conference table.

Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin sat in a straight-backed chair with his suit jacket draped neatly over the armrest and his cuffs rolled once at the wrists. Across from him sat Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang, his posture upright despite the late hour, his sharp eyes scanning the final page of the evening's joint briefing.

"The tally is confirmed," Li Dakang said, setting the printed report down beside his teacup. "Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping and Chief Procurator Ji Changming have completed the formal detention filings. Gao Yuliang, Zhao Ruilong, and Qi Tongwei are all in secure custody, along with eleven county officials who signed off on their backroom deals."

Sha Ruijin lifted the porcelain pot and poured hot tea into both cups. "That closes the first phase, Dakang. Now comes the harder part."

Li Dakang picked up his cup, took a slow sip, and frowned thoughtfully. "Seeing the full case files laid out side by side made my stomach turn, Secretary Sha. For years, everyone assumed Shanshui Group's greed stayed inside luxury clubs and high-level land auctions in Jingzhou. Looking at the Yunxi County dossiers tonight, I realized how badly we underestimated the reach of that rot."

"It never stays at the top," Sha Ruijin said calmly. "When a Deputy Provincial Secretary trades political protection for corporate kickbacks, and a Public Security Director treats county police badges like private debt-collection tools, the damage runs all the way down to the street level. Look at those Yunxi files. Shanshui's local brokers used fabricated safety violations and trumped-up police complaints to squeeze small hardware shops and family storefronts off Old Street just to assemble a cheap commercial parcel."

Li Dakang set his teacup down with a crisp click. "That is what infuriates me most. For five years I have been traveling across the country begging reputable manufacturers and logistics firms to invest in Jingzhou. I promise them clean approvals, fair courts, and reliable infrastructure. Meanwhile, right under our noses, Qi Tongwei's county cronies were shaking down honest shopkeepers who have paid their taxes for thirty years."

"A city's business climate is judged by its weakest link," Sha Ruijin replied, his voice steady and incisive. "Big corporations can hire teams of lawyers to protect themselves. A small merchant running a storefront in Yunxi County only has the local precinct and the county court. When those institutions fail him, word spreads across the whole province in a week, and no serious investor will trust our contracts."

Li Dakang leaned forward, resting his forearms on the mahogany table. "So arresting Gao Yuliang and Zhao Ruilong only clears the infection. If we stop at headlines and disciplinary announcements, the same habits will grow back under new names within two years."

"Exactly," Sha Ruijin agreed. "The real test ahead for Handong is rebuilding institutional discipline from the ground up. First, we need a full independent audit of the Guangming Peak redevelopment approvals and every municipal credit line issued by the Four National Banks over the past four years. Second, we must separate county public security budgets from local development fees so no precinct chief ever answers to a real estate broker again."

"I already drafted a strict rulebook for the Jingzhou port expansion and the autumn logistics tenders," Li Dakang said immediately, tapping the slim notebook beside his report. "Every bid will be logged on an open provincial portal with blind technical scoring. No closed-door pre-approvals, no last-minute qualification changes, and zero tolerance for shell companies tied to former officials."

Sha Ruijin nodded approvingly. "Good. Pair that with an open grievance review channel for small businesses across Jingzhou and Yunxi County. Any merchant who was hit with an unlawful fine, a forced lease termination, or confiscated stock during Shanshui Group's expansion deserves a fast, transparent hearing and full restitution."

"I will meet with the municipal court president at 8:30 AM tomorrow to set up the review desk," Li Dakang said, his eyes bright with renewed focus. "We will also publish the full list of revoked illegal land permits before Friday so local contractors know the rules have truly changed. Jingzhou's autumn investment summit opens in three weeks. If visiting executives see that we cleaned house cleanly and opened the port bidding to honest competition, capital will come back faster than anyone expects."

"Do not rush the numbers at the expense of the rules, Dakang," Sha Ruijin reminded him gently. "Steady, honest growth beats flashy figures built on hollow land grabs every single time."

"Taken to heart, Secretary Sha," Li Dakang said with a short, genuine smile. He drank the rest of his tea, gathered his notebook, and stood up. "You should get some rest too. Tomorrow's Standing Committee session will run long."

"I will wrap up in ten minutes," Sha Ruijin replied, walking him to the office door.

After Li Dakang's brisk footsteps faded down the quiet hallway, Sha Ruijin returned to the tall window and looked out over the rain-washed lights of Jingzhou City. Traffic moved thinly along the wet boulevards below, calm and orderly. The storm that had hovered over Handong for years had finally broken, and in the cool midnight air, the hard, honest work of rebuilding had already begun.
