# Chapter 93: One Cup of Tea

Inside the cedar pavilion, the morning air was warm around the glowing charcoal brazier.

Hearing footsteps cross the wooden threshold, the ninety-year-old founding Supreme Elder placed both hands on the edge of the bench and began to push himself upright to greet his guest.

Chen Xuan stepped forward smoothly and rested a gentle, steady hand on the elder's forearm, easing him back down onto the thick indigo cushion.

"Please stay seated, Elder," Chen Xuan said softly, his voice carrying the exact unforced warmth he used with his own grandfather, Chen Mingde. "The lake breeze is cool this morning. There is no need to stand on ceremony with someone my age."

The old man paused, looking up into Chen Xuan's calm, open face. A genuine smile crinkled the deep lines beside his eyes.

"A polite young man," the founding Supreme Elder chuckled softly, settling back against the cushion. "And thoughtful, too. This cotton pad is softer than the chair in my study, and the water is just right. Sit down, sit down."

Chen Xuan took the plain wooden stool across the low table.

Without waiting to be asked, he lifted the brown clay teapot with steady, practiced hands. He rinsed the two white porcelain cups with a quick swirl of hot water, poured the rinse cleanly into the bamboo waste bowl, and then filled each cup three quarters full with clear, pale-green tea.

The fragrance of fresh spring leaves rose softly between them, light and clean.

Chen Xuan lifted the first cup with both hands and set it gently in front of the ninety-year-old elder.

"Plain spring leaves, brewed light," Chen Xuan said mildly. "It will warm your stomach without keeping you awake tonight."

"Thank you," the elder said quietly. He wrapped his thin, scarred fingers around the warm porcelain and took a slow sip. "Good. Very good. I have drunk tea in grand state halls with gold-rimmed cups, yet none of them tasted as restful as this."

For several minutes, neither of them spoke of nations, finance, or secret wars.

The morning sun broke through the mist over Lanshui Lake, casting shimmering gold ripples across the ceiling beams of the pavilion. A white egret stepped delicately through the shallow reeds twenty paces from the pilings.

"Your family lives in Lanshui City?" the elder asked gently.

"My parents, Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang, live back in our hometown, along with my grandparents Chen Mingde and Wu Lanying," Chen Xuan answered easily, cradling his own warm cup. "My uncle, Chen Fugen, runs a small repair shop, and I work here at Hengxin Logistics. It is a quiet life. The canteen downstairs makes decent braised pork, and the 6:10 bus usually has an open seat by the window if I leave right on time."

The founding Supreme Elder listened with deep, quiet attention, nodding slowly as if each ordinary detail were precious.

"My own granddaughter tells me I worry too much about how ordinary people are faring," the elder murmured, his gaze drifting out across the sunlit water. "When you live inside tall walls for too many years, you begin to see only numbers on paper. You forget the sound of a morning bus or the smell of a repair shop."

He turned his clear eyes back to Chen Xuan. The gentle smile faded into a profound, steady gravity.

"Two days ago, they brought me the full file on Aegis Meridian," the elder said softly. "One hundred trillion yuan in covert foreign hooks buried deep inside our ports, our banks, and our provincial ministries. Men who swore oaths to guard this country had sold its spine piece by piece."

The old man's scarred hand rested quietly beside his teacup.

"If that web had snapped shut in winter, factories would have closed across twelve provinces. Ordinary workers would have lost their wages overnight. And we did not even see the full depth of the blade until you had already broken it in silence."

Chen Xuan met the elder's gaze without pride and without false modesty.

"I live here too, Elder," Chen Xuan said simply. "My family eats from the same grain markets, drives on the same roads, and keeps their savings in the same banks. When someone tries to burn down the house you live in, you put out the fire. There is nothing grand about it."

The founding Supreme Elder stared at him for a long, quiet moment.

In ninety years of war, reconstruction, and statecraft, he had met emperors of industry, decorated marshals, and brilliant scholars. Almost every one of them wanted a monument, a footnote in history, or a seat closer to the center of power.

Sitting across from him was a young man who had quietly dismantled a global financial syndicate and neutralized four foreign assassins without waking a single sleeping child, and he spoke of it as casually as sweeping rain off a front porch.

"You never asked for credit," the elder said, his voice dropping to a deeply human quietness. "You never asked for a title, a medal, or a word of thanks from the capital. Yet an old man cannot accept a debt this heavy without asking one question."

The founding Supreme Elder leaned forward slightly, his eyes clear and earnest.

"Tell me honestly, young man. Is there anything you want? A position, special protection for your family, a charter, a favor for your friends? While this old bone still draws breath, whatever is right and within my strength, you have only to speak it."

Inside the wooden pavilion, only the gentle lap of lake water filled the silence.

Chen Xuan looked down at his white porcelain cup. A single tender green tea leaf floated slowly to the bottom, settling peacefully in the warm water.

He lifted his head and offered the ninety-year-old elder a mild, genuine smile.

"Just keep the rules fair for ordinary people like my uncle, keep the roads smooth, and let me leave work on time."

The words were spoken softly, without rhetoric and without flourish.

The founding Supreme Elder sat completely still.

For three heartbeats, he did not speak. Then a slow, luminous warmth rose in his old eyes, glistening quietly at the corners before he blinked it back with a soft, deeply moved laugh.

"Keep the rules fair," the old man repeated gently, his voice thick with emotion. "Keep the roads smooth. And let a man leave work on time."

He shook his silver head slowly, looking at Chen Xuan as if he were looking at the very reason his generation had marched through snow and fire seventy years ago.

"In ninety years," the founding Supreme Elder whispered, "I have sat in many rooms and heard many grand promises. That single sentence is the finest answer I have ever heard."

He lifted his plain white teacup with both hands.

"I give you my word, young man. As long as there are honest people standing watch in the Western Hills, the rules will stay fair, the roads will stay smooth, and nobody will disturb your 6:10 bus."

Chen Xuan lifted his own cup lightly, clinking the porcelain rim gently against the elder's.

"Then this tea is more than enough, Elder," Chen Xuan said warmly.

They drank together in peaceful silence while the autumn sun climbed higher over Lanshui Lake, two generations bridged by nothing more than one pot of plain green tea and a shared love for an ordinary, unbroken morning.
