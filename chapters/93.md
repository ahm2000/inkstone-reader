# Chapter 93: Zhao Dehai's Midnight Reflection

Past midnight in Sha City, only two corner windows on the sixth floor of the Provincial Party Committee building still showed light. Inside the office, thick cigarette smoke hung under the green desk lamp.

Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai sat behind his wooden desk. His jacket hung over his chair, and his shirt collar was unbuttoned. Beside his left elbow sat a heavy crystal ashtray full of crushed cigarette butts. In front of him lay two folders: the provincial midnight arrest count on the right, and the slim, red-tabbed Yunxi County file on the left.

A quiet knock sounded at the wooden door.

"Come in," Zhao Dehai said without looking up.

His chief of staff, Xu Haoran, walked in carrying a sealed report folder. The moment the door clicked shut, Xu Haoran coughed lightly against the smoke and stepped forward with stiff, careful steps.

"Secretary Zhao, the midnight report just arrived from the Provincial Joint Inspection Group and the Discipline Commission," Xu Haoran said, setting the folder beside the ashtray. His fingers shook slightly as he pulled his hand back.

Zhao Dehai tapped a fresh cigarette from the pack, lit it with a sharp snap, and blew a gray stream across the lamp. "Read me the top line."

Xu Haoran swallowed hard and adjusted his glasses. "Across Sha Province and Shanghai, forty-one mid-level and senior officials have been taken into custody since Sunday night. That includes three city bureau directors in Shanghai, the former leaders of the Yunxi County Public Security Bureau, two vice chairmen from the provincial building group, and the entire business chain tied to Deng Yongchang."

"Any pushback?" Zhao Dehai asked, his eyes scanning the rows of stamped names.

"None," Xu Haoran said, his voice dropping. "That is what has everyone downstairs shaken, Secretary Zhao. Not one phone call came from Beijing asking for mercy. Not one retired backer tried to step in for Deng Yongchang or his city allies. Men who always bragged about their capital ties signed confessions within three hours."

Zhao Dehai tapped ash into the full tray, his face hard as stone. "They made no calls because the lines they used to dial went dead before sunset."

Xu Haoran shifted uneasily. A thin shine of sweat formed at his temples. He glanced down at the slim Yunxi County file under Zhao Dehai's left hand.

"And Yunxi County?" Zhao Dehai asked quietly.

"Acting Director Wei Kun sent in his night report at ten-thirty," Xu Haoran replied. "The county finance office cleared the shop repair payment voucher this evening. Wei Kun carried it in person to Chen Mingde at Mingde Hardware on Old Street. No escort, no reporters, no fuss."

Zhao Dehai nodded once as smoke curled past his fingers. "Wei Kun knows how to keep his head low and his hands clean. Push his permanent job through the standing committee before Friday."

"Yes, Secretary Zhao," Xu Haoran said. Yet he did not leave. His knuckles tightened around the clipboard pressed against his chest.

Zhao Dehai's eyes flicked sideways through the smoke. "You have been standing there for ten seconds, Xu Haoran. Speak."

Xu Haoran let out a nervous breath and glanced once toward the closed door.

"Secretary Zhao, I have worked in this building for twelve years," Xu Haoran began, his voice tight with quiet dread. "I understand why Deng Yongchang's crime ring was torn down and why his Shanghai backers fell with him. What I cannot understand is the Yunxi County file."

Zhao Dehai leaned back in his leather chair, his eyes unreadable behind the drifting smoke. "Go on."

"Look at the central order that hit our secure desk yesterday," Xu Haoran said, stepping closer and lowering his voice to a whisper. "It set off a sweep across several provinces, hit four hundred people, and crushed three deep-rooted networks in forty-eight hours. Yet the very first line gave exact, step-by-step orders on how to handle one ordinary hardware shopkeeper in Yunxi County."

Xu Haoran's throat tightened. "Just Chen Mingde, a man who sells hinges on Old Street. The Provincial Joint Inspection Group was told to drive Chen Mingde home in person, hand him a paper clearing his name at his front door, make sure neither he nor any family member ever stepped inside a police station, and pay for his shop repairs by tonight."

Heavy silence pressed down on the room as Zhao Dehai's cigarette burned down another half inch.

"Why?" Xu Haoran asked, his voice cracking on the single word. "Who is Chen Mingde to the people who signed that order? I checked his family sheet twice tonight. His brother Chen Jianguo is a retired warehouse worker. His sister-in-law Liu Meifang ran a small stall years ago. His nephew Chen Xuan works as an ordinary office clerk at Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai making twelve thousand yuan a month. There is no political rank anywhere in that family, Secretary Zhao. None."

Zhao Dehai watched Xu Haoran's pale, tense face for five full seconds. Slowly, Zhao Dehai leaned forward and pressed the glowing tip of his cigarette into the full ashtray, grinding it out until the last thin curl of smoke died.

"You checked his family sheet twice?" Zhao Dehai asked, his tone dropping to an icy calm.

Xu Haoran froze at once, the color draining from his cheeks. "Only the basic civil page attached to the county report, Secretary Zhao. Nothing outside the rules."

"Stop checking," Zhao Dehai said flatly.

Xu Haoran's breath caught. "Secretary Zhao?"

Zhao Dehai's eyes locked onto him, sharp, cold, and completely unreadable. "Have you already forgotten what happened in Sha City at nine o'clock tonight? Every outside guard posted around our provincial buildings and airports vanished without leaving a single sign-in record. How many offices in this country can send inspection teams, freeze national crime rings, and post unlisted guards across twelve cities without telling a Provincial Party Secretary?"

Xu Haoran's lips parted, then closed tightly. He shook his head once.

"Then stop measuring an ocean with a county ruler," Zhao Dehai said, resting his palm flat over the Yunxi County folder. "Do not guess. Just make sure Yunxi County is run so cleanly from tonight on that nobody ever has a reason to look at our province twice."

A visible chill ran down Xu Haoran's spine. He straightened sharply, holding his clipboard tight against his ribs.

"I understand completely, Secretary Zhao," Xu Haoran said, fear turning into sharp focus. "Tomorrow morning I will call in the Yunxi County Party Committee and Shanghai's leaders. Zero mercy for shakedown gangs, zero mercy for crooked officers, and monthly checks on every office."

"Do it quietly," Zhao Dehai instructed, waving him toward the door. "No speeches, no banners on Old Street. Leave Chen Mingde and his family completely in peace. If a single petty official tries to flatter them or ask for favors, remove that official before sundown."

"Understood. I will write the orders tonight."

Xu Haoran bowed slightly and slipped out, closing the heavy door without a sound.

Alone in the stale smoke, Zhao Dehai opened the Yunxi County folder one last time. His eyes rested briefly on a single plain line near the bottom: *Nephew: Chen Xuan, twenty-six, office clerk, Hengxin Logistics, Shanghai.*

Zhao Dehai stared at that line for three quiet seconds, closed the folder, and switched off the desk lamp.
