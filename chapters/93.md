# Chapter 93: Sha Ruijin and Li Dakang's Midnight Reflection

Around midnight in Jingzhou City, light rain tapped steadily against the tall windows of the Handong Provincial Party Committee building. On the sixth floor, only the corner office remained lit. Inside, the air was cool and quiet, carrying the faint, clean aroma of freshly steeped green tea from a white porcelain pot on the mahogany conference table.

Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin sat in a straight-backed chair with his suit jacket draped neatly over the armrest and his cuffs rolled once at the wrists. Across from him sat Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang, his posture upright despite the late hour, his sharp eyes scanning the final page of the evening's joint briefing.

"The tally is confirmed," Li Dakang said, setting the printed report down beside his teacup. "Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping and Chief Procurator Ji Changming signed off on the detention orders less than an hour ago. Deputy Provincial Secretary Gao Yuliang is in custody. Shanshui Group head Zhao Ruilong has been detained. Public Security Director Qi Tongwei was stripped of his post and taken in before he could leave his compound."

Sha Ruijin nodded slowly, folding his hands over the polished wood. "And the secondary network?"

"Collapsed," Li Dakang replied bluntly. "Once the evidence tying Gao Yuliang and Zhao Ruilong back to Ministry Division Chief Zhao Dehan and former Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen hit the desk, the rest of the Handa Faction folded like wet paper. Half of them turned themselves in before Hou Liangping even sent cars."

For a moment, only the soft patter of rain against the glass filled the room.

Li Dakang stood up, walked two paces toward the dark window overlooking the wet avenues of Jingzhou City, and let out a long breath. He had spent years fighting to attract clean investment and keep Jingzhou's municipal finances straight, clashing time and again with the entrenched Handa Faction. Tonight's sweep was everything he had hoped to see for Handong Province.

Yet a sharp puzzle still sat heavy in his chest.

"Secretary Sha," Li Dakang said, turning back with his brows knit together, "I have no doubts about the charges. Gao Yuliang sheltered corrupt officials for a decade. Qi Tongwei turned the provincial police into his private shield. Zhao Ruilong drained public land and coal deals through Shanshui Group. Every single one of them earned their cell."

"But?" Sha Ruijin asked quietly, already knowing the question before it left Li Dakang's mouth.

"But look at the timeline," Li Dakang said, gesturing toward the report. "For years, the Handa Faction blocked every inquiry we tried to open. Then a pair of local thugs and a dirty county official try to shake down an simple hardware shop in Yunxi County, and within twenty-four hours a central directive lands directly on our desks, Beijing locks down every escape route, and the entire faction is swept clean."

Li Dakang stepped back to the table, his voice dropping low. "Who is that hardware shop owner in Yunxi County? Why did an simple county family trigger a storm straight from the top?"

Sha Ruijin regarded his colleague for a steady moment. He trusted Li Dakang's integrity completely, which was the only reason he had asked him to stay past midnight.

Slowly, Sha Ruijin reached to the left side of his desk and slid a thin, heavily sealed folder with a crimson tab across the polished mahogany.

Li Dakang hesitated, then opened the cover.

Inside lay only a single heavy sheet of thick cream paper bearing an official central seal. There was no personal profile, no family tree, no photograph, and no name. Across the center, bold red block letters carried one stark instruction:

UNTOUCHABLE CIVILIAN HOUSEHOLD. DO NOT INVESTIGATE.

Li Dakang stared at the page for ten full seconds. The muscles along his jaw tightened slightly as the full weight of that blank space sank in.

"No name," Li Dakang murmured softly. "Not even for the Provincial Party Secretary?"

"Not even for me," Sha Ruijin answered calmly. "When Premier Gu Chengshan's office relayed the directive, the order was simple. Remove the rot in Handong, restore order in Yunxi County, and never look behind that curtain."

Li Dakang closed the red-tabbed folder carefully and slid it back across the desk. A quiet chill mixed with sudden clarity ran down his spine. "Whoever stands behind that household did not even bother to send us a message through provincial channels. They simply drew a line in the sand, and Beijing moved."

"And that is why I showed you this tonight, Dakang," Sha Ruijin said firmly, meeting his eyes. "You run Jingzhou City, and your people work fast. I need your word right now. Never let any clerk, any investigator, or any well-meaning official pry into Yunxi County. Do not send gifts, do not pay courtesy visits, and do not try to guess who the benefactor is."

"You have my word," Li Dakang replied without a second of hesitation. "I have zero interest in digging where I am told not to dig. My job is Jingzhou's economy and the people's livelihood. If that household wants to live in peace and sell brass hinges on Old Street, then peace is exactly what they will have."

Sha Ruijin let a faint, relieved smile touch his lips and lifted the porcelain pot to refill Li Dakang's cup. "Good. Now that the Handa Faction is out of the way, Handong finally has room to breathe. Tomorrow morning, the real work begins."

Li Dakang took the warm cup, looking once more at the rain-washed skyline of Jingzhou City. Somewhere out there in a quiet county town sat a boundary no official in Handong would ever cross again.
