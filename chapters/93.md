# Chapter 93: Zhao Dehai's Midnight Reflection

Past midnight in Sha City, only two corner windows on the sixth floor of the Provincial Party Committee building still showed light. Thick cigarette smoke hung under the green desk lamp.

Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai sat behind his desk, jacket over his chair, shirt collar unbuttoned. Beside his left elbow sat a crystal ashtray full of crushed butts. In front of him lay two folders: the midnight arrest count on the right, and the slim, red-tabbed Yunxi County file on the left.

A quiet knock sounded.

"Come in," Zhao Dehai said without looking up.

His chief of staff, Xu Haoran, walked in carrying a sealed folder. Once the door clicked shut, Xu Haoran stepped forward stiffly.

"Secretary Zhao Dehai, the midnight report just arrived from the Provincial Joint Inspection Group and the Discipline Commission," Xu Haoran said, setting the folder beside the ashtray. His fingers shook slightly as he pulled back.

Zhao Dehai set down his pen. "Read me the top line."

Xu Haoran adjusted his glasses. "Across Sha Province and Shanghai, forty-one mid-level and senior officials have been taken into custody since Sunday night. That includes three city bureau directors in Shanghai, the former leaders of the Yunxi County Public Security Bureau, two vice chairmen from the provincial building group, and the entire business chain tied to Deng Yongchang."

"Any pushback?" Zhao Dehai asked, scanning the stamped names.

"None," Xu Haoran said, voice dropping. "That is what has everyone downstairs shaken, Secretary Zhao Dehai. Not one phone call came from Beijing asking for mercy. Not one retired backer stepped in for Deng Yongchang or his city allies. Men who always bragged about their capital ties signed confessions within three hours."

Zhao Dehai tapped ash into the full tray, face hard as stone. "They made no calls because the lines they used to dial went dead before sunset."

Xu Haoran shifted uneasily, sweat shining at his temples as he glanced at the slim Yunxi County file under Zhao Dehai's left hand.

"And Yunxi County?" Zhao Dehai asked quietly.

"Acting Director Wei Kun sent his night report at ten-thirty," Xu Haoran replied. "The county finance office cleared the shop repair payment voucher this evening. Wei Kun carried it in person to Chen Mingde at Mingde Hardware on Old Street. No escort, no reporters, no fuss."

Zhao Dehai nodded once through the smoke. "Wei Kun knows how to keep his head low and his hands clean. Push his permanent job through the standing committee before Friday."

"Yes, Secretary Zhao Dehai," Xu Haoran said, yet he did not leave. His knuckles tightened around the clipboard against his chest.

Zhao Dehai's eyes flicked sideways. "You have been standing there ten seconds, Xu Haoran. Speak."

Xu Haoran let out a nervous breath and glanced toward the closed door.

"Secretary Zhao Dehai, I have worked in this building twelve years," Xu Haoran began, his voice tight with dread. "I understand why Deng Yongchang's crime ring fell and why his Shanghai backers fell with him. What I cannot understand is the Yunxi County file."

Zhao Dehai leaned back, unreadable behind the drifting smoke. "Go on."

"Look at the central order that hit our secure desk yesterday," Xu Haoran whispered, stepping closer. "It set off a sweep across several provinces, hit four hundred people, and crushed three deep-rooted networks in forty-eight hours. Yet the very first line gave exact orders on how to handle one ordinary hardware shopkeeper in Yunxi County."

Xu Haoran's throat tightened. "Just Chen Mingde, a man who sells hinges on Old Street. The Provincial Joint Inspection Group was told to drive Chen Mingde home in person, hand him a cleared-name paper at his front door, make sure neither he nor any family member stepped inside a police station, and pay for his shop repairs by tonight."

Heavy silence pressed down as Zhao Dehai's cigarette burned another half inch.

"Why?" Xu Haoran asked, voice cracking. "Who is Chen Mingde to the people who signed that order? I checked his family sheet twice tonight. His brother Chen Jianguo is a retired warehouse worker. His sister-in-law Liu Meifang ran a small stall years ago. His nephew Chen Xuan works as an ordinary office clerk at Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai making twelve thousand yuan a month. There is no political rank anywhere in that family, Secretary Zhao Dehai. None."

Zhao Dehai watched Xu Haoran's pale face for five full seconds, then leaned forward and ground his cigarette out in the full ashtray until the last curl of smoke died.

"You checked his family sheet twice?" Zhao Dehai asked with icy calm.

Xu Haoran froze, color draining from his cheeks. "Only the basic civil page attached to the county report, Secretary Zhao Dehai. Nothing outside the rules."

"Stop checking," Zhao Dehai said flatly.

Xu Haoran's breath caught. "Secretary Zhao Dehai?"

Zhao Dehai's eyes locked onto him, sharp and cold. "Have you already forgotten what happened in Sha City at nine o'clock tonight? Every outside guard posted around our provincial buildings and airports vanished without leaving one sign-in record. How many offices in this country can send inspection teams, freeze national crime rings, and post unlisted guards across twelve cities without telling a Provincial Party Secretary?"

Xu Haoran's lips parted, then closed tightly. He shook his head once.

"Then stop measuring an ocean with a county ruler," Zhao Dehai said, resting his palm flat over the Yunxi County folder. "Do not guess. Just make sure Yunxi County is run so cleanly from tonight on that nobody ever has a reason to look at our province twice."

A chill ran down Xu Haoran's spine. He straightened sharply, gripping his clipboard.

"I understand completely, Secretary Zhao Dehai," Xu Haoran said, fear turning into sharp focus. "Tomorrow morning I will call in the Yunxi County Party Committee and Shanghai's leaders. Zero mercy for shakedown gangs, zero mercy for crooked officers, and monthly checks on every office."

"Do it quietly," Zhao Dehai instructed, waving him toward the door. "No speeches, no banners on Old Street. Leave Chen Mingde and his family completely in peace. If a single petty official tries to flatter them or ask for favors, remove that official before sundown."

"Understood. I will write the orders tonight."

Xu Haoran bowed slightly and slipped out, closing the door without a sound.

Alone in the stale smoke, Zhao Dehai opened the Yunxi County folder one last time. His eyes rested on a single plain line near the bottom: *Nephew: Chen Xuan, twenty-nine, office clerk, Hengxin Logistics, Shanghai.*

Zhao Dehai stared at that line for three quiet seconds, closed the folder, and switched off the desk lamp.
