# Chapter 93: Zhao Dehai's Midnight Reflection

Past midnight in Lanshui, only two corner windows on the sixth floor of the Provincial Party Committee compound remained lit. Inside the office, thick cigarette smoke hung beneath the green-shaded desk lamp.

Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai sat behind his mahogany desk, his jacket draped over his chair and his collar unbuttoned. Beside his left elbow sat a heavy crystal ashtray overflowing with crushed filters. In front of him lay two folders: the provincial midnight arrest tally on the right, and the slim, red-tabbed Yunxi County file on the left.

A quiet knock sounded at the oak door.

"Come in," Zhao Dehai said without looking up.

His chief of staff, Xu Haoran, entered carrying a fresh encrypted dispatch folder. The moment the door clicked shut, Xu Haoran coughed faintly against the smoke and approached with stiff, careful steps.

"Secretary Zhao, the midnight consolidation just arrived from the Provincial Joint Inspection Group and the Discipline Inspection Commission," Xu Haoran said, setting the folder beside the ashtray. His fingers trembled slightly as he drew back.

Zhao Dehai tapped a fresh cigarette from the pack, lit it with a sharp snap, and exhaled a grey stream across the lamp. "Read me the top line."

Xu Haoran swallowed hard and adjusted his glasses. "Across Lanshui Province and Qingyan Municipality, forty-one officials at deputy-section level and above have been placed under retention since Sunday evening. Three municipal bureau directors in Qingyan, the former leadership of Yunxi County Public Security Bureau, two vice chairmen from the provincial construction association, and the full commercial chain tied to Deng Yongchang."

"Any resistance?" Zhao Dehai asked, his eyes scanning the columns of stamped names.

"None," Xu Haoran said, his voice dropping. "That is what has everyone downstairs shaken, Secretary Zhao. Not one phone call came from Beijing asking for leniency. Not one retired patron tried to intercede for Deng Yongchang or his municipal backers. Men who usually boast about their capital connections signed confessions within three hours."

Zhao Dehai tapped ash into the overflowing tray, his expression carved from stone. "They made no calls because the lines they used to dial went dead before sunset."

Xu Haoran shifted uneasily, a thin sheen of sweat forming at his temples. He glanced down at the slim Yunxi County file under Zhao Dehai's left hand.

"And Yunxi County?" Zhao Dehai asked quietly.

"Acting Director Wei Kun filed his night report at ten thirty," Xu Haoran replied. "The county finance office cleared the shop-damage compensation voucher this evening. Wei Kun delivered it personally to Chen Mingde at Mingde Hardware on Old Street. No escort, no press, no disturbance."

Zhao Dehai nodded once, cigarette smoke curling past his fingers. "Wei Kun knows how to keep his head low and his hands clean. Push his permanent appointment through the standing committee before Friday."

"Yes, Secretary Zhao," Xu Haoran said. He did not leave, his knuckles tightening around the clipboard pressed to his chest.

Zhao Dehai's eyes flicked sideways through the haze. "You have been standing there for ten seconds, Xu Haoran. Speak."

Xu Haoran exhaled nervously, glancing once toward the closed door.

"Secretary Zhao, I have worked in this building for twelve years," Xu Haoran began, his voice tight with barely suppressed dread. "I understand why Deng Yongchang's syndicate was dismantled and why the Qingyan patrons fell with him. What I cannot understand is the Yunxi County file."

Zhao Dehai leaned back in his leather chair, eyes unreadable behind the drifting smoke. "Go on."

"Look at the central directive that hit our encrypted desk yesterday," Xu Haoran said, stepping closer and lowering his voice to a whisper. "It triggered a multi-province sweep across four hundred people and flattened three entrenched networks in forty-eight hours. Yet the very first clause gave explicit, line-by-line instructions on how to handle one ordinary hardware shopkeeper in Yunxi County."

Xu Haoran's throat tightened. "Just Chen Mingde, a man who sells hinges on Old Street. The Provincial Joint Inspection Group was ordered to escort Chen Mingde home personally, hand him an exoneration certificate at his front door, ensure neither he nor any family member ever stepped inside a police station, and settle his shop compensation by tonight."

Heavy silence pressed down on the room as Zhao Dehai's cigarette burned down another half inch.

"Why?" Xu Haoran asked, his voice cracking on the single word. "Who is Chen Mingde to the people who signed that directive? I checked his family summary twice tonight. His brother Chen Jianguo is a retired depot worker. His sister-in-law Liu Meifang ran a small stall years ago. His nephew Chen Xuan works as an ordinary logistics clerk at Hengxin Logistics in Qingyan making twelve thousand yuan a month. There is no political rank anywhere in that household, Secretary Zhao. None."

Zhao Dehai watched Xu Haoran's pale, strained face for five full seconds. Slowly, Zhao Dehai leaned forward and pressed the glowing tip of his cigarette firmly into the overflowing ashtray, grinding it out until the last curl of smoke died.

"You checked his family summary twice?" Zhao Dehai asked, his tone dropping to an icy calm.

Xu Haoran stiffened immediately, blood draining from his cheeks. "Only the standard civil sheet attached to the county report, Secretary Zhao. Nothing outside protocol."

"Stop checking," Zhao Dehai said flatly.

Xu Haoran's breath caught. "Secretary Zhao?"

Zhao Dehai's eyes locked onto him, sharp, cold, and completely unreadable. "Have you forgotten what happened in Lanshui at nine o'clock tonight? Every external sentry posted outside our provincial compounds and airports vanished without leaving a single sign-in log. How many offices in this country can dispatch provincial inspection teams, freeze national syndicates, and post unrecorded sentries across twelve cities without notifying a Provincial Party Secretary?"

Xu Haoran's lips parted, then closed tightly. He shook his head once.

"Then stop measuring an ocean with a county ruler," Zhao Dehai said, resting his palm flat over the Yunxi County folder. "Don't guess. Just make sure Yunxi County is governed so cleanly from tonight onward that nobody ever has reason to look at our province twice."

A visible chill ran down Xu Haoran's spine. He straightened sharply, clutching his clipboard against his ribs.

"I understand completely, Secretary Zhao," Xu Haoran said, dread sharpening into grim discipline. "Tomorrow morning I will summon the Yunxi County Party Committee and Qingyan's leadership. Zero tolerance for protection rackets, zero tolerance for crooked enforcement, and monthly audits on every bureau."

"Do it quietly," Zhao Dehai instructed, waving him toward the door. "No speeches, no banners on Old Street. Leave Chen Mingde and his family completely undisturbed. If a single petty official tries to flatter them or ask for favors, remove that official before sundown."

"Understood. I will draft the standing orders tonight."

Xu Haoran bowed slightly and slipped out, closing the oak door without a sound.

Alone in the stale smoke, Zhao Dehai opened the Yunxi County folder one last time. His gaze rested briefly on a single plain line near the bottom: *Nephew: Chen Xuan, twenty-six, clerical staff, Hengxin Logistics, Qingyan.*

Zhao Dehai stared at that line for three quiet seconds, closed the binder, and switched off the desk lamp.
