# Chapter 197: Perimeter in the Snow

The wind along the northern ridge carried sharp needles of dry snow. At minus 16 degrees Celsius, even the steel barrels of automatic rifles frosted white within minutes. Along the dark timberline overlooking the old mine entrance, Viktor Orlov's outer sentries stamped their heavy boots and huddled behind snow-covered boulders.

Two mercenaries crouched beneath a wide spruce tree near the eastern slope. One lowered his thermal binoculars and muttered through his scarf in accented English. "Nothing moving out there. Even the wolves have enough sense to stay asleep in this storm."

His partner checked the short-range radio clipped to his collar. "Orlov wants a check-in every fifteen minutes until the morning chopper arrives. Keep your eyes on the logging track."

Neither man noticed the faint rustle of snow dropping from the heavy pine branch directly above their heads.

Number 12 dropped down without a sound, boots sinking softly into a deep drift behind the boulders. Before either sentry could turn, his hands moved in two swift, practiced strikes against the sides of their necks. Both men went limp instantly. Number 12 caught their rifles before the metal could clatter against the rock, eased the unconscious sentries down onto the snow, and clicked their radio switches off.

Down on the narrow logging track, Chen Xuan walked unhurriedly through the falling snow, his green thermos tucked comfortably under one arm. His footsteps were even and relaxed, as if he were taking an evening stroll along the Pudong riverfront instead of walking straight toward an armed mountain bunker.

Higher up the rocky ledge to the west, two snipers lay under white camouflage sheets overlooking the tunnel portal. Number 44 emerged from the dark shadow of a granite outcrop behind them. He moved like part of the blowing snow. First, he clipped the thin wire connecting a buried motion sensor to its battery pack. Then he stepped behind the two snipers, disabling both in three quiet seconds and binding their wrists neatly with heavy plastic ties.

No gunshots echoed through the valley. No warning flares lit the dark sky.

One by one, across the snowy slopes and timbered hollows, the remaining outer sentries vanished from their posts. Near a frozen stream bed to the north, a patrol pair stopped to warm their gloved hands around a pocket heater. Number 12 closed in through the blowing powder, swept both men off their feet in one fluid motion, and tapped them unconscious before either could reach his sling. Farther down the slope, Number 44 neutralized the two roving guards near the diesel exhaust vents and cleared their spare magazines into the snow. Within eight minutes, all twelve of Orlov's exterior guards had been neutralized and carried into an insulated wooden generator shed beside the ridge so they would not freeze in the night storm.

Near the mouth of the concrete tunnel stood the real obstacle.

Heavy steel blast doors sat recessed ten meters into a solid granite face, framed by rusted mine rails half buried in snowdrifts. Thick frost coated the concrete arch above the entrance, where red warning paint had faded decades ago. Bolted along that reinforced concrete arch were six steel crates packed tightly with industrial demolition blocks. Thick black trigger cables ran from the crates through a steel conduit straight into the underground bunker.

Number 7 climbed up to the concrete ledge and studied the wiring under the dim beam of a tiny red penlight. Beside the primary cable ran a thinner copper return line.

Number 7 knew immediately how Viktor Orlov had built the trap. If anyone simply cut the main cable or jammed the bunker's electronics, the sudden drop in circuit resistance would trigger a backup relay and blow the mountain mouth shut.

Instead of using electronic jammers, Number 7 pulled a small folding toolkit from his parka pocket. His gloved fingers worked with steady, unhurried precision. He unscrewed the waterproof brass collars on each steel crate, one after another, and slid the mechanical primer caps cleanly out of the demolition blocks. He left the main electric wires untouched and clipped a tiny bridge resistor across the junction box so the control panel inside the vault would continue reading a steady green signal.

The explosives were now nothing more than harmless blocks of clay sitting in the snow.

Down near the tunnel apron, Number 12 finished securing the side air-intake grate and wired a silent cutoff into the exterior security camera junction box. Above the ridge, Number 44 planted a small remote breaker on the hidden satellite dish feed, then descended quietly to the heavy hydraulic override box set into the concrete wall beside the blast door. Everything was staged and waiting on a single command.

Snow continued to drift lazily across the concrete apron in front of the tunnel. Number 7 dropped smoothly back to the ground, brushed the frost from his knees, and walked down the slope to meet Chen Xuan.

"Young Master, the outer perimeter is completely clear," Number 7 reported in a quiet, steady voice. "All twelve sentries are tied up inside the heated generator shed. Every demolition charge on the tunnel arch has been mechanically disconnected while keeping the interior indicator circuit live. Number 12 and Number 44 are set to drop their cameras and satellite link on your word."

Chen Xuan stopped a short distance from the recessed steel door and glanced at the screen of his phone. The digital clock read 10:18 PM. Back in Shanghai, Qin Yuhan was probably finishing her evening skincare routine and getting ready for bed.

"Not bad," Chen Xuan said calmly, slipping his phone away. "No need to leave them waiting in the dark. Cut the lines and open the door."

"Yes, Young Master," Number 7 replied, tapping his earpiece once.

At that exact second, the exterior security cameras blinked off in crisp sequence and the hidden satellite dish above the ridge went dead. Beside the wall, Number 44 bypassed the magnetic lock on the hydraulic override box with a clean mechanical turn and pulled the manual release lever. Deep inside the frozen rock, heavy hydraulic pistons hissed to life.
