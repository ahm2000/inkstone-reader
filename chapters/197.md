# Chapter 197: Perimeter in the Snow

A thick silence hung over the snow valley of Changbai. The wind through the high pines made only a faint, rushing sigh, and the fresh snow muffled every natural sound along the mountain slope.

Inside the heated wooden cabin of the northern watch post, two mercenary sentries wearing white winter camouflage vests sat beside an electric stove. A heavy assault rifle rested against the wall between them, and a video monitor on the desk showed four flickering black and white views of the perimeter fence.

"Cold out there," the younger guard muttered in Russian, blowing into his cupped hands as he glanced at the snow piling against the double-paned window. "Another two hours until the shift change. When does the supply truck arrive from the valley?"

The older sentry did not answer. He was staring at the second screen on the console.

The infrared sensor covering the western fence line had not tripped, and the motion alarm remained completely silent. Yet on the monitor, a faint dark silhouette seemed to stand right in the middle of the cleared snow lane between the spruce trees.

"Did you see that?" the older guard whispered, reaching for his rifle.

"See what? A deer?" the other asked, turning toward the glass.

Before his hand could touch the wooden rifle stock, the heavy timber door of the watch post opened without a latch click, without a creak, and without a breath of wind.

A tall figure clad in matte-black tactical gear stood silently on the threshold. A plain black balaclava concealed every feature of the face, leaving only two calm, dark eyes visible beneath the hood. There were no unit badges, no flags, no ranks, and no serial numbers anywhere on the black fabric.

The older mercenary lunged for the alarm button beneath the desk.

He never reached it.

The black figure moved forward with unnatural speed, closing the four-meter distance across the wooden floor without making a single sound. A gloved hand struck with surgical precision, landing on the nerve junction at the side of the guard's neck. The older mercenary collapsed instantly onto the wooden bench, his eyes rolling back as unconsciousness took him.

The younger guard spun around in horror, opening his mouth to yell a warning into his radio collar.

A second black-clad operative appeared behind him like a shadow cast by the winter trees. With one swift, downward palm strike to the base of the skull, the younger guard went limp and dropped quietly into his chair.

Neither guard had managed to fire a shot, click a radio button, or trip a single perimeter wire.

The first black-clad operator stepped to the desk, pulled a thumb-sized black override device from his utility belt, and plugged it directly into the master junction box of the video monitor. The surveillance feed flickered once, looped a thirty-second recording of empty snow, and locked into a permanent freeze.

Outside the cabin, the same silent harvest took place across the entire northern valley.

Along the eastern ridge, three hidden sniper nests built into hollowed pine logs were cleared within forty seconds. In each nest, the mercenary marksmen were neutralized before their gloved fingers could tighten around their triggers. Down by the heavy steel gate of the timber mill, four armed guards patrolling the fence were caught from behind, disarmed, bound with zip ties, and dragged quietly into the heated maintenance shed.

Five minutes after the operation began, twenty-eight elite mercenaries guarding the outer compound had been placed into silent custody without a single gunshot echoing across the mountains.

On the snowy slope fifty meters back, Chen Xuan walked unhurriedly down the cleared flagstone trail. He held his scratched green thermos in his left hand, his warm dark coat buttoned neatly, stepping lightly across the powdery drifts.

Number 7 and Number 12 walked three paces behind him in their clean white winter jackets, carrying the master security schematics on a flat tablet.

"Outer perimeter is completely secured, sir," Number 7 reported in a quiet tone. "All twenty-eight outer guards have been neutralized and detained. Surveillance cameras are feeding looped imagery to the central command bunker. The surface timber mill is ours."

Chen Xuan paused near an ancient snow-dusted birch tree, looked at the quiet lumber yard below, and unscrewed his thermos. A thin wisp of fragrant steam curled into the icy air as he took a sip of hot tea.

"Any movement from the underground entrance?" Chen Xuan asked gently.

"None, sir," Number 12 replied, checking the sensor telemetry. "The heavy blast doors are sealed from the inside. Viktor Orlov and Kou Zhenshan are both in the lower control center on the third basement level. Orlov's personal security team of twelve men remains stationed around the vault entrance."

"And the demolition circuit?"

"Our reconnaissance specialists identified the master detonator line running through the ventilation shaft," Number 12 said. "The Black Guard technical team has already cut the remote antenna feed and placed a mechanical bypass across the blasting caps. Even if Orlov presses his panic switch ten times, not a single grain of dynamite will ignite."

Chen Xuan gave an easy smile, screwing the green lid back on until it locked into place. "Good. It would be a terrible shame if 300 tons of gold were buried in the mud just because an angry mercenary threw a tantrum."

He stepped off the snowy path and walked toward the entrance of the largest timber warehouse at the center of the yard.

Inside the warehouse, beneath stacks of heavy pine logs and rough-sawn lumber, a massive steel trapdoor set flush into the concrete floor stood waiting. A heavy electronic lock gleamed beneath a yellow warning sign.

Two black-clad operatives stood beside the hatch, their gloved hands resting loosely at their belts. When Chen Xuan approached, they stepped back half a pace and bowed their heads in absolute deference, their dark eyes focused entirely on the floor.

"Open it," Chen Xuan said simply.

One of the black operatives stepped forward, touched an encrypted bypass key to the magnetic lock, and pulled the heavy steel handle upward. With a deep hiss of hydraulic pressure, the two-ton blast door swung smoothly open, revealing a brightly lit concrete staircase descending deep into the frozen mountain rock.
