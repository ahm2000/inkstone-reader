# Chapter 7: Fuxing New Village

Yunxi County settled into sleep early once the autumn wind blew off the river. By nine in the evening, the stalls along West Street had pulled down their iron shutters, leaving only a late-night pharmacy and a steamed-bun shop casting pale squares onto the damp concrete.

Number Fourteen guided the understated black sedan off the main avenue and stopped two blocks south of Fuxing New Village beneath an overhanging camphor tree out of sight of the compound gate.

"Two hundred meters straight ahead to the east arch, Boss," Number Fourteen said quietly. "Building Two is on the left past the bicycle shed. Our perimeter team confirmed no Hongrui Real Estate lookouts followed your father home from Old Street Station."

"Thank you," Chen Xuan said, stepping out with his scuffed canvas bag and dark-green steel thermos. "Keep me updated only if something changes with Chen Mingde or Old Street. Otherwise, let the teams work."

"Yes, Boss."

The door clicked shut softly, and the dark sedan glided silently around the corner into the shadows.

Chen Xuan adjusted the canvas strap over his faded gray jacket and walked into Fuxing New Village, a thirty-year-old compound of six-story red-brick walk-ups with peeling cream plaster and balconies draped in laundry lines. The evening smells of his childhood wrapped around him: simmering ginger, stir-fried cabbage, and damp stairwell cement.

Ahead, beneath the dim yellow corner streetlamp outside Building Two, a lone figure was pacing back and forth beside an old dark-blue electric scooter whose front wire basket held a folded yellow rain poncho.

Chen Jianguo was fifty-four, a man who had spent thirty years driving municipal maintenance trucks without complaining of a sore back. Tonight, his shoulders were slumped deep inside his thin brown corduroy jacket, his head bowed toward the wet gravel as he paced three steps left and three steps right. Four hours earlier, before Chen Xuan's jet had lifted off from Qingyan, Chen Jianguo had ridden that scooter to Old Street Station with a thermos of jasmine tea and a clean sweater for his younger brother Chen Mingde. A young duty officer had refused to let him inside or pass the sweater through the window, slamming the glass shut in his face. Chen Jianguo had stood outside the iron gate in the cold drizzle for an hour before riding home defeated.

As Chen Xuan stepped closer, his rubber-soled shoes crunched softly against loose grit.

Chen Jianguo spun around instantly. Seeing his eldest son emerge from beneath the flickering streetlamp, relief and frantic desperation collided on his weathered face.

"Chen Xuan!" Chen Jianguo hurried forward three strides and gripped his son's right forearm with both hands. His palms were dry, rough with calluses, and trembling from the evening chill and hours of helpless tension. "How did you get back so fast? I thought you would not make it until tomorrow morning."

"I caught a ride straight into Lanshui and came over from there, Dad," Chen Xuan answered gently, his voice steady and warm. "Why are you standing down here in the wind? Your hands are freezing."

"How can I sit upstairs?" Chen Jianguo's voice cracked, hoarse from worry. He glanced anxiously toward the lit sixth-floor windows of Building Two. "Your Second Aunt Wu Lanying has been crying since four in the afternoon. Your Second Uncle Chen Mingde has never even argued loudly with a neighbor in his life, and they put iron handcuffs on him in front of the whole street!"

Chen Jianguo tightened his grip on Chen Xuan's sleeve, leaning closer as panic edged back into his eyes. "Listen to me, Chen Xuan. Your Mom does not know you are down here yet. Come with me right now. Get on the back of my scooter and we will ride back to Old Street Station tonight. Station Director Cheng Biao is still on duty; I saw his black car parked inside the courtyard earlier. If they will not open the gate, I will kneel right there on the concrete outside his office until he listens to reason. Chen Mingde has a bad lower back! He cannot sleep on a cold wooden bench in a holding cell all night!"

Chen Jianguo tugged Chen Xuan toward the electric scooter, his breathing shallow and uneven, the pride of a lifetime stripped bare by a brother's terror.

Chen Xuan did not move an inch.

His feet stayed planted calmly on the damp concrete. Instead of pulling away, Chen Xuan shifted his green thermos into his left hand and placed his right hand gently over both of Chen Jianguo's trembling hands. His palm was warm, steady, and completely immovable.

Chen Xuan looked directly into his father's reddened eyes. His tone did not rise, yet every word landed with quiet, absolute finality.

"Dad, listen to me," Chen Xuan said softly. "Neither you, nor Mom, nor I will ever step foot inside that police station. Second Uncle will be fine. Let's go upstairs."

The night air around Building Two seemed to go still. Across the courtyard, a television murmured behind a closed window, and a bicycle bell chimed faintly beyond the archway.

Chen Jianguo froze, his fingers loosening against his son's sleeve as he stared up into Chen Xuan's face beneath the streetlamp.

In Chen Jianguo's memory, his eldest son had always been quiet, polite, a little too thin, the kind of boy who kept his head down at an ordinary office desk in Qingyan and never raised his voice. Yet looking into Chen Xuan's dark eyes tonight, Chen Jianguo felt a sudden, startling jolt in his chest. There was no youthful panic there, no helplessness, and no uncertainty. There was only an unfathomable, mountain-deep calm that made Chen Jianguo's frantic breathing slow down almost against his will.

"Chen Xuan," Chen Jianguo murmured, his voice dropping an octave. "You have been in Qingyan for years. That Hongrui Real Estate boss has ties everywhere, and Station Director Cheng Biao is his drinking partner. What if they force Chen Mingde to sign a confession before morning?"

"Begging at an iron gate will not soften a man like Cheng Biao, Dad," Chen Xuan replied evenly, his thumb resting reassuringly against his father's calloused hand. "If you kneel outside Old Street Station tonight, he will only think our family is easy to crush and squeeze Second Uncle twice as hard tomorrow. Trust me. I have already contacted reliable people who handle these matters properly. Right now, Second Uncle is safe, warm, and unharmed. Nobody inside that building can force him to sign anything."

Chen Jianguo stared at his son for three long seconds. He could not guess what kind of people an ordinary clerk in Qingyan could reach in a single evening, yet the quiet weight in Chen Xuan's voice settled over his chest like a heavy blanket.

A long, shaky breath left Chen Jianguo's lips. Slowly, the tension drained from his slumped shoulders. He nodded once, clicked the wheel lock onto his scooter, and reached gruffly for his son's canvas bag to hide the moisture stinging his eyes.

"Give it here," Chen Jianguo muttered. "Six floors is still a climb, and you worked all day."

Together, father and son stepped into the dim stairwell of Building Two. The motion-sensor bulb flickered on with a soft buzz, lighting the worn terrazzo steps as they climbed floor by floor toward the warm yellow light leaking from under the sixth-floor door.
