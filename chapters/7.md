# Chapter 7: The Zero Guard

That night, an hour before Chen Xuan opened the sealed line, the bench in Chen Mingde's cell was bare concrete. The cold came straight up through it.

Chen Mingde sat on his folded jacket with his hands tucked under his arms.

At half past nine, Lei Bo had stopped by with Hongrui's sale form and a pen.

"Sign, Chen Mingde. Go home, take a hot shower. Why put yourself through this?" He tapped the pen against the bars. "Your daughter studies in Wuhan, right? She graduates next summer. The big firms run a background check on every applicant. A father with a detention record fails that check. Sign, and the record is never written."

"Please take it away." Chen Mingde used the same tone he used on customers who asked for an unreasonable discount.

His heart pounded so hard he was sure the whole cell could hear it. Still, he did not sign.

He thought of his daughter in her dorm. She would never know any of this unless he told her.

At about ten o'clock, footsteps came down the corridor for the night check.

Wei Kun, the thin duty officer with tired eyes, stopped at the steel door with a clipboard under his arm. Cheng Biao had ordered the night shift to give the Old Street holdouts nothing warm, and let the cold bench do the talking.

Under the corridor camera, Wei Kun paused as if he were inspecting the cell. Then he slid a paper cup of hot water through the slot.

"Drink it slowly while it's hot. Is your heart holding up?"

"Thank you, Officer." Chen Mingde held the cup in both hands. "Will someone tell my family tomorrow why I am here?"

Wei Kun glanced at the camera and kept his voice flat. "By procedure, the written notice goes out within twenty-four hours. Keep your jacket under you, so the cold doesn't settle in."

"You're a good man, Officer. Don't get yourself in trouble for me."

Wei Kun gave one stiff nod, marked his clipboard, and walked on. Back at the duty desk, he sat down in front of the blank time box on the detention form.

For twelve years he had written whatever Cheng Biao told him to write. Tonight, for the first time, he knew what he would not put down: a false hour.

* * *

Minutes after Chen Xuan sent his order, a large map glowed on the study wall of Number One, head of the Black Pine Guard. The study stood in a walled courtyard in the east of Qingyan City. Two hundred and fourteen green dots shone across the map.

Number One had read Number Thirty-four's report that evening. Since then he had obeyed the Boss's order: record everything, touch nothing. His hand had been tight on his teacup all evening.

One dot, in Yunxi County, turned yellow and began to blink. The duty captain's voice came over the speaker, low and angry.

"Number One, every team on that street is asking for permission to move."

"The order stands. Record, and touch nothing."

"Yes, Number One. A man came up to the Boss's door with a paintbrush and left. Our snipers had him in their sights the whole way, and two of our people waited behind the fifth-floor door with guns drawn. He carried no weapon, so under the Boss's rule that nothing shows, we held fire. The Boss threw the brush in the trash and went into the bathroom."

Before Number One could answer, the console on his desk chimed once. A dark crimson border lit up. It had not lit in years.

*ABSOLUTE ORDER TRIGGERED DIRECTLY BY SUPREME. ZERO GUARD ACTIVE. SECTOR: YUNXI COUNTY.*

Number One's cup hit the saucer so hard that the saucer cracked down the middle. The color left his face.

He did not hold an activation key for the Zero Guard. He had no command and no permission over them. Only Chen Xuan could wake them. Nobody else in the Black Pine Guard even knew a force above their roster existed.

Worse, the Zero Guard had no small mode and no half measures. Once woken under an Absolute Order to audit a compromised perimeter and root out an unknown hostile network touching the Boss's family, their built-in protocol automatically fired at full scale: suspending Black Pine, sealing a hundred-kilometer shell, and freezing every official channel across the province and capital so not a single tip-off, wire transfer, or escape could slip through while they dug up every root.

Number One took up the handset. "Every armed Black Pine unit comes outside the ten-kilometer line. Those living as civilians stay where they are. They watch, record, and touch nothing. Tell Number Seven not to touch Yan Tiehu. That man no longer belongs to us. Do not ask who is coming, do not look at them, and do not get in their way."

"Number One, who is coming?"

Before the captain finished, the heavy doors of the study swung open without a sound.

Five figures in matte-black gear and featureless full-face masks walked in. Two stood by the door, two by the windows, and one across from Number One's desk. The wall map flickered, and the green dots died. A crimson banner replaced them.

*BLACK PINE GUARD COMMAND SUSPENDED. ALL OPERATIONAL AUTHORITY REVOKED. STANDBY UNDER ZERO GUARD MONITORING.*

"Number One!" The captain's voice cracked with raw panic. "Every console in our command van just locked. Five masked figures walked through our sealed doors. They took our weapon keys."

The figure across from Number One laid a black card on the desk. Its voice was flat, neither a man's nor a woman's.

"Zero Order. Black Pine suspended."

Then it stepped back two paces and stayed mute.

Number One did not argue. He stood straight, bowed his head once, and spoke into the speaker so every officer on the line could hear.

"You heard the order. Every armed operative puts down his weapons and stands still under Zero watch. Those of you who live among civilians stay in place. You may watch, record and report. You may not move, touch or stop anything until the Boss gives the Order back." His voice was heavy with shame. "Remember tonight. We grew soft around him. When the Zero Guard hands the watch back, every man on this roster will be ten times more careful, or he will not wear the Black Pine again."

Across the Black Pine network, two hundred and fourteen operatives stood frozen under masked eyes, or sat still in their kitchens, vans and offices, watching. None of them had the power to lift a finger.
