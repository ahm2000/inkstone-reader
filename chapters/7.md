# Chapter 7: Fuxing New Village

By nine in the evening, most shutters along Yunxi County's West Street had rolled shut. Number Fourteen eased the black sedan off the avenue and stopped two blocks south of Fuxing New Village, out of sight of the compound gate.

"Two hundred meters straight ahead to the east arch," Number Fourteen said quietly. "Building Two is on the left past the bicycle shed. Our perimeter team confirmed no Hongrui Real Estate lookouts followed your father home."

"Good," Chen Xuan said, lifting his scuffed canvas bag and dark-green steel thermos. "Message me only if something changes with Chen Mingde or on Old Street. Otherwise, let the teams work."

"Understood."

Chen Xuan stepped out and closed the door softly. The black sedan glided around the corner and vanished.

Adjusting the strap over his faded gray jacket, Chen Xuan walked into Fuxing New Village, a thirty-year-old compound of six-story red-brick walk-ups smelling faintly of simmering ginger and damp cement.

Outside Building Two, beneath a flickering corner streetlamp, a lone man paced beside an old dark-blue electric scooter with a folded yellow rain poncho in its wire basket.

Chen Jianguo was fifty-four, a municipal maintenance driver who had spent thirty years hauling heavy iron without complaint. Tonight his shoulders hunched inside his thin corduroy jacket, his head bowed toward the gravel.

Chen Xuan's shoes crunched softly on loose grit.

Chen Jianguo spun around instantly. Seeing his eldest son step into the light, stunned relief and frantic desperation collided on his weathered face.

"Chen Xuan!" Chen Jianguo hurried forward three strides and gripped Chen Xuan's forearm with both hands. His calloused palms were cold and shaking. "How did you get back so fast? Your mother said the late train would not reach Lanshui until midnight!"

"I caught a direct ride into Lanshui and came straight over, Dad," Chen Xuan said gently. "Why are you standing down here in the wind? Your hands are freezing."

"How could I sit upstairs?" Chen Jianguo's voice cracked, raw and hoarse. He glanced toward the lit sixth-floor windows. "Your Second Aunt Wu Lanying has been crying since four o'clock. Your Second Uncle Chen Mingde has never argued loudly with a neighbor in twenty years, and they snapped handcuffs on him in front of the whole street!"

Chen Jianguo's fingers dug tighter into Chen Xuan's sleeve, his eyes bloodshot with exhaustion and helpless shame.

"I rode to Old Street Station four hours ago," Chen Jianguo whispered rapidly. "I packed hot jasmine tea and his thick wool sweater. The young officer at the gate would not even pass the sweater through the glass. He slammed the window in my face and told me to move. I stood outside that gate in the rain for an hour, Chen Xuan. Nobody even came out."

A brief, cold shadow crossed Chen Xuan's eyes before vanishing. "You should not have stood in the rain, Dad."

"What else could I do?" Chen Jianguo tugged urgently at Chen Xuan's arm, glancing back at the scooter. "Listen to me. Your Mom does not know you are down here yet. Get on the back of my scooter right now. We will ride back to Old Street Station together. Station Director Cheng Biao is still on duty. I saw his black sedan inside the courtyard."

"Dad," Chen Xuan said quietly.

"Hear me out!" Chen Jianguo's breathing turned shallow, the pride of a lifetime stripped bare by a brother's terror. "If they refuse to open the gate, I will kneel on the concrete outside Cheng Biao's office until he listens! Chen Mingde has a bad lower back! He cannot sleep on a cold wooden bench in a holding cell all night!"

Chen Jianguo pulled harder on his son's sleeve.

Chen Xuan did not move an inch.

His feet stayed planted squarely on the damp concrete. Shifting his thermos into his left hand, Chen Xuan placed his right palm gently over both of Chen Jianguo's trembling hands. His grip was warm, steady, and immovable.

Chen Xuan looked straight into his father's reddened eyes. His voice did not rise, yet every word fell with quiet, absolute finality.

"Dad, listen to me," Chen Xuan said softly. "Neither you, nor Mom, nor I will ever step foot inside that police station. Second Uncle will be fine. Let us go upstairs."

Chen Jianguo froze, his fingers loosening against Chen Xuan's sleeve as he stared into his eldest son's face.

In Chen Jianguo's memory, Chen Xuan had always been quiet, polite, a little too thin, keeping his head down at an ordinary office desk in Qingyan. Yet looking into those dark eyes tonight, Chen Jianguo felt a sudden jolt in his chest. There was no youthful panic there, no helplessness, no uncertainty. Only an unfathomable, mountain-deep stillness that forced Chen Jianguo's frantic breathing to slow against his will.

"Chen Xuan," Chen Jianguo murmured, his voice dropping an octave. "Hongrui Real Estate's Chairman Deng has ties in every bureau, and Station Director Cheng Biao drinks at his table every week. If we just sit upstairs, what if they force Chen Mingde to sign a confession before morning?"

"Begging at an iron gate will not soften Cheng Biao, Dad," Chen Xuan replied evenly, his thumb resting reassuringly across his father's knuckles. "If you kneel outside Old Street Station tonight, he will not feel pity. He will decide our family is easy to crush and squeeze Second Uncle twice as hard tomorrow."

Chen Jianguo's jaw tightened, his eyes dropping to the handlebars. "Then what do we do? Just wait for dawn?"

"We let the right people handle it," Chen Xuan said calmly. "On my way back, I contacted reliable people who deal with matters like this properly. They are already working on it tonight. Right now, Second Uncle is safe, warm, and unharmed. Nobody inside that building can touch him or force him to sign anything."

"Reliable people?" Chen Jianguo searched his son's face line by line. "Chen Xuan, do not tell me fairy tales just to calm me down. Who can an ordinary clerk in Qingyan call at nine on a Friday night?"

Chen Xuan did not flinch or look away. A small, reassuring crease softened the corners of his eyes. "Have I ever lied to you about something that mattered, Dad?"

Chen Jianguo opened his mouth, then closed it, staring at his son for three long seconds. Chen Xuan had never boasted, never exaggerated, and never made a promise he could not keep. The quiet weight in his voice settled over Chen Jianguo's chest like a steady hand.

A long, shaky breath left Chen Jianguo's lips. Slowly, the rigid knot between his shoulders gave way. He blinked hard, turned quickly toward the scooter to hide the moisture stinging his eyes, and clicked the steel lock through the rear wheel.

Turning back, his expression gruff again, he pulled the canvas bag straight off Chen Xuan's shoulder.

"Give it here," Chen Jianguo muttered, clearing his throat. "Six floors is still a climb, and you worked all day."

"It is not heavy, Dad," Chen Xuan said with a faint smile.

"Less talking, more walking. Your mother has been keeping soup hot since seven."

Side by side, father and son stepped into the dim stairwell of Building Two. Chen Jianguo climbed ahead with a noticeably steadier stride, glancing back once at the second-floor landing to make sure his son was right behind him.
