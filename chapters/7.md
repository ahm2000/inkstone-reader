# Chapter 7: Fuxing New Village

By nine in the evening, most shop shutters along Yunxi County's West Street had rolled down. Number 14 turned the black sedan off the main road and stopped two blocks south of Fuxing New Village, out of sight of the front gate.

"Two hundred meters straight ahead to the east arch," Number 14 said quietly. "Building Two is on the left past the bike shed. Our watch team confirmed no Hongrui Real Estate lookouts followed your father home."

"Good," Chen Xuan said, picking up his worn canvas bag and dark-green steel thermos. "Message me only if something changes with Chen Mingde or on Old Street. Otherwise, let the teams work."

"Understood."

Chen Xuan stepped out and shut the door softly. The black sedan slid around the corner and vanished into the dark.

Adjusting the strap over his faded gray jacket, Chen Xuan walked into Fuxing New Village. It was a thirty-year-old yard of six-story red-brick buildings smelling faintly of warm ginger soup and damp concrete.

Outside Building Two, under a flickering corner streetlamp, a lone man paced beside an old dark-blue electric scooter with a folded yellow raincoat in its wire basket.

Chen Jianguo was fifty-four, a city repair driver who had spent thirty years hauling heavy iron without a single complaint. Tonight his shoulders hunched inside his thin corduroy jacket, his head bowed toward the ground.

Chen Xuan's shoes crunched softly on loose gravel.

Chen Jianguo spun around right away. Seeing his eldest son step into the light, stunned relief and frantic worry clashed across his weathered face.

"Chen Xuan!" Chen Jianguo hurried forward three steps and grabbed Chen Xuan's forearm with both hands. His rough palms were cold and shaking. "How did you get back so fast? Your mother said the late train would not reach Sha City until midnight!"

"I caught a direct ride into Sha City and came straight over, Dad," Chen Xuan said gently. "Why are you standing down here in the wind? Your hands are freezing."

"How could I sit upstairs?" Chen Jianguo's voice cracked, raw and hoarse. He glanced up at the lit sixth-floor windows. "Your Second Aunt Wu Lanying has been crying since four o'clock. Your Second Uncle Chen Mingde has never argued loudly with a neighbor in twenty years, and they snapped handcuffs on him in front of the whole street!"

Chen Jianguo's fingers gripped tighter on Chen Xuan's sleeve. His eyes were bloodshot with tiredness and helpless shame.

"I rode to Old Street Station four hours ago," Chen Jianguo whispered fast. "I packed hot jasmine tea and his thick wool sweater. The young officer at the gate would not even pass the sweater through the window. He slammed the glass in my face and told me to move. I stood outside that gate in the rain for an hour, Chen Xuan. Nobody even came out."

A brief, cold shadow crossed Chen Xuan's eyes before fading. "You should not have stood in the rain, Dad."

"What else could I do?" Chen Jianguo tugged urgently at Chen Xuan's arm, looking back at the scooter. "Listen to me. Your Mom does not know you are down here yet. Get on the back of my scooter right now. We will ride back to Old Street Station together. Station Director Cheng Biao is still on duty. I saw his black sedan inside the yard."

"Dad," Chen Xuan said quietly.

"Hear me out!" Chen Jianguo's breathing turned shallow, a lifetime of pride stripped bare by fear for his brother. "If they will not open the gate, I will kneel on the concrete outside Cheng Biao's office until he listens! Chen Mingde has a bad lower back! He cannot sleep on a cold wooden bench in a holding cell all night!"

Chen Jianguo pulled harder on his son's sleeve.

Chen Xuan did not move an inch.

His feet stayed planted firmly on the damp concrete. Shifting his thermos to his left hand, Chen Xuan placed his right palm gently over both of Chen Jianguo's shaking hands. His grip was warm, steady, and unmoving.

Chen Xuan looked straight into his father's red eyes. His voice did not rise, yet every word landed with quiet, solid weight.

"Dad, listen to me," Chen Xuan said softly. "Neither you, nor Mom, nor I will ever step foot inside that police station. Second Uncle will be fine. Let us go upstairs."

Chen Jianguo froze. His fingers loosened on Chen Xuan's sleeve as he stared into his eldest son's face.

In Chen Jianguo's mind, Chen Xuan had always been quiet, polite, a little too thin, keeping his head down at an ordinary desk in Shanghai. Yet looking into those dark eyes tonight, Chen Jianguo felt a sudden jolt in his chest. There was no young panic there, no helplessness, no doubt. Only a deep, rock-solid calm that forced Chen Jianguo's fast breathing to slow down on its own.

"Chen Xuan," Chen Jianguo murmured, his voice dropping low. "Hongrui Real Estate's Chairman Deng has friends in every office, and Station Director Cheng Biao drinks at his table every week. If we just sit upstairs, what if they force Chen Mingde to sign a confession before morning?"

"Begging at an iron gate will not soften Cheng Biao, Dad," Chen Xuan replied evenly, his thumb resting calmly across his father's knuckles. "If you kneel outside Old Street Station tonight, he will not feel sorry for us. He will decide our family is easy to crush and squeeze Second Uncle twice as hard tomorrow."

Chen Jianguo's jaw tightened, and his eyes dropped to the handlebars. "Then what do we do? Just wait for morning?"

"We let the right people handle it," Chen Xuan said calmly. "On my way back, I called reliable people who deal with matters like this the right way. They are already working on it tonight. Right now, Second Uncle is safe, warm, and unharmed. Nobody inside that building can touch him or force him to sign anything."

"Reliable people?" Chen Jianguo searched his son's face line by line. "Chen Xuan, do not tell me fairy tales just to calm me down. Who can an ordinary clerk in Shanghai call at nine on a Friday night?"

Chen Xuan did not flinch or look away. A small, reassuring smile touched the corners of his eyes. "Have I ever lied to you about something that mattered, Dad?"

Chen Jianguo opened his mouth, then closed it, staring at his son for three long seconds. Chen Xuan had never bragged, never stretched the truth, and never made a promise he could not keep. The quiet certainty in his voice settled over Chen Jianguo's chest like a steady hand.

A long, shaky breath left Chen Jianguo's lips. Slowly, the tight knot between his shoulders eased. He blinked hard, turned quickly toward the scooter to hide the tears stinging his eyes, and clicked the steel lock through the back wheel.

Turning back with his usual gruff face, he pulled the canvas bag straight off Chen Xuan's shoulder.

"Give it here," Chen Jianguo muttered, clearing his throat. "Six floors is still a climb, and you worked all day."

"It is not heavy, Dad," Chen Xuan said with a faint smile.

"Less talking, more walking. Your mother has been keeping soup hot since seven."

Side by side, father and son stepped into the dim stairwell of Building Two. Chen Jianguo climbed ahead with a noticeably steadier step, looking back once at the second-floor landing to make sure his son was right behind him.
