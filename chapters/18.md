# Chapter 18: The Basement Ledger of Yunxi Land Reserve

By midafternoon on Saturday, the Yunxi County Land Reserve Center stood locked and silent behind its iron gate. In the front guard booth, an elderly watchman dozed beside a murmuring television. Around the back, a steel ventilation louver in the foundation wall swung quietly inward. Two figures in dark utility jackets, matte gloves, and featureless charcoal face coverings dropped lightly into the basement corridor.

Neither looked at the other's face. Inside Black Pine Guard, names did not exist, faces were irrelevant, and every mission ran on numbers and exact timing.

"Corridor clear," Number Forty-two murmured into the throat mic, glancing at a palm-sized thermal reader. "Watchman has not moved from the ground booth. Motion sensors down here run on a thirty-second loop from 2014 hardware."

"Loop bridged," Number Sixty-eight replied evenly, clipping a small bypass module onto the junction box beside the fire door. "Forty minutes before the self-test resets."

"We only need twenty-five," Number Forty-two said. "Move."

They reached the steel door marked Archive Vault B. Unlike the public zoning files upstairs, which had been uploaded to the municipal network three years earlier, this vault held the county's original paper land ledgers, old geological surveys, and an air-gapped archive server that never touched an outside line. Whatever County Magistrate Sun Weiming and his predecessors wanted out of provincial view lived behind this lock.

Number Forty-two knelt beside the dual-cylinder mechanical lock and worked two slender tension tools with unhurried precision. Fourteen seconds later, the bolt slid back with a muted click. They stepped inside and eased the door shut.

Number Sixty-eight went straight to the dusty desktop terminal and locked beige server tower against the far wall. Removing the side panel with a magnetized driver, Number Sixty-eight clipped a silent read-only solid-state tap onto the drive bus. A matte-black slate flickered to life, pulling a bit-for-bit mirror of twelve years of land-transfer records, zoning amendments, and sealed tender addenda without writing a single byte to the host access log.

"Tap is live," Number Sixty-eight reported quietly. "Mirror will finish in twelve minutes."

"Pulling the paper shelves," Number Forty-two replied, moving down the third aisle. Gloved fingers traced stenciled spines from 2012 through 2024 before dropping to the bottom rack. Tucked behind late-nineties flood-control ledgers sat three cloth-bound archival folders stamped in faded red ink: Yunxi County Civil Defense Engineering Survey, 1974 to 1978.

Number Forty-two laid the heaviest folio on a rolling metal cart beneath a silent optical scanner. Page by page, yellowed vellum maps and hand-drawn cross-sections passed under the soft light bar, capturing every survey line, depth mark, and drainage contour.

Twelve minutes later, Number Sixty-eight's slate gave a barely audible chime. "Twelve-year digital mirror complete," Number Sixty-eight said, fingers already flying across the glass. "Running an anomaly sort on Hongrui Real Estate and Old Street Parcel Four."

Number Forty-two closed the civil-defense binder, aligned its spine to the exact dust line on the steel shelf, and walked over to the terminal table. "What do the numbers say?"

Number Sixty-eight's shoulders stiffened slightly as spreadsheets and auction records scrolled past. "The commercial math is completely fake."

"Define fake," Number Forty-two prompted, leaning in.

"Hongrui Real Estate paid three hundred and ten million yuan for the riverside redevelopment tract, plus an off-book demolition reserve of one hundred and forty million," Number Sixty-eight said, tapping the ledger figures. "Even if they covered the entire block with high-rise apartments and sold every square meter at peak Yunxi County prices, they would still lose more than two hundred million yuan."

Number Forty-two's gaze sharpened behind dark lenses. "Deng Yongchang is greedy, not suicidal. Nobody burns two hundred million yuan to build apartments in a fourth-tier county. Where is the return?"

"Not in the public filings," Number Sixty-eight muttered. "Hold on. There is a password-protected directory buried inside the 2023 drainage-reinforcement archive. Never submitted to the county housing bureau. It sits only on this air-gapped drive under a contractor code."

"Crack it," Number Forty-two ordered.

Eight seconds later, the encryption broke and a high-resolution CAD blueprint unfolded across the dark slate. Number Sixty-eight let out a slow, sharp breath. "You need to see this."

Number Forty-two studied the dense subterranean lines for two seconds, then spoke quietly. "Overlay the 1976 civil-defense map."

Number Sixty-eight merged the two layers. Instantly, the faded red lines of the 1970s survey snapped into exact alignment with the crisp blue lines of Hongrui Real Estate's hidden foundation plan.

For three full seconds, neither operative moved.

Directly beneath the south corner of Old Street, centered almost straight under the brick courtyard of Chen Mingde's Mingde Hardware and the neighboring riverside block, lay the main intake shaft of a decommissioned 1970s civil-defense tunnel network. Carved into solid riverbank bedrock forty years earlier, the main gallery ran twelve meters underground, wide enough for two heavy trucks abreast, sloping gently northeast to the river. Sealed with brick bulkheads in 1984, it had been forgotten by nearly everyone in Yunxi County.

Number Sixty-eight zoomed in on the blue structural layer stamped with Hongrui Real Estate's private engineering seal. "Look at the reinforcement specs," Number Sixty-eight said, voice noticeably colder. "They are not sealing or filling the old tunnel. They are lining the bedrock gallery with thirty-centimeter blast-grade concrete, installing an independent ventilation shaft disguised as a storm drain, and carving a four-thousand-square-meter subterranean cargo vault beneath the Old Street block."

Number Forty-two's gloved finger traced the eastern extension of the blueprint. "Four thousand square meters underground in a county town. Where does the exit spur terminate?"

"Four hundred meters downriver," Number Sixty-eight answered, scrolling east. "Directly beneath the concrete retaining wall of Pier Four. The blueprint includes a concealed hydraulic rail spur exiting below the autumn waterline, hidden behind the old floodgate sluice."

Number Forty-two went completely still. "Pier Four was declared decommissioned and structurally unsafe six years ago. No public road access, no customs cameras, no municipal river patrol."

"And look straight above the main intake," Number Sixty-eight added, tapping a highlighted red rectangle on the surface layer. "Mingde Hardware sits directly over the only vertical access shaft wide enough to lower heavy excavation machinery without alerting the county road office. That is why Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong are desperate to crush Chen Mingde before month-end. They do not care about a hardware shop. They need the ground beneath it."

"Which means this is no longer a local demolition dispute," Number Forty-two said evenly, checking the wristwatch beneath the cuff. "Nineteen minutes elapsed. Disconnect and clean."

Number Sixty-eight unclipped the tap, re-seated the server panel, and brushed the dust line across the screws until the casing looked untouched for a decade. Number Forty-two swept the aisle with an ultraviolet penlight. No fibers, no smudges, no shifted spines.

"Vault clean," Number Forty-two confirmed.

"Ready to exit," Number Sixty-eight replied.

Five minutes later, the vault door clicked shut, the bypass module vanished from the junction box, and the rear ventilation louver eased back into place. From an unmarked maintenance van three blocks away, Number Forty-two transmitted the blueprint overlay and twelve-year archive mirror straight to the Lanshui command channel with a single priority flag: Pier Four requires immediate visual and thermal surveillance before nightfall.
