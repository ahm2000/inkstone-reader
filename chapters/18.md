# Chapter 18: The Recount

The next morning, five minutes before the recount, Zhang Pengfei stood beside a stack of printouts three inches thick. Dark rings shadowed his eyes. He had stayed up checking numbers until two in the morning.

"Just watch," he whispered to Pan Xiulan. "He leaves at five sharp every day, so his overtime is zero. Once General Manager Sun sees my nineteen late nights, first place comes straight back to me."

The glass doors swung open. Hu Dewei held one side wide, sweating lightly, as Sun Haoran walked in.

Thirty chairs scraped back in unison. Thirty clerks stood up.

"Good morning, General Manager Sun!"

"Please, sit." Sun Haoran waved a hand. Nobody sat.

He ignored both the whiteboard and the tower of printouts. Instead, his gaze settled on the scratched green thermos beside Chen Xuan's keyboard, studying it like a man sizing up a vault.

"General Manager!" Zhang Pengfei hurried forward, cradling his stack of papers in both arms. "Here is the complete recount! Page one lists nineteen late nights for me and zero overtime for Supervisor Chen."

Sun Haoran flipped one page, then another. Without a word, he handed the entire stack off to Hu Dewei.

"Zhang Pengfei, whose initials are written in blue ink on the second container's invoice?"

Zhang Pengfei looked down. All the blood drained from his face. Two blue letters sat plainly on the sheet: Z.P.

"That invoice crossed your desk, and you signed off on it," Sun Haoran said mildly. "You missed a duplicate storage charge. On Friday morning, Supervisor Chen caught it. Yesterday the port refunded fourteen thousand yuan to our account."

"I didn't know about the bonus rule!" Zhang Pengfei whirled on his department head. "Manager Hu, you never told me!"

"I told you to read page twelve!" Hu Dewei snapped. "Three times! You claimed you knew it by heart!"

"You said it wasn't important!"

"That's enough. Company policy is clear: recovering more than ten thousand yuan earns 1.5 bonus points. Manager Hu, update the board."

Hu Dewei wiped away 93.6 and wrote: Chen Xuan, 95.1.

Zhang Pengfei had stayed up half the night to beat Chen Xuan. Instead, he had delivered the signed proof of his own blunder.

Chen Xuan looked up from his steamed bun. "I only read page twelve. Anyone could have spotted it."

The remark was mild and matter-of-fact, yet it landed on Zhang Pengfei like a slap.

Sun Haoran, a man who routinely let people go twice a year without blinking, walked over to the corner desk and inclined his head slightly.

"Supervisor Chen. Manager Hu tells me your tea smells extraordinary. Could you spare half a cup?"

"Morning, General Manager Sun. Of course." Chen Xuan poured half a paper cup. "Careful, it's hot."

Sun Haoran accepted the cup with both hands. He breathed in the steam, then took a sip. For three full seconds he stood motionless.

He had smelled this tea only once before, from a tiny porcelain jar his old mentor kept locked away. Barely twenty jin were harvested each spring. And here it sat in a forty-yuan thermos beside a half-eaten steamed bun.

"Who is this Hao Jingshan?" Sun Haoran asked softly. "A man who sells leaves like this for thirty yuan either doesn't know what he holds, or he is extraordinarily generous."

"He's a good neighbor. His shop barely gets any foot traffic, so I feel bad and buy two bags." Chen Xuan reached into his canvas tote and set a small paper packet on the desk. "I have a spare. Thirty yuan, same as he charged me."

Sun Haoran's pulse jumped. He would have gladly paid a month's salary for that packet, yet Chen Xuan offered it as casually as a spare umbrella.

Sun Haoran pulled a crisp bill from his wallet and presented it with both hands, the way one offers a gift to an elder. Chen Xuan said, "Thanks," and pocketed it as casually as a vendor making change.

"Everyone!" Sun Haoran turned back to the room, beaming. "Supervisor Chen took first place and saved the company fourteen thousand yuan. This Friday, the company is treating the entire Operations Department to dinner at the Yunhai Grand Hotel!"

The floor erupted in cheers.

"Thank you, General Manager Sun! Thank you, Supervisor Chen!"

Chen Xuan raised a hand to wave it off, then quietly rubbed his forehead when he thought nobody was looking.

As he sat back down, he noticed something odd. His office chair, which had squeaked for two straight years, made no sound at all.

Over by the mop bucket, Ge Qinghe hummed quietly to herself and did not look up.

"Ge Qinghe. Did you oil this?"

"A tea egg is worth a little oil. Sit, sit."

"Thanks. Really."

A company executive had just paid him thirty yuan for a bag of leftovers, and the whole office was clapping as if he had cured a disease.

At his own desk, Zhang Pengfei dug his fingernails into his palms.

Keeping his phone hidden beneath the desk, he typed a message.

*Qi Zihao, it's Zhang Pengfei. Could I borrow your Yunhai Silver card for Friday night? Company dinner. I'll take good care of it.*

A reply came back thirty seconds later from Qi Zihao, a wealthy young man who held a Silver membership at the hotel.

*Silver costs 20,000 a year. That's nearly three months of your pay. Lose it or bend it, and my father's people will review the berth permits your wife's uncle signed last year. Tell the front desk it's mine. Bring it back Saturday morning.*

Zhang Pengfei smiled for the first time all morning. It was not a pleasant smile.

* * *

At lunchtime, Chen Xuan was carrying a twelve-yuan canteen tray when his phone rang.

"Chen Xuan!" His Second Uncle Chen Mingde's voice trembled with excitement through the crackling line. "The provincial inspectors visited Old Street yesterday! They froze all eleven shop contracts on the spot! If any owner still wants to sell, the county has to pay the full eight thousand per square meter, not 3,800!"

"Second Uncle, that's wonderful news. Are you sitting down?"

"My shop is sixty square meters, Chen Xuan! That's 252,000 yuan more than their first offer! I dropped right onto a crate of nails and your aunt had to fan me!"

Chen Mingde was practically breathless. "And Wei Kun, the officer who brought me hot water! This morning the county appointed him Acting Deputy Chief of Old Street Station. Second in command for now, and if he does well, they'll drop the Acting title. Instructor Qiu Guoqiang is back in charge of the station, too. Wei Kun stopped by for a box of nails, grinning ear to ear, and I refused to take a single fen from him!"

Chen Xuan set down his chopsticks. The quiet tightness that had sat in his chest since the weekend finally eased, and his eyes warmed.

"The shop is safe," he said softly, a genuine smile spreading across his face. "Second Uncle, that's the best news I've heard all year."

"Heaven has eyes, Chen Xuan! Pure luck that the inspectors arrived two days early! And are you eating properly? Your aunt says you're getting thin."

"I'm eating, I promise. Give Second Aunt a hug for me." He let out a quiet, steady laugh. "Yes. We really are lucky."

When he hung up, his tofu had already gone cold. He ate it anyway, still smiling.

* * *

That night in the quiet courtyard, An Zhiyuan, Chief of the Detail, sat beside a locked drawer that held two ledgers.

The first listed everyone who had ever shown kindness to Chen Xuan. Beside each name sat a note recording what was owed, a debt of gratitude to be repaid many times over.

The second listed what others owed Chen Xuan. Every page was blank. He never kept score of favors.

Tang Liqun, captain of the hidden van team, set down a cup of tea. "Chief, People's Hospital just reported in. Zhong Tiantian, Zhong Hai's young son, has been moved to a fourth-floor bed. His surgery is set for Thursday. A shell charity fund is covering the bill, so nothing can be traced back to the Boss."

"And Zhong Hai?"

"On Sunday he walked up to Wei Kun's desk and confessed everything on his own, before anyone even questioned him. Wei Kun didn't forgive him. He only told him to go sit with his son."

An Zhiyuan wrote a single line in the ledger, then added a second beneath it: *The law will weigh the cooperation of a witness. The hospital bed stays.*

"One more thing," said Pei Ruihan, the team's deputy. "Number Fourteen oiled his chair in exchange for a tea egg. And Hengxin Logistics has booked a Friday dinner for twenty people at the Yunhai Grand Hotel."

An Zhiyuan's pen paused above the page.

Then, for the first time all week, he smiled. It was small and dry, like a man watching an old wager come due.

"The Yunhai, of all the hotels in the city." He closed the ledger. "Pull Friday's guest list. And make sure nobody tells him what that hotel really is."

"Chief?"

"Hengxin is just an ordinary logistics firm. The Boss works there like any other clerk and owns none of it. The Yunhai, however, belongs to a holding company, and three years ago its trustee gave the hotel manager one strict instruction: one day a young man will lay a black card on your counter. Serve him, ask nothing, tell nobody. The Boss thinks it's just a dining card a friend gave him."

Tang Liqun frowned slightly. "And the manager?"

"He's about to have the worst and best Friday of his life." An Zhiyuan slid the drawer shut.
