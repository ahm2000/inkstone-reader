# Chapter 18: The Basement Ledger of Yunxi Land Reserve

By midafternoon on Saturday, the Yunxi County Land Reserve Center stood locked behind its iron gate. In the guard booth, an elderly watchman dozed beside a television. Around back, a steel air vent swung inward. Two men in dark jackets, gloves, and plain gray masks dropped lightly into the basement hallway.

"Hallway is clear," Number 42 murmured into his collar mic, checking a small heat scanner. "The watchman has not moved. The motion alarms down here are old 2014 units that reset every thirty seconds."

"Alarm loop bypassed," Number 68 replied evenly, clipping a tiny wire box onto the wall switch by the fire door. "We have forty minutes before the system checks itself."

"We only need twenty-five," Number 42 said. "Move."

They reached a heavy steel door marked Archive Room B. Unlike the public files uploaded to the city network three years earlier, this room held the county's original paper land books, old surveys, and an offline computer. Whatever County Magistrate Sun Weiming and his predecessors wanted hidden from provincial eyes lived behind this lock.

Number 42 knelt by the double lock and worked two thin picks. Fourteen seconds later, the bolt slid back with a soft click. They stepped inside and eased the door shut.

Number 68 went straight to the dusty desk and gray computer tower against the far wall. Opening the side cover with a magnetic screwdriver, Number 68 clipped a silent read-only cable onto the hard drive. A small tablet lit up, copying twelve years of land-sale records, zoning changes, and sealed contract notes without leaving a single trace.

"Copy is running," Number 68 reported quietly. "Done in twelve minutes."

"Checking the paper shelves," Number 42 replied, walking down the third aisle. Gloved fingers traced labeled binders from 2012 through 2024 before dropping to the bottom shelf. Tucked behind late-nineties flood books sat three cloth-bound folders stamped in faded red ink: Yunxi County Air-Raid Shelter Survey, 1974 to 1978.

Number 42 laid the heaviest folder on a cart under a silent handheld scanner. Page by page, yellowed maps and hand-drawn tunnel sketches passed under the light bar, saving every depth mark and water pipe note.

Twelve minutes later, Number 68's tablet chimed faintly. "Twelve-year copy is done," Number 68 said, fingers flying across the screen. "Checking Hongrui Real Estate and Old Street Lot Four."

Number 42 closed the binder, lined its spine with the exact dust mark on the shelf, and walked over. "What do the numbers say?"

Number 68's shoulders stiffened as account sheets scrolled past. "The business math is completely fake."

"How fake?" Number 42 asked, leaning in.

"Hongrui Real Estate paid 310 million yuan for the Guangming Peak riverside lot, plus a hidden buyout fund of 140 million," Number 68 said, tapping the ledger. "Even if they packed the block with tall apartments and sold every room at top Yunxi County prices, they would still lose more than 200 million yuan."

Number 42's eyes sharpened. "Deng Yongchang is greedy, not crazy. Nobody burns 200 million yuan to build apartments in a small county town. Where is the profit?"

"Not in the public files," Number 68 muttered. "Wait. There is a locked folder buried inside the 2023 storm-drain repair file. It was never sent to the housing office. It sits only on this offline drive under a builder's code."

"Open it," Number 42 ordered.

Eight seconds later, the password broke and a sharp blueprint unfolded across the tablet. Number 68 drew a sharp breath. "You need to see this."

Number 42 studied the thick underground lines for two seconds. "Lay the 1976 air-raid map on top of it."

Number 68 matched the two maps. Instantly, the faded red lines of the 1970s survey locked straight onto the bright blue lines of Hongrui Real Estate's secret plan.

For three full seconds, neither man moved.

Directly under the south corner of Old Street, centered right beneath the brick yard of Chen Mingde's Mingde Hardware and the neighboring riverside block, lay the main entrance shaft of a 1970s air-raid tunnel. Carved into solid riverbank rock forty years earlier, the tunnel ran twelve meters underground, wide enough for two heavy trucks side by side, sloping northeast toward the river. Bricked shut in 1984, it had been forgotten by almost everyone in Yunxi County.

Number 68 zoomed in on the blue layer bearing Hongrui Real Estate's private builder seal. "Look at the wall plans," Number 68 said, voice turning cold. "They are not filling the old tunnel. They are lining it with heavy concrete, adding a hidden air shaft disguised as a storm drain, and carving out a four-thousand-square-meter underground storage room beneath Old Street."

Number 42's gloved finger traced the east end. "Four thousand square meters underground in a small county town. Where does the far end come out?"

"Four hundred meters downriver," Number 68 answered, sliding the map east. "Directly beneath the concrete retaining wall of Pier Four. The plan includes a hidden rail track exiting under the autumn waterline, tucked behind the old floodgate."

Number 42 went still. "Pier Four was closed and marked unsafe six years ago. No public road, no customs cameras, and no river patrol."

"And look straight above the main entrance," Number 68 added, tapping a red box on the street map. "Mingde Hardware sits right over the only drop shaft wide enough to lower heavy digging machines without the road office seeing a thing. That is why Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong are desperate to crush Chen Mingde before month's end. They do not care about a hardware shop. They need the ground under it."

"Which means this is no longer a local buyout fight," Number 42 said evenly, checking his watch. "Nineteen minutes gone. Pack up and clean."

Number 68 unclipped the cable, screwed the cover back on, and brushed dust across the screws until the computer looked untouched for ten years. Number 42 swept the aisle with a checking light. No loose threads, no prints, no moved binders.

"Room is clean," Number 42 confirmed.

"Ready to leave," Number 68 replied.

Five minutes later, the steel door clicked shut, the wire box vanished, and the air vent eased back into place. From a repair van three blocks away, Number 42 sent the matched maps and twelve-year copy to the Jingzhou City base with one urgent note: Pier Four needs close eye and heat-camera watch before dark.
