# Chapter 18: The Basement Ledger of Yunxi Land Reserve

By midafternoon on Saturday, the Yunxi County Land Reserve Center stood locked and quiet behind its iron gate. In the front guard booth, an elderly watchman dozed beside a low television. Around the back, a steel air vent in the lower wall swung quietly inward. Two men in dark jackets, dark gloves, and plain gray face masks dropped lightly into the basement hallway.

"Hallway is clear," Number 42 murmured into his collar mic, glancing at a small heat scanner. "The watchman has not moved from the front booth. The motion alarms down here are old 2014 units that reset every thirty seconds."

"Alarm loop bypassed," Number 68 replied evenly, clipping a tiny wire box onto the wall switch beside the fire door. "We have forty minutes before the system checks itself."

"We only need twenty-five," Number 42 said. "Move."

They reached a heavy steel door marked Archive Room B. Unlike the public land files upstairs, which had been uploaded to the city network three years earlier, this room held the county's original paper land books, old earth surveys, and an offline computer that never touched the internet. Whatever County Magistrate Sun Weiming and the leaders before him wanted hidden from provincial eyes lived behind this lock.

Number 42 knelt beside the double lock and worked two thin metal picks with calm, steady hands. Fourteen seconds later, the bolt slid back with a soft click. They stepped inside and eased the door shut.

Number 68 went straight to the dusty desk and locked gray computer tower against the far wall. Opening the side cover with a magnetic screwdriver, Number 68 clipped a silent read-only copying cable onto the hard drive wire. A small black tablet lit up, pulling a full copy of twelve years of land-sale records, zoning changes, and sealed contract notes without leaving a single mark on the computer's access record.

"Copy is running," Number 68 reported quietly. "It will finish in twelve minutes."

"Checking the paper shelves," Number 42 replied, walking down the third aisle. Gloved fingers traced labeled binders from 2012 through 2024 before dropping to the bottom shelf. Tucked behind old late-nineties flood books sat three cloth-bound folders stamped in faded red ink: Yunxi County Air-Raid Shelter Survey, 1974 to 1978.

Number 42 laid the heaviest folder on a rolling metal cart under a silent handheld scanner. Page by page, yellowed paper maps and hand-drawn tunnel sketches passed under the soft light bar, saving every line, depth mark, and water pipe note.

Twelve minutes later, Number 68's tablet gave a faint chime. "Twelve-year computer copy is done," Number 68 said, fingers already flying across the screen. "Checking for odd numbers on Hongrui Real Estate and Old Street Lot Four."

Number 42 closed the old binder, lined its spine up with the exact dust mark on the steel shelf, and walked over to the desk. "What do the numbers say?"

Number 68's shoulders stiffened as account sheets and auction records scrolled past. "The business math is completely fake."

"How fake?" Number 42 asked, leaning in.

"Hongrui Real Estate paid 310 million yuan for the riverside land lot, plus a hidden buyout fund of 140 million," Number 68 said, tapping the ledger numbers. "Even if they packed the whole block with tall apartment buildings and sold every single room at top Yunxi County prices, they would still lose more than 200 million yuan."

Number 42's eyes sharpened. "Deng Yongchang is greedy, not crazy. Nobody burns 200 million yuan just to build apartments in a small county town. Where is the profit?"

"Not in the public files," Number 68 muttered. "Wait. There is a locked folder buried inside the 2023 storm-drain repair file. It was never sent to the county housing office. It sits only on this offline hard drive under a builder's code."

"Open it," Number 42 ordered.

Eight seconds later, the password broke and a sharp building blueprint unfolded across the dark tablet. Number 68 let out a slow, sharp breath. "You need to see this."

Number 42 studied the thick underground lines for two seconds, then spoke quietly. "Lay the 1976 air-raid map on top of it."

Number 68 matched the two maps together. Instantly, the faded red lines of the 1970s survey locked straight onto the bright blue lines of Hongrui Real Estate's secret building plan.

For three full seconds, neither man moved.

Directly under the south corner of Old Street, centered almost right beneath the brick yard of Chen Mingde's Mingde Hardware and the neighboring riverside block, lay the main entrance shaft of an old 1970s air-raid tunnel. Carved into solid riverbank rock forty years earlier, the main rock tunnel ran twelve meters underground, wide enough for two heavy trucks side by side, sloping gently northeast toward the river. Sealed off with brick walls in 1984, it had been forgotten by almost everyone in Yunxi County.

Number 68 zoomed in on the blue layer stamped with Hongrui Real Estate's private builder seal. "Look at the wall plans," Number 68 said, his voice turning cold. "They are not filling in the old tunnel. They are lining the rock tunnel with thick heavy concrete, adding a hidden air shaft dressed up as a storm drain, and carving out a four-thousand-square-meter underground storage room right beneath the Old Street block."

Number 42's gloved finger traced the east end of the drawing. "Four thousand square meters underground in a small county town. Where does the far end come out?"

"Four hundred meters downriver," Number 68 answered, sliding the map east. "Directly beneath the concrete retaining wall of Pier Four. The plan includes a hidden rail track that exits under the autumn waterline, tucked right behind the old floodgate."

Number 42 went completely still. "Pier Four was closed and marked unsafe six years ago. No public road, no customs cameras, and no river patrol."

"And look straight above the main entrance," Number 68 added, tapping a glowing red box on the street map. "Mingde Hardware sits directly over the only straight drop shaft wide enough to lower heavy digging machines without the county road office seeing a thing. That is why Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong are desperate to crush Chen Mingde before the end of the month. They do not care about a hardware shop. They need the ground right under it."

"Which means this is no longer a local shop buyout fight," Number 42 said evenly, checking the watch under his cuff. "Nineteen minutes gone. Pack up and clean."

Number 68 unclipped the copying cable, screwed the side cover back on, and brushed dust back across the screws until the computer looked untouched for ten years. Number 42 swept the aisle with a small checking light. No loose threads, no fingerprints, no moved binders.

"Room is clean," Number 42 confirmed.

"Ready to leave," Number 68 replied.

Five minutes later, the steel door clicked shut, the wire box vanished from the wall switch, and the back air vent eased back into place. From a plain repair van three blocks away, Number 42 sent the matched maps and the twelve-year computer copy straight to the Lanshui base with one urgent note: Pier Four needs close eye and heat-camera watch before dark.
