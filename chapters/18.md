# Chapter 18: The Recount

The next morning, five minutes before the recount, Zhang Pengfei stood beside a stack of printouts three inches thick. He had dark rings under his eyes. He had checked numbers until two.

"Watch," he whispered to Pan Xiulan. "He leaves at five every day, so his overtime is zero. When General Manager Sun reads my nineteen nights, first place comes back to me."

The glass doors swung open. Hu Dewei held one door, sweating. Sun Haoran, the General Manager, walked through.

Thirty chairs scraped. Thirty people stood up.

"Good morning, General Manager Sun!"

"Sit, please." Sun Haoran waved one hand. Nobody sat.

He ignored the whiteboard and the paper tower. He looked at a scratched green thermos next to Chen Xuan's keyboard, like a man looking at a safe.

"General Manager!" Zhang Pengfei carried his stack forward in both arms. "The full recount! Page one shows nineteen late nights for me and zero overtime for Supervisor Chen."

Sun Haoran turned a page. He turned another. Then he let the stack rest on Hu Dewei's arm.

"Pengfei, whose initials are in blue ink on the second container's invoice?"

Zhang Pengfei looked down. The blood drained from his face. Two blue letters sat on that page: Z.P.

"The invoice passed your desk, and you signed it," said Sun Haoran gently. "You missed a double storage fee. On Friday morning Supervisor Chen found it. Yesterday the port sent fourteen thousand yuan back to us."

"I didn't know about the bonus rule!" Zhang Pengfei turned to his boss. "Manager Hu, you never told me!"

"I told you to read page twelve!" Hu Dewei snapped. "Three times! You said you knew it by heart!"

"You said it was nothing!"

"Enough. Company rule: if you recover more than ten thousand yuan, you get 1.5 bonus points. Manager Hu, the board."

Hu Dewei wiped out 93.6 and wrote: Chen Xuan, 95.1.

Zhang Pengfei had stayed up all night to beat Chen Xuan. Instead he had signed his own name on the proof.

Chen Xuan looked up from his bun. "I only read page twelve. Anyone can."

It was mild and plain. It hit Zhang Pengfei like a slap.

Sun Haoran, who fired people twice a year, walked to the corner desk and bent his back a little.

"Supervisor Chen. Manager Hu says your tea smells wonderful. Half a cup, if you can spare it."

"Morning, General Manager. Of course." Chen Xuan poured half a paper cup. "Careful, it's hot."

Sun Haoran held the cup in both hands. He smelled the steam and drank. For three seconds he did not move.

He had smelled this tea once, from a tiny jar his old teacher kept. Only twenty jin were picked each spring. And here it sat in a forty-yuan thermos beside a half-eaten bun.

"Who is this Uncle Hao?" Sun Haoran said softly. "A man who sells that for thirty yuan either does not know its price or is a very kind man."

"He's a nice man. His shop never has customers, so I feel bad and buy two." Chen Xuan dug in his canvas bag and set a paper packet on the desk. "A spare. Thirty yuan. That's what he charged me."

Sun Haoran's heart thumped. He would have paid a month's salary for that packet. Chen Xuan offered it like a spare umbrella.

Sun Haoran took a crisp bill from his wallet and handed it over with both hands, the way you give a gift to an elder. Chen Xuan said, "Thanks," and took it like a man selling vegetables.

"Everyone!" Sun Haoran turned around, beaming. "Supervisor Chen came first, and he saved us fourteen thousand. This Friday the company takes the whole Operations Department to dinner at the Yunhai Grand Hotel!"

The floor exploded.

"Thank you, General Manager Sun! Thank you, Supervisor Chen!"

Chen Xuan lifted a hand to say it was nothing. Then he put it over his face when he thought nobody saw.

Then he noticed that his chair, which had squeaked for two years, made no sound at all.

By the mop bucket, Ge Qinghe hummed and did not look at him.

"Auntie Ge. Did you oil this?"

"A tea egg is worth a little oil. Sit, sit."

"Thanks. Really."

A man had paid him thirty yuan for a bag of leftovers. The whole room was clapping as if he had cured a disease.

At his desk, Zhang Pengfei pressed his fingernails into his palm.

He texted under his desk.

*Brother Qi, it's Pengfei. Can I borrow your Yunhai Silver card Friday night? Company dinner. I'll take care of it.*

The answer came in half a minute from Qi Zihao, a rich young man with a Silver hotel card.

*Silver costs 20,000 a year. That's about three months of your pay. Lose it or bend it, and my father's people will check the berth permits your wife's uncle signed last year. Tell the front desk it's mine. Bring it back to me Saturday morning.*

Zhang Pengfei smiled for the first time that morning. It was not a nice smile.

* * *

At lunch, Chen Xuan was holding a twelve-yuan tray when his phone rang.

"Little Xuan!" His Second Uncle Chen Mingde's voice shook with joy. The phone crackled. "The provincial people came to Old Street yesterday. They froze all eleven shop contracts on the spot! If any owner still wants to sell, the county must pay the full eight thousand per square meter. Not 3,800!"

"Second Uncle, that's good news. Are you sitting down?"

"My shop is sixty square meters, Little Xuan. The county would pay me 252,000 yuan more than their first offer! I sat down on a box of nails and your aunt had to fan me!"

Chen Mingde could hardly breathe. "And Wei Kun! The one who brought me hot water! This morning the county named him Acting Deputy Chief of Old Street Station. That means second in command, for now, and the word Acting goes away if he does well. Instructor Qiu Guoqiang runs the station again. Wei Kun came by for a box of nails, glowing, and I wouldn't take his money!"

Chen Xuan put down his chopsticks. His throat was tight, and his eyes filled. He turned toward the canteen's back door so nobody would see.

"The shop is saved." He began to smile. A tear slid down, and he wiped it with his sleeve. "Second Uncle, I'm so happy. I'm so happy I could cry. I'm crying into my tofu right now."

"Heaven has eyes, Little Xuan! Pure luck that they came two days early! And are you eating? Your aunt says you're thin."

"I'm eating, I promise. Hug Second Aunt for me." He laughed, and his voice cracked. "Lucky. We're so lucky."

He hung up and found the tofu had gone cold. He ate it anyway, still smiling.

* * *

That night in the quiet courtyard, An Zhiyuan, Chief of the Detail, kept two ledgers in a locked drawer.

One listed everyone who had ever been kind to Chen Xuan. Each name had a note: what he owed them, a debt of thanks he would repay far greater.

The second listed what others owed Chen Xuan. Its page was empty. He never counted favors.

Tang Liqun, captain of the hidden van team, brought in a cup. "Chief. People's Hospital reports. Zhong Tiantian, Zhong Hai's little son, has the fourth-floor bed. The operation is Thursday. A made-up 'charity fund' is paying, so no one can trace it to the Boss."

"And Zhong Hai?"

"On Sunday he walked to Wei Kun's window and told everything himself. Before anyone asked. Wei Kun didn't forgive him. He only said: Go sit with your son."

An Zhiyuan wrote one line. Under it he wrote a second: *The law will weigh the help of a witness. The bed stays.*

"One thing more," said Pei Ruihan, the team's deputy. "Fourteen oiled his chair for a tea egg. And Hengxin Logistics has a Friday dinner booked for twenty. At the Yunhai Grand Hotel."

An Zhiyuan's pen stopped.

Then, for the first time all week, he smiled. It was small and dry, like a man whose old bet had come due.

"The Yunhai. Of all the hotels in the city." He closed the file. "Pull Friday's guest list. And let nobody tell him what that hotel is."

"Chief?"

"Hengxin is only an ordinary company. The Boss works there like any clerk and owns nothing in it. But the Yunhai belongs to a holding company, and three years ago its trustee told the hotel manager: One day a young man will put a black card on your counter. Serve him, ask nothing, tell nobody. The Boss thinks it is a dinner card a friend gave him."

Tang Liqun frowned. "And the manager?"

"He's about to have the worst and best Friday of his life." An Zhiyuan shut the drawer.
