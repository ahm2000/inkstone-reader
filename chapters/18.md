# Chapter 18: The Basement Ledger of Yunxi Land Reserve

By midafternoon on Saturday, low slate clouds settled over the administrative quarter of Yunxi County, turning the courtyard of the Yunxi County Land Reserve Center a dull, rainless gray. The six-story concrete building stood dark behind locked iron gates. Inside the glass booth beside the north driveway, an elderly watchman slumped in a plastic chair while an old opera broadcast murmured from a small television.

At the rear of the building, where a narrow service lane bordered a dry drainage ditch, a steel ventilation louver set flush into the foundation wall swung inward without a sound. Two figures in plain dark utility jackets, matte gloves, and featureless charcoal face coverings slipped through the maintenance crawlway and dropped lightly onto the painted concrete floor of the basement corridor.

Neither operative looked at the other's face. Inside the Black Pine Guard, names did not exist, faces were irrelevant, and every mission ran on clean numbers and exact timing.

"Corridor clear," Number Forty-two murmured into the throat mic, checking a palm-sized thermal reader. "Guard remains stationary on the ground floor. Basement motion sensors run on a thirty-second loop from 2014 hardware."

"Loop bridged," Number Sixty-eight replied calmly, clipping a thumbnail-sized bypass module onto the junction box beside the fire door. "Forty minutes before self-test resets."

"We need twenty-five," Number Forty-two said.

They moved down the cold corridor toward the heavy steel door marked Archive Vault B. Unlike the public zoning records upstairs, which had been uploaded to the municipal network three years earlier, the basement vault housed the county's original paper land-transfer ledgers, historical geological surveys, and an air-gapped archive server that had never touched an outside cable. Whatever County Magistrate Sun Weiming and his predecessors preferred to keep out of provincial databases sat behind this door.

Number Forty-two knelt beside the dual-cylinder mechanical lock, working two slender tension tools with unhurried precision. Fourteen seconds later, the bolt retracted with a muted click. They stepped inside, eased the door shut, and stood in the cool hum of an industrial dehumidifier.

Rows of gray steel cabinets stretched into the gloom, smelling of old glue, ink, and damp concrete. Along the far wall sat a dusty desktop terminal connected to a locked beige server tower.

Number Sixty-eight moved straight to the terminal, removed the side panel with a magnetized driver, and attached a silent read-only solid-state tap to the drive bus. A compact matte-black slate flickered to life, pulling a bit-for-bit mirror of twelve years of land-transfer records, zoning amendments, and sealed tender addenda without writing a single byte to the host access log.

While the digital mirror ran, Number Forty-two walked down the third aisle, gloved fingers gliding across stenciled cardboard spines. Years 2012 through 2024 filled the upper shelves. On the bottom rack, tucked behind late-nineties flood-control ledgers, sat three cloth-bound archival folders stamped in faded red ink: Yunxi County Civil Defense Engineering Survey, 1974 to 1978.

Number Forty-two laid the heaviest folio on a rolling metal cart beneath a silent handheld optical scanner. Page by page, yellowed vellum maps and hand-drawn cross-sections slid under the soft white light bar, capturing every survey line, depth mark, and drainage contour in crisp detail.

Twelve minutes later, Number Sixty-eight's slate chimed once at a barely audible frequency. "Twelve-year digital mirror complete," Number Sixty-eight reported. "Running anomaly sort on Hongrui Real Estate and Old Street Parcel Four."

Number Forty-two closed the civil-defense binder, aligned its spine to the exact dust ring on the steel shelf, and walked over to the terminal table. "What does the ledger show?"

"The commercial math is fake," Number Sixty-eight said, scrolling swiftly through spreadsheets and land-auction records. "Hongrui Real Estate paid three hundred and ten million yuan for the riverside redevelopment tract, plus an off-book demolition reserve of one hundred and forty million. Even if they built high-rise apartments across the whole block and sold every square meter at peak Yunxi County prices, they would lose over two hundred million yuan."

Number Forty-two's gaze sharpened behind dark lenses. "Deng Yongchang is greedy, not suicidal. Nobody burns two hundred million yuan to build apartments in a fourth-tier county. Pull the supplementary engineering filings."

Number Sixty-eight decrypted a password-protected folder buried inside the 2023 drainage-reinforcement archive. It had never been filed with the county housing bureau; it sat only on this air-gapped drive under a contractor's code. A high-resolution CAD blueprint unfolded across the dark glass.

"Overlay the 1976 civil-defense map," Number Forty-two instructed quietly.

Number Sixty-eight merged the layers. Immediately, the faded red lines of the 1970s survey snapped into exact alignment with the crisp blue lines of Hongrui Real Estate's hidden foundation plan.

For three full seconds, only the low hum of the dehumidifier filled the vault.

Directly beneath the south corner of Old Street, centered almost straight under the brick courtyard of Chen Mingde's Mingde Hardware and the neighboring riverside block, lay the main intake shaft of a decommissioned 1970s civil-defense tunnel network. Carved forty years earlier into solid riverbank bedrock, the main gallery ran twelve meters underground, wide enough for two heavy trucks abreast, sloping gently northeast straight to the riverfront. Sealed with brick bulkheads in 1984, it had been forgotten by almost everyone in Yunxi County.

Number Sixty-eight zoomed in on the blue structural layer stamped with Hongrui Real Estate's private engineering seal. "Look at the reinforcement specifications," Number Sixty-eight said, voice colder now. "They are not filling the old tunnel in. They are lining the bedrock gallery with thirty-centimeter blast-grade concrete, installing an independent ventilation shaft disguised as a storm drain, and carving a four-thousand-square-meter subterranean cargo vault beneath the Old Street block."

Number Forty-two traced the eastern extension of the blueprint with one gloved finger. "Where does the exit spur terminate?"

"Four hundred meters downriver," Number Sixty-eight answered. "Directly beneath the concrete retaining wall of Pier Four. The blueprint includes a concealed hydraulic rail spur exiting below the autumn waterline, shielded by the old floodgate sluice."

"Pier Four was marked decommissioned and structurally unsafe six years ago," Number Forty-two said evenly. "No public road access, no customs cameras, no municipal patrol."

"And Mingde Hardware sits directly over the only vertical access shaft wide enough to lower heavy excavation machinery without alerting the county road office," Number Sixty-eight added. "That is why Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong are desperate to crush Chen Mingde before month-end. They do not care about a hardware shop. They need the ground beneath it."

Number Forty-two checked the wristwatch beneath the cuff. Nineteen minutes elapsed.

"Disconnect and clean," Number Forty-two ordered.

Number Sixty-eight unclipped the tap, seated the server panel, and brushed the dust line across the screws until the casing looked untouched for a decade. Number Forty-two swept the shelves with an ultraviolet penlight. No fibers, no smudges, no shifted spines.

Five minutes later, the vault door clicked shut, the bypass vanished from the junction box, and the rear ventilation louver eased back into place. From an unlit maintenance van three blocks away, Number Forty-two transmitted the blueprint overlay and twelve-year archive mirror straight to the Lanshui command channel with a single priority flag: Pier Four requires immediate visual and thermal surveillance before nightfall.
