# Chapter 18: The Convoy

Yunxi County Government building, front gate. Monday morning.

By Monday morning, Fan Weiguo had planned every part of the welcome except how to stop his hands from shaking.

A red banner hung over the gate: *Warmly Welcome the Provincial Inspection Team to Yunxi.* Under it sat a fruit basket as big as a washtub, with a bow on top.

One step behind Fan's left shoulder stood County Magistrate Tao Shu, smiling kindly, like a man who had never wanted Fan's chair in his life.

A provincial inspection team could ask for any file and any person, and what it wrote decided careers. One bad page could end thirty years of work!

Everything had to be perfect today. Fan had checked the banner twice, the fruit twice and his tie three times.

The team was due at nine. Fan had a welcome speech ready, a tea room ready and a lunch table booked for twelve.

At 8:42, three plain black cars turned in at the gate. No flags. No sirens. Nothing to cheer for, and nothing to wave at!

"They are early," an aide whispered. "Eighteen minutes early, Secretary!"

"How thoughtful of the province to surprise us," Tao murmured at Fan's ear, still smiling.

"Say nothing," Fan hissed back.

Early?

Fan's heart dropped, because a guest who comes early sees the house as it really is, before anybody has swept it!

The middle car stopped, but its back door stayed shut.

Through the glass, Fan saw Vice Director Gao Zhengyi, the man who led the provincial team sent to judge the county. He sat alone, pulled his coat straight and tugged his tie flat. Then he pressed his shoulders back and sat very tall, the way a schoolboy sits before the headmaster walks in.

Nobody else was in that car.

So who was Gao getting ready to face?

Was it the old gentleman at the reservoir, the secret letter that an anonymous writer had sent to the Governor's office about Old Street, or the man smiling one step behind Fan's shoulder?

Fan had no answer.

Click.

The back door opened at last, and Fan hurried forward with both hands out.

"Vice Director Gao, welcome to Yunxi!" he said. "We have prepared a small welcome, a tea room and a speech!"

"Secretary Fan. Magistrate Tao." Gao shook both hands, quick and polite, then looked up at the banner. "Please take that down. The team works by the book, with no fuss."

Fan's stomach turned over. Those were almost the same words as Sunday's call from the Provincial Party Committee office!

Was that a coincidence?

"And please give that to the families of your gate guards," Gao said, glancing at the fruit basket. "The team accepts nothing, not even an apple."

The old gate guard stared at the washtub, too stunned to reach for it.

"For my family?" he stammered.

"For your wife and your grandchildren," Gao said kindly, "and please tell them it comes with the team's thanks."

"And please cancel the lunch," Gao added. "The team eats in the canteen, at the same price as everyone else."

Fan felt the twelve-person lunch table he had booked quietly die inside him.

Not one thing in the plan was working!

"No banner, no lunch, and not even an apple?" someone whispered behind him.

"These people must be monks," another voice muttered.

Tao stepped forward at once, warm and helpful. "Vice Director Gao, we do not want you to lose a single day. I have prepared a county self-inspection team, and I can hand you every Old Street file by tonight."

"That's very kind, Magistrate Tao," Gao said with a pleasant nod. "The team will do its own reading."

The words were gentle. The door they closed was not.

"Of course, Vice Director," Tao said, still smiling. "Whatever the team prefers!"

Tao's smile stayed exactly where it was, but a muscle in his cheek jumped, and his fingers went stiff at his sides!

Nearby, one of the county drivers had already sidled up to the team's driver with a pack of cigarettes, asking very sincerely whether the team preferred the north road or the south road back to the hotel. He was a man who had not spoken two words to Fan's staff in three years.

Fan felt a small, cheap flash of pleasure, and then it froze inside him.

If Gao did not trust Tao's files, whose files did he trust, Fan's?

"The team will work from the County Discipline Inspection Commission office," Gao said. "Secretary Kang has the third floor ready. But we go to Old Street Police Station first."

Tally: one banner, one fruit basket, three speeches written, zero speeches given.

---

Over at Old Street Police Station, County Police Director Mai Zhenhua had stood at the door since seven, and he had polished his shoes twice.

Beside him stood Instructor Qiu Guoqiang, the station's second in command, who ran the place day to day. Two weeks ago, Cheng Biao had sent him off on a "training course," so nobody could block Cheng's plans.

Qiu had come back Sunday morning and slept two hours on an office sofa. He still could not decide whether he felt furious or relieved!

"Instructor Qiu," Mai muttered, "have you touched the old detention files?"

"Not once, Director. I came back only yesterday."

"Good. Neither have I." Mai wiped his palms on his trousers. "That is our whole defense!"

"And if they ask about the training course, Director?"

"Then you tell the truth, and you tell it plainly."

Mai felt only one thing, and it was fear! The city police chief had given him one order, and since midnight Saturday he had followed it to the letter: *don't touch another document.*

But would not touching look honest, or like hiding?

Behind them, officers whispered in the doorway.

"A provincial vice director, for our little station?"

"This must be the end of Cheng."

The three black cars pulled up, and Gao stepped out and shook their hands.

"Welcome, Vice Director," Mai said stiffly. "The station is at your service!"

"Director Mai. Instructor Qiu." His voice was even and tired and kind. "I will not tour the building, because I need two things. First, all eleven earlier detention notices, the originals, not copies."

"Second, the duty officer who wrote the true time on Chen Mingde's paper. Where is Wei Kun?"

Silence.

Wei Kun?

Mai and Qiu looked at each other. How did a provincial official already know that name?

Inside the lobby, behind the service glass, a round-faced officer with a half-eaten sesame bun heard his own name through the open door.

The bun stopped halfway to his mouth.
