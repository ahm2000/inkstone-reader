# Chapter 18: The Convoy

It was Monday morning at the front gate of the Yunxi County Government building, and Fan Weiguo had planned every part of it except how to stop his hands from shaking.

A red banner hung over the gate: *Warmly Welcome the Provincial Inspection Team to Yunxi.* Under it sat a fruit basket as big as a washtub, with a bow on top. One step behind Fan's left shoulder stood County Magistrate Tao Shu, smiling kindly, like a man who had never wanted Fan's chair in his life.

A provincial inspection team could ask for any file and any person, and what it wrote decided careers. One bad page could end thirty years of work.

The team was due at nine. Fan had a welcome speech ready, a tea room ready, and a lunch table booked for twelve.

At 8:42, three plain black cars turned in at the gate. No flags. No sirens. Nothing to cheer for, and nothing to wave at!

They were eighteen minutes early! Fan's heart dropped. A guest who comes early sees the house as it really is, before anybody has swept it.

The middle car stopped, but its back door stayed shut.

Through the glass, Fan saw Vice Director Gao Zhengyi, the man who led the provincial team sent to judge the county, sitting alone. Gao pulled his coat straight. He tugged his tie flat. He pressed his shoulders back and sat up very tall, the way a schoolboy sits before the headmaster comes in.

Nobody was in that car but Gao. So who was he getting ready to face?

The old gentleman at the reservoir? The secret letter to the Governor's office? Or the man smiling one step behind Fan's shoulder? Fan couldn't answer, and the door finally opened.

"Secretary Fan. Magistrate Tao." Gao shook both hands, quick and polite, then looked up at the banner. "Please take that down. The team works by the book, with no fuss."

Fan's stomach turned over. Almost the same words as Sunday's phone call from the Provincial Party Committee office! Was that a coincidence?

Gao then glanced at the fruit basket. "And please give that to the families of your gate guards. The team accepts nothing, not even an apple."

The old gate guard stared at the washtub, too stunned to reach for it!

"And please cancel the lunch," Gao added. "The team eats in the canteen, at the same price as everyone else."

Fan felt the twelve-person lunch table he had booked quietly die inside him.

Tao Shu stepped forward at once, warm and helpful. "Vice Director Gao, we don't want you to lose a single day. I've prepared a county self-inspection team. I can hand you every Old Street file by tonight."

"That's very kind, Magistrate Tao," Gao said, with a pleasant nod. "The team will do its own reading."

The words were gentle. The door they closed was not.

Tao's smile stayed exactly where it was, but his fingers stiffened at his sides. Behind them, one of the county drivers had already sidled up to the inspection team's own driver, offering cigarettes and asking, with great sincerity, whether the team preferred the north route or the south route back to the hotel, the same driver who hadn't said two words to Fan Weiguo's own staff in three years. Fan felt a small, cheap flash of pleasure, and then it froze inside him. If Gao didn't trust Tao Shu's files, whose files did he trust? Fan's?

"The team will work from the County Discipline Commission office," Gao said. "Secretary Kang has the third floor ready. But we go to Old Street Police Station first."

Tally: one banner, one fruit basket, three speeches written, zero speeches given.

By 9:25, at Old Street Police Station, County Police Director Mai Zhenhua had been standing at the door since seven, his shoes polished twice.

Beside him stood Instructor Qiu Guoqiang, the station's second in command, who ran the place day to day. Two weeks ago, Cheng Biao had sent him away on a "training course" so nobody could block Cheng's plans. Qiu had come back Sunday morning and slept two hours on an office sofa. He still couldn't decide whether he felt furious or relieved!

Mai felt only one thing, and it was fear! Since midnight Saturday, he had followed the city police chief's order to the letter: *don't touch another document.* But would "not touching" look honest, or look like hiding?

The three black cars pulled up. Gao stepped out and shook their hands.

"Director Mai. Instructor Qiu." His voice was even and tired and kind. "I won't tour the building. I need two things. First, all eleven earlier detention notices, the originals, not copies. Second, the duty officer who wrote the true time on Chen Mingde's paper. Where is Wei Kun?"

Mai and Qiu looked at each other. How did a provincial official already know that name?

Inside the lobby, behind the service glass, a round-faced officer with a half-eaten sesame bun heard his own name through the open door. The bun stopped halfway to his mouth.
