# Chapter 73: The Doors of Holding Cell Two

Heavy boots and rattling chains echoed down the narrow cell hallway of Old Street Station.

Two provincial special police officers marched Station Director Cheng Biao and Deputy Director Zhou Quan toward the back exit. Zhou Quan kept his head hung low, shoulders shaking. Cheng Biao dragged his boots across the concrete, his face gray with empty shock.

As they passed Holding Cell Two, Cheng Biao's head snapped sideways.

Behind the iron bars, Chen Mingde sat on the wooden bench with a faded wool blanket wrapped around his shoulders. Twelve hours earlier, Cheng Biao had blown cigarette smoke through those bars, laughing that an old hardware shopkeeper could rot inside until he signed away his shop.

Now Ji Changming stood before Holding Cell Two with the brass cell key in hand.

Cheng Biao slowed half a step, eyes wide with fear and confusion as he stared past Ji Changming at the quiet, silver-haired man. "Wait," Cheng Biao choked out. "Who is he? Who does Chen Mingde know in the provincial capital? He only sells nails and wrenches on Old Street!"

"Keep moving," the officer behind him ordered flatly, gripping Cheng Biao's elbow and pushing him through the back steel door.

Cheng Biao twisted for one last terrified look before the door shut behind him.

Quiet returned to the hallway. Ji Changming slid the brass key into the heavy lock, turned it with a crisp click, and swung the iron door wide.

Chen Mingde blinked in the brighter light, weathered hands tightening on the wool blanket. He looked at the open door, the crisp provincial uniform before him, and the empty hallway where Cheng Biao had just been dragged away in handcuffs.

"Was that Station Director Cheng Biao?" Chen Mingde asked, voice hoarse from a sleepless night. He stood slowly, joints creaking, and stared at Ji Changming in confusion. "Officer, what is going on? Are they moving me to the county jail?"

Ji Changming removed his police cap, tucked it neatly under his left arm, and bowed slightly with warm respect.

"Comrade Chen Mingde, on behalf of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau, we are deeply sorry for this wrongful arrest," Ji Changming said clearly. "The Provincial Procuratorate has withdrawn the charge in writing. You are free to go home."

For three long seconds, Chen Mingde did not move, blinking as if afraid to wake from a dream.

"Free?" Chen Mingde repeated quietly, fingers trembling on the blanket. "Dropped? On Friday Cheng Biao showed me a hospital scan and swore I broke a worker's ribs. He said I was going to prison for three years unless I signed Mingde Hardware over to Chairman Deng Yongchang."

"That scan was faked using a three-month-old traffic crash record," Ji Changming explained gently as a provincial clerk stepped forward with a red-stamped folder and a clear tray. "Your arrest warrant was illegally typed twelve minutes before any patrol car reached your shop. Cheng Biao, Zhou Quan, Police Chief Liang Guodong, and County Magistrate Sun Weiming are all under arrest this morning."

Color rushed back into Chen Mingde's pale cheeks. He let out a long, shaky breath. "All of them? Even Chief Liang Guodong and County Magistrate Sun Weiming?"

"Every single one, Comrade Chen Mingde," Ji Changming confirmed steadily. "Yan Tiehu's demolition crew has been rounded up by provincial special police. Nobody will ever threaten your shop or Old Street again."

Chen Mingde's knees felt weak. He steadied himself on the iron doorframe, eyes shining with urgent worry and relief. "My wife Wu Lanying! My brother Chen Jianguo, my sister-in-law Liu Meifang, my nephew Chen Xuan! Did they come here looking for me? Are they outside?"

"Please do not worry," Ji Changming said warmly. "Not one member of your family had to come near this station. Our officer checked Old Street at eight o'clock. Wu Lanying and your relatives are safe and calm at home. We wanted to clear your name completely first and drive you straight to your front door."

"Thank goodness," Chen Mingde murmured as the tight knot finally left his shoulders. "Wu Lanying has high blood pressure. Seeing me behind iron bars would have scared her half to death."

Ji Changming held out the red-stamped folder with both hands. "This is your official Release Order and Cleared Name Paper, stamped by the Provincial Prosecutor's Office and the Provincial Police Department. Your record is completely clean. Here are your wallet, shop keys, watch, and reading glasses. Please check that nothing is missing."

Chen Mingde folded the blanket onto the bench, wiped his palms on his jacket, and took the folder with trembling fingers. He stared down at the bright red stamp, then at his brass shop keys.

"Everything is here," Chen Mingde whispered, blinking against sudden tears. He looked up at Ji Changming in quiet wonder. "Leader Ji Changming, forgive an old man for asking. How did the province find out so fast? All weekend I thought nobody outside Yunxi County would ever hear a word from Old Street."

Ji Changming met his eyes with calm dignity. "The law has eyes, Comrade Chen Mingde. A provincial special check uncovered Hongrui Real Estate's fake land prices and Yunxi County's fake arrest records last night. When officials abuse their badge to bully honest people, the province steps in right away."

Chen Mingde nodded slowly, pressing the paper to his chest. "Good. That is good. I told Cheng Biao on Friday that heaven still has eyes, and he laughed right in my face."

"He is not laughing anymore," Ji Changming said softly. "Come, Comrade Chen Mingde. A provincial car is waiting outside to drive you home to Mingde Hardware."

As Chen Mingde walked through the front lobby beside Ji Changming, the room fell dead silent. The four young patrol officers and the duty clerk snapped to attention behind the counter, faces burning with shame, heads bowed low.

"Comrade Chen Mingde, we are truly sorry," the duty clerk blurted, his voice shaking.

Chen Mingde paused and looked at the ashamed young officers with a quiet sigh. "Just wear your uniforms honestly from now on," he told them gently. "That is enough."

Outside in the bright morning sun, the doors of a dark prisoner van clanged shut over Cheng Biao's slumped shadow. Across the yard, Ji Changming personally opened the back door of a clean provincial car, shielding the doorframe with his hand so Chen Mingde would not bump his head.

For the first time in three days, Chen Mingde leaned back against the soft seat, closed his fist around his brass shop keys, and smiled.
