# Chapter 73: The Doors of Holding Cell Two

The narrow hallway behind the front lobby of Old Street Station echoed with heavy boots and the dry rattle of steel chains.

Two provincial special police officers marched Station Director Cheng Biao and Deputy Director Zhou Quan past the iron-barred cells toward the back exit. Zhou Quan kept his head hung low against his chest, his shoulders shaking. Cheng Biao walked slower, dragging his boots on the concrete floor. His face was gray with empty shock.

As they passed Holding Cell Two, Cheng Biao's head snapped to the side.

Behind the iron bars, Chen Mingde sat on the edge of the narrow wooden bench. He held a faded wool blanket around his shoulders against the morning chill. Twelve hours earlier, Cheng Biao had blown cigarette smoke through those very bars, laughing that an old hardware shopkeeper could rot inside until he signed away his shop.

Now Han Zhiyuan stood right in front of Holding Cell Two with the brass cell key in his hand.

Cheng Biao slowed half a step. His eyes went wide with fear and confusion as he stared past Han Zhiyuan at the quiet, silver-haired man inside. "Wait," Cheng Biao choked out, his voice rough. "Who is he? Who does Chen Mingde know in the provincial capital? He only sells nails and wrenches on Old Street!"

"Keep moving," the officer behind Cheng Biao ordered flatly. He gripped Cheng Biao's elbow and pushed him through the back steel door.

Cheng Biao twisted his neck for one last terrified look before the hallway door shut behind him.

Quiet returned to the narrow hall.

Han Zhiyuan slid the brass key into the heavy iron lock and turned it with a sharp metal click. He swung the iron door wide open until it hit the wall, then stepped neatly to the doorway.

Inside, Chen Mingde blinked in the brighter hallway light. His rough, weathered hands tightened around the wool blanket. He looked at the open iron door, then at the crisp provincial uniform in front of him, and then down the hall where Cheng Biao had just been dragged away in handcuffs.

"Was that Station Director Cheng Biao?" Chen Mingde asked, his voice hoarse from a sleepless night on the hard bench. He stood up slowly, his joints creaking, and stared at Han Zhiyuan in confusion. "Officer, what is going on? Are they moving me to the county jail?"

Han Zhiyuan took off his police cap, tucked it neatly under his left arm, and bowed slightly with warm respect.

"Comrade Chen Mingde, on behalf of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group, we are deeply sorry for this wrongful arrest," Han Zhiyuan said clearly. "The fake assault charge has been dropped completely. You are free to go home."

For three long seconds, Chen Mingde did not move. His lips parted, and he blinked hard as if afraid he might wake up from a dream.

"Free?" Chen Mingde repeated quietly, his fingers trembling on the folded blanket. "Dropped? Last night Cheng Biao showed me a hospital scan and swore I broke a worker's ribs. He said I was going to prison for three years unless I signed Mingde Hardware over to Chairman Deng Yongchang."

"That hospital scan was faked using a three-year-old traffic crash record," Han Zhiyuan explained gently. A provincial clerk stepped forward carrying a red-stamped folder and a clear tray. "Your arrest warrant was illegally typed up twelve minutes before any patrol car even reached your shop. Cheng Biao, Zhou Quan, Police Chief Liang Guodong, and County Magistrate Sun Weiming are all under arrest this morning."

Color rushed back into Chen Mingde's pale cheeks. His chest rose and fell in a long, shaky breath. "All of them? Even Chief Liang Guodong and County Magistrate Sun Weiming?"

"Every single one of them, Comrade Chen Mingde," Han Zhiyuan confirmed steadily. "Yan Tiehu's demolition crew has been rounded up by provincial special police. Nobody will ever threaten your shop or Old Street again."

Chen Mingde's knees suddenly felt weak. He steadied himself with one rough hand on the iron doorframe, his eyes shining with urgent worry and relief. "My wife Wu Lanying! My brother Chen Jianguo, my sister-in-law Liu Meifang, my nephew Chen Xuan! Did they come down here looking for me? Are they waiting outside?"

"Please do not worry at all," Han Zhiyuan said warmly. "Not one member of your family had to come near this station. Our officer checked Old Street at eight o'clock. Your wife Wu Lanying and your relatives are safe and calm at home. We wanted to clear your name completely first and drive you straight to your own front door."

"Thank goodness," Chen Mingde murmured, letting out a heavy breath as the tight knot finally left his shoulders. "Wu Lanying has high blood pressure. Seeing me locked behind iron bars would have scared her half to death."

Han Zhiyuan held out the red-stamped folder with both hands. "This is your official Release Order and Cleared Name Paper, stamped by both the Provincial Prosecutor's Office and the Provincial Police Department. Your record is completely clean. Here are your wallet, shop keys, watch, and reading glasses. Please check that nothing is missing."

Chen Mingde folded the wool blanket neatly back onto the wooden bench. He wiped his palms on his jacket and took the folder with trembling, careful fingers. He stared down at the bright red stamp, then at his familiar brass shop keys.

"Everything is here," Chen Mingde whispered, blinking hard against sudden tears. He looked up at Han Zhiyuan, quiet wonder mixing with his gratitude. "Leader Han Zhiyuan, please forgive an old man for asking. How did the province find out so fast? Last night I thought nobody outside Yunxi County would ever hear a word from Old Street."

Han Zhiyuan met his eyes with calm dignity. "The law has eyes, Comrade Chen Mingde. A provincial special check uncovered Hongrui Real Estate's fake land prices and Yunxi County's fake arrest records last night. When officials abuse their badge to bully honest people, the province steps in right away."

Chen Mingde nodded slowly, pressing the cleared name paper tightly to his chest. "Good. That is good. I told Cheng Biao last night that heaven still has eyes, and he laughed right in my face."

"He is not laughing anymore," Han Zhiyuan said softly. "Come, Comrade Chen Mingde. A provincial car is waiting outside to drive you home to Mingde Hardware."

As Chen Mingde walked out through the front lobby beside Han Zhiyuan, the room fell dead silent. The four young patrol officers and the duty clerk snapped straight to attention behind the counter. Their faces burned with shame, and they bowed their heads low as he passed.

"Comrade Chen Mingde, we are truly sorry," the young duty clerk blurted out, his voice shaking.

Chen Mingde paused for a second. He looked at the ashamed young officers and let out a quiet sigh. "Just wear your uniforms honestly from now on," he told them gently. "That is enough."

Outside in the bright morning sun, the back doors of a dark prisoner van clanged shut over Cheng Biao's slumped shadow. Across the yard, Han Zhiyuan personally opened the back door of a clean provincial car, holding his hand over the doorframe so Chen Mingde would not bump his head.

For the first time in twenty-four hours, Chen Mingde leaned back against the soft seat, closed his fist tight around his brass shop keys, and smiled.
