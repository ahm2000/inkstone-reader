# Chapter 73: The Doors of Holding Cell Two

The narrow corridor behind the duty lobby of Old Street Station echoed with heavy boots and the dry rattle of steel chains.

Two provincial counter-terrorism officers marched Station Director Cheng Biao and Deputy Director Zhou Quan past the iron-barred doors toward the rear transport exit. Zhou Quan kept his head buried against his chest, shoulders shaking. Cheng Biao walked slower, boots dragging on the concrete, his face gray with hollow disbelief.

As they passed Holding Cell Two, Cheng Biao's head snapped to the side.

Through the vertical bars sat Chen Mingde on the edge of the narrow wooden bench, clutching a faded wool blanket over his shoulders against the morning chill. Twelve hours earlier, Cheng Biao had blown cigarette smoke through these bars, laughing that an old hardware shopkeeper could rot until he signed away his deed.

Now Han Zhiyuan stood directly in front of Holding Cell Two, brass cell key in hand.

Cheng Biao slowed half a step, eyes widening with bewildered dread as he stared past Han Zhiyuan at the quiet, silver-haired man inside. "Wait," Cheng Biao choked out, voice raw. "Who is he? Who does Chen Mingde know in the provincial capital? He only sells nails and wrenches on Old Street!"

"Keep moving," the officer behind Cheng Biao ordered flatly, gripping his elbow and steering him through the rear steel exit.

Cheng Biao twisted his neck for one last terrified stare before the corridor door shut behind him.

Quiet returned to the hallway.

Han Zhiyuan slid the brass key into the heavy iron lock and turned it with a crisp metallic clack. He swung the iron door wide until it met the wall stop, then stepped neatly to the threshold.

Inside, Chen Mingde blinked in the brighter hallway light. His weathered hands tightened around his wool blanket. He looked at the open iron door, then at the crisp provincial uniform before him, and then down the hallway where Cheng Biao had just been hauled away in handcuffs.

"Was that Station Director Cheng Biao?" Chen Mingde asked, voice hoarse from a sleepless night on the hard bench. He rose slowly, joints creaking, staring at Han Zhiyuan in bewilderment. "Officer, what is happening? Are they moving me to the county detention center?"

Han Zhiyuan removed his peaked cap, tucked it respectfully beneath his left arm, and bowed slightly with warm, formal respect.

"Comrade Chen Mingde, on behalf of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group, we apologize for the wrongful detention," Han Zhiyuan said clearly. "The fabricated assault charge is formally voided. You are free to go home."

For three long seconds, Chen Mingde did not move. His lips parted, eyes blinking as if afraid he might wake from a dream.

"Free?" Chen Mingde repeated quietly, fingers trembling on the folded blanket. "Voided? Last night Cheng Biao showed me a hospital scan and swore I broke a worker's ribs. He said I faced three years in prison unless I signed Mingde Hardware over to Chairman Deng Yongchang."

"That hospital scan was forged from a three-year-old traffic accident record," Han Zhiyuan explained gently as a provincial clerk stepped forward carrying a crimson-sealed folder and a clear tray. "Your detention warrant was illegally backdated twelve minutes before any patrol car even reached your shop. Cheng Biao, Zhou Quan, Public Security Chief Liang Guodong, and County Magistrate Sun Weiming are all in criminal detention this morning."

Color rushed back into Chen Mingde's pale cheeks. His chest rose and fell in a long, shaky breath. "All of them? Even Chief Liang Guodong and County Magistrate Sun Weiming?"

"Every single one, Comrade Chen Mingde," Han Zhiyuan confirmed steadily. "Yan Tiehu's demolition crew has been rounded up by provincial counter-terrorism units. Nobody will ever threaten your shop or Old Street again."

Chen Mingde's knees felt suddenly light. He steadied one calloused hand against the iron doorframe, eyes shining with sudden, urgent relief. "My wife Wu Lanying! My brother Chen Jianguo, my sister-in-law Liu Meifang, my nephew Chen Xuan! Did they come down here looking for me? Are they outside?"

"Please put your mind completely at ease," Han Zhiyuan said warmly. "Not a single member of your family had to come near this station. Our liaison checked Old Street at eight o'clock. Your wife Wu Lanying and your relatives are safe and undisturbed at home. We wanted to clear your name completely first and bring you straight to your own doorstep."

"Thank goodness," Chen Mingde murmured, exhaling heavily as the rigid tension finally left his shoulders. "Wu Lanying has high blood pressure. Seeing me behind iron bars would have terrified her half to death."

Han Zhiyuan presented the crimson-sealed folder with both hands. "This is your official Wrongful-Detention Rescission Order and Full Exoneration Certificate, stamped jointly by the Provincial People's Procuratorate and the Provincial Public Security Department. Your record is spotless. Here are your wallet, shop keys, wristwatch, and reading glasses. Please check that nothing is missing."

Chen Mingde folded the wool blanket neatly back onto the wooden bench, wiped his palms on his jacket, and accepted the document with trembling, reverent fingers. He stared down at the bold crimson seal, then at his familiar brass shop keys.

"Everything is here," Chen Mingde whispered, blinking hard against sudden tears. He looked up at Han Zhiyuan, quiet confusion lingering beneath his gratitude. "Leader Han Zhiyuan, forgive an old man for asking. How did the province find out so fast? Last night I thought nobody outside Yunxi County would ever hear a word from Old Street."

Han Zhiyuan met his gaze with calm dignity. "The law has eyes, Comrade Chen Mingde. A provincial special audit uncovered Hongrui Real Estate's illegal land appraisals and Yunxi County's forged detention logs overnight. When officials abuse the badge to bully honest citizens, the province acts without delay."

Chen Mingde nodded slowly, pressing the exoneration certificate tightly to his chest. "Good. That is good. I told Cheng Biao last night that heaven still has eyes, and he laughed in my face."

"He is not laughing anymore," Han Zhiyuan said softly. "Come, Comrade Chen Mingde. A provincial car is waiting outside to drive you home to Mingde Hardware."

As Chen Mingde walked out through the front lobby beside Han Zhiyuan, the room fell utterly silent. The four junior patrolmen and the duty clerk snapped to rigid attention behind the counter, faces flushed with shame, bowing their heads deeply as he passed.

"Comrade Chen Mingde, we are truly sorry," the young duty clerk blurted out, voice shaking.

Chen Mingde paused for a heartbeat, looked at the embarrassed young officers, and let out a quiet sigh. "Just wear your uniforms honestly from now on," he told them gently. "That is enough."

Outside in the bright morning sun, the rear doors of a dark prisoner van clanged shut over Cheng Biao's slumped silhouette. Across the yard, Han Zhiyuan personally opened the passenger door of a clean provincial sedan, shielding the doorframe as Chen Mingde stepped inside.

For the first time in twenty-four hours, Chen Mingde leaned back against the seat, closed his fist around his brass shop keys, and smiled.
