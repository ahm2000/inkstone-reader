# Chapter 73: The Doors of Holding Cell Two

Heavy boots and the metallic clink of handcuffs echoed down the narrow concrete corridor of Old Street Police Station.

Dampness rose from the floor tiles, and weak morning light filtered through a high, barred vent at the end of the hall. Two provincial special police officers marched Station Director Cheng Bo and Deputy Director Zhou Quan past the row of iron cells toward the rear transport exit.

Zhou Quan kept his head buried against his chest, shoulders trembling with quiet sobs. Cheng Bo dragged his scuffed leather shoes across the concrete, his face gray with hollow disbelief.

As they passed Holding Cell Two, Cheng Bo's head jerked sideways.

Behind the vertical iron bars, Chen Mingde sat upright on the narrow wooden bench. A faded wool blanket rested across his shoulders. A dull purple bruise marked his left cheekbone where one of Hongrui Real Estate's hired thugs had shoved him the evening before, yet his back was straight and his eyes were clear.

Twelve hours ago, Cheng Bo had stood outside those exact bars, sneering that a stubborn hardware shopkeeper could rot in the dark until his fingers went numb and he begged to sign away his shop.

Now, Chief Procurator Ji Changming stood directly in front of Holding Cell Two with the brass cell key in his hand. Beside him stood Hou Liangping, Chief of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau.

Cheng Bo slowed half a step, his eyes widening in sudden, choking terror as he watched two high-ranking provincial leaders stand respectfully outside an simple shopkeeper's cell.

"Keep moving," an escort officer barked, shoving Cheng Bo forward toward the exit door.

Ji Changming slid the brass key into the heavy padlock. With a loud snap, the lock fell open. He pulled the iron gate wide and stepped back respectfully to clear the doorway.

Chen Mingde blinked in the brighter light from the hallway. He folded the faded blanket neatly, set it on the bench, and stood up slowly. After a long, sleepless night, his voice was hoarse, yet steady.

"Are you transferring me to the county detention center?" Chen Mingde asked calmly. "I will tell you the same thing I told them last night. I did not hit anyone. Those men smashed my display counter and tried to force my fingerprint onto a blank land contract. Even if you lock me up for a year, I will not sell my family's shop for pennies."

Hou Liangping stepped forward and bowed slightly at the waist, a gesture of deep, unmistakable sincerity. Beside him, Ji Changming did the same.

"Mr. Chen Mingde, please accept our deepest apologies," Hou Liangping said clearly. "I am Hou Liangping, Chief of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau, and this is Chief Procurator Ji Changming. You are not being transferred anywhere. You are a free man."

Chen Mingde froze, his weathered hands tightening slightly at his sides. "Provincial leaders?"

"Every charge fabricated against you by Old Street Police Station has been officially annulled," Ji Changming added warmly. He held out a clean plastic evidence bag with both hands. Inside were Chen Mingde's old steel wristwatch, his worn leather wallet, and his ring of hardware shop keys. "Your record is completely spotless. County Magistrate Sun Weiming, Public Security Chief Liang Guodong, and Station Director Cheng Bo have all been arrested."

Chen Mingde stared at his shop keys resting in the Chief Procurator's hands. For a moment, he wondered if he was still dreaming on that hard wooden bench. Then his eyes fell on the red official seals pinned to the officers' collars, and the tightness in his chest finally broke.

"My wife," Chen Mingde said quickly, his voice catching. "My wife Wu Lanying, and my brother's family, are they safe? Did those Hongrui thugs go back to Old Street?"

"They are completely safe, Mr. Chen," Hou Liangping assured him immediately. "Provincial officers are patrolling Old Street right now, and Hongrui Real Estate's operations in Yunxi County are being shut down as we speak. Not a single brick of your hardware shop will be touched."

Chen Mingde's eyes turned red. He took his watch and keys with trembling fingers, bowing back to both men. "Thank you. Thank you, leaders. I never thought the province would hear about a small shopkeeper like me so fast."

Hou Liangping and Ji Changming exchanged a brief, silent glance. Neither of them knew the name of the person in that Yunxi household who had triggered the Black Order, and neither of them dared speak a single word about Beijing's directive.

"Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin has zero tolerance for corrupt officials bullying honest citizens," Hou Liangping said simply. "Restoring justice in Yunxi County is our duty. Please, let us escort you outside. We have a car ready to take you straight home to your family."

As Chen Mingde walked down the narrow hallway between the two provincial officials, the heavy iron gate of the station courtyard swung open ahead of them.

Bright morning sunshine poured across his face. He raised one hand to shield his eyes, breathing in the crisp, free air.

Outside the police station gates, a crowd of more than fifty neighbors from Old Street had already gathered along the sidewalk. Word of the provincial raid had spread through the market like wildfire.

The moment they saw Chen Mingde step out beside two senior provincial leaders while Cheng Bo and Zhou Quan were pushed headfirst into a barred transport van, the crowd erupted.

"Old Chen is out!" shouted the butcher from down the lane.

"Look at Cheng Bo! He is the one in handcuffs now!"

"Justice finally came to Yunxi County!"

Cheers and loud applause rolled down the street. Chen Mingde looked at the smiling, tearful faces of his neighbors, then glanced back once at the prison van pulling away from the curb. A quiet, proud smile spread across his bruised face as he stepped into the waiting sedan to go home.
