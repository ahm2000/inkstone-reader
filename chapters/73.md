# Chapter 73: The Corner Table Speaks

Crystal chandeliers blazed over the Grand Ballroom of the Lanshui International Convention Hotel, turning four hundred white-linened tables into a sea of polished glass and silver.

Day Two of the Lanshui Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum had reached its centerpiece event. Across the elevated main stage stretched a wide crimson banner in gold characters: Provincial Smart-Logistics Consolidation Plan Formal Signing Ceremony.

Municipal commerce directors, port authority heads, bank presidents, and provincial television crews filled the front and middle sections. Leather-bound signing folders lay aligned along the stage table, each paired with a gold-nibbed fountain pen.

At the very back of the ballroom, tucked beneath a marble pillar beside the service doorway, sat Table Forty-Two.

General Manager Sun Haoran sat rigid in his newly pressed dark suit, dabbing his damp palms against a folded napkin. Every few seconds he glanced nervously toward the stage, where provincial heavyweights laughed easily before the cameras.

Beside him sat Chen Xuan in his clean, washed gray jacket and plain white shirt. A ceramic mug of jasmine tea sat untouched by his left hand. With a cheap blue ballpoint pen, he quietly turned the pages of the sixty-page printed technical annex distributed at registration, reading the dense eight-point legal text with the same unhurried focus he gave a monthly fuel spreadsheet at Hengxin Logistics. Occasionally he made a tiny checkmark in the margin.

"Chen Xuan, put that booklet away for a minute," Sun Haoran whispered urgently, leaning sideways. "Deputy Provincial Governor Wu Zhenbang is about to speak. Do not look like we are studying for an audit."

"I am almost finished, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan replied mildly, his voice gentle and even. "There are two unusual lines near the back of the annex. I wanted to make sure I did not misread the decimal points."

Sun Haoran sighed softly, too anxious to ask what decimal points mattered to Hengxin Logistics. They had only been invited to fill out the regional representation quota. All Sun Haoran wanted was to survive the morning and catch the high-speed train back to Qingyan City intact.

* * *

A sharp chime quieted the four hundred guests.

Deputy Provincial Governor Wu Zhenbang strode confidently to the central lectern, adjusting his silver-rimmed glasses beneath the television lights. His tailored navy suit was spotless, and his smile radiated provincial authority.

"Distinguished guests, industry leaders, and friends from the media," Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang began, his resonant voice filling the hall. "Today marks a historic turning point for Lanshui Province. For too long, our logistics backbone has been fragmented into scattered county depots and outdated family-run trucking yards."

He gestured warmly toward the stage front row, where Song Baisheng stood and bowed to polite applause.

"Under the visionary leadership of Chairman Song Baisheng and the Smart-Logistics Consortium," Wu Zhenbang continued, glowing with praise, "our province will unify port manifests, rail scheduling, highway freight, and mineral transport onto one modern cloud architecture. This is the future of provincial commerce."

Song Baisheng beamed beneath the lights, clasping his hands with practiced modesty.

In the center of the VIP front row, Li Weiming sat with relaxed elegance in a charcoal suit tailored in Zurich, watching the stage with calm approval. Beside him sat two European clearing directors from Singapore, equally serene.

Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang's tone sharpened as his gaze swept the rear tables.

"Of course, progress always encounters inertia," Wu Zhenbang said smoothly, a faint sneer touching his mouth. "There are still small, stubborn municipal outfits clinging to paper ledgers and diesel habits, firms like Hengxin Logistics sitting out in the back rows today. Small, fragmented operators are outdated obstacles. Either they integrate cleanly into Chairman Song Baisheng's unified platform, or the market will sweep them aside before winter."

Polite, condescending chuckles rippled through the middle tables. Several executives glanced back toward Table Forty-Two.

Sun Haoran's face flushed crimson, then drained pale. His fingers clenched his napkin beneath the tablecloth, his shoulders hunching as if struck.

Chen Xuan did not flinch or frown. He closed the sixty-page technical annex, laid his blue ballpoint pen neatly parallel to its spine, and took a calm sip of jasmine tea.

"Do not take it to heart, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan murmured quietly. "Speeches like that are usually written to make people sign faster."

* * *

After Song Baisheng delivered a brief speech praising Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang's leadership, the forum moderator stepped forward for procedural formality.

"Before our municipal directors and consortium partners proceed to the signing table," the moderator announced brightly, "we have five minutes for brief technical questions from attending delegates. Does anyone have a question regarding the consolidation framework?"

The question period was purely decorative. Sun Haoran stared hard at his water glass, praying the five minutes would vanish in ten seconds.

At Table Forty-Two, Chen Xuan raised his right hand politely to shoulder height.

Sun Haoran froze in sheer horror. "Chen Xuan! Put your hand down! What are you doing?"

Before Sun Haoran could grab his sleeve, a forum usher beside the pillar spotted the raised hand and hurried over with a cordless microphone.

On stage, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang squinted toward the back corner, spotting the gray jacket beside Hengxin Logistics' nameplate. Amused contempt flickered across his face.

"Ah, a representative from Hengxin Logistics," Wu Zhenbang said with smooth, patronizing patience. "By all means, young man. If a small county dispatcher has trouble understanding cloud consolidation, Chairman Song Baisheng will clarify the basics for you."

Soft laughter echoed from the VIP tables. Song Baisheng smiled indulgently at the podium.

Chen Xuan stood up smoothly, holding the cordless microphone lightly with both hands like an ordinary office clerk presenting a quarterly shipping variance.

"Good morning, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang, Chairman Song Baisheng, and everyone here," Chen Xuan said clearly. His voice carried calmly through the ballroom speakers, mild, polite, and unhurried. "My name is Chen Xuan, an operations clerk with Hengxin Logistics in Qingyan City. I have two brief spreadsheet questions regarding Article 14 of the printed technical annex."

The soft laughter faded at the mention of a specific clause.

"First," Chen Xuan continued, opening the annex effortlessly to page forty-two, "under Subsection 14.3, every participating provincial port, rail freight yard, and municipal logistics hub is legally required to mirror real-time vessel weights, domestic port manifests, and strategic mineral transit schedules onto an encrypted overseas clearing server physically hosted in Zurich."

A sudden stillness gripped the front half of the ballroom.

"My question is simple," Chen Xuan said politely, genuinely curious rather than accusatory. "Why are domestic freight schedules between inland cities in Lanshui Province legally required to mirror live onto a foreign server in Switzerland?"

No one chuckled now. Across the middle tables, several municipal port directors stopped whispering and reached for their unopened annex booklets.

Chen Xuan turned one page gently, his tone still calm and respectful.

"And my second question concerns Subsection 14.7," Chen Xuan added. "That clause grants the offshore Swiss trust holding the root decryption key permanent exemption from provincial audit, while charging domestic freight firms a mandatory routing fee five times higher than our own state-certified provincial cloud centers. From an accounting standpoint, could Chairman Song Baisheng explain why our province is paying five times the normal cost to hand live strategic mineral data to an unaudited overseas trustee?"

Chen Xuan lowered the microphone politely, waiting with quiet, ordinary attentiveness.

Across the vast Grand Ballroom, not a single chair creaked. Four hundred people stared in absolute, stunned silence.
