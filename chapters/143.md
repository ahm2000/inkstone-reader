# Chapter 143: The Pavilion by the Lake

The black sedan climbed steadily up the winding north garden road into the Western Hills, where tall pines and golden autumn maples shaded the quiet pavement.

At the outer stone archway two kilometers below the lake, the wooden barrier already stood raised. Not a single sentry, aide, or general stood anywhere near the gatehouse.

Inside the car, Number 7 checked the mirrors with quiet admiration. "Elder Song truly cleared the entire mountain ridge," he said softly. "Every camera pole along the fence has been powered down, and all security posts were pulled back to the south valley at dawn. There is not another living soul between here and the lake."

"When a ninety year old founder gives his word, he does not do things halfway," Chen Xuan replied, looking calmly out at the sunlit trees.

Five minutes later, the narrow road opened into a quiet gravel courtyard tucked beneath ancient cedars at the base of the lakeside trail.

The driver brought the sedan to a smooth stop beside a mossy stone wall. Before Number 7 stepped out, his gaze flicked toward the cedar grove fifty meters back down the lane.

The unmarked dark van had pulled silently into the deep shade of the pines. Three masked figures in plain black gear stood motionless beneath the branches without numbers or badges, keeping their strict fifty meter perimeter while facing outward toward the lower mountain slope.

Number 7 stepped out in his crisp white gear and opened the rear door with a respectful bow.

"No generals, no guards, and no cameras anywhere around the water, sir," Number 7 reported quietly. "Only Elder Song is up at the pavilion."

"Good," Chen Xuan said with a light smile. He stepped onto the gravel, adjusted the collar of his cheap dark windbreaker, and shifted his scratched green thermos into his left hand. "Wait here by the car, Number 7. Pour yourself a cup of hot tea from the cabin flask and stretch your legs. I will be back in half an hour."

"Yes, sir," Number 7 said, inclining his head. "Take your time."

Chen Xuan turned and walked unhurriedly up the stone path alone. His worn canvas shoes made soft, steady taps against the clean gray flagstones. Halfway up the slope, the path curved around old cypress trees, and the Western Hills lake opened before him, flat and clear as pale green glass under the morning sun.

Not a single guard or attendant stood along the shore. The entire ring of hills around the water was hushed.

At the end of the stone path, right beside the water's edge, stood an open stone pavilion with gray tiled eaves and four weathered pillars. Inside sat a round stone table and two plain stone stools.

Seated completely alone on the far stool was a thin, silver haired old man.

Elder Song wore a plain dark blue cotton jacket buttoned neatly to the throat, loose dark cloth trousers, and simple black cloth shoes. At ninety years old, his shoulders were slightly curved, yet his white hair was neatly combed and his eyes shone bright and clear as winter water.

On the stone table before him sat a small purple clay teapot, a tiny clay stove glowing with white charcoal ash, and two simple white porcelain cups.

Hearing steady footsteps on the flagstones, Elder Song turned his head toward the path.

He watched a calm young man in his twenties walk up the steps wearing a cheap dark windbreaker and worn canvas shoes, carrying a dented fifteen yuan green metal thermos in his left hand.

For three full seconds, ninety year old Elder Song simply stared at him. Then, slowly, the old man's weathered face broke into a wide, delighted grin.

"Well, I will be!" Elder Song exclaimed in a soft, raspy voice filled with warmth. "Here I sat all morning wondering what kind of formidable dragon holds the Black Order and commands a fortune larger than forty ministries combined. I half expected a stern graybeard in a silk robe, and instead up walks a polite young fellow in canvas shoes carrying an old green water bottle!"

Chen Xuan stepped into the pavilion, bowed respectfully to the ninety year old elder, and chuckled warmly as he set his green thermos on the edge of the stone table.

"Good morning, Elder Song," Chen Xuan said gently. "If I wore a silk robe on the Shanghai subway, people would think I escaped from a costume drama. A cheap windbreaker is much easier to wash."

Elder Song threw his head back and laughed aloud, the sound echoing lightly across the empty lake. "A costume drama on the subway! Oh, I like you already, young man! Come, sit down before the lake breeze cools your ears. Look around. There is not a single general, secretary, or guard within two kilometers of us. Just you, me, and the fish in the lake."

Chen Xuan sat down comfortably on the cool stone stool across from him. Up close, he could see the faint pale scar above Elder Song's left eyebrow, an old mark from a winter campaign seventy years ago, paired with eyes that looked at him like a kind grandfather welcoming a long awaited guest.

Elder Song lifted the small purple clay teapot with a steady hand and poured two cups of clear amber tea. The roasted scent of aged Wuyi rock tea drifted across the table.

"Look at that thermos of yours," Elder Song teased fondly, tapping a finger toward the scratched green metal cylinder as he pushed a cup across to Chen Xuan. "Two dents on the bottom and half the paint rubbed off. Tell an old man the truth. How much did that treasure cost you?"

"Fifteen yuan at a hardware stall three years ago," Chen Xuan smiled, wrapping his hands around the warm cup. "It keeps my wife's jasmine tea hot for eight hours without leaking a drop."

"Fifteen yuan!" Elder Song clapped his knee with a raspy chuckle. "A young man who gives the national treasury 300,000,000,000,000 yuan for free drinks his wife's jasmine tea out of a fifteen yuan thermos! If those greedy rascals we locked up last week knew who swept them out of their chairs, they would faint on their cell floors!"

"That is one reason I prefer to stay quiet," Chen Xuan said playfully, taking a slow sip of the fragrant rock tea. "Besides, your Wuyi tea tastes much better than anything served at fancy banquets, Elder Song."

Elder Song beamed with pride, lifting his own cup with a gentle nod. "Drink up, young friend. I cleared the whole mountain today so we could talk from the heart without a single ear listening in."
