# Chapter 143: The Pavilion by the Lake

The plain black sedan climbed steadily up the winding northern garden road into the Western Hills. Tall evergreen pines stood shoulder to shoulder with crimson maples and golden ginkgos, their late November leaves drifting across the quiet gray pavement whenever the mountain wind stirred.

Two kilometers below the lake, the road passed beneath an old carved stone archway. Normally, this outer checkpoint held a full squad of uniformed guards and automated barriers. Today, the painted wooden barrier stood raised high against the sky, and the stone gatehouse sat completely empty.

Inside the car, Number 7 wore a plain dark wool coat and checked the mirrors and the silent camera poles along the roadside with genuine admiration.

"Elder Song truly kept every word of his promise, Young Master," Number 7 said softly as he steered smoothly around a gentle bend. "Every lens along the fence is powered down, and all security posts within three kilometers have been cleared until noon."

"A ninety-year-old man who fought through hard decades does not play petty games," Chen Xuan said from the passenger seat, resting his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos across his knees. "Stop at the bottom of the stone steps, Number 7. You can stay in the car and listen to the radio."

"Understood, Young Master. I will wait right here until you come back down."

The sedan rolled to a gentle halt beside a mossy stone lantern where a narrow footpath wound up through a grove of bamboo toward the water. Chen Xuan opened the door, stepped out into the crisp Beijing mountain air, and zipped his simple navy jacket against the morning chill.

He tucked his green thermos under his arm and strolled up the stone steps at an easy pace. Dried maple leaves crunched softly beneath his sneakers. Through the thinning autumn branches, the wide surface of the Western Hills lake began to gleam like polished silver under the pale November sun.

At the end of the path stood a small octagonal pavilion with weathered cedar eaves and red lacquered pillars.

Inside the pavilion, a thin old man in a padded dark blue cotton jacket sat alone on a round stone stool. His hair was pure white, his back was still remarkably straight, and a pair of bright, sharp eyes looked out across the water. Beside him on the stone table sat a tiny red clay charcoal stove with a copper kettle humming gently over glowing embers, along with a small purple clay teapot and two plain white porcelain cups.

Hearing footsteps on the stone steps, Elder Song turned his head.

For a long moment, the ninety-year-old elder simply stared at the young man walking toward him in sneakers, a simple jacket, and carrying a dented green metal thermos under one arm. Then, slowly, a delighted laugh broke across Elder Song's weathered face.

"Well now," Elder Song chuckled, his voice raspy and warm like dry autumn leaves. "I spent half the night guessing what kind of stern, gray-haired scholar or towering financier might climb these steps. I never expected a cheerful young fellow who looks like he just stepped out of a neighborhood park after morning stretches."

Chen Xuan smiled and gave a polite, respectful nod as he stepped beneath the cedar eaves. "Good morning, Elder Song. I hope I did not keep you waiting in the cold."

"Not at all, not at all! Sit down, young friend," Elder Song said, gesturing warmly to the stone stool across from him. "The wind up here keeps an old man's head clear, and the water just reached a rolling boil."

Chen Xuan sat down comfortably and placed his dented 15-yuan green thermos on the corner of the stone table.

Elder Song's eyes dropped to the chipped green paint on the thermos, then flicked back up to Chen Xuan's relaxed face. The old man's shoulders shook with another hearty laugh.

"I brought out my last tin of forty-year-old Wuyi rock tea from the cliffs," Elder Song teased, lifting the copper kettle to rinse the small purple clay teapot with steaming water. "And you brought a green tin canteen from a corner market. You really know how to humble an old man's collection."

"Do not sell your tea short, Elder Song," Chen Xuan grinned. "I can smell that roasted cliff aroma from three steps away. My thermos just holds regular jasmine leaves. It is nothing fancy, but it stays piping hot through a whole afternoon of sorting shipping forms."

Elder Song poured two delicate cups of clear amber tea and pushed one across the stone table. "Try this first. Tell me honestly if it beats your jasmine."

Chen Xuan picked up the warm porcelain cup, took a careful sip, and let the rich, mineral sweetness settle on his tongue. "Smooth, clean, and zero bitterness. That is genuinely good tea, Elder Song."

"Ha! You have a good palate," Elder Song said with obvious pleasure. Then his eyes twinkled mischievously. "Now pour me a splash from that dented green thermos of yours. I have not had proper street-corner jasmine tea in twenty years."

Chen Xuan laughed, unscrewed the battered lid, and poured a small stream of fragrant golden tea into the spare lid cup. Elder Song took a careful sip, nodded appreciatively, and let out a contented sigh.

"Sweet and honest," Elder Song remarked, setting the lid down. "Half the stiff fellows down in the city ministries would lose their minds if they saw us sitting up here trading sips from a 15-yuan thermos. They spend all day writing thick reports trying to imagine the mysterious giant behind the Black Order, and here you are enjoying the breeze."

"Reports make people tired," Chen Xuan replied easily, warming his hands around his porcelain teacup. "A quiet morning and a hot cup of tea are much better for the heart."

Elder Song looked across the table at the calm young man, his laughter softening into deep, genuine warmth. The mountain wind swept lightly over the lake, carrying the scent of pine needles and autumn leaves into the quiet pavilion.
