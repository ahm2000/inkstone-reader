# Chapter 143: The Pavilion by the Lake

The plain black sedan climbed steadily up the winding northern garden road into the Western Hills. Tall evergreen pines stood shoulder to shoulder with crimson maples and golden ginkgos, their late November leaves drifting across the quiet gray pavement whenever the mountain wind stirred.

Two kilometers below the lake, the road passed beneath an old carved stone archway. Normally, this outer checkpoint held a full squad of uniformed guards and automated barriers. Today, the painted wooden barrier stood raised high against the sky, and the stone gatehouse sat completely empty.

Inside the car, Number 7 checked the mirrors and the silent camera poles along the roadside with genuine admiration.

"Elder Song truly kept every word of his promise, Young Master," Number 7 said softly. "Every lens along the fence is powered down, and all security posts were pulled back to the south valley before dawn. Between this archway and the lakeside pavilion, there is not another living soul on the ridge."

"When a ninety-year-old founder gives his word, he does not do things halfway," Chen Xuan replied calmly, resting his chin lightly against his hand as he watched the sunlit trees slide past the window.

Five minutes later, the narrow road opened into a quiet gravel clearing framed by bare weeping willows. Beyond a low stone wall, a flagstone footpath curved upward through a grove of red maples toward a cedar pavilion perched beside a mirror-still mountain lake.

Number 7 brought the sedan to a gentle stop, stepped out, and opened the rear door.

"I will remain right here by the car, Young Master," Number 7 said respectfully. "Take all the time you need."

"Thank you, Number 7. Get some rest while you wait."

Chen Xuan stepped out into the crisp Beijing air, tucked his dented green metal thermos under his arm, and walked unhurriedly up the flagstone path. His worn canvas shoes crunched lightly over dry maple leaves. The air smelled of pine needles, cold lake water, and the faint, inviting scent of woodsmoke drifting down from the pavilion ahead.

As he rounded the final bend of the path, the cedar pavilion came into full view.

Inside, seated on a round stone stool beside a small stone table, was a slender old man with neatly combed silver hair. He wore a thick dark gray padded coat buttoned to the throat and a soft wool scarf looped neatly around his neck. Beside his knee sat a tiny clay charcoal stove, its red embers glowing warmly beneath a dark purple clay teapot that whispered with thin white steam.

Hearing footsteps on the flagstones, ninety-year-old Elder Song slowly turned his head.

His face was deeply lined by nearly a century of wind and frost, yet his eyes were astonishingly clear, bright, and steady. When his gaze landed on Chen Xuan, a flash of genuine surprise crossed the old man's face.

Clearly, Elder Song had expected a weathered elder, a gray-haired financier, or a stern master carrying the weight of empires. Instead, standing before him was a relaxed young man in a cheap dark windbreaker and canvas shoes, holding a scratched 15-yuan green metal thermos under his arm.

For two quiet seconds, neither of them spoke. Then Elder Song threw his head back and let out a warm, raspy laugh that echoed softly across the water.

"Well now!" Elder Song chuckled, his eyes crinkling with pure delight as he gestured toward the opposite stone stool. "I have spent ninety years guessing people's minds, and I still missed the mark by half a century! Please, young friend, come sit down before the wind chills your tea."

Chen Xuan smiled respectfully, stepped into the pavilion, and bowed slightly before taking his seat across the stone table. "Good morning, Elder Song. Thank you for inviting me up to such a peaceful spot."

"Do not thank me yet, wait until you taste the tea," Elder Song said briskly, lifting the purple clay teapot with surprisingly steady hands. Amber liquid arced cleanly into two tiny white porcelain cups, releasing the rich, roasted fragrance of top-grade Wuyi rock tea. "I brought this pot up myself and lit the charcoal with my own two hands. Not even my doctor knows I slipped out here without an overcoat liner."

"If your doctor finds out, I might get blamed for helping you sneak out," Chen Xuan laughed softly, picking up his warm cup.

"Let him scold me! At ninety years old, a man has earned the right to drink one good pot of tea without three nurses hovering behind his ears," Elder Song said with a mischievous twinkle. His gaze dropped to the scratched green cylinder sitting beside Chen Xuan's elbow, and his smile widened. "Still, young friend, I have to ask. You moved 300 trillion yuan without blinking an eye, yet you carry a 15-yuan green thermos all the way from the south?"

Chen Xuan glanced affectionately at his thermos and chuckled. "My wife filled it with hot jasmine tea before I left home this morning. If I ever forget it on a trip or lose it somewhere, I will be sleeping on the living room sofa for a week."

Elder Song laughed so hard he had to pat his own chest to catch his breath. "A man who fears his wife's scolding is always a man who loves his home! Good, very good. That already tells me more about your character than a hundred intelligence files ever could."

The laughter settled gently into the quiet morning. Out on the lake, a soft ripple brushed the reeds along the far shore.

Elder Song cupped his warm porcelain teacup between his thin palms, his expression turning thoughtful and deeply sincere.

"In my youth," the old man said quietly, looking out over the clear autumn water, "I watched good men freeze in thin cotton jackets just to build a single stretch of railway. I watched whole counties count every grain of millet so our children could have a roof over their heads. I never dreamed I would live long enough to see someone place a mountain of wealth into the people's hands and then ask for nothing in return, not even a single line in the history books."

Chen Xuan sipped his Wuyi rock tea slowly, letting the warm roasted flavor linger on his tongue. "The people who built those railways did the hard part, Elder Song. I only helped make sure their grandchildren do not have to start over."

Elder Song looked back at him, his bright old eyes shining with quiet emotion, and reached for the teapot to pour a second round.
