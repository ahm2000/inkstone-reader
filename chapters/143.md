# Chapter 143: The Pavilion by the Lake

The black sedan climbed steadily up the quiet north garden road into the Western Hills. Tall pines and golden ginkgo trees shaded both sides of the lane from the bright morning sun.

At the first stone archway, two guards in plain olive coats stood beside a raised barrier. Neither man asked for papers or peered into the rear window. As soon as the black sedan slowed, the older guard gave a crisp, silent nod and waved them straight through.

Inside the car, Number 7 watched the mirrors closely. "General Qin Zhengguo cleared the whole north approach," he said quietly. "Not a single camera pole along the fence has its red light on."

"He is a man who keeps his word," Chen Xuan replied, looking calmly out at the passing trees.

Five minutes later, the road opened into a small gravel courtyard tucked beneath old cedars. Beyond the courtyard, a wide stone path wound gently upward toward the Western Hills lake.

The driver brought the sedan to a smooth stop beside the low stone wall. Before Number 7 could step out, his eyes flicked toward the far side of the grove, fifty meters away.

The unmarked dark van had already parked neatly behind a bend in the outer lane. Two masked figures in plain black gear stood motionless in the shade of the cedars, while a third Black Guard member stood near the edge of the stone wall. Not one of them made a sound.

Near the far corner of the courtyard, two of Elder Song's own guards noticed the dark shapes in the trees. Their hands shifted slightly toward their belts, their backs stiffening. Then a low crackle sounded in the lead guard's earpiece. He listened for two seconds, relaxed his shoulders, and turned politely back toward the courtyard gate, leaving the masked figures alone.

Number 7 let out a quiet breath and stepped out to open the rear door.

"General Qin Zhengguo must have told his men not to touch them," Number 7 murmured.

"Smart soldiers do not start fights with people who are only standing under a tree," Chen Xuan said with a light smile. He stepped out onto the gravel, adjusted the collar of his cheap dark windbreaker, and shifted his scratched green thermos into his left hand. "Wait here by the car, Number 7. Have a rest and drink some hot water. I will not be long."

"Yes, sir," Number 7 said, inclining his head respectfully. "I will be right here."

Chen Xuan turned and walked unhurriedly up the stone path. His worn canvas shoes made soft, steady taps against the gray flagstones. Halfway up the slope, the path curved around old cypress trees, and the Western Hills lake came into view, flat as pale green glass under the morning sun.

Fifty paces before the lakeside pavilion, a tall, broad-shouldered man stood waiting beside an ancient pine tree.

He wore a plain olive uniform without a single medal on his chest, yet his straight back and weathered face carried the unmistakable weight of forty years in the field. It was four-star General Qin Zhengguo. Two silent aides stood twenty paces further back in the trees, keeping their eyes politely turned away.

As Chen Xuan approached, General Qin Zhengguo stepped slightly to the side of the flagstones to leave the path wide open. He kept his chin level, his hands resting cleanly at his sides, and lowered his eyes with quiet, soldierly deference.

"Good morning, sir," Qin Zhengguo said in a deep, gravelly voice. "Elder Song is waiting for you in the pavilion. The tea water is already hot."

"Thank you for arranging this, General Qin," Chen Xuan replied warmly. "Sorry to take up your Saturday morning."

"No trouble at all," Qin Zhengguo answered firmly, keeping his gaze respectfully fixed ahead rather than staring at the young man's face or hands. "It is my honor to stand watch while Elder Song has his tea."

Chen Xuan gave a polite nod and walked smoothly past him on the right side of the wide path, his scratched green thermos hanging casually in his left hand, tucked cleanly out of the general's line of sight.

Qin Zhengguo remained motionless beside the pine tree as Chen Xuan's footsteps faded along the stone path, his broad shoulders steady and his eyes watching the lower trail.

Fifty paces ahead, beside the water's edge, stood a simple stone pavilion with gray tiled eaves and four weathered pillars. There were no carved dragons, no red lanterns, and no soft cushions. Only a round stone table and two plain stone stools sat at its center.

Seated alone on the far stool was a thin, silver-haired old man.

Elder Song wore a plain dark blue cotton jacket buttoned to the throat, dark cloth trousers, and simple black cloth shoes. At ninety years old, his back was slightly curved and his hands were marked with age spots, yet his white hair was neatly combed and his eyes were bright and clear as winter water.

On the stone table before him sat a small purple-clay teapot, a tiny charcoal brazier glowing faintly with white ash, and two simple white porcelain cups. A thin ribbon of steam rose gently from the spout.

Hearing footsteps on the stone steps, Elder Song lifted his head.

He looked at the young man walking up in a cheap dark windbreaker and worn canvas shoes, carrying a dented, scratched green metal thermos in one hand. For two full seconds, Elder Song simply looked at him. There was no shock on the old man's face, no suspicion, and no stiff formality. Instead, the corners of his eyes crinkled into a slow, warm smile.

"Look at that," Elder Song said in a soft, raspy voice that still carried quiet strength. "General Qin told me our guest liked to keep things simple, and he was not exaggerating. Come in, young man. Sit down before the lake wind cools your ears."

Chen Xuan stepped into the pavilion, bowed his head respectfully from the waist, and set his scratched green thermos gently on the edge of the stone table.

"Good morning, Elder Song," Chen Xuan said softly. "I hope I did not keep you waiting long."

"At ninety years old, waiting beside a quiet lake is the easiest job I have left," Elder Song chuckled, waving a thin hand toward the opposite stool. "Sit, sit. No need to stand on ceremony with an old man."

Chen Xuan sat down comfortably on the cool stone stool. Up close, he could see the faint pale scar above Elder Song's left eyebrow, an old mark from a winter battle seventy years ago.

Elder Song lifted the small clay teapot with a steady hand and poured two cups of clear amber tea. The roasted scent of old Wuyi rock tea drifted across the table.

"I almost asked the kitchen for a tray of sweet cakes," Elder Song said with a faint twinkle in his eyes as he pushed a cup toward Chen Xuan. "Then I thought, a man who brings his own scratched thermos to the Western Hills probably does not care much for fancy pastries."

Chen Xuan smiled easily, wrapping his fingers around the warm cup. "You guessed right, Elder Song. I ate two scallion pancakes on the plane. This tea is more than enough."

Elder Song laughed softly, lifting his own cup and looking across the table with deep, quiet clarity.

"Two scallion pancakes and a fifteen-yuan thermos," Elder Song murmured, shaking his head gently. "And yet half the ministries in the capital could not sleep last month because of you. Come. Drink while it is hot. An old man has waited a long time to share this cup."
