# Chapter 52: Berth Seven

The heavy rosewood doors of the thirty-eighth floor office swung open without a knock.

Zhou Mingcheng strode across the marble floor, his jaw clenched tight and his designer tie pulled loose at the collar. A faint damp ring still lingered near his cuff where tea had splashed across the tablecloth at noon.

Behind the mahogany desk, Zhou Guodong did not immediately look up from his shipping ledgers. As Chairman of Shenghua Maritime Group, he had spent twenty years turning Qingyan Port's Berth Seven into his private kingdom, and he rarely hurried for anyone.

"If you are going to barge in like a dockhand, at least close the door," Zhou Guodong said, setting down his fountain pen. "You look like someone dragged you through a fish market. What happened at the Haiyue lunch?"

Zhou Mingcheng stopped before the desk, his chest rising and falling. He had rehearsed the story twice in the elevator, stripping out every detail that made him look small.

He did not mention how Miss Qin Yuhan had barely spared him a glance. He did not mention how she had poured tea with her own hands for an ordinary clerk in a gray shirt, or how she had politely dismantled his bid in front of the dining room.

"Haiyue Hotel Group is being played for fools, Dad," Zhou Mingcheng said, keeping his voice low and indignant. "A third-rate outfit called Hengxin Logistics got into Miss Qin Yuhan's ear before I sat down. They undercut our coastal supply rates with garbage numbers and fed her lies about Shenghua Maritime's turnaround times."

Zhou Guodong leaned back in his leather chair, folding his hands across his stomach. His sharp eyes studied his son's flushed cheeks.

"Hengxin Logistics," Zhou Guodong repeated slowly. "Sun Haoran's little company on the twelfth floor of the Dongfang Building? They barely move forty containers a month."

"That is what makes it intolerable," Zhou Mingcheng snapped, leaning his knuckles against the desk edge. "A clerk from their office, some nobody named Chen Xuan, sat across from me and acted like Shenghua Maritime did not exist. When I reminded him who controls the deepwater lanes at Qingyan Port, he smiled in my face and practically laughed us out of the room."

Zhou Guodong's gaze hardened. Two things mattered above all else in Qingyan's shipping circle: Haiyue Hotel Group's annual contract, and the rule that nobody stepped on Shenghua Maritime at the harbor.

"Miss Qin Yuhan is Qin Zhengming's daughter," Zhou Guodong murmured, tapping the blotter. "I cannot touch Haiyue Hotel Group directly."

"So we swallow the insult?" Zhou Mingcheng demanded. "By tomorrow, every broker along Harbor Road will hear that a clerk from Hengxin Logistics embarrassed Shenghua Maritime at Haiyue's table. Who will respect our rates next quarter?"

Zhou Guodong gave a cold grunt. "Did I say we were swallowing anything? Sit down. You are giving me a headache."

He reached for the tablet beside his teacup and tapped into the Qingyan Port dispatch portal, using an internal login Shenghua Maritime had quietly shared with friendly clerks for years.

Within thirty seconds, Hengxin Logistics' active manifest list appeared on the glowing screen. Zhou Guodong stopped on a cluster of three forty-foot containers staged in the south apron of Berth Seven.

"Look at that," Zhou Guodong said, a slow smile lifting the corner of his mouth. "Three priority containers booked onto the *Eastern Crane*. Vessel cutoff is Friday morning at ten o'clock sharp. One carries imported kitchen fixtures for Haiyue's southern resort, and the other two hold precision commercial cooling units for a factory in Ninghai."

Zhou Mingcheng leaned over the desk, his eyes lighting up. "If they miss Friday's vessel cutoff, the next direct sailing is ten days out."

"Worse than that," Zhou Guodong replied smoothly. "Sun Haoran signed a strict on-time delivery bond to win that Ninghai order. A missed sailing triggers a liquidated penalty of two million yuan. For a firm the size of Hengxin Logistics, two million yuan is not a fine. It is a coffin nail."

* * *

Zhou Guodong lifted a dark clay teapot and poured himself a small cup of aged pu'er tea. Men who shouted at the port lasted two years; men who knew which clerk stamped which form lasted twenty.

He picked up his plain silver mobile phone, the one that never appeared on company filings, and dialed a saved number.

It rang twice before a smooth, nasal voice answered. "Chairman Zhou. Rare for you to call before dinner. Everything smooth at the yard?"

"Deputy Wang," Zhou Guodong said warmly. "Smooth as always, thanks to your steady hand. By the way, I heard your wife's knee has been bothering her in this damp weather. I had my secretary pick up two boxes of wild ginseng from Changbai Mountain. I will send them to your driver tomorrow."

On the other end, Wang Zhiming, Deputy Director of Inspection at Qingyan Port Customs, gave a quiet chuckle. "Chairman Zhou is always too thoughtful. You never need to stand on ceremony with me. What is on your mind?"

"A small matter of port hygiene," Zhou Guodong said lightly. "There is a minor forwarding company, Hengxin Logistics. They have three containers at Berth Seven for Friday's *Eastern Crane*. I heard their packing declarations look sloppy. It would be a shame if unverified cargo slipped past your team."

A brief pause followed while Wang Zhiming tapped a keyboard at his desk.

"I see them," Wang Zhiming said, dropping his tone into businesslike efficiency. "Three forty-foot boxes under Hengxin Logistics. Everything looks clean on the automated risk screen, Chairman Zhou. The system already gave them green clearance."

"Automated systems miss things all the time, Deputy Wang," Zhou Guodong replied, swirling his dark tea. "That is why the port needs experienced eyes like yours. Flag them for an extended manual inspection hold. Use a secondary documentation code so brokers cannot clear it online. Keep the locks on until Friday at two in the afternoon."

Wang Zhiming exhaled softly. "Friday at two means they miss the ten o'clock sailing. Sun Haoran will be screaming at the port desk by nine."

"Let him scream," Zhou Guodong said gently. "An honest company never minds cooperating with customs. And when your team finishes, remind the yard supervisor that our six containers of agricultural machinery in the bonded lane are still scheduled for Friday noon release."

"Already marked and cleared in my folder, Chairman Zhou," Wang Zhiming replied without hesitation. "Consider the Hengxin Logistics boxes frozen. I will enter the manual override tonight."

"You are a reliable friend, Deputy Wang. Enjoy your evening."

Zhou Guodong ended the call and set the silver phone neatly beside his ledger. Across the desk, Zhou Mingcheng let out a slow, satisfied breath.

"Two million yuan," Zhou Mingcheng murmured, straightening his collar. "By Friday morning, Sun Haoran will drag that Chen Xuan to Berth Seven just to kneel in front of my car and plead for a phone call."

Zhou Guodong took a measured sip of tea. "Do not make a spectacle on the dock, son. Let them sweat until nine thirty on Friday. If Sun Haoran withdraws his Haiyue proposal and apologizes properly, maybe Deputy Wang finds his keys. If not, Hengxin Logistics can go bankrupt and teach Qingyan Port a quiet lesson."

"I will be at Berth Seven at eight thirty sharp on Friday," Zhou Mingcheng said, smiling coldly as he turned toward the doors. "I want to see Chen Xuan's face when the clock runs out."
