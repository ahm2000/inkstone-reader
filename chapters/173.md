# Chapter 173: White Order

For one stunned second, nobody in the waterside dining hall moved.

Then Shen Jingzhou threw his head back and roared with mocking laughter, nearly choking on his cigarette smoke.

"Did you hear him, Grandfather?" Shen Jingzhou gasped, pointing wildly at Chen Xuan. "He says he gave us a chance to eat dinner in peace! Who does this Shanghai clerk think he is, the Emperor of Shenzhen Bay? Liang Biao, stop standing there and smash his teeth in!"

"Wait," Koo Tianming murmured, his sharp eyes narrowing behind his gold-rimmed glasses.

Something about Chen Xuan's total lack of panic sent a cold prickle down Koo Tianming's spine.

Chen Xuan did not even blink.

He calmly reached into the left pocket of his dark trousers and drew out a sleek, plain matte-white secondary phone.

Resting his elbow lightly on the table, he tapped the bright screen once with his thumb and spoke two quiet, calm words into the open line.

"White Order."

Then he ended the call and set the matte-white phone face down beside his porcelain saucer.

Liang Biao sneered, shaking off his hesitation. "White what? Calling an ambulance for yourself, boy? Too late!"

Liang Biao swung his heavy steel baton straight toward Chen Xuan's shoulder.

At that exact second, out on the quiet coastal road beyond the villa walls, twelve pairs of bright white headlights flared to life all at once.

No sirens wailed.

Twelve matte-white sedans glided smoothly out from beneath the banyan trees in tight, silent formation, sweeping across both lanes of the harbor road to seal the entire villa in less than ten seconds.

Before Liang Biao's baton could finish its downward swing inside the dining hall, a sharp metallic snap echoed from the front courtyard gates.

Snap!

The thick steel crossbar outside did not even groan. A white-sealed cutter sheared cleanly through the heavy lock, and both iron gates swung wide open.

Through the open archway poured forty figures in crisp white-trimmed dark uniforms, moving with silent, breathtaking speed.

At their front strode three unmistakable leaders.

On the left moved Number 7, tall and sharp-eyed, carrying a matte-white baton tucked against his forearm. On the right moved Number 19, calm and steady, holding a white-sealed case in one hand while scanning the courtyard. In the center charged Number 88, broad-shouldered and fast as a striking leopard, leading the forward line straight across the stone bridge over the koi pond.

"What the hell is that?" a villa gate guard yelled, reaching for his radio.

He did not even reach the button. Two White Guard members stepped into his path, plucked the radio from his hand, and locked his wrists behind his back with white zip ties in two seconds flat.

Inside the dining hall, Chen Xuan did not even rise from his chair as Liang Biao's steel baton whistled toward his shoulder.

Chen Xuan leaned two inches to the right. His right hand lifted smoothly, caught Liang Biao's thick wrist right above the leather glove, and twisted once with effortless leverage.

Crack!

Liang Biao howled as his fingers sprang open, dropping the steel baton onto the stone floor with a loud clang. Before the Macau enforcer could throw a left punch, the tall glass doors of the terrace slid open with a rush of sea breeze.

Number 88 stepped straight into the hall like a sudden gust of winter wind.

With one clean sweep of his arm, Number 88 caught Liang Biao by the collar, spun him around, and drove him flat onto the marble tiles three meters away from Qin Yuhan's chair.

"Move in!" Liang Biao roared from the floor, his face purple with shock and rage. "Kill them all!"

The remaining eleven Macau enforcers surged forward at once, swinging steel batons from every doorway and corridor.

What followed was not a brawl. It was a sixty-second clinic in pure, disciplined control.

Thirty-seven White Guard members flowed through every doorway and glass archway at the exact same instant, forming an immediate protective ring around Qin Yuhan's side of the table so tightly that no flying baton or stray elbow came within three meters of her.

Two enforcers charged Number 7 near the wine cabinet. Number 7 sidestepped the first swing without shifting his breathing, tapped the man's inner elbow with his matte-white baton, and swept both legs out from under the second man in one smooth turn. Both enforcers hit the rug groaning.

Near the side corridor, Number 88 blocked an overhead strike with his forearm, drove a crisp palm strike into the enforcer's chest, and dropped two more men in four quick steps. Around the koi pond terrace, White Guard pairs disarmed the remaining hired thugs with silent ease, snapping white restraints onto every single wrist.

Fifty-two seconds after the front gates opened, the last Macau enforcer hit the stone patio floor with a dull thud.

Fifty-eight seconds in, every steel baton lay stacked neatly beside the courtyard fountain.

At sixty seconds flat, total silence returned to Shen Wanshan's harbor villa.

Not a single chandelier had swayed. Not a single wine glass on Qin Yuhan's side of the table had rattled. Not a single hair on her head was out of place, and not one scratch had touched her skin.

Twelve notorious Macau enforcers lay face down in neat rows across the marble floor and stone terrace, bound tightly in white restraints, staring upward in dazed terror.

Qin Yuhan sat motionless in her chair, her dark eyes wide for the first time all evening as she looked from the forty disciplined White Guard members to the quiet young man beside her.

Across the table, Shen Wanshan's fingers went numb. His burning cigarette slipped out of his hand, dropping straight onto his expensive silk Tang jacket. He did not even notice the small wisp of smoke scorching the dark silk.

Beside him, Shen Jingzhou stumbled backward so hard his chair crashed onto the floor. His face was drained of color, his knees shaking visibly as he stared at the white-trimmed guards lining every wall.

Koo Tianming stood frozen behind his chair, his smooth poise shattered completely. His face turned pale as chalk as his gaze locked onto the plain matte-white phone resting beside Chen Xuan's teacup.

"Matte-white sedans," Koo Tianming whispered, his voice trembling for the first time in twenty years. "Numbered guards in white trim. No. That is impossible. How could a shipping clerk from Shanghai issue a White Order?"

Number 7 stepped smoothly through the parted line of guards, stopped three paces behind Chen Xuan's chair, and gave a crisp, respectful bow.

"Sir," Number 7 reported clearly. "Courtyard and harbor pier secured in sixty seconds. Twelve armed trespassers disarmed and restrained. Zero injuries to President Qin."

Chen Xuan nodded slightly, picking up his white porcelain teacup as if nothing more exciting than a summer rainstorm had passed outside.

"Good," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Keep the front gate open. Chairman Shen's real guests are almost here."
