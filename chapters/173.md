# Chapter 173: White Order

Within five minutes, the grand dining hall of Harbor Villa had been transformed into an active federal crime scene. Uniformed Guangdong Customs officers sealed off the hallway leading to Shen Wanshan's private study, while Maritime Enforcement specialists began photographing the soaked transfer contract on the rosewood table.

Shen Wanshan sat slumped in his high-backed chair under the watchful eyes of two officers, his breathing shallow and uneven. Across from him, Kwok Wing-fai stared grimly at the floor, his sharp confidence replaced by tight, sweating silence.

A polite female investigator from Director Zhou's team approached Qin Yuhan with a notepad. "President Qin, we saw the transfer folder on the table and noticed the locked doors and private guards when we entered. Could you step over to the side alcove and give us a brief witness statement about what happened before we arrived?"

"Of course," Qin Yuhan replied, still trying to process the dizzying reversal. She glanced at Chen Xuan. "Wait here a moment, Xuan. I will be right back."

"Take your time," Chen Xuan said easily. "I am going to step into the side corridor for a breath of fresh air. The smell of spilled baijiu in here is a bit strong."

With a relaxed nod to the officer standing near the archway, Chen Xuan strolled out of the bright dining hall into the quieter, wood-paneled side corridor that ran along the west wing of the villa. Tall mullioned windows lined the outer wall, looking down over the darkened garden and the faint amber glow of the harbor beacons far below.

Chen Xuan tucked his hands into his charcoal jacket pockets and stood near a window, listening to the soft rustle of late November wind through the coastal pines. He looked completely unhurried, like a husband waiting outside a dressing room while his wife finished shopping.

A moment later, crisp footsteps sounded from the far end of the corridor.

A tall, composed man in his early thirties walked smoothly down the hall. He wore a clean, tailored dark navy business suit, a white shirt, and a muted silver tie. There was no mask, no tactical gear, and no stiffness in his stride. To any passerby, he looked like a senior corporate auditor or a legal courier from a top Beijing firm. Under his left arm, he carried a thick kraft portfolio sealed with a plain white wax stamp.

It was Number 7 of the White Guard.

Almost at the same instant, Director Zhou stepped out of the dining hall archway, having just received a priority alert on his encrypted tablet from headquarters in Beijing. Seeing the man in the navy suit, Director Zhou straightened his shoulders and walked briskly forward.

"You are the courier from the independent financial audit desk?" Director Zhou asked in a low, respectful voice.

"Yes, Director Zhou," Number 7 answered cleanly, stepping forward and handing over the heavy portfolio with both hands. "Inside are the verified offshore ledger trails, encrypted shipping manifests, and twelve years of Shen Family tax evasion and illicit port kickback records. The verified sum comes to 3.4 billion yuan. Every bank routing number and shell company signature has already been cross-checked with mainland clearing records."

Director Zhou accepted the sealed portfolio, feeling its substantial weight. He inspected the clean wax seal and let out a quiet breath of relief. With the three intercepted cargo vessels, he already had enough to detain Shen Wanshan for smuggling, but this sealed dossier would lock down the entire Shen Family network and every corrupt intermediary backing them without a single legal loophole.

"This saves our task force six months of offshore forensics," Director Zhou said sincerely. "May I ask which department should be credited in the internal case log?"

"No credit is needed, Director," Number 7 replied smoothly, keeping his tone polite and matter-of-fact. "The audit packet was compiled strictly on verified public and customs data. Your team has full authority to prosecute."

Director Zhou knew better than to press further. He nodded firmly, tucked the portfolio securely under his arm, and hurried back into the dining hall to brief his lead prosecutors.

For ten quiet seconds, the wood-paneled corridor was completely empty except for Chen Xuan by the window and Number 7 standing two paces away.

Number 7 did not make any dramatic gesture. He simply turned slightly toward the window, lowered his gaze with genuine respect, and spoke in a voice barely above a whisper so it could never carry back through the open archway.

"Young Master," Number 7 murmured evenly. "The customs ledgers and Hong Kong transfer records are all in Director Zhou's hands. Every captain and accountant on the three seized vessels has already cooperated. Shen Wanshan has no legal exit left."

Chen Xuan did not even turn away from the dark harbor view. He gave a tiny, lazy nod.

"Clean work," Chen Xuan replied quietly. "Head back and get some warm food. It is cold by the water tonight, and I do not want any extra commotion near my wife."

"Understood, Young Master," Number 7 said softly.

Without a single wasted motion, Number 7 turned and walked calmly down the corridor toward the west courtyard exit, vanishing quietly into the autumn night before anyone else stepped out of the dining hall.

Half a minute later, soft heels clicked against the wooden floorboards. Qin Yuhan walked out of the dining hall, wrapping her camel wool coat tighter around her shoulders as she joined Chen Xuan by the window.

"I finished giving my statement," she said, letting out a long sigh. She glanced down the empty corridor toward the side exit. "Did you see that sharp-looking man in the navy suit who walked past a moment ago? He looked like a senior auditor from Beijing."

Chen Xuan turned toward her with an innocent shrug and reached out to smooth a loose strand of hair behind her ear.

"I saw him pass by with a brown folder," Chen Xuan said casually. "Probably a lawyer or an accountant working with the customs office. These big joint raids always bring a dozen paperwork people along."

Qin Yuhan nodded, completely convinced. She leaned briefly against his shoulder, still marveling at how quickly the nightmare had collapsed. "I have never seen Shen Wanshan look so pale in my life. Still, he is stubborn. His lawyer just arrived and is already whispering about bail and calling his banks."

Chen Xuan smiled faintly, looking back toward the glowing archway of the dining hall.

"He can call whoever he likes," Chen Xuan said gently. "I doubt the banks are in the mood to answer his favors tonight."
