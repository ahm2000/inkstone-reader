# Chapter 172: The Toast That Never Landed

For two full seconds, nobody in the grand dining hall spoke. The only sound was the faint hum of the central heating and the distant wash of late November waves against the seawall below the cliff.

Then Shen Wanshan threw his head back and laughed, a dry, grating sound that echoed off the stone walls.

"Did you hear that, Mr. Kwok?" Shen Wanshan jeered, waving his crystal glass of baijiu toward Chen Xuan. "A junior shipping clerk is telling his wife to eat shrimp while sitting inside a locked room. Young man, you spend your days stamping parcel slips at a desk. You have no idea what kind of power runs this coastline."

Kwok Wing-fai adjusted his silver cufflinks and cast a brief look of disdain at Chen Xuan before fixing his cold eyes back on Qin Yuhan. He lifted his own crystal glass.

"Your husband is amusing, President Qin, but my patience is not," Kwok said smoothly. "You have three minutes left before we cut the buyout offer from 150 million yuan down to 80 million yuan. Let us toast to a sensible decision."

"Do not touch the pen, Yuhan," Chen Xuan said mildly, pouring himself half a cup of warm oolong tea. "The shrimp really are decent, and the steamed grouper smells fresh. It would be a shame to let good food go cold."

Qin Yuhan bit her lip, torn between shock at her husband's sheer calm and fierce anger at the two men across the table. She knew Shen Wanshan had deep roots along the southern coast, and with a dozen hired guards blocking every exit, she could not see any clean way out of the hall.

Shen Wanshan's smile turned ugly. He leaned forward, thrusting his baijiu glass toward Kwok's to seal the threat. "To the new owners of the southern routes, and to a lesson in knowing your place!"

Before the rims of the two crystal glasses could touch, a deafening boom shook the far end of the dining hall.

The heavy oak double doors burst inward, their brass latch snapping clean off the frame. A rush of cold coastal night air swept through the room, making the silk drapes billow violently.

Shen Wanshan froze with his arm suspended in midair. Kwok's hand jerked, spilling clear liquor across his knuckles.

Steady, measured footsteps rang out across the marble floor. These were not three or four local guards who could be waved away with a phone call, but a disciplined column of more than twenty officers filing rapidly into the hall. At the front marched a stern, square-jawed man in his late forties wearing a dark wool coat over a formal charcoal suit, flanked by uniformed senior officers from the Federal Customs Anti-Smuggling Bureau and the Maritime Enforcement Administration.

"What is the meaning of this?" Shen Wanshan barked, recovering his bluster as he slammed his free palm onto the table. "This is private property! Who gave you permission to break down my doors?"

Two of Shen Wanshan's burly guards stepped forward instinctively, reaching toward their jackets.

"Federal Customs joint enforcement!" a senior officer snapped, raising a metallic badge high in the bright chandelier light. "Hands in plain sight! Step back against the walls immediately, or you will be charged with obstructing a federal criminal investigation!"

The heavy steel ring of authority in his voice stripped the fight out of the hired guards in an instant. They exchanged frightened looks, raised both palms, and backed quickly against the stone pillars.

The square-jawed man at the front walked straight up to the rosewood table, stopping two paces from Shen Wanshan. He held up a crimson-stamped federal warrant and spoke in a crisp, flat tone.

"Shen Wanshan, I am Director Zhou, leading the Federal Customs Joint Enforcement Team," he announced. "Together with Guangdong Customs and Maritime Enforcement, we are executing an immediate search, asset freeze, and detention warrant covering Harbor Villa and all Shen Group shipping subsidiaries."

Shen Wanshan's face darkened from angry red to a sickly gray. "Director Zhou, there has to be a mistake! I am a respected member of the coastal chamber of commerce. My shipping fleet has passed every port inspection for ten years. Who fed you this nonsense?"

"Three of your registered bulk carriers were intercepted by Maritime Enforcement patrol cutters off the Pearl River estuary two hours ago," a senior Maritime commander said crisply, tapping a rugged tablet screen. "Hidden holds inside all three vessels contained undeclared high-tariff luxury electronics, restricted industrial components, and falsified customs manifests. Your captains have already signed written confessions."

Shen Wanshan swayed slightly, his knees suddenly feeling hollow. "No, that is impossible! Those ships were cleared through the outer channel!"

"And simultaneously," Director Zhou continued without raising his voice, "our joint financial unit traced 1.8 billion yuan in illegal underground money-house transfers over the past eighteen months. Every single tranche was disguised as maritime freight insurance and routed through offshore shell companies managed by Mr. Kwok Wing-fai."

At the mention of his name, Kwok went completely rigid. His polished composure cracked wide open. Quietly, he tried to slide his left hand toward his coat pocket to wipe his phone.

"Do not move that hand, Mr. Kwok," a Customs investigator warned sharply, stepping behind his chair and plucking the phone cleanly from his pocket into a clear evidence bag. "Your cross-border exit permit was revoked thirty minutes ago. You are not boarding any flight back to Hong Kong tonight."

The entire dining hall fell into stunned silence. The arrogant smugness that had filled the room only a minute earlier had vanished like smoke in the sea wind.

Shen Wanshan stared at the federal warrant, then at the uniformed officers fanning out toward his study and private safe. His trembling fingers lost their grip on the crystal glass he had raised to mock Qin Yuhan.

The glass slipped from his hand, struck the edge of the rosewood table, and shattered on the marble floor. Sharp baijiu splashed across the navy legal folder, soaking the unsigned Haiyue Group transfer contract.

Qin Yuhan sat frozen in her chair, her wide eyes darting from the broken glass to Director Zhou and then back to her husband.

Chen Xuan casually finished chewing his prawn, wiped his fingers with a warm white napkin, and gave his wife a mild, cheerful smile.

"See?" Chen Xuan murmured softly so only she could hear. "I told you he was about to lose his appetite. The timing of the maritime patrol tonight is really something."
