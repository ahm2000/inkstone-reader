# Chapter 172: The Toast That Never Landed

Instead of waiters carrying steamed grouper and wine decanters, the side corridors of the dining hall echoed with the heavy thud of leather boots.

Koo Tianming stood up smoothly from his rosewood chair, lifting a small white porcelain cup of clear rice wine toward Qin Yuhan with a cold, triumphant smile.

"President Qin," Koo Tianming said softly, his voice floating across the quiet table. "Before we eat, allow me to offer a toast. To Haiyue Group's hard work in Shanghai, and to a smooth handover of your southern coastal shipping routes tonight."

Qin Yuhan did not lift her cup. Her dark eyes narrowed slightly, her back staying straight.

"Handover?" Qin Yuhan asked coolly. "Mister Koo, I do not remember putting Haiyue Group's southern routes up for sale."

"You are selling them tonight," Koo Tianming replied gently.

With a light flick of his thumb, he tapped the rim of his porcelain cup twice.

Clink. Clink.

Outside in the dark garden, a heavy metallic boom shook the quiet air. The tall iron gates of the harbor villa slammed shut, followed immediately by the loud slide of a thick steel crossbar locking into place.

At the same instant, twelve broad-shouldered men in black short-sleeved shirts stepped out from the side hallways and terrace archways. Every doorway and glass exit was blocked in five seconds. They wore dark leather gloves and carried short, heavy steel batons hanging from their wrists.

At their front stood a scarred, thick-necked man in his late thirties named Liang Biao, a notorious enforcer hired straight out of Macau's underground dockyards. Liang Biao rolled his shoulders, cracking his neck with a dull pop as his cold eyes scanned the room.

Shen Jingzhou burst into a loud, ugly laugh, throwing himself back into his chair and lighting a cigarette with shaky glee.

"Look at your faces now!" Shen Jingzhou sneered, pointing his cigarette straight at Chen Xuan. "What happened to that big mouth from Shenzhen Pier Nine? Think you can walk into the Shen Family's harbor villa in a cheap taxi and stroll back out? Tonight, nobody leaves until we say so!"

Shen Wanshan did not raise his voice. The silver-haired patriarch leaned back, exhaling a slow cloud of smoke across the table. All the warm hospitality from ten minutes ago had vanished, replaced by the blunt greed of an old sea wolf.

"President Qin, do not blame Old Shen for being direct," Shen Wanshan said calmly, tapping ash into a crystal tray. "My family has ruled Shenzhen's docks for twenty years. You came down from Shanghai, took two of our best lines, and embarrassed my grandson in front of the port. Did you think we would applaud?"

"If you wanted to compete, Chairman Shen, you could have competed on price and delivery times," Qin Yuhan answered without fear. "Locking guests inside a villa with thugs from Macau is a crime."

"A crime?" Koo Tianming chuckled, setting his untouched wine cup down. "These twelve men are only here so nobody interrupts our talk. What will end Haiyue Group tonight is right here."

Koo Tianming pulled a slim black voice recorder from his suit jacket and set it in the center of the table beside a printed contract and a gold pen.

He pressed play.

A faint hiss of static filled the dining hall, followed by a voice that sounded shockingly like Qin Yuhan's own.

"Make sure the customs desk at Pier Nine receives the extra 800k yuan before Friday," the recorded voice said calmly. "I do not care how you label the invoice. Just clear the Haiyue Star before the inspection office opens the hold."

A rough male voice answered on the tape. "Understood, President Qin. Same offshore account in Hong Kong as last month?"

"Yes," the voice replied smoothly. "Keep it quiet."

Koo Tianming pressed stop.

Dead silence hung over the table.

Qin Yuhan stayed ice-calm, though her fingertips tightened slightly in her lap. She stared straight at the black recorder with pure disgust.

"A stitched voice clip," Qin Yuhan said clearly, her tone sharp as a blade. "You lifted my voice from last month's Shanghai shipping conference and spliced it with a hired actor. Any real audio lab in Beijing or Shenzhen can prove that tape is fake in two hours."

"In two hours, yes," Koo Tianming agreed with a silky smile, adjusting his gold-rimmed glasses. "Perhaps even in one hour. Yet by the time a lab proves it fake, that clip will already be playing on every financial news channel in Shanghai and Shenzhen. Tomorrow morning at nine o'clock, Haiyue Group's shares will crash to the floor. Your banks will freeze your credit, your buyers will cancel every contract, and customs officers will detain you for questioning while the rumor burns your company to ashes."

Shen Wanshan nodded slowly, blowing another ring of smoke toward the ceiling.

"Koo Tianming is right, President Qin," Shen Wanshan said smoothly. "In business, truth moves slowly, while panic kills in an hour. You built Haiyue Group with ten years of sweat. Why watch it burn over pride?"

Koo Tianming pushed the contract and gold pen across the wood until they stopped right in front of Qin Yuhan.

"Sign this transfer agreement tonight," Koo Tianming said softly. "Hand over Haiyue Group's entire southern coastal routes and your Shenzhen Pier Nine berth rights to Shenhai Shipping for a clean 100k yuan. Once the ink dries, I will crush this recorder under my heel, open the courtyard gates, and let you fly back to Shanghai tomorrow morning with your reputation intact."

"And if I refuse?" Qin Yuhan asked coldly.

"Then this recording goes out to twenty reporters and the customs hotline at nine o'clock sharp," Koo Tianming replied, his smile turning razor-thin. "And before you leave this room, Liang Biao and his men will make sure your mouthy shipping clerk learns how hard Macau steel feels on human bones."

Shen Jingzhou grinned savagely, slapping the table. "Start with his hands, Liang Biao! Let us see how he holds his green thermos with ten broken fingers!"

Liang Biao let out a low, ugly laugh. He raised his steel baton and took two heavy steps forward, his shadow falling across Qin Yuhan's shoulder and Chen Xuan's chair.

Qin Yuhan did not flinch, did not reach for the pen, and did not look away from Shen Wanshan.

"Send your fake tape wherever you like," Qin Yuhan said evenly, her voice ringing clear across the hall. "Haiyue Group does not kneel to harbor rats, and I will not sign a single letter of that garbage."

"Stubborn woman!" Shen Wanshan barked, his calm mask cracking into fury. "Liang Biao! Hold her arm down and press her thumbprint onto that page! Throw that clerk onto the patio and break both his legs!"

Liang Biao grinned and lunged forward, reaching straight for Qin Yuhan's wrist.

Before his fingers came within two feet of her sleeve, a soft, crisp sound cut cleanly through the noisy hall.

Clink.

Chen Xuan had calmly set his white porcelain teacup back onto its saucer.

The sound was tiny, barely louder than a raindrop hitting glass, yet Liang Biao's hand paused midair as Chen Xuan looked up.

There was no anger on Chen Xuan's face and no hurry in his eyes. He simply looked across the table at Shen Wanshan and Koo Tianming with quiet, level calm.

"The tea was actually pretty good," Chen Xuan said softly. "I gave you a chance to eat dinner in peace. Since you do not want to eat, we can finish this tonight instead."
