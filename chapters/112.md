# Chapter 112: Checking the Old Street Trail

At seven that evening, the Harbor View Hotel coffee bar was nearly empty. Two foreign men sat in a corner booth behind a potted palm over black coffee.

Carl Mercer was in his late forties, broad-shouldered, with short gray hair and a faded brown jacket. Across from him sat Anton Novak, thinner, quieter, his pale eyes checking the glass doors every ten seconds. Both had spent twenty years tracking people, bank trails, and leaks across Europe and Asia.

Li Weiming slid into the booth beside Carl Mercer and set a thick brown envelope on the table.

"There is 100k in cash inside," Li Weiming said in low English. "Another 100k lands in your Zurich account the moment you bring me a real name."

Carl Mercer did not touch the envelope right away. "Who are we looking for?"

"I do not have a name yet," Li Weiming replied, laying the folded sheet beside the envelope. "On Tuesday night, 318 officials and two major financial hubs went down in one sweep. Buried at the bottom of the same central arrest file is a small demolition dispute in Yunxi County, around Old Street and Pier Four."

Anton Novak leaned forward, eyes narrowing. "A county street?"

"Yes," Li Weiming said. "Local people here are too scared to talk. You two speak decent Mandarin, look like ordinary overseas buyers, and nobody in Yunxi knows your faces. Leave before sunrise tomorrow. Walk Old Street and Pier Four. Ask around without pushing. Find out who lived there, who fought the demolition, and whether anyone on that street has ties to Shanghai or the capital."

Carl Mercer slipped the envelope inside his jacket. "Simple enough. We stay clean, take notes, and call you by noon."

"Do not touch anyone," Li Weiming warned, looking straight at Anton Novak. "Do not pick fights. I only want to see who pulled the trigger."

"We are trackers, Marcus, not street thugs," Carl Mercer said with a dry smile. "We know how to keep our shoes clean."

By seven Friday morning, they parked a rented silver sedan two blocks from Yunxi Old Street.

Morning mist hung low over the river. Old brick houses lined the narrow lane. A woman swept leaves off her steps, steam rose from a soy milk stall, and a shopkeeper rolled up a hardware store shutter.

Anton Novak strolled down the lane and bought two cups of hot soy milk while Carl Mercer followed ten paces behind, studying every doorway and face.

"Nothing here looks like a war zone," Anton Novak said quietly in English as they reached the steps to Pier Four.

"Look at the ground by the dock gate," Carl Mercer replied.

Down by Pier Four, heavy bulldozer tracks cut into the damp mud near the river wall, yet no machines remained. A rusted fence stood half open, a torn demolition notice lying in a puddle.

An old boatman mended a net on an overturned bucket beside a small tea stall. Carl Mercer smiled politely and spoke in clear, accented Mandarin.

"Good morning, uncle. We are looking at river transport for small timber shipments. Is Pier Four still taking boats, or are they tearing the dock down?"

The old boatman chuckled and spat into the grass. "Tearing it down? Not anymore. The crews packed up and vanished Tuesday night. Left half their tools behind."

"Really?" Carl Mercer asked lightly. "Why the sudden rush?"

"Because the county officials pushing the job got hauled off in black cars the same night," the tea-stall owner chimed in, pouring two cups. "Whole street heard the sirens around midnight. Serves them right, trying to push Old Street families out for pennies."

"Must be someone important living on Old Street," Anton Novak said casually as he took a cup.

"Nobody important," the owner laughed. "Just ordinary folk. Old Chen Jianguo and his wife, the tailor, the noodle shop couple. Plain hardworking people. Though Old Chen's boy does have a steady office job in Shanghai."

Carl Mercer paid for the tea and drifted with Anton Novak toward the empty wooden dock warehouse shed.

The door hung unlocked. Inside, damp cargo sheets and dusty ledgers lay scattered on a long table.

Anton Novak knelt beside a broken bench near the back wall. Half under a cracked clipboard sat a crisp white sheet far newer than anything else in the room. He picked it up by the corner.

It was a shipping receipt from Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai, dated earlier that month, for a routine shipment through Yunxi Pier Four. At the bottom right, on the receiving clerk line, sat a neat handwritten signature.

Chen Xuan.

Anton Novak held it up for Carl Mercer.

"Hengxin Logistics," Carl Mercer murmured. "Shanghai Port. And the tea woman just told us Old Chen Jianguo's son works in an office in Shanghai."

"A shipping clerk?" Anton Novak frowned. "You really think an ordinary port clerk brought down 318 officials?"

"Of course not," Carl Mercer said, snapping three sharp photos with a slim camera phone. "A clerk does not have that kind of power. Yet he sits right on the bridge between Yunxi Old Street and Shanghai Port. If someone big stepped in to protect Old Street, they did it for somebody on that lane. Maybe this clerk works for them. Maybe someone watches over his family."

Carl Mercer slipped the receipt back under the clipboard exactly as they found it.

Ten minutes later, from their parked sedan outside town, Carl Mercer dialed Li Weiming on a disposable phone.

"We found one link between Old Street and Shanghai Port," Carl Mercer said briskly. "A young man named Chen Xuan. His parents live on Old Street. He works as a clerk at Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai."

Li Weiming went quiet for five seconds. "Just a clerk?"

"Looks like it," Carl Mercer said. "Small pay, small desk, ordinary family. Nothing stands out on paper. Want us to drop it?"

"No," Li Weiming said sharply. "Drive back to Shanghai right now. Sit outside Hengxin Logistics this afternoon. Do not talk to him or let him see you. Follow him when he leaves work and see who he meets, what car picks him up, or who watches his back. Call me at five sharp."

"Understood," Carl Mercer said.

He hung up and pointed the silver sedan back toward the Shanghai highway.

By one in the afternoon, the car sat parked under plane trees across from the modest gray building of Hengxin Logistics, engine off and windows cracked, waiting for Chen Xuan to walk out the front gate.
