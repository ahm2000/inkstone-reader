# Chapter 112: Checking the Old Street Trail

At seven that evening, the coffee bar off the Harbor View Hotel lobby was mostly empty. Only two foreign men sat in a corner booth behind a potted palm, nursing plain black coffee.

Carl Mercer was in his late forties, broad-shouldered, with short gray hair and a faded brown jacket. Across from him sat Anton Novak, thinner, quieter, with pale eyes that checked the glass doors every ten seconds. Both men had spent twenty years tracking people, bank trails, and quiet leaks across Europe and Asia.

Li Weiming slid into the booth beside Carl Mercer and placed a thick brown envelope on the table between them.

"There is 100k in cash inside," Li Weiming said in low, steady English. "Another 100k lands in your Zurich account the moment you bring me a real name."

Carl Mercer did not touch the envelope right away. "Who are we looking for?"

"I do not have a name yet," Li Weiming replied. He took the folded sheet from his jacket and laid it beside the envelope. "On Tuesday night, 318 officials and two major financial hubs went down in one sweep. Buried at the bottom of the same central arrest file is a small demolition dispute in Yunxi County, right around Old Street and Pier Four."

Anton Novak leaned forward slightly, his eyes narrowing. "A county street?"

"Yes," Li Weiming said. "Local people here are too scared to talk. You two speak decent Mandarin, you look like ordinary overseas buyers, and nobody in Yunxi knows your faces. Leave before sunrise tomorrow. Walk Old Street. Walk Pier Four. Ask around without pushing too hard. Find out who lived there, who fought the demolition, and whether anyone on that street has ties to Qingyan or the capital."

Carl Mercer slipped the brown envelope inside his jacket. "Simple enough. We stay clean, we take notes, we call you by noon."

"Do not touch anyone," Li Weiming warned, looking straight at Anton Novak. "Do not pick fights. I only want to see who pulled the trigger."

"We are trackers, Marcus, not street thugs," Carl Mercer said with a dry smile. "We know how to keep our shoes clean."

By seven on Friday morning, the two men parked a rented silver sedan two blocks away from Yunxi Old Street.

Morning mist still hung low over the river. Old brick houses lined both sides of the narrow lane. A woman swept fallen leaves off her stone steps, steam rose from a breakfast stall selling soy milk and fried dough sticks, and a shopkeeper rolled up the metal shutter of a small hardware store.

Anton Novak walked slowly down the lane with his hands in his pockets, stopping to buy two paper cups of hot soy milk. Carl Mercer strolled ten paces behind, taking in every doorway, every parked bicycle, and every face.

"Nothing here looks like a war zone," Anton Novak said quietly in English as they reached the stone steps leading down to Pier Four.

"Look at the ground by the dock gate," Carl Mercer replied.

Down by Pier Four, heavy tire tracks from bulldozers were still pressed into the damp mud near the river wall, yet not a single machine remained. A rusted metal fence stood half open, and a faded red demolition notice lay torn in a puddle.

An old boatman sat on an overturned wooden bucket near the water, mending a net while a small tea stall boiled water behind him. Carl Mercer walked over, smiled politely, and spoke in clear, accented Mandarin.

"Good morning, uncle. We are looking at river transport for small timber shipments. Is Pier Four still taking boats, or are they tearing the dock down?"

The old boatman chuckled and spat into the grass. "Tearing it down? Not anymore. The crews packed up and vanished in the middle of Tuesday night. Left half their tools behind."

"Really?" Carl Mercer asked, keeping his tone light. "Why the sudden rush?"

"Because the county officials who pushed the job got hauled off in black cars the same night," the tea-stall owner chimed in, pouring two cups of hot tea. "Whole street heard the sirens around midnight. Serves them right. Trying to push Old Street families out of their homes for pennies."

"Must be someone important living on Old Street," Anton Novak said casually as he accepted a cup of tea.

"Nobody important," the tea-stall owner laughed, waving a hand. "Just ordinary folk. Old Chen Jianguo and his wife, the tailor, the noodle shop couple. Plain hardworking people. Though Old Chen's boy does have a steady office job out in Qingyan."

Carl Mercer nodded politely, paid for the tea, and drifted with Anton Novak toward the empty wooden shed that had served as the dock warehouse office.

The door hung unlocked. Inside, old cargo sheets and dusty ledgers sat scattered across a long table. Most of the papers were years old, stained with river damp.

Anton Novak knelt beside a broken wooden bench near the back wall. Tucked half under a cracked plastic clipboard was a crisp white sheet of paper that looked far newer than anything else in the room.

He picked it up carefully by the corner.

It was a standard port shipping receipt from Hengxin Logistics in Qingyan, dated earlier that month, covering a small routine shipment routed through Yunxi Pier Four. At the bottom right, on the line marked receiving clerk, sat a neat handwritten signature.

Chen Xuan.

Anton Novak held the sheet up so Carl Mercer could see it.

"Hengxin Logistics," Carl Mercer murmured. "Qingyan Port. And the tea woman just told us Old Chen Jianguo's son works in an office in Qingyan."

"A shipping clerk?" Anton Novak asked, his brow furrowing. "You really think an ordinary port clerk brought down 318 officials?"

"Of course not," Carl Mercer said, pulling out a slim camera phone and snapping three sharp photos of the receipt. "A clerk does not have that kind of power. Yet he sits right on the bridge between Yunxi Old Street and Qingyan Port. If someone big stepped in to protect Old Street, they did it for somebody on that lane. Maybe this clerk works for them. Maybe someone watches over his family."

Carl Mercer placed the receipt back under the clipboard exactly as they had found it.

Ten minutes later, sitting inside their parked sedan outside town, Carl Mercer dialed Li Weiming on a disposable phone.

"We found one link between Old Street and Qingyan Port," Carl Mercer said briskly. "A young man named Chen Xuan. His parents live on Old Street. He works as a clerk at Hengxin Logistics in Qingyan."

On the other end of the line, Li Weiming went quiet for five seconds.

"Just a clerk?" Li Weiming asked.

"Looks like it," Carl Mercer said. "Small pay, small desk, ordinary family. Nothing stands out on paper. Want us to drop it?"

"No," Li Weiming said sharply. "Drive back to Qingyan right now. Sit outside Hengxin Logistics this afternoon. Do not talk to him. Do not let him see you. Just follow him when he leaves work and see who he meets, what car picks him up, or who watches his back. Call me at five sharp."

"Understood," Carl Mercer said.

He ended the call, turned the key in the ignition, and pointed the silver sedan back toward the highway to Qingyan.

By one in the afternoon, the car sat parked under a row of plane trees across the street from the modest gray office building of Hengxin Logistics, its engine off and its windows half cracked, waiting for Chen Xuan to walk out the front gate.
