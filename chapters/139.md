# Chapter 139: The Trustee's Signature

On the 46th floor of an old stone-and-glass tower in Shanghai's Lujiazui district, there was no company signboard beside the elevator doors.

Only a plain brass plate sat on the walnut wall. Warm lamplight fell across wooden shelves and a wide desk where a silver-haired man in a dark wool sweater sat reading a paper ledger.

Gu Shenzhi was sixty-four years old. Most months, he drank oolong tea, checked quarterly reports, and let loud billionaires argue on television without ever knowing his name.

At 3:05 on Monday afternoon, the plain black telephone on the corner of his desk rang twice.

That private line had not rung once in nearly four months.

Gu Shenzhi set down his fountain pen, straightened his back, and picked up the receiver.

"Gu Shenzhi speaking," he said calmly.

"Good afternoon, Master Gu," Number 1's steady voice came through. "Verification seven, pine, eastern gate."

Gu Shenzhi checked a small leather card in his drawer. "Verified. Clear water, third bridge. Speak freely, Number 1."

"The young master just called from Shanghai," Number 1 said briskly. "Have you seen Haiyue Group's shares today?"

"I noticed the drop at noon," Gu Shenzhi replied, leaning back. "An overseas short-selling fund called Aegis Meridian dumped borrowed shares while spreading a fake customs rumor. Crude, though effective against a mid-sized port company."

"Northern Ledger in Zurich is backing them," Number 1 explained. "They plan to drop another 4 billion yuan in short orders at nine thirty tomorrow morning to break Haiyue Group and force a sale of its docks. Here is the young master's exact order: Haiyue Group is clean. Tell our Shanghai trustee to buy every share Aegis Meridian sells tomorrow morning."

Gu Shenzhi sat motionless for two seconds.

Then a slow, genuine smile warmed his eyes.

"Every single share," he repeated softly, chuckling once. "In ten years, the young master never touched a yuan for himself, yet he opens the vault for a port chairwoman who stood up beside him in a meeting hall. Just like his grandfather."

"He wants the price driven straight to the daily limit and locked solid," Number 1 added. "No borrowed money, no bank loans, and nothing tied back to Shanghai."

"Clean cash only," Gu Shenzhi said smoothly. "Tell the young master to sleep well tonight and drink his morning tea on time. We will handle the rest before the bell."

"Thank you, Master Gu."

The line clicked softly.

Gu Shenzhi took one sip of oolong tea and pressed his intercom button.

"Shen Wenhua and Zhou Ming," he said. "Please step into my office."

Less than a minute later, two senior trust managers walked in. Shen Wenhua, a sharp-eyed woman in her late forties wearing a neat gray suit, carried a tablet. Behind her came Zhou Ming, a veteran trader who had spent twenty years running quiet accounts across Shanghai and Hong Kong.

"Close the door," Gu Shenzhi said. "We are working late tonight."

Shen Wenhua sat down, sensing the shift in the room. "Did the private line ring, Master Gu?"

"It did," Gu Shenzhi answered evenly. He laid a heavy cream-colored authorization sheet flat on the desk. "Tomorrow morning at nine thirty, an overseas fund named Aegis Meridian plans to dump 4 billion yuan in short orders on Haiyue Group. Our order is simple. Buy every share they sell until Haiyue's stock hits the daily limit and stays locked."

Zhou Ming's eyebrows shot up.

"Aegis Meridian," he muttered. "I know Julian Ash's style. He is a vulture. If he is putting 4 billion yuan into a morning ambush, he has 10 billion more in backup credit from Zurich. To swallow his attack and force a short squeeze, where sellers must buy back at rising prices, we need a wall big enough to crush his brokers' nerve in the first fifteen minutes."

"How much cash do we have liquid right now across our clean accounts?" Gu Shenzhi asked.

Shen Wenhua tapped her tablet and paused as she read the total.

"Across six domestic custody banks and six offshore reserve accounts in Hong Kong and Singapore, we have 15 billion yuan in immediate overnight cash," she reported.

"We do not need all 15 billion," Gu Shenzhi said mildly. "10 billion yuan will be more than enough."

Even Zhou Ming let out a low breath. "10 billion yuan in pure cash for one morning? Master Gu, that is nearly a third of Haiyue Group's market value, barely 35 billion yuan before today. With that behind the bid, Aegis Meridian will not just lose their trade. Their whole fund will be wiped out before ten o'clock."

"That is the point," Gu Shenzhi replied with a polite smile. "When someone walks into your neighbor's courtyard swinging a club, you take the club out of their hands so they never swing it again."

He uncapped his black fountain pen, wrote the date, filled in the exact figure of **10 billion yuan**, and signed his name across the bottom line in dark blue ink with one calm, steady stroke.

Then he slid the paper across the desk to Shen Wenhua.

"Split the 10 billion yuan across twelve trading desks," Gu Shenzhi instructed. "Six domestic desks in Shanghai and Shenzhen, and six clean offshore desks in Hong Kong. Use our family holding accounts that have not moved a share in two years. Zero borrowed margin. Pure cash settlement."

Shen Wenhua picked up the signed sheet with both hands. "If we move 10 billion yuan through ordinary channels tonight, word might leak to foreign brokers before morning."

"We do not use ordinary brokers," Zhou Ming said, a fierce grin breaking across his face. "Our twelve private custody banks clear straight through our overnight accounts. By the time Aegis Meridian's traders sit down with coffee in Zurich, the money will already be sitting on the books like dark water under thin ice."

"Exactly," Gu Shenzhi said, capping his pen with a soft click. "Tell all twelve desk leads to show zero big buy orders during the nine fifteen auction. Let Julian Ash see a scared market and dump his whole 4 billion yuan wave at nine thirty."

Zhou Ming nodded, his eyes gleaming. "Let him commit every borrowed share first. At nine thirty-one, all twelve desks open together and eat every sell order straight up to the daily limit."

"Once the stock hits the limit," Gu Shenzhi added, "stack 5 billion yuan of fresh buy orders behind the limit price and leave them there until close so Aegis Meridian cannot buy back a single share."

Shen Wenhua stood up straight. "I will notify the twelve banks and wake the desk leads now, Master Gu. Every desk will be funded and ready by midnight."

"Good," Gu Shenzhi said warmly. "Order hot dumplings and strong tea for the night crew."

By eleven thirty, twelve trading rooms across Shanghai, Shenzhen, and Hong Kong glowed behind closed blinds. Across seventy-two screens, 10 billion yuan in clean domestic and overseas funds sat lined up and waiting for Tuesday's 9:30 AM opening bell.
