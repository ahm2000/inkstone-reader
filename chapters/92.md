# Chapter 92: The First Evening of Peace on Old Street

Later that evening, Old Street glowed with warm yellow light for the first time in weeks. Shop shutters that had stayed pulled down after dusk stood half open again, spilling golden rectangles across the worn flagstones. At the corner, Old Zhao's noodle cart sent up ribbons of savory steam as neighbors sat on low wooden stools trading jokes over bowls of hot broth.

Inside Mingde Hardware, the bright ceiling tubes illuminated neat rows of wrenches, coiled wire, and freshly swept concrete. Chen Mingde stood on a low wooden stool lining up green cardboard boxes of brass hinges along the upper rack. His left shoulder was still slightly stiff from the rough treatment he had suffered earlier in the week, yet the gray exhaustion that had aged his face was completely gone.

"Hand me the two-inch bolts, Chen Jianguo," Chen Mingde called down cheerfully. "Keep the coated steel separate from the plain iron."

"I ran a supply room for twenty years," Chen Jianguo shot back, lifting the heavy tin tray up to his brother. "You think I cannot tell steel from iron?"

"You could twenty years ago," Wu Lanying teased from behind the wooden counter, wiping dust off the blue account ledger with a clean rag. "Last month you tightened our kitchen pipe and left the rubber washer sitting right on the cutting board."

Liu Meifang laughed so hard she nearly spilled the barley tea she was pouring into four chipped enamel mugs. "Do not expose him in front of his son, sister. He still claims that washer was an extra factory part."

Chen Jianguo grumbled under his breath, though the corners of his eyes crinkled with amusement.

Near the front display rack, Chen Xuan sat on a small folding stool with a wooden-handled screwdriver, quietly tightening a loose bracket on the lower nail shelf. In his plain gray jacket and faded jeans, he looked no different from any young nephew helping his uncle tidy up before closing time.

"Leave that bracket for tomorrow, Xuan," Chen Mingde said, stepping carefully down from the stool and rolling his shoulder. "You have an early flight to Shanghai in the morning. Sit down and drink some hot tea."

"Almost done, Uncle," Chen Xuan said, giving the final screw a firm half-turn and testing the shelf with his palm. "Now it will not wobble when customers pull out the heavy boxes."

Wu Lanying slid a steaming mug of barley tea across the counter toward him. "Look at the street outside. Three old customers stopped by just after dinner, not even needing nails, just wanting to see our lights on again. A whole week of gloom lifted in a single day."

Chen Mingde wiped his hands on a shop towel and looked out through the wide glass doorway. "It really did. For a few days there, I thought we would have to lock this place up and leave Yunxi County for good."

"Nobody is leaving anywhere," Chen Jianguo said firmly, taking a sip of tea. "Good people should be able to keep their doors open without looking over their shoulders."

Outside on the cobblestones, the quiet hum of the evening street parted slightly as a man in a plain dark windbreaker walked slowly along the curb. It was Wei Kun, the newly appointed Acting Director of the Yunxi Public Security Bureau. Two young uniformed officers trailed five paces behind him, walking at a calm, unhurried pace with their flashlights clipped at their belts.

Wei Kun had spent the entire afternoon overseeing the suspension of corrupt county officers and restoring regular foot patrols across the market district. As he neared Mingde Hardware, his stride slowed for just a single heartbeat.

Through the bright open doorway, he saw the modest hardware shelves, the warm steam rising from enamel tea mugs, and a simple family laughing together over trays of bolts.

Wei Kun did not step over the threshold. He did not knock, did not salute, and did not announce himself. The directive handed down from Jingzhou City had been crystal clear, and his own instincts were even sharper: this shop belonged to the untouchable Yunxi household, and the highest respect he could show was to leave their peace completely undisturbed.

He did not know the name of the quiet young man sitting by the nail shelf, nor did he dare guess who stood behind this modest storefront. He only knew that as long as he wore the badge in Yunxi County, no bully, no crooked clerk, and no shadow would ever touch Old Street again.

From the edge of the sidewalk, Wei Kun inclined his head in a brief, silent gesture of respect toward the warm light of the shop. Then he resumed his steady pace down the lane.

"Keep the night patrol loop quiet along Old Street," Wei Kun murmured to the junior officer beside him. "No sirens unless there is a real emergency. Let the shopkeepers enjoy their evening."

"Understood, Director Wei," the young officer replied softly.

Inside the shop, Chen Mingde glanced toward the doorway as the dark figure passed calmly beneath the lantern light. He smiled, a deep weight finally slipping off his chest.

"Even the new police director walks the street himself now," Chen Mingde remarked warmly, picking up his tea mug. "Tomorrow morning I am opening the shutters early, just like the old days."

"And I will bring over fresh steamed buns before we head to the airport," Liu Meifang promised.

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of warm barley tea, listening to the gentle clink of brass hinges and the easy voices of his family. All the storms in Beijing and Handong felt a thousand miles away. Right here, under the bright bulbs of Old Street, everything he cared about was whole.
