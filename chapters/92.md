# Chapter 92: The First Evening of Peace on Old Street

By nine forty-five on Monday night, Old Street glowed warmly for the first time in months. Shop shutters that had stayed locked after dusk stood half raised again, and voices drifted easily down the swept lane.

Inside Mingde Hardware, Chen Mingde stood on a low stool lining up boxes of brass hinges. His left shoulder was still stiff, yet the grey tension that had aged his face all week was gone.

"Pass me the two-inch bolts, Chen Jianguo," Chen Mingde called down. "Do not mix galvanized steel with plain iron."

"I worked in a depot for twenty years," Chen Jianguo retorted, lifting the heavy tray up to his older brother. "I know my bolts."

"You knew them twenty years ago," Wu Lanying said from behind the counter, wiping dust off the ledger book. "Last month you fixed our faucet and left the rubber washer on the table."

Liu Meifang laughed softly beside Chen Xuan as she stacked trays of screwdrivers. "Do not argue with her, Chen Jianguo. She is only teasing you because she finally has her strength back."

Wu Lanying paused, her rag resting on the wood, and looked up at Chen Mingde. "I truly thought we would never open this shutter again."

Before Chen Mingde could reply, Yu Changshun stepped across the threshold carrying two bolts of fresh gray-blue cloth under one arm and a rolled canvas strip over his shoulder.

"Still awake at ten?" Yu Changshun said, resting the canvas against the doorframe. "I saw your light from down the block. Brought clean cloth for your counter and heavy canvas to replace that torn awning."

Chen Mingde climbed down from the stool, his face brightening. "Yu Changshun, you did not have to haul that over tonight. Business has been hard enough on you."

"Save your breath," Yu Changshun waved him off, eyes crinkling warmly. "Those bolts sat untouched all winter because nobody dared walk down Old Street after dark. Tonight, three families bought quilts from me before eight. Do you know when that last happened?"

Chen Jianguo felt the thick weave. "How much do we owe you?"

"Mention money and I take it back," Yu Changshun snapped good-naturedly. "Chen Mingde took the worst of it for this whole street. Half the block saw the provincial inspection car bring him home this afternoon with that stamped exoneration certificate. The dry goods shopkeeper next door actually wept behind his counter."

Chen Mingde's throat worked once. He patted Yu Changshun's forearm. "Sit down. Wu Lanying, pour hot water."

"No need to fuss," Yu Changshun said, pulling up a stool. "I just wanted to see you behind your own counter."

A minute later, a young runner from Gong's Noodles jogged up carrying a red metal basket stacked with four steaming ceramic bowls.

"Mingde Hardware?" the runner asked, setting the basket on the counter. "Four bowls of braised beef noodles, extra tendon, and pickled radish. Already paid."

Wu Lanying blinked, her hand hovering over the teapot. "Young man, you have the wrong shop. We did not order noodles."

"Right shop," the runner insisted, lifting the lids as rich beef broth filled the room. "A quiet fellow in a dark windbreaker paid at the counter ten minutes ago and told me to bring them straight to Chen Mingde's family while they were piping hot. Said he is a regular on Old Street."

Chen Xuan glanced across the road toward the side alley. Beside a parked white van, Number Twenty-three stood with his hands in his pockets. He met Chen Xuan's gaze for half a second, gave an almost invisible nod, and stepped out of sight.

"Do not let Gong's broth turn cold," Yu Changshun urged. "Someone on this street owes you a meal, Chen Mingde. Eat."

Chen Mingde chuckled softly. "Bring two extra bowls, Wu Lanying. We will split four bowls six ways. Yu Changshun, you are not leaving without eating."

They crowded around the wooden counter on mismatched stools, chopsticks clinking warmly through the rising steam.

Five minutes later, measured leather footsteps stopped outside the doorway.

Everyone looked up.

Standing just beyond the threshold was Acting Director Wei Kun in a clean, crisply pressed dark blue uniform, his cap tucked neatly beneath his left arm. He had come alone, with no patrol car parked outside.

The laughter died instantly. Chen Mingde's hand froze over his bowl. Wu Lanying's shoulders went rigid, her fingers whitening around her chopsticks. Even Yu Changshun lowered his bowl, his jaw tightening out of old habit.

Wei Kun noticed immediately and bowed his head slightly before taking a single step.

"Mr. Chen Mingde, Mrs. Wu Lanying," Wei Kun said, his voice calm and completely free of arrogance. "I apologize for interrupting your meal. May I have two minutes?"

Chen Mingde laid his chopsticks across his bowl and stood straight, his eyes guarded. "Director Wei. Please come in."

Wei Kun stepped just inside, keeping a respectful distance from the counter. With both hands, he set a sealed brown envelope beside the ledger book.

"This is the official shop-damage compensation voucher approved by the county finance office and countersigned by the Provincial Joint Inspection Group," Wei Kun said steadily. "It covers your shattered glass, damaged doorframe, and lost inventory. You do not need to visit the bureau or sign another paper. The county bank branch on Dongfeng Road will honor it directly tomorrow morning."

Wu Lanying stared at the red official seal, her breath catching.

Wei Kun straightened, met Chen Mingde's eyes squarely, and lowered his head in a deep, unhurried bow.

"As Acting Director of the Yunxi County Public Security Bureau, I owe your family an unreserved apology," Wei Kun said quietly. "The men who wore this uniform before me failed their duty. They colluded with thugs and forced an honest citizen to suffer an injustice that never should have happened here. I cannot erase the fear you endured, yet I give you my word tonight: while I hold this post, no extortionist and no corrupt officer will harass Old Street again."

Silence held inside the shop. Chen Mingde studied Wei Kun's tired, earnest face until the tightness finally left Chen Mingde's jaw.

"Director Wei," Chen Mingde said softly, "I only sell nails and hinges. All we ever wanted was to open our door at dawn and sleep without dread."

"You will have that," Wei Kun answered firmly. "My direct desk number is inside the folder."

Wu Lanying rose, her expression softening as she reached for the kettle. "Director Wei, at least have a cup of hot tea."

"Thank you sincerely, Mrs. Wu Lanying," Wei Kun replied with a brief, genuine smile. "I will not let your noodles go cold."

As Wei Kun turned toward the street, his gaze met Chen Xuan's for one brief, unreadable second. Chen Xuan sat quietly on his stool holding his chopsticks, completely ordinary. Wei Kun gave no sign of recognition, nodded politely to the family, and stepped back into the quiet night.

Only when his footsteps faded did Chen Mingde sit back down and let out a long, trembling breath.

"In thirty years on Old Street," Yu Changshun murmured, staring at the voucher, "I never thought I would see a county bureau director bow his head in a hardware shop."

Chen Mingde picked up his chopsticks, his eyes bright beneath the bare bulb. "Eat up, everyone. Tomorrow morning, Mingde Hardware opens at seven sharp."
