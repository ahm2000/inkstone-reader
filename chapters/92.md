# Chapter 92: The Tea House by the Lake

Saturday morning arrived over Lanshui Lake in a veil of pale silver mist.

Twenty minutes outside the bustle of Lanshui City, the eastern shore curved into a quiet cove sheltered by weeping willows and tall autumn reeds. Perched on weathered cedar pilings over the shallow water stood a modest wooden pavilion with a tiled eaves roof and simple lattice windows.

There were no red welcome banners draped across the dirt lane. There were no police barricades, no flashing blue lights, no black-suited retinues sweeping the reeds with earpieces.

Two hundred meters back behind a stand of green bamboo, a single plain dark domestic sedan sat parked with its engine off and its windows rolled halfway down.

Inside the cedar pavilion, the ninety-year-old founding Supreme Elder sat comfortably on a thick indigo cotton cushion that had been laid across the wooden bench. A folded wool shawl rested around his narrow shoulders against the cool morning breeze.

On the low unpainted table before him sat a small clay brazier glowing with white-ash charcoal, a plain brown earthenware teapot, and two ordinary white porcelain cups.

The elder rested his palms near the warm clay pot, watching thin wisps of steam curl upward and vanish into the cool lake air. His face was peaceful, untouched by the haste of the capital.

Just outside the open doorway of the pavilion, standing on the wooden landing beside the steps, waited General Qin Zhengguo.

Today, the highest serving four-star commander in the country wore no olive uniform and no gold stars. He wore a plain dark civilian windbreaker, dark trousers, and sensible leather walking shoes. Yet his spine remained straight as a flagstaff, his hands folded calmly behind his back, his weathered eyes scanning the misted water with quiet vigilance.

Thirty paces further back along the shore, beneath the drooping curtain of an old willow tree, Number One stood in complete stillness. He wore a simple gray coat and kept his hands at his sides, blending so naturally into the quiet landscape that a passing fisherman would have thought him a retired schoolteacher admiring the fog.

Further out along the winding bends of the dirt lane, Number Seven and Number Nine kept silent watch out of sight, ensuring no stray joggers or weekend anglers wandered into the cove.

Yet the true stillness of Lanshui Lake did not come from the men visible beneath the willows.

Fifty meters out from the pavilion, dispersed across the reed beds, the shaded timber boathouse, and the low pine ridge overlooking the shore, the permanent Zero Guard team held the perimeter in absolute silence.

No twig snapped. No fabric rustled. No radio crackled.

Standing by the pavilion steps, General Qin Zhengguo felt the fine hairs on the back of his neck lift slightly as a cool breeze drifted off the pines.

He had spent forty years on border plateaus and forward command decks. His instincts had been sharpened by real gunpowder and real nights under hostile skies. Slowly, without turning his head abruptly, General Qin Zhengguo let his eyes sweep the pine ridge and the far reeds.

He saw nothing. No glint of glass, no shadow out of place, no silhouette against the fog.

Yet every soldier's instinct in his chest told him the exact same truth. An invisible ring of absolute lethality had just settled around the cove, quiet as winter snow and infinitely colder.

General Qin Zhengguo exhaled slowly through his nose, a quiet awe settling over his face.

"They are already here," he murmured under his breath, so softly that not even the water beneath the pilings could carry the words.

A moment later, the faint, unhurried crunch of footsteps sounded along the gravel path leading down from the suburban bus road.

General Qin Zhengguo turned toward the willow lane.

Walking calmly out of the thinning silver mist came a young man in his late twenties. He wore a clean, slightly faded navy windbreaker over a plain white cotton shirt, simple gray trousers, and comfortable walking shoes with a touch of road dust along the soles.

In his left hand, carried by its worn nylon strap, swung a scratched green metal thermos.

General Qin Zhengguo stood completely still as he watched him approach.

He had read the sealed files in the Western Hills. He had seen the financial charts showing how Aegis Meridian's hundred-trillion-yuan shadow web had been dismantled in forty-eight hours. He had read the medical reports of four elite foreign mercenaries broken in mind without a single bruise on their skin.

Part of him had expected a figure wrapped in cold authority, or at least the sharp, unmistakable tension of a man who held empires in his palm.

Instead, the man walking down the stone path looked like an ordinary office worker who had just stepped off the suburban morning bus after buying a paper bag of warm soy milk.

His steps were unhurried. His shoulders were relaxed. His eyes took in the weeping willows and the drifting lake mist with genuine, quiet appreciation.

As he passed beneath the willow tree, Chen Xuan gave Number One a mild, familiar nod. Number One inclined his head deeply in silent return, remaining by the tree so the cove stayed quiet.

Chen Xuan continued to the foot of the wooden steps and paused politely before General Qin Zhengguo.

"Good morning," Chen Xuan said, his voice calm, clear, and entirely unpretentious. "I hope I did not keep the elder waiting long."

General Qin Zhengguo straightened automatically, his broad shoulders squaring out of pure, instinctive respect before he caught himself and stepped gently aside.

"Not at all, sir," General Qin Zhengguo replied, his deep baritone unusually gentle. "You are right on time. The Old Elder arrived ten minutes ago and has just finished heating the water."

"Thank you for troubling yourself to come out here on a Saturday," Chen Xuan said mildly.

General Qin Zhengguo's eyes widened just a fraction at the ordinary courtesy of those words, then softened with deep regard. He gestured respectfully toward the open cedar doorway.

"Please go inside, sir. I will remain out here so the two of you can speak in peace."

Chen Xuan nodded politely, climbed the three low wooden steps, and set his scratched green metal thermos neatly on the dry outer ledge of the pavilion rail.

Then, with calm and natural grace, he stepped across the threshold into the warm scent of glowing charcoal and fresh spring tea.

Outside, the silver mist lifted gently off Lanshui Lake, revealing a wide, peaceful expanse of sunlit water held in silent, invisible guardianship.
