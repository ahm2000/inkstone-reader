# Chapter 92: The First Evening of Peace on Old Street

Beneath that unseen shield, Old Street finally breathed easy again. For the first time in weeks, warm yellow light spilled from almost every doorway along the worn flagstones. Shop shutters that had stayed pulled down after dusk stood wide open, and the familiar clatter of woks and cheerful voices drifted down the narrow lane.

Inside Mingde Hardware, bright ceiling tubes lit up tidy rows of wrenches, coiled copper wire, and freshly swept concrete. Chen Mingde stood on a low wooden stool lining up green cardboard boxes of brass hinges along the upper shelf. His left shoulder was still slightly stiff from the rough handling earlier in the week, yet the tired gray shadow that had aged his face was completely gone.

"Hand me the two-inch bolts, Jianguo," Chen Mingde called down with a relaxed grin. "The carpenter from East Lane wants three boxes first thing tomorrow morning."

Chen Jianguo lifted a sturdy carton off the wooden counter and passed it up to his younger brother. "Take it easy on that shoulder, Mingde. The bolts are not going to walk away if we stack them tomorrow."

"If I sit around doing nothing, my joints get stiffer," Chen Mingde laughed, setting the carton cleanly into place before stepping down off the stool.

Behind the counter, Wu Lanying wiped down the glass display case with a damp cloth and poured steaming jasmine tea into four thick ceramic mugs. Liu Meifang sat beside her folding clean blue work aprons, shaking her head fondly at the two brothers.

"Let him putter around, Jianguo," Liu Meifang said warmly. "You know Mingde only sleeps well when his shelves are straight. Xuan, come help your aunt move that crate of iron brackets before you fall asleep in your chair."

Near the open doorway, Chen Xuan sat on a low bamboo stool in a soft dark cotton jacket, sorting steel washers into small plastic trays. His single everyday smartphone rested face down beside his teacup on a wooden crate. He looked up with an easy smile and set the tray down.

"I am not asleep, Mom," Chen Xuan teased, standing up and stretching his arms lazily. "I am conducting high-precision quality control on these washers. Look how round they are."

Wu Lanying laughed and swatted his arm lightly with a folded towel. "Listen to this boy talk. Sit back down after you move the crate and eat some of these roasted peanuts. You have been running back and forth all week."

Chen Xuan hoisted the heavy crate of brackets effortlessly, tucked it neatly under the back workbench, and wandered back to his bamboo stool. As he cracked a peanut, he glanced casually out across the flagstones. Across the lane, a retired uncle in a faded windbreaker strolled past walking a small brown dog. Beside the corner fruit stand, two college-aged youths laughed while sharing a paper bag of hot roasted chestnuts. Nobody looked tense, stiff, or out of place. They blended into the autumn evening like lifelong neighbors, and a quiet, amused warmth settled in Chen Xuan's chest as he took a slow sip of his tea.

A few doors down, savory steam rose in white ribbons from Gong's Noodles. Old Gong stood behind his bubbling broth pot ladling fresh noodles into ceramic bowls for a pair of construction workers ending their shift.

Steady footsteps approached along the flagstones from the north end of the lane. Acting Director Wei Kun of the Old Street Police Station walked his regular evening neighborhood beat in a neat dark uniform, a small flashlight clipped to his belt. He paused briefly by a side alley to check that the iron gate to the dry-goods storage yard was latched properly, then waved to an elderly woman packing up baskets of autumn pears.

When Wei Kun reached the corner noodle stall, Old Gong wiped his hands on his apron and grinned. "Evening, Director Wei. Out walking the lane yourself tonight?"

"Good evening, Old Gong," Wei Kun replied with an easy smile, stepping up beside the steaming counter. "I wanted to check on that broken streetlamp by the west bend. The municipal crew finally swapped out the ballast at 4:00 PM, and the whole corner looks much brighter now."

"It makes a huge difference," Old Gong said, gesturing toward the illuminated flagstones. "Last week people were tripping over the loose curbstone after dark. Now even the older folks feel comfortable coming out for supper."

"That is how it should be," Wei Kun said. He glanced down the narrow lane as a young man on an electric scooter slowed to a walking pace before turning past the shops. "I also asked the neighborhood committee to post two clear signs at the entrance reminding delivery riders to keep their speed down after sunset. If any scooter cuts too close to your tables, let the duty desk know."

Old Gong reached for a clean porcelain bowl. "Wait a minute, Director. Let me scoop you a bowl of beef noodles with extra greens. On the house."

Wei Kun raised a hand and laughed politely. "Keep your broth for paying customers, Old Gong. I already ate a big plate of dumplings at the station, and I still need to walk the south loop by the bus stop before my shift wraps up. Have a good evening."

"You too, Director Wei. Walk safe!" Old Gong called after him.

Wei Kun continued down the lane at an unhurried pace. Passing the bright open front of Mingde Hardware, he offered Chen Mingde a friendly, natural nod of greeting, received a warm wave in return, and walked smoothly on toward the main road.

Inside the shop, Chen Mingde clicked the brass latch on the display case and looked around at his wife, his brother, his sister-in-law, and his nephew.

"Alright, everyone," Chen Mingde said happily. "That is plenty of work for one day. Let us pull the shutter halfway down and boil some fresh dumplings in the back room."

Chen Xuan picked up his smartphone, tucked it into his jacket pocket, and grinned. "Now that sounds like the best plan all evening."

Warm laughter filled the bright little hardware shop as the crisp autumn night settled peacefully over Old Street.
