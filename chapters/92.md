# Chapter 92: The First Evening of Peace on Old Street

By nine-forty-five on Monday night, Old Street glowed with warm light for the first time in months. Shop shutters that had stayed locked after dark stood half open again, and easy laughter drifted down the clean lane.

Inside Mingde Hardware, Chen Mingde stood on a low stool lining up boxes of brass hinges. His left shoulder was still stiff, yet the gray worry that had aged his face all week was gone.

"Pass me the two-inch bolts, Chen Jianguo," Chen Mingde called down. "Do not mix coated steel with plain iron."

"I worked in a warehouse for twenty years," Chen Jianguo shot back, lifting the heavy tray up. "I know my bolts."

"You knew them twenty years ago," Wu Lanying said from behind the counter, wiping dust off the account book. "Last month you fixed our kitchen tap and left the rubber ring on the table."

Liu Meifang laughed softly beside Chen Xuan as she stacked screwdrivers. "Do not argue with her, Chen Jianguo. She is only teasing you because she finally feels strong again."

Wu Lanying paused with her rag on the wood and looked up at Chen Mingde. "I truly thought we would never raise this shutter again."

Before Chen Mingde could answer, Yu Changshun stepped through the doorway carrying two rolls of gray-blue cloth and a strip of heavy canvas.

"Still awake at ten?" Yu Changshun asked, leaning the canvas against the doorframe. "I saw your light from down the block. I brought clean cloth for your counter and thick canvas to replace that torn awning."

Chen Mingde climbed down, his face lighting up. "Yu Changshun, you did not have to carry all that over tonight. Business has been hard enough for you."

"Save your breath," Yu Changshun said warmly, waving a hand. "Those rolls sat untouched all winter because nobody dared walk Old Street after dark. Tonight, three families bought quilts from me before eight. Do you know when that last happened?"

Chen Jianguo felt the thick cloth. "How much do we owe you?"

"Mention money and I take it right back," Yu Changshun snapped good-naturedly. "Chen Mingde took the worst of it for this whole street. Half the block saw the provincial inspection car bring him home this afternoon with that stamped paper clearing his name. The dry goods shopkeeper next door actually cried behind his counter."

Chen Mingde's throat tightened. He patted Yu Changshun's arm. "Sit down. Wu Lanying, pour some hot water."

"No need to fuss," Yu Changshun said, pulling up a stool. "I just wanted to see you behind your own counter again."

A minute later, a delivery boy from Gong's Noodles jogged up with a red metal basket stacked with four steaming bowls.

"Mingde Hardware?" the boy asked, setting the basket on the counter. "Four bowls of braised beef noodles, extra tendon, and pickled radish. Already paid for."

Wu Lanying blinked, stopping over the teapot. "Young man, wrong shop. We did not order noodles."

"Right shop," the boy insisted, lifting the lids as rich beef smell filled the room. "A quiet man in a dark jacket paid ten minutes ago. He told me to bring them straight to Chen Mingde's family piping hot. He said he is a regular on Old Street."

Chen Xuan glanced across the road toward the side alley. Beside a parked white van, Number 23 stood with his hands in his pockets, met Chen Xuan's eyes for half a second, gave a tiny nod, and stepped out of sight.

"Do not let Gong's soup get cold," Yu Changshun urged. "Someone on this street owes you a meal, Chen Mingde. Eat."

Chen Mingde chuckled softly. "Bring two extra bowls, Wu Lanying. We will split four bowls six ways. Yu Changshun, you are not leaving without eating."

They gathered around the wooden counter on mismatched stools, chopsticks clinking warmly through the steam.

Five minutes later, steady footsteps stopped outside the doorway.

Everyone looked up.

Standing outside was Acting Director Wei Kun in a neatly pressed dark blue uniform, cap tucked under his left arm. He had come alone, with no police car on the street.

Laughter stopped at once. Chen Mingde's hand froze over his bowl. Wu Lanying's shoulders went stiff, fingers tightening on her chopsticks. Even Yu Changshun set his bowl down, jaw tense from habit.

Wei Kun noticed right away and bowed his head slightly.

"Mr. Chen Mingde, Mrs. Wu Lanying," Wei Kun said, calm and free of pride. "Sorry to interrupt your meal. May I have two minutes?"

Chen Mingde laid his chopsticks across his bowl and stood straight, eyes guarded. "Director Wei Kun. Please come in."

Wei Kun stepped just inside, keeping polite distance. With both hands, he placed a sealed brown envelope beside the account book.

"This is the official shop repair payment voucher approved by the county finance office and signed by the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau," Wei Kun said steadily. "It pays for your broken glass, damaged doorframe, and lost goods. You do not need to visit the police bureau or sign another form. The county bank on Dongfeng Road will cash it directly tomorrow morning."

Wu Lanying stared at the red official stamp, breath catching.

Wei Kun stood straight, met Chen Mingde's eyes squarely, and lowered his head in a deep, slow bow.

"As Acting Director of Old Street Station, I owe your family a full apology," Wei Kun said quietly. "The men who wore this uniform before me failed their duty. They worked with thugs and forced an honest citizen to suffer an injustice that never should have happened. I cannot erase the fear you went through, yet I give you my word tonight: as long as I hold this post, no bully and no crooked officer will bother Old Street again."

Silence filled the shop. Chen Mingde studied Wei Kun's tired, honest face until the tension finally left Chen Mingde's jaw.

"Director Wei Kun," Chen Mingde said softly, "I only sell nails and hinges. All we ever wanted was to open our door at dawn and sleep without fear."

"You will have that," Wei Kun answered firmly. "My direct desk number is inside the folder."

Wu Lanying stood, her face softening as she reached for the kettle. "Director Wei Kun, please stay for a cup of hot tea."

"Thank you kindly, Mrs. Wu Lanying," Wei Kun replied with a brief smile. "I will not let your noodles get cold."

As Wei Kun turned toward the street, his eyes met Chen Xuan's for one brief, unreadable second. Chen Xuan sat quietly holding his chopsticks, looking completely ordinary. Wei Kun showed no sign of knowing him, nodded politely to the family, and stepped into the quiet night.

Only when his footsteps faded did Chen Mingde sit back down and let out a long, shaking breath.

"In thirty years on Old Street," Yu Changshun murmured, staring at the voucher, "I never thought I would see a county police director bow his head inside a hardware shop."

Chen Mingde picked up his chopsticks, eyes bright under the bulb. "Eat up, everyone. Tomorrow morning, Mingde Hardware opens at seven sharp."
