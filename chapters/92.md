# Chapter 92: The First Evening of Peace on Old Street

By nine-forty-five on Monday night, Old Street glowed with warm light for the first time in months. Shop shutters that had stayed locked after dark stood half open again. Easy laughter and friendly voices drifted down the clean lane.

Inside Mingde Hardware, Chen Mingde stood on a low stool lining up boxes of brass hinges. His left shoulder was still stiff, yet the gray worry that had aged his face all week was finally gone.

"Pass me the two-inch bolts, Chen Jianguo," Chen Mingde called down. "Do not mix the coated steel with plain iron."

"I worked in a warehouse for twenty years," Chen Jianguo shot back, lifting the heavy tray up to his older brother. "I know my bolts."

"You knew them twenty years ago," Wu Lanying said from behind the counter, wiping dust off the account book. "Last month you fixed our kitchen tap and left the rubber ring sitting on the table."

Liu Meifang laughed softly beside Chen Xuan as she stacked trays of screwdrivers. "Do not argue with her, Chen Jianguo. She is only teasing you because she finally feels strong again."

Wu Lanying paused with her rag resting on the wood. She looked up at Chen Mingde. "I truly thought we would never raise this shutter again."

Before Chen Mingde could answer, Yu Changshun stepped through the doorway. He carried two rolls of fresh gray-blue cloth under one arm and a rolled strip of heavy canvas over his shoulder.

"Still awake at ten?" Yu Changshun asked, leaning the canvas against the doorframe. "I saw your light from down the block. I brought clean cloth for your counter and thick canvas to replace that torn awning."

Chen Mingde climbed down from the stool, his face lighting up. "Yu Changshun, you did not have to carry all that over tonight. Business has been hard enough for you."

"Save your breath," Yu Changshun said with a wave of his hand, his eyes crinkling warmly. "Those rolls sat untouched all winter because nobody dared walk down Old Street after dark. Tonight, three families bought quilts from me before eight o'clock. Do you know when that last happened?"

Chen Jianguo felt the thick cloth. "How much do we owe you?"

"Mention money and I take it right back," Yu Changshun snapped good-naturedly. "Chen Mingde took the worst of it for this whole street. Half the block saw the provincial inspection car bring him home this afternoon with that stamped paper clearing his name. The dry goods shopkeeper next door actually cried behind his counter."

Chen Mingde's throat tightened for a second. He patted Yu Changshun on the arm. "Sit down. Wu Lanying, pour some hot water."

"No need to fuss," Yu Changshun said, pulling up a stool. "I just wanted to see you behind your own counter again."

A minute later, a young delivery boy from Gong's Noodles jogged up to the door. He carried a red metal basket stacked with four steaming bowls.

"Mingde Hardware?" the boy asked, setting the basket on the counter. "Four bowls of braised beef noodles, extra tendon, and pickled radish. Already paid for."

Wu Lanying blinked, her hand stopping over the teapot. "Young man, you have the wrong shop. We did not order any noodles."

"Right shop," the boy insisted, lifting the lids as rich beef smell filled the room. "A quiet man in a dark jacket paid at the counter ten minutes ago. He told me to bring them straight to Chen Mingde's family while they were piping hot. He said he is a regular on Old Street."

Chen Xuan glanced across the road toward the side alley. Beside a parked white van, Number 23 stood with his hands in his pockets. He met Chen Xuan's eyes for half a second, gave a tiny nod, and stepped out of sight.

"Do not let Gong's soup get cold," Yu Changshun urged. "Someone on this street owes you a meal, Chen Mingde. Eat."

Chen Mingde chuckled softly. "Bring two extra bowls, Wu Lanying. We will split four bowls six ways. Yu Changshun, you are not leaving without eating."

They gathered around the wooden counter on mismatched stools. Chopsticks clinked warmly through the rising steam.

Five minutes later, steady footsteps stopped outside the doorway.

Everyone looked up.

Standing just outside the door was Acting Director Wei Kun in a clean, neatly pressed dark blue uniform. He held his cap tucked under his left arm. He had come completely alone, with no police car parked on the street.

The laughter stopped at once. Chen Mingde's hand froze over his bowl. Wu Lanying's shoulders went stiff, her fingers tightening around her chopsticks. Even Yu Changshun set his bowl down, his jaw tense from old habit.

Wei Kun noticed right away and bowed his head slightly before taking a step.

"Mr. Chen Mingde, Mrs. Wu Lanying," Wei Kun said, his voice calm and free of pride. "I am sorry to interrupt your meal. May I have two minutes?"

Chen Mingde laid his chopsticks across his bowl and stood up straight, his eyes guarded. "Director Wei. Please come in."

Wei Kun stepped just inside, keeping a polite distance from the counter. With both hands, he placed a sealed brown envelope beside the account book.

"This is the official shop repair payment voucher approved by the county finance office and signed by the Provincial Joint Inspection Group," Wei Kun said steadily. "It pays for your broken glass, damaged doorframe, and lost goods. You do not need to visit the police bureau or sign another form. The county bank on Dongfeng Road will cash it directly tomorrow morning."

Wu Lanying stared at the red official stamp, her breath catching.

Wei Kun stood straight, met Chen Mingde's eyes squarely, and lowered his head in a deep, slow bow.

"As Acting Director of the Yunxi County Public Security Bureau, I owe your family a full apology," Wei Kun said quietly. "The men who wore this uniform before me failed their duty. They worked with thugs and forced an honest citizen to suffer an injustice that never should have happened. I cannot erase the fear you went through, yet I give you my word tonight: as long as I hold this post, no bully and no crooked officer will bother Old Street again."

Silence filled the small shop. Chen Mingde studied Wei Kun's tired, honest face until the tension finally left Chen Mingde's jaw.

"Director Wei," Chen Mingde said softly, "I only sell nails and hinges. All we ever wanted was to open our door at dawn and sleep without fear."

"You will have that," Wei Kun answered firmly. "My direct desk phone number is inside the folder."

Wu Lanying stood up, her face softening as she reached for the kettle. "Director Wei, please stay for a cup of hot tea."

"Thank you kindly, Mrs. Wu Lanying," Wei Kun replied with a brief, warm smile. "I will not let your noodles get cold."

As Wei Kun turned toward the street, his eyes met Chen Xuan's for one brief, unreadable second. Chen Xuan sat quietly on his stool holding his chopsticks, looking completely ordinary. Wei Kun showed no sign of knowing him. He nodded politely to the family and stepped back out into the quiet night.

Only when his footsteps faded did Chen Mingde sit back down and let out a long, shaking breath.

"In thirty years on Old Street," Yu Changshun murmured, staring at the voucher, "I never thought I would see a county police director bow his head inside a hardware shop."

Chen Mingde picked up his chopsticks, his eyes bright under the ceiling bulb. "Eat up, everyone. Tomorrow morning, Mingde Hardware opens at seven sharp."
