# Chapter 53: The Handoff Protocol

The Main Security Hall went silent the second the first officials walked in at five fifty on Monday morning.

At the head table sat Premier Gu Chengshan, General Qin Zhengguo, Central Commission for Discipline Inspection Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi, and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua. None of the four looked pleased or proud. Between Qin Zhengguo and Zhou Zhenhua sat an overflowing glass ashtray, thin smoke still rising from freshly crushed cigarettes.

Yet nobody looked at the head table for more than a second. Every eye locked onto the steel tables in the center of the floor.

Forty-two flat-black locked cases sat in two neat rows. Each case was sealed with plain metal latches and carried a faint white case number on the lid.

Three steps behind the center table stood a single figure.

Dressed in plain black gear from neck to boot, with no rank badge, shoulder patch, or ID number, the figure wore a smooth, seamless black mask covering the entire head. It stood completely still, hands resting loosely at its sides. It showed no nerves and no special respect toward the ministers and generals walking in.

"Take your seats," Premier Gu Chengshan said quietly. "Every department will stay seated until the handoff is finished."

Chairs creaked as sixty senior officials sat down. Near the front of the Public Security row, Deputy Director of Public Security Kang Deming stayed half-standing, his broad face turning blotchy red. His eyes jumped over the forty-two cases, then snapped toward the empty chairs where Director Dai Chunsheng and two bureau chiefs belonged.

"Premier Gu," Kang Deming said loudly, his voice shaking between anger and plain panic. "Before anyone signs a single paper, the Ministry of Public Security needs an answer."

Across the aisle, Vice Minister Shen Weiguo grabbed Kang Deming's sleeve and hissed, "Sit down, Kang Deming."

Kang Deming yanked his arm free and stepped toward the steel table, pointing a thick, shaking finger at the masked figure.

"Three senior officers from my ministry disappeared between midnight and four o'clock!" Kang Deming barked, his voice bouncing off the paneled walls. "No arrest warrants went through Public Security. No holding orders crossed the Supreme Procuratorate. No notice reached our night desk! Who is this masked unit? Under what law do you claim the power to snatch sitting vice-ministerial officials in the middle of the night without a single warrant?"

Several rattled officials in the court and economic rows leaned forward. Deputy Finance Minister Pei Rongsheng gripped the edge of his desk until his nails turned pale. Supreme People's Court Vice President Han Qingshan watched with narrowed eyes, waiting to see if Kang Deming's shouting forced an answer.

General Qin Zhengguo did not raise his voice or blink. His eyes stayed fixed on the masked figure, his jaw set hard. Only the four leaders at the head table knew that even White Guard's Number 1 had been ordered to stand down and stay under watch the second Black Guard stepped in.

Beside the forty-two black cases, the smooth black mask turned a tiny fraction of an inch toward Kang Deming.

No breath fogged the mask. No shoulders moved.

When the voice spoke, a built-in voice changer stripped away every hint of age, warmth, or gender. Flat, cold, and sharp as ice, it did not get loud at all, yet it cut across the hall like steel scraping glass.

"Black Guard authority is outside your law books. The proof is checked and complete. The suspects are ready for legal handoff. Sign to accept."

The hall went dead still.

Kang Deming's mouth opened, then snapped shut with a sharp click of his teeth. Every drop of color drained from his cheeks in two seconds, leaving his face as white as wet chalk. He stumbled half a step back, his raised finger dropping slowly to his side as his chest heaved in silent shock.

"Black Guard," Supreme People's Court Vice President Han Qingshan breathed so softly only the man beside him heard. His fingers went ice-cold. Even among thirty-year veterans in Beijing, those two words belonged to whispered founding-era stories nobody dared ask about in daylight.

At the head table, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua let out a tight breath and pressed both palms flat on the table to steady them. Across the military row, Lieutenant General Tang Jiandong sat stiff as iron. At last he understood why three air-defense zones had gone dark at two in the morning without a single entry in the logbook.

Premier Gu Chengshan stood up slowly.

He did not scold Kang Deming or give a speech to calm the shaken room. In complete silence, the Premier walked down from the head table, crossed the floor, and stopped right in front of the center steel table.

A flat black electronic sign-in pad lay beside Case One.

Premier Gu Chengshan picked up the pen, pressed his thumb to the glass reader, and checked the total count scrolling across the dark screen. Forty-two sealed case files. One hundred and nineteen arrested suspects held across three secure sites under full legal rules. Zero bystanders hurt. Zero leaks.

Without asking a single question, Premier Gu Chengshan signed his full name across the screen and set the pen down.

"Handoff accepted," Premier Gu Chengshan said clearly. "The state takes full legal custody."

The Black Guard member did not nod. The guard spoke one last time.

"Handoff complete. Custody recorded. Black Guard leaving."

The masked figure turned cleanly, walked past the silent rows of generals and ministers without making a sound, and stepped out through the side security door. The heavy lock clicked shut with a soft thud.

For ten full seconds, nobody moved.

Then Kang Deming dropped back into his chair so hard the frame groaned. He wiped cold sweat from his forehead with a shaking hand, unable to look at anyone around him.

Premier Gu Chengshan turned to face the forty-plus departments, his eyes sweeping across every pale face in the room.

"Now you know why you were called in at five o'clock," Premier Gu Chengshan said, his voice hard as iron. "These forty-two cases hold complete money records, secret messages, sworn witness statements, and physical proof across seventeen provinces and twelve national ministries. Every file is ready for immediate court action."

General Qin Zhengguo stood beside the head table, lighting a cigarette with steady hands. "Ten military offices will stay in the east hall after this briefing," Qin Zhengguo said coldly. "Anyone whose subordinates show up in Cases One through Eleven will turn in their command seals within the hour."

Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi stood up next, holding a thick red binder. "Public Security, State Security, and the Supreme Procuratorate will form joint teams right now. Case Forty-Two lists a provincial and county protection ring in Southern Province, centered on Yunxi County. Notice that the local suspects there were left untouched tonight. Lawful local task forces will sweep them up cleanly under provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism laws."

Premier Gu Chengshan looked straight at Vice Minister Shen Weiguo. "Send a clean provincial strike team before sunrise ends. Every arrest down to the county level must follow strict legal steps, with zero cover-ups and zero trouble for ordinary citizens."

"Understood, Premier," Shen Weiguo answered at once, jumping to his feet.

As sixty senior officials walked toward the steel tables to claim their files, not one of them knew what small spark in a quiet county had brought the roof down on half of Beijing. They only knew that by sunrise, the whole country would feel the shake.
