# Chapter 53: The Handoff Protocol

The Main Security Hall fell silent the instant the first officials crossed the threshold at five fifty on Monday morning.

At the head table sat Premier Gu Chengshan, General Qin Zhengguo, Central Commission for Discipline Inspection Secretary Gao Zhengyi, and Minister of State Security Zhou Zhenhua. None of the four wore an expression of triumph. Between Qin Zhengguo and Zhou Zhenhua sat an overflowing glass ashtray, thin smoke still curling from freshly crushed cigarettes.

Yet nobody looked at the podium for more than a second. Every gaze locked onto the steel tables across the center floor.

Forty-two matte-black biometric cases rested in two immaculate rows, sealed with unnumbered metallic clasps and bearing faint white dossier indexes on their lids.

Three paces behind the center table stood a single figure.

Clad in featureless matte-black tactical gear from collar to boot, with no rank tab, shoulder patch, or serial number, the figure wore a smooth, seamless matte-black mask covering the entire head. It stood utterly motionless, hands resting loosely at its sides, showing neither tension nor deference toward the ministers and generals filing in.

"Take your seats," Premier Gu Chengshan said quietly. "Every agency will remain seated until the handoff protocol is complete."

Chairs creaked as sixty senior officials settled into place. Near the front of the Public Security delegation, Deputy Director of Public Security Kang Deming stayed half-risen, his broad face flushed mottled red. His eyes darted over the forty-two cases, then snapped toward the empty chairs where Director Ma Zhenyu and two bureau chiefs belonged.

"Premier Gu," Kang Deming said loudly, his voice wavering between outrage and raw panic. "Before anyone signs anything, the Ministry of Public Security requires an explanation."

Across the aisle, Vice Minister Shen Weiguo grabbed Kang Deming's sleeve and hissed, "Sit down, Kang Deming."

Kang Deming jerked free and stepped toward the steel table, pointing a thick, trembling finger at the masked figure.

"Three senior officers from my ministry vanished between midnight and four o'clock!" Kang Deming barked, his voice echoing off the paneled walls. "No arrest warrants were logged through Public Security. No detention orders crossed the Supreme Procuratorate. No notice reached our duty room! Who is this masked unit? Under what statutory registry do you claim authority to seize sitting vice-ministerial officials in the night without a single warrant?"

Several rattled officials in the judicial and economic rows leaned forward slightly. Deputy Finance Minister Pei Rongsheng gripped his desk edge until his nails turned pale. Supreme People's Court Vice President Han Qingshan watched with narrowed eyes, waiting to see if Kang Deming's bluster forced an answer.

General Qin Zhengguo did not raise his voice or blink. His eyes remained fixed on the masked figure, his jaw set hard. Only the four leaders at the head table knew that even Black Pine Guard's Number One had been ordered to stand down and submit to monitoring the instant Zero Guard activated.

Beside the forty-two biometric cases, the featureless matte-black mask turned a fraction of an inch toward Kang Deming.

No breath misted the surface. No shoulders shifted.

When the voice spoke, an acoustic baffle stripped away every trace of age, warmth, or gender. Flat, neutral, and chilled to the bone, it did not rise a single decibel, yet it cut through the hall like steel across glass.

"Zero Guard authority is outside your registry. Evidence is verified. Suspects are staged for legal transfer. Acknowledge receipt."

The hall went dead still.

Kang Deming's mouth opened, then snapped shut with a sharp click of teeth. Every drop of color drained from his cheeks in two seconds, leaving his face like wet chalk. He stumbled half a step backward, his raised finger dropping slowly to his side as his chest heaved in silent shock.

"Zero Guard," Supreme People's Court Vice President Han Qingshan breathed so faintly only his neighbor heard. His fingers went cold. Even among thirty-year veterans in the capital, those two words belonged to whispered founding-era myths nobody dared verify in daylight.

At the head table, Minister of State Security Zhou Zhenhua exhaled tightly and pressed his palms flat against the tabletop to steady them. Across the military row, Lieutenant General Tang Jiandong sat rigid as iron, finally understanding why three air-defense corridors had gone dark at two in the morning without a single log entry.

Premier Gu Chengshan stood up slowly.

He did not scold Kang Deming or offer a speech to soothe the trembling room. In dead silence, the Premier walked down from the podium, crossed the floor, and stopped directly before the center steel table.

The matte-black biometric transfer slate lay beside Case One.

Premier Gu Chengshan lifted the stylus, pressed his thumb to the verification pad, and checked the master checksum scrolling across the dark glass. Forty-two sealed dossiers. One hundred and nineteen detained principals staged across three secure holding facilities under legal custody protocols. Zero collateral casualties. Zero leaks.

Without asking a single question, Premier Gu Chengshan signed his full name across the transfer slate and set the stylus down.

"Receipt acknowledged," Premier Gu Chengshan said clearly. "The state assumes full legal custody."

The Zero Guard representative did not nod. The flat, genderless chilled voice spoke once more.

"Transfer complete. Custody logged. Zero Guard exiting."

The masked figure pivoted cleanly, walked past the silent rows of generals and ministers without a sound, and exited through the side security door. The magnetic lock engaged with a soft thud.

For ten full seconds, nobody moved.

Then Kang Deming collapsed back into his chair so heavily the frame groaned, wiping cold sweat from his forehead with a shaking hand.

Premier Gu Chengshan turned to face the forty-plus departments, his gaze sweeping every pale face.

"Now you know why you were summoned at five o'clock," Premier Gu Chengshan said, his voice iron-hard. "The forty-two biometric cases hold complete financial ledgers, encrypted communications, sworn witness chains, and physical evidence across seventeen provinces and twelve national ministries. Every case is verified for immediate judicial processing."

General Qin Zhengguo stood beside the podium, lighting a cigarette with steady hands. "Ten military directorates will remain in the east annex after this briefing," Qin Zhengguo said coldly. "Anyone whose subordinates appear in Cases One through Eleven will surrender their command seals within the hour."

Secretary Gao Zhengyi rose next, holding up a thick red binder. "Public Security, State Security, and the Supreme Procuratorate will form joint reception teams immediately. Case Forty-Two lists a provincial-and-county protection umbrella in Southern Province, specifically Yunxi County. Notice that local suspects there were left untouched tonight so lawful local task forces can sweep them cleanly under provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism mandates."

Premier Gu Chengshan looked straight at Vice Minister Shen Weiguo. "Dispatch a clean provincial strike team before sunrise finishes. Every arrest down to the county level must follow strict legal procedure, with zero cover-ups and zero blowback on ordinary citizens."

"Understood, Premier," Shen Weiguo answered instantly, springing to his feet.

As sixty senior officials moved toward the steel tables to claim their cases, none knew what small spark in a quiet county had brought the roof down on half the capital. They only knew that by sunrise, the whole country would feel the tremor.
