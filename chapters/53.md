# Chapter 53: Three Containers

By nine o'clock on Tuesday morning, the twelfth floor of the Dongfang Building felt like a ship taking on water.

The dispatch printer spat out pages stamped with thick red borders. Phones rang across three rows of cubicles without pausing, and Pan Xiulan hurried down the aisle hugging customs folders to her chest.

Inside the glass-walled office at the back, General Manager Sun Haoran stood behind his desk with both palms braced against the blotter. His face had gone the flat, drained gray of wet ash.

"Check the portal again," Sun Haoran said, his voice hoarse. "Call Li Weidong at the port brokerage. Tell him there has to be a mistake. Those three containers cleared the automated green channel yesterday afternoon."

"I called him twice, General Manager Sun," Pan Xiulan said softly, laying the printed hold notices before him. "It was not an automated flag. Someone inside Qingyan Port Customs entered a manual extended inspection hold last night. The inspection slot is not scheduled until Friday at two."

Sun Haoran sank into his chair as if someone had cut the tendons behind his knees.

"Friday at two," he whispered. "The *Eastern Crane* closes its gangway at ten o'clock Friday morning. If those three containers sit on the dock past ten, the Ninghai factory triggers the two-million-yuan penalty immediately. Our cash reserve is barely one point six million. That penalty buries us."

The glass door swung open, and Manager Hu Dewei rushed inside, dabbing his forehead with a crumpled tissue. Dark half-moons of sweat had soaked straight through his pale blue shirt.

"General Manager Sun, my cousin at the Harbor Road freight guild just called," Hu Dewei panted. "This is not a random inspection. Word is out on the docks. Shenghua Maritime's Zhou Mingcheng is behind it."

Sun Haoran's head snapped up. "Zhou Mingcheng? Why would Shenghua Maritime crush us?"

"Because of yesterday's lunch!" Hu Dewei cried, waving his tissue toward the main floor. "Miss Qin Yuhan ignored Zhou Mingcheng and spent the whole meal talking to Chen Xuan! Zhou Mingcheng ran to his father, Zhou Guodong, and now Berth Seven is locked down on us! We cannot fight Shenghua Maritime. They own half the cranes at Qingyan Port!"

* * *

Ten seconds later, Manager Hu Dewei marched across the main floor to the quiet desk in the back corner by the window.

Every keyboard on the twelfth floor stopped clicking. Twenty pairs of eyes turned toward Chen Xuan, who sat calmly in his neat gray shirt entering delivery receipts. A green insulated thermos rested beside his mouse pad, and a small white porcelain cup sent up a faint curl of steam.

"Chen Xuan," Hu Dewei said, gripping the metal partition with sweating palms. His voice cracked between panic and pleading. "Drop everything right now. Go downstairs, buy a fruit basket and high-grade tea, and take a taxi straight to Shenghua Maritime's tower."

Chen Xuan looked up mildly. "Manager Hu, is there a client delivery there?"

"Are you pretending not to hear?" Hu Dewei wiped his forehead with his wrist. "Three of our containers at Berth Seven were just frozen by customs! If they miss Friday's cutoff, Hengxin Logistics owes two million yuan! The company dies on Friday morning, and everyone on this floor loses their rice bowl!"

An anxious murmur rippled through the cubicles. Several junior clerks stared silently at their monitors.

"You offended Zhou Mingcheng yesterday," Hu Dewei hissed, leaning closer. "I am begging you, Chen Xuan. Do not be stubborn today. Go to Shenghua Maritime, bow your head, apologize to Zhou Mingcheng until his anger cools, and ask him to lift the Berth Seven hold. What is a little pride compared to twenty families' livelihoods?"

Before Chen Xuan could speak, a chair scraped loudly across the floor two rows over.

Zhang Pengfei stood up and walked straight into the aisle between Hu Dewei and Chen Xuan's desk. A month ago, Zhang Pengfei would have been the first to pile onto Chen Xuan. Today, his face was steady and resolute.

"Manager Hu, stop talking like that," Zhang Pengfei said clearly, his voice carrying across the silent office. "That is not fair, and you know it."

Hu Dewei blinked, stunned. "Zhang Pengfei, stay out of this!"

"I am not staying out of it," Zhang Pengfei replied, squaring his shoulders. "I helped prepare the Haiyue Hotel Group bid with Chen Xuan. Our pricing was honest, our schedule was clean, and Miss Qin Yuhan chose us on merit. Chen Xuan did not provoke Zhou Mingcheng. Zhou Mingcheng was the one bullying a smaller firm."

Zhang Pengfei glanced briefly at Chen Xuan, quiet gratitude in his eyes, then turned back to Hu Dewei.

"Even if Chen Xuan knelt in Shenghua Maritime's lobby right now, Zhou Mingcheng would not lift that hold," Zhang Pengfei said firmly. "Men like Zhou Mingcheng do not stop when you beg. They step harder."

Hu Dewei's mouth opened and closed without a sound. At the doorway of his glass office, General Manager Sun Haoran watched with a heavy frown, saying nothing.

* * *

Chen Xuan lifted his porcelain cup and took a slow, unhurried sip of warm Cloud Peak tea.

"Thank you, Zhang Pengfei," Chen Xuan said softly, setting the cup back on its coaster. "Sit down. No need to raise your voice."

Zhang Pengfei gave a short nod and returned to his desk.

Chen Xuan looked up at Manager Hu Dewei with the same calm, polite expression he wore every Tuesday. "Manager Hu, apologizing to Zhou Mingcheng will not move those containers. He wants Hengxin Logistics out of Qingyan Port."

"Then what do we do?" Hu Dewei groaned, slumping against the partition. "Wait for Friday and watch the bailiffs lock the elevator?"

"General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan called evenly. "May I see the printed customs hold notice from Berth Seven?"

Sun Haoran walked over and placed the three red-bordered sheets on Chen Xuan's desk. "Here. Our broker says there is no appeal window before Friday afternoon."

Chen Xuan read the top page calmly from header to footer, like a man checking a grocery receipt. Near the bottom right corner, his eyes stopped on a small alphanumeric stamp.

*Hold Authority: Manual Override, Code 704-B. Approving Officer: Wang Zhiming, Deputy Director of Inspection. Staging Lane: Berth Seven, Bonded Annex East.*

The second and third pages bore the exact same manual Code 704-B, entered at 7:42 PM the previous evening, long after the automated customs engine had cleared the cargo.

More telling still was the staging lane. Code 704-B was an obsolete physical-reweigh directive used almost exclusively to shunt boxes into Bonded Annex East, the quiet side apron of Berth Seven where Shenghua Maritime routed its own private manifests out of regular inspectors' sight.

To hide his favor for Zhou Guodong, Wang Zhiming had carelessly stamped Hengxin Logistics' three clean boxes with the exact code tied to his own dirty lane.

Chen Xuan aligned the three papers neatly with two fingers and set them down.

"General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Do not cancel the vessel booking on the *Eastern Crane*, and do not pay early cancellation fees to the Ninghai factory."

Sun Haoran stared at him. "Chen Xuan, the cutoff is Friday at ten."

"Keep the booking active," Chen Xuan replied, his voice mild and steady as still water. "Manual holds with improper codes rarely survive a proper review. We still have three days."

Something in the utter stillness of Chen Xuan's eyes made the panic catch in Hu Dewei's throat. Without another word, Chen Xuan took another quiet sip of Cloud Peak tea and returned to his Tuesday morning spreadsheet.
