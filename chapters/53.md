# Chapter 53: The Handoff Protocol

The Main Security Hall went silent the second the first officials walked in at five fifty on Monday morning.

At the head table sat Premier Gu Chengshan, General Qin Zhengguo, Central Commission for Discipline Inspection Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi, and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua. None of the four looked pleased. Between Qin Zhengguo and Zhou Zhenhua sat an overflowing glass ashtray, thin smoke rising from crushed cigarettes.

Yet every eye locked onto the steel tables in the center of the floor.

Forty-two flat-black locked cases sat in two neat rows, each sealed with plain metal latches and a faint white case number on the lid.

Three steps behind the center table stood a single figure in plain black gear from neck to boot, with no rank badge, shoulder patch, or ID number, and a smooth black mask covering the entire head. It stood completely still, hands loose at its sides, showing neither nerves nor deference toward the ministers and generals filing in.

"Take your seats," Premier Gu Chengshan said quietly. "Every department stays seated until the handoff is finished."

Chairs creaked as sixty senior officials sat. Near the front of the Public Security row, Deputy Director of Public Security Kang Deming stayed half-standing, his broad face turning blotchy red. His eyes jumped from the forty-two cases to the empty chairs where Director Dai Chunsheng and two bureau chiefs belonged.

"Premier Gu," Kang Deming said loudly, his voice shaking between anger and panic. "Before anyone signs a single paper, the Ministry of Public Security needs an answer."

Across the aisle, Vice Minister Shen Weiguo grabbed Kang Deming's sleeve and hissed, "Sit down, Kang Deming."

Kang Deming yanked his arm free and stepped toward the steel table, pointing a thick, shaking finger at the masked figure.

"Three senior officers from my ministry disappeared between midnight and four o'clock!" Kang Deming barked. "No arrest warrants went through Public Security. No holding orders crossed the Supreme Procuratorate. No notice reached our night desk! Who is this masked unit? Under what law do you snatch sitting vice-ministerial officials in the middle of the night without a single warrant?"

Several rattled officials leaned forward. Deputy Finance Minister Pei Rongsheng gripped his desk until his nails turned pale. Supreme People's Court Vice President Han Qingshan watched with narrowed eyes, waiting to see if Kang Deming forced an answer.

General Qin Zhengguo did not blink, his jaw set hard. Only the four leaders at the head table knew that even White Guard's Number 1 had been ordered to stand down and stay under watch the second Black Guard stepped in.

Beside the forty-two black cases, the smooth black mask turned a fraction of an inch toward Kang Deming.

A built-in voice changer stripped every trace of age or warmth from the words. Flat and sharp as steel scraping glass, it did not rise in volume, yet cut cleanly across the hall.

"Black Guard authority is outside your law books. The proof is checked and complete. The suspects are ready for legal handoff. Sign to accept."

The hall went dead still.

Kang Deming's mouth opened, then snapped shut with a click of his teeth. Every drop of color drained from his cheeks, leaving his face as white as wet chalk. He stumbled half a step back, his raised finger dropping to his side as his chest heaved in silent shock.

"Black Guard," Han Qingshan breathed so softly only the man beside him heard. His fingers went ice-cold. Even among thirty-year veterans in Beijing, those two words belonged to whispered founding-era stories nobody dared mention in daylight.

At the head table, Zhou Zhenhua let out a tight breath and pressed both palms flat on the table. Across the military row, Lieutenant General Tang Jiandong sat stiff as iron. At last he understood why three air-defense zones had gone dark at two in the morning without a single logbook entry.

Premier Gu Chengshan stood up slowly.

He did not scold Kang Deming or give a speech to calm the room. In complete silence, he walked down from the head table and stopped in front of the center steel table, where a flat black electronic sign-in pad lay beside Case One.

Premier Gu Chengshan checked the scrolling count on the glass reader: forty-two sealed case files, three hundred and eighteen arrested suspects held across three secure sites under full legal rules, zero bystanders hurt, zero leaks.

Without a single question, he beckoned Vice Minister Shen Weiguo, who pressed his thumb to the reader, signed the custody receipt across the screen, and set the pen down.

"Handoff accepted," Premier Gu Chengshan said clearly. "The state takes full legal custody."

The Black Guard member did not nod. "Handoff complete. Custody recorded. Black Guard leaving."

The masked figure turned cleanly, walked past the silent rows of generals and ministers without a sound, and stepped out through the side security door. The heavy lock clicked shut.

For a long moment, nobody moved.

Then Kang Deming dropped back into his chair so hard the frame groaned. He wiped cold sweat from his forehead with a shaking hand, unable to meet anyone's eyes.

Premier Gu Chengshan turned to face the forty-plus departments.

"Now you know why you were called in at five o'clock," Premier Gu Chengshan said, his voice firm. "These forty-two cases hold complete money records, secret messages, sworn witness statements, and physical proof across fourteen provinces and forty national ministries. Every file is ready for immediate court action."

General Qin Zhengguo stood beside the head table, lighting a cigarette with steady hands. "Ten military offices stay in the east hall after this briefing," Qin Zhengguo said coldly. "Anyone whose subordinates appear in Cases One through Eleven turns in their command seals within the hour."

Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi stood up next with a thick red binder. "Public Security, State Security, and the Supreme Procuratorate form joint teams right now. Case Forty-Two lists a provincial and county protection ring in Handong Province, centered on Yunxi County. Notice the local suspects there were left untouched tonight. Lawful local task forces will sweep them up cleanly under provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism laws."

Premier Gu Chengshan looked straight at Vice Minister Shen Weiguo. "Send a clean provincial strike team before sunrise ends. Every arrest down to the county level must follow strict legal steps, with zero cover-ups and zero trouble for ordinary citizens."

"Understood, Premier," Shen Weiguo answered, jumping to his feet.

As sixty senior officials walked toward the steel tables to claim their files, not one knew what small spark in a quiet county had brought the roof down on half of Beijing. They only knew that by sunrise, the whole country would feel the shake.
