# Chapter 164: Leaving on Time

Chaos erupted at the entrance of Pier Nine. Shen Jingzhou's four burly security guards scrambled like frightened rabbits, fumbling with heavy key rings as they sprinted toward the chained steel gates. Two drivers jumped into the blocking black SUVs, revving the engines hard and reversing out of the customs lane with squealing tires.

Click. Click.

Both brass padlocks snapped open. The heavy iron chains rattled loudly as they hit the concrete.

At 5:00 PM sharp, the massive steel gates of Pier Nine swung wide open with a deep metallic groan, clearing the full width of the customs lane without a single second to spare.

Almost immediately, a neat convoy of three Shenzhen Port Authority SUVs and two white Shenzhen Customs inspection vans glided smoothly down the access road and pulled up right beside the open gate. There was zero theatrics and zero wasted motion, only crisp, unmistakable authority.

A silver-haired senior director in a dark customs uniform stepped out of the lead van, holding an electronic clearance tablet. Shen Jingzhou hurried forward, wiping sweat from his pale face with a trembling hand, trying to force a polite greeting. "Director Fang, thank goodness you are here! That chain was just a minor shift misunderstanding."

Director Fang walked straight past Shen Jingzhou as if he were thin air, not sparing him a single glance. He stopped neatly in front of Qin Yuhan and offered a courteous, professional nod.

"President Qin of Haiyue Group?" Director Fang asked politely.

"Yes, I am Qin Yuhan," she replied, keeping her voice composed despite the whirlwind of the last ten minutes.

"I am Director Fang from Shenzhen Customs," he said, turning his tablet slightly so she could see the green verification stamps across the screen. "Your 40 containers have passed expedited priority clearance with zero discrepancies. The maritime departure window for the Haiyue Star has been confirmed, and the port authority has formally logged today's unlawful obstruction by the terminal operator. Haiyue Group bears zero delay liability and zero penalty."

Lin Jian let out a shaky breath that sounded half like a laugh and half like a sob of pure relief. "Thank you, Director Fang! Thank you so much!"

"Just doing our job properly, Manager Lin," Director Fang said calmly. He signaled to the port dispatchers behind him. Within seconds, the amber warning lights atop the towering gantry cranes flickered to life. Deep mechanical hums filled the crisp late November air as heavy container trucks rumbled smoothly through the wide-open gates toward Berth Three.

Not a single crate of Haiyue Group's cargo had a scratch on it.

Director Fang gave Qin Yuhan one last polite nod, glanced briefly and respectfully at the quiet man standing beside her as nothing more than President Qin's husband, and walked back toward the inspection vans to oversee the loading line.

Behind them, Shen Jingzhou stood rooted to the concrete, his face gray with humiliation. His proud charcoal suit looked wrinkled and hollow now. Slowly, under the watchful eyes of his own stunned lawyers, he walked over to Qin Yuhan and bowed stiffly at the waist.

"President Qin," Shen Jingzhou said, his voice hoarse and stripped of every trace of arrogance. "Today was my reckless mistake. The Shen Family will cover all terminal handling fees for the Haiyue Star, and I give you my word we will never interfere with Haiyue Group's southern shipping route again. Please accept my sincere apology."

Qin Yuhan looked down at him calmly. She had spent years fighting tough battles in boardrooms from Shanghai to Shenzhen, yet she had never seen a proud local heir fold so completely in less than ten minutes.

"Haiyue Group does not want favors or free handling fees, Mr. Shen," Qin Yuhan answered evenly. "We only expect the rules to be respected. Pay what is fair, keep the lane open, and we have nothing further to discuss."

"Yes, President Qin. Of course, President Qin," Shen Jingzhou stammered, bowing once more before retreating stiffly toward his sedans.

Lin Jian hurried up to Qin Yuhan, beaming from ear to ear. "President Qin, the cranes are already loading container one! The Haiyue Star will easily catch the evening tide! I will stay here and supervise until the last box is strapped down."

"Thank you, Old Lin," Qin Yuhan smiled warmly. "Make sure the crew gets a proper hot dinner tonight on the company account."

As Lin Jian jogged happily toward Berth Three, Qin Yuhan finally exhaled a long breath. She turned to her husband, her bright eyes still shining with stunned disbelief.

Chen Xuan glanced down at his simple stainless-steel wristwatch. It read 5:06 PM.

"Well," Chen Xuan said with a lazy, satisfied smile as he tapped the face of his watch. "Back in Shanghai, I clock out at 4:55 PM sharp. That means I am already eleven minutes into unpaid overtime today."

Qin Yuhan blinked at him, then let out a bright, ringing laugh that scattered the last trace of tension from the harbor air. "Unpaid overtime? All you did was stand here drinking jasmine tea and holding my handbag!"

"Holding a designer handbag in a sea breeze is skilled labor," Chen Xuan protested solemnly, shifting the beige leather strap higher on his wrist. "So, President Qin, are we done working for the day? Because if we leave right now, we can beat the evening traffic to MixC Mall and get that Cantonese roast goose while the skin is still crisp."

Qin Yuhan smiled softly, stepping close to slip her arm through his. The late autumn sun dipped behind the distant hills across the bay, painting the sky in warm streaks of orange and violet. Behind them, Pier Nine roared back to life with the steady rhythm of working cranes and rolling trucks.

"Lead the way, Mr. Chen," she murmured, resting her head briefly against his shoulder as they walked toward their waiting car. "You earned your dinner tonight."
