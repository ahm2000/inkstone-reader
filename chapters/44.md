# Chapter 44: The Garrison Logistics Officer

At three in the morning on Monday, the electronic lock on the second basement computer room inside Southern Theater Command's supply wing clicked open under Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's palm. He slipped inside, shut the steel door quietly behind him, and pressed his damp forehead against the metal, listening to the steady hum of cooling fans.

Sweat soaked his green uniform shirt. He had not buttoned his collar or grabbed his cap after the duty screen flashed a base-wide blackout ten minutes earlier. In his right hand, Ma Zhenyu gripped a heavy magnetic wipe tool. In his left, he held his security card and a metal wipe drive.

"Just wipe the supply records," Ma Zhenyu muttered, lips dry and cracked as he hurried between the humming racks. "Clear the old backups back to two thousand seventeen, burn the entry logs, and walk upstairs before morning roll call. Nobody checks the archive drives until the next quarter."

He reached Cabinet Four, where the main supply and troop records sat behind dark glass. His fingers shook so badly the card rattled against the reader twice before the green light blinked. He pulled the glass open, plugged the wipe drive into the side port, and reached for the wipe switch.

The screen did not show a menu.

Instead, the ceiling lights shifted from dull yellow to flat, harsh white.

Ma Zhenyu's hand froze on the switch. A cold chill raced up his neck and locked his shoulders. Slowly, stiffly, he turned to look down the narrow aisle between the black computer racks.

Four motionless figures stood inside the room.

Two stood beside the back exit. Two more stood right between Ma Zhenyu and the steel door he had just walked through. They had not tripped a sensor or opened a lock. They had been waiting in the dark before he even swiped his card.

All four wore plain black gear with smooth, dark visors. No unit patches, no armbands, and no rank stars showed anywhere. They stood so still that the humming fans suddenly sounded loud.

Ma Zhenyu's breath caught. He backed up until his spine hit Cabinet Four. Years of army training fought against the fear filling his gut, forcing his chin up even as his knees shook.

"Who are you?" Ma Zhenyu barked, his voice tight and too loud. "Name yourselves right now! I am Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu, Deputy Director of Supply for Southern Theater Command! This is a top-secret military base! How did you pass the gate without a duty officer? Did General Qin Zhengguo order a surprise check?"

None of the four guards moved an inch.

The figure nearest the door tilted its helmet slightly. Its voice came out flat, cold, and completely genderless, stripping every bit of power from Ma Zhenyu's uniform.

"Southern Theater Command Supply Wing is under Zero lockdown. General Qin Zhengguo holds no authority inside this room."

Ma Zhenyu's eyes went wide. His chest tightened like an iron band. Every senior officer knew the whispered rumors of the unnumbered units above all regular bureaus. Those units answered to no base commander and filed no military court papers before they moved.

"You have the wrong officer," Ma Zhenyu stammered, pulling his hand away from the wipe drive as if the metal had turned burning hot. "I came down here because the main phone line went dead! I was running a quick check on the fuel records! Check my duty list!"

"Ma Zhenyu," the flat, cold, genderless voice read evenly. "First stolen border-troop supply list: October fourteenth, nine years ago. Thirty-seven more transfers sent through Qingyan Port and the Shanghai relay. Total money sent to Zurich Northern Ledger account CH-41-ZRH-2290 under your cousin, Ma Deliang: 41.6 million yuan."

Silence crashed down over the computer room.

Ma Zhenyu's fingers went numb. His security card slipped from his left hand and clattered onto the floor tile. A second later, the heavy wipe tool dropped from his right hand with a dull bang and rolled against his polished shoe.

He did not bend to pick either up.

Hard shakes seized his shoulders and ran down his legs. His mouth opened and closed before a ragged, choking sound tore from his throat. His army posture collapsed all at once. He slid down Cabinet Four until he was half-crouched on the floor, hands pressed flat against his shaking thighs.

"Nine years," Ma Zhenyu whispered, tears spilling down his grey cheeks. "It was nine years ago. I never meant to betray the army. I swear, I never wanted to touch a single secret file!"

He stared up wildly at the blank black visor. Words poured out in a raw, terrified flood as twenty-five years of silence broke apart.

"It started in Macau!" Ma Zhenyu gasped, clutching his uniform shirt. "My brother-in-law dragged me into a VIP gambling room on leave! I lost 600k yuan in one night, borrowed from the house to win it back, and by the third morning I owed 4.2 million yuan! The casino manager smiled and put a photo of me in my colonel's uniform right beside the debt slip!"

His throat clicked as his voice cracked into a desperate plea. "Two men met me in a private tea room in Zhuhai the next week. They paid off the 4 million yuan debt that afternoon. They told me all they wanted was a simple fuel report for the southern border depots. Just how much diesel we used and when trucks got fixed! I told myself nobody gets killed over diesel numbers!"

Ma Zhenyu dragged shaking hands down his face. "Then they opened the Zurich account in Ma Deliang's name and sent 2 million yuan without asking! When I tried to say no the second time, they sent a recording of our Zhuhai meeting and a copy of the bank form with my thumbprint! Every year after that, they asked for more! First truck schedules! Then border troop schedules! Then six months ago they demanded to know when the coast navy radar stations near Qingyan Port shut down for repairs!"

He let out a broken sob, his forehead dropping toward his knees. "I could not stop! Every time I thought about walking into General Qin Zhengguo's office to confess, I thought about my daughter at college and my mother in the hospital! I told myself one more file, just one more, and I would retire next spring! Please! Do not send me to a black cell! Hand me to the Southern Theater military court! Let me face a firing squad in uniform so my family knows where my body is buried!"

The four black-clad guards stood motionless in the white light, showing neither anger nor pity.

"Confession recorded," the flat, cold, genderless voice said calmly. "Pull the wipe drive from Cabinet Four and seal the port. Bind Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu and escort him to transport."

Two guards stepped forward without a sound. Ma Zhenyu did not pull away as they lifted him and locked his wrists. His eyes stared straight ahead, glassy and empty, leaving his fallen security card lying alone on the floor.
