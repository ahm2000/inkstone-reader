# Chapter 44: What Tang Xiaoyu Was Told

Ms. Ouyang from the Civil Affairs Office had come on Monday, looked at the flat, the beds and the rib pot, and signed a paper. The shelter was full, so the Chen family became Tang Xiaoyu's temporary guardians. It was legal, and it was boring, and Liu Meifang had cried over it anyway.

On Friday, Chen Xuan left the office at 4:55 and reached Building 2 at ten to six.

His father was waiting at the door of Room 601, and he did not look well.

"Xuan. Come here." Chen Jianguo pulled him into the hall and lowered his voice. "Ms. Ouyang called at noon. The inspectors found out about the girl's father. He is gone, son. He fell at work two months ago. The company hid it."

Chen Xuan said nothing. His throat moved.

"We have to tell her tonight," Chen Jianguo said. "I will say the wrong thing. I always do. She listens to you, and she doesn't listen to me. Will you?"

"Dad." Chen Xuan's voice was small and unsteady. "I don't know how."

"Nobody does." His father rubbed his glasses on his sleeve. "Just be there. That is most of it."

* * *

Dinner had not started yet. The ribs were on the table, and Liu Meifang had put out the good bowls.

Chen Xuan knocked on Chen Yuan's door.

"Xiaoyu. Can you come out on the balcony for a minute? Bring a jacket."

She looked at him once, and he thought she already knew. She took the big jacket and followed him without a word.

The balcony was cold. Below them, lamps were coming on over the roofs of Fuxing New Village.

Chen Xuan leaned on the rail and spoke slowly, so that he would not make a mistake.

"Your dad did not go north. He had an accident at work, two months ago. He fell from a scaffold. He died, Xiaoyu. I am so sorry."

She did not move.

"The company did not tell anybody. They hid it. A lawyer found out, and some people from the Labor office checked the papers. They proved it. The money the company paid to hide it has been found. It is going into an account with your name on it, and it will wait there until you are grown. The company is being closed."

She still did not move. He thought of a wall that had stood a very long time.

"I knew," she said.

"Xiaoyu."

"I knew," she said again, to the street. "He called every Sunday. For nine weeks he did not call. Peng Hu told me he went north. But he would not go anywhere without calling. He always said 'Eat well. I'll bring you a doll.'"

Her voice was flat and thin and perfectly steady.

"Is it okay to be mad?" she asked. "At the company?"

"It is more than okay."

"Okay." She nodded at the lamps. "Then can we eat? Your mom made ribs."

Chen Xuan opened his mouth, and shut it. Then he opened the glass door, and she walked in ahead of him.

* * *

They ate. She ate everything. When the bowls were empty, she took them to the sink and began to wash.

She washed the first bowl, and the second. At the third, the tap was running, and she was not moving at all, and her shoulders began to shake.

She did not make a sound.

Liu Meifang put down her chopsticks. She crossed the kitchen with a dishcloth in her hands and put her arm around the girl's thin back. Then she was crying too, loud, into the cloth, and it did not matter who was holding whom.

Chen Hao looked at the ceiling. Chen Yuan reached across the table and took her brother's hand under it, without saying why.

Chen Jianguo stood up. He opened his mouth with great solemnity.

"As the old saying goes," he began. Then he stopped. "I don't have one."

"Dad," Chen Yuan whispered. "That is the best one yet."

Chen Xuan sat with his eyes burning and his hands under the table. He did not go to the sink. It was his mother's place and his sister's place, and he wanted to be anywhere but a place that needed a speech.

When he finally trusted his voice, he said a small thing.

"Xiaoyu. Do you want to stay?"

She did not turn from the sink. She did not say anything. After a while Liu Meifang took the bowl from her hands, and the girl let her.

* * *

On Sunday, Zhou Guilan came with a bag of pickled eggs and a thick red sweater she had knitted in three nights.

"This is yours," she said, and held it up to Tang Xiaoyu's shoulders. "Too long in the arms. You will grow into it. You are too thin."

Tang Xiaoyu stood very straight. She took the sweater. She did not say thank you, but she put it on.

Chen Hao came out of his room with an old blue school bag with two zippers, his name still inked inside the flap in his grade-six handwriting.

"It is a bit old," he said. "It has a lot of space. I used to hide candy in the second pocket. There is probably still some."

There was. It was seven years old. She laughed, one short surprised laugh, and it was the first one anyone had heard from her.

Chen Yuan stuck a piece of paper on the fridge with a banana-shaped magnet. She had written it in thick red marker.

*HOUSE RULE 1: DINNER IS AT SIX.*

"No exceptions," Chen Yuan said. "Not even for Dad's proverbs."

Chen Xuan sat on the front step with Tang Xiaoyu at the end of the afternoon. She had been very quiet all day, and he did not push.

"I asked," he said finally.

"Asked what?"

"Mom and Dad. If you could stay. They said yes before I finished the question." He looked at his shoes. "They would have said yes on Friday. I think they were waiting for you."

She turned her head. Her eyes were red.

"Stay?" she said. "For real?"

"Stay. There. I asked."

She nodded once. She did not say a word, and she did not need to. She leaned against his arm for exactly as long as it took a bus to pass, and then she sat up straight again, as if nothing had happened.

"Yunxi No. 2 Middle School," she said, in her old stiff voice. "Mrs. Ouyang says I start tomorrow."

"Ms. Ouyang. You'll like it. The cafeteria is terrible."

"I'll eat anyway."

The shelter seat was saved for another child. The school had found her a desk by the window. Everything was settled, and nobody in Room 601 knew how many hands had worked on it.

* * *

That night Liu Meifang ironed Chen Xuan's gray shirt on the kitchen table.

"A guest from the company on Monday, at noon." She sniffed at the collar. "Nobody wears this to meet a guest. Take the blue tie. And the polish for your shoes."

"It's only a free lunch, Mom."

"Free lunch with a company lady," Chen Yuan said from the sofa. "Brother. Is it a date?"

"It is a work lunch."

"With a lady."

"With a manager from a hotel group." Chen Xuan took a piece of pickled egg. "She is probably fifty and very serious."

Tang Xiaoyu, on the floor with her new school bag, looked up from her homework.

"Will they have rice?"

"I hope so."

"Then it is good," she said, and bent her head back down.

Chen Xuan was eating an egg and thinking about nothing at all. On Monday at noon, Miss Qin Yuhan would sit across from him, and he had no idea what he was in for.
