# Chapter 44: Sit With Him

On Monday evening, Liu Meifang held the phone so close that her whole face filled the screen.

"Is that a pimple? Chen Hao, get the cream. Xuan, are you eating?"

"I'm eating, Mom." Chen Xuan sat on his narrow bed in Qingyan with a box of noodles on his knee. "Look. Noodles."

"Noodles. For dinner. With no vegetables. Chen Jianguo, tell your son."

"Eat a vegetable, boy," Chen Jianguo said from somewhere off the screen.

Behind them, the Fuxing dining table was crowded. Chen Hao was fighting Chen Yuan for the last rib. At the far corner, Tang Xiaoyu ate quietly, with her bowl close to her chest and one eye on the phone.

"Hi," she said, very softly. It was the first word she had ever said to him on a screen.

Chen Xuan's chopsticks stopped. "Hi, Xiaoyu. Is the rice okay?"

"It's too much rice."

"That's the only way my mother knows how to cook," Chen Yuan said. "You get used to it."

Chen Jianguo put down his bowl. He liked to bring news to the table.

"Han Guifu's cousin cleans rooms at the Riverside Inn," he said. "Dong Haisheng is staying there. The police took his passport, and he is not allowed to leave the county. They say he sits in Room 1208 and orders noodles and looks out of the window."

"Good," Chen Hao said. "Let him eat hotel noodles."

"Does he have anybody with him?" Chen Xuan asked, with a noodle halfway to his mouth.

"Only his driver, Hua Qiang. Nine years with that man. Loyal as a dog, they say."

"Why do you care?" Liu Meifang said. "That man took half the street's savings. Eat your own dinner."

"Okay, Mom," Chen Xuan said, and ate his own dinner.

* * *

At nine that night, Dong Haisheng sat in Room 1208 with his tie off and his shoes under the bed.

He had not been out of the county in six days. He had not slept more than two hours a night. Every time his phone rang he flinched, and most of the time it was only the lawyers.

Hua Qiang stood by the door with his hands folded in front of him. He was thirty-eight, broad, and quiet. His eyes were on the carpet.

"Boss Dong, there is a man downstairs," he said. "From the Chairman."

"Huang Boren sent someone?" Dong Haisheng sat up. For six days nobody had called him except people who wanted something. "Send him up."

Hua Qiang did not move for a second. Then he opened the door.

The man who came in was mild and middle-aged, in a gray cardigan and thin glasses. He looked like an accountant on his way to an annual meeting. His name was Lian Cheng, and in his hand he carried a steel flask.

"Boss Dong. The Chairman asked me to sit with you." His voice was soft and polite. "He does not want you to be alone in a bad week. And he sent his own tea."

Hua Qiang's face was gray. He had been told to let this man in and ask nothing. Eleven days ago, his mother had a stroke, and a stranger had paid 90,000 yuan for her hospital bed. He had understood very well what the stranger would ask for.

Downstairs, Lian Cheng had put his flask on the front desk to sign the guest book. The receptionist, who was Number Ninety-five's partner in the Black Pine Guard, wiped one drop from its lid with a test strip. Behind the desk, in the small hotel clinic, Number Ninety-five held the strip under a lamp.

It turned pink. It was a heart drug. A cupful would stop a heart within the hour, and no doctor would ask why.

"The tea was meant for the man in 1208," Number Ninety-five said into her phone. "I can get a cup there. Give me four minutes."

Upstairs, Lian Cheng set out two cups.

"Longjing, from the Chairman's own shelf." He poured one cup for Dong Haisheng and a second for himself. "Drink it while it is hot."

Dong Haisheng's eyes were wet. He lifted the cup in both hands.

Somebody knocked.

"Fresh towels, sir." A young floor attendant stood in the door with a tray. "And a complaint about noise from the room below. May I check your window?"

Lian Cheng's smile did not change. "Of course."

The attendant crossed the room and checked the window. On the way back she stopped beside the little table.

"Oh, sir, your cup has a crack. I am so sorry. Let me change it."

She took the cup from Dong Haisheng's hands, set a clean one in its place, and poured from the small hotel pot on her tray. It was the same color. She carried the old cup away on her tray, and nobody saw her face.

Dong Haisheng drank the new tea. It tasted of hot water and nothing else.

Lian Cheng set his own cup down untouched. He stayed for twenty minutes and talked about the weather, the price of steel, and how a man in trouble should rest. Then he stood, buttoned his cardigan and said good night.

He walked to the elevator with a calm face. He was a man who had finished a small job, and he thought he would hear the result in the morning.

* * *

Ten minutes later, there was a quiet knock again.

Number Ninety-five stood outside in a nurse's coat. She did not smile.

"Sir, I am the hotel's nurse. The attendant took a cup out of this room because the smell was wrong. We tested it." She held up the pink strip. "This color means a heart drug. In the cup that was meant for you."

Dong Haisheng stared at it. He remembered his own hands, lifted and ready.

"Lucky," he said, and his lips shook. "I was lucky."

"Sir," she said gently. "The man who brought it left his own tea untouched."

Dong Haisheng sat down on the bed. He had known Huang Boren for fourteen years. He knew exactly how Huang Boren dealt with a thread he did not want anyone to pull.

His body began to shake, and it did not stop for a long time.

When he looked up, Hua Qiang was standing in the doorway with his hands at his sides. He was crying without any noise.

"Boss Dong." His voice broke. "My mother. They paid for her bed. I did not know what was in it, I swear. I only knew I was not to ask."

Dong Haisheng looked at him for a long moment.

"I know," he said. "Sit down, Hua Qiang. We are both going to say a lot of things tonight."

He picked up his phone. His thumb was not steady.

"Get me the number of the Discipline Commission. Not a lawyer. The commission. I will tell them everything about Huang Boren, and I want to start before he thinks of something else."

In a quiet courtyard in Qingyan, Number One heard the nurse's report. His face did not move.

"Tell Number Seven," he said. "Lian Cheng is in a gray Passat in the hotel lot. Do not touch Dong Haisheng's driver. Take Huang Boren's man alive. The Boss wants the Chairman, not the errand boy."
