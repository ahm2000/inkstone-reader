# Chapter 106: Suit Shopping at Plaza 66

Crisp autumn air drifted down Nanjing Road on Saturday afternoon, carrying golden ginkgo leaves past the tall glass doors of Plaza 66. Inside, the marble halls gleamed under warm crystal chandeliers. Wealthy shoppers strolled past luxury boutiques in tailored coats and polished heels, their voices hushed by the quiet elegance of the atrium.

Walking along the third floor, Chen Xuan wore a soft gray polo shirt, dark casual trousers, and clean white sneakers. In his right hand, he swung his scratched 15-yuan green enamel thermos by its thin wire handle, taking an unhurried sip of warm jasmine tea every few steps.

Beside him, Qin Yuhan wore a fitted cream trench coat over a pale silk blouse. As the young president of Haiyue Group, she could walk into any boardroom in Lujiazui without blinking. Today, however, her gaze kept drifting sideways to the dented green thermos swinging beside her husband's knee.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan said, keeping her voice calm as they turned toward a quiet menswear salon on the corner. "Could you please tuck that thermos into your canvas bag for thirty minutes? My parents fly in from Beijing tomorrow, and we need to pick out a proper suit for dinner."

Chen Xuan blinked and looked down at his trusty companion. "What is wrong with my thermos? Last Sunday, Grandpa told me tea brewed in enamel tastes twice as smooth as tea from fancy bone china. He even asked where I bought it."

"Grandpa also wears twenty-year-old cotton slippers to the courtyard gate," Qin Yuhan replied, a faint smile tugging at the corner of her mouth. "My mother is different. She notices every crease on a collar and every scuff on a shoe. If you walk into a bespoke salon swinging a green thermos, the tailors will think you came to repair their air conditioning."

Chen Xuan chuckled softly and slid the thermos into his faded canvas bag, zipping it partway so the lid would not rattle. "Fair enough. For my wife's peace of mind, I will hide my treasure until we leave."

The menswear salon was quiet and smelled faintly of cedarwood and fresh wool. Soft autumn sunlight filtered through tall sheer curtains, illuminating rows of neatly pressed jackets. A middle-aged stylist in a crisp vest stepped forward immediately, recognizing Qin Yuhan's poise even before noticing the understated luxury of her coat.

"Good afternoon, Miss Qin," the stylist greeted with a polite nod. "Welcome to the private fitting gallery. Are we looking for formal business attire today?"

"Something understated and well-tailored for a family dinner," Qin Yuhan said, glancing over the racks. Her fingers paused on a dark navy wool suit with a fine matte weave. "This one. Pair it with a crisp white dress shirt and a muted slate silk tie."

"An excellent choice," the stylist said, taking the hanger down. "The shoulders are lightly structured, so it wears comfortably without looking stiff. Right this way, sir."

Chen Xuan followed the stylist into the spacious fitting room without complaint. He was used to loose polo shirts and cotton jackets that made it easy to stretch at his desk on the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics or lean over a hot wok at home. Still, if wearing a tailored suit made tomorrow's dinner easier for Qin Yuhan, he was more than happy to play the part.

Ten minutes later, the heavy velvet curtain slid open.

Qin Yuhan, who had been checking a message on her phone while seated on the leather sofa, looked up casually. Her fingers froze against the glass screen.

Standing before the tall brass mirror, Chen Xuan had straightened his posture without even thinking about it. The dark navy wool hugged his broad shoulders with effortless precision, tapering cleanly down to his waist. The crisp white collar framed his calm, handsome face, and the quiet stillness in his dark eyes wiped away every trace of the lazy logistics clerk who usually slouched behind a spreadsheet.

For a brief second, the entire room seemed to grow quieter. Even the veteran stylist paused with his measuring tape halfway lifted, startled by the sudden, natural authority radiating from a man who had walked in wearing plain white sneakers five minutes earlier.

Chen Xuan turned slightly toward the mirror, lifted one arm, and tested the seam across his shoulder.

"Not bad at all," Chen Xuan said easily, breaking the hush with a warm grin. "The fabric is light. Tell me honestly, Yuhan, do you think I can still flip braised pork ribs in a wok without tearing the elbow?"

The spell snapped at once. Qin Yuhan let out a soft laugh, shaking her head as warmth rose to her cheeks. "If you ever wear a suit like this into the kitchen, I will confiscate your spatula. Turn around so the tailor can check the sleeve length."

The stylist recovered his composure with a polite smile and stepped forward with a small cushion of silver pins. "The fit across the shoulders is almost custom already, sir. We only need to hem the trousers by half a centimeter, which will take twenty minutes in our back workshop."

Before Chen Xuan could answer, Qin Yuhan's phone buzzed in her palm. She glanced at the caller name and frowned slightly. It was her executive assistant calling from the 38th floor of Haiyue Tower about a delayed port customs clearance that needed her verbal sign-off before the weekend shift changed.

"I have to take this," Qin Yuhan said apologetically, rising from the sofa. "Wait here while they finish the measurements. I will step out to the terrace corridor so I do not disturb the salon."

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said gently. "Take your time. I will browse around until you get back."

Qin Yuhan gave him a quick, grateful smile and walked out through the glass archway. Chen Xuan waited until the stylist finished pinning the trouser cuff and hurried off toward the workshop, then changed back into his polo shirt and trousers. With twenty minutes to spare, he picked up his canvas bag and strolled toward the bright display counters on the far side of the gallery.
