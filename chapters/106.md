# Chapter 106: Trouble at Shanghai Port Gate Seven

Wednesday morning in Shanghai started with a light gray drizzle and the steady clatter of keyboards inside Hengxin Logistics.

Chen Xuan sat at his corner desk by the window, wearing his clean, faded dark windbreaker over a plain white shirt. Beside his mouse pad stood his scratched green thermos, filled with hot jasmine tea. He checked three regional truck sheets and took a slow sip from the steel cap.

At nine-fifteen, the supervisor's office door clicked open. Hu Dewei hurried out with a thick manila folder, a thin sheen of sweat along his hairline despite the cool air.

He skipped past two younger clerks who quickly pretended to type and walked straight to Chen Xuan's desk.

"Chen Xuan," Hu Dewei said, dropping the folder onto the table. "Leave those local sheets for now. I have an urgent job for you."

Chen Xuan set his thermos cap down neatly. "What is it, Supervisor Hu?"

"Our client shipment of precision valves is stuck at Shanghai Port Gate Seven," Hu Dewei said quickly, smoothing his thinning hair. "Driver Zhao Kang has been waiting in the yard since yesterday afternoon. If that truck misses the noon release window, the Chengnan factory hits us with a late penalty."

"Did the customs papers have an error?" Chen Xuan asked calmly, opening the folder.

"No, every stamp is clean," Hu Dewei waved impatiently. "The clearance hall at Gate Seven is backed up, and the brokers down there are being difficult. I would go myself, of course, but I have a ten o'clock review with the regional manager. You have a steady temper and never miss a detail. Take the bus to Gate Seven, get the release slip, and roll Zhao Kang's truck out before twelve."

Across the aisle, clerk Wang Lei glanced over with a quiet wince before ducking behind his monitor. Everyone knew there was no ten o'clock review. Hu Dewei had heard that Gate Seven's shipping brokers were shaking down small freight firms for extra fees, and he wanted Chen Xuan's name on the errand in case the delivery failed.

Chen Xuan checked the three stamped sheets inside. The cargo list, tax receipt, and pre-inspection code were completely in order.

"All right," Chen Xuan said evenly, closing the folder. "I will head over now."

Hu Dewei's shoulders dropped in relief. "Good. Do not argue with the port staff, and do not call me unless the truck is actually rolling out the gate. Handle it cleanly."

"I will," Chen Xuan replied.

He tucked the folder into his plain canvas satchel, screwed the lid tight on his scratched green thermos, and caught the harbor shuttle bus downstairs.

By ten-ten, Chen Xuan stepped off the bus near Shanghai Port Gate Seven. Damp salt air and diesel smoke hung over the wide concrete docks.

Near the glass doors of the Port Clearance Hall, Hengxin Logistics' senior truck driver Zhao Kang spotted him and hurried over, rubbing his tired eyes.

"Chen Xuan, thank goodness they sent someone," Zhao Kang said. "I have been stuck in that cab since four yesterday. My cargo seals are clean and my manifest is cleared in the system, yet they refuse to hand over the gate release slip."

"Who is holding it up?" Chen Xuan asked calmly.

Zhao Kang lowered his voice and nodded toward the crowded glass doors. "A shipping broker named Zhou Mingcheng. His company runs half the clearance desks at Gate Seven. Everyone on the dock knows his uncle, Zhou Desheng, is a deputy director in the port authority. Since Monday, Zhou Mingcheng has been forcing every small freight firm to pay an extra fast-lane fee before the window clerks stamp their slips."

"How much?" Chen Xuan asked lightly.

"200k per company account for the quarter, or fifty thousand just to push one truck through today," Zhao Kang muttered bitterly. "Hengxin Logistics is a small outfit. Hu Dewei would never sign off on that, and I cannot pay it myself."

"Wait by your truck, Master Zhao," Chen Xuan said easily. "Save your fuel. I will go inside and take a look."

"Be careful," Zhao Kang warned. "Zhou Mingcheng has a nasty temper and keeps rough men hanging around the yard."

Chen Xuan nodded once and pushed open the glass door.

Inside, the Gate Seven Clearance Hall was noisy and cramped. Only Counter Two and Counter Three were open, while forty tired freight clerks packed the wooden benches, staring anxiously at the digital queue board. Not a single number had moved in twenty minutes.

Right in front of Counter Three leaned a tall man in his early thirties wearing a sharp navy jacket, a thick gold watch, and polished leather shoes. Between his fingers burned an imported cigarette, directly beneath a large red no-smoking sign.

That was Zhou Mingcheng.

Standing before him was a thin, anxious freight clerk named Lin Xiaodong, clutching a stack of blue shipping bills.

"Manager Zhou, please," Lin Xiaodong pleaded, his voice tight. "We have two refrigerated trucks of fresh sea bass in Lane Four. If we miss eleven o'clock, the ice melts and the whole load spoils. Our company already paid every port tax on time."

Zhou Mingcheng blew a lazy stream of smoke toward the ceiling and tapped his gold watch on the marble counter.

"Lin Xiaodong, how many times do I have to explain this?" Zhou Mingcheng said loudly so the whole hall heard. "Port traffic is heavy this week. Standard review takes three full days. If your little firm wants the fast lane, sign the brokerage form and wire 200k to Mingcheng Freight Services. The moment the receipt hits my phone, your two trucks roll out in ten minutes."

Lin Xiaodong's face went pale. "Manager Zhou, 200k is more than our company nets in two months. We did not even hire your brokerage. We filed our own forms directly at the port window."

Zhou Mingcheng's smile turned ugly. He flicked gray cigarette ash straight onto the toe of Lin Xiaodong's damp sneaker.

"Then sit on the bench and wait three days," Zhou Mingcheng sneered. "Or keep raising your voice at Counter Three, and I will tell the yard team to pull both seafood trucks for a full manual crate check. By the time they finish counting every fish, you can sell them as fertilizer."

An angry silence fell over the hall. Several clerks clenched their fists, yet nobody dared step forward. Everyone knew who Zhou Mingcheng's uncle was at Shanghai Port.

Lin Xiaodong stood frozen, his shoulders shaking as he stared down at the ash on his shoe.

Chen Xuan walked quietly to the ticket machine, pressed the button for standard release, and took slip number forty-eight. He tucked the paper inside his manila folder, unscrewed his scratched green thermos, and took a calm sip of warm jasmine tea.

Across the hall, near the quiet glass partition by the side wall, a pair of cool, sharp eyes lifted from a slim leather folder and noticed the plain man standing completely at ease in the middle of the storm.
