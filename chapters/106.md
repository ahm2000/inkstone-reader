# Chapter 106: Suit Shopping at Plaza 66

Back in Shanghai, Saturday afternoon sunlight spilled across the marble atrium of Plaza 66, where warm crystal chandeliers gleamed above quiet luxury boutiques. Well-dressed shoppers strolled past polished glass storefronts in tailored coats and leather heels, their voices lowered to match the calm elegance of the hall.

Walking along the third floor, Chen Xuan looked entirely out of place in a soft gray polo shirt, dark casual trousers, and clean white sneakers. In his right hand, he swung his scratched green enamel thermos by its thin wire handle, taking an unhurried sip of warm jasmine tea every few steps.

Beside him, Qin Yuhan wore a fitted cream trench coat over a pale silk blouse. As the president of Haiyue Group, she could walk into any boardroom in Lujiazui without blinking. Today, however, her gaze kept drifting sideways to the dented green thermos swinging beside her husband's knee.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan said, keeping her voice calm as they turned toward a quiet menswear salon on the corner. "Could you please tuck that thermos into your canvas bag for thirty minutes? My parents fly in from Beijing tomorrow for Sunday dinner, and we need to pick out a proper suit for you."

Chen Xuan blinked and looked down at his trusty companion. "What is wrong with my thermos? You told me yourself that Grandpa still drinks his morning tea out of a chipped army mug. Enamel keeps jasmine tea twice as smooth as fancy bone china."

"Grandpa can wear twenty-year-old cotton slippers to the courtyard gate and nobody dares say a word," Qin Yuhan replied, a faint smile tugging at the corner of her mouth. "My mother is different. She notices every crease on a collar and every scuff on a shoe. If you walk into a bespoke salon swinging a green thermos, the tailors will think you came to repair their air conditioning."

Chen Xuan chuckled softly and slid the thermos into his faded canvas bag, zipping it partway so the lid would not rattle. "Fair enough. For my wife's peace of mind, I will hide my treasure until we leave. Though if I catch a dry throat in there, you owe me a pot of tea after dinner."

"Deal," Qin Yuhan said lightly, her fingers brushing his sleeve as she pushed open the heavy glass door.

The menswear salon was hushed and smelled faintly of cedarwood and fresh wool. Soft autumn light filtered through tall sheer curtains, illuminating rows of neatly pressed jackets and silk ties arranged by shade. A middle-aged stylist in a crisp charcoal vest stepped forward immediately, recognizing Qin Yuhan's poise even before noticing the understated cut of her coat.

"Good afternoon, Miss Qin," the stylist greeted with a polite nod. "Welcome to the private fitting gallery. Are we looking for formal business attire today?"

"Something understated and well-tailored for a family dinner," Qin Yuhan said, glancing calmly along the racks. She passed over the glossy tuxedos and bold pinstripes until her fingers paused on a dark navy wool suit with a fine matte weave. "This one. Pair it with a crisp white dress shirt and a muted slate silk tie."

"An excellent choice," the stylist said, lifting the hanger down with practiced care. "The shoulders are lightly structured, so the jacket drapes naturally without feeling stiff. Right this way, sir."

Chen Xuan followed the stylist into the spacious fitting room without complaint. He preferred loose cotton shirts and soft jackets that made it easy to stretch at his desk at Hengxin Logistics or lean over a hot stove at home. Still, he knew how much tomorrow evening meant to Qin Yuhan. Her parents had only seen him briefly over a video call from Beijing, and if wearing a tailored suit saved her from a single awkward remark, he was more than happy to play the part.

Outside, Qin Yuhan settled onto the low leather sofa and glanced at a work message on her phone while the salon attendant brought over a small tray of sparkling water.

Ten minutes later, the heavy velvet curtain slid open.

Qin Yuhan looked up casually from her screen. Her fingers froze against the glass.

Standing before the tall brass mirror, Chen Xuan had straightened his posture without even thinking about it. The dark navy wool hugged his broad shoulders with effortless precision, tapering cleanly down to his waist. The crisp white collar framed his calm, handsome face, and the quiet stillness in his dark eyes wiped away every trace of the relaxed logistics clerk who usually slouched behind a shipping spreadsheet.

For a brief second, the entire room seemed to grow quieter. Even the veteran stylist paused with his measuring tape halfway lifted, startled by the sudden, natural authority radiating from a man who had walked in wearing plain white sneakers ten minutes earlier.

Chen Xuan turned slightly toward the mirror, lifted one arm, and tested the seam across his shoulder.

"Not bad at all," Chen Xuan said easily, breaking the hush with a warm grin. "The fabric is light. Tell me honestly, Yuhan, do you think I can still toss stir-fried greens or pan-sear sea bass in a wok without tearing the elbow?"

The spell snapped at once. Qin Yuhan let out a soft laugh, shaking her head as warmth rose to her cheeks. "If you ever wear a suit like this into the kitchen, I will confiscate your spatula. Turn around so the tailor can check the sleeve length."

The stylist recovered his composure with a polite smile and stepped forward with a small cushion of silver pins. "The fit across the shoulders is almost custom already, sir. We only need to hem the trousers by half a centimeter, which will take twenty minutes in our back workshop."

Before Chen Xuan could answer, Qin Yuhan's phone buzzed in her palm. She glanced at the caller name and frowned slightly. It was her assistant, Lin Wei, calling from Haiyue Tower about a delayed port customs clearance that needed her verbal sign-off before the weekend shift changed.

"I have to take this," Qin Yuhan said apologetically, rising from the sofa. "Lin Wei only calls on Saturday when the port office is holding up a container. Wait here while they finish the measurements. I will step out to the terrace corridor so I do not disturb the salon."

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said gently. "Take your time. I will browse around until you get back."

Qin Yuhan gave him a quick, grateful smile and walked out through the glass archway. Chen Xuan waited patiently until the stylist finished pinning the trouser cuff and hurried off toward the back workshop, then stepped behind the velvet curtain to change back into his polo shirt and casual trousers. With twenty minutes to spare, he slung his canvas bag over one shoulder and strolled toward the bright display counters on the far side of the gallery.
