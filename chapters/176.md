# Chapter 176: Back at Shanghai Hongqiao

The afternoon flight touched down smoothly at Shanghai Hongqiao Airport under a crisp late November sky. As the cabin doors opened, a cool breeze drifted through the jet bridge, carrying the dry scent of late autumn.

Chen Xuan stepped out first, rolling two suitcases with easy grace. Tucked into the side pocket of his worn canvas backpack was his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos, its painted lid scratched from years of daily use. Beside him walked Qin Yuhan in a soft camel wool coat and dark trousers, her hair tied back in a neat knot.

"You really brought that thermos all the way on a trip and back," Qin Yuhan said, glancing down at the green cylinder with an amused smile.

"Airport water tastes like warm plastic," Chen Xuan replied seriously. "My green thermos keeps jasmine tea hot for six hours."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly and tucked her arm through his as they walked toward the arrivals hall. On the 38th floor of Haiyue Group, everyone rushed around her with tight shoulders and thick folders. Beside Chen Xuan, the world always slowed down.

Before they cleared the glass doors of the arrivals gate, a loud shout rang out across the waiting area.

"Brother Xuan! Sister-in-law Yuhan! Over here!"

Standing near the silver guardrail were two young figures waving wildly. On the left was Chen Hao, Chen Xuan's twenty-one-year-old cousin from Yunxi County in Handong, wearing a thick navy down jacket and a wide grin. Beside him stood his younger sister, Chen Yuan, a bright college freshman with a high ponytail, a red scarf, and two bulging woven cloth bags resting by her sneakers.

Their high-speed train from Handong had arrived at Hongqiao Railway Station twenty minutes earlier, and they had walked over through the connecting tunnel just as promised.

"Easy there, Hao," Chen Xuan called out with a warm grin. "People will think we are smuggling a celebrity through customs."

"You promised us a weekend in Shanghai!" Chen Hao said, hurrying up to grab a suitcase. "Sister-in-law, you look even prettier in person than in the wedding photos!"

Qin Yuhan's cheeks warmed slightly. "Welcome to Shanghai, Hao, Yuan. Was the train ride comfortable?"

"Super fast!" Chen Yuan chimed in, hugging Qin Yuhan's arm shyly. "Grandma made us bring these for you." She pointed at the two cloth bags. "Dried sweet potatoes, two jars of pickled radish, and roasted peanuts Auntie shelled last night."

Chen Xuan hefted both bags easily in one hand. "Good timing. My wife has been complaining that Pudong supermarket peanuts have zero flavor."

"I said that once last Tuesday," Qin Yuhan protested gently, taking Chen Yuan's backpack. "Come on, the car is in the garage. Let us get home before Friday traffic builds up."

They walked down to the parking deck, where Qin Yuhan's silver sedan waited. Chen Xuan loaded the luggage into the trunk and slid into the driver's seat while Qin Yuhan sat beside him.

As the sedan merged onto the elevated expressway heading toward Pudong, golden plane tree leaves tumbled across the barriers in the November wind. Chen Hao leaned close to the rear window, staring in open wonder as the glass skyscrapers of Lujiazui rose against the fading afternoon light.

"Whoa," Chen Hao breathed. "Back in Yunxi County, the six-story post office used to block the sun. Here, you have to tilt your whole neck back just to see the roof."

"Do not stare too hard, or you will get a crick in your neck before dinner," Chen Xuan teased through the rearview mirror.

"Sister-in-law," Chen Yuan asked curiously, "is your office really inside one of those giant glass towers?"

"On the 38th floor," Qin Yuhan answered warmly. "Though I promise it is much less exciting than it looks. Most days it is just endless spreadsheets and meetings."

"And then she comes home and tells me her shoulders ache," Chen Xuan added lightly, earning a playful poke on his arm from Qin Yuhan.

By 5:20 PM, the sedan glided through the tree-lined gates of Emerald Riverside Villa in Pudong. The late autumn sun cast a warm amber glow over the courtyard garden and the two-story stone house beside the canal.

Chen Hao stepped onto the gravel drive, his jaw dropping. "Brother Xuan, this place is huge! I thought you worked in logistics!"

"I do," Chen Xuan said calmly, lifting the cloth bags out of the trunk. "Twelfth floor at Hengxin Logistics, 12,000 yuan a month, clock out at 4:55 PM sharp. This house belongs to your sister-in-law. I am just the live-in chef and handyman."

Qin Yuhan shook her head, smiling as she unlocked the front door. "Do not listen to his nonsense. Come inside, both of you. I prepared the two sunny guest rooms upstairs. Set your bags down and rest."

The living room smelled faintly of cedarwood and tangerines. Qin Yuhan poured steaming cups of osmanthus tea and set out sliced pears and walnuts for the cousins. Meanwhile, Chen Xuan washed his hands, tied on his faded blue apron, and walked straight into the kitchen.

Soon, the rich aroma of caramelized sugar, ginger, and soy sauce drifted into the hallway. Chen Hao hovered in the kitchen doorway, watching Chen Xuan toss pork ribs in the iron wok.

"Brother Xuan, Grandma told me you beat Grandpa Qin at chess three times last month," Chen Hao whispered, grinning.

"Only when I forget to lose on purpose," Chen Xuan murmured back. "Now carry those garlic sprouts to the table. If you want braised pork ribs, earn your keep."

By 6:40 PM, the dining table was crowded with steaming dishes: glossy braised pork ribs, stir-fried bamboo shoots, crisp green beans, and a clay pot of Handong tofu soup. They clinked glasses of warm plum juice and hot tea under the soft yellow light.

Chen Yuan took one bite of the ribs and closed her eyes happily. "Sister-in-law, I see why you married Brother Xuan. This tastes even better than the banquet hall back home!"

Qin Yuhan laughed, placing an extra rib into Chen Yuan's bowl. "Eat plenty tonight. Tomorrow morning, we are taking you both to Nanjing Road and IFC Mall. Wear comfortable shoes."

Outside, the cool November evening settled over the riverside courtyard. Inside, laughter rose easily around the table, warm and unhurried.
