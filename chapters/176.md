# Chapter 176: Back at Shanghai Hongqiao

The afternoon sun slanted through the oval window of the plane, turning the clouds below into a bright sea of white cotton.

Inside the quiet first-class cabin of the flight from Shenzhen back to Shanghai, Qin Yuhan sat with her legs crossed, scrolling through the financial news on her tablet.

Qin Yuhan read two paragraphs, then let out a soft, helpless laugh.

"Look at this," Qin Yuhan said, her eyes bright with amusement. "Shenhai Shipping called an emergency board meeting this morning. Three of their biggest southern buyers tore up their contracts before noon, and Shenzhen Port Inspection Office suspended Chief Inspector Lu Guobin for an internal review. No headline mentions last night's arrests yet. The Shen Family lost more than 400 million yuan before lunch."

Chen Xuan barely glanced at the screen.

"Sounds like they had a rough morning," Chen Xuan said mildly.

Qin Yuhan set the tablet down and turned to study him. Her sharp eyes softened with playful disbelief. "A rough morning? Chen Xuan, let us count together. Last month, you went to Jingzhou City for a quiet family errand, and two local tycoons ended up in handcuffs before Sunday. This week, you flew down to Shenzhen with me on a simple business trip to check six containers at Pier Nine, and a billion-yuan shipping family practically collapsed in two days."

She leaned closer, resting her chin lightly on her fingers. "Tell me honestly. Do billion-yuan families just fall apart every time you buy a plane ticket?"

Chen Xuan screwed the lid back onto his thermos and gazed calmly out the window.

"I think it is the weather," Chen Xuan answered with a straight face.

Qin Yuhan blinked, then burst out laughing.

"The weather?" Qin Yuhan repeated, shaking her head. "Shenzhen was thirty degrees and sunny, Chen Xuan. How does good weather make a port inspector lose his job and a shipping heir cry in front of his grandfather?"

"Sunny days make people careless," Chen Xuan explained evenly. "When the sun is bright, greedy folks get too bold. They step into the open, forget to watch their feet, and trip over themselves. Nothing mysterious about it."

Qin Yuhan watched his calm profile for a moment. Any other man in Shanghai would have bragged nonstop if he had helped save Haiyue Group 8 million yuan a day at Shenzhen Pier Nine. Yet Chen Xuan sat there like a regular office clerk deciding whether to buy cabbage or green onions on his way home.

"You really stick to your story, do you not?" Qin Yuhan teased softly. "Still just an ordinary clerk at Hengxin Logistics?"

"My badge is still in my bag," Chen Xuan replied easily. "If I miss Monday check-in, Manager Wang will dock my bonus."

Qin Yuhan rolled her eyes lightly, though warmth lingered in her gaze. "Fine. Keep your secrets. Still, tell me one thing. When Shen Jingzhou had six bodyguards blocking that locked gate and shouted that he would toss you into the harbor, you did not even blink. Most company chairmen I know would have stepped back. How do you stay that calm?"

Chen Xuan thought for two seconds. "I used to move heavy boxes in a warehouse," he said simply. "When you carry forty kilos up three flights of stairs, you learn not to waste energy getting excited. Shouting does not make a heavy box any lighter."

Qin Yuhan stared at him, then shook her head with a genuine smile. "Talking to you is bad for my blood pressure. You always sound completely reasonable, even when nothing about you is ordinary."

An announcement chimed overhead as the plane began its descent toward Shanghai Hongqiao Airport. Twenty minutes later, the wheels touched the runway smoothly.

Inside the bright terminal of Shanghai Hongqiao Airport, the cool autumn air felt crisp after Shenzhen's heat. Chen Xuan slung his canvas backpack over one shoulder and walked beside Qin Yuhan toward the arrival exit. Even after a two-hour flight, Qin Yuhan looked poised and striking in her beige trench coat, drawing quiet glances from passing travelers.

Outside the glass sliding doors, her silver sedan waited at the curb. Her driver, Lao Wu, hurried out to open the rear door.

"Get in," Qin Yuhan said, turning to Chen Xuan. "Lao Wu can drop you at your apartment gate on our way to Lujiazui."

"No need for a detour," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile. "Metro Line 2 is right downstairs. It takes thirty minutes to my stop, and I want to buy fresh fruit on the way home."

"Buying fruit?" Qin Yuhan raised an eyebrow. "Expecting company?"

"My younger sister Chen Yuan and my brother Chen Hao are coming to Shanghai on Saturday morning," Chen Xuan said, his expression softening warmly. "It is their first trip to the city. My brother is seventeen, in his last year of high school, and he has talked about seeing the Oriental Pearl Tower for three years."

Qin Yuhan's face lit up at once. "Your sister and brother are visiting? Let me send a car to pick them up at the train station, and I can book a nice riverfront table for all of you."

"Thank you, President Qin, really," Chen Xuan declined gently. "They want to ride the subway, walk Nanjing Road, and eat hot dumplings from street shops like normal visitors. A luxury car would make Chen Hao sit stiff as a board the whole ride."

Qin Yuhan paused, then nodded with understanding. "You know them best. Just call me if you need anything while they are here."

"I will," Chen Xuan promised.

Before stepping into the car, Qin Yuhan paused with one hand on the door frame. The playful teasing faded from her eyes, replaced by quiet sincerity.

"Thank you for Shenzhen, Chen Xuan," she said softly. "Without you, Haiyue Group would have bled heavily at Pier Nine. I owe you a real dinner once your family heads home."

"A bowl of scallion oil noodles will do," Chen Xuan replied lightly. "Have a safe drive back."

Qin Yuhan smiled, stepped inside, and the silver sedan glided smoothly into airport traffic.

Chen Xuan watched the taillights merge onto the elevated road, then walked calmly toward the escalators leading down to Metro Line 2. Forty meters behind him in the flow of commuters, two quiet men in plain matte-black jackets moved at an unhurried pace, blending completely into the crowd.

Chen Xuan pulled out his plain white phone and sent a short message to Number 1.

Keep Saturday and Sunday completely clear. No calls, no reports, no office matters unless the sky falls.

Three seconds later, a brief reply appeared on the clean white screen.

Understood. Everything is quiet. Enjoy the weekend with your family.

Chen Xuan pocketed the phone and stepped onto the waiting subway train with a relaxed smile. For the next two days, he was simply going to be an older brother showing his family around Shanghai.
