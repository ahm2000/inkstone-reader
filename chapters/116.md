# Chapter 116: Flying to Beijing with the Wife

Back in Shanghai, a quiet workweek at Hengxin Logistics came to an end at exactly 4:55 PM. On the 12th floor, Chen Xuan shut down his desktop computer right on the second and pushed his chair neatly under the desk.

He screwed the lid onto his dented green metal thermos and tucked a small porcelain jar of Yunnan red tea leaves into his worn canvas shoulder bag, ready to head straight out the door.

Across the aisle, Zhang Pengfei leaned back in his swivel chair and clicked his tongue. "Leaving at 4:55 PM on the dot again, Chen Xuan? Department Manager Hu Dewei has not even locked his office yet. What is the big rush this Friday? Going to buy half-price cabbage at the evening market?"

"Nothing that exciting, Pengfei," Chen Xuan smiled, slinging his canvas bag over his shoulder. "My wife has a family birthday trip tonight, and I promised to carry her suitcase."

Before Zhang Pengfei could make another joke, senior clerk Pan Xiulan nudged his elbow and shook her head. "Leave him alone, Pengfei. Look at you, still single at twenty-eight because you stay here staring at spreadsheets until dark. Chen Xuan clocks out on time, cooks dinner, and treats his wife like treasure. You should take notes."

Zhang Pengfei groaned and waved a hand in defeat. "Fine, fine! Have a safe trip, Chen Xuan. Bring back some Beijing candied hawthorns on Monday!"

"If I remember," Chen Xuan chuckled, giving his coworkers a lazy wave as he walked toward the elevators.

Down on the ground floor, the cool autumn air felt refreshing after a long day under office lights. Chen Xuan stepped through the revolving doors and spotted a sleek black sedan parked neatly by the curb. Before he reached the door, the rear window rolled down, revealing Qin Yuhan in a soft cream cashmere coat over a dark autumn dress.

She looked a little tired from a full week of executive meetings at Haiyue Tower, yet the tight line between her eyebrows vanished the moment she saw his dented thermos peeking out of his canvas bag.

"Right on time as always," Qin Yuhan said softly as Chen Xuan climbed into the back seat.

"Of course. Never keep the wife waiting," Chen Xuan said. He pulled a clean travel flask from his bag and poured a small cup of warm honey ginger water for her, then handed over a warm paper bag. "I bought roasted chestnuts from the old uncle near the subway entrance. Eat a few before we reach the airport."

Qin Yuhan took the warm cup with both hands, letting the gentle heat sink into her fingers. "You always remember these small things."

"That is my whole job," Chen Xuan replied easily, cracking open a glossy chestnut and placing the sweet golden nut into her palm. "You run Haiyue Group, and I keep you fed."

As the driver steered onto the elevated highway toward Hongqiao Airport, Qin Yuhan leaned back against the leather headrest and let out a quiet sigh. "My parents flew to Beijing yesterday to help prepare for Grandpa's seventy-eighth birthday tomorrow. When we get to the courtyard, my Second Aunt and my cousin Qin Wenbo will be there too."

Chen Xuan peeled another chestnut without looking up. "Let me guess. They still think you married a lazy clerk who makes 12,000 yuan a month and has zero ambition."

"My mother still complains about it sometimes," Qin Yuhan admitted, her voice dropping slightly. "And Second Aunt loves comparing salaries and titles. If they say anything rude tomorrow, just ignore them. You do not need to prove anything to anyone."

"Do not worry about me," Chen Xuan said with a warm, unbothered smile. "My skin is thicker than the city wall. As long as Grandpa likes the tea I brought, I will consider the trip a complete success."

Qin Yuhan glanced at the modest cloth-wrapped jar sticking out of his bag and could not help laughing. "Only you would bring a plain jar of tea to a retired four-star general's birthday."

"Good tea does not need a gold box," Chen Xuan said lightly. "Besides, you already bought the fancy jade gift and the cashmere coat. One of us has to keep things down to earth."

At Hongqiao Airport, they checked in smoothly and boarded the evening flight to Beijing. Qin Yuhan's assistant had booked front-cabin seats so she could rest after her tiring week. Once the plane climbed above the clouds, Chen Xuan folded a wool blanket neatly over her knees, checked that her pillow sat comfortably behind her neck, and tilted his own seat back with a contented sigh.

Three rows behind them, two quiet men in simple dark autumn jackets sat reading newspapers without speaking a word. Neither Qin Yuhan nor the flight attendants paid them any mind. They were members of the Black Order, silent and unrecorded, keeping their permanent quiet watch over Chen Xuan wherever he went.

Far away on a private tarmac in Shanghai, a sleek white business jet sat parked under the floodlights with its engines off. Beside a white sedan, Number 1 checked the flight departure on a slim white tablet and turned to Number 7.

"Young Master's commercial flight has departed on schedule," Number 1 said in a calm, low voice. "Make sure our people in Beijing stay well out of sight near the northern lakes. Young Master wants a peaceful family weekend with Madam. No white cars anywhere near that hutong."

"Understood," Number 7 answered crisply. "Young Master will not even notice we are in the city."

Two hours later, the plane touched down at Beijing Capital Airport under a clear, starry autumn sky. A crisp northern wind greeted them outside the terminal, noticeably cooler than the air in Shanghai. Chen Xuan immediately pulled a soft wool scarf from his canvas bag and looped it loosely around Qin Yuhan's neck before rolling her suitcase toward the waiting hotel car.

"You packed a spare scarf for me?" Qin Yuhan asked, looking up at him with warm eyes.

"Beijing nights in autumn always bite harder than people expect," Chen Xuan said, opening the car door for her. "Let us get a good night of sleep at the hotel. Tomorrow morning, we go wish Grandpa a happy birthday."
