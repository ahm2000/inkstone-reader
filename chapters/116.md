# Chapter 116: Flying to Beijing with the Wife

At four fifty-five on Friday afternoon, the golden sunlight over Lujiazui slanted across the twelfth-floor windows of Hengxin Logistics.

Chen Xuan shut down his desktop computer right on the second, screwed the lid onto his scratched green enamel thermos, and tucked a small porcelain jar of Yunnan tea leaves into his canvas shoulder bag.

Across the aisle, Zhang Pengfei leaned back in his swivel chair and clicked his tongue. "Leaving at four fifty-five on the dot again, Chen Xuan? Department Manager Hu Dewei has not even locked his office yet. What is the big rush this Friday? Going to buy half-price cabbage at the evening market?"

"Nothing that exciting, Pengfei," Chen Xuan smiled, slinging his canvas bag over his shoulder. "My wife has a family birthday trip tonight, and I promised to carry her suitcase."

Before Zhang Pengfei could make another joke, senior clerk Pan Xiulan nudged his elbow and pointed toward the window overlooking the street corner below. "Stop talking nonsense, Pengfei! Look downstairs. That black Maybach is parked by the camphor trees again!"

Chen Xuan waved his green thermos cheerfully at his stunned coworkers and took the elevator down to the lobby.

One block south on the shaded avenue, Qin Yuhan sat in the back seat of her black Maybach S680 wearing an elegant cream wool coat over a tailored navy dress. Fifty meters behind her car, an unmarked black sedan carrying a four-man team of the Black Order sat quietly at the curb, keeping its permanent watch.

As soon as Chen Xuan opened the car door and slid onto the soft leather seat beside her, he heard a sharp, anxious woman's voice coming from the phone in Qin Yuhan's hand.

"Yuhan, listen to your mother just this once!" Lin Shuyin pleaded over the line from Beijing. "Tomorrow is your grandfather Elder Qin Zhengguo's seventy-eighth birthday! Half the senior families in Beijing and three retired ministry directors will be sitting in our courtyard. Even Young Master Gu Mingzhe from the Capital Asset Recovery Office is coming to pay his respects!"

Qin Yuhan pinched the bridge of her nose, her cool expression stiffening. "Mom, Chen Xuan and I are already on our way to Hongqiao Airport."

"You actually brought him?" Lin Shuyin gasped. "Yuhan, have you lost your mind? When your father and I came back from Shanghai on Monday, your second aunt and third uncle kept asking about your new husband. How am I supposed to introduce a twelve-thousand-yuan logistics clerk who carries a ten-yuan green thermos in front of Beijing's top circle? At least leave him at Emerald Riverside Villa for the weekend and tell your grandfather his shipping office had overtime!"

Sitting next to Qin Yuhan, Chen Xuan reached over, took a warm hand-warmer pouch from his canvas bag, and tucked it gently into Qin Yuhan's free hand.

Qin Yuhan curled her fingers around the warm pouch, looked at Chen Xuan's calm, unbothered smile, and spoke into the receiver with quiet firmness.

"Mom, Chen Xuan never works overtime past four fifty-five, and I am not lying to Grandfather," Qin Yuhan said evenly. "He is my husband. If the Qin courtyard in Beijing has no chair for him tomorrow, then I will turn around at the airport right now and celebrate Grandfather's birthday by sending a video call from Shanghai."

"You stubborn girl!" Lin Shuyin sputtered on the other end. "Your grandfather has been asking to see you all week! Fine, bring him if you insist on embarrassing me, but at least tell him not to bring that chipped green jug into the main hall!"

The call clicked off.

Qin Yuhan lowered her phone and let out a small sigh, looking at the green enamel thermos resting beside Chen Xuan's knee. "My mother has been calling me every two hours since lunch. Are you nervous about meeting my grandfather tomorrow? Elder Qin Zhengguo spent forty years in the northern military command. Everyone in the family is terrified of his temper."

"Why would I be nervous?" Chen Xuan chuckled, tapping the small porcelain jar inside his canvas bag. "He is a seventy-eight-year-old grandfather who loves his granddaughter, not a tiger. Besides, I brought him a quarter pound of spring tea from my Second Uncle Chen Mingde's friend in Yunnan. Old soldiers always like a strong cup of red tea after lunch."

Qin Yuhan looked at the plain white porcelain jar with no brand label on it and could not help laughing softly. "My cousins in Beijing spend months bidding on antique jade and hundred-thousand-yuan tea bricks for his birthday, and you are bringing an unlabeled jar from your uncle's friend?"

"Tea is for drinking, not for framing on the wall," Chen Xuan said easily, lacing his warm fingers through hers as the Maybach merged onto the elevated road toward Hongqiao Airport. "Wait until your grandfather tastes it tomorrow. He will like my jar better than those dusty bricks."

Thirty minutes later, at the first-class lounge of Hongqiao Airport, Chen Xuan happily ate two small plates of complimentary walnut pastries while Qin Yuhan reviewed a final shipping report from Haiyue Group on her tablet.

At the same time in Beijing, inside the quiet study of the central leadership compound, Premier Gu Chengshan and Elder Song stood over a financial map provided by Number 1 of the White Order. With the 300 trillion yuan sovereign fund anchoring China Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China, and Sha Ruijin, Li Dakang, Hou Liangping, and Ji Changming wrapping up the remaining asset transfers in Handong Province, the national treasury was stronger than it had been in decades. Neither Premier Gu nor Elder Song had any idea that the unseen master of the White Order and Black Order was currently boarding a regular evening flight to Beijing in the window seat next to his wife.

As the cabin climbed above the glowing evening skyline of Shanghai, Qin Yuhan rested her head against Chen Xuan's shoulder and closed her eyes.

"Wake me up when we land in Beijing," she murmured softly.

"Sleep well, President Qin," Chen Xuan whispered, pulling a soft wool airline blanket up to her chin and opening his history book under the dim reading light.
