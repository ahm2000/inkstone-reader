# Chapter 188: The Northern Freight Order

Afternoon on the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics always moved at a slower pace. The warm air from the ceiling vents hummed steadily, keyboards clicked in a soft rhythm, and half the operations team nursed cups of strong tea to fight off post-lunch sleepiness.

Chen Xuan sat comfortably at his corner desk, his green thermos within arm's reach, working through a thick folder of December cross-regional dispatch manifests that Sister Pan Xiulan had dropped off for routine compliance checking. Most of the paperwork was completely standard winter freight: thermal jackets headed for Harbin, industrial steel bearings bound for Shenyang, and boxed medical supplies scheduled for Changchun.

He stamped each verified manifest neatly and placed it in the outbound tray. Near the very bottom of the folder, however, his hand paused over a thin blue transfer sheet.

It was a private priority freight order routed through a subcontracted northern carrier. Hengxin was only handling the initial Yangtze Delta consolidation paperwork before handing the sealed crates off to a private flatbed convoy heading north into Jilin Province.

On the top summary line, the cargo was declared as "forestry surveying equipment and timber drying components," addressed to an obscure private logging station near the eastern ridge of Changbai Mountain.

Chen Xuan leaned back slightly in his chair, tapping his black ballpoint pen gently against the lid of his thermos. His expression stayed relaxed and half-bored, yet his eyes sharpened as he scanned the numbers on page two.

Something about the math did not sit right.

First, timber drying fans and optical surveying tripods were bulky and relatively light. Yet these fourteen wooden crates carried an average gross weight of nearly 1,400 kilograms each, packed into compact reinforced boxes lined with lead-grade moisture sheeting. That density belonged to heavy industrial smelting parts or dense chemical drums, not forestry tools.

He flipped quietly to the third page, where a subcontracted customs and safety addendum sat stapled in tiny gray print. Buried under vague "winter soil stabilization" codes were commercial-scale quantities of sodium cyanide reagents, borax flux, and high-temperature graphite crucible liners.

You did not need sodium cyanide and graphite crucibles to dry pine timber in the snow. You used them for one specific purpose: extracting and refining raw gold ore on an industrial scale.

Chen Xuan's gaze dropped to the bottom right corner of the settlement sheet. The freight fee had been wired through a Hong Kong trade brokerage with an alphanumeric clearing code he recognized instantly. It belonged to the same coastal shell network that had surfaced briefly during the southern port audits, a quiet financial channel tied to Kou Zhenshan's northern mining interests.

A deep mountain valley in Changbai Mountain in the middle of December, closed off by heavy snow and far from regular inspection roads, supplied with unregistered refining chemicals disguised as logging parts. Whoever built that site had chosen the winter freeze on purpose so no hikers, park rangers, or casual drones would wander nearby.

"Hey, Chen Xuan, are you asleep with your eyes open?"

Hu Dewei's voice broke the quiet. He had strolled past with a fresh mug of instant coffee, still looking faintly embarrassed from Sister Pan's morning teasing and eager to find someone else slacking off.

Chen Xuan blinked slowly, letting out a natural, unhurried yawn. "Almost, Old Hu. The afternoon heater on this side of the room is too warm. Makes my eyelids heavy."

"Well, do not let that 20,000-yuan bonus go to your head," Hu Dewei muttered, though his tone lacked any real bite now. "Finish those manifests before 4:30 PM or Sister Pan will lecture both of us."

"Right away," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

Once Hu Dewei wandered back to his own cubicle, Chen Xuan casually slid his single regular smartphone out of his trouser pocket. Resting his forearm naturally on the desk as if checking a personal message, he angled the lens cleanly and snapped two sharp photos: one of the main waybill with the crate weights and chemical codes, and a second showing the subcontracted carrier's GPS handover coordinates outside Baishan, Jilin.

He opened a quiet encrypted chat thread on the exact same phone, attached both images, and typed a single short line.

"Check where these crates actually unload. Look closely at the eastern ridge."

He tapped send, closed the thread, and locked the screen. The entire motion took less than fifteen seconds. Across the office, Sister Pan Xiulan was chatting about supermarket tangerines, and two junior clerks were arguing over weekend football scores. Nobody noticed a thing.

Chen Xuan picked up his rubber verification stamp, pressed it neatly into the red ink pad, and stamped the routine office copy as cleared for standard transit. If he flagged or delayed the shipment from a Shanghai logistics desk, the northern handlers would smell trouble immediately and scatter before anyone saw what they were hiding in the mountains. Letting the crates roll straight to their destination was the cleanest way to follow the trail to the front door.

He tucked the completed folder into the outbound basket, took another slow sip of warm jasmine tea from his green thermos, and watched the winter sunlight slant pale orange across the glass towers outside.

At 4:55 PM on the dot, he shut down his computer, slipped into his dark wool jacket, wrapped Qin Yuhan's charcoal scarf around his neck, and walked out with the evening crowd. Tonight was lamb hotpot night, and he still needed to stop by the neighborhood market for fresh cabbage and tofu.
