# Chapter 188: The Northern Freight Order

By two o'clock, the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics had settled back into its steady rhythm.

True to his word, Chen Xuan had brought up twenty-two cups of warm brown-sugar pearl milk tea from downstairs.

"Brother Chen, sorry to dump another stack on you," Zheng Qian said, dropping a pile of manila folders beside Chen Xuan's keyboard. "Shanghai Port just cleared the afternoon batch of northern winter freight. General Manager Sun wants the stamps verified before five so the night trucks roll out on time."

"No problem," Chen Xuan said calmly, setting his mug aside. "Winter freight always piles up before December blizzards hit the northeast."

"Tell me about it," Pan Xiulan sighed across the aisle. "Every year, northern factories wait until highways ice over, then scream that everything is an emergency."

Chen Xuan smiled lightly and got to work.

The first six folders were completely ordinary: cotton rolls for Jinan, auto parts for Tianjin, meat compressors for Harbin.

Then he opened the seventh folder.

His pen stopped half an inch above the paper.

Chen Xuan did not frown, and he did not stiffen. Anyone glancing over from the next desk would have thought he was squinting at a smudged port stamp. Only the sudden stillness in his dark eyes showed something on the page had caught his full attention.

The cover sheet looked plain enough at first glance. It was labeled as a routine winter transit order crossing Shanghai Port from a private bonded yard near Yangshan, headed north by six heavy flatbed trucks toward the foothills of Changbai Mountain in Jilin Province.

Chen Xuan read the cargo list line by line.

Six German-made deep-bore mining drills packed in heavy waterproof crates. Four industrial cold-weather diesel generators rated to run nonstop at minus forty degrees. Twenty tons of reinforced steel casing. And tucked quietly on the third page, three sealed wooden crates listed as high-pressure chemical refining valves.

Chen Xuan's eyes lingered on the description. The shipper had checked the box for "Ordinary Forestry and Winter Road Maintenance." Nobody needed six deep-bore mining drills and chemical refining valves to clear snow off a forestry road.

Slowly, his gaze dropped to the bottom right box of the freight sheet, where the receiving company's full name stood printed in neat black ink.

Beishan Timber and Cold-Chain Trading Co., Ltd.

Below it sat a rush-fee clearance stamp paid at triple the standard winter rate, settled not through one of the big banks, but through a regional transfer routed out of an offshore trading account in Hong Kong.

Chen Xuan's memory never slipped.

Only the day before, in the living room at Tomson Riviera, Number 11 had laid a thick white-sealed file across the pale marble table. That file held every northern shell company, dormant warehouse, and border logistics route linked to the Seven-Harbor Consortium over the past ten years.

Right in the middle of page fourteen of Number 11's Tomson Riviera file, circled in faint gray ink as a long-dormant northern front near Changbai Mountain, stood those exact words.

Beishan Timber and Cold-Chain Trading Co., Ltd.

Word for word. Letter for letter.

Qin Yuhan's words from lunch behind Jin Mao Tower echoed quietly in Chen Xuan's mind. Someone had been paying triple rush fees at Shanghai Port since Friday night for cold-weather flatbeds heading north.

Chen Xuan leaned back slightly in his creaky chair, his face completely relaxed, and tapped a few keys on his computer to pull up the matching Hengxin Logistics dispatch schedule.

According to the dispatch log, the six heavy flatbed trucks were already loaded and sealed inside Shanghai Port's northern holding yard. They were scheduled to clear the port gate at five o'clock tomorrow morning, push straight up the expressways with rotating drivers, and arrive at a gravel transfer yard on the northern slope of Changbai Mountain by late Wednesday night.

Even more telling was the freight broker who had got a Yangshan gate pass for the folder without opening a single crate. Chen Xuan recognized the agency immediately. Before Shenzhen Pier Nine fell apart, that exact broker had handled overflow shipping paperwork for Seven-Harbor's southern front companies.

"Hey, Brother Chen, you alright?" Zheng Qian called out lazily from the next desk, spinning a pen between his fingers. "Is the seventh folder missing a port gate seal again? That Yangshan yard always forgets page three."

Chen Xuan glanced up with an easy smile. "No, the stamp is there. Just a little faded. I was checking the weight limit on the flatbed axles so they do not get fined at the Jiangsu highway checkpoint."

"Good catch," Zheng Qian chuckled, turning back to his monitor. "You really earned that twenty thousand yuan bonus."

Chen Xuan looked back down at the folder.

For two seconds, he weighed his choices. With one call, he could freeze all six flatbeds inside Shanghai Port before sunrise tomorrow. Port police would open the crates, seize the German drills and refining valves, and arrest six hired truck drivers who knew nothing beyond a delivery address.

And the moment those trucks failed to appear on the highway, whoever was hiding deep in the snows of Changbai Mountain would know the trap had sprung. They would burn their ledgers, pack up whatever they were hiding, and vanish across the northern border before anyone could trace a clue.

A quiet, cool gleam passed through Chen Xuan's dark eyes.

You do not spook a fox while it is still carrying food back to its den. You let it walk right home, and you follow its footprints in the snow.

Calmly, Chen Xuan picked up his red Hengxin Logistics ink stamp and pressed a crisp approval mark onto the corner of the Beishan Timber order.

Before sliding the folder into his finished tray, he took a plain sticky note and jotted down four lines in neat, tiny handwriting: the six truck plate numbers, the container seal codes, the Yangshan dispatch time, and the exact road marker listed for the northern Changbai Mountain drop-off.

He folded the slip of paper once and tucked it cleanly into the inner pocket of his dark-blue windbreaker.

By five-thirty, the sky outside the twelfth-floor windows had faded into chilly gray dusk. Chen Xuan finished the last folder, stacked the tray neatly on Zheng Qian's desk, and washed out his porcelain mug.

"Heading home, Brother Chen?" Pan Xiulan asked warmly by the elevator. "Buy yourself a nice dinner tonight to celebrate your bonus!"

"I will," Chen Xuan smiled easily, slinging his canvas bag over one shoulder. "Maybe I will add an extra egg to my noodles."

Out on the busy Shanghai street, Chen Xuan walked unhurriedly toward the subway station, blending into the evening crowd.

The Seven-Harbor Consortium thought they could quietly slip equipment out of Shanghai Port under a routine winter order and finish their northern business in the snow.

They had just picked the wrong desk to send their paperwork across.
