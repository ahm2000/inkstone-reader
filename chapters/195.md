# Chapter 195: Heading North to Changbai

The next morning, crisp winter sunlight spilled through the frosted kitchen windows of Emerald Riverside Villa. Inside, the house was warm and smelled of millet porridge, pan-fried scallion pancakes, and fresh ginger tea.

Chen Xuan stood at the kitchen counter in a soft charcoal sweater, sliding a golden pancake onto a ceramic plate. Beside the foyer bench sat a small canvas duffel bag and his green thermos, already filled with hot jasmine tea.

Light footsteps sounded on the stairs. Qin Yuhan came down wearing a cream wool coat, with the new cashmere scarf he had bought on Nanjing Road looped neatly around her neck.

"This scarf is ridiculously soft," Qin Yuhan said, wrapping her arms around his waist from behind for a warm hug. "Where did you find cashmere this good on a rainy Friday night?"

"An old shop tucked off the main square," Chen Xuan grinned, handing her a steaming bowl of porridge. "Eat while it is hot. You have that charity brunch at 10:00 AM, right?"

Qin Yuhan sat at the kitchen counter and took a spoonful before spotting the duffel bag by the front door. Her brows knit lightly.

"You packed a bag? I thought you were sleeping in today."

"Change of plans," Chen Xuan said easily, pouring himself ginger tea. "My department manager at Hengxin Logistics called last night. A northern transit warehouse up near Jilin has a year-end inventory gap, and winter snow is slowing down the audit team. Nobody else wanted to fly north on a Saturday morning, so I volunteered to sign off the sheets."

Qin Yuhan puffed her cheeks in playful annoyance. "Hengxin barely pays you enough to cover groceries, and they still send you into freezing snow on a weekend? Your manager takes advantage of how easygoing you are."

"It is just one night," Chen Xuan chuckled, tapping the tip of her nose. "The company covers the flight and a warm hotel. I will be back Sunday afternoon. Pick up some sliced lamb and Napa cabbage tomorrow, and I will make lamb hot pot for dinner."

Her expression softened at once. "Deal. If you miss Sunday hot pot, I am locking you out on the balcony."

At the front door, Qin Yuhan unzipped his winter coat pocket, tucked two self-heating hand-warmer packets inside, and straightened his collar.

"Text me the moment you land," she murmured, rising onto her toes to kiss him softly on the lips. "And drink hot water."

"Yes, my lady," Chen Xuan smiled, kissing her forehead. "See you tomorrow evening."

He picked up his duffel bag and thermos and stepped out into the crisp December air. Near the iron villa gate, a cheerful neighborhood auntie walking a fluffy white terrier waved at him with a warm smile.

"Heading out early, Xuan?" she called out comfortably. "The morning radio says heavy snow up north! Keep your neck covered!"

"Will do, Auntie! Have a nice walk!" Chen Xuan called back with a relaxed wave.

A plain black sedan waited outside the gate. Forty minutes later, the car rolled past a discreet barrier onto a private executive tarmac on the western edge of Shanghai.

Cold December wind swept across the wet concrete. Parked near the far hangar sat a sleek white long-range jet, its twin engines humming with a steady vibration.

Standing at the foot of the boarding stairs was a tall man in a clean dark wool overcoat and plain leather shoes, looking entirely like a composed private aviation director.

As Chen Xuan stepped out of the sedan, Number 7 bowed respectfully at the waist.

"Good morning, Young Master," Number 7 said clearly against the winter breeze. "The flight corridor to Changbai Mountain is cleared. Local weather shows twelve centimeters of fresh snow on the ground and falling steadily."

"Good morning, Number 7," Chen Xuan replied easily, climbing the carpeted steps into the warm cabin. "Let us hope the runway plow up there earned its breakfast."

Inside the cabin, Chen Xuan settled into a cream leather seat by the window and poured a cup of hot jasmine tea from his thermos.

His phone chimed softly. First came a text from Qin Yuhan with a photo of her coffee cup: Just finished breakfast. Don't freeze up there!

Chen Xuan smiled and typed back: Boarding now. Save your appetite for Sunday hot pot.

A second, silent message sat beneath it on the same screen, routed cleanly through his private channel. Shanghai State Security had already cracked Kieran Cole's Macau escrow trail, linking the hit directly to Kou Zhenshan and Victor Leung. Both men had slipped out of Hong Kong (+8:00 GMT) before dawn, flying north to a private snowbound mountain resort at the foot of Changbai Mountain where Seven-Harbor kept its final offshore clearing servers and master ledgers.

Number 7 stepped smoothly to the cabin divider, waiting until Chen Xuan looked up.

"Young Master," Number 7 said respectfully. "Number 1 reports that the Hong Kong financial freeze is ready on your word, and the northern roads outside Changbai are quiet. Should we signal Number 1 to close the southern net now, or wait until you reach the resort?"

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of jasmine tea. Outside the window, the white jet turned onto the main runway, its engines swelling into a smooth roar as it accelerated and lifted effortlessly into the pale December sky.

"Let Victor Leung think he made it to safety for a few more hours," Chen Xuan said with a quiet, relaxed smile. "It is easier to clean the whole table when every piece sits in one warm room. Tell Number 1 we wrap everything up tonight. I promised my wife lamb hot pot tomorrow evening, and I am not going to be late for dinner."

"Understood, Young Master," Number 7 bowed once and stepped back toward the cockpit.

Far below the rising white wings, Shanghai shrank into a neat grid of silver rivers and winter mist as the jet turned north toward the snow-covered peaks of Changbai Mountain.
