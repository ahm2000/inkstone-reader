# Chapter 195: Heading North to Changbai

Saturday morning dawned bright and washed clean over Shanghai.

Last night's rain had swept the gray haze from the sky, leaving the air crisp and cool as pale gold sunlight glinted off the glass towers of Lujiazui in the far distance.

At seven-thirty in the morning, a matte-white sedan rolled smoothly past the private security gate of Shanghai Hongqiao Executive Aviation Terminal without slowing down. The tarmac guards snapped to attention and waved the car straight across the apron toward a secluded hangar bay.

The rear door opened, and Chen Xuan stepped out onto the damp concrete.

He still looked nothing like a man who owned a private jet. He wore a clean charcoal winter coat over a dark sweater, plain dark trousers, and sturdy walking shoes. On his way to the airport, he had even asked the driver to stop for two minutes outside a corner courier shop so he could mail Chen Hao's college-prep math books to Yunxi County in time for Monday morning.

In his left hand sat his familiar scratched green metal thermos, freshly filled with hot jasmine tea.

Waiting at the foot of the silver boarding stairs stood a sleek, long-range pearl-white private jet, its spotless wings catching the early morning sun. Beside the stairs stood Number 7, dressed in a sharp dark overcoat with a small matte-white lapel pin gleaming neatly against his collar. He held a slim white-sealed folder tucked under one arm.

"Morning, sir," Number 7 greeted with a respectful nod and a faint, easy smile. "The cabin is warmed up, the flight plan north is cleared straight through, and the northern runway at Changbai Mountain has already been swept of fresh snow."

"Morning, Number 7," Chen Xuan said calmly, glancing up at the pearl-white fuselage. "Did you sleep at all last night, or did you stay up watching Hong Kong?"

Number 7 chuckled softly as he followed Chen Xuan up the carpeted boarding stairs. "A little of both, sir. Mostly watching Victor Leung and Hao Zhenhai lose their tempers over their private phone lines."

They stepped into the quiet, sunlit cabin. Soft cream leather seats lined both sides of the aisle, and a fresh pot of boiled water sat ready on the side credenza, though Chen Xuan preferred his own green thermos. He settled comfortably into his favorite window seat and set the thermos on the polished folding table.

The heavy cabin door hissed shut behind them. Within minutes, the twin engines hummed into a smooth, low vibration, and the pearl-white jet began rolling smoothly toward the main runway.

Number 7 took the seat across the aisle and opened his white-sealed folder.

"Quick update on last night near Nanjing Road, sir," Number 7 said evenly. "Shanghai State Security brought all four foreign killers in before ten-thirty. Kieran Cole, Viktor Orlov, Tomasz Novak, and Rico Alvarez. None of them suffered permanent injuries, just bruised ribs and very sore necks. Captain Zhou Ming's team traced their flight tickets and Macau wire transfer straight back to Victor Leung and Hao Zhenhai's penthouse in Central, Hong Kong."

Chen Xuan unscrewed the cap of his scratched green thermos, letting the fragrant steam of hot jasmine tea curl upward into the cool cabin air. He took a slow, relaxed sip.

"8 million yuan for a four-man crew," Chen Xuan remarked mildly, his tone almost amused. "They must really be running out of ideas in Hong Kong."

"More than running out, sir. They are panicking," Number 7 replied, his eyes sharp. "Our scouts from the White Order spotted their northern patrol teams doubling the guards around the Changbai Mountain vault before dawn. Victor Leung even ordered their mountain supervisor to prepare trucks to haul out the gold bars and the old ledgers if the weather breaks."

"They will not get a single crate off that mountain," Chen Xuan said quietly.

At that moment, the pearl-white jet turned onto the open runway at Shanghai Hongqiao Airport. The engines surged cleanly, pressing both men gently back into the cream leather seats. Seconds later, the landing gear lifted off the tarmac, and the jet climbed steeply into the clear Saturday sky.

Through the oval cabin window, Shanghai unfolded below in crisp morning light. Far to the east across the curving silver ribbon of the Huangpu River, the tall spheres of the Oriental Pearl Tower, the soaring spiral of Shanghai Tower, and the tiered spire of Jin Mao Tower shone brightly before slipping beneath a thin veil of white clouds.

The jet banked smoothly onto a straight northern heading toward Changbai Mountain in Jhengbai.

Fifteen minutes later, once the aircraft leveled off at cruising altitude above a bright sea of clouds, a matte-white satellite phone resting on the cabin console chimed twice with a soft, clear tone.

Number 7 picked up the white handset, glanced at the caller line, and handed it respectfully across the table.

"It is Number 1, sir."

Chen Xuan accepted the white phone and held it to his ear while looking out at the sunlit clouds. "Go ahead, Number 1."

Over the crystal-clear cabin line, Number 1's steady, composed voice came through from the command room.

"Sir, all preparations along the northeast border are complete," Number 1 reported calmly. "I spoke with Beijing's State Security liaison twenty minutes ago. They have sealed every port and airport exit watchlist tied to the Seven-Harbor Consortium from Hong Kong to Macau. Victor Leung, Marcus Vance, and Hao Zhenhai cannot leave the harbor without triggering an immediate arrest warrant."

Chen Xuan nodded slightly, resting his elbow on the window armrest. "And the mountain roads around Changbai Mountain?"

"General Qin Zhengguo's northern garrison moved into position at five this morning," Number 1 answered smoothly. "Under the cover of routine early-winter mountain drills, General Qin Zhengguo placed checkpoints on every highway, logging track, and rail spur within forty kilometers of the Changbai Mountain vault. Not even a snowmobile can leave Jhengbai without passing his soldiers."

"What about the banks?" Chen Xuan asked, taking another slow sip from his green thermos.

"All Four National Banks just signed off on the final freeze," Number 1 replied, a quiet note of satisfaction entering his voice. "China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank have locked the last offshore transit accounts tied to the Seven-Harbor Consortium's gold brokers. Right now, Beijing's State Security, General Qin Zhengguo's northern garrison, and the heads of all Four National Banks are watching the northeast border."

Number 1 paused for half a breath.

"Everyone is waiting for one signal, sir. They are waiting for the Seven-Harbor Consortium's final fortress on Changbai Mountain to fall."

Across the aisle, Number 7 watched Chen Xuan quietly, sensing the calm, unstoppable weight in the cabin.

Outside the oval window, the sky ahead had already turned a deeper, colder northern blue, stretching toward the distant snow-capped ridges of Changbai Mountain.

Chen Xuan gazed out across the bright sea of white clouds, his reflection calm and steady in the glass. He lowered his scratched green thermos lightly onto the table and spoke into the matte-white phone in a voice as ordinary and unhurried as if he were planning a weekend dinner.

"Tell Number 12 and the northern team to stay out of sight until we land," Chen Xuan said softly. "And tell Beijing and General Qin Zhengguo to keep their tea warm. We are ending the Seven-Harbor Consortium before Sunday night."

He ended the call and handed the white phone back to Number 7.

High above the clouds, the pearl-white private jet sliced cleanly northward through the cold morning air, carrying an ordinary Shanghai clerk straight toward the snowy fortress waiting in Changbai Mountain.
