# Chapter 213: Port Terminal Six

By 6:15 PM, a freezing December fog had rolled in off the East China Sea, wrapping Pudong Port Terminal Six in damp white mist. Towering yellow gantry cranes loomed like silent giants above the berth, their amber floodlights glowing faintly against the dark water.

Tied tight to Berth Four, the fully loaded cargo vessel Haiyue Star sat motionless. Its blue containers were stacked eight high, its engine crew stood ready, and two harbor tugboats idled patiently fifty yards off the bow, waiting for a departure clearance that refused to come.

Inside the heated port inspection office overlooking the pier, the glass windows were beaded with cold condensation. Captain Lu leaned back in his swivel chair, pouring steaming oolong tea into two white paper cups. Across the scarred metal desk sat Qian Weidong, the regional operations director of Tianhai International, his cashmere coat draped neatly over the chair back.

"Drink up, Director Qian," Captain Lu said with a smug chuckle, sliding a cup across the desk. "It is brutal out on the concrete tonight. Let Haiyue Group's little shipping manager freeze by the gangway while we stay warm."

Qian Weidong took a slow sip and glanced at the bright red detention stamp sitting beside Captain Lu's elbow. "Are you sure the paperwork will hold up if Haiyue sends their lawyers down here tomorrow morning?"

"Relax," Captain Lu waved a plump hand dismissively. "I cited a suspected ballast water valve leak and an unverified pressure gauge log. Under maritime safety rules, I have full authority to hold any outbound hull for a seventy-two-hour technical review. Even if the Haiyue Star has the cleanest engine room in Asia, nobody can override my safety hold until Monday morning at the earliest."

Qian Weidong checked his watch. It was nearly 6:30 PM. "Good. The ship has to clear the outer channel before 9:00 PM to catch the tide. Once they miss that window, their European client triggers the 80 million yuan penalty clause automatically. By Monday, Haiyue Group will be begging for credit, and Tianhai International will walk right back into that export contract."

Captain Lu smiled, tapping the pocket of his navy uniform jacket. "For 500k yuan, I always deliver clean results. Let them sweat in the fog."

Fifteen miles away, the warmth inside Emerald Riverside Villa felt like another world.

At 7:10 PM, the front door clicked open. Qin Yuhan stepped into the entryway, bringing a gust of cold river mist with her. Her camel wool coat was damp across the shoulders, and her normally composed face looked pale and drawn beneath the warm foyer lights. She slipped off her heels, set her leather handbag on the bench, and let out a long, tired breath while her phone buzzed nonstop against her palm.

From the open kitchen came the rich, comforting aroma of simmering winter melon, tender pork ribs, and fresh ginger.

Chen Xuan walked out wearing a soft gray sweater and a faded cotton apron, carrying a steaming clay pot toward the dining table. His dented 15-yuan green metal thermos sat neatly beside two ceramic bowls, right where he had left it after clocking out of his twelfth-floor desk at Hengxin Logistics at 4:55 PM.

One glance at his wife's tight shoulders told him everything. He set the clay pot down on a wooden trivet, walked over, and gently helped her out of her damp coat.

"Long evening at Haiyue Tower?" Chen Xuan asked softly, handing her a warm damp towel.

Qin Yuhan pressed the hot towel to her face for a quiet moment, letting the steam ease the ache behind her eyes. "It is not the office, Xuan. It is Terminal Six. Someone is holding our ship."

Chen Xuan guided her to the dining chair, pulled it out for her, and ladled a generous bowl of clear, fragrant winter melon and sparerib soup. "Drink two spoonfuls while it is hot. Whatever happened at the dock will still be there after you warm up."

Qin Yuhan picked up the porcelain spoon and took a sip. The sweet, savory broth slid down her throat, melting the December chill out of her bones. Slowly, the tightness in her voice began to loosen, and she told him the whole story.

"Tianhai International's Qian Weidong lost our European export contract last month," she said, setting her spoon down briefly as her phone lit up with another anxious message from the thirty-eighth floor crisis team. "Now a maritime inspection officer named Captain Lu has slapped a bogus ballast violation on the Haiyue Star at Terminal Six. My engineering chief checked the valves twice, and there is nothing wrong with the ship. It is a blatant setup."

"How much time do you have?" Chen Xuan asked calmly, placing a tender rib into her bowl.

"Less than two hours," Qin Yuhan murmured, her fingers tightening around her ceramic spoon. "If the Haiyue Star does not clear the harbor before 9:00 PM, we lose the tide, pay an 80 million yuan penalty, and lose a major client we spent two years winning. Our legal director says a formal appeal through the port bureau takes at least three days. By then, the damage is permanent."

She looked up at him, forcing a small, apologetic smile. "Sorry for bringing work gloom to the dinner table. Your soup is really good tonight."

Chen Xuan stepped behind her chair and rested his warm hands gently on her shoulders, kneading out the stiff knots with easy, unhurried pressure.

"Why apologize to your own husband?" he said lightly. "Finish your soup, go upstairs, and take a long hot shower. Bad luck never survives past dinner in this house. Sometimes greedy people trip over their own shoelaces right when they think they have won."

Qin Yuhan leaned back against his hands, letting out a soft laugh despite her worry. "You always make everything sound so simple, Xuan. If only the port worked like your logistics spreadsheet."

"Trust me," Chen Xuan smiled, refilling her bowl. "Eat up first."

Looking at her relaxed, warm-hearted husband in his faded apron, Qin Yuhan felt her racing pulse slow down. She finished the soup, kissed his cheek gratefully, and headed upstairs to wash away the winter chill, never once guessing that the quiet man clearing her bowl was about to bring the storm down on Terminal Six.
