# Chapter 97: The 4:55 Slacker on the Twelfth Floor

The elevator doors chimed open at Hengxin Logistics in Lujiazui, greeting Chen Xuan with the familiar hum of printers and the morning clatter of keyboards.

He strolled out at his usual unhurried pace, carrying his dented green metal thermos in his right hand and the red plastic bag with his mother's glass jar of pickled chilies in his left. Stopping beside the glass entrance, he pressed his thumb calmly against the wall scanner.

"Attendance recorded," the machine chirped, flashing 8:56 AM on its tiny blue screen.

"Look who finally came back from Handong Province!" Pan Xiulan called out from her desk near the pantry, grinning widely. "Chen Xuan, if you had arrived four minutes later, Supervisor Hu Dewei would have fined you 50 yuan without blinking."

"Four minutes is plenty of time to walk from the elevator and pour a cup of hot water, Sister Pan," Chen Xuan replied cheerfully. He walked over and set the heavy glass jar of bright red pickled chilies right beside her desk calendar. "Fresh from my mom's kitchen in Yunxi County. She made extra because she heard you liked the last batch."

Pan Xiulan's eyes lit up at once. "Your mother is too kind! Wait until lunch, I am sharing this with everyone at our table."

Across the aisle, young clerk Xiao Zhou leaned over his monitor, sniffing the air playfully. "Brother Xuan, I can smell the garlic and chili through the glass. Save me a spoonful for my rice bowl later!"

"Plenty to go around," Chen Xuan laughed, strolling back to his corner desk near the window.

Through the tall glass panes, the early autumn sun shone brightly across the skyscrapers of Lujiazui, and a cool breeze stirred the golden leaves along the avenue twelve stories below. Chen Xuan plugged in his computer, filled his dented green thermos with hot water from the pantry dispenser, and sat down with a quiet sigh of satisfaction.

Being back at his modest logistics desk felt wonderfully simple. He opened the morning freight dispatch spreadsheet, verified sixteen coastal shipping manifests, and cleared three routine warehouse confirmations in twenty minutes flat. Because he had a sharp eye for numbers, work that took others half a morning barely cost him any effort, leaving him plenty of calm time to sip jasmine tea.

Peace, however, rarely lasted long when Supervisor Hu Dewei was in a sour mood.

Midway through the morning, the glass door of the supervisor's office swung open with a sharp click. Hu Dewei, a balding man in a tight gray shirt whose belt always looked one notch too snug, marched straight toward Chen Xuan's corner carrying a thick stack of pink and white customs forms.

He dropped the folder onto Chen Xuan's desk with a heavy thud.

"Chen Xuan," Hu Dewei said in a stiff, loud voice meant for the whole twelfth floor to hear, "you took several days of personal leave right during our autumn peak. The Ningbo port shipment has been stuck in review since yesterday afternoon. Sort out every single discrepancy on these manifests before lunch. If it is not finished, do not even think about clocking out at 4:55 PM today."

Pan Xiulan frowned from her desk, opening her mouth to protest, because everyone knew the Ningbo shipment belonged to Hu Dewei's own brother-in-law in the dispatch group.

Chen Xuan merely raised a calm hand to stop her, then smiled politely up at Hu Dewei. "No problem at all, Supervisor Hu. Let me take a quick look so we do not delay the client."

He flipped open the thick folder, his gaze gliding cleanly over the columns of container numbers, weights, and tariff codes. Within thirty seconds, his finger stopped on the second page, then the fifth.

"Ah, I see why customs flagged it," Chen Xuan said in a helpful, clear voice that carried easily across the quiet cubicles. "On page two, the container serial code is missing its last two digits. And on page five, the declared weight is listed in pounds instead of kilograms, which makes the shipment look twice as heavy as the truck can legally carry."

Hu Dewei's round face stiffened. "That is why I gave it to you! Stop talking and fix it!"

"I will fix it in five minutes, Supervisor Hu," Chen Xuan replied pleasantly, holding up the bottom of page five so the bright red stamp was clearly visible. "I just wanted to double check with you first, because your personal approval signature is right underneath both mistakes. If I change the figures without your new stamp, the audit team upstairs might think someone altered your signed record."

A muffled snort escaped Xiao Zhou's desk, quickly disguised as a cough. Pan Xiulan lowered her head behind her monitor, her shoulders shaking silently.

Hu Dewei's neck flushed as red as Liu Meifang's pickled chilies. He glared at the stamp, realizing he had just embarrassed himself in front of twenty clerks. "Just print the corrected sheets and bring them to my desk!" he snapped, spinning on his heel and marching back into his glass office with the door clicking shut behind him.

Chen Xuan chuckled softly, typed the two corrections cleanly into the system, printed the updated manifests, and dropped them neatly in Hu Dewei's inbox three minutes later.

When he returned to his desk, his phone buzzed gently on the wooden surface. It was a text message from his mother, Liu Meifang: *Xuan, do not eat instant noodles on your first day back in Shanghai. Walk down to a proper café near your building, get a warm lunch, and try to meet a nice girl!*

Chen Xuan smiled at the screen. Instant noodles were off the menu today. When the morning shift wrapped up for lunch, he grabbed his green thermos and strolled toward the elevator, deciding to visit a quiet coffee shop over near Jin Mao Tower for a pot of green tea and a breath of crisp autumn air.
