# Chapter 97: The Watch Under the Trees

Peace never lasted by accident.

In Qingyan City, tucked behind an unmarked gray brick wall in the eastern district, a quiet courtyard held a single ancient pine tree. Its needles stayed dark green even as early winter frost dusted the roof tiles.

Inside the main hall, a brass desk lamp cast a steady pool of warm light across a long mahogany table.

Number One sat straight in a wooden chair, his silver hair neatly combed, his black wool tunic buttoned to the collar. In front of him lay two leather-bound volumes.

The first was the active roster of the Black Pine Guard. Two hundred and fourteen operatives filled its pages, listed only by numbers, postings, shift rotations, and readiness grades inked in crisp strokes.

Number Seven stood three paces from the desk, hands folded behind his back, his posture like a drawn sword.

"Monthly readiness inspection is complete," Number Seven reported in a low, even voice. "Number Nine and Number Twelve completed the eastern logistics sweep yesterday. Number Nineteen and Number Fifty-six verified the provincial road cameras between Qingyan and Yunxi County. Number Ninety-five and Number One Hundred and Four finished the background audit on every new vendor along Old Street."

Number One turned a page slowly. His finger paused beside a clean column of checkmarks.

"Any anomalies?" Number One asked.

"None," Number Seven replied. "After the autumn cleanings, every gray network in three neighboring cities has pulled back fifty kilometers from Yunxi County. Even petty pickpockets avoid the express bus terminal."

"Good," Number One said calmly. "Complacency is how rust enters iron. Keep every rotation razor-sharp. Never let our people grow sleepy just because the sky looks clear."

"Understood. Number Eleven has already doubled the night drills."

Number One closed the roster and drew the second leather volume closer. Its cover was worn smooth at the corners. This was the gratitude ledger.

Page after page held quiet entries: a village clinic funded without a donor's name, a scholarship paid anonymously for an honest teacher's daughter, a struggling supplier given a fair five-year contract, a county doctor sent to the capital for training.

Every single entry traced back to someone who had once offered a kind word, an umbrella, a fair deal, or a bowl of hot water to Chen Xuan or his family.

"Third-quarter accounts are settled," Number Seven added quietly. "Every debt of kindness on the list has been repaid tenfold, through legal channels, with zero trace back to the Young Master."

Number One rested his palm lightly on the closed ledger. For a brief second, the stern lines around his eyes softened.

"The Young Master remembers every small warmth," Number One murmured. "We make sure those good people never face a cold winter again."

* * *

Sixty kilometers away, steam billowed out of a small noodle shop on Old Street in Yunxi County.

Inside, Number Twenty-three wore a faded blue apron dusted with flour. He gripped a copper ladle with steady, scarred hands and poured rich, fragrant beef broth over a bowl of hand-pulled noodles.

"Extra cilantro, no chili oil," Number Twenty-three called out in a warm, gravelly voice, sliding the bowl across the counter to a bundled-up schoolboy. "Eat slow. The soup is hot."

"Thank you, Uncle!" the boy beamed, paying with a ten-yuan note.

Number Twenty-three wiped the counter with a clean cloth. To every neighbor on Old Street, he was just a dependable widower who opened at six and made the best beef broth in the county.

Yet as he ladled the next bowl, his eyes flicked once through the foggy front window.

A silver van with out-of-town plates rolled slowly past the stone archway.

In two seconds, Number Twenty-three noted the registration number, the weight on the rear axle, and the two men inside wearing matching delivery vests.

He stepped behind the spice rack, tapped three quick characters into a matte-black phone beside the vinegar jars, and returned to his boiling pot before the broth could bubble over.

Four seconds later, a silent green confirmation blinked on the screen. Legitimate county appliance delivery, cleared by Number Ninety-five an hour earlier.

Number Twenty-three nodded once to himself and dropped a fresh handful of noodles into the rolling water.

Across the lane, Chen Mingde laughed with a customer outside Mingde Hardware. Neither man noticed the noodle shop owner watching the street with the calm, unblinking vigilance of a sentinel on a mountain pass.

* * *

Back in Qingyan City, the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics was quiet at half past seven in the morning.

The pale winter sun slanted through tall glass windows, touching thirty empty desks that smelled faintly of lemon floor wax.

Number Fourteen walked down the aisle carrying a plastic watering can and a small toolbox. In his gray maintenance vest and soft-soled shoes, nobody in the building ever looked twice at him.

He stopped beside a corner desk near the window, where a neat stack of shipping folders sat next to a white coaster waiting for a scratched green thermos.

Number Fourteen knelt smoothly beside the desk chair. He checked the power strip beneath the partition, inspected the cord, and clicked the switch on the thin heated cushion resting over the seat.

A tiny amber light glowed softly on the side of the pad. By nine o'clock, the chair would be comfortably warm without ever feeling too hot.

Number Fourteen stood, checked the nearby water dispenser to ensure the heating tank was full and sanitized, and walked away. He left no note and sought no credit.

By eight o'clock, when the first clerks stepped out of the elevator yawning into their coffee cups, Number Fourteen was already two floors down tightening a loose hinge, completely invisible in plain sight.

* * *

Evening settled over Yunxi County like a heavy blue blanket.

In the older residential compound off Willow Lane, warm yellow light glowed behind the curtains of Room 601 in Building 2. The faint clatter of dishes drifted down into the courtyard.

Fifty meters away, beneath the bare branches of winter poplars along the low boundary wall, the shadows looked deeper than the night around them.

No footsteps crunched on dry leaves. No breath misted visibly in the cold air.

Three figures stood motionless in the dusk, clad in featureless matte-black gear from boot to crown. Smooth, blank masks covered their faces completely, catching not even a glint from the distant streetlamp.

They were the permanent Zero Guard team assigned to Chen Xuan's fifty-meter perimeter.

Across the country, men with stars on their shoulders and elders behind red walls knew what those blank masks meant. Wherever Chen Xuan walked, slept, rode a bus, or sat at his desk, that silent ring moved with him, never closer than fifty meters unless the sky fell, and never a single step farther away.

A soft click sounded inside the team leader's concealed earpiece.

A flat, neutral, genderless chilled voice spoke across the encrypted channel.

"Perimeter check. Fifty-meter radius clear. Thermal signatures nominal. Local grid stable."

"Confirmed," replied the second figure in the exact same flat, neutral, genderless chilled voice. "Target is inside Room 601. Family dinner in progress. Zero threats detected."

"Maintain silent watch," the leader answered.

No emotion colored the words.

Fifty meters away, the kitchen window of Room 601 steamed up gently as someone lifted the lid off a pot of simmering soup. Inside that warm yellow square of light sat the quietest sovereign in the country, laughing with his mother over dinner.

And under the winter trees, the shadows stood still, keeping the whole world politely outside the gate.
