# Chapter 54: Monday Morning in Fuxing New Village

At six thirty on Monday morning, while forty national agencies in Beijing scrambled over forty-two biometric dossiers, the small kitchen in Fuxing New Village smelled of ginger, scallions, and warming pork-rib soup.

Chen Xuan stood calmly before the twin-burner gas stove in a faded cotton sweater. He tested six eggs rolling in an aluminum pot, then lowered the flame under the ceramic soup pot so the broth stayed hot without boiling over.

In the narrow doorway behind him, his mother, Liu Meifang, twisted the hem of her apron between restless fingers. Dark circles sat beneath her eyes from a sleepless night.

"Xiao Xuan," Liu Meifang said tightly. "How do you have the heart to stand there peeling ginger? Your Second Uncle Chen Mingde has been locked inside Old Street Station for twelve hours. He did not even take his blood-pressure pills last night."

"I set a small bowl of plain broth aside for him, Mom," Chen Xuan replied easily. "He will be hungry when he gets back, and heavy grease on an empty stomach will bother him."

Liu Meifang stared at her son's relaxed shoulders, frustration catching in her throat. "You keep saying when he gets back! Do you not understand who he offended? Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao work straight for Chairman Deng Yongchang. Half the demolition contracts in Yunxi County belong to Deng Yongchang's company, and Director Sun Weiming drinks tea in his office every week!"

At the folding dining table just outside the kitchen, Second Aunt Wu Lanying let out a stifled sob. She hunched over a chipped porcelain teacup, clutching it with both hands until her knuckles turned white.

"It is my fault," Wu Lanying whispered, her shoulders trembling. "I should have stopped Chen Mingde when Cheng Biao shoved him at the stall. We should have signed Deng Yongchang's compensation paper and taken whatever scraps they offered. Now Officer Zhou Quan wrote up an assault report claiming Mingde swung an iron scale at Cheng Biao's men. If they transfer him to the county detention center this morning, how will Chen Hao and Chen Yuan face anyone?"

"Auntie, do not blame yourself," Chen Xuan said warmly, turning with a clean kitchen towel. "Second Uncle did nothing wrong. Refusing a crooked contract is not a crime."

"Right and wrong does not open an iron cell door, Xiao Xuan," his father, Chen Jianguo, muttered from a low wooden stool near the balcony.

Chen Jianguo leaned forward with his elbows on his knees, smoking a cheap cigarette down to the yellow filter. His fingers shook slightly as he tapped gray ash into a tin lid. He had not slept a minute since midnight.

"Look at the clock," Chen Jianguo said, clearing his throat nervously. "It is past six thirty. In an hour, Old Street Station changes shifts and transfers overnight detainees to the county bureau. Once Director Sun Weiming or Deputy County Magistrate Liang Guodong stamps that file, ordinary families like ours cannot pull a person out even if we sell this apartment."

Liu Meifang hurried to her husband's side, eyes wide with panic. "Jianguo, we still have three thousand yuan in the drawer. Should we buy two cartons of Zhonghua cigarettes at the corner store and walk over to Old Street Station right now? If we catch Officer Zhou Quan before the shift change and beg nicely, maybe he will let Mingde come home."

Wu Lanying lifted her tear-streaked face instantly. "I will go with you, Brother-in-law! I will kneel outside Sun Weiming's office if I have to. As long as Mingde comes out without a criminal record, we will sign Deng Yongchang's demolition notice today."

Chen Jianguo crushed his cigarette into the tin lid, his jaw locking with bitter helplessness. He looked up at Chen Xuan, lowering his voice. "Xiao Xuan, I know you told us last night to stay home and not panic. You have always been steady since starting work in the city. Still, Yunxi County is small, and Deng Yongchang has long arms. Should your mother and I at least walk over to Old Street Station and beg?"

The apartment went quiet except for steam hissing softly from the soup pot. Three pairs of anxious eyes fixed on Chen Xuan.

Chen Xuan did not sigh or raise his voice. He scooped a freshly boiled egg from the aluminum pot, ran it briefly under cold tap water, and cracked the shell cleanly against the counter. In four smooth motions he peeled the egg, walked to the dining table, and pressed it warm into Chen Jianguo's rough palm.

Then Chen Xuan smiled calmly.

"Eat first, Dad," Chen Xuan said, his voice steady and unhurried. "Nobody is going to the station. By nine o'clock, they will bring Second Uncle home themselves."

Chen Jianguo froze, staring at the warm egg in his hand. "Bring him home themselves?" he echoed, eyebrows knotting. "Xiao Xuan, are you feverish? Why would Zhou Quan or Sun Weiming bring your Second Uncle home?"

"Because they cannot afford to keep him," Chen Xuan answered simply, ladling four steaming bowls of pork-rib soup onto the folding table. "Not for another morning, and not for an hour past shift change."

Liu Meifang wrung her hands, torn between exasperation and a sudden hush in her chest. "Xiao Xuan, stop talking in riddles! Did you call some manager from your company in the city? Even a city manager cannot order County Director Sun Weiming around!"

"I did not ask any company manager for favors, Mom," Chen Xuan said lightly, pulling out a chair for Wu Lanying and placing a spoon beside her bowl. "Drink some hot soup while it is fresh, Second Aunt. If you faint before nine o'clock, Second Uncle will scold all of us when he walks in."

Wu Lanying looked down at the fragrant broth, then up into Chen Xuan's calm, unbothered eyes. Something in his total lack of fear slowed the trembling in her fingers. "Xiao Xuan," she asked softly, "do you really mean it? Nobody is going to hurt Mingde?"

"Not a hair on him," Chen Xuan promised quietly.

The back bedroom latch clicked open. Chen Yuan poked her head out rubbing sleep from her eyes, with Chen Hao shuffling behind her in an oversized hoodie.

"Brother Xuan, is that pork-rib soup?" Chen Yuan asked warily, glancing at her mother's red eyes. "Is Dad back yet?"

"Almost," Chen Xuan told his cousin with an easy grin. "Wash your faces, both of you. I am going downstairs to buy hot meat buns and red-bean buns from Wang Deshun's stall before the morning rush cleans him out."

"Xiao Xuan, wait," Chen Jianguo called out, still gripping the peeled egg as doubt and desperate hope fought across his face. "Do you have enough change?"

"Plenty, Dad," Chen Xuan said, slipping on his dark cotton jacket by the door. "Eat your egg while it is warm. I will be back in five minutes."

As the latch clicked shut behind him, Chen Jianguo stared down at the warm egg, wondering where his ordinary son had learned to sound so certain while the sky was falling.
