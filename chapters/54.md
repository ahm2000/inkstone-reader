# Chapter 54: Monday Morning in Fuxing New Village

At six thirty on Monday morning, while forty national departments in Beijing scrambled over forty-two sealed case files, the small kitchen in Fuxing New Village smelled of ginger, green onions, and pork-rib soup.

Chen Xuan stood calmly at the stove in a faded cotton sweater, checking six boiling eggs before lowering the flame under the soup pot.

In the doorway behind him, his mother, Liu Meifang, twisted her apron, dark circles under her eyes from a sleepless night.

"Xiao Xuan," Liu Meifang said tightly. "How can you stand there peeling ginger? Your Second Uncle Chen Mingde has been locked inside Old Street Station since Friday night. He did not even take his blood-pressure pills."

"I set plain broth aside for him, Mom," Chen Xuan answered easily. "Heavy grease on an empty stomach will upset him when he gets back."

Liu Meifang stared at his relaxed shoulders, frustration catching in her throat. "You keep saying when he gets back! Do you not understand who he angered? Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao work straight for Chairman Deng Yongchang. Half the demolition deals in Yunxi County belong to his company, and County Magistrate Sun Weiming drinks tea in his office every week!"

At the folding table outside, Second Aunt Wu Lanying let out a quiet sob, gripping a chipped teacup until her knuckles turned white.

"It is all my fault," Wu Lanying whispered, shoulders shaking. "I should have stopped Chen Mingde when Cheng Biao shoved him at the stall. We should have signed Deng Yongchang's paper. Now Officer Zhou Quan wrote a fake assault report claiming Mingde swung an iron scale at Cheng Biao's men. If they move him to county jail this morning, how will Chen Rui and Chen Lin face anyone?"

"Auntie, do not blame yourself," Chen Xuan said warmly, turning with a towel. "Second Uncle did nothing wrong. Saying no to a crooked deal is not a crime."

"Right and wrong does not open a cell door, Xiao Xuan," his father, Chen Jianguo, muttered from a low stool near the balcony.

Chen Jianguo leaned forward with elbows on his knees, smoking a cheap cigarette down to the filter, fingers shaking slightly as he tapped ash into a tin lid.

"Look at the clock," Chen Jianguo said nervously. "Past six thirty. In an hour, Old Street Station changes shifts and sends overnight prisoners to the county bureau. Once Sun Weiming or Public Security Chief Liang Guodong stamps that file, ordinary families like ours cannot pull a person out even if we sell this apartment."

Liu Meifang hurried to his side, eyes wide with panic. "Jianguo, we still have three thousand yuan in the drawer. Should we buy two cartons of Zhonghua cigarettes and walk over to Old Street Station right now? If we catch Officer Zhou Quan before shift change and beg, maybe he will let Mingde come home."

Wu Lanying lifted her tear-streaked face. "I will go with you, Brother-in-law! I will kneel outside Sun Weiming's office if I have to. As long as Mingde comes out without a criminal record, we will sign Deng Yongchang's demolition paper today."

Chen Jianguo crushed his cigarette out, jaw locking with bitter helplessness. He looked up at Chen Xuan. "Xiao Xuan, I know you told us last night to stay home. You have always been steady since starting work in the city. Still, Yunxi County is small, and Deng Yongchang has long arms. Should your mother and I at least walk over and beg?"

The apartment went quiet except for steam hissing from the soup pot. Three pairs of worried eyes fixed on Chen Xuan.

Chen Xuan did not sigh. He scooped a hot boiled egg out of the pot, ran it under cold water, tapped the shell against the counter, peeled it in four smooth moves, and pressed it warm into Chen Jianguo's palm.

Then Chen Xuan smiled calmly.

"Eat first, Dad," Chen Xuan said, steady and unhurried. "Nobody is going to the station. By nine o'clock, they will bring Second Uncle home themselves."

Chen Jianguo froze, staring at the warm egg. "Bring him home themselves?" he echoed, brows knotting. "Xiao Xuan, do you have a fever? Why on earth would Zhou Quan or Sun Weiming bring your Second Uncle home?"

"Because they cannot afford to keep him," Chen Xuan answered simply, ladling four steaming bowls of soup onto the table. "Not for another morning, and not for one hour past shift change."

Liu Meifang wrung her hands, caught between anger and a strange quiet. "Stop talking in riddles! Did you call some manager from your company in the city? Even a big city manager cannot order County Magistrate Sun Weiming around!"

"I did not ask any company manager for favors, Mom," Chen Xuan said lightly, pulling out a chair for Wu Lanying. "Drink some hot soup, Second Aunt. If you faint before nine o'clock, Second Uncle will scold all of us when he walks in."

Wu Lanying looked up into Chen Xuan's calm, unbothered eyes. Something in his total lack of fear slowed the shaking in her fingers. "Xiao Xuan," she asked softly, "do you really mean it? Nobody is going to hurt Mingde?"

"Not a single hair on him," Chen Xuan promised quietly.

The bedroom door clicked open. Chen Lin poked her head out rubbing sleep from her eyes, Chen Rui shuffling behind her in a big hoodie.

"Brother Xuan, is that pork-rib soup?" Chen Lin asked carefully, glancing at her mother's red eyes. "Is Dad back yet?"

"Almost," Chen Xuan told his cousin with an easy grin. "Go wash your faces. I am going downstairs to buy hot meat buns and red-bean buns from Wang Deshun's stall before the morning crowd cleans him out."

"Xiao Xuan, wait," Chen Jianguo called out, still holding the peeled egg as doubt and hope fought across his face. "Do you have enough change?"

"Plenty, Dad," Chen Xuan said, slipping on his cotton jacket. "Eat your egg while it is warm. I will be back in five minutes."

The door latch clicked shut. Left in the quiet room, Chen Jianguo stared down at the warm egg in his hand, wondering where his ordinary son had learned to sound so completely sure of himself while the sky seemed to be falling.
