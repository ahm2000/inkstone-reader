# Chapter 54: Monday Morning in Fuxing New Village

At six thirty on Monday morning, while forty national departments in Beijing scrambled over forty-two sealed case files, the small kitchen in Fuxing New Village smelled of ginger, green onions, and warm pork-rib soup.

Chen Xuan stood calmly in front of the two-burner gas stove wearing a faded cotton sweater. He checked six eggs rolling in a metal pot, then turned the flame down under the soup pot so the broth stayed hot without boiling over.

In the narrow doorway behind him, his mother, Liu Meifang, twisted the edge of her apron between restless fingers. Dark circles sat under her eyes from a sleepless night.

"Xiao Xuan," Liu Meifang said tightly. "How do you have the heart to stand there peeling ginger? Your Second Uncle Chen Mingde has been locked inside Old Street Station since Friday night. He did not even take his blood-pressure pills last night."

"I set a small bowl of plain broth aside for him, Mom," Chen Xuan answered easily. "He will be hungry when he gets back, and heavy grease on an empty stomach will upset him."

Liu Meifang stared at her son's relaxed shoulders, frustration catching in her throat. "You keep saying when he gets back! Do you not understand who he angered? Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao work straight for Chairman Deng Yongchang. Half the demolition deals in Yunxi County belong to Deng Yongchang's company, and County Magistrate Sun Weiming drinks tea in his office every week!"

At the folding dining table just outside the kitchen, Second Aunt Wu Lanying let out a quiet sob. She hunched over a chipped white teacup, gripping it with both hands until her knuckles turned white.

"It is all my fault," Wu Lanying whispered, her shoulders shaking. "I should have stopped Chen Mingde when Cheng Biao shoved him at the stall. We should have signed Deng Yongchang's payout paper and taken whatever scraps they offered. Now Officer Zhou Quan wrote up a fake assault report claiming Mingde swung an iron scale at Cheng Biao's men. If they move him to the county jail this morning, how will Chen Rui and Chen Lin ever face anyone?"

"Auntie, do not blame yourself," Chen Xuan said warmly, turning with a clean kitchen towel in his hand. "Second Uncle did nothing wrong. Saying no to a crooked deal is not a crime."

"Right and wrong does not open an iron cell door, Xiao Xuan," his father, Chen Jianguo, muttered from a low wooden stool near the balcony.

Chen Jianguo leaned forward with his elbows on his knees, smoking a cheap cigarette down to the yellow filter. His fingers shook slightly as he tapped gray ash into a tin lid. He had not slept a single minute since midnight.

"Look at the clock," Chen Jianguo said, clearing his throat nervously. "It is past six thirty. In an hour, Old Street Station changes shifts and sends overnight prisoners to the county bureau. Once County Magistrate Sun Weiming or Public Security Chief Liang Guodong stamps that file, ordinary families like ours cannot pull a person out even if we sell this apartment."

Liu Meifang hurried to her husband's side, her eyes wide with panic. "Jianguo, we still have three thousand yuan in the drawer. Should we buy two cartons of Zhonghua cigarettes at the corner store and walk over to Old Street Station right now? If we catch Officer Zhou Quan before the shift change and beg nicely, maybe he will let Mingde come home."

Wu Lanying lifted her tear-streaked face at once. "I will go with you, Brother-in-law! I will kneel outside Sun Weiming's office if I have to. As long as Mingde comes out without a criminal record, we will sign Deng Yongchang's demolition paper today."

Chen Jianguo crushed his cigarette into the tin lid, his jaw locking with bitter helplessness. He looked up at Chen Xuan and lowered his voice. "Xiao Xuan, I know you told us last night to stay home and not panic. You have always been steady since starting work in the city. Still, Yunxi County is small, and Deng Yongchang has long arms. Should your mother and I at least walk over to Old Street Station and beg?"

The small apartment went quiet except for steam hissing softly from the soup pot. Three pairs of worried eyes fixed on Chen Xuan.

Chen Xuan did not sigh or raise his voice. He scooped a hot boiled egg out of the pot, ran it briefly under cold tap water, and tapped the shell cleanly against the counter. In four smooth moves he peeled the egg, walked over to the table, and pressed it warm into Chen Jianguo's rough palm.

Then Chen Xuan smiled calmly.

"Eat first, Dad," Chen Xuan said, his voice steady and unhurried. "Nobody is going to the station. By nine o'clock, they will bring Second Uncle home themselves."

Chen Jianguo froze, staring at the warm egg in his hand. "Bring him home themselves?" he echoed, his eyebrows knotting together. "Xiao Xuan, do you have a fever? Why on earth would Zhou Quan or Sun Weiming bring your Second Uncle home?"

"Because they cannot afford to keep him," Chen Xuan answered simply, ladling four steaming bowls of pork-rib soup onto the folding table. "Not for another morning, and not for one hour past shift change."

Liu Meifang wrung her hands, caught between anger and a strange sudden quiet in her chest. "Xiao Xuan, stop talking in riddles! Did you call some manager from your company in the city? Even a big city manager cannot order County Magistrate Sun Weiming around!"

"I did not ask any company manager for favors, Mom," Chen Xuan said lightly, pulling out a chair for Wu Lanying and setting a spoon beside her bowl. "Drink some hot soup while it is fresh, Second Aunt. If you faint before nine o'clock, Second Uncle will scold all of us when he walks through that door."

Wu Lanying looked down at the warm soup, then up into Chen Xuan's calm, unbothered eyes. Something in his total lack of fear slowed the shaking in her fingers. "Xiao Xuan," she asked softly, "do you really mean it? Nobody is going to hurt Mingde?"

"Not a single hair on him," Chen Xuan promised quietly.

The back bedroom door clicked open. Chen Lin poked her head out rubbing sleep from her eyes, with Chen Rui shuffling behind her in a big hoodie.

"Brother Xuan, is that pork-rib soup?" Chen Lin asked carefully, glancing at her mother's red eyes. "Is Dad back yet?"

"Almost," Chen Xuan told his cousin with an easy grin. "Go wash your faces, both of you. I am going downstairs to buy hot meat buns and red-bean buns from Wang Deshun's stall before the morning crowd cleans him out."

"Xiao Xuan, wait," Chen Jianguo called out, still holding the peeled egg as doubt and desperate hope fought across his face. "Do you have enough change?"

"Plenty, Dad," Chen Xuan said, slipping on his dark cotton jacket by the door. "Eat your egg while it is still warm. I will be back in five minutes."

The door latch clicked shut behind him. Left in the quiet room, Chen Jianguo stared down at the warm egg in his hand, wondering where his ordinary son had learned to sound so completely sure of himself while the sky seemed to be falling.
