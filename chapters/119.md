# Chapter 119: The Second Month Review

Thin blue layers of cigarette smoke hung beneath the ceiling lights of the inner conference room in Beijing's Great Hall. Outside, Saturday afternoon was gray and quiet. Inside, heavy doors stayed locked, and five men sat around the long walnut table with lidded porcelain teacups and plain gray folders.

Premier Gu Chengshan sat at the head, tapping ash into a glass tray. To his left sat Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and State Security Minister Ding Guoping. To his right sat General Qin Zhengguo in a plain olive uniform without medals, and Discipline Office Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi.

"Straight to the numbers," Gu Chengshan said steadily, opening his folder. "Two full months since the first night. Give us the clean total, Gao Zhengyi."

Gao Zhengyi adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses and paused lightly on the top line of his summary sheet before speaking.

"As of nine o'clock this morning," Gao Zhengyi read clearly, "a total of 46 trillion yuan in stolen state funds, hidden shares, overseas property, gold certificates, and shell holdings has been recovered outright or locked inside state-controlled trusts."

For five seconds, nobody spoke. Even General Qin Zhengguo let out a slow breath through his nose.

"46 trillion yuan," Zhou Zhenhua repeated softly through a ribbon of smoke. "That was nearly a decade of public wealth drained through back doors, and we pulled it back in sixty days."

"Every province is accounted for," Gao Zhengyi continued, turning a page. "Three hundred and forty-two senior officials have been removed and formally charged. In forty-one prefectures and counties, including Yunxi County this morning, open court verdicts have been read aloud. Stolen factory funds and shop losses are going straight back to ordinary people."

"What about the old families in Beijing?" Gu Chengshan asked quietly, glancing up over his teacup. "Did anyone try to bend the rules behind closed doors?"

Gao Zhengyi let out a dry, quiet chuckle. "Not a single one dared. Two second-tier cousins tried calling Elder Lin last month hoping to soften a port seizure in Sha Province. Elder Lin did not even pour them tea. He sent them straight to my office with their own folders. Ever since Elder Song, Elder Qin, Elder Ye, and Elder Lin signed the Great Hall witness bond, every political and military family in Beijing has kept its hands strictly on the table."

Gu Chengshan nodded slowly and looked at Qin Zhengguo. "And the three theater commands?"

"Completely cleaned out," Qin Zhengguo answered crisply, his back straight. "Every crooked procurement officer, every contractor paying kickbacks for military land, and every officer taking outside money is facing military court. All three theater commands finished live readiness drills on Thursday without one delay. Morale is higher than it has been in ten years."

"Good," Gu Chengshan murmured, exhaling thin smoke. "An army needs clean bones to stand straight. Now tell me about the banks and streets, Zhou Zhenhua."

Zhou Zhenhua stubbed out his cigarette, lit a fresh one with a soft click of his brass lighter, and smiled faintly.

"Zero panic," Zhou Zhenhua said simply. "Two months ago, half the ministry advisors swore seizing 46 trillion yuan in dirty assets would cut the Shanghai market in half and send savers lining up outside bank doors. Instead, that 300 trillion yuan free legal stabilization fund parked across China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank held the whole floor solid as bedrock."

"Not one county branch ran short of cash," Zhou Zhenhua added, tapping his folder. "Fang Zhengming, Han Guoliang, Tang Wenhao, and Liang Qingshan kept every healthy factory funded, every port moving, and every worker's Friday paycheck on time. Grain and vegetable prices barely moved two percent. Foreign traders who tried shorting the yuan in Hong Kong twice last month broke their teeth on Bank of China's desk and never came back."

"Ordinary families barely felt a bump," Gao Zhengyi agreed, smiling faintly. "All they saw was corrupt officials going to prison and local bullies vanishing off the streets. Shopkeepers in Sha City and Shanghai are opening earlier than last year. Public confidence rose this week."

Gu Chengshan looked around the table, his face deeply thoughtful. He knew how nearly impossible it was to cut out a rot this deep without shaking the whole house down.

"We all know why this went so smoothly," Gu Chengshan said quietly. "Every ledger, offshore account, recorded call, and crooked name landed on our desks verified before those men knew they were watched. Then the same unseen hand placed 300 trillion yuan of clean, legal cash straight into our Four Major National Banks for free so no ordinary family got hurt during the surgery."

Minister Ding Guoping leaned forward calmly. "My teams checked every file from Number 1's channel. Not one false charge. Not one innocent official caught in the crossfire. Our technical teams could not find a single trace left on any outside network."

Silence settled over the walnut table.

Through the drifting smoke, Zhou Zhenhua sipped his tea and glanced from Ding Guoping to Qin Zhengguo.

"Have you noticed the strangest part of the last two months?" Zhou Zhenhua asked barely above a whisper.

"Say it," Gu Chengshan replied.

"We arrested hundreds of powerful people," Zhou Zhenhua said slowly. "Some had ties to foreign banks, overseas media, and underground networks in four countries. Yet across sixty days, not one whisper, not one newspaper rumor, and not one online post has leaked a single word about Black Guard or the 300 trillion yuan reserve."

Ding Guoping nodded soberly. "Nothing. Our monitors watched every domestic platform and overseas intelligence wire around the clock. The outside world thinks this clean-up was planned by our own central offices and backed by hidden central bank gold. No foreign agency knows Black Guard exists."

"Because the few traitors who saw plain black masks step out of the dark on the first night either did not live to gossip or lost their nerve on the spot," Qin Zhengguo said quietly. "And every leader and elder who signed the Great Hall order knows better than to breathe a word outside these walls."

Gu Chengshan stubbed out his cigarette and closed his gray folder.

"Keep it that way," Gu Chengshan ordered calmly. "The state trusts will take a year to sort every last factory and land deed cleanly, and the courts will finish every trial strictly by law. As for Black Guard and the person behind Number 1, we show our respect by never prying, never pushing, and never breaking our word."

"Agreed," Gao Zhengyi and Ding Guoping said together.

General Qin Zhengguo checked his plain silver watch, thinking of the afternoon tea he had promised Founding Elder Song out in the Western Hills. He closed his folder and nodded once.

"Everything is on track," Qin Zhengguo said softly. "The country is breathing clean air again."

Around the walnut table, five men sat in fading smoke, knowing history had turned a massive page without one headline ever naming the quiet hand that steadied the wheel.
