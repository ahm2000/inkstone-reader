# Chapter 119: The Second Month Review

Thin blue layers of cigarette smoke hung gently beneath the warm ceiling lights of the inner conference room in the Great Hall. Outside, Saturday afternoon in the capital was calm and gray. Inside, the heavy wooden doors were closed tight, and only five men sat around the long walnut table, each with a lidded porcelain teacup and a plain gray folder in front of him.

Premier Gu Chengshan sat at the head of the table, tapping the ash from his cigarette into a heavy glass ashtray. To his left sat Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and Minister of State Security Ding Guoping. To his right sat General Qin Zhengguo in a plain olive uniform without medals, and CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi.

"Let us go straight to the numbers," Gu Chengshan said in a low, steady voice. He opened his gray folder and looked across at Gao Zhengyi. "Two full months since the first night. Give us the clean total."

Gao Zhengyi adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses and turned to the summary sheet at the front of his binder. Even after thirty years of handling major cases, his fingers paused lightly on the top line before he spoke.

"As of nine o'clock this morning," Gao Zhengyi read clearly, "a total of 46 trillion yuan in stolen state funds, hidden shares, overseas real estate, gold certificates, and shell company holdings has been either recovered outright or locked inside state-controlled trusts."

For a full five seconds, nobody spoke. Even General Qin Zhengguo, who had seen enough storms for three lifetimes, let out a slow breath through his nose.

"46 trillion yuan," Zhou Zhenhua repeated softly, resting his elbow on the armrest as a thin ribbon of smoke curled upward from the cigarette between his fingers. "That is not a small leak in a pipe. That was nearly a decade of public wealth being drained out through back doors, and we pulled it back in sixty days."

"Every single province is accounted for," Gao Zhengyi continued, turning a page. "Three hundred and forty-two senior officials have been removed from office and placed under formal legal charges. In forty-one prefectures and counties, including Yunxi County this morning, open court verdicts have already been read aloud to the public. Stolen local factory funds and shop losses are being returned directly to the people."

Gu Chengshan nodded slowly and turned his eyes toward General Qin Zhengguo. "What about the three theater commands?"

"Completely cleaned out," Qin Zhengguo answered crisply, his back straight and his voice firm. "Every corrupt procurement officer, every private contractor who paid kickbacks for military land, and every officer who took outside money has been relieved of duty and handed over to military courts. All three theater commands finished their live readiness drills on Thursday without a single delay or discipline incident. The troops are steady, and morale is higher than it has been in ten years."

"Good," Gu Chengshan murmured, exhaling a thin stream of smoke. "An army must have clean bones before it can stand straight. Now tell me about the streets and the markets, Zhou Zhenhua."

Zhou Zhenhua stubbed out his finished cigarette, reached for a fresh one from the plain white pack beside his teacup, and lit it with a soft click of his brass lighter.

"Zero panic," Zhou Zhenhua said simply, letting out a quiet, satisfied breath. "When we began seizing assets worth tens of trillions, half the financial advisors in the ministry were terrified the stock market would drop by half and people would line up outside banks to pull out their savings. Instead, the central bank stepped in calmly, the state trusts kept every healthy factory running, wages were paid on time, and grain and vegetable prices did not move by two percent."

"Ordinary families barely felt a bump," Gao Zhengyi agreed, a faint smile touching his mouth. "All they saw was corrupt officials going to prison and local bullies disappearing off their streets. Consumer confidence actually rose this week."

Gu Chengshan looked around the table, his expression serious and deeply thoughtful. He knew better than anyone in the country how nearly impossible it was to remove a rot this deep without tearing the house down around them.

"We all know why this went so smoothly," Gu Chengshan said quietly. "It was not because we suddenly grew smarter overnight. Every ledger, every hidden offshore account, every recorded phone call, and every crooked officer's name landed on our desks clean, complete, and verified before those men even knew they were being watched."

Minister Ding Guoping leaned forward slightly, his expression calm and watchful. "My teams checked every single file that came through Number 1's channel. Not one false accusation. Not one innocent official caught in the crossfire. Even the tech team could not find a single trace left behind on any outside network."

Silence settled over the walnut table again. The faint hum of the air vent was the only sound in the room.

Through the drifting haze of cigarette smoke, Zhou Zhenhua lifted his teacup, took a slow sip, and set it back down with a soft clink. His eyes moved quietly from Ding Guoping to General Qin Zhengguo.

"Have you noticed the strangest part of the entire past two months?" Zhou Zhenhua asked in a voice barely above a whisper.

"Say it," Gu Chengshan replied.

"We arrested hundreds of powerful people," Zhou Zhenhua said slowly, watching the smoke rise toward the ceiling. "Some of them had connections reaching into foreign banks, private media groups, and underground networks in four different countries. And yet, across two full months, not a single whisper, not one newspaper rumor, and not one online post has ever leaked a single word about Zero Guard."

Ding Guoping nodded once, his face completely sober. "Nothing. Our monitoring offices watched every domestic platform and every major overseas intelligence wire around the clock. The outside world thinks this entire clean-up was planned in total secrecy by our own central offices. Not a single foreign agency has any idea Zero Guard even exists."

"Because the people who actually saw them during the early arrests either did not live to gossip or lost their nerve the moment they heard those flat voices," Qin Zhengguo said quietly. "And our own people inside this room know better than to breathe a word outside these walls."

Gu Chengshan stubbed out his cigarette and closed his gray folder neatly.

"Keep it that way," Gu Chengshan ordered calmly. "The state trusts will take another year to sort every last factory and land deed cleanly, and the courts will finish every public trial by the rules of law. As for Zero Guard and the person behind Number 1, we show our respect by never prying, never pushing, and never breaking our word."

"Agreed," Gao Zhengyi and Ding Guoping said together.

General Qin Zhengguo checked his plain silver watch, thinking quietly of the afternoon tea he had promised Founding Elder Song out in the Western Hills once this meeting adjourned. He closed his gray folder and nodded once, his eyes deep and steady.

"Everything is on track," Qin Zhengguo said softly. "The country is breathing clean air again."

Around the long walnut table, the five men sat quietly in the fading smoke, each knowing that history had turned a massive page without a single headline ever naming the quiet hand that had steadied the wheel.
