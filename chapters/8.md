# Chapter 8: Pork-Rib Soup and Broken Teacups

Before Chen Jianguo could fit his brass key into the lock of Apartment 602, the steel security door swung open from inside.

Warm air spilled onto the landing, rich with simmering ginger, white radish, and pork-rib soup. Liu Meifang stood in the doorway wearing her faded floral apron, her eyes red from worry yet instantly shifting into urgent maternal fretting the moment she saw her eldest son.

"Chen Xuan! You really came back tonight!" Liu Meifang pulled him inside by the sleeve, touching his cheek with warm fingers. "Why are you even thinner than at Spring Festival? Do they not give you a proper lunch break in Qingyan? Take off your shoes quickly. Have you eaten on the road?"

"I am fine, Mom," Chen Xuan said gently, stepping into his blue slippers and setting his dark-green thermos on the cabinet.

"I kept the clay pot simmering all evening just in case," Liu Meifang said, hurrying back to the kitchen. "Sit down, let me ladle you hot pork-rib soup."

Chen Xuan entered the modest living room. On the low coffee table sat two cracked ceramic teacups and a damp mound of crumpled tissues. Hunched on the worn gray sofa sat Second Aunt Wu Lanying, clutching a wrung-out wet towel against her chest, her eyes swollen nearly shut from hours of crying. Seeing Chen Xuan, she tried awkwardly to wipe her cheeks.

"Chen Xuan, you should not have rushed back from Qingyan," Wu Lanying whispered, tears spilling afresh. "Your Second Uncle is innocent. You know Chen Mingde. Twenty years on Old Street, he has never hurt a fly! Those men from Hongrui Real Estate smashed our glass counter and shoved him into the shelves, and then the police put handcuffs on him and claimed he beat their foreman!"

"Second Aunt, take a slow breath," Chen Xuan said softly, pouring her a fresh cup of warm water from the sideboard flask. "Second Uncle will come home cleanly."

Beside the balcony glass door, a folding stool scraped sharply across the tiles.

Seventeen-year-old Chen Hao sat hunched forward in his half-zipped school jacket, his knuckles bleached white around his clenched fists. His young face burned with raw, helpless fury.

"How is he going to come home cleanly?" Chen Hao burst out, his voice cracking. "I walked past Old Street right after study hall! I saw them twisting Second Uncle's arms behind his back and snapping handcuffs onto his wrists while Yan Tiehu stood laughing and smoking under the awning! Half the street watched behind their curtains and nobody stepped out! If I had a brick in my bag, I would have smashed Yan Tiehu's windshield!"

"Chen Hao! Sit down and close your mouth!" Chen Jianguo snapped from the hallway, setting Chen Xuan's canvas bag down against the wall. "Do you want to end up in a cell next to your Second Uncle? You have college entrance exams in seven months. Do not let me hear another word about bricks!"

Chen Hao bit his lower lip pale and glared furiously at the dark balcony glass, staying on his stool.

At the corner of the round dining table, nineteen-year-old sister Chen Yuan slowly locked her phone and set it face down on the plastic tablecloth, dark shadows of exhaustion under her eyes.

"It is useless, Big Brother," Chen Yuan said quietly, her voice tight with bruised weariness. "Since five o'clock, I called six local acquaintances here in Yunxi. Classmates whose uncles work in county offices, a teacher's cousin at the supply depot, even the neighborhood committee clerk who bought hinges from Second Uncle every month. Four did not pick up at all. The other two heard the words Hongrui Real Estate and Old Street Station, muttered an excuse, and hung up."

Silence pressed heavily over the small room. Outside, a distant train horn echoed faintly across the river valley.

Chen Jianguo stood near the table for a long moment. He cleared his throat loudly, tilted his chin upward toward the bright ceiling light so the tears stinging his eyes would not spill in front of the children, and slapped his palms lightly against his thighs.

"What is everyone drooping their heads for?" Chen Jianguo said in a rough, stubborn voice, squaring his shoulders. "Remember the old saying: a slow ox still reaches the field. Panic never pulled a plow straight. We have right on our side. Nobody in this family is collapsing tonight."

Chen Xuan looked at his father's reddened eyes and straight back, feeling a quiet warmth in his chest. Chen Jianguo had invented half his proverbs during hard winters so his wife and children would have something solid to lean against.

Liu Meifang walked out from the kitchen carrying a large white ceramic bowl brimming with steaming pork-rib soup, pale radish slices and bright green scallions floating in the golden broth. She set it firmly in front of Chen Xuan. "Drink while it is hot. Everyone else, have a bowl too."

Chen Xuan lifted his porcelain spoon, letting the warm ginger steam brush his face. On the sofa, Wu Lanying lowered her head and spoke in a voice barely louder than a whisper.

"Not everyone on Old Street is heartless," Wu Lanying murmured, twisting the damp towel between her fingers. "All week, ever since Yan Tiehu's men started watching our doorway, even old neighbors crossed to the far side of the road. The only person who visited Mingde Hardware all week was your old classmate, Chen Xuan. Wang Lei."

Chen Xuan's spoon paused an inch above the steaming broth.

"Yes, Wang Lei," Wu Lanying whispered with trembling gratitude. "He has a hard life now, driving a battered three-wheeled electric delivery tricycle around the county, his jacket stained with road dust and oil. Yesterday afternoon, right in front of Hongrui Real Estate's men, Wang Lei parked outside our door, laid down crumpled five-yuan and one-yuan bills, and bought a single iron door hinge he did not even need. He just stood there drinking boiled water with your Second Uncle for ten minutes so he would not feel alone."

For three full seconds, Chen Xuan did not move.

Above the rising steam, a sharp memory surfaced from senior year of high school. When Chen Jianguo broke his wrist repairing a municipal pipe and the family scraped every coin together, Chen Xuan stopped eating at the school cafeteria to save money. Every single day for four months, when Chen Xuan returned from the hallway tap, eight yuan in folded one-yuan bills sat tucked beneath the ruler inside his tin pencil case. Wang Lei had never admitted putting it there, only shrugging whenever asked.

Chen Xuan lowered his spoon and took a slow sip of hot soup. His face stayed completely calm, yet Wang Lei's name was already carved deep in his mind.

Suddenly, Wu Lanying gasped softly, clutching her cardigan in fresh alarm.

"Oh no," Wu Lanying breathed, looking up at Chen Jianguo. "The shop! When the police dragged Chen Mingde into the patrol car, I dropped the brass padlock on the counter and ran after them! The shutter is only pulled halfway down, and the hardware shop was left unlocked!"

Chen Jianguo pushed his chair back immediately. "Do not worry, Wu Lanying, I will go lock it right now."

Chen Xuan set his spoon neatly beside his bowl and rose beside his father. "I will go with you, Dad."
