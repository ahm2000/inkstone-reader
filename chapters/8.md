# Chapter 8: Pork-Rib Soup and Broken Teacups

Before Chen Jianguo could fit his key into Apartment 602, the steel door swung open from inside.

Liu Meifang stood in her faded apron, the scent of ginger and pork-rib soup drifting behind her. Her eyes were red from worry, yet seeing her eldest son, urgent maternal fretting swept everything else aside.

"Chen Xuan! You really came back tonight!" Liu Meifang pulled him inside and pressed her warm palm briefly against his cheek. "Why is your face even thinner than at Spring Festival? Do they not let you eat on time in Qingyan? Take off your shoes quickly."

"I am fine, Mom," Chen Xuan said gently, slipping into his slippers and setting his green thermos on the shoe cabinet.

"Sit down," Liu Meifang urged, hurrying back to the kitchen. "I kept the clay pot simmering since seven. Let me ladle you hot soup."

Chen Xuan entered the modest living room. Two cracked ceramic teacups and a damp mound of tissues sat on the low table. Hunched on the worn sofa, Second Aunt Wu Lanying clutched a wrung-out towel against her chest, her eyes swollen nearly shut. Seeing Chen Xuan, she tried clumsily to dab her cheeks.

"Chen Xuan, you should not have rushed back from Qingyan," Wu Lanying whispered, fresh tears slipping past her fingers. "Twenty years on Old Street, your Second Uncle has never cursed at a customer! Those men from Hongrui Real Estate smashed our glass counter and shoved him into the shelves, and then the police handcuffed him and claimed he beat their foreman!"

"Second Aunt, catch your breath," Chen Xuan said softly, pouring her warm water and setting the cup between her trembling fingers. "Have you eaten anything since noon?"

Wu Lanying shook her head, her throat hitching. "How could I eat while Mingde is in a cell?"

"He will come home cleanly," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Drink a little water first. If you collapse before he walks in, he will scold all of us."

Wu Lanying blinked up at his steady eyes and took a shaky sip.

Beside the balcony door, a folding stool scraped sharply across the tiles.

Seventeen-year-old Chen Hao sat hunched in his school jacket, knuckles bleached white around his clenched fists, his face burning with helpless fury.

"How is he coming home cleanly, Big Brother?" Chen Hao burst out, his voice cracking. "I passed Old Street after study hall! I saw them twisting Second Uncle's arms behind his back while Yan Tiehu stood under the awning smoking and laughing! Half the street peeked behind their curtains and nobody stepped out! If I had a brick in my bag, I would have smashed Yan Tiehu's windshield!"

"Chen Hao! Close your mouth and sit still!" Chen Jianguo snapped from the hallway, dropping Chen Xuan's bag. Anger masked his fear for his younger son. "Do you want a cell beside your Second Uncle? You have college entrance exams in seven months! Let me hear one more word about bricks!"

Chen Hao's jaw locked. "So we just let them bully us in broad daylight?"

"Shouting here will not open a cell door," Chen Xuan said quietly, meeting his brother's eyes. His tone was calm, yet Chen Hao's shoulders stiffened. "Keep your head cool, Chen Hao. Losing your temper hands Yan Tiehu what he wants."

Chen Hao bit his lip pale, glared at the dark balcony glass, and sank back onto his stool.

At the dining table, nineteen-year-old sister Chen Yuan locked her phone and set it face down, dark shadows under her eyes.

"It is useless, Big Brother," Chen Yuan said quietly, her voice tight with exhaustion. "Since five o'clock, I called six local contacts in Yunxi. Classmates whose uncles work in county offices, a teacher's cousin at the supply depot, even the neighborhood committee clerk who bought hinges from Second Uncle every month."

She let out a short, bitter laugh close to a sob. "Four never picked up. The other two sounded polite until I mentioned Hongrui Real Estate and Old Street Station. Then both suddenly remembered an urgent meeting and hung up."

Silence pressed over the room. Chen Yuan stared at her knuckles, her shoulders slumping.

"You did what you could, Chen Yuan," Chen Xuan said softly. "You did not need to beg people like that."

Chen Jianguo stood near the table for a long moment. Suddenly he cleared his throat loudly, tilted his chin toward the ceiling light so his tears would not fall in front of the children, and slapped both palms against his thighs.

"What is everyone drooping their heads for?" Chen Jianguo asked in a rough, stubborn voice, squaring his shoulders. "Remember the old saying: a slow ox still reaches the field. Panic never pulled a plow straight. We have right on our side. Nobody in this family is collapsing tonight."

Chen Xuan watched his father's reddened eyes and straight back, feeling a quiet warmth in his chest. Chen Jianguo had invented half his proverbs during lean winters so his family would always have something solid to lean against.

Liu Meifang set a steaming white bowl of pork-rib soup in front of Chen Xuan. "Listen to your father. Drink while it is hot. Chen Yuan, Chen Hao, get your bowls."

Chen Xuan lifted his porcelain spoon. On the sofa, Wu Lanying lowered her gaze and spoke barely above a whisper.

"Not everyone on Old Street is heartless," Wu Lanying murmured, twisting her damp towel. "All week, ever since Yan Tiehu's men watched our door, old neighbors crossed to the far side of the road. The only person who walked into Mingde Hardware was your old classmate, Chen Xuan. Wang Lei."

Chen Xuan's spoon paused an inch above the steaming broth.

"Wang Lei?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"Yes, Wang Lei," Wu Lanying whispered with trembling gratitude. "He has a hard life now, driving a battered electric delivery tricycle around the county, his jacket stained with dust and oil. Yesterday afternoon, right in front of Hongrui Real Estate's men, he parked outside our door, laid down crumpled five-yuan and one-yuan bills, and bought a single iron door hinge he did not need. He stood drinking boiled water with your Second Uncle for ten minutes so he would not feel alone."

For three full seconds, Chen Xuan did not move.

A sharp memory surfaced from senior year of high school. When Chen Jianguo broke his wrist repairing a municipal pipe and the family scraped every coin together, Chen Xuan stopped eating at the cafeteria to save money. Every single day for four months, eight yuan in folded one-yuan bills sat tucked beneath the ruler inside his tin pencil case. Whenever Chen Xuan asked, Wang Lei only shrugged and kept reading.

Chen Xuan lowered his spoon and took a slow sip of hot soup. His face stayed calm, yet Wang Lei's name was carved deep in his mind.

"Does he still run deliveries near Old Street?" Chen Xuan asked quietly.

"Every afternoon," Wu Lanying murmured. Then her face went pale. She gasped and clutched her cardigan.

"Oh no!" Wu Lanying breathed, looking up at Chen Jianguo in alarm. "The shop! When the police dragged Chen Mingde into the patrol car, I dropped the brass padlock on the counter and ran after them! The shutter is halfway down, and Mingde Hardware was left unlocked!"

Chen Jianguo pushed his chair back immediately. "Do not panic, Wu Lanying. Drink your soup. I will go lock it right now."

Chen Xuan set his spoon beside his bowl and rose beside his father. "I will go with you, Dad."
