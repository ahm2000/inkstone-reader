# Chapter 39: The Vault Number

At twenty-five minutes before two on Monday morning, the air inside General Qin Zhengguo's study at the Central Military Commission compound hung thick with tobacco smoke.

Colonel Zhao Ming stood stiffly near the closed oak doors, having just delivered Qin Zhengguo's stand-down order to the inner guard detachment. His jacket was buttoned properly now, yet his eyes kept flicking nervously toward the desk.

Qin Zhengguo ignored the standard encrypted red telephones on the left side of his blotter. He had already tested two of them five minutes earlier and found both locked in dead, silent hold. Instead, he turned a small brass key in the steel drawer on the right side of his desk and lifted out a plain, matte-black handset with no screen, no indicator lights, and no ministry serial plate.

An armored cable ran straight from the base down through the floorboards into an independent subterranean vault trunk.

"General," Zhao Ming said quietly, his brow furrowing, "what line is that? I have never seen you unlock that drawer."

"Because you are not cleared to know it exists," Qin Zhengguo replied without looking up. He clamped a freshly lit cigarette between his teeth, squinting against the rising smoke, and lifted the heavy receiver. "Stand by the door and keep quiet."

As one of the republic's highest military commanders, Qin Zhengguo had known of Black Pine Guard's existence for twenty years. On the night he received his fourth star, the outgoing vice-chairman had handed him the brass key to this drawer along with a twelve-digit routing sequence memorized once and never written down: Black Pine Guard's direct vault line.

He punched the twelve digits cleanly into the dial pad.

On the second ring, a sharp, cold mechanical click echoed across the circuit, the unmistakable footprint of a secondary monitoring bridge locking onto the call. Half a second later, the receiver lifted on the far end.

"Vault channel open," a calm, gravelly voice answered. "This is Number 1."

Qin Zhengguo took the cigarette from his lips and tapped ash into the glass tray. His voice dropped into the iron cadence of a battlefield commander. "Number 1. This is Qin Zhengguo, Central Military Commission. Explain to me right now why five Zero Guard sentries are standing outside my study doors and why every military red line in this compound is frozen."

A brief, measured silence passed on the line. When Number 1 spoke again, there was no deference to Qin Zhengguo's four stars and no trace of panic, only cold institutional discipline.

"General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 said evenly. "Black Pine Guard has been formally stood down. Our command nodes are sealed, our operatives are off the street, and this channel is under live monitoring by Zero Guard. I have no operational authority over the sentries outside your compound."

Qin Zhengguo's jaw clenched hard. Smoke drifted across his sharp eyes. "Do not recite procedure to me like a clerk, Number 1. I know Zero Guard does not wake up on a Monday morning without an Absolute Order. Right now, I cannot reach the northern theater commander. I cannot reach the naval fleet duty officer. I cannot reach the Premier. Tell me what happens if a foreign carrier group or a border brigade tests our perimeter while the Central Military Commission sits blind and mute!"

Across the room, Zhao Ming's shoulders tensed at the sheer force in Qin Zhengguo's voice.

On the far end, Number 1's tone did not waver by a degree. "External defense channels, theater radar links, and strategic deterrence relays have not been touched, General Qin Zhengguo. Your northern and southern border commands remain fully operational and independent. Only internal capital coordination and political red lines are held under suspension."

"That is not good enough!" Qin Zhengguo snapped, his palm hitting the blotter with a dull thud. "You are telling a sitting four-star general that the capital's command brain has been severed from its own headquarters, and you expect me to sit here smoking cigarettes until sunrise without a briefing? Who is the target, Number 1? How deep is the breach inside my armed forces?"

Silence hung over the line for three agonizing seconds. Somewhere on the bridge between them, Qin Zhengguo could almost feel the cold, silent presence of Zero Guard's monitoring node weighing every syllable.

"General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 replied, noticeably colder now, "you know the charter better than almost anyone alive in that building. Your rank grants you access to the vault line during standard Black Pine Guard operations. It does not grant you disclosure during a Zero Guard lockdown. Without the founding-era override phrase, I am forbidden from confirming scope, targets, or findings. Press further without authorization, and protocol requires me to terminate this call and log the inquiry."

Qin Zhengguo froze, his fingers gripping the heavy black handset so tightly the tendons stood out along his wrist. His eyes burned with suppressed fury, yet his mind remained clear. He knew Number 1 was not bluffing. Under Zero monitoring, if Number 1 leaked a single syllable without the override phrase, Zero Guard would enforce the protocol on Number 1 before the receiver hit the cradle.

"You have nothing to say without the founding phrase," Qin Zhengguo said slowly, his voice dropping to a dangerous murmur.

"I am bound by the charter, General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 answered steadily. "If you have the phrase, speak it now. If not, I will close the circuit in ten seconds."

Qin Zhengguo exhaled a long plume of smoke toward the lamp. His cigarette had burned halfway down, a cylinder of gray ash dropping onto the blotter untouched.

Fewer than three people in the republic's history had ever been entrusted with the founding-era override phrase, and only one of them was still breathing. Ninety years old, retired in the Western Hills, long stepped back from daily politics, yet still carrying the final key forged in nineteen forty-nine.

"Keep this circuit open," Qin Zhengguo ordered flatly. "Tell whoever is listening on your bridge that I am making one call to the Western Hills. Give me ten minutes."

A faint click echoed on the monitoring bridge, as if an unseen arbiter had weighed the request in silence and chosen not to sever the line.

"Ten minutes, General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 said calmly. "The channel remains on hold."

Qin Zhengguo laid the handset carefully beside the ashtray, pushed his chair back with a sharp scrape, and looked straight at his pale adjutant.

"Zhao Ming," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice hard as flint. "Go to the communications cabinet in the anteroom and patch the old analog copper wire to the Western Hills. Not the red exchange. The direct point-to-point military wire."

Zhao Ming's eyes widened. "General, you are calling Elder Song at two in the morning? What if Zero Guard cut that wire too?"

"They did not cut it," Qin Zhengguo said, lighting another cigarette as he strode toward the anteroom. "If Zero Guard is walking the capital tonight, they are standing in Elder Song's courtyard too, and they know better than to pull the plug on the old man who helped write their charter. Patch the line."
