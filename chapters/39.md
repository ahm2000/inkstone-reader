# Chapter 39: The Vault Number

At twenty-five before two on Monday morning, cigarette smoke hung thick inside General Qin Zhengguo's study at the Central Military Commission compound.

Colonel Ye Chenglin stood stiffly near the closed oak doors after passing Qin Zhengguo's stand-down order to the inside guard squad. His jacket was buttoned now, yet his eyes kept flicking nervously toward the desk.

Qin Zhengguo ignored the private red phones on his left, having already found them locked in silent hold. Instead, he turned a brass key in the steel drawer on his right and lifted out a plain black phone with no screen, lights, or office plate.

A heavy shielded cable ran from its base straight through the floorboards into a deep underground line.

"General," Ye Chenglin asked quietly, frowning, "what phone is that? I have never seen you unlock that drawer."

"Because you do not have clearance to know it exists," Qin Zhengguo replied without looking up. He clamped a freshly lit cigarette between his teeth and lifted the heavy handset. "Stand by the door and stay quiet."

As one of the republic's highest commanders, Qin Zhengguo had known about White Guard for twenty years. The night he received his fourth star, the outgoing vice-chairman gave him the brass key and a twelve-digit code memorized once and never written down: White Guard's direct vault line.

He punched the twelve digits cleanly into the keypad.

On the second ring, a sharp click sounded on the line, the clear mark of a second listening line locking on. Half a second later, someone answered.

"Vault line open," a calm, gravelly voice said. "This is Number 1."

Qin Zhengguo pulled the cigarette from his lips and tapped ash into the tray, his voice dropping into battlefield iron. "Number 1. This is Qin Zhengguo, Central Military Commission. Tell me right now why five Black Guard sentries are standing outside my study doors and why every army red phone in this compound is frozen."

A steady pause followed. When Number 1 spoke again, there was no bending to four stars and no panic, only rigid discipline.

"General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 said evenly. "White Guard has been officially stood down. Our command rooms are sealed, our agents are off the street, and Black Guard is listening to this line right now. I have no power over the guards outside your doors."

Qin Zhengguo clenched his jaw. "Do not read rules to me like an office clerk, Number 1. I know Black Guard does not wake up on a Monday morning without a Black Order. Right now, I cannot reach the northern army commander, the navy fleet duty officer, or the Premier. What happens if a foreign carrier group or border force tests our lines while the Central Military Commission sits blind and mute?"

Across the room, Ye Chenglin's shoulders stiffened at the force in Qin Zhengguo's voice.

Number 1's tone did not waver. "Border defense lines, army radar links, and defense launch systems have not been touched, General Qin Zhengguo. Your northern and southern border commands are running at full strength on their own. Only inside capital calls and political red phones are on hold."

"That is not good enough!" Qin Zhengguo snapped, slapping the desk. "You tell a sitting four-star general that the capital's command brain is cut off from headquarters, and expect me to sit here smoking until sunrise without a single report? Who is the target, Number 1? How deep is the rot inside my armed forces?"

Silence hung for three heavy seconds. Along the wire, Qin Zhengguo could almost feel Black Guard weighing every word.

"General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 replied, colder now, "you know the charter better than almost anyone in that building. Your rank grants access to this vault line during normal White Guard work. It does not grant answers during a Black Guard lockdown. Without the founding override phrase, I am forbidden from giving you the size of the sweep, the targets, or what we found. Push further without it, and the rules require me to cut this call and log your question."

Qin Zhengguo froze, veins standing out along his wrist as he gripped the phone. His eyes burned with anger, yet his mind stayed sharp. Number 1 was not bluffing. Under Black Guard's watch, leaking one word without the phrase would bring Black Guard down on Number 1 before the handset hit the desk.

"You have nothing to say without the founding phrase," Qin Zhengguo rumbled.

"I am bound by the charter, General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 answered steadily. "If you have the phrase, say it now. If not, I close the line in ten seconds."

Qin Zhengguo blew smoke toward the lamp, letting gray ash drop untouched onto the desk.

Fewer than three people in the republic's history had ever held the founding override phrase, and only one was still alive. Ninety years old, retired in the Western Hills, yet still holding the final key forged in nineteen forty-nine.

"Keep this line open," Qin Zhengguo ordered flatly. "Tell whoever is listening that I am making one call to the Western Hills. Give me ten minutes."

A faint click sounded on the listening line, as if an unseen judge weighed the request and chose not to cut the call.

"Ten minutes, General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 said calmly. "The line stays on hold."

Qin Zhengguo set the phone beside the ashtray, pushed his chair back, and faced his pale aide.

"Ye Chenglin," Qin Zhengguo said, voice hard as stone. "Go to the side room and connect the old copper wire to the Western Hills. Not the red switchboard. The direct army wire."

Ye Chenglin's eyes widened. "General, you are calling Elder Song at two in the morning? What if Black Guard cut that wire too?"

"They did not cut it," Qin Zhengguo said, lighting another cigarette as he strode toward the side room. "If Black Guard is walking the capital tonight, they are standing in Elder Song's yard too, and they know better than to pull the plug on the old man who helped write their charter. Connect the line."
