# Chapter 39: The Vault Number

At twenty-five minutes before two on Monday morning, the air inside General Qin Zhengguo's study at the Central Military Commission compound hung thick with cigarette smoke.

Colonel Ye Chenglin stood stiffly near the closed oak doors. He had just passed Qin Zhengguo's stand-down order to the inside guard squad. His jacket was buttoned properly now, yet his eyes kept flicking nervously toward the desk.

Qin Zhengguo ignored the regular private red phones on the left side of his desk. He had already tested two of them five minutes earlier and found both locked in dead, silent hold. Instead, he turned a small brass key in the steel drawer on the right side of his desk and lifted out a plain black phone with no screen, no lights, and no office plate.

A heavy shielded cable ran straight from the base down through the floorboards into its own deep underground line.

"General," Ye Chenglin said quietly, frowning, "what phone is that? I have never seen you unlock that drawer."

"Because you do not have clearance to know it exists," Qin Zhengguo replied without looking up. He clamped a freshly lit cigarette between his teeth, squinted against the rising smoke, and picked up the heavy phone. "Stand by the door and stay quiet."

As one of the republic's highest army commanders, Qin Zhengguo had known about White Guard for twenty years. On the night he received his fourth star, the outgoing vice-chairman had handed him the brass key to this drawer along with a twelve-digit phone code memorized once and never written down: White Guard's direct vault line.

He punched the twelve digits cleanly into the keypad.

On the second ring, a sharp, cold click sounded on the line, the clear mark of a second listening line locking onto the call. Half a second later, someone picked up on the far end.

"Vault line open," a calm, gravelly voice answered. "This is Number 1."

Qin Zhengguo took the cigarette from his lips and tapped ash into the glass tray. His voice dropped into the hard, iron tone of a battlefield commander. "Number 1. This is Qin Zhengguo, Central Military Commission. Tell me right now why five Black Guard sentries are standing outside my study doors and why every army red phone in this compound is frozen."

A short, steady pause followed on the line. When Number 1 spoke again, there was no bending to Qin Zhengguo's four stars and no hint of panic, only cold, rigid discipline.

"General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 said evenly. "White Guard has been officially stood down. Our command rooms are sealed, our agents are off the street, and Black Guard is listening to this line right now. I have no power over the guards outside your doors."

Qin Zhengguo clenched his jaw hard. Smoke drifted past his sharp eyes. "Do not read rules to me like some office clerk, Number 1. I know Black Guard does not wake up on a Monday morning without an Black Order. Right now, I cannot reach the northern army commander. I cannot reach the navy fleet duty officer. I cannot reach the Premier. Tell me what happens if a foreign carrier group or a border force tests our lines while the Central Military Commission sits blind and mute!"

Across the room, Ye Chenglin's shoulders stiffened at the sheer force in Qin Zhengguo's voice.

On the far end, Number 1's tone did not shake by even a hair. "Border defense lines, army radar links, and defense launch systems have not been touched, General Qin Zhengguo. Your northern and southern border commands are still running at full strength on their own. Only inside capital calls and political red phones are put on hold."

"That is not good enough!" Qin Zhengguo snapped, slapping his palm down on the desk with a dull thud. "You are telling a sitting four-star general that the capital's command brain has been cut off from its own headquarters, and you expect me to sit here smoking cigarettes until sunrise without a single report? Who is the target, Number 1? How deep is the rot inside my armed forces?"

Silence hung over the line for three heavy seconds. Somewhere along the wire between them, Qin Zhengguo could almost feel the cold, silent presence of Black Guard weighing every single word.

"General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 replied, noticeably colder now, "you know the charter better than almost anyone alive in that building. Your rank gives you access to this vault line during normal White Guard work. It does not give you answers during a Black Guard lockdown. Without the founding override phrase, I am forbidden from telling you the size of the sweep, the targets, or what we found. Push further without that phrase, and the rules require me to cut this call and log your question."

Qin Zhengguo froze. His fingers gripped the heavy black phone so tightly the veins stood out along his wrist. His eyes burned with tight anger, yet his head stayed completely clear. He knew Number 1 was not bluffing. Under Black Guard's watch, if Number 1 leaked even one word without the override phrase, Black Guard would come down on Number 1 before the phone even hit the desk.

"You have nothing to say without the founding phrase," Qin Zhengguo said slowly, his voice dropping to a low, dangerous rumble.

"I am bound by the charter, General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 answered steadily. "If you have the phrase, say it now. If not, I will close the line in ten seconds."

Qin Zhengguo blew a long cloud of smoke toward the lamp. His cigarette had burned halfway down, and a stick of gray ash dropped untouched onto the desk.

Fewer than three people in the republic's history had ever been trusted with the founding override phrase, and only one of them was still alive. Ninety years old, retired in the Western Hills, long stepped back from daily politics, yet still holding the final key forged in nineteen forty-nine.

"Keep this line open," Qin Zhengguo ordered flatly. "Tell whoever is listening on your line that I am making one call to the Western Hills. Give me ten minutes."

A faint click sounded on the listening line, as if an unseen judge had weighed the request in silence and decided not to cut the call.

"Ten minutes, General Qin Zhengguo," Number 1 said calmly. "The line stays on hold."

Qin Zhengguo set the black phone carefully beside the ashtray, pushed his chair back with a sharp scrape, and looked straight at his pale aide.

"Ye Chenglin," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice hard as stone. "Go to the phone box in the side room and connect the old copper wire to the Western Hills. Not the red switchboard. The direct army wire."

Ye Chenglin's eyes went wide. "General, you are calling Elder Song at two in the morning? What if Black Guard cut that wire too?"

"They did not cut it," Qin Zhengguo said, lighting another cigarette as he strode toward the side room. "If Black Guard is walking the capital tonight, they are standing in Elder Song's yard too, and they know better than to pull the plug on the old man who helped write their charter. Connect the line."
