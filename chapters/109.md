# Chapter 109: One Mug of Ginger Tea

By the time they saw Qin Zhengming and Lin Shuyin back to their riverfront hotel near the Bund, a steady autumn rain had begun turning the asphalt boulevards into ribbons of reflected streetlight.

Minutes later, the black Maybach rolled quietly through the wrought-iron gates of Emerald Riverside Villa, leaving the rain-washed riverside avenue peaceful and empty beneath the camphor trees.

Inside the warm villa, Chen Xuan hung his dark navy suit jacket neatly on a wooden hanger in the hallway closet. He loosened his slate tie, rolled the cuffs of his white dress shirt up to his forearms, and headed straight toward the kitchen.

"Do not go upstairs yet," Chen Xuan called out over the gentle patter of raindrops against the floor-to-ceiling glass. "The breeze off the Huangpu River was chilly tonight, and your fingers were cold when you held the steering wheel. Give me five minutes."

Qin Yuhan slipped off her heels on the woven rug by the entryway, hung her cream trench coat over the wooden stand, and walked barefoot across the warm oak floor to lean against the kitchen doorframe.

Under the soft yellow pendant lamps, Chen Xuan moved with unhurried ease. He rinsed a knob of fresh ginger, sliced it into thin coins on the cutting board, and tossed them into a small ceramic saucepan with three crushed red dates and a spoonful of dark brown sugar. Within two minutes, a gentle simmer rose from the stove, filling the quiet kitchen with a warm, sweet, spicy scent.

Qin Yuhan watched the steady line of his shoulders and felt the lingering tension of the evening fade away.

"I am sorry about my mother tonight," she said softly, folding her arms loosely over her silk blouse. "She did not mean to sound harsh about your job at Hengxin. In Beijing, relatives constantly compare titles and company sizes. She only worries that people will gossip behind our backs."

Chen Xuan glanced over his shoulder with an easy grin as he stirred the simmering tea with a wooden spoon. "Why are you apologizing? Auntie actually went easy on me. She did not even ask me to recite a five-year career plan or draw a chart of my promotions."

"I am serious, Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan murmured, stepping closer to the kitchen counter. "You never lose your temper, even when people underestimate you. Sometimes I wonder if I am being selfish by letting you put up with my family's expectations."

"Selfish?" Chen Xuan turned off the flame and poured the steaming amber liquid through a small mesh strainer into a wide porcelain mug. He slid the mug across the kitchen counter toward her. "Listen to me, Yuhan. I get to sleep in a warm house, cook whatever I crave, take slow walks by the river after dinner, and come home every afternoon to the prettiest wife in Shanghai. If answering two polite questions over steamed river perch is the price, I got the bargain of the century."

Qin Yuhan could not help laughing softly. She wrapped both cold hands around the warm mug, letting the steady heat seep into her palms before taking a careful sip. The ginger carried just enough gentle kick to warm her chest, balanced by the mellow sweetness of the red dates.

"It is really good," she said quietly, lowering her eyes to the mug. As she tilted her wrist, the mother-of-pearl dial of her new watch caught the warm kitchen light. "Chen Xuan, tell me the truth. Even with an autumn store promotion, a Swiss watch at Plaza 66 is not cheap. Did you empty your savings account for this?"

Chen Xuan leaned his elbows on the opposite side of the kitchen counter, holding a plain glass of warm water. "Not even close. Do I look like a man who would leave himself short of grocery money? I still have plenty tucked away for braised beef, autumn hairy crabs, and your favorite strawberries."

Qin Yuhan shook her head, smiling warmly even though she still suspected he was downplaying the cost to keep her from worrying. To her, that was simply who Chen Xuan was: a quiet, grounded man who never bragged, never chased status, and spent his modest pay on making her daily life comfortable.

"My parents actually liked you tonight," she added after another sip, her voice bright with quiet relief. "When we walked them into the hotel lobby, Dad pulled me aside and said your suggestion about the port shift handover was smarter than half the reports sitting on his desk. Even Mom admitted you have better manners than most of the young directors she meets in Beijing."

"Then our mission was a complete success," Chen Xuan said cheerfully. "And your dad gets a box of decent tea for his office in Beijing. That should keep him happy until we visit."

"He already told me to remind you not to let me skip lunch at the office," Qin Yuhan said, a soft laugh escaping her. "I think you won him over faster than anyone else has in ten years."

"Now finish your ginger tea and go get some sleep," Chen Xuan said, nodding toward the stairs. "You have to drive them to Hongqiao Airport in the morning, and this autumn rain makes the elevated ring road slippery."

Qin Yuhan finished the last warm swallow, rinsed her mug in the sink despite his quiet protest, and reached out to smooth the rolled sleeve of his white shirt. "Good night, Chen Xuan. Thank you for tonight."

"Sleep well, President Qin," he teased softly.

When the gentle sound of her footsteps faded upstairs, the villa settled into deep, peaceful quiet. Chen Xuan switched off the kitchen pendant lamps and stood for a moment beside the rain-streaked garden window, sipping his warm water while watching the rain fall across the lawn.

Beyond the wrought-iron gates, a neighborhood security guard in a yellow raincoat strolled casually past the camphor trees humming an old folk tune, while a late-night convenience store clerk across the avenue locked up his awning and stretched his shoulders. Quiet and unhurried, the Zero Guard folded seamlessly into the rainy Shanghai night, leaving the warm glow behind the villa's glass completely undisturbed.
