# Chapter 109: Noodles by the Dock

Across the wet avenue outside Shanghai Port Gate Seven, under a faded blue tarp, sat a small roadside noodle stall. White steam rose from a battered stockpot, smelling of beef bones, ginger, and scallions.

Chen Xuan took a corner table, set his satchel by his foot, and placed his scratched green thermos beside the vinegar bottle.

"One bowl of beef noodles, extra scallions, light chili oil," Chen Xuan told the older owner with an easy smile.

"Coming right up, young man."

Two minutes later, a steaming bowl landed in front of him. Chen Xuan had just picked up his chopsticks when leather heels stopped beside his table.

He looked up. Standing outside the tarp with a folded black umbrella and a slim leather folder was Qin Yuhan.

"Is this seat taken?" Qin Yuhan asked with a composed smile.

Chen Xuan gestured to the red plastic stool across from him. "Not at all, Director Qin. Though the stool wobbles, and the steam might get into your wool coat."

"Wool dries," Qin Yuhan replied easily. She leaned her umbrella against the table, sat down, and called to the owner. "Uncle, one bowl of clear-broth beef noodles, please. No chili."

"Right away, Miss!"

Qin Yuhan placed her folder on her lap and folded her hands on the worn wood, her earlier cool distance softening into curiosity.

"I did not get your name inside," she said.

"Chen Xuan," he answered calmly, sipping broth. "Shipping clerk, Hengxin Logistics."

"Qin Yuhan, Haiyue Group," she said, glancing at the green thermos. "Quite a morning at Gate Seven, Chen Xuan."

"A bit noisy," Chen Xuan agreed mildly. "At least the rain stopped."

Qin Yuhan let out an amused breath. "You know that is not what I meant. Most clerks at Hengxin Logistics do not quote Joint Notice Nineteen from memory, and they do not make a broker's license vanish five minutes after sending a text."

Chen Xuan laid his chopsticks across his bowl and met her eyes without blinking.

"Director Qin, you give me too much credit," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Joint Notice Nineteen was pinned in large black letters beside the water cooler at Counter Three. I waited twelve minutes with a low phone battery, so I read the wall."

Qin Yuhan's lips curved slightly. "And the text? Five minutes before Chief Shen Guohua walked out of the main building with six Anti-Smuggling officers?"

"Even simpler," Chen Xuan said with a dry smile. "Shanghai has been cleaning up bad offices all week. When a man smokes under a no-smoking sign, demands 200k in front of forty witnesses, and blocks a customs scale while shouting his uncle's name, all it takes is one message to the harbor tip line. Chief Shen was probably already upstairs waiting for an excuse to sweep Gate Seven."

The owner set a steaming bowl of clear-broth noodles before Qin Yuhan. She thanked him and stirred the scallions gently.

"A very tidy answer," Qin Yuhan observed, looking up. "Not a single gap. Except for one tiny thing."

"Oh?" Chen Xuan asked, lifting his chopsticks.

"I watched Chief Shen Guohua when he waved your truck through," Qin Yuhan said quietly. "Before he turned away, he looked straight at you. Not the way an official looks at a random citizen who sent a tip. The way a soldier looks at someone he would never dare interrupt."

For a heartbeat, only the hiss of the noodle pot and a distant ship horn filled the space.

Chen Xuan did not stiffen or look away. He took a calm bite of noodles, chewed unhurriedly, and smiled faintly.

"Maybe Chief Shen likes green thermoses," Chen Xuan said lightly. "My mother bought this one at the county store six years ago. It keeps tea hot all day."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, a bright sound that warmed her sharp features. "Fine. Keep your secrets, Chen Xuan. If a man does not want to brag, it is rude to push him."

"I truly have nothing to brag about," Chen Xuan said sincerely. "Twelve thousand yuan a month, a quiet corner desk, and free weekends. Compared to running coastal supply for Haiyue Group, my life is much easier."

"Easier is not always simple," Qin Yuhan replied thoughtfully, tasting the warm broth. "Every week I meet dozens of men in tailored suits who talk loudly about knowing this director or that banker. Nine out of ten fold the moment real trouble hits. Watching someone drink his tea calmly and let a bully trip over his own feet is rare."

"Loud people usually do the hard work themselves," Chen Xuan said. "You just stay out of their way while they fall."

Qin Yuhan nodded slowly. "My grandfather used to say something almost identical. He always told me that real weight never makes a sound when you set it on the table."

"Your grandfather sounds like a wise old man," Chen Xuan said warmly.

"He is," Qin Yuhan smiled. "Stubborn, too. He still refuses to let anyone carry his groceries."

They finished their noodles in easy company, trading light remarks about Shanghai's wet autumn weather and slow harbor buses.

When Qin Yuhan set her chopsticks down, she drew an ivory business card from her coat, wrote eleven digits on the back with a silver pen, and slid it across the table.

"The front is my office line at Haiyue Group," Qin Yuhan said. "The back is my personal mobile. Hengxin Logistics is a small firm, and your supervisor clearly dumps messy errands on you. If you ever run into another port headache, or want a fairer place to work, call me."

Chen Xuan tucked the card into his shirt pocket.

"Thank you, Director Qin," he said politely. "Though I like my quiet desk at Hengxin. And I already paid for both bowls when I walked up to get vinegar."

Qin Yuhan blinked, then smiled as she stood and took her umbrella. "Then next time, the noodles are on me. Take care, Chen Xuan."

"Safe drive, Director Qin."

She walked smoothly toward a black sedan forty meters down the curb, where her driver opened the rear door.

Chen Xuan stayed another minute, finishing the last warm sip of jasmine tea before slinging his satchel over his shoulder.

Fifty meters down the dock road, motionless in the shadow of a rusted blue shipping container, two tall figures in plain dark coats watched the street in silence. High collars and plain black masks hid their faces. Neither moved as Qin Yuhan's sedan pulled into traffic.

Only when Chen Xuan stepped out from under the tarp and walked toward the harbor bus stop did the two quiet figures from Black Guard shift by a single step, drifting silently along the far side of the avenue to hold their exact fifty-meter distance.
