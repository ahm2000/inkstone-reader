# Chapter 109: One Mug of Ginger Tea

By the time they saw Qin Zhengming and Lin Shuyin back to their riverfront hotel near the Bund, a steady rain had begun turning the asphalt boulevards into ribbons of reflected streetlight.

Minutes later, the black Maybach rolled quietly through the wrought-iron gates of Emerald Riverside Villa. A short distance down the rain-washed road outside the perimeter wall, an unmarked matte-black sedan pulled smoothly beneath the camphor trees and clicked its headlights off, vanishing into silence and shadow.

Inside the warm villa, Chen Xuan hung his dark navy suit jacket neatly on a wooden hanger in the hallway closet. He loosened his slate tie, rolled the cuffs of his white dress shirt up to his forearms, and headed straight toward the kitchen.

"Do not go upstairs yet," Chen Xuan called out over the gentle patter of autumn raindrops against the floor-to-ceiling glass. "The breeze off the Huangpu River was chilly tonight, and your fingers were cold when you held the steering wheel. Give me five minutes."

Qin Yuhan slipped off her heels on the woven rug by the entryway, hung her cream trench coat over the stand, and walked barefoot across the warm oak floor to lean against the kitchen doorframe.

Under the soft yellow pendant lamps, Chen Xuan moved with unhurried ease. He washed a knob of fresh ginger, sliced it into thin coins on the wooden board, and tossed them into a small ceramic saucepan with three crushed red dates and a spoonful of dark brown sugar. Within two minutes, a gentle simmer rose from the stove, filling the quiet kitchen with a warm, sweet, spicy scent.

Qin Yuhan watched the steady line of his shoulders and felt the lingering fatigue of the weekend fade away.

"I am sorry about my mother tonight," she said softly, folding her arms loosely over her silk blouse. "She did not mean to be harsh about your job at Hengxin. In Beijing, relatives constantly compare titles and company sizes. She only worries that people will gossip behind our backs."

Chen Xuan glanced over his shoulder with an easy grin as he stirred the simmering tea with a wooden spoon. "Why are you apologizing? Auntie actually went easy on me. She did not even ask me to recite my five-year career plan."

"I am serious, Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan murmured, stepping closer to the marble island. "You never lose your temper, even when people underestimate you. Sometimes I wonder if I am being selfish by letting you put up with my family's expectations."

"Selfish?" Chen Xuan turned off the flame and poured the steaming amber liquid through a small mesh strainer into a wide porcelain mug. He slid the mug across the counter toward her. "Listen to me, Yuhan. I get to sleep in a warm house, eat good food, play chess with Grandpa on weekends, and come home every afternoon to the prettiest wife in Shanghai. If putting up with two polite questions over river perch is the price, I got the bargain of the century."

Qin Yuhan could not help laughing softly. She wrapped both cold hands around the warm mug, letting the steady heat seep into her palms before taking a careful sip. The ginger carried just enough gentle kick to warm her chest, balanced by the mellow sweetness of the red dates.

"It is really good," she said quietly, lowering her eyes to the mug. As she tilted her wrist, the mother-of-pearl dial of her new watch caught the warm kitchen light. "Chen Xuan, tell me the truth. Even with an autumn store promotion, a Swiss watch at Plaza 66 is not cheap. Did you empty your savings account for this?"

Chen Xuan leaned his elbows on the opposite side of the island, holding a plain glass of warm water. "Not even close. Do I look like a man who would leave himself short of grocery money? I still have plenty tucked away for pork ribs, fresh crabs, and your favorite strawberries."

Qin Yuhan shook her head, smiling warmly even though she still suspected he was downplaying the cost to keep her from worrying. To her, that was simply who Chen Xuan was: a quiet, grounded man who never bragged, never chased status, and spent his modest modest salary on making her daily life comfortable.

"My parents actually liked you tonight," she added after another sip, her voice bright with quiet relief. "When we dropped them at the hotel lobby, Dad told me your suggestion about the port shift handover was smarter than half the reports on his desk. Even Mom admitted you have better manners than most of the young directors she meets in Beijing."

"Then our mission was a complete success," Chen Xuan said cheerfully. "Now finish your ginger tea and go get some sleep. You have to drive them to Hongqiao Airport in the morning, and the autumn rain makes the ring road slippery."

Qin Yuhan finished the last warm swallow, rinsed her mug in the sink despite his protest, and reached out to smooth the rolled sleeve of his shirt. "Good night, Chen Xuan. Thank you for tonight."

"Sleep well, President Qin," he teased softly.

When the soft sound of her footsteps faded upstairs, the villa settled into deep, peaceful quiet. Chen Xuan turned off the kitchen pendant lamps and stood for a moment beside the rain-streaked garden window, sipping his warm water.

Beyond the wrought-iron gates, a short distance down the dark, glistening boulevard, the unmarked matte-black sedan of the Black Guard remained utterly motionless beneath the wet camphor trees. Not a single light flickered inside, yet its unseen occupants kept watch over the quiet autumn night, ensuring nothing in the world could disturb the simple warmth behind the villa's glass.
