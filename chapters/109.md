# Chapter 109: One Mug of Ginger Tea

By nine-thirty on Sunday night, a light spring rain had begun to fall over Shanghai.

After seeing Qin Zhengming and Lin Shuyin back to their riverfront hotel near the Bund, the black Maybach rolled quietly through the wrought-iron gates of Emerald Riverside Villa. Fifty meters down the rain-washed boulevard outside, the unmarked black sedan of the Black Order pulled smoothly beneath the camphor trees, its headlights clicking off into the mist.

Inside the warm villa, Chen Xuan hung his dark navy suit jacket neatly in the closet, rolled up the sleeves of his white dress shirt, and headed straight into the kitchen.

"Do not go upstairs yet," Chen Xuan called out over the gentle tap of raindrops against the glass. "The breeze off the river was damp tonight, and your hands were cold in the car. Give me five minutes."

Qin Yuhan slipped off her heels by the living room rug and walked over to the kitchen doorway. She had changed into a soft cashmere cardigan and loose home trousers, her long dark hair falling over her shoulders. On her slender left wrist sat the classic mother-of-pearl wristwatch with the navy leather strap from Plaza 66.

She leaned lightly against the doorframe, watching Chen Xuan slice fresh yellow ginger root with effortless rhythm before dropping the slices into a small copper pot of simmering water with two blocks of old brown sugar.

"You only boiled enough for one mug?" Qin Yuhan asked, watching him pour the steaming amber liquid into a wide ceramic mug.

"If I pour two mugs, you always leave half of yours on the table until it turns cold," Chen Xuan chuckled, carrying the single warm mug out to the covered second-floor terrace. "Sharing one mug keeps it hot till the last sip."

The covered terrace faced west across the bend of the Huangpu River. Rain pattered softly against the glass canopy overhead, while across the dark water, the bright lights of Lujiazui, Jin Mao Tower, and the glowing pink-and-silver spheres of the Oriental Pearl Tower reflected like floating lanterns on the river.

Chen Xuan sat down on the wide wicker sofa and held out the steaming ceramic mug.

Qin Yuhan walked over, sat down close beside him, and wrapped both hands around the warm porcelain. She blew gently across the surface and took a small sip. The sweet warmth of brown sugar and spicy ginger slid down her throat, chasing away the evening chill in an instant.

"Not bad for a twelve-thousand-yuan logistics clerk," Qin Yuhan said, her eyes curving into a soft smile.

"Careful, President Qin," Chen Xuan teased, resting one arm lazily along the back of the sofa behind her shoulders. "Keep praising my kitchen skills and I might ask you to cover my four-yuan subway fare tomorrow morning."

Qin Yuhan held the mug out toward his lips. "Drink half before you start bragging."

Chen Xuan did not take the mug from her hands. Instead, he leaned forward slightly and drank a warm sip right from the rim while her slender fingers still held the cup. His dark eyes met hers over the rising steam.

A soft pink warmth touched Qin Yuhan's cheeks under the terrace lamp. She lowered the mug to her lap, looked down at the mother-of-pearl wristwatch gleaming softly on her wrist, and stroked the smooth leather strap with her thumb.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan said quietly. "Lucky draws at Plaza 66 only hand out five-hundred-yuan perfume vouchers. They do not hand out 680k yuan Swiss watches."

Chen Xuan did not blink. He took the mug gently from her hands, set it on the low wooden table, and smiled. "The store manager said it was a special tenth-anniversary grand prize for the ten-thousandth customer who walked in carrying a green enamel thermos. If you do not believe me, we can go back next Saturday and ask him."

Qin Yuhan looked at his calm, shameless grin and let out a quiet laugh, shaking her head. She assumed he must have saved up every fen from his past few years of working or sold some old family heirloom from Yunxi County just to make sure she and her father would not lose face in front of her mother. The thought made a warm, tender ache swell inside her chest.

Without saying another word, the cool president of Haiyue Group leaned sideways and rested her head gently against Chen Xuan's broad shoulder.

Chen Xuan paused for half a breath, then lowered his chin slightly against her soft hair, letting the quiet sound of the spring rain fill the balcony.

"Before you moved into Emerald Riverside Villa," Qin Yuhan murmured softly, her voice lighter than the rain outside, "this house was always dead silent after nine o'clock. Every night I came home from Haiyue Tower, drank a glass of cold water in an empty kitchen, and read shipping contracts until midnight. My mother wanted me to marry some famous heir from Beijing so our two families could merge balance sheets. Even my grandfather, Elder Qin Zhengguo, thought I was being reckless when I chose you."

She lifted her left hand slightly, watching the balcony light catch the mother-of-pearl dial on her wrist.

"Today at dinner, when my mother tried to corner you, you did not get angry and you did not act small," Qin Yuhan whispered, a quiet warmth in her voice. "You just poured the tea, picked the bones out of my fish, and helped my father avoid a huge port trap without even asking for credit. Chen Xuan, I think marrying you was the smartest decision I ever made."

Chen Xuan curved his arm gently around her shoulder, pulling her closer against the cool night breeze.

"Of course it was," Chen Xuan said softly, a warm smile in his voice. "Where else in Shanghai could President Qin find a husband who clocks out at four fifty-five, never argues about company stock, and boils top-grade ginger tea on rainy nights?"

Qin Yuhan laughed softly against his collar, picked up the ceramic mug again, and held it back up to his lips. "Here. Finish your half before it cools."

That same rainy Sunday night in Beijing, Premier Gu Chengshan and Elder Song finished reading the weekly dispatch brought in by Number 1. Across Handong Province and China Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China, the 300 trillion yuan sovereign reserve had stabilized every provincial market without a ripple.

And on a quiet balcony in Shanghai, the man who owned it all sat happily sharing one mug of five-yuan ginger tea with his wife.
