# Chapter 114: Grandma's 81st Birthday in Hongshi Village

The next morning, the red brick courtyard of Grandmother Zhou Guilan's country house in Hongshi Village was already bustling with laughter and the rich aroma of stewed pork and longevity noodles. Five round wooden tables stood beneath the tall old pear tree in the center of the yard, its branches heavy with late ripe pears.

Down the newly paved village road by the stone bridge, a couple of friendly fruit vendors parked their electric tricycles in the shade and chatted easily with passing villagers while relatives streamed through the open gate for the old lady's 81st birthday.

Inside the sunlit courtyard, eighty-one-year-old Zhou Guilan sat in her cushioned rattan armchair wearing the festive burgundy silk jacket that Qin Yuhan had brought from Shanghai. Her silver hair was combed back neatly, and her wrinkled face was shining with happiness. From the moment Qin Yuhan had knelt beside her chair to hand her the box of soft ginseng honey cake and called her "Grandma" in her gentle voice, the old lady had refused to let go of Qin Yuhan's hand.

"Look at these hands," Zhou Guilan said fondly, patting the back of Qin Yuhan's wrist while smiling at the surrounding aunts and village neighbors. "So warm and gentle. Our Little Xuan must have done something very good in his past life to marry a girl like you."

"Grandma, Chen Xuan takes very good care of me in Shanghai," Qin Yuhan replied with a soft, sincere smile. "He cooks for me every single evening and never lets me skip meals."

"Good, good! That is how a husband should be!" Zhou Guilan laughed, her eyes crinkling into happy crescents. "If he ever dares to be lazy or make you angry, you call me right away. Even at eighty-one, I can still wave my bamboo broom at him."

A burst of warm laughter rippled across the courtyard as relatives arrived carrying baskets of fresh autumn persimmons, home-grown peanuts, and red birthday envelopes.

Over in the brick stove kitchen along the east wall, Chen Xuan had already rolled the sleeves of his gray cotton jacket up to his elbows. Standing beside the crackling wood-fired earthen stove, he lifted the heavy iron wok with effortless ease, tossing golden slices of bamboo shoot and cured village pork in a cloud of fragrant steam.

"Little Xuan, your wrist work is even better than mine now!" Second Uncle Chen Mingde laughed as he fed another dry pine branch into the stove. "How did an office clerk in Shanghai learn to cook a country banquet like a head chef?"

"Practice," Chen Xuan answered with an easy grin, sliding the glossy dish cleanly onto a wide porcelain platter. "I have a wife to feed now, Second Uncle. I cannot let her eat takeout."

Qin Yuhan walked over to the kitchen doorway holding a woven bamboo tray of freshly shelled autumn peanuts. She stood quietly for a moment watching her husband work beside the glowing hearth, his forehead dusted with a light sheen of sweat and his expression completely relaxed. In the bustling boardrooms of Shanghai, men wore tailored suits and fought over 80 million yuan deals, yet none of them looked half as steady and handsome to her as Chen Xuan standing by a country stove.

At noon, the five round tables beneath the golden pear tree were filled with smiling family members and neighbors. Steam rose from platters of braised village chicken, stewed pork belly, crispy river fish, and stir-fried autumn greens.

Chen Xuan carried out the most important dish of all: a large blue-and-white porcelain bowl filled with delicate hand-pulled longevity noodles in clear golden chicken broth, topped with a tender poached egg and bright green scallions. He set it gently on the table in front of Grandmother Zhou Guilan.

Together, Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan poured two cups of warm chrysanthemum tea and raised them respectfully.

"Grandma, happy 81st birthday," Chen Xuan said warmly. "May you stay healthy, eat well every day, and live past a hundred."

"Happy birthday, Grandma," Qin Yuhan echoed softly.

Zhou Guilan took a happy sip of tea and ate a long strand of longevity noodles while the whole courtyard applauded. Then, with a mischievous twinkle in her eyes, the old lady reached into the pocket of her burgundy jacket and pulled out a small, worn red silk pouch. She opened the drawstring and took out a pair of simple, smoothly polished silver bangles.

"Yuhan, hold out your hand," Grandma said gently.

"Grandma, I cannot accept something so precious," Qin Yuhan began softly, startled by the heirloom.

"Take it," Zhou Guilan insisted, slipping one silver bangle carefully over Qin Yuhan's slim wrist right beside her wristwatch. "My mother gave these to me when I married into Hongshi Village sixty years ago. I kept them safe for Little Xuan's wife. Now they belong to you."

Qin Yuhan's eyes glistened with genuine emotion. She lowered her head and hugged the old lady gently. "Thank you, Grandma. I will treasure it forever."

Later that afternoon, the village chief stopped by with a pot of fresh autumn pears to offer his birthday greetings. Sitting by the stone table, he looked proudly out toward the smooth black asphalt road winding past the golden fields.

"Auntie Zhou, you have great fortune," the village chief beamed. "Not only did Little Xuan bring home such a wonderful wife, but our whole Hongshi Village finally has a proper paved road and clean running water before winter arrives. The county officials told us Secretary Li Dakang pushed the rural-aid funds through right after an anonymous benefactor helped clear the province's troubles and brought the big banks in to support simple villages."

Everyone around the table nodded and praised the good times ahead. Qin Yuhan listened with a warm smile, while Chen Xuan simply leaned back in his wooden chair, calmly peeled a crisp autumn pear with a small fruit knife, and handed the sweetest slice to his wife.

He did not need anyone in Hongshi Village to know whose quiet word had paved that road. Seeing Grandma laugh beneath the golden pear tree was reward enough.
