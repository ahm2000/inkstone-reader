# Chapter 114: Li Weiming's Cold Sweat

By six on Friday evening, the sun had dropped behind the tall cranes of Shanghai Port, turning the river dark gray.

Inside his top-floor suite at the Harbor View Hotel, Li Weiming stood by the wide window, hands behind his back, an untouched glass of whiskey sitting on the coffee table behind him.

Carl Mercer and Anton Novak were supposed to call at five sharp. At five-fifteen, he had blamed rush-hour traffic. At five-thirty, their disposable phone had gone straight to a recording saying it was turned off.

Now the bedside clock clicked to 6:00 PM.

Li Weiming walked to the desk and picked up his private locked phone. It ran on a private Zurich network with no local number. Only six people in the world had its address, and Carl Mercer was one of them.

He called Carl Mercer's backup line. One dull ring sounded, then dead silence. No busy tone, no recording.

Li Weiming's jaw tightened. He reached for his regular mobile to call Feng Guoqiang's office. If local police had picked up two foreigners near the port, a lawyer could find out in ten minutes.

Before his thumb touched the screen, the private locked phone buzzed once against the wood.

Li Weiming grabbed it, expecting a code word from Carl Mercer. The screen glowed bright white with one new message inside the private chat box.

No sender name. No number. No words at all. Just an empty white box stamped 6:01 PM.

A cold knot tightened in Li Weiming's stomach. Nobody outside Zurich even knew this phone existed, let alone how to push a silent blank message into its locked inbox.

He dialed Feng Guoqiang's office with his other hand. The call failed before ringing. Feng Guoqiang's personal cell was dead too.

"Time to leave," Li Weiming muttered, his voice dry in the quiet room.

He no longer cared about Zurich's 500k fee, the Northern Ledger, Xu Wenbin, or the 318 officials in interrogation rooms. Whatever had swallowed Carl Mercer and Anton Novak had already found his private phone.

Moving fast, he zipped his laptop, passport, and cash envelope into his leather suitcase, grabbed his wool coat, and stepped into the empty carpeted hallway to check both ends before heading for the service stairs.

Then he remembered his second passport, under his real name, was still inside the bedside safe.

Cursing under his breath, he turned back and tapped his room keycard against the brass reader.

The light flashed red. A sharp double beep echoed in the corridor.

Li Weiming frowned and held the card flat against the sensor. Red light. Double beep. He wiped the card on his sleeve and tried a third time. The lock did not click. His keycard had been shut off from the front desk.

A chill crept up his spine. Without knocking or calling housekeeping, he kept his face calm, rolled his suitcase to the elevator, and rode down to the marble lobby.

Soft piano music played over the bright, quiet hall. Li Weiming walked evenly to the reception desk, where a young clerk in a dark blazer looked up.

"Good evening, Mr. Vance," the clerk said politely, his smile stiff. "How may I help you?"

"My keycard for Suite 2802 just stopped working," Li Weiming said, setting it on the marble. "Please reset it. I left a document inside."

"Certainly, sir," the clerk replied without touching the encoder. "Our duty manager is reviewing your room registration right now. If you have a seat in the lounge, he will bring a fresh card straight over."

"I have a flight to catch," Li Weiming said, his voice dropping. "Just open the door for two minutes."

"I am sorry, Mr. Vance. The system will not let me print a new key until the manager finishes his check."

Li Weiming watched the clerk's eyes. The young man was not looking at his screen. His gaze kept flicking nervously past Li Weiming's left shoulder toward the center of the lobby.

Slowly, Li Weiming turned around.

Twenty feet away, in two brown leather armchairs facing the revolving glass doors, sat two Chinese men in their late thirties wearing plain gray jackets over dark sweaters. Both held open evening newspapers beside paper cups of water. They had no luggage, keys, or laptops. Neither turned a page. Both sat upright and relaxed, watching the elevators, reception desk, and main exit.

Li Weiming shifted his eyes right, toward the side door to the underground garage. A third man in a dark windbreaker sat at the coffee bar, stirring an untouched cup of tea, angled straight toward the garage corridor.

State Security. Plainclothes officers. Not local police, not port security, not hired muscle.

Li Weiming's hands turned ice-cold. Sweat rolled down the back of his neck beneath his collar.

In twenty years of fixing quiet messes across Europe and Asia, he had dealt with angry financiers, corrupt judges, and local police chiefs. He had always known how to buy time, call a lawyer, or slip out a back door.

Here, there was no shouting, no flashing lights, no warning.

He had sent two armed veterans to look at an ordinary shipping clerk in a quiet park. Less than three hours later, both had vanished without a gunshot or call, his private Zurich phone had taken a blank message, his suite lock had died, and State Security sat calmly in his hotel lobby waiting for him.

His knuckles turned white around his suitcase handle. Zurich had no idea what they were poking at. The Northern Ledger thought they were fighting a rival group, never realizing they had brushed against something silent, bottomless, and far deadlier than Switzerland imagined.

Inside his pocket, the private locked phone buzzed once more. Li Weiming did not reach for it. He already knew the screen was blank.

Across the marble floor, the taller officer in the gray jacket folded his newspaper, set it beside his paper cup, and stood up. Looking straight into Li Weiming's eyes, he walked calmly toward the desk.
