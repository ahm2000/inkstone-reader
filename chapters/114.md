# Chapter 114: Li Weiming's Cold Sweat

By six o'clock on Friday evening, the sun had dropped behind the tall cranes of Qingyan Port, turning the river dark gray.

Inside his top-floor suite at the Harbor View Hotel, Li Weiming stood near the wide glass window with his hands behind his back. On the low coffee table behind him sat a half-full glass of whiskey that he had not touched in forty minutes.

Carl Mercer and Anton Novak were supposed to call in at five sharp.

At five-fifteen, Li Weiming had told himself they were stuck in Friday rush-hour traffic. At five-thirty, he had dialed their disposable phone. The line had gone straight to a flat recorded voice saying the number was turned off.

Now the digital clock on the bedside table clicked over to 6:00 PM.

Li Weiming walked back to the desk, picked up his private locked phone, and stared at the dark screen. This phone ran on a private Zurich network with no local number linked to it. Only six people in the world had the address for it, and Carl Mercer was one of them.

He tried calling Carl Mercer's backup line.

One dull ring sounded in his ear. Then the line cut off completely. No busy tone. No voice recording. Just dead silence.

Li Weiming's jaw tightened. He lowered the phone and reached for his regular mobile to call Feng Guoqiang's office. If the local police had picked up two foreigners near the port, a lawyer could find out within ten minutes.

Before his thumb touched the screen, the private locked phone on the desk buzzed once against the wood.

Li Weiming grabbed it immediately, expecting a short code word from Carl Mercer.

The screen lit up bright white. There was one new message inside the private chat box.

No sender name. No phone number. No words at all.

Just a completely blank message box stamped 6:01 PM.

Li Weiming stared at the empty white box. A cold knot tightened in his stomach. Nobody outside Zurich even knew this phone existed, let alone how to push a silent blank message straight into its locked inbox.

He dialed Feng Guoqiang's office number with his other hand. The call failed before it even rang. He tried Feng Guoqiang's personal cell phone. Dead.

"Time to leave," Li Weiming muttered aloud. His voice sounded dry in the quiet room.

He did not stop to think about the 500k fee from Zurich anymore. He did not care about the Northern Ledger or Xu Wenbin or the 318 officials sitting in interrogation rooms. Whatever had swallowed Carl Mercer and Anton Novak had already found his private phone.

He moved fast. He zipped his laptop, passport, and cash envelope into his slim leather suitcase, grabbed his wool coat off the chair, and walked to the heavy suite door. He stepped out into the quiet carpeted hallway to check both ends of the corridor before heading to the service stairs.

The hallway was empty. No housekeeping carts. No guests. Just soft yellow wall lamps and silence.

Then he realized his second passport, the one under his real name, was still tucked inside the bedside safe.

He cursed under his breath, turned back to the suite door, and tapped his white plastic room keycard against the brass reader.

The small light flashed red. A sharp double beep sounded in the quiet corridor.

Li Weiming frowned and tapped the card again, holding it flat against the sensor.

Red light. Double beep.

He wiped the plastic card against his coat sleeve and tried a third time. The lock did not even click. His keycard had been shut off from the front desk.

A thin chill crept up his spine. He did not knock on the door or call housekeeping. Keeping his face calm, he rolled his small suitcase down the hall, pressed the elevator button, and rode smoothly down to the main marble lobby.

The lobby was bright and quiet. Soft piano music played from speakers in the ceiling.

Li Weiming walked straight toward the long reception desk, keeping his stride even. A young desk clerk in a neat dark blazer looked up as he approached.

"Good evening, Mr. Vance," the clerk said politely, though his smile looked stiff and practiced. "How may I help you?"

"My keycard for Suite 2802 just stopped working," Li Weiming said, laying the plastic card on the marble counter. "Please reset it. I left a document inside."

"Certainly, sir," the clerk replied without reaching for the card encoder. "Our duty manager is reviewing your room registration right now. If you would like to have a seat in the lounge for a few minutes, he will bring a fresh card straight over."

"I have a flight to catch," Li Weiming said, his voice dropping half an octave. "Just open the door for two minutes."

"I am sorry, Mr. Vance. The system will not let me print a new key until the manager finishes his check."

Li Weiming looked closely at the clerk's eyes. The young man was not looking at the computer screen. His gaze kept flicking nervously past Li Weiming's left shoulder toward the center of the lobby.

Slowly, Li Weiming turned around.

Twenty feet away, seated in two brown leather armchairs facing the main glass revolving doors, sat two Chinese men in their late thirties. Both wore plain gray jackets over dark sweaters. Both held open copies of the evening newspaper, with plain paper cups of water resting on the small table between them.

They did not look like hotel guests. They did not have luggage, room keys, or laptops. Neither man turned a page of his newspaper. Both sat in upright, relaxed postures that gave them a clear view of the elevators, the reception desk, and the main exit.

Li Weiming shifted his eyes slowly to the right, toward the side door leading out to the underground parking garage.

A third man in a plain dark windbreaker sat at the edge of the coffee bar, stirring an untouched cup of tea, his chair angled directly toward the garage corridor.

State Security. Plainclothes officers. Not local police, not port security, not hired muscle from a rival company.

Li Weiming's hands turned ice-cold. Sweat rolled down the back of his neck beneath his shirt collar.

In twenty years of fixing quiet messes across Europe and Asia, he had dealt with angry financiers, corrupt judges, and local police chiefs. He had always known how to buy time, call a lawyer, or slip out a back door before handcuffs arrived.

Here, there was no shouting, no flashing lights, and no warning.

He had sent two armed veterans to look at an ordinary shipping clerk in a quiet park. Less than three hours later, both men had vanished without a gunshot or phone call, his private Zurich phone had taken a silent blank message, his suite lock had died, and State Security officers sat calmly in his hotel lobby waiting for him.

His fingers tightened around his suitcase handle until his knuckles turned white.

Zurich had no idea what they were poking at. The Northern Ledger thought they were fighting a rival group. They did not realize they had brushed against something silent, bottomless, and far deadlier than Switzerland ever imagined.

Inside his pocket, the private locked phone buzzed once more. Li Weiming did not reach for it. He already knew the screen was blank.

Across the marble floor, the taller officer in the gray jacket slowly folded his newspaper, set it beside his paper cup, and stood up. He looked straight into Li Weiming's eyes and walked calmly toward the desk.
