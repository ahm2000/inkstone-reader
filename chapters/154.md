# Chapter 154: Long-Life Noodles

The moment the quiet hum of the black sedans faded down the village road, the courtyard burst into excited chatter.

"Did you see that?" an old neighbor named Uncle Wang gasped, slapping his thigh. "Secretary Zhao himself walked right through that wooden gate! And he held young Xuan's hand with both of his own!"

"I saw him on television only last night," another villager beamed at Chen Jianguo and Chen Mingde. "Jianguo, how does your boy know the Provincial Party Secretary?"

Chen Jianguo was still blinking in disbelief, though a proud smile was already spreading across his face.

"Xuan probably helped with a city shipping project through his company," Chen Jianguo said modestly. "Secretary Zhao is just a kind leader who cares about ordinary people."

"Right, a very kind leader," Chen Mingde agreed, straightening his collar with a wide grin. "And he praised our whole family."

Over by the brick wall, nobody was smiling.

Liu Guoliang stood stiffly with his hands jammed into his coat pockets, his face shifting from chalk white to a dull, burning red.

Chen Hao leaned back on his stool, tossing a roasted peanut into his mouth with a bright, innocent grin.

"Hey, Uncle Guoliang," Chen Hao called out cheerfully. "You were telling us about real connections earlier. Do those port managers from the Shen Family down in Shenzhen also have the Provincial Party Secretary drop by their courtyards for noodles?"

Several younger cousins snickered behind their hands.

Liu Guoliang's mouth opened and closed without a sound. He fumbled in his pocket, pulled out his phone even though it had not rung, and pressed it stiffly to his ear.

"Ah, yes, hello?" Liu Guoliang stammered loudly, avoiding everyone's eyes. "The warehouse? Right now? All right, I am on my way!"

He lowered the blank screen and gave Grandma Zhou Guilan a hasty, crooked nod across the yard. "Aunt Zhou, something urgent came up in the city. I have to run. Happy birthday again!"

He did not dare walk out the front gate past the watching neighbors. Instead, Liu Guoliang spun around, ducked behind the firewood stack, unlatched the small wooden back gate, and slipped into the narrow alley like a startled rabbit. A minute later, his sedan roared hastily in reverse down the dirt lane.

Grandma Zhou Guilan chuckled softly, shaking her head. "Goodness, that boy is always in a rush. Come on, everyone, lunch is almost ready!"

The tension vanished completely, replaced by laughter and the warm smell of simmering soup.

Inside the small brick kitchen, steam rose from the big iron pot over the wood stove. Liu Meifang was slicing green onions while Wu Lanying rolled out thin sheets of handmade noodle dough.

Before Liu Meifang could tell her to rest outside, Qin Yuhan stepped quietly into the kitchen, hung her cream coat on a wooden peg, rolled up her sweater sleeves, and tied on a spare blue cotton apron.

"Aunt Liu, Aunt Wu, let me help," Qin Yuhan said with an easy smile. "I can fry the eggs and wash the greens."

"Oh, no, Yuhan, you are a guest!" Liu Meifang protested at once. "Your hands will smell like oil. Go sit with Xuan in the sun."

"I love cooking in a real kitchen," Qin Yuhan replied lightly, cracking an egg cleanly with one hand into the hot pan so the edges turned crisp and golden without a splash. "Back in the city, my kitchen barely gets used. Please let me help."

Liu Meifang stopped protesting and watched in delight as Qin Yuhan moved around the stove with calm, practiced ease. She rinsed the winter greens, scooped golden fried eggs onto a white plate, and ladled steaming bone broth into ceramic bowls without spilling a drop.

Just outside the kitchen doorway, Grandma Zhou Guilan sat wrapped in Chen Xuan's dark red wool shawl, watching Qin Yuhan through the open door. The old woman's face lit up with a huge, joyful smile.

She leaned closer when Liu Meifang stepped out for garlic. "Meifang," Zhou Guilan whispered happily. "Look at that girl. Not a shred of pride, even though the Provincial Secretary knows her family. Look how gently she handles the bowls. Where did Xuan find such a wonderful girl?"

"I told you she was special, Mom," Liu Meifang whispered back, beaming. "She is kind, smart, and treats us like family."

Ten minutes later, two round wooden tables in the sunny yard were loaded with braised pork, stir-fried greens, peanuts, pickled radish, and big ceramic bowls of handmade long-life noodles topped with golden fried eggs and fragrant sesame oil.

Liu Meifang carried the largest bowl to Grandma Zhou Guilan first, setting it down with clean bamboo chopsticks.

"Grandma, happy eighty-first birthday," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Eat the noodles slowly so you live to be a hundred."

"One hundred? Then I will still be nagging you to eat lunch twenty years from now!" Zhou Guilan laughed heartily, her joyful voice ringing across the courtyard.

Everyone lifted their teacups and small cups of warm rice wine together.

"Happy birthday, Grandma!" Chen Yuan and Chen Hao shouted in unison.

"To good health and long life!" Chen Jianguo and Chen Mingde added, clinking cups with bright, relaxed smiles.

The afternoon passed in unhurried warmth. Chen Hao finished two full bowls in five minutes and got teased by Chen Yuan until he turned red. Beside Grandma Zhou Guilan, Qin Yuhan poured warm mountain tea from her bamboo tins, listening with genuine delight as the old woman told funny stories about Chen Xuan climbing persimmon trees at age seven. Chen Xuan sat quietly across the table, eating a tangerine and meeting Qin Yuhan's amused gaze with a small, warm smile.

By late afternoon, the winter sun dipped behind the low hills. After helping Liu Meifang wash every bowl, Qin Yuhan untied her apron and said goodbye to Grandma Zhou Guilan, who insisted on stuffing bags of homegrown peanuts and dried persimmons into her hands.

Chen Xuan walked Qin Yuhan down the lane toward her car.

"Thank you for coming today, Yuhan," Chen Xuan said softly near the stone bridge. "Grandma has not smiled that much in years."

"I am glad I came," Qin Yuhan replied, tucking a strand of hair behind her ear. "Sitting in that little kitchen felt better than any banquet in the city."

She paused beside her car door, her expression turning slightly thoughtful. "Enjoy tomorrow's rest while you can. My uncle Qin Zhengrong called while I was driving here. Our first ship on the new Shenzhen line arrives Monday, and he said the local docks down there are already acting strange."

Chen Xuan looked across the darkening fields for a quiet second, then smiled calmly. "Do not worry about work today. Drive safely back to Shanghai."

Fifty meters back by the bridge, the quiet Black Guard member stood motionless in the dusk until Qin Yuhan's taillights vanished around the bend and Chen Xuan walked peacefully back toward the warm lights of his grandmother's home.
