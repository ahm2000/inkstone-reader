# Chapter 154: Driving Back to Emerald Riverside

When it came time to head back to the city, the visit felt far too short. By the time Chen Xuan backed the silver sedan up to the courtyard gate, Grandma Zhou Guilan and Second Uncle Chen Mingde had already turned the trunk into a miniature harvest warehouse.

Two sturdy bamboo baskets brimming with firm orange persimmons sat beside a heavy canvas sack of red skinned sweet potatoes. Tucked into every spare corner were bags of dried mountain mushrooms, roasted village peanuts, and three glass jars of Grandma's homemade chili sauce wrapped carefully in old newspapers.

"Grandma, if you add one more sack, the rear tires are going to rub the fenders," Chen Xuan laughed, gently easing the trunk lid shut until the latch clicked.

"Nonsense, young people need good food," Grandma scolded lightly, adjusting the collar of Qin Yuhan's coat one last time. "Drive slowly on the highway, Xuan. Call me the moment you reach home."

"We will, Grandma," Qin Yuhan promised, giving the elderly woman a warm hug before waving to Second Uncle and the cousins.

"See you next Saturday, Brother Xuan! See you, Sister in law!" Chen Hao and Chen Yuan shouted from the stone steps as the car rolled smoothly down the village lane.

Once they merged onto the expressway heading back toward Shanghai, the late autumn sun began to dip toward the western hills, painting the bare treetops in rich shades of amber and gold. Inside the car, soft music played at a low volume and the faint, sweet scent of ripe persimmons drifted forward from the trunk.

Qin Yuhan slipped off her sneakers, leaned her seat back slightly, and reached into the small paper bag on her lap. She peeled a soft, honey ripe persimmon with neat fingers and waited until they exited the expressway into Pudong and stopped at a quiet red light.

"Open up," she said softly, holding a sweet slice toward him.

Chen Xuan leaned over and took a bite, smiling as the cool sweetness melted on his tongue. "Not bad. Tastes even better when someone else peels it."

"Enjoy it while it lasts," she teased, wiping her fingertips with a tissue. "Tomorrow morning it is back to spreadsheets, board meetings, and my twelve thousand yuan husband pretending he cannot lift a stapler."

By the time they pulled into the quiet driveway of Emerald Riverside Villa, twilight had settled over the Huangpu River and the warm yellow garden lamps had flickered on along the stone path. Together they carried the heavy baskets and sacks into the bright kitchen, lining the wooden counter with glowing orange fruit and fresh mountain produce.

"Grandma really packed enough for a month," Qin Yuhan laughed, arranging a neat pyramid of persimmons in a wide ceramic bowl for the living room coffee table. "I should bring a box over to Grandpa Qin's house later this week, and you can take a bag to your coworkers at Hengxin tomorrow morning."

"Already set a bag aside for Wang Lei and the team," Chen Xuan replied, washing his hands and tying on his faded blue apron. "And I will air out both guest rooms on Friday so Chen Hao and Chen Yuan have fresh sheets waiting."

He scrubbed six of the smallest village sweet potatoes, pricked their skins lightly with a fork, and slid them into the oven to roast slowly until their natural sugars began to caramelize. While the oven hummed, he peeled two pears, sliced fresh ginger, and set a small ceramic pot to simmer a light, soothing soup to take off the late autumn evening chill.

Forty minutes later, the villa smelled like roasted honey and warm ginger. They ate a relaxed dinner at the kitchen counter, chatting about how excited Chen Hao had looked when talking about Fudan University.

"By the way," Qin Yuhan said casually as she spooned up the last of her warm pear soup, "I might be a little busy starting tomorrow. Haiyue Group's maritime logistics division has a major shipment of precision industrial equipment arriving through the southern coast early this week. Our operations director even signed a local inland subcontract with a Shanghai firm to handle final delivery."

Chen Xuan nodded mildly, peeling the crisp dark skin off a roasted sweet potato for her. "Sounds like a big project. Do not skip meals if meetings run late."

"I will try," she smiled gratefully, taking the steaming sweet potato. "I am going to take a hot shower first. My shoulders still feel yesterday's digging."

While water ran softly upstairs, Chen Xuan rinsed the soup bowls, poured himself a mug of warm green tea, and stepped out onto the quiet riverfront terrace. A cool late November breeze brushed over the dark water of the Huangpu, carrying the distant hum of a passing barge.

Inside his jacket pocket, his single everyday smartphone vibrated once with a short, soft buzz.

Chen Xuan pulled it out and glanced at the glowing screen. It was an encrypted text from Number 1, clean and concise as always.

"Young Master, our southern coastal watch logged unusual port delays at Shenzhen Pier Nine over the past forty eight hours. Private terminal operators tied to the Shen Family appear to be tightening berth inspections on inbound Shanghai cargo. Standing by for instructions."

Chen Xuan's gaze paused on the words for two calm seconds, his expression completely unhurried as he sipped his tea.

"Keep watching," he typed back with one thumb. "Do not move yet. Let them show their hand."

He locked his smartphone, slid it back into his pocket, and returned to the warm living room just as Qin Yuhan came downstairs in soft flannel pajamas, her hair still damp. They curled up together on the wide sofa with an old movie playing softly on the screen and a plate of warm village sweet potatoes resting between them, enjoying the last peaceful hours of the weekend.
