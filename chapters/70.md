# Chapter 70: The Convoy on the Yunxi Highway

Cool morning mist clung to the steel guardrails along the Yunxi County highway tollgate. Dew darkened the asphalt, and the air smelled of wet pine and river silt. Ahead of the booths, every red and white barrier arm stood locked straight up.

Two tollbooth workers leaned out of their sliding windows with their mouths open. They watched a long black convoy sweep through the open lanes without tapping the brakes or slowing for a ticket.

Eighteen unmarked black sedans led the column, followed by four armored special police vans and six patrol cars pulled from neighboring districts. No sirens wailed. No roof lights flashed. Only the steady, heavy rumble of twenty-eight engines rolled down the highway toward Yunxi County.

In the back seat of the lead sedan, Hou Liangping, Chief of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau, sat beside Ji Changming, Chief Procurator of the Handong Provincial Procuratorate. Thick kraft folders stamped with red wax seals rested across their knees.

"We cross the river bridge in five minutes," Ji Changming said, glancing through the rain-streaked glass. "The technical unit finished their work before dawn. Every landline inside the Yunxi County Government Compound and the local public security bureau is cut off under an emergency cable repair notice. Mobile relays within two kilometers are throttled."

Hou Liangping adjusted his cuffs and looked down at the arrest warrants in his lap. "Good. Sun Weiming and Liang Guodong have ruled this county like a private estate for ten years. If even one phone call gets out, they will shred their ledgers before we reach the gate."

"They will try to call Qi Tongwei first," Ji Changming said evenly. "Everyone in Handong Province knows how tightly the Handa Faction protects its county chiefs. Even after Gao Yuliang and Zhao Ruilong were taken into custody, the mid-level officials down here still believe the storm will stop at Jingzhou City."

"Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin made his order clear last night," Hou Liangping replied. His voice carried a quiet, unyielding weight. "No protected zones. No delayed arrests. Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang has already sealed the municipal side of the paperwork. Today, we pull the roots out of Yunxi County."

Ji Changming let out a slow breath and tapped the top folder. Inside lay the financial trail linking Hongrui Real Estate, Shanshui Group's local flagship front, to a dozen forced demolitions along Old Street. Beneath those bank records sat a single overnight detention slip from Old Street Police Station.

"Secretary Sha was unusually strict about one detail," Ji Changming murmured, lowering his voice even though the partition glass was up. "He told us that the hardware shopkeeper held in Old Street Station belongs to an untouchable Yunxi household. Word came straight from the highest level in Beijing. Whoever holds the Black Order does not want a single hair on that family's head touched."

Hou Liangping's expression turned grave. He did not know the name of the person behind the Black Order, and he knew better than to ask. When the top leadership in Beijing moved with that kind of speed, a provincial bureau chief only needed to know two things: the law had been broken, and justice had to be delivered without a single mistake.

"We do not need to know who they are," Hou Liangping said firmly. "Even without a word from Beijing, fabricating criminal charges to steal an honest merchant's shop is enough to put every single one of those officials behind bars. The fact that they kicked an iron plate just speeds up their fall."

Outside, the convoy swept onto the wide concrete span of the Yunxi River Bridge. Morning sunlight broke through the low clouds, flashing off the brown river water below. Ahead, the low rooftops of the old county town stretched out beneath a single gleaming twenty-story glass tower.

Hongrui Plaza stood over the county like a monument to Shanshui Group's money. Giant gold letters shone across its roof, visible from three miles away.

"Look at that building," Hou Liangping said, his eyes narrowing. "Built on stolen land, funded by dirty loans, and guarded by crooked police. Zhao Ruilong thought nobody in Handong would ever dare touch his backyard."

"That ends this morning," Ji Changming said. He checked his wristwatch. "The county leadership holds its weekly roll call at 8:30 AM. Every department head will be sitting in the same third-floor room."

Hou Liangping picked up the encrypted radio handset from the center console. He pressed the transmit button, his tone crisp and calm.

"All units, prepare to split at the main avenue intersection," Hou Liangping ordered. "Team One follows my sedan straight into the Yunxi County Government Compound. Lock every exit before 8:30 AM. Team Two takes the eastern ring road to Old Street Police Station and secures the holding cells. Team Three holds two blocks west of Hongrui Plaza until I give the seal order."

Short, disciplined confirmations crackled back over the channel.

As the convoy rolled off the bridge and entered the tree-lined streets of Yunxi County, early commuters on electric scooters pulled hurriedly to the curb. Shopkeepers raising their metal shutters paused in mid-motion, staring at the endless line of dark provincial license plates gliding silently past their doors.

No one in the street knew who was inside those cars, yet everyone felt the sudden drop in temperature across the town.

Inside the lead sedan, Hou Liangping closed his folder and looked straight ahead at the gray concrete gates of the county compound.

"Driver," Hou Liangping said softly. "Take us right to the front steps."

The black sedans accelerated in smooth formation, splitting cleanly at the crossroads like a drawn blade cutting straight into the heart of Yunxi County.
