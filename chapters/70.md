# Chapter 70: The Convoy on the Yunxi Highway

Cool autumn mist clung to the steel guardrails along the Yunxi County highway tollgate. Dew darkened the asphalt, and the early morning air smelled of wet pine, river silt, and fallen ginkgo leaves. Ahead of the booths, every red and white barrier arm stood locked straight up.

Two tollbooth workers leaned out of their sliding windows and stared in silence as a long black column swept through the open lanes without tapping the brakes or slowing for a ticket.

Eighteen unmarked black sedans led the formation, followed by four armored special police vans and six patrol cars pulled from neighboring districts. No roof lights flashed across the grey morning sky. Only the steady, heavy rumble of twenty-eight engines rolled down the highway toward Yunxi County.

In the back seat of the lead sedan, Hou Liangping, Chief of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau, sat beside Ji Changming, Chief Procurator of Handong Province. A thick brown case binder lay open across Hou Liangping's knees, its pages marked with yellow tabs and bank transfer receipts.

"That is the third tollgate we cleared without a single delay," Hou Liangping said, checking his wristwatch. It was 7:35 AM. "Secretary Sha Ruijin wanted the road net sealed before the county offices wake up, and the traffic bureau followed the order to the minute."

Ji Changming adjusted his glasses and looked out at the rows of yellowing poplars flashing past the tinted window. "Speed matters today, Liangping. If word reaches the Yunxi County leadership before 8:30 AM, the real estate ledgers at Hongrui will turn into ash before we reach the front gate."

Hou Liangping turned a page in the binder and tapped a stamped land-transfer chart. "Let us review the chain one more time before we split the teams. Hongrui Real Estate is registered as a local county developer, yet 85 percent of its working capital flows straight through two shell holding companies controlled by Shanshui Group in Jingzhou. Over the past nine months, Hongrui bought out fourteen parcels along Old Street at less than a third of market value."

"Not bought out," Ji Changming corrected quietly. "Squeezed out. Our forensic team compared the county court dockets against Old Street Police Station's detention logs. Every time a small shopkeeper refused Hongrui's lowball relocation offer, the station opened a fabricated administrative case within forty-eight hours. Fire code violations, unlicensed storage, disturbing public order. Three families signed away their leases inside an interrogation room just to get their relatives released."

Hou Liangping's eyes narrowed. "And last night they pushed the same playbook against Mingde Hardware on the corner lot. That single corner parcel is the final bottleneck blocking Hongrui's commercial redevelopment tender, which closes at noon today. They sent county officers and hired demolition brokers to slap a fake detention charge on the owner so they could clear the block before the provincial audit team arrived."

"They got greedy," Ji Changming said. "More importantly, they thought their umbrella in Jingzhou would keep provincial investigators out of a small county street."

"That umbrella is cracking as we speak," Hou Liangping replied. "Secretary Sha Ruijin made his position clear during the 6:00 AM briefing. Handong Province cannot clean up Shanshui Group and its backers in Jingzhou if county police stations and land bureaus are operating as private debt collectors for property syndicates. If we let a county proxy network run unchecked, every merchant in Handong learns that the law belongs to whoever pays the highest bribe."

Ji Changming nodded, resting one hand on a leather briefcase containing twelve signed detention warrants and six corporate asset-freeze orders. "That is why we pulled patrol officers from neighboring districts instead of notifying Yunxi County Public Security. Every warrant in this briefcase was signed directly by the provincial procuratorate before dawn. Every account freeze is backed by audited clearing records from the provincial banking regulator."

"How are the three task groups dividing the sweep?" Hou Liangping asked.

"Group One goes straight to Hongrui Real Estate's accounting office to secure their physical ledgers, tax drives, and contract vaults before the morning clerks arrive," Ji Changming said. "Group Two enters the county land and urban planning bureau to seal the Old Street redevelopment tender files and detain Deputy Director Fang for questioning. You and I take Group Three directly to Old Street Police Station."

Hou Liangping closed the binder and slipped it into his satchel. "Good. The station chief, Deputy Chief Ma, and their shift supervisors are all on Hongrui's monthly payroll according to the bank traces. Once we relieve the station leadership and secure their duty logs, we will find out which officers actually upheld the law and which ones sold their badges."

"One more thing," Ji Changming said, lowering his voice slightly. "Secretary Sha wants the entire operation handled strictly by the book. No public spectacle, no leaking names to the press before formal indictments are filed, and zero disruption to the morning market on Old Street. The shopkeepers there have endured months of intimidation. They need to see calm, lawful authority restoring order, not another wave of chaos."

"Understood," Hou Liangping said. "We detain the corrupt officials, freeze the illicit accounts, and hand the honest residents their street back before the 8:30 AM roll call."

The driver slowed smoothly as the convoy exited the Yunxi highway ramp and merged onto the wide, tree-lined avenue leading into town. Golden autumn sunlight broke over the distant hills, catching the rooftops of Old Street where breakfast stalls were just lifting their shutters and steaming baskets of buns sent thin white plumes into the crisp morning air.

Hou Liangping picked up his radio handset and spoke cleanly into the channel. "All three teams, we are five minutes from the county junction. Maintain formation, execute on my mark, and keep every procedure airtight."

Twenty-eight engines answered in a steady, measured hum as the convoy rolled into Yunxi County.
