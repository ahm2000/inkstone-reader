# Chapter 70: The Convoy on the Yunxi Highway

At eight fifteen on Monday morning, the barrier arms at the Yunxi County highway tollgate stood locked straight up. Two tollbooth workers leaned out their windows with open mouths, watching the black convoy sweep through without slowing for a ticket.

Eighteen unmarked black provincial CCDI sedans led the way, followed by four armored counter-terrorism vans and six patrol cars from neighboring districts. No sirens wailed. No roof lights flashed. Only the roar of twenty-eight engines rolled toward Yunxi County.

In the back seat of the lead sedan, Provincial CCDI Secretary Hou Liangping and Provincial PSB Deputy Chief Ji Changming sat side by side with folders spread across their knees. Up front, the driver kept his eyes glued to the road.

"We clear the river bridge in five minutes," Ji Changming said, checking the dash clock. "The tech team confirmed on the secure radio. Yunxi County PSB's radio towers are tracked and ready to drop to dead static the second we cross the county line."

"Good," Hou Liangping replied, scanning his case summary. "Walk me through the five main pieces of the Yunxi case one last time before we split up. I want zero loose ends when we put Sun Weiming and Cheng Biao in front of the cameras."

Ji Changming turned to Tab One and tapped two X-ray prints clipped side by side. "Piece One: Chen Mingde's fake hospital X-ray. On Friday night, Yunxi County People's Hospital Deputy Director Peng Shulin altered the file details on a chest X-ray taken three months ago from an unrelated car-crash patient. Peng Shulin stamped Chen Mingde's name and Friday's date onto two cracked ribs to fake a serious assault charge against the hardware shopkeeper."

Hou Liangping's mouth pressed into a thin line. "Do we have the original hospital computer log?"

"Pulled from the provincial medical insurance system at four o'clock," Ji Changming confirmed crisply. "The original record does not match the print in Cheng Biao's folder. Peng Shulin signed the fake report seventeen minutes after Cheng Biao called his private phone. That puts Peng Shulin and Cheng Biao away for faking criminal proof."

"Move to Piece Two," Hou Liangping said, rolling an unlit cigarette between his fingers.

Ji Changming flipped to Tab Two. "Piece Two: Old Street Station Director Cheng Biao's pre-typed arrest warrant. After Yan Tiehu's thugs failed to scare Chen Mingde into selling his shop on the old commercial street, Cheng Biao sent two patrol vans to drag Chen Mingde out without a real summons. But before the patrol van left the yard, Cheng Biao typed the warrant into the provincial police system under his director code, at eight eighteen, twelve minutes before the fake call came in."

"Twelve minutes," Hou Liangping repeated coldly, disgust tightening his jaw. "Did Cheng Biao really think provincial police computers fail to keep a locked record of every keystroke?"

"Eleven years of acting like a little king in Yunxi County rots a man's brain," Ji Changming muttered. "He thought nobody above county level would ever check a hardware shopkeeper's arrest time. He never dreamed the logs would land on Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua's desk before sunrise."

Hou Liangping shot Ji Changming a sharp glance. "Watch the names, Ji Changming. Even inside this car."

Ji Changming exhaled slowly and nodded. "Understood. Provincial authority only. Piece Three: Yan Tiehu's Pier Four smuggling."

Ji Changming pulled out drone photos and truck scale slips. "Gang leader Yan Tiehu runs three unregistered steel barges out of Yunxi County's Pier Four every Tuesday and Friday night. Under Cheng Biao's police escort, Yan Tiehu unloads untaxed factory machines, smuggled copper bars, and stripped electronics shipped downriver from Shanghai Port's Pier Seven. We have satellite tracking, fuel slips, and signed confessions from two deckhands who flipped at Pier Seven an hour ago."

Hou Liangping cracked the rear window half an inch. Cold highway wind swept through the car.

"Piece Four," Hou Liangping said, opening his red-tabbed banking binder. "Chairman Deng Yongchang's 4.8 billion yuan shell pipeline. Linking Pier Four in Yunxi County and Pier Seven in Shanghai Port, Deng Yongchang built twenty-seven shell shipping, demolition, and hardware-supply companies. Over five years, those shells moved 4.8 billion yuan through provincial trade accounts, washing smuggling profits while stealing highway and city rebuild money."

Ji Changming let out a low whistle. "4.8 billion yuan moving through a county hardware-supply shell web. No wonder Deng Yongchang wanted Chen Mingde's old commercial street torn down so badly. That block sits right next to the Pier Four road."

"Not just land," Hou Liangping said, eyes narrowing through the smoke. "Piece Five ties the whole chain around County Magistrate Sun Weiming's neck. Read Tab Five."

Ji Changming scanned the typed pages. His eyebrows rose, and a cold smile touched his mouth.

"County Magistrate Sun Weiming's tea-room recordings," Ji Changming murmured. "Three clear audio recordings made inside the private bamboo tea room behind Yunxi County Guest House over the past four months. Sun Weiming is on tape laughing with Chairman Deng Yongchang over a twelve-percent kickback split on the old street rebuild, bragging that Deputy Secretary Gao Yuliang protects them in Jingzhou City, and telling Cheng Biao to bury any stubborn shopkeeper who refuses to sign."

Ji Changming snapped the binder shut as the Yunxi County highway sign flashed past.

"Think about how crazy this is, Hou Liangping," Ji Changming said quietly, rubbing his forehead. "A vice governor in Jingzhou City, a customs director in Shanghai, a senior colonel in Southern Theater Command, a billionaire kingpin like Deng Yongchang, a county magistrate, and a police chief. Every single one of them falls in broad daylight today because they could not leave one stubborn fifty-four-year-old hardware shopkeeper alone."

Hou Liangping looked ahead as Yunxi County's rooftops rose along the skyline.

"Do not guess who handed that file upstairs, Ji Changming," Hou Liangping said, voice level and sharp. "Our job is not to stare at the sky. Our job is to put every corrupt official in Yunxi County in handcuffs before nine o'clock, and make sure Chen Mingde walks through his own front door clean, unharmed, and untouched."

The dash radio crackled. Commander Wu Zheng's crisp voice came through. "Lead sedan, Columns One through Four are past the tollgate. Yunxi County line in three minutes. Yunxi PSB radios are offline."

Hou Liangping lifted the black handset without a pause.

"Copy that, Commander Wu Zheng," Hou Liangping ordered coldly. "It is eight twenty. Split into four strike teams at the main crossroads. Column Four secures Chen Mingde at Old Street Station first. Columns One, Two, and Three lock down County Government, Yunxi PSB, and Pier Four at the same time. Make every arrest in broad daylight."

Ahead of the lead car, the black convoy spread across three lanes, closing over Yunxi County like a steel net.
