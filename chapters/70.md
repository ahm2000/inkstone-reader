# Chapter 70: Tea with Qin Yuhan

Late afternoon sunlight slanted softly across the fourth-floor garden terrace of the Lanshui International Convention Hotel, turning the maple leaves along the balustrade a warm shade of copper.

Two floors below, inside the gilded VIP Conservatory, jazz music floated above the clinking of crystal glasses as provincial tycoons, bankers, and officials crowded around the cocktail reception.

Out on the quiet terrace, Chen Xuan sat alone at a wrought-iron table with a white ceramic pot of plain green tea and the glossy blue contract booklet resting beside his elbow.

Light, unhurried footsteps clicked across the stone tiles.

Chen Xuan looked up and smiled warmly as Qin Yuhan stepped through the glass doors, wearing a crisp cream-colored blazer over a dark silk blouse, her hair pinned cleanly with a single jade clasp.

"I thought I might find you out here," Qin Yuhan said, a genuine smile softening the coolness in her eyes. "The VIP reception downstairs is packed with people trying to buy Chairman Song Baisheng a drink. I lasted ten minutes before the air gave me a headache."

"Sit down, Miss Qin Yuhan," Chen Xuan said gently, pouring her a steaming cup of pale green tea. "The wind is nice out here, and the hotel tea is decent once it steeps properly."

Qin Yuhan took the chair across from him, resting her fingers around the warm porcelain cup. Representing Haiyue Hospitality at the forum, she had sat near the front VIP tables all day, close enough to see every whisper near the stage.

After a slow sip of tea, her expression turned serious.

"Chen Xuan, I did not come up here just to escape the cocktails," Qin Yuhan said quietly, glancing toward the empty terrace doors. "I wanted to warn you and General Manager Sun Haoran before tomorrow morning's session."

"About Wanfeng Freight Group's consolidation plan?" Chen Xuan asked calmly.

"Yes," Qin Yuhan confirmed, her brows knitting. "This is not an ordinary industry proposal. During the afternoon recess, I saw Song Baisheng and that European partner, Li Weiming, leaving the twenty-eighth floor private conference suite together. Deputy Provincial Governor Wu Zhenbang walked out three minutes later."

Chen Xuan nodded mildly, showing no alarm. "Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang is pushing the plan through?"

"More than pushing it," Qin Yuhan replied grimly. "My father, Qin Zhengming, made two quiet calls to old friends in the provincial commerce office an hour ago. Word came back that Wu Zhenbang has instructed the committee to fast-track the Provincial Freight Alliance resolution into an executive pilot decree tomorrow morning at nine o'clock."

She paused, looking at Chen Xuan with unmistakable concern.

"Once Wu Zhenbang signs that decree, Song Baisheng does not need a majority vote from smaller carriers. Wanfeng Freight Group will immediately control coastal port clearance slots. Hengxin Logistics will be squeezed out of Lanshui East Port within a month, and even Haiyue Group's regional supply chain will feel the chokehold."

"Thank you for telling me, Miss Qin Yuhan," Chen Xuan said sincerely. "Please thank Chairman Qin Zhengming for checking on our behalf."

Qin Yuhan studied his unhurried face, bewildered by how relaxed he looked while his company stood hours from being swallowed.

"You do not look worried at all," Qin Yuhan murmured. "Sun Haoran looked like he aged five years during the morning panel. Do you plan to persuade the other county carriers to protest?"

"Protesting an executive decree rarely works when a deputy governor sits in the front row," Chen Xuan replied mildly, refilling his cup.

Then, with two fingers, he slid the glossy blue Wanfeng Freight Group booklet across the table, already opened to page forty-eight.

"Actually, Miss Qin Yuhan, since you read international commercial contracts far more often than an ordinary clerk like me, could I ask a small question?" Chen Xuan asked politely.

"Of course," Qin Yuhan said, glancing down at the booklet. "What is it?"

"Near the bottom of page forty-eight, under Article 14," Chen Xuan said evenly, his tone casual as commenting on the weather. "I noticed a small paragraph about automated port telemetry. Why would a domestic provincial trucking alliance require every coastal port manifest, container weight, and rare-earth logistics schedule to be mirrored in real time to a third-party analytics server in Zurich?"

Silence fell instantly over the terrace.

Qin Yuhan's fingers froze on the glossy margin. She leaned forward sharply, scanning the tiny pale-gray print of Article 14, Section 3, line by line.

Her breath caught softly.

As the heir to Haiyue Group, Qin Yuhan knew domestic shipping law inside out. Regional logistics platforms were strictly required to keep port manifests and strategic mineral transit records on domestic provincial servers under customs oversight. Routing live rare-earth freight schedules and coastal port throughput directly to an unregulated private server in Zurich was no software quirk.

It was a national security breach disguised as supply-chain optimization.

Qin Yuhan's eyes widened as she looked up from the page to Chen Xuan's peaceful face.

"Rare-earth transit schedules, customs timestamps, and Lanshui East Port deep-water manifests," Qin Yuhan whispered, her voice tight with shock. "Buried on page forty-eight in eight-point gray font under an equipment appendix. Not one lawyer in the VIP hall read past page twelve today. How did you spot this in ten minutes?"

"I am used to checking freight manifests carefully," Chen Xuan answered modestly, sipping his tea. "If one decimal point is wrong on a county delivery sheet, Manager Hu Dewei makes me stay late to fix it. So I always read the back pages first."

Qin Yuhan stared at him for three seconds, a chill running down her spine as the full picture clicked into place.

"Li Weiming," Qin Yuhan breathed softly. "Nordic-Pacific Capital is registered through Zurich. They are not funding Song Baisheng for county trucking fees. They are using Wanfeng Freight Group and Wu Zhenbang's signature to build a legal backdoor straight into Lanshui Province's coastal ports."

"It does look improper, does it not?" Chen Xuan remarked mildly, setting his cup down. "Especially for a provincial modernization project."

Qin Yuhan closed the booklet slowly, her heart racing. "Chen Xuan, if we hand this to the wrong provincial office tonight, Wu Zhenbang will bury it before sunrise and quietly strip Article 14 from the final draft. We need someone Wu Zhenbang cannot touch."

"Do not stress yourself tonight, Miss Qin Yuhan," Chen Xuan said gently, rising as the evening breeze cooled. "Eat a good dinner downstairs and rest well. When a building has a rotten beam in its foundation, it never lasts until morning."

Watching Chen Xuan walk calmly back through the glass doors with the booklet under his arm, Qin Yuhan realized her anxiety had vanished. Whoever drafted Article 14 in Zurich had made the worst mistake of their lives by handing a copy to Chen Xuan.
