# Chapter 37: Miss Qin Next Week

Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. Friday, 7:40 p.m.

The 6:10 bus had been late by twelve minutes, and Chen Xuan climbed six flights of stairs holding the railing, tired and very hungry.

Dinner was already on the round table. There were pork ribs, cabbage with garlic, a plate of cold radish, and a pot of soup. In the middle of it all sat Tang Xiaoyu, holding a folded piece of paper in both hands, looking like someone waiting outside an exam room.

"She won't eat until you see it!" Liu Meifang announced, banging the table with her ladle. "Show him, Xiaoyu!"

It was her first school report from Yunxi No. 2 Middle School.

Chen Xuan read it carefully. Math, 94, first in her class. Chinese, 88. English, 71. Under it, her homeroom teacher had written one line: *Quiet and very careful. Laughs less than she should. Please encourage her.*

"Ninety-four in math!" Chen Hao leaned over, forgetting his earbud. "I never got that in junior high!"

"English needs love," said Chen Yuan, and Xiaoyu hid her face behind her bowl.

"As the old proverb says," Chen Jianguo began, lifting a finger, "a girl who is good at math will never be short of change!"

"Dad, that one is almost true," said Chen Yuan. "I'm worried."

Just then a streak of orange leapt onto the table. Juzi, who had grown fatter in a week, landed beside the pork ribs, grabbed one in her teeth, and ran under the sofa with the whole family shouting!

"No cats at the table!" Liu Meifang yelled. A moment later she slipped a shred of chicken under the sofa. "Eat, you thief. Don't tell anyone."

Chen Xuan was laughing quietly into his soup when Liu Meifang's phone buzzed. Zhou Guilan's face filled the screen, her eyes very close to the camera.

"Xuan! Did you think about it?"

"Think about what, Grandma?"

"My granddaughter-in-law!"

Everyone stopped talking at once. Liu Meifang's ladle stopped in midair.

"Mother!" she cried. "You asked him without me?! And what do you mean, think? He doesn't even think about the weather!"

"He has the dating life of a thermos," said Chen Yuan.

"A man without a wife is a boat without a, a..." Chen Jianguo hesitated. "Anchor. Yes. An anchor."

"Soup, anyone?" said Chen Xuan, lifting the pot.

Later, when the plates were cleared and Zhou Guilan had hung up, Xiaoyu appeared at his elbow with her hands behind her back.

"I made something," she said. "It's not good."

It was a drawing on notebook paper, in colored pencil. A round table, and around it the whole family: Liu Meifang with her ladle, Chen Jianguo with one finger raised, Chen Yuan with crossed arms, Chen Hao with one earbud, a small orange dot sitting right on the table with a rib in its mouth. Beside Liu Meifang sat a small girl with short braids.

And in the far corner, small and a little apart, sat a man with a thermos on his knee.

Chen Xuan did not say anything for a long moment.

"You put me in the corner," he said at last.

"You always sit there." Xiaoyu looked at her feet. "From there you can see everyone's face. So nobody is left out."

He looked at the paper for a very long time. Three years of Friday buses and canvas bags and quiet rented rooms, and nobody had ever told him what his seat looked like from the outside.

"Thank you," he said. His voice was a little rough. "May I keep it?"

She nodded hard. He pinned it on the fridge, right next to the house rules, with the magnet shaped like a fish.

Qingyan City, a quiet courtyard in the east. Friday, 10:40 p.m.

Chief An sat under the lamp with a thin folder, and Mo Lin stood in front of him, as straight as the wall.

"Chief, the visitor from Haiyue Hospitality on Monday is Miss Qin Yuhan," said Mo Lin. "Twenty-eight. She runs the hospitality division of Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group, and her father, Qin Zhenhai, is its chairman. Her family is wealthy by the usual measure, a few billion yuan. She is clean in every record we checked. She has never once been late."

"And the Boss?"

"He knows only 'Miss Qin.' Manager Hu told him on Tuesday. Nobody has told him her name, her family, or who she is. He has not asked."

Chief An closed the folder. For forty years he had planned the movements of men who held half a province in their hands, and now he was planning a lunch.

"Number Nine, we will not tell him," he said at last. "That is not our place. The stay-ordinary order covers a lunch as much as a ballot box. He won it fairly, so let it be ordinary."

"Yes, Chief."

"And Mo Lin. Put one extra person near the restaurant door. Quietly. And see that the tea is hot."

Room 601. Friday, 11:55 p.m.

Chen Xuan stood in the dark kitchen with a glass of water, looking at the drawing on the fridge.

On Monday at noon, a free lunch would be waiting in a private room somewhere with a visitor he had never met. He had promised Hu Dewei he'd go. He would wear his clean shirt and bring his thermos.

It was only lunch, after all.
