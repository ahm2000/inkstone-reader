# Chapter 37: The General's Study

Inside the Central Military Commission compound, four-star General Qin Zhengguo still had his desk lamp on at a quarter past one on Monday morning. Three northern army readiness folders lay open across his desk, and two crushed cigarette stubs rested in the glass ashtray beside his elbow.

The heavy oak doors of his study flew open without a knock.

His aide, Colonel Ye Chenglin, burst into the room half-dressed. His uniform jacket hung unbuttoned over a white undershirt, his hair stuck up in clumps, and his right hand gripped his gun inside its holster. Sweat beaded on his forehead, and his eyes jumped wildly from the hallway back to the desk.

"General Qin Zhengguo!" Ye Chenglin barked, out of breath. "We may have a coup inside the compound! Five armed men are standing right outside your study doors!"

Qin Zhengguo did not look up right away. He finished underlining a column of fuel numbers with his fountain pen, screwed the cap back on with a crisp click, and set the pen neatly beside the folder. Only then did he lift his eyes to his shaking aide.

"Close the door behind you, Ye Chenglin," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice low and flat. "You are letting the cold air in."

Ye Chenglin did not take his hand off his holster. His knuckles were pale, and his chest rose and fell fast. "General, five men in plain black gear, full masks, no badges, and no patches. They are standing dead still in the hall ten paces from this desk. Sergeant Major Chen Kai and the entire inner guard squad are standing against the wall with their rifles slung down. They did not stop them. They did not pull the alarm. General, give me the order to sound the siren and call in the quick-response troops right now!"

Qin Zhengguo reached calmly for his pack of cigarettes. He tapped one out, put it between his lips, and struck a match. The bright flare briefly lit his weathered, stone-hard face. He took a long, slow drag, shook the match out, and blew a thick cloud of gray smoke across the desk.

His eyes locked straight onto Ye Chenglin's white-knuckled hand on the holster.

"Take your hand off your gun," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice cutting through the room like cold steel. "Those are Black Guard. If this were a coup, neither of us would still be breathing."

Ye Chenglin froze as if someone had dumped ice water down his back. His fingers slowly uncurled from the pistol grip, hovered for one stunned second, and then dropped stiffly to his side.

"Black Guard?" Ye Chenglin stammered, swallowing hard with a deep frown. "General, I have served on the Central Military Commission staff for twelve years. I hold top-secret clearance across four army regions. I have never seen a unit called Black Guard on any staff list or defense budget. Are they a team from White Guard?"

Qin Zhengguo leaned back in his chair as smoke curled around his collar. "Sit down, Ye Chenglin. You look like you are about to faint on my rug."

"General, I prefer to stand," Ye Chenglin said stiffly, though his knees looked anything but steady. "If they are White Guard, why would they seal off a four-star general's hall without telling the duty chairman?"

"Because they are not White Guard," Qin Zhengguo replied calmly, tapping ash into the glass tray. "White Guard answers to the founding charter. They have numbered agents, command lines, and files that a few of us at the top know exist. White Guard is the knife the republic keeps in the dark to cut out rot before it reaches the bone. Black Guard is not a knife, Ye Chenglin. Black Guard is the executioner's axe."

Ye Chenglin's eyes went wide, and he swallowed dryly. "Not even White Guard commands them?"

"When Black Guard steps onto the street, White Guard puts down its guns, steps back, and sits under full watch," Qin Zhengguo said heavily. "Even Number 1 has no authority over the five men outside my door. They have no names in any army file, no unit numbers in any budget, and no loyalty to any faction, ministry, or general in this building."

Cold dread washed across Ye Chenglin's face. His back stiffened, and his voice dropped to a tight whisper. "General Qin Zhengguo, why are five of them outside your study? Did someone file a fake report against you? Are they here to arrest you?"

Qin Zhengguo let out a dry, grim laugh, smoke drifting from his nose. "Use your head, Ye Chenglin. You are a colonel, not a new recruit. If Black Guard had a warrant with my name on it, would they stand silent in the hallway letting you burst in here with your hand on a gun?"

Ye Chenglin blinked as his training kicked back in. "No, sir. They would have come in before I even woke up. They let me walk straight past them into your study."

"Right," Qin Zhengguo said, nodding once. "They did not stop you from coming in, which means they are not here to take me away. Now ask yourself what happens if you try to walk back out of this compound and drive down the road."

Ye Chenglin's jaw locked tight. "They would stop me."

"Without even raising their voices," Qin Zhengguo said. He took another deep drag, his eyes narrowing behind the smoke. "Five masked guards outside my door means a Black Order has been triggered. Right now, I would bet my stars that every Vice Premier, every ministry head, every senior elder, and every military vice-chairman in the capital is staring at those same masks outside his front steps."

Silence settled over the study, broken only by the faint crackle of burning tobacco. Ye Chenglin stared at the closed oak doors, picturing the five silent figures waiting just beyond the wood.

"A Black Order," Ye Chenglin repeated softly, a shake returning to his voice. "General, I read one blacked-out note about that rule at officer school ten years ago. My teacher said it had not been used in decades. What on earth could have happened tonight to wake Black Guard after all these years?"

Qin Zhengguo's face turned hard as iron. He crushed his half-smoked cigarette into the ashtray with slow, heavy force.

"Something big enough to rot the foundation of the republic," Qin Zhengguo said quietly. He turned toward the locked steel drawer built into the right side of his desk. "Shut that door tight, Ye Chenglin, and keep your mouth closed. Tell Sergeant Major Chen Kai over the desk speaker not to touch a gun and not to challenge a single guard. Then come right back here."

"Yes, General," Ye Chenglin said, fear still lingering in his eyes. "What are you going to do?"

Qin Zhengguo rested his hand on the brass key in his pocket. "I am going to find out who sold out this country, and whether our borders are still standing before sunrise."
