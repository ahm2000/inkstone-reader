# Chapter 37: The General's Study

Inside the Central Military Commission compound, four-star General Qin Zhengguo had not yet turned off his desk lamp at a quarter past one on Monday morning. Three northern theater readiness folders lay open across his blotter, and two crushed cigarette stubs rested in the glass ashtray beside his elbow.

The oak doors of his study flew open without a knock.

His adjutant, Colonel Zhao Ming, burst across the threshold half-dressed. His uniform jacket hung unbuttoned over a white undershirt, his hair stood disheveled, and his right hand was clamped hard around his holstered service pistol. Sweat beaded on his forehead, and his eyes darted wildly from the corridor back to the desk.

"General Qin Zhengguo!" Zhao Ming barked, breathless. "We may have a coup inside the compound! Five armed men are standing outside your study doors right now!"

Qin Zhengguo did not raise his head immediately. He finished underlining a column of fuel figures with his fountain pen, screwed the cap back on with a crisp click, and set the pen neatly beside the folder. Only then did he lift his eyes to his trembling adjutant.

"Close the door behind you, Zhao Ming," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice low and flat. "You are letting the draft in."

Zhao Ming did not move his hand from his holster. His knuckles were pale, and his chest heaved. "General, five men in completely featureless matte-black gear, full masks, zero insignia, zero badges. They are standing motionless in the corridor ten paces from this desk. Sergeant Major Chen Kai and the entire inner guard squad are standing against the wall with their rifles slung down. They did not challenge them. They did not reach for the alarm. General, give me the order to sound the siren and summon the rapid-response battalion right now!"

Qin Zhengguo reached calmly for his pack of cigarettes. He tapped one out, placed it between his lips, and struck a match. The flare briefly lit his weathered, granite-hard face. He took a long, unhurried draw, shook the match out, and blew a thick plume of blue-gray smoke across the desk.

His eyes locked onto Zhao Ming's white-knuckled hand on the holster.

"Take your hand off your gun," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice slicing through the study like cold steel. "Those are Zero Numbers. If this were a coup, neither of us would still be breathing."

Zhao Ming froze as though ice water had been poured down his spine. His fingers slowly uncurled from the pistol grip, hovering for a stunned second before dropping stiffly to his side.

"Zero Numbers?" Zhao Ming stammered, swallowing hard as his brow furrowed. "General, I have served in the Central Military Commission staff for twelve years. I hold top-secret clearance across four theaters. I have never seen a unit called Zero Numbers in any personnel ledger or defense budget. Are they a detachment of Black Pine Guard?"

Qin Zhengguo leaned back in his chair, smoke curling around his collar. "Sit down, Zhao Ming. You look like you are about to faint on my carpet."

"General, I prefer to stand," Zhao Ming said stiffly, though his knees looked anything but steady. "If they are Black Pine Guard, why would they seal off a four-star general's corridor without notifying the duty chairman?"

"Because they are not Black Pine Guard," Qin Zhengguo replied calmly, tapping ash into the glass tray. "Black Pine Guard answers to the founding charter. They have numbered operatives, command relays, and files that a handful of us at the top know exist. Black Pine is the scalpel the republic keeps in the dark to cut out rot before it reaches the bone. Zero Guard is not a scalpel, Zhao Ming. Zero Guard is the executioner's axe."

Zhao Ming's eyes widened, and his throat worked silently. "Even Black Pine Guard does not command them?"

"When Zero Numbers step onto the pavement, Black Pine Guard puts down its weapons, steps back, and submits to full monitoring," Qin Zhengguo said heavily. "Even Number 1 has no authority over the five men outside my door. They carry no recorded names, no recorded numbers in any military archive, and no loyalty to any faction, ministry, or general in this building."

Cold dread washed over Zhao Ming's face. His posture stiffened, and his voice dropped to a strained whisper. "General Qin Zhengguo, why are five of them outside your study? Did someone file a false report against you? Are they here to detain you?"

Qin Zhengguo let out a dry, grim chuckle, smoke drifting from his nostrils. "Use your head, Zhao Ming. You are a colonel, not a recruit. If Zero Guard had a warrant with my name on it, would they stand mute in the hallway letting you burst in here with your hand on a pistol?"

Zhao Ming blinked, his tactical instincts snapping back. "No, sir. They would have entered before I woke up. They let me walk straight past them into your study."

"Exactly," Qin Zhengguo said, nodding once. "They did not stop you from entering, which means they are not here to take me away. Now ask yourself what happens if you try to walk back out of this compound and drive down the avenue."

Zhao Ming's jaw tightened sharply. "They would stop me."

"Without raising their voices," Qin Zhengguo confirmed. He took another deep drag, his eyes narrowing behind the smoke. "Five matte-black sentries outside my door means an Absolute Order has been triggered. Right now, I would wager my stars that every Vice Premier, every ministry head, every senior elder, and every military vice-chairman in the capital is staring at the exact same masks outside his front steps."

Silence fell over the study, broken only by the faint crackle of burning tobacco. Zhao Ming stared at the closed oak doors, picturing the five silent figures waiting just beyond the wood.

"An Absolute Order," Zhao Ming repeated softly, a tremor returning to his voice. "General, I read a single redacted footnote about that protocol at staff college ten years ago. My instructor said it had not been activated in decades. What on earth could have happened tonight to wake Zero Guard after all these years?"

Qin Zhengguo's expression turned hard as iron. He crushed his half-smoked cigarette into the ashtray with deliberate force.

"Something big enough to rot the foundation of the republic," Qin Zhengguo said quietly. He turned toward the locked steel drawer built into the right side of his desk. "Close that door tight, Zhao Ming, and keep your mouth shut. Tell Sergeant Major Chen Kai over the intercom not to touch a weapon and not to challenge a single sentry. Then come back here."

"Yes, General," Zhao Ming said, fear still lingering in his eyes. "What are you going to do?"

Qin Zhengguo's hand rested on the brass key in his pocket. "I am going to find out who sold out this country, and whether our borders are still standing before sunrise."
