# Chapter 37: Miss Qin Next Week

The Friday six-ten bus had been late by twelve minutes, and Chen Xuan climbed the stairs holding the railing, tired and very hungry.

Dinner was already on the round table, with pork ribs, garlic cabbage, a plate of cold radish, and a pot of soup, and in the middle of it all sat Tang Xiaoyu, holding a folded paper in both hands like someone waiting outside an exam room.

"She won't eat until you see it!" Liu Meifang announced, banging the table with her ladle. "Show him, Xiaoyu!"

It was her first school report from Yunxi No. 2 Middle School, and since Xiaoyu had lived with the family for only a few weeks, it was also her first chance to show them anything of her own.

Chen Xuan read it carefully: math, ninety-four, first in her class; Chinese, eighty-eight; English, seventy-one. Under it, her homeroom teacher had written one line. *Quiet and very careful, laughs less than she should, please encourage her.*

"Ninety-four in math, and I never got that in junior high!" Chen Hao leaned over, forgetting his earbud.

"English needs love," said Chen Yuan, and Xiaoyu hid her face behind her bowl.

"As the old proverb says," Chen Jianguo began, lifting one finger, "a girl who is good at math will never be short of change!"

"Dad, that one is almost true," said Chen Yuan. "Now I'm worried."

Thump!

Just then, a streak of orange leapt onto the table!

It was Juzi, fatter again, and she landed beside the pork ribs, grabbed one in her teeth, and ran under the sofa while the whole family shouted.

"No cats at the table!" Liu Meifang yelled, and a moment later she slipped a shred of chicken under the sofa. "Eat, you thief, and don't tell anyone."

Chen Xuan was laughing quietly into his soup when Liu Meifang's phone buzzed.

Zhou Guilan's face filled the screen, her eyes very close to the camera.

"Xuan, did you think about it?"

"Think about what, Grandma?"

"My granddaughter-in-law!"

Silence.

Everyone stopped talking at once, and Liu Meifang's ladle froze in midair, because in this house a question about marriage was never a small one.

"Mother, you asked him without me?!" she cried. "And what do you mean, think, when he doesn't even think about the weather?!"

"He has the dating life of a thermos," said Chen Yuan.

"The Li family's boy got married at twenty-six," Zhou Guilan's voice crackled through the speaker, undeterred. "Twenty-six, and he works nights at a gas station!"

"A man without a wife is a boat without a, a..." Chen Jianguo hesitated, and then he found it. "Anchor! Yes, an anchor!"

Chen Xuan could face a hard boss with a calm voice, but he had no defense against four relatives and one phone screen. So he used the only one he had.

"Soup, anyone?" he said, lifting the pot.

Later.

When the plates were cleared and Zhou Guilan had hung up, Xiaoyu appeared at his elbow with her hands behind her back.

"I made something, but it's not good," she said.

It was a drawing on notebook paper, in colored pencil. A round table, and around it the whole family: Liu Meifang with her ladle, Chen Jianguo with one finger raised, Chen Yuan with crossed arms, Chen Hao with one earbud, and a small orange dot right on the table with a rib in its mouth. Beside Liu Meifang sat a small girl with short braids.

And in the far corner, small and a little apart, sat a man with a thermos on his knee.

Chen Xuan did not say anything for a long moment, and nobody else in the room did either.

"You put me in the corner," he said at last.

"You always sit there," Xiaoyu said, looking at her feet. "From there you can see everyone's face, so nobody is left out."

He looked at the paper for a very long time. Three years of Friday buses and canvas bags and quiet rented rooms, and nobody had ever told him what his seat looked like from the outside.

"Thank you," he said, a little roughly. "May I keep it?"

She nodded hard, and he pinned it on the fridge, right next to the house rules, with the magnet shaped like a fish.

---

That night in the quiet eastern courtyard, Chief An sat under the lamp with a thin folder, and Mo Lin stood in front of him, as straight as the wall.

"Chief, the visitor from Haiyue Hospitality on Monday is Miss Qin Yuhan," said Mo Lin. "She is twenty-eight and runs the hospitality division of Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group, and her father, Qin Zhenhai, is its chairman. Her family is wealthy by the usual measure, a few billion yuan. She is clean in every record we checked, and she has never once been late."

"And does the Boss know who she is?"

"He knows only 'Miss Qin,' because Manager Hu told him on Tuesday. Nobody has told him her name or her family, and he has not asked."

"Will he look her up himself?" Chief An asked.

"Has he ever looked anyone up, Chief?"

Chief An closed the folder. For forty years he had planned the movements of men who held half a province in their hands, and now he was planning a lunch.

"Number Nine, we will not tell him," he said at last. "That is not our place. The stay-ordinary order covers a lunch as much as a ballot box. He won it fairly, so let it be ordinary."

"Yes, Chief."

"And Mo Lin. Put one extra person near the restaurant door, quietly, and see that the tea is hot."

Mo Lin did not ask why. After all, a Detail does not ask why a Boss's lunch needs a guard.

---

Late that night in Room 601, Chen Xuan stood in the dark kitchen with a glass of water, looking at the drawing on the fridge.

On Monday at noon, a free lunch would be waiting in a private room somewhere, with a visitor he had never met. Why would a company that big want to eat with a logistics supervisor? He had promised Hu Dewei he would go, so he would wear his clean shirt and bring his thermos.

It was only lunch, but at noon on Monday, a woman he had never met would be waiting at the door.
