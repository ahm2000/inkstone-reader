# Chapter 37: The Cloud Sea Hall

The Cloud Sea Hall had a painted-cloud ceiling, two round tables as wide as pond lids, and a glass wall that showed the whole river city at night.

Twenty clerks from Hengxin Logistics stood just inside the door. Nobody wanted to be the first to sit.

Dai Wenbin did not sit at all. The hotel's General Manager poured the first cup of tea himself, standing at Chen Xuan's elbow.

"Manager Dai, please sit and eat with us," Chen Xuan said.

"Thank you, sir. I will stand."

"Then at least take a cup."

"I cannot, sir." Dai Wenbin's hand shook, and the tea rattled in the pot. "Please. Eat."

Chen Xuan scratched his ear. "Okay. It's your hotel."

"It is not," Dai Wenbin said, much too quickly. Then he went red and bowed.

The first dish came out on the master chef's own hands. It was tomato and egg.

"Home-style, as you asked," the chef said. "My mother's way. The tomatoes come from a farm outside the city, picked this morning."

Chen Xuan took one bite and closed his eyes.

"Oh, nice."

The chef stood very straight, like a boy who had just been given a gold star. Behind him, six waiters carried in braised pork, steamed fish, cabbage in clear broth and a pot of rice that smelled like a harvest.

Lin Dong, the youngest clerk, had found a small gold card standing beside the teapot. He read it, and his mouth fell open.

"Tomato and egg is 380 yuan. The fish is 1,880. This whole table is 21,000, and there are two tables." He tapped his phone. "That is 42,000 yuan. It is three and a half months of Supervisor Chen's pay. It is more than six months of mine."

"Lin Dong, eat quietly," Pan Xiulan said. She was already sitting beside Chen Xuan, and she had already filled his bowl twice.

"On Monday you called him a sleepwalker," Lin Dong whispered.

"Sleepwalkers see far." Pan Xiulan lifted her chin. "I always said a man who naps has a plan."

Hu Dewei stood up with a small cup of rice wine. His hand shook so much that wine ran down his fingers.

"Colleagues. This quarter, our department did well. This is because of the company. And because of Supervisor." He looked at Chen Xuan, and his throat made a small noise. "And because of dinner. To dinner."

"To dinner," twenty people said.

Hu Dewei drank, sat down and wiped his forehead with a napkin. Pan Xiulan leaned over to him. "Manager, you meant to thank Supervisor Chen."

"That too," Hu Dewei whispered.

Down the table, a clerk raised her cup toward Chen Xuan. "Supervisor Chen, eating up here must feel like living in a palace."

"It's only a bun with a view," Chen Xuan said. He was on his third bowl of rice.

The whole table laughed. Chen Xuan did not know why, but he smiled back, because laughing people were nice to sit with.

When the fish was almost done, he waved to a waiter. "Could I have a box? There's a lot of pork left. My mom says wasting food is a sin."

"Chen Xuan." Pan Xiulan nearly dropped her chopsticks. "You cannot pack leftovers in the Cloud Sea Hall."

"My mom's not here. That's why I'm packing it. If she asked, I'd have to lie."

Before the waiter could move, Dai Wenbin crossed the room. He came back with three boxes, a paper bag with handles and a hot towel, and packed the pork himself, with two big hands that were not used to it.

"Thanks, Manager Dai. One box was fine."

"Sir, it is my honor." Dai Wenbin's voice cracked on the last word.

At the far end of the second table, by the door, Zhang Pengfei sat alone with a full bowl he had not touched. He had left the sunglasses in the car. He had also left his red tie loose, as if it were choking him.

All evening, he had been hearing the same sentence in his head. *Silver is our entry-level membership.* The card was still in his pocket. He had to bring it back to Qi Zihao in the morning, along with the car, and tell him what had happened in the lobby.

Chen Xuan looked down the table. He picked up the fish plate, and a waiter passed it along, hand to hand.

"Zhang Pengfei. Try the fish. It's good."

Zhang Pengfei took it with both hands. His ears burned. "Thanks, Supervisor Chen."

That was when his phone rang. The name on the screen made his stomach drop: Deng Weidong, his wife's uncle at the Port Authority.

"Uncle?"

The voice was so loud that the whole table heard it, even over the clink of cups.

"Auditors! Eight of them, in my office, at nine at night. They have a double storage charge on a container, with my stamp on it and your initials. An invoice, Zhang Pengfei. One little invoice. Did you sign it? Who found it? Tell me who found it."

The table went still. Twenty pairs of chopsticks stopped in the air.

Zhang Pengfei slowly turned his head. He looked straight at Chen Xuan.

Chen Xuan was lifting a piece of fish to his mouth. He chewed, swallowed and thought about it, like a man asked what he had for lunch.

"I only checked the paperwork," he said.

Every word was true. That was what made it so cold.

Zhang Pengfei lowered the phone. His mouth opened, and no word came out. Chen Xuan nodded at the plate in front of him.

"Eat, Zhang Pengfei. It's getting cold."

* * *

At eleven that night, Qi Jiancheng stood in his dark study with the phone at his ear. The unknown number on the screen had been ringing for the second time.

"Do not hang up," said a man's voice. It was calm and dry, like a bank clerk reading a form. "Tonight your friend in the Organization Department opened a file he should never have touched. He has been reported. You will not call him again."

"Who are you?"

"Your son shouted at a hotel clerk named Xia Mengyao. He threatened her job and her family's hometown. By nine tomorrow morning, he will stand at the front desk of the Yunhai Grand Hotel and apologize to her in person, with flowers. After that you will not ask about anyone again."

"And if he does not?"

"The review team has your company's three berth permits and its four hundred room-night contract on its desk. They have not been opened. Monday morning, they can be."

The line clicked.

Qi Jiancheng's knees felt weak. He had paid for dinners with governors. Nobody in thirty years had told him what time his son would apologize to a clerk.

He walked down the hall, pushed open his son's door and turned on the light.

"Get up."

"Dad?" Qi Zihao rolled over. "It's eleven o'clock."

"You are buying flowers. Big ones. What time do the flower shops open?"

"Seven. Why?"

"Because you are going to be standing at that hotel counter at eight forty-five." Qi Jiancheng's voice shook, and it was not anger. "And if you say one word that is not sorry, I will take the car, the card and the phone."

* * *

Near midnight, the lobby of the Yunhai Grand Hotel was empty and quiet. Only the fountain outside kept talking.

Xia Mengyao sat on a bench by the pillar. Her shift had ended at ten. She had not gone home. Her bag lay in her lap, and her hands were tight on the strap.

Dai Wenbin came out of the elevator, saw her and stopped.

"Xia Mengyao. Why are you still here?"

"General Manager." She stood up at once. "Qi Zihao said he would find my ID and my hometown. If somebody goes to my mother's door, I do not know what I will do."

Dai Wenbin held up his right hand. Across the back of it, in black ink, was her name. It had smeared, but every letter was still there.

"I wrote it myself, in front of him." His voice was steady, though his collar was soaked. "It will not wash off for a week, and I do not plan to wash it. You keep your job, your pay and your shifts. Nobody is going to your mother's door."

She nodded. A tear ran down her cheek, and she wiped it fast.

"General Manager. Who is he?"

Dai Wenbin looked at the elevator, where the number thirty-eight was just going dark.

"I do not know who he is," he said. "I know that tonight I have never been so afraid and so proud."
