# Chapter 62: The Sealed Tribunal

The tribunal hall beneath the national capital had no windows, no press gallery, and no cameras.

Cold white light hummed down from recessed ceiling panels onto dark granite tiles polished to a dull mirror. Twelve armed sentries in plain gray uniforms stood along the walls, their boots planted shoulder width apart, their eyes fixed straight ahead.

Behind a wall of reinforced glass stood four men who, ten days earlier, had approved multi-billion-yuan projects across finance, maritime trade, and provincial infrastructure. Today their tailored suits were gone, replaced by coarse navy detainee jackets without collars or pockets.

At the high mahogany bench, the chief military tribunal judge adjusted a stack of red-bound dossiers.

"Case file zero-nine-seven, classified at the highest national level," the judge said, his voice flat and resonant in the sealed room. "For twenty-one years, the defendants acted as domestic relays for an overseas financial intelligence syndicate, funneling sovereign shipping data, credit models, and port rights across shell clearinghouses. Compromised exposure exceeds one hundred trillion yuan."

Inside the glass box, the former Deputy Minister of Commerce pressed both palms against the thick pane. His face had gone the color of wet chalk.

"I demand consular observation!" he shouted, his voice thinning to a tinny rattle through the overhead speaker. "Our legal counsel in Geneva has already filed an international arbitration notice!"

Nobody at the bench looked up. Two stenographers kept typing in steady, mechanical rhythm.

"Arbitration applies to commercial disputes," the judge replied without raising his voice. "Espionage against national economic security falls under the Sealed Military Statute. For the principal coordinator, death sentence with immediate execution upon supreme review. For the remaining three defendants, life imprisonment without commutation, and full confiscation of domestic and offshore assets."

The wooden gavel struck once. The sharp crack echoed off the concrete walls and died cleanly.

The former Deputy Minister's knees buckled. Two bailiffs stepped into the glass box, took him by the elbows, and guided him through the heavy steel door at the back. The other three did not shout. They simply stared at the granite floor, realizing too late that the invisible net they had trusted for two decades had vanished overnight.

* * *

Above ground, a crisp northern wind swept dry ginkgo leaves across a quiet stone courtyard behind the central compound.

General Qin Zhengguo stood beside a low carved parapet in a plain dark wool coat with no insignia on the shoulders. Beside him stood the Senior Vice Premier, dark shadows pressed beneath his eyes after forty-eight hours of emergency cabinet sessions.

General Qin Zhengguo pulled an unmarked tin box from his coat pocket and tapped two plain white-paper cigarettes halfway out. He offered one to the Senior Vice Premier.

"Doctors told you to quit three years ago," the Senior Vice Premier said, though he took the cigarette anyway.

"Doctors do not sit through forty-eight hours of treason briefings," General Qin Zhengguo replied.

He struck a tarnished brass lighter, cupping the flame against the wind until both tips glowed orange. Thin ribbons of gray smoke curled upward and dissolved against the pale autumn sky.

"Thirty-two ministerial and provincial moles," the Senior Vice Premier said softly. "Banking regulators, customs directors, port commissioners, even two deputy secretaries in coastal provinces. We hunted leaks for six years and caught only clerks. Then, in a single night, a complete encrypted ledger lands on our desk with every offshore trust and every bloodstained signature."

He turned slightly to study General Qin Zhengguo's weathered profile.

"General Qin Zhengguo, we have known each other for thirty years. Tell me plainly. Who handed us that knife?"

General Qin Zhengguo took a slow draw and exhaled into the cold wind.

"You saw the wax seal," General Qin Zhengguo said calmly. "No agency stamp. No personal signature. Only a single pine branch pressed into black wax."

The Senior Vice Premier exhaled heavily. "Only three people in the capital still remember that archive number, and none of them have the authority to summon it. What puzzles me is the geography."

"Geography?"

"Every domino fell from one starting point," the Senior Vice Premier murmured. "A small city in Lanshui Province called Qingyan. A demolition dispute on Yunxi Old Street. A private club run by Huang Boren. A shipping boss named Zhou Guodong. A branch desk of Huaxin Bank. One thread pulled in Qingyan City, and a twenty-year global syndicate collapsed from the top down."

General Qin Zhengguo tapped ash into a stone tray. A faint smile touched the corner of his mouth.

"My younger brother Qin Zhengming retired to Qingyan City," General Qin Zhengguo said lightly. "He says the tea there is good, and the weather is mild."

The Senior Vice Premier gave him a sharp glance, then shook his head with a dry chuckle. "Keep your secrets, then. As long as that hand stands on our side of the border, I will not ask who owns it."

* * *

Brisk footsteps crunched across the gravel walkway.

The Minister of State Security approached alone, carrying a thin black leather folder sealed with a crimson band. He stopped two paces away and lowered his voice.

"We just decrypted a priority burst from three offshore relay nodes," the Minister of State Security said. "The overseas syndicate behind the one-hundred-trillion-yuan network has broken radio silence."

General Qin Zhengguo's eyes narrowed slightly. "The Northern Ledger."

"Yes," the Minister of State Security confirmed, opening the folder. "For twenty years they hid behind sovereign wealth wrappers and European family offices. Losing thirty-two domestic assets and their entire Lanshui clearing channel in seventy-two hours has thrown their board into open panic."

"Let them panic," the Senior Vice Premier said coldly.

"They do not believe this was a standard state audit," the Minister of State Security replied grimly. "Their analysts noticed the speed of the purge. They are convinced an unknown domestic power in Lanshui Province triggered the blackout in Yunxi and Qingyan."

He pulled a glossy photograph from the folder showing a sharp-featured man in rimless glasses and a tailored charcoal overcoat walking through an airport corridor.

"Li Weiming," the Minister of State Security said. "Dual passport holder. Overseas name, Marcus Vance. Formally a senior partner for a Zurich private equity fund. In reality, he is the Northern Ledger's chief Asia-Pacific fixer. He landed in Lanshui Province four hours ago under a clean investment visa with a private security detail."

"He is going to Qingyan City," General Qin Zhengguo said flatly.

"His itinerary leads straight south to Qingyan City," the Minister of State Security confirmed. "He intends to inspect the wreckage of Huaxin Bank's Lanshui desk, Huang Boren's Banyan Club, and Zhou Guodong's Shenghua Maritime in person, locate whoever lit the match, and cut the threat quietly."

The Senior Vice Premier frowned. "Should we revoke his visa or place provincial surveillance directly on his tail?"

"No," General Qin Zhengguo said at once, crushing his cigarette out in the stone tray.

Both men looked at him.

"Track his communications from high altitude," General Qin Zhengguo said, his tone hard as winter iron. "Keep provincial security officers outside a ten-kilometer radius of whatever he touches in Qingyan City."

The Minister of State Security hesitated. "General, if Li Weiming brings trained foreign operatives into a civilian city, we cannot risk collateral damage."

General Qin Zhengguo turned back toward the autumn wind.

"There will be no collateral damage," General Qin Zhengguo said softly. "You are worried about a wolf walking into Qingyan City. I am worried about your provincial officers accidentally stepping in front of the door when the wolf gets thrown out."
