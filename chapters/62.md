# Chapter 62: CPPCC: The Quiet Reckoning

Five minutes before eight thirty on Monday morning, the meeting hall of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference smelled of stale smoke and cold sweat. Ten committee chairs sat around the round table. Not a single face looked calm.

Vice Chairman Zhao Yunsheng stood at the head of the table, looking thin and drained after leaving the war room. His hand no longer shook the way it had beside Premier Gu Chengshan. A cold, merciless hardness sat in his eyes.

"Turn off every phone and drop it in the tray by the door," Zhao Yunsheng ordered, blowing out a sharp stream of gray smoke. "Nobody calls an assistant. Nobody calls a secretary. Nobody calls Zurich."

At the word Zurich, Overseas Chinese and Foreign Affairs Chair Kong Weiguo froze with his teacup halfway to his lips. Across from him, Commerce and Industry Federation Chair Hu Zhenbang set his gold fountain pen down with a sharp click.

"Zhao Yunsheng," Retired Provincial Chair and Senior Adviser Pei Yongnian said, forcing a polite smile that never reached his eyes. "Rumors have been flying since six o'clock. Three hundred officials arrested before dawn? Surely the Standing Committee deserves a calm briefing instead of secrecy."

"You want a briefing, Pei Yongnian?" Zhao Yunsheng pushed ten gray binders across the wood. "Open page one. Read the donor records of Shengyuan Capital."

Ten binders flipped open. Within seconds, every calm mask shattered.

Culture and Philanthropy Advisory Chair Rong Shichang went pale as chalk, clutching page two until the paper wrinkled. "This cannot be real. The Shengyuan Heritage Fund? Fang Qiming donated 300 million yuan to our southern museum fund four years ago! That went through official charity channels!"

"Turn to page six, Rong Shichang," Social and Legal Affairs Committee Chair Du Qingshan said coldly, smoke curling between his fingers. "Look at where the money actually went underneath your grant. 40 million repaired roofs. 260 million bounced through a fake art company in Luxembourg straight into Zurich bank accounts owned by the sons of Vice Governor Lin Zhengde and Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang."

Rong Shichang sank back into his chair as if someone had hit him. "I knew nothing about Luxembourg! Fang Qiming shook hands with provincial leaders and signed a big display check! You cannot blame an advisory chair for overseas money laundering!"

"Nobody says you sent the Zurich wire, Rong Shichang," Proposals and Advisory Review Chair Wen Ruolan cut in sharply, scanning the pages. "Discipline Inspection wants to know why your office gave Fang Qiming's deputy a permanent VIP travel pass on three European trips with no customs checks."

Rong Shichang's mouth opened, then closed. He wiped a shaking hand across his forehead.

Beside him, Hu Zhenbang loosened his silk tie, his jaw tight over page nine. "Why is the Private Business Roundtable on this list? Yes, Shengyuan Capital sponsored our coastal summit, and Fang Qiming sat on my planning committee. Half the billionaires in this country sit on that committee! Treat every donor like a traitor, and private money will panic before the stock market opens at nine thirty!"

"Do not hide behind private business, Hu Zhenbang!" Economic Affairs Committee Chair Cai Boxiang snapped, jabbing his cigarette so hard that hot ash fell onto the table cloth. "Read line fourteen of your own guest list! Twelve provincial investment chiefs met Fang Qiming's Swiss bankers in the back suites of your coastal hotel! While you toasted business owners in the ballroom, they packaged fake demolition loans from counties like Yunxi at fourteen times their real value!"

"I am an unpaid adviser, not a policeman!" Hu Zhenbang shouted, veins bulging above his collar as his face turned bright red. "How was I supposed to know what businessmen talked about in private rooms? I barely spoke ten sentences to Fang Qiming all year!"

"Ten sentences?" United Front Liaison Chair Jiang Chengzhi spoke softly, sliding one photograph across the table. "Here you are on Fang Qiming's yacht off Hong Kong last October, drinking Moutai with Customs Director Xu Wenbin."

Hu Zhenbang's eyes went wide. Color drained from his cheeks so fast he looked ten years older. "That was a business group reception! Forty people stood on that deck!"

"And thirty-two of them are sitting in Discipline Inspection questioning rooms right now," Du Qingshan said flatly.

Silence crushed the hall. Resource and Environment Advisory Chair Xie Guangming took a long, shaky drag of his cigarette. His fingers visibly trembled as he closed his binder.

"I have served twelve years since retiring from the provincial committee," Pei Yongnian said quickly, raising both hands to distance himself. "For the record, I went to two ribbon-cuttings for Fang Qiming's green-mining charity three years ago. I never took a fee, never pushed a deal, and never made a call for him. If Shengyuan Capital dirtied our committees, throw those people out right now."

"How handy that you remember your exact visits so fast, Pei Yongnian," Wen Ruolan murmured without looking up. "Your grandson's shipping firm got a 13 million yuan fee from Shengyuan Capital's coastal branch two weeks after the second ribbon-cutting."

Pei Yongnian's raised hands dropped heavily onto the table. His lips parted with no sound, his chest heaving in short, panicked breaths.

Kong Weiguo crushed his cigarette out hard, sweat beading on his forehead. "Zhao Yunsheng, stop this! Kick out half our wealthy advisers in one morning, and foreign business groups in London, Singapore, and Zurich will scream that Beijing is attacking the private sector! Let the tainted members step down quietly next month for health reasons! Save face for this house!"

Zhao Yunsheng stared at Kong Weiguo through the thick blue smoke until the man shifted in his seat.

"Save face?" Zhao Yunsheng jammed his cigarette dead in the overflowing ashtray. The sharp snap echoed around the silent ring. "Kong Weiguo, at eight o'clock, Premier Gu Chengshan told ten heads of state bodies that over 100 trillion yuan in twenty-year state wealth was drained behind polite committees like ours. Do you know what happens to a house that hands quiet medical retirements to traitors' money men?"

Nobody answered. Three chairs pulled their hands back from the binders as if the covers burned.

"No quiet resignations," Zhao Yunsheng declared, his voice cold and final. "No medical retirements, and no polite goodbyes. Starting right now, I am launching a ruthless clean-up across every advisory, business, and overseas committee."

He tapped the binders with a red pen.

"Du Qingshan and Wen Ruolan will run the emergency review team now. Every national and provincial adviser whose name, family fund, or charity shows up in Fang Qiming's Shengyuan Capital donor records is suspended by ten o'clock and stripped of titles by noon. Passport blocks go to Public Security Minister Guo Zhengming within thirty minutes. Anyone at this table who wants to confess before Gao Zhengyi's investigators knock on your door has until nine o'clock sharp."

In the thick smoke, nobody argued. Wealthy tycoons and retired provincial elders stared at their own shaking hands as the quiet shelter of honorary titles crashed down over their heads.
