# Chapter 62: CPPCC: The Quiet Reckoning

Five minutes before eight thirty on Monday morning, the consultation hall of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference smelled of burnt tobacco and cold sweat. Ten committee chairs sat around the circular table, not a single face calm.

Vice Chairman Zhao Yunsheng stood at the head of the ring, gaunt after leaving the war room. His hand no longer shook as it had beside Premier Gu Chengshan. A brittle, merciless hardness sat in his eyes.

"Turn off every phone and put it in the tray by the door," Zhao Yunsheng ordered, exhaling a sharp stream of gray smoke. "Nobody calls an assistant. Nobody calls a secretary. Nobody calls Zurich."

At the word Zurich, Overseas Chinese and Foreign Affairs Chair Kong Weiguo froze with his teacup halfway to his lips. Across from him, Commerce and Industry Federation Chair Hu Zhenbang set his gold fountain pen down with a sharp click.

"Zhao Yunsheng," Retired Provincial Chair and Senior Adviser Pei Yongnian said, forcing a dignified smile that failed to reach his eyes. "Rumors have been flying since six o'clock. Three hundred officials detained before dawn? Surely the Standing Committee deserves a calm briefing rather than secrecy."

"You want a briefing, Pei Yongnian?" Zhao Yunsheng pushed ten gray binders across the wood. "Open page one. Read the donor ledgers of Shengyuan Capital."

Ten binders flipped open. Within seconds, aristocratic poise shattered.

Culture and Philanthropy Advisory Chair Rong Shichang went pale as chalk, clutching page two until the paper crinkled. "This cannot be real. The Shengyuan Heritage Endowment? Fang Qiming donated three hundred million yuan to our southern museum fund four years ago! That was vetted through charity channels!"

"Turn to page six, Rong Shichang," Social and Legal Affairs Committee Chair Du Qingshan said coldly, smoke curling between his knuckles. "Look at the wire routing underneath your grant. Forty million repaired roofs. Two hundred and sixty million bounced through a Luxembourg art-appraisal shell into Zurich accounts held by the sons of Vice Governor Lin Zhengde and Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang."

Rong Shichang dropped back into his chair as if struck. "I knew nothing of Luxembourg! Fang Qiming shook hands with provincial leaders and signed a ceremonial check! You cannot blame an advisory chair for offshore laundering!"

"Nobody accuses you of writing the Zurich wire, Rong Shichang," Proposals and Advisory Review Chair Wen Ruolan cut in sharply, scanning the ledger. "Discipline Inspection is asking why your office gave Fang Qiming's deputy a permanent diplomatic-courtesy pass on three European delegations without customs inspection."

Rong Shichang's mouth opened, closed, and he wiped a trembling hand across his brow.

Beside him, Hu Zhenbang loosened his silk tie, jaw clenched over page nine. "Why is the Private Enterprise Roundtable here? Yes, Shengyuan Capital sponsored our coastal summit, and Fang Qiming sat on my steering committee. Half the billionaires in this country sit on that committee! Treat every donor as a treason suspect, and private capital panics before markets open at nine thirty!"

"Do not hide behind private capital, Hu Zhenbang!" Economic Affairs Committee Chair Cai Boxiang snapped, jabbing his cigarette so fiercely hot ash fell onto the felt. "Read line fourteen of your own guest list! Twelve provincial urban-investment heads met Fang Qiming's Swiss brokers in the back suites of your coastal hotel! While you toasted entrepreneurs in the ballroom, they packaged forged demolition bonds from counties like Yunxi at fourteen-to-one leverage!"

"I am an unpaid adviser, not a policeman!" Hu Zhenbang shouted, veins bulging along his collar as his face flushed red. "How was I supposed to know what businessmen discussed in private suites? I barely spoke ten sentences to Fang Qiming all year!"

"Ten sentences?" United Front Liaison Chair Jiang Chengzhi spoke softly, sliding one photograph across the table. "Here you are on Fang Qiming's yacht off Hong Kong last October, drinking Moutai with Customs Director Xu Wenbin."

Hu Zhenbang's eyes widened. Color drained from his cheeks so fast he looked ten years older. "That was a chamber-of-commerce reception! Forty people stood on that deck!"

"And thirty-two of them are sitting in Discipline Inspection interrogation rooms right now," Du Qingshan said flatly.

Silence crushed the hall. Resource and Environment Advisory Chair Xie Guangming took a long, shaky drag of his cigarette, fingers visibly trembling as he shut his binder.

"I have served twelve years since retiring from the provincial committee," Pei Yongnian said hastily, raising both palms to distance himself. "For the record, I attended two ribbon-cuttings for Fang Qiming's green-mining foundation three years ago. I never took a fee, never recommended a concession, and never placed a call for him. If Shengyuan Capital corrupted our subcommittees, remove those members immediately."

"How convenient that you recall your exact attendance so quickly, Pei Yongnian," Wen Ruolan murmured without looking up. "Your grandson's logistics consultancy received a twelve-million-yuan retainer from Shengyuan Capital's coastal subsidiary two weeks after the second ribbon-cutting."

Pei Yongnian's raised hands dropped heavily onto the table. His lips parted without sound, chest heaving in shallow, panicked breaths.

Kong Weiguo stubbed his cigarette out violently, forehead beaded with sweat. "Zhao Yunsheng, stop this! Expel half our wealthy advisory delegates in one morning, and foreign chambers in London, Singapore, and Zurich will scream that Beijing is purging the private sector! Let compromised members resign quietly next month for health reasons! Preserve face for the institution!"

Zhao Yunsheng stared at Kong Weiguo through thick blue haze until the man shifted uneasily.

"Preserve face?" Zhao Yunsheng crushed his cigarette dead in the overflowing ashtray, the sharp snap echoing around the silent ring. "Kong Weiguo, at eight o'clock, Premier Gu Chengshan told ten heads of state organs that over one hundred trillion yuan in twenty-year sovereign wealth was drained behind polite committees like ours. Do you know what happens to an institution that hands medical retirements to traitors' bagmen?"

Nobody answered. Three chairs pulled their hands back from the binders as if the covers burned.

"No quiet resignations," Zhao Yunsheng declared, voice cold and final. "No medical retirements, and no polite farewells. Effective immediately, I am launching a merciless internal purge across every advisory, commerce, and overseas liaison committee."

He tapped the binders with a red pen.

"Du Qingshan and Wen Ruolan form the emergency verification panel now. Every national and provincial adviser whose name, family trust, or foundation appears in Fang Qiming's Shengyuan Capital donor ledgers is suspended by ten o'clock and stripped of credentials by noon. Passport holds go to Public Security Minister Guo Zhengming within thirty minutes. Anyone at this table wanting to make a voluntary disclosure before Gao Zhengyi's investigators knock on your door has until nine o'clock sharp."

In the suffocating smoke, nobody protested. Wealthy magnates and retired provincial pillars stared at their own shaking hands as the quiet shelter of honorary titles collapsed over their heads.
