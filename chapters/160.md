# Chapter 160: The Gate of Shenzhen Pier Nine

The coastal wind smelled faintly of salt and diesel outside the massive steel entrance of Shenzhen Pier Nine. Towering red-and-white gantry cranes stretched across the hazy late-autumn sky, and behind the high chain-link fencing stood endless rows of blue and orange shipping containers stacked six high.

Haiyue Group's black MPV rolled to a stop in the concrete plaza outside Gate Three. Two heavy iron barrier arms blocked the lane into the bonded yard, and a thick steel padlock hung conspicuously across the inner sliding gate. Near the brick guardhouse, a dozen port workers in rumpled blue shirts leaned against metal railings, smoking cigarettes and watching the newly arrived car with lazy indifference.

Qin Yuhan stepped out first, her posture straight and dignified in her charcoal blazer. Manager Lin Jian and the two legal assistants followed closely, carrying thick folders of stamped customs documents. Chen Xuan climbed out last, taking his time as he slung his canvas backpack over one shoulder and tucked his dented green metal thermos comfortably under his arm.

Waiting beside the guardhouse was a stocky man in his mid-forties wearing a glossy dark silk shirt with the top two buttons undone. A heavy gold watch glinted on his thick wrist as he flicked cigarette ash onto the pavement. Beside him stood a thin young supervisor clutching a metal clipboard.

"President Qin, that is Manager Fang," Zhou Ming murmured quietly. "He runs Pier Nine's bonded yard for Shen Chao."

Qin Yuhan walked forward calmly and stopped three steps from the barrier. "Manager Fang, I am Qin Yuhan, President of Haiyue Group. Here are the stamped customs release papers, the paid berth receipt, and the municipal port authority clearance for the Haiyue Star. Every procedure is complete. Please open the gate so our feeder trucks can begin loading."

One of the legal assistants stepped up and held out the open binder.

Manager Fang did not even glance at the papers. He blew a slow ring of smoke into the afternoon air and gave a lazy, mocking chuckle. "President Qin from Shanghai, right? Welcome to Shenzhen. I am sure those stamps look very pretty up north, but Pier Nine is managed by the Shen Family. Our yard rules are our yard rules."

He tapped the iron barrier with his knuckles. "Right now, your containers are flagged for special berth congestion and compliance review. Pay the 12 million yuan coordination fee into our designated logistics account, and I will unlock this gate in five minutes. Otherwise, your cargo can sit right there and enjoy the sea breeze."

Zhou Ming's face flushed with anger. "Manager Fang, we already paid every official port fee in full! 12 million yuan is pure extortion. There is no congestion notice on the municipal port system!"

"Watch your tone, little director," Manager Fang sneered, his eyes narrowing coldly. "This is Pier Nine, not a Shanghai office building. Here, what the Shen Family says goes. You want to file a lawsuit? Go right ahead. By the time a court even schedules a hearing, your European buyer will have hit you with a 20 million yuan breach penalty. 12 million is actually a bargain. I am doing President Qin a favor."

Several of the lounging dock workers behind him snickered loudly.

Qin Yuhan did not flinch or raise her voice. Her gaze stayed clear and steady. "Haiyue Group does not pay extortion fees, Manager Fang. If you refuse to open this gate, our legal team will submit an emergency report to the Shenzhen Maritime Bureau and Port Authority immediately, along with full video records of this refusal."

"Go ahead and report us," Manager Fang laughed arrogantly, spreading his hands. "See how many days it takes for a paper complaint to reach this gate."

As his mocking laughter faded, his eyes drifted past Qin Yuhan and landed on Chen Xuan, who was leaning comfortably against the front fender of the MPV, taking a slow sip from his dented green thermos.

Manager Fang stared at the chipped green paint on the metal bottle, then burst out laughing again. "Wait a minute, President Qin. Who is this guy? Your personal driver, or a temporary errands clerk you dragged off the street? Carrying a fifteen-yuan tin thermos to a port negotiation? If Haiyue Group is already reduced to bringing shabby nobodies to stand around at my gate, maybe you really cannot afford the 12 million yuan."

The thin supervisor beside him joined in with a sharp snicker, glancing dismissively at Chen Xuan's plain navy windbreaker.

A cold spark flashed in Qin Yuhan's eyes. She took half a step sideways, shielding her husband with quiet dignity. "Manager Fang, that is my husband. You may argue business with me all you like, but if you insult him again, this will not end with a simple port complaint."

"Oh, your husband!" Manager Fang mocked, wiping a fake tear from the corner of his eye. "My apologies, President Qin. I did not realize the famous lady president married a clerk who carries his own soup bottle to work."

Chen Xuan did not look angry in the slightest. He screwed the cap neatly back onto his dented green thermos, set it gently on the hood of the MPV, and walked unhurriedly over to stand beside his wife. He lightly patted Qin Yuhan's hand to reassure her, then turned his calm gaze toward Manager Fang.

"Manager Fang," Chen Xuan said in a quiet, conversational tone. "I have one simple question. Are you completely sure the Shen Family wants to keep that padlock on the gate for the next twenty minutes?"

Manager Fang blinked, then let out another loud, scornful laugh. "Listen to this little clerk talking big! Twenty minutes? Kid, even if you stand here for twenty days, this gate is not moving an inch until Haiyue pays up! What are you going to do, call your logistics manager in Shanghai to cry about it?"

Chen Xuan smiled faintly. "Just making sure you will not regret your answer later."

Without another word, Chen Xuan reached into his windbreaker pocket, pulled out his single everyday smartphone, and tapped one number on the screen. As the coastal wind swept across the quiet concrete plaza, he raised the phone calmly to his ear.
