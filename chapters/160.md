# Chapter 160: The Gate of Shenzhen Pier Nine

At ten minutes to four, the silver sedan pulled up along the wide concrete dock road outside Shenzhen Pier Nine.

Beyond the tall iron fence, the afternoon sun beat down on the massive hull of the Haiyue Star. Six hundred containers sat silent on her deck, and the red port cranes stood frozen overhead. A thick chain and brass padlock barred the main gate, sealed with a red slip from the Shenzhen Port Inspection Office.

In front of the gate stood a waiting crowd.

Leaning against a black luxury sedan was a sharp-faced young man in a tailored light-gray linen suit, tapping a cigarette lazily against a silver case.

Shen Jingzhou, eldest grandson of the Shen Family.

Flanking him on the left stood two port officials in dark-blue uniforms, led by Chief Inspector Lu Guobin. On his right stood six broad-shouldered bodyguards in dark suits. Nearby, three Haiyue dock clerks stood sweating in helpless anger.

The silver sedan stopped. Qin Yuhan opened the rear door before her driver could reach it, stepping onto the hot concrete with her back straight and her gaze cool. One step behind her, Chen Xuan climbed out calmly, holding his plain cloth folder in one hand and his scratched green thermos in the other.

Shen Jingzhou slid his dark sunglasses off with two fingers and let a slow, mocking smile spread across his face.

"Well, well," Shen Jingzhou drawled, pushing off the car hood and strolling forward. "President Qin Yuhan herself, all the way from Shanghai! You look even better in person than in the business magazines. Forgive the heat. Shenzhen sun is rough on northern guests."

Qin Yuhan did not offer her hand. She stopped three meters away, her expression cool as glass.

"Young Master Shen," Qin Yuhan said crisply. "I did not fly two thousand kilometers to discuss the weather. The Haiyue Star passed a full national safety check in Shanghai four days ago. Here is the stamped report."

She held the thin folder toward Chief Inspector Lu Guobin. "Chief Inspector Lu, my captain offered to run the ballast valve test right in front of your team yesterday. Why is my vessel still chained behind this gate?"

Lu Guobin coughed behind his hand, avoiding Qin Yuhan's sharp eyes. He glanced sideways at Shen Jingzhou before puffing out his chest.

"President Qin, please do not raise your voice at port officials," Lu Guobin said stiffly. "Shanghai's check is Shanghai's business. Shenzhen Port has its own safety rules. Our paperwork shows a possible valve reading error, and the review committee needs seven working days to approve a re-check. Until that stamp clears, nobody opens Pier Nine."

"Seven working days," Qin Yuhan repeated coldly. "Which just happens to be three days after my southern delivery contracts expire."

Shen Jingzhou chuckled softly. He lit his cigarette with a gold lighter and blew a thin stream of smoke into the salty breeze.

"President Qin, why blame Chief Inspector Lu?" Shen Jingzhou said smoothly, his voice dripping with fake sympathy. "He is only keeping Shenzhen Harbor safe. Still, I know how painful this delay must be for Haiyue Group. Docking fees, cold-chain power, late delivery penalties: that comes to roughly 8 million yuan every single day, does it not?"

Qin Yuhan's jaw tightened slightly, though her voice stayed level. "Stop playing games, Shen Jingzhou. You asked me to meet you here at four o'clock. Say what you want."

Shen Jingzhou's smirk widened. He snapped his fingers. An assistant stepped out from behind the luxury sedan holding a thick printed contract and a black fountain pen.

"I like a woman who speaks plainly," Shen Jingzhou said, stepping closer until only two paces separated them. "Shenzhen Port has been the Shen Family's home for twenty years. You came down last autumn, took two southern supply lines right under our noses, and never even paid respects to my grandfather."

"We won those contracts through open bidding," Qin Yuhan answered sharply.

"Open bidding means nothing at Pier Nine," Shen Jingzhou scoffed, his politeness vanishing. He pointed lazily at the contract. "Here is my offer, President Qin. Hand over 30% of Haiyue Group's southern coastal routes to Shenhai Shipping, and let my family handle all of Haiyue's unloading at Shenzhen Port. Sign right now, and I will ask Chief Inspector Lu to rush his review. Your cranes will move before dark."

He leaned forward with smug triumph in his eyes.

"If you refuse, the Haiyue Star will rot right there at the dock until your buyers tear up their contracts and beg Shenhai Shipping to carry their cargo instead. Your choice."

Behind Qin Yuhan, Haiyue's local port manager clenched his fists until his knuckles turned white.

Qin Yuhan did not even glance at the contract. Her eyes met Shen Jingzhou's with icy contempt.

"You are dreaming, Shen Jingzhou," Qin Yuhan said clearly. "Haiyue Group built those routes with honest work. I would rather eat the loss than hand a single percent of our southern lines to dock bullies hiding behind a fake inspection stamp."

The smile vanished from Shen Jingzhou's face. A dark flush crept up his neck.

"Fine!" Shen Jingzhou snapped, dropping his cigarette and crushing it under his heel. "Since President Qin is so stubborn, let her burn 8 million yuan a day! Chief Inspector Lu, pack up and go enjoy an early dinner. Pier Nine stays locked for seven days! Nobody moves a single crate!"

"Yes, Young Master Shen!" Lu Guobin replied promptly, turning toward his van.

Shen Jingzhou shot Qin Yuhan a sneering look and turned to walk back to his luxury sedan.

Just as he took his first step, a quiet figure stepped calmly forward from behind Qin Yuhan.

Chen Xuan walked to the center of the concrete road, stopping right between Shen Jingzhou and the locked iron gate. He looked completely ordinary in his plain white cotton shirt and dark trousers, holding a cloth folder under one arm and a scratched green thermos in his hands.

Shen Jingzhou halted, scowling. He looked Chen Xuan up and down and let out a loud, scornful laugh.

"Who the hell is this?" Shen Jingzhou sneered. "President Qin, did you run out of guards and bring an office tea-boy to block my car?"

"I work at Hengxin Logistics," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Six of our containers are on that ship."

"Hengxin Logistics?" Shen Jingzhou laughed harder, and his bodyguards chuckled behind him. "Some tiny shipping office from Shanghai? Get out of my sight before my men toss you and your rusty bottle into the harbor!"

Two broad-shouldered bodyguards stepped forward at once, reaching toward Chen Xuan's shoulders.

Chen Xuan did not flinch or step back. He did not even look at them.

Instead, with slow, easy calm, he unscrewed the dented lid of his scratched green thermos. A faint puff of warm steam drifted into the sea breeze, carrying the clean scent of jasmine tea. Chen Xuan raised the thermos, took one calm sip, and screwed the lid back on with a soft metallic click.

The two bodyguards stopped half a pace away, strangely unsettled by the total lack of fear in his eyes.

Chen Xuan lowered the green thermos and looked straight into Shen Jingzhou's stunned, angry face. His voice was calm and flat, yet every word cut clearly through the salty afternoon air.

"You have until five o'clock to open the gate. I do not like working overtime."

Dead silence fell over the gate of Shenzhen Pier Nine.
