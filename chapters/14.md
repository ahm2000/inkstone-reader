# Chapter 14: Hongrui Real Estate's Glass Office

The twelfth floor of Hongrui Plaza was the tallest lit space in Yunxi County at midnight. Floor-to-ceiling tinted glass wrapped three sides of the corner suite, looking down over the rain-slick rooftops of the new district and the dark ribbon of the Yunxi River beyond.

At the center of the marble reception hall sat a four-meter architectural scale model under a clear acrylic hood. Tiny plastic apartment towers, illuminated riverfront promenades, and manicured green parks stood right where the low brick storefronts of Old Street still sat in reality.

Deng Yongchang paced past the glowing model for the tenth time in five minutes, a freshly lit cigarette pinched between his stubby fingers while the previous butt still smoldered in a crystal ashtray on his desk. The collar of his tailored silk shirt was unbuttoned, damp with sweat despite the cool hum of the central air conditioning.

Beside the leather sofa, Hongrui Real Estate's finance director, Qian Weidong, held a glowing smartphone pressed to his left ear and a landline receiver against his right. His round face was pale beneath the recessed ceiling spotlights.

"Still nothing?" Deng Yongchang snapped, spinning on his heel beside the miniature riverfront bridge. "It has been twenty minutes, Qian Weidong! We keep forty million in revolving deposits at Lanshui Commercial Bank alone. Why is Vice President Liu Gang not answering his private number?"

Qian Weidong lowered both phones with a stiff shake of his head. "His phone went straight to power-off five minutes ago, Boss Deng Yongchang. The county branch manager answered once, sounded terrified, and told me the freeze order did not come from Yunxi County or even Lanshui. The clearing block came down through the national interbank risk desk with an encrypted compliance code. He cannot lift it, and he cannot even print the reason."

Deng Yongchang stopped dead beside the glass table, ash dropping unnoticed onto his polished shoe. "National interbank risk desk? On a Friday night before midnight? For a county real estate developer?"

He crushed the half-smoked cigarette into the crystal dish and reached for another from the gold-trimmed pack on his desk. His hand shook slightly as the lighter flared.

Before Qian Weidong could reply, the heavy double doors at the far end of the glass office swung open with a sharp bang.

Yan Tiehu strode in, dripping rainwater onto the pale marble floor. His black leather jacket hung open over a damp shirt, and his left hand gripped his right wrist, flexing the joint with a grimace of lingering pain. His thick brows were knotted with humiliated fury.

"Boss Deng Yongchang!" Yan Tiehu barked, ignoring Qian Weidong completely. "Tell Cheng Biao to send a full squad down to Old Street tomorrow morning. That Chen family has someone backing them, or that clerk son of theirs is completely out of his mind!"

Deng Yongchang's eyes snapped up, cold and brittle. "What are you shouting about, Yan Tiehu? You were supposed to keep Old Street quiet until Chen Mingde signed over the hardware store."

"I tried to talk sense to him!" Yan Tiehu spat, gesturing awkwardly with his sore wrist. "Chen Jianguo's son, Chen Xuan, walked down to Mingde Hardware in the rain like he owned the pavement. Calm as a dead fish. Did not blink when six of my boys blocked the lane. Grabbed my wrist without shifting his feet and nearly snapped the bone clean through! Before I could call the rest of the crew from the van, half a dozen quiet men over at Gong's Noodles put their chopsticks down at the exact same second and stared at us. Not a word out of them, Boss Deng Yongchang. Just dead, flat eyes like soldiers out of uniform."

Deng Yongchang stared at Yan Tiehu. Smoke from his cigarette curled straight up past his tense jaw.

First, Chen Jianguo and his family never showed up at Old Street Station. Second, four corporate escrow accounts across three banks locked simultaneously from above the province. Third, Yan Tiehu got brushed aside by an office clerk from Qingyan while silent, disciplined men watched from Gong's Noodles across the wet lane.

Deng Yongchang did not believe in three coincidences arriving on the same Friday night.

"Shut your mouth, Yan Tiehu," Deng Yongchang said in a flat, venomous voice. "Pull every single one of your boys off Old Street right now. Do not go near Mingde Hardware. Do not go near Fuxing New Village. Do not even drive past Gong's Noodles."

Yan Tiehu froze, his jaw slackening. "Pull them off? Boss Deng Yongchang, if word spreads on Old Street that some skinny clerk from Qingyan made me back down, none of the remaining holdouts will sign the buyout sheets!"

"To hell with your face on Old Street!" Deng Yongchang roared, slamming his palm onto the acrylic hood of the architectural model so hard the miniature streetlights rattled. "Our clearing accounts are frozen! Do you understand what happens if the upstairs buyers in Lanshui and Qingyan miss Monday's settlement window because you started a street brawl over a two-room hardware shop? Lay low! Disappear to the north warehouse until I tell you to breathe again!"

Yan Tiehu recoiled half a step, silenced by the naked fear behind Deng Yongchang's rage. He muttered under his breath, turned sharply on his heel, and marched back out through the glass doors.

In the heavy silence that followed, Deng Yongchang lowered his gaze to the miniature riverfront beneath his palm. "Qian Weidong," he said quietly. "Go down to the basement server room and verify that the off-ledger backup drive is still offline. Nobody outside this building is supposed to know what runs through those escrow shells."

Three kilometers away along Riverside Road, inside the darkened cargo bay of the Qinghe Fresh Florals van, the air was cool and silent save for the whisper of cooling fans.

Number Eleven sat in the center seat, eyes fixed calmly above his charcoal half-mask on two tall columns of synchronized ledger code. As the Black Pine Guard's lead financial intelligence analyst, he had requested the overnight compliance freeze through the Boss's silent regulatory channels as a containment lock so Deng Yongchang could not wipe or shift balances while the forensic mirror ran.

"Basement server extraction complete," Number Thirty-four murmured from the signals console. "The accounting machine was air-gapped, except their electrician ran its backup power through the plaza lobby's smart meter circuit. We pulled a passive mirror of the encrypted partition and matched the corporate registry three minutes ago."

"Decryption key verified?" Number Seven asked quietly behind Number Eleven's shoulder.

"Verified," Number Eleven answered, and tapped enter.

Rows of obfuscated ledgers snapped into plain columns across the display: corporate filings, municipal demolition escrow accounts, bridge loans, and four years of interbank clearing batches linked to Hongrui Real Estate. Number Eleven scrolled past mundane concrete invoices and local buyouts until he reached the third tab of the off-ledger reconciliation workbook.

Number Eleven's hand stopped dead on the trackball.

Above the rim of his charcoal half-mask, his eyes narrowed sharply. He leaned closer to the glowing glass, checking the decimal placement twice before running a summation query across four consecutive fiscal years.

The total appeared in stark white digits at the bottom right corner of the screen.

"Number Seven," Number Eleven said, his calm voice suddenly razor-sharp. "Stop the local wrap-up brief. Tell Number Fourteen to come look at this right now."
