# Chapter 14: Hongrui Real Estate's Glass Office

At midnight, the twelfth floor of Hongrui Plaza was the only lit office in the new district. Inside the glass corner room, Deng Yongchang paced past the glowing tabletop model of his riverside buildings. He smoked one cigarette after another, his silk collar unbuttoned and damp with sweat.

Beside the sofa, finance director Qian Weidong sat pale-faced. He held a cell phone to his left ear and a desk phone to his right.

"Still nothing?" Deng Yongchang snapped, spinning around beside the toy bridge. "Twenty minutes, Qian Weidong! We keep 40 million in deposits at Jingzhou City Commercial Bank alone. Why is Vice President Liu Gang not answering his private number?"

Qian Weidong lowered both phones and gave a stiff shake of his head. "His phone went straight to turned-off five minutes ago, Chairman Deng. The county branch manager picked up once, terrified, and hung up in twenty seconds. He said the freeze did not come from Yunxi County or Jingzhou City. It dropped straight from the national banking office in the capital with a locked review code. He cannot lift it, and his computer will not even show the reason."

Deng Yongchang stopped dead, cigarette ash falling onto his shoe. "The national banking office? On a Friday night before midnight? For a county real estate company?"

"Chairman Deng," Qian Weidong said tightly, wiping his sweaty palms on his pants, "should I call Director Song in Jingzhou City right now? If Monday's payment deadline passes while those accounts stay locked, the big buyers upstairs will tear us apart."

"Do not call anyone in Jingzhou City yet!" Deng Yongchang barked. He crushed his cigarette into the tray and lit another with shaking fingers. "You want to leave a midnight phone record linking our numbers while a bank review is active? Let me think!"

Before Qian Weidong could answer, the double doors banged open.

Yan Tiehu marched in dripping rainwater. He gripped his swollen right wrist, his face twisted in angry shame.

"Chairman Deng!" Yan Tiehu shouted, ignoring Qian Weidong. "Tell Cheng Biao to send a full police squad down to Old Street at dawn. That Chen family has someone backing them, or that clerk son of theirs is completely crazy!"

Deng Yongchang's eyes snapped up, cold and sharp. "What are you yelling about, Yan Tiehu? You had one job: keep Old Street quiet until Chen Mingde signed over his shop."

"I tried!" Yan Tiehu spat, waving his sore wrist. "Chen Jianguo's son, Chen Xuan, walked down to Mingde Hardware in the rain like he owned the whole street. He did not even blink when six of my boys blocked the alley. He grabbed my wrist without moving his feet and nearly snapped the bone clean through! Before I could call my van crew, half a dozen men inside Gong's Noodles put their chopsticks down at once and stared straight at us. Not a word, Chairman Deng. Just flat, cold eyes like soldiers out of uniform."

Deng Yongchang froze, his lighter unlit under his cigarette.

Qian Weidong stared up from the sofa, his mouth falling open in dread. "Half a dozen men moving on cue? And our bank accounts froze the exact same night?"

Deng Yongchang stared at Yan Tiehu's bruised wrist as three facts snapped together in his head. First, Chen Jianguo's family never showed up at Old Street Station. Second, four company bank accounts across three banks locked at the same minute from above the province. Third, Yan Tiehu got brushed aside by a Shanghai office clerk while silent, trained men watched from across the street.

Deng Yongchang did not believe in three lucky breaks on one Friday night.

"Shut your mouth, Yan Tiehu," Deng Yongchang said in a flat, ugly rasp. "Pull every single boy off Old Street tonight. Stay away from Mingde Hardware, Fuxing New Village, and Gong's Noodles."

Yan Tiehu's jaw dropped. "Pull them off? Chairman Deng, if word gets out tomorrow that some skinny clerk made me back down, none of the other shopkeepers will sign! My name in Yunxi County will be a joke!"

"To hell with your name on Old Street!" Deng Yongchang roared, slamming his palm onto the plastic cover of the building model so hard the tiny streetlights rattled. "Our bank accounts are frozen! Do you know what happens to us if the big buyers in Jingzhou City and Shanghai miss Monday's payment window because you started a street fight over a two-room hardware store? Lay low! Hide out at the north warehouse and do not make a sound until I call you!"

Yan Tiehu stepped back in shock. Behind Deng Yongchang's anger sat pure, trapped fear. Swallowing his words, Yan Tiehu muttered a curse and marched out through the glass doors.

Deng Yongchang kept his palms flat on the plastic cover until his breathing slowed down.

"Qian Weidong," he said quietly without looking up.

"Yes, Chairman Deng?" Qian Weidong stood up fast, his knees shaking.

"Go down to the basement computer room yourself," Deng Yongchang ordered. "Check the door lock and make sure the secret backup drive is still unplugged. Nobody outside this building is supposed to know what runs through those shell accounts."

Qian Weidong hurried toward the elevator.

Three kilometers away on Riverside Road, inside the dark Qinghe Fresh Florals van, Number 11 sat at the middle desk watching neat rows of numbers scroll past above his gray half-mask. As the White Guard's top money expert, he had triggered the overnight bank freeze through a contact inside the central bank's anti-money-laundering office, who sent the review code. It was a safety lock so Deng Yongchang could not empty the accounts while they copied the files.

"Basement computer copy is done," Number 34 said quietly from the signals desk. "Their main computer had no internet line, except their electrician wired its backup power straight into the lobby's smart power meter. We pulled a silent copy of the locked files and matched them to the company records three minutes ago."

Number 7 stepped up behind Number 11's shoulder. "Does the unlock key work?"

"It works," Number 11 said evenly, and hit enter.

Scrambled lines snapped into plain tables: company papers, buyout bank accounts, short-term loans, and four years of bank transfers linked to Hongrui Real Estate.

"Ordinary county cheating at first look," Number 7 said, scanning the top lines. "Fake concrete bills, rigged land prices, and local bribes."

"Wait," Number 11 murmured. He scrolled past the local shop buyouts and opened the third sheet of the secret account book.

His fingers stopped dead on the mouse.

Number 7 noticed how suddenly still his shoulders went. "What did you find?"

Number 11 did not answer right away. Above his gray half-mask, his eyes narrowed sharply. He checked the numbers twice and ran a four-year total across every linked bank batch.

A single total popped up in bright white digits in the bottom right corner.

Number 11 lifted his hand from the keyboard. His calm voice suddenly turned razor-sharp.

"Number 7," Number 11 said. "Stop the local report. Tell Number 14 to come look at this right now."
