# Chapter 14: Hongrui Real Estate's Glass Office

At midnight, the twelfth floor of Hongrui Plaza was the only lit suite in the new district. Inside the glass corner office, Deng Yongchang paced past the illuminated scale model of his riverfront project, chain-smoking with his silk collar unbuttoned and damp with sweat.

Beside the sofa, finance director Qian Weidong sat pale-faced with a smartphone pressed to his left ear and a landline receiver to his right.

"Still nothing?" Deng Yongchang snapped, spinning around beside the miniature bridge. "Twenty minutes, Qian Weidong! We keep 40 million in revolving deposits at Lanshui Commercial Bank alone. Why is Vice President Liu Gang not answering his private number?"

Qian Weidong lowered both handsets with a stiff shake of his head. "His phone went straight to power-off five minutes ago, Chairman Deng. The county branch manager answered once, terrified, and hung up in twenty seconds. He said the freeze did not come from Yunxi County or Lanshui. It dropped straight from the national interbank risk desk with an encrypted compliance code. He cannot lift it, and his terminal will not print the reason."

Deng Yongchang stopped dead, ash dropping onto his shoe. "National interbank risk desk? On a Friday night before midnight? For a county real estate developer?"

"Chairman Deng," Qian Weidong said tightly, wiping his palms on his trousers, "should I call Director Song in Lanshui right now? If Monday's settlement window closes while those accounts stay locked, the upstairs buyers will tear us apart."

"Do not call anyone in Lanshui yet!" Deng Yongchang barked, crushing his cigarette into the dish and lighting another with shaking fingers. "You want to leave a midnight call log linking our phones while an audit flag is live? Let me think!"

Before Qian Weidong could reply, the double doors banged open.

Yan Tiehu strode in dripping rainwater, gripping his swollen right wrist with a grimace of humiliated rage.

"Chairman Deng!" Yan Tiehu shouted, ignoring Qian Weidong. "Tell Cheng Biao to send a full squad down to Old Street at dawn. That Chen family has someone behind them, or that clerk son of theirs is out of his mind!"

Deng Yongchang's eyes snapped up, cold and brittle. "What are you shouting about, Yan Tiehu? You had one job: keep Old Street quiet until Chen Mingde signed over his shop."

"I tried!" Yan Tiehu spat, gesturing with his sore wrist. "Chen Jianguo's son, Chen Xuan, walked down to Mingde Hardware in the rain like he owned the pavement. Calm as a dead fish. Did not blink when six of my boys blocked the lane. He caught my wrist without shifting his feet and nearly snapped the bone clean through! Before I could call the van crew, half a dozen quiet men inside Gong's Noodles set their chopsticks down at the exact same second and stared at us. Not a word, Chairman Deng. Just flat, dead eyes like soldiers out of uniform."

Deng Yongchang froze, his lighter unlit beneath his cigarette.

Qian Weidong stared up from the sofa, lips parting in dread. "Half a dozen men moving on cue? And our clearing accounts froze the same night?"

Deng Yongchang kept his gaze locked on Yan Tiehu's bruised wrist as three facts clicked together. First, Chen Jianguo's family never showed up at Old Street Station. Second, four corporate escrow accounts across three banks locked simultaneously from above the province. Third, Yan Tiehu got brushed aside by a Qingyan clerk while silent, disciplined men watched from across the lane.

Deng Yongchang did not believe in three coincidences on one Friday night.

"Shut your mouth, Yan Tiehu," Deng Yongchang said in a flat, venomous rasp. "Pull every boy off Old Street tonight. Stay away from Mingde Hardware, Fuxing New Village, and Gong's Noodles."

Yan Tiehu's jaw went slack. "Pull them off? Chairman Deng, if word spreads tomorrow that some skinny clerk made me back down, none of the remaining holdouts will sign! My name in Yunxi County will be a joke!"

"To hell with your name on Old Street!" Deng Yongchang roared, slamming his palm onto the acrylic hood of the model so hard the miniature streetlights rattled. "Our clearing accounts are frozen! Do you know what happens to us if the upstairs buyers in Lanshui and Qingyan miss Monday's settlement window because you started a street brawl over a two-room hardware store? Lay low! Disappear to the north warehouse and do not breathe until I call you!"

Yan Tiehu recoiled half a step. Behind Deng Yongchang's rage sat naked, cornered terror. Swallowing his protest, Yan Tiehu muttered a curse and marched out through the glass doors.

Heavy silence settled over the suite. Deng Yongchang kept his palms flat on the acrylic hood until his breathing steadied.

"Qian Weidong," he said quietly without looking up.

"Yes, Chairman Deng?" Qian Weidong rose quickly, knees unsteady.

"Go to the basement server room yourself," Deng Yongchang ordered. "Check the physical lock and verify the off-ledger backup drive is still offline. Nobody outside this building is supposed to know what runs through those escrow shells."

Qian Weidong nodded twice, gray-faced, and hurried toward the elevator.

Three kilometers away on Riverside Road, inside the darkened Qinghe Fresh Florals van, Number 11 sat at the center console watching synchronized columns of code above his charcoal half-mask. As the Black Pine Guard's lead financial intelligence analyst, he had triggered the overnight compliance freeze through Chen Xuan's silent regulatory channels as a containment lock so Deng Yongchang could not wipe balances while the forensic mirror ran.

"Basement server extraction complete," Number 34 murmured from the signals desk. "Their accounting machine was air-gapped, except their electrician wired its backup power through the lobby's smart meter circuit. We pulled a passive mirror of the encrypted partition and matched the corporate registry three minutes ago."

Number 7 stepped behind Number 11's shoulder. "Decryption key verified?"

"Verified," Number 11 said evenly, and tapped enter.

Obfuscated entries snapped into plain columns: corporate filings, demolition escrow accounts, bridge loans, and four years of interbank clearing batches linked to Hongrui Real Estate.

"Standard county graft at first glance," Number 7 observed, scanning the top rows. "Inflated concrete invoices, rigged appraisals, local kickbacks."

"Wait," Number 11 murmured. He scrolled past the local buyouts and opened the third tab of the off-ledger reconciliation workbook.

His fingers stopped dead on the trackball.

Number 7 caught the sudden stillness in his shoulders. "What did you find?"

Number 11 did not answer at once. Above his charcoal half-mask, his eyes narrowed sharply. He checked the decimal placement twice and ran a four-year summation query across every linked clearing batch.

A single total appeared in stark white digits at the bottom right corner.

Number 11 lifted his hand from the keyboard, his calm voice suddenly razor-sharp.

"Number 7," Number 11 said. "Stop the local wrap-up brief. Tell Number 14 to come look at this right now."
