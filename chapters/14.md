# Chapter 14: The Five A.M. Call

It was ten past five on Sunday morning when the duty-room desk phone jangled so violently that Wei Kun nearly tumbled off his folding chair.

He'd dozed off with his uniform boots still laced and half a cold sesame bun resting on his chest. Scrambling upright, he snatched the receiver. "Old Street Station, duty officer Wei Kun speaking."

"Wei Kun!"

The thunderous, flat voice on the line belonged to Mai Zhenhua, Director of the County Police Bureau and also a Deputy County Magistrate, the highest law-enforcement boss in Yunxi County. To a low-level duty officer like Wei Kun, hearing the Director call personally at five in the morning on a Sunday felt like lightning striking the desk!

"Listen to every word," Mai Zhenhua snapped. "Chen Mingde, the hardware shop owner in your holding cell. Release him immediately! Not after breakfast, not in an hour. Right now! You held a citizen for eighteen hours without a signed detention order, relied on a forged private clinic note dated fifteen minutes before the arrest, and every single Old Street detention paper from the past nine weeks is under investigation by the city Discipline Commission! I am already in my car. If Chen Mingde is still behind bars when I step through your front door, you can trade places with him!"

The line clicked dead.

Wei Kun sat staring blindly at the humming receiver while the cold sesame bun rolled off his lap and thumped onto the concrete floor.

What on earth had happened overnight?!

Last night at ten, a quiet noodle-shop owner in a flour-dusted apron had dropped off a steaming bowl of beef noodles with an extra egg and a thick blanket through the family window, strictly by station rules, while Cheng Biao was out drinking. And now, before sunrise on Sunday, the County Police Director himself was racing down the street in a panic, and the city anti-corruption commission was descending on Yunxi?!

Was it because of the timestamp? His honest 7:50 a.m. timestamp? The one stubbornly truthful line he'd refused to fake?!

And suddenly Wei Kun thought of Instructor Qiu Guoqiang and the deputy station chief, both conveniently shipped off to a "training course" two weeks ago so nobody would stand in Cheng Biao's way. His chest hammered with a wild tangle of fear and relief!

By 5:40 a.m., word had already rippled through the waking dormitory upstairs. One officer elbowed another, not quite a whisper: "Odds are it's Wei Kun who takes the fall. He signed the sheet." "Not me," the other muttered back. "I didn't touch the forms. Officially, I was at lunch." Three bleary-eyed officers hurried down into the hallway just as the back door banged open and Cheng Biao stormed inside, his leather jacket half buttoned and his face gray with panic.

"Wei Kun! Who the hell called Director Mai?!" Cheng Biao barked, his toothpick gone and his swagger shattered. "Did that office clerk file a complaint?!"

"No one called me from Yunxi, Cheng Biao," a cold voice cut in from the front doorway.

Mai Zhenhua stood there with his coat collar turned up, two stern-faced internal affairs officers flanking his shoulders. "The city police chief woke me up past midnight, and he sounded ready to tear this whole bureau apart. Effective immediately, you are suspended from duty. Hand your station keys to Wei Kun. Instructor Qiu is returning from his 'training course' this morning, and this station is back under his command!"

Cheng Biao's jaw dropped open, and for the first time in his career, not a single smooth excuse came out!

"Director Mai, there has to be a misunderstanding! Everything we did on Old Street followed normal police rules!"

"Save your excuses for the Municipal Discipline Commission," Mai Zhenhua said icily. "Their investigators will be sitting in our meeting room at nine sharp."

Down the hallway, hushed whispers exploded among the watching officers!

"Did you hear that? Suspended right away!"

"The city Discipline Commission is coming at nine?!"

"Wait, wasn't that over the quiet hardware shopkeeper from Old Street? Who does that old man know?!"

Cheng Biao stood rooted to the tile floor, cold sweat trickling down his spine.

Who on earth had pulled this trigger?! Chen Mingde was a nobody! His older brother was a retired factory foreman! His nephew was just a mild-mannered logistics supervisor from Qingyan City! Could an ordinary clerk possibly have turned the entire city anti-corruption bureau loose overnight?!

Suddenly, Cheng Biao remembered that calm, polite young man standing outside the service glass yesterday morning, asking mildly, *"Is the lawyer reception window open on Saturdays?"*

A sick, hollow dread swallowed Cheng Biao's stomach. His brother-in-law's 38-million-yuan demolition contract. His wife's new villa. Next Wednesday. Everything he'd built over fifteen years had vanished in a single night, like water poured onto dry sand!

At 6:20 a.m., pale morning light washed across the front steps of Old Street Police Station as Chen Mingde walked slowly out into the cool air, wearing yesterday's jacket and holding a paper cup of hot tea.

County Police Director Mai Zhenhua walked him personally to the top of the steps, shook his rough hand firmly with both of his own, and delivered a stiff, formal apology on behalf of the bureau before turning briskly back inside, leaving Cheng Biao standing humiliated behind the glass doors in full view of the waking station. Behind Chen Mingde, the office clerk stammered hurried apologies, and someone had nervously tucked a fresh pack of cigarettes into his coat pocket. Chen Mingde held his tea with fingers that still trembled slightly, blinking in the sunrise.

Minutes earlier, at the front counter, the clerk had pushed back his property bag: wallet, phone, belt, house keys. Chen Mingde had read the receipt twice before signing it with the clerk's pen.

"Brother Chen," Wei Kun hurried down the steps beside him, bowing stiffly. "We are truly sorry. This was a terrible mistake on our part."

"Did I win the lottery or something?" Chen Mingde murmured in a weak, bewildered voice.

Then he paused on the bottom step and turned back to look directly into the round-faced officer's tired eyes.

"The hot water Friday night," Chen Mingde said softly. "Thank you, young man."

Wei Kun's eyes stung hotly. He didn't trust his voice to answer. He simply bent from the waist and bowed lower than anyone at Old Street Station had ever seen him bow, holding it in silence until the old shopkeeper walked out into the morning light.
