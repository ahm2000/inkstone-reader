# Chapter 14: The Handoff

Inside the deep subterranean bunkers of the military establishment, ten separate internal command meetings burned through cartons of cigarettes before sunrise: the Joint Staff Department, the Political Work Department, the Logistics Support Department, the Equipment Development Department, the Strategic Support Force, the Rocket Force Command, the Naval Command, the Air Force Command, the Military Intelligence Bureau, and all five Theater Commands on encrypted link.

In the Defense Procurement and Science Command Hall, the smoke was thickest of all. Three senior generals stood hunched over a steel map table, cigarettes clenched between gritted teeth as they stared at high-resolution forensic intercepts from the Zero Guard dossier.

"Next-generation naval propulsion telemetry," a lieutenant general read aloud, his voice shaking with pure, murderous rage. "Missile-guidance calibration tables. Coastal power-grid override keys. Agricultural seed-bank genetics. All queued inside an encrypted burst relay scheduled to transmit overseas at four o'clock this morning."

He slammed his fist onto the steel table with a bang that made two junior colonels flinch. "Three hours! If the Zero Guard's electronic jammers hadn't turned the eastern corridor into a black hole last night, foreign analysts would be reading the blueprint to cripple our coastal grid over breakfast!"

"Who signed off on those test-range transfers?" the senior defense director demanded, cold sweat shining on his forehead as he crushed his cigarette into a tin tray. "Wait. Don't answer. Look at the signature block on page nine. That is a deputy director from our own Materials Institute."

A sick, freezing silence settled over the bunker. Every general in the room felt the same chill crawl up his spine: they were not fighting an outside army. The rot had been sitting beside them in their own meeting rooms, drinking their own tea.

* * *

At half past four in the morning, beneath the concrete aprons of the Ministry of State Security, the Central Discipline Commission, and the Supreme Military Procuratorate, heavy steel blast doors rolled open simultaneously.

Line after line of unmarked matte-black Zero Guard vans glided silently down the concrete ramps without headlights or sirens.

National agency commanders and armed tactical guards waited along the yellow floor markings, their palms damp on their holsters, barely daring to breathe as the rear doors swung open.

Fully masked, featureless Zero Guard operatives stepped out in dead silence. No skin showed. No badges showed. Not a single operative spoke a word to the generals and directors watching them.

One by one, they unloaded the night's catch: hooded, bound senior bureau chiefs from the Third Bureau, a trembling deputy director from Defense Materials, regional vice presidents from Huaxin Bank, and rows of sealed titanium server drives and encrypted satellite uplinks intercepted before dawn.

Every single national-level traitor and compromised intermediary had already been neutralized, stripped of electronics, and bagged with airtight forensic chains before the national agencies even finished reading their own internal briefings.

The lead masked figure walked up to the stunned Deputy Minister of State Security, laid a single matte-black handoff manifest into his hands, and spoke four flat, neutral, genderless words.

"Capture complete. Cleanup transferred."

At the bottom of the manifest sat one red-bordered directive covering the origin point of the trace: *YUNXI COUNTY AND QINGYAN LOCAL LAYER (CHENG BIAO, LU WENZHE, DONG HAISHENG, HUANG BOREN): HELD UNDER SILENT BORDER AND ACCOUNT LOCK. DO NOT LAUNCH NATIONAL RAID. LEAVE FOR LOCAL LEGAL RESOLUTION AFTER STAND-DOWN.*

Without waiting for a salute or a signature, the masked operatives stepped back into the black vans, rolled smoothly back up the concrete ramps, and vanished into the pre-dawn fog like smoke.

The Deputy Minister stared down at the row of hooded traitors kneeling on his own basement floor, his throat completely dry. A ghost unit nobody in his ministry had ever seen had just caught every mole in the capital in four hours and dumped them at his feet to clean up.

* * *

Ten minutes later, in the outer corridor of the Ministry of Public Security, two bureau chiefs leaned against a tiled wall, chain-smoking rapidly as their hands shook.

"Did you see who they unloaded at the Discipline Commission ramp?" one whispered, sucking hard on his cigarette. "Two ministerial deputies and four bank presidents. All bagged before sunrise without a single shot fired."

"Keep your voice down," the other hissed, glancing nervously at the mute black-masked figures still watching the stairwell. "The Vice Premier just summoned every department head to the State Council dark-wood hall. Whatever happens in that room in twenty minutes is going to rewrite half the government."
