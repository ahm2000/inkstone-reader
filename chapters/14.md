# Chapter 14: The Five A.M. Call

Before dawn in the duty room of Old Street Police Station, the desk phone jangled so loudly that Wei Kun nearly fell off his folding chair.

He had dozed off with his boots still laced and half a cold sesame bun on his chest, and he scrambled upright and grabbed the receiver. "Old Street Station, duty officer Wei Kun speaking."

"Wei Kun!"

The flat, thunderous voice belonged to Mai Zhenhua, and to put it simply, Mai was the Director of the County Police Bureau and also a Deputy County Magistrate, the top law-enforcement boss in all of Yunxi.

A low-level duty officer like Wei Kun rarely heard from him at noon, let alone at five on a Sunday!

"Listen to every word," Mai snapped. "Chen Mingde, the hardware owner in your holding cell. Release him! Not after breakfast, not in an hour, now! You held a citizen for eighteen hours without a signed order, and forty hours in all, you leaned on a forged clinic note dated fifteen minutes before the arrest, and every Old Street detention paper from the past nine weeks is under investigation by the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission! I am already in my car. If Chen Mingde is still behind bars when I walk through your door, you can take his place!"

Click.

Wei Kun stared at the humming receiver while the cold bun rolled off his lap and thumped onto the floor. What on earth had happened overnight?

Last night at ten, a quiet noodle-shop owner in a flour-dusted apron had brought a bowl of beef noodles with an extra egg and a thick blanket to the family window, strictly by station rules, while Cheng Biao was out drinking, and now the Director himself was racing down the road before sunrise!

Was it the timestamp, his own honest line of seven-fifty in the morning, the one line he had refused to fake?

Then he thought of Instructor Qiu Guoqiang and the deputy station chief, who had both been sent off to a "training course" two weeks ago so that nobody would stand in Cheng Biao's way.

His heart hammered with fear and relief at the same time!

Soon.

Within half an hour, the news had rippled through the dormitory upstairs.

"Odds are it is Wei Kun who takes the fall," one officer whispered. "He signed the sheet."

"Not me," another muttered. "I never touched the forms. Officially, I was at lunch."

Three bleary-eyed officers hurried down to the hallway just as the back door banged open.

Cheng Biao stormed in with his leather jacket half buttoned and his face gray with panic.

To put it nicely, Cheng Biao was the man who ran Old Street Station, and to put it bluntly, nobody there dared to say no to him.

"Wei Kun! Who the hell called Director Mai?" he barked, with his toothpick gone and his swagger gone with it. "Did that office clerk file a complaint?"

"It was not a clerk who woke me, Cheng Biao," a cold voice said from the front doorway.

Mai Zhenhua stood there with his coat collar turned up and two stern internal affairs officers at his shoulders, and the whole hallway went quiet.

"The city police chief woke me after midnight, and he sounded ready to tear this whole bureau apart. Effective immediately, you are suspended. Hand your station keys to Wei Kun. Instructor Qiu returns from his training course this morning, and this station is back under his command!"

Cheng Biao's jaw dropped, and for the first time in his career, no smooth excuse came out. He dropped his keys into Wei Kun's palm without a word.

"Yes, Director," Wei Kun said, and he took them with both hands.

"Director Mai, this is a misunderstanding! Everything we did on Old Street followed normal police rules!"

"Save it for the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission," Mai said icily. "Their investigators will be in our meeting room at nine sharp."

Silence.

Then, down the hallway, whispers burst out among the watching officers.

"Suspended on the spot!"

"The city Commission is coming at nine?"

"Was this really over that quiet hardware shopkeeper? Who does that old man know?"

Cheng Biao stood rooted to the tile floor, and cold sweat ran down his spine.

"Who pulled the trigger?" he whispered. "Chen Mingde is a nobody! His older brother is a retired factory foreman, and his nephew is a mild logistics supervisor from Qingyan City!"

Could an ordinary clerk turn the whole city anti-corruption bureau loose in a single night?

No, no, no.

Then Cheng Biao remembered the calm, polite young man at the service glass yesterday morning, asking mildly, "Is the lawyer window open on Saturdays?"

A sick, hollow dread filled his stomach. His brother-in-law's thirty-eight-million-yuan demolition contract, his wife's new villa, and the inspectors coming on Wednesday would all be swept away, and fifteen years of work had vanished in one night!

At sunrise, pale light fell across the front steps of Old Street Police Station.

Chen Mingde walked slowly out into the cool air, wearing the same jacket he had worn in on Friday and holding a paper cup of hot tea. Mai Zhenhua walked him personally to the top of the steps and shook his rough hand with both of his own.

"Mr. Chen, on behalf of the bureau, I apologize," he said stiffly. "You did nothing wrong, and we failed you."

Then Mai turned briskly back inside, and Cheng Biao was left standing behind the glass doors in full view of the waking station.

"Sorry, sorry, so sorry," the office clerk stammered behind Chen Mingde, and someone had tucked a fresh pack of cigarettes into his coat pocket.

Minutes earlier, at the front counter, the clerk had pushed back his property bag with the wallet, phone, belt and house keys, and Chen Mingde had read the receipt twice before signing it. Now he stood blinking in the sunrise, still holding his tea with fingers that trembled slightly.

"Brother Chen," Wei Kun hurried down the steps beside him and bowed stiffly. "We are truly sorry. This was a terrible mistake on our part."

"Did I win the lottery or something?" Chen Mingde murmured in a weak, bewildered voice.

Then he stopped on the bottom step and turned to look straight into the round-faced officer's tired eyes.

"The hot water on Friday night," he said softly. "Thank you, young man."

Wei Kun's eyes stung, and he did not trust his voice to answer.

He bent from the waist and bowed lower than anyone at Old Street Station had ever seen him bow, and he held it in silence until the old shopkeeper walked out into the morning light.
