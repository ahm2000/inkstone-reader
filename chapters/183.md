# Chapter 183: The Trail to Changbai Mountain

Sunlight slanted lower across the Huangpu River, casting long gold reflections across the marble floor of the Tomson Riviera apartment. Outside the glass, the Oriental Pearl Tower stood crisp and tall against the afternoon sky.

Inside the quiet living room, the scent of fresh jasmine tea drifted above the map spread across the stone table.

Chen Xuan leaned back comfortably in his chair, holding his teacup while looking down at the detailed map of the northeast. A thin red circle marked a deep, snow-covered valley tucked inside the pine forests of Changbai Mountain, also called Jhengbai by the old locals along the border.

"Changbai Mountain," Chen Xuan murmured calmly. "A southern smuggling ring operating out of Hong Kong, Macau, Shenzhen, and Shanghai hides its deepest vault in the frozen mountains of the northeast. Clever."

"Very clever, Sir," Number 11 agreed, tapping a white pen against the marked valley. "Everyone watching the Seven-Harbor Consortium looks south toward Hong Kong banks, Macau gambling rooms, or cargo ships leaving Yantian Port and Shenzhen Pier Nine. Nobody expects a coastal shipping syndicate to bury its real treasure three thousand kilometers north under two meters of winter snow."

Number 34 turned her matte-white laptop slightly so Chen Xuan could see the overhead photos and cargo records glowing on the screen.

"Sir, look at these freight invoices from Koo Tianming's second drive," Number 34 said crisply. "For twelve years, the Seven-Harbor Consortium bought illegal raw ore from unlicensed mines in southern provinces and smuggled high-grade rare metals through their seven coastal ports. Instead of shipping raw metal straight overseas where customs scanners would catch the weight and grade, they packed it inside timber trucks and northern coal trains heading straight into Jilin Province."

She tapped a key, bringing up a sharp winter photo of a narrow mountain valley surrounded by steep white ridges and dense pines.

"This valley sits forty kilometers east of the main tourist slopes of Changbai Mountain," Number 34 continued. "On paper, it is an abandoned state tungsten mine shut down twenty-two years ago. In reality, our thermal checks from last night show heavy industrial power lines running underground into the old mine shafts."

"A hidden rare-metal refinery," Chen Xuan said evenly.

"Yes, Sir," Number 11 confirmed, sliding a financial summary sheet beside the map. "Not just a refinery. It is their deepest inland vault. Inside those reinforced mountain tunnels, they smelt and purify rare-earth metals, high-grade gold, and specialty alloys used in foreign defense electronics. Then they pack the finished bars into small high-value crates and slip them across the mountain border at night."

Number 11 paused, his eyes steady. "According to Koo Tianming's private inventory updated three days before his arrest, the Changbai Mountain tunnels currently hold at least 60 billion yuan worth of refined rare metals, foreign gold bars, and unnumbered cash reserves. More importantly, the master ledger listing every domestic protector and every overseas buyer behind the Seven-Harbor Consortium is kept inside that vault on paper, never online."

Number 1 poured fresh hot water into the porcelain teapot, his expression calm and unhurried. "60 billion yuan in one mountain valley, plus the names of everyone backing them from overseas. No wonder Koo Tianming panicked the moment he saw a White Order in Shenzhen."

"He knew the southern ports were only the front gate," Chen Xuan remarked quietly, taking a slow sip of jasmine tea. "Once Shen Wanshan fell, Koo Tianming knew somebody would open his drives and follow the money north."

"There is one more problem inside that valley, Sir," Number 34 spoke up, opening a second folder on her matte-white laptop. Grainy nighttime photos appeared on the screen, showing bearded men in winter camouflage carrying modern foreign rifles beside armored snow vehicles.

"These are not street thugs like Liang Biao's baton men in Shenzhen," Number 34 reported seriously. "We cross-checked three faces from Koo Tianming's payroll logs. They are former foreign soldiers hired through a private armed contractor firm registered in Eastern Europe and paid through Bank of China overseas transfer shells in Hong Kong. At least eighty armed men rotate through the Changbai Mountain valley in winter shifts."

Number 11 nodded grimly. "And the valley has only one narrow frozen road leading in through the pine forest. Every ridge overlooking that road has hidden cameras and snow sensors. Worse, Koo Tianming's safety plan states that if regular troops or armed police try to force their way in, the foreign guards have standing orders to blow the main tunnel supports and retreat over the border ridge on snowmobiles."

"If they blow those tunnels," Number 1 added evenly, "60 billion yuan in national rare metals and every paper ledger naming their foreign backers will be buried under a mountain of rock before a single officer reaches the vault doors."

For several quiet seconds, nobody spoke. Outside the floor-to-ceiling windows, a slow river barge sounded a low horn beneath the shadow of the Oriental Pearl Tower.

Chen Xuan set his porcelain teacup down gently on the marble table. His face remained completely unbothered, without a trace of rush or worry.

"Number 1," Chen Xuan asked mildly. "Did the Four National Banks find the same northern money trail yet?"

"Yes, Sir," Number 1 answered immediately. "Before dawn this morning, our people passed Koo Tianming's first banking summary cleanly into the normal audit desks of China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank. By noon today, all four national headquarters in Beijing flagged the fake forestry loans and power-grid payments pouring into Changbai Mountain. State Security Minister Ding Guoping called an emergency meeting in Beijing for this evening."

Chen Xuan smiled faintly, leaning back into the soft leather sofa. "Good. That saves me from having to make a single phone call."

"Sir, should I prepare our northern White Guard teams to move toward Changbai Mountain tonight?" Number 11 asked, sitting straighter. "We have matte-white winter gear and cold-weather vehicles ready outside Harbin and Changchun."

"Not yet," Chen Xuan replied smoothly. "Let Beijing look at the map first. Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and General Qin Zhengguo are not fools. Once they see foreign armed contractors sitting inside a snow valley near the border with explosives strapped to 60 billion yuan of rare metals, they will realize right away that loud boots and helicopters will only ruin the whole catch."

Number 34 blinked slightly, then a small knowing smile touched her lips. "You want to see how Beijing moves before we step into the snow, Sir?"

"Always let the people holding the official stamp do their own math first," Chen Xuan said calmly, rising from the sofa and picking up his faded dark-blue windbreaker. "Number 11, keep two quiet observation pairs outside the thirty-kilometer outer ring of Changbai Mountain. Do not trip a single sensor, and do not engage anyone. Number 34, keep watching the Hong Kong and overseas accounts in case their foreign backers try to order an early evacuation."

"Understood, Sir," Number 11 and Number 34 replied together.

"Number 1," Chen Xuan added lightly as he walked toward the private elevator doors. "Drop me two blocks from my old apartment on West Nanjing Road. Tomorrow is Monday, and my desk at Hengxin Logistics still has a stack of ordinary shipping manifests waiting for me."

Number 1 inclined his head with a faint smile. "Your pearl-white sedan is waiting downstairs, Sir."

As the elevator doors slid shut over the sunlit Tomson Riviera living room, Chen Xuan slipped his hands calmly into his windbreaker pockets. Up in Beijing, the cigarette smoke was already beginning to thicken.
