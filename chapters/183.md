# Chapter 183: The Trail to Changbai Mountain

While the radish and pork rib soup simmered gently over a low flame, Chen Xuan stepped out through the glass doors onto the back terrace of Emerald Riverside Villa. The late November air had sharpened as dusk settled over Pudong, carrying the clean, damp scent of the nearby river. Through the window behind him, he could see Qin Yuhan tucked comfortably under her blanket on the sofa, her eyes moving calmly across her tablet.

The single smartphone in his trouser pocket vibrated with a short, steady pulse. Chen Xuan slipped it out, glanced at the quiet numeric ID on the screen, and accepted the call without changing his relaxed posture.

"Speak," Chen Xuan said softly, leaning one elbow on the stone railing.

"Young Master," Number 1's steady, respectful voice came through the receiver. "We finished tracing the Shenzhen and Hong Kong gold clearing trail. The coastal shell firms were only transfer stations. Every physical shipment was routed north by private rail freight under forged timber and machinery manifests."

"Where does the line stop?" Chen Xuan asked calmly.

"Changbai Mountain," Number 1 answered cleanly. "Deep inside an abandoned state logging valley on the northern slope. Our people confirmed a hidden refinery built inside an old reinforced mine tunnel. They even restored a small mountain hydroelectric turbine from the old forestry station so their smelters never draw suspicious power from the provincial grid. Over the past fourteen months, roughly 300 tons of laundered and stolen gold bullion have been moved into that valley."

Chen Xuan's eyes narrowed a fraction as he looked out over the dark garden. Three hundred tons of gold was not a minor smuggling ring. It was a direct drain on national credit reserves, disguised beneath layers of bad provincial loans.

"Who is guarding the valley?" he asked.

"Armed foreign mercenaries hired through the Seven-Harbor network," Number 1 reported. "Roughly sixty men on rotation, equipped with heavy demolition charges wired along the tunnel entrance. They are not local thugs, Young Master. If anyone rushes the valley recklessly, they will collapse the mine."

Chen Xuan nodded slowly to himself. "Keep watching from a safe distance. Do not stir the mountain yet. Make sure the financial paper trail stays completely clean and visible for the state banks and Beijing's auditors to follow. They need to see the whole picture themselves."

"Understood, Young Master," Number 1 replied firmly. "We will hold position and await your next word."

The call ended cleanly. Chen Xuan slipped his single smartphone back into his pocket, took one last breath of the crisp night air, and walked back inside. From the sofa, Qin Yuhan glanced over the top of her tablet and asked with a smile if the radish soup was almost ready. Chen Xuan lifted the lid, let the rich savory steam rise into the kitchen, and promised her fifteen more minutes.

More than three hundred kilometers away in Jingzhou, the late autumn wind rattled the courtyard windows of Elder Chen Yanshi's modest residence. Inside the warm study, a pot of dark Pu'er tea steamed on a low wooden table surrounded by thick folders of provincial audit records.

Elder Chen Yanshi, retired Vice Procurator-General of Handong and foster father of Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin, poured four cups with a steady hand. Across from him sat Handong CDIC Secretary Tian Guofu and Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping. Beside the elder sat his son, Chen Hai, wearing a thick wool cardigan. Though still recovering from his long ordeal, Chen Hai's eyes were sharp and focused as he tapped a highlighted column in a blue binder.

"Look at these three guarantee companies," Chen Hai said, sliding the ledger toward Hou Liangping. "Before Zhao Ruilong was arrested, Shanshui Group did not stop at Jingzhou's land deals. Between last autumn and this summer, more than 800 million earmarked for the Luzhou Airport expansion and the Crescent Lake supporting roads vanished through these three firms. Even part of the Crescent Lake water cleanup budget was bundled into the same shell transfers."

Hou Liangping leaned forward, scanning the stamped transfer vouchers with a deep frown. "No wonder Yi Xuexi in Luzhou kept hitting a brick wall every time he asked why the airport contractor payments were stalled. The provincial budget approved the money, and Zhao Ruilong's old network bled it sideways before it ever reached the runway. For years they sidelined honest cadres like Yi Xuexi just to keep anyone from checking those accounts."

"Where did the three guarantee firms send it?" Tian Guofu asked, his voice low and incisive.

"North," Chen Hai replied, flipping to the next page of bank clearances. "Into a cluster of forestry development and timber-processing loans registered around Changbai Mountain. Every single company on the receiving end shares the same three offshore auditors in Hong Kong."

Tian Guofu exhaled slowly and set down his teacup. "That links our local anti-corruption sweep directly to the cross-border financial inquiry in Beijing. Secretary Sha Ruijin has already been briefed on the Luzhou funding gap, and he wants every yuan accounted for so Luzhou Airport can finally move forward. Because this trail crosses provincial borders into the northeast, Beijing's central banking and discipline teams will have to take the lead."

Elder Chen Yanshi looked thoughtfully at the steam rising from his cup. "When you pull up a rotten tree, you cannot just trim the dead branches in Handong," the old man said quietly. "If the deepest root runs all the way into the northern mountains, then Beijing has to dig it out completely."

Hou Liangping closed the binder with a firm snap. "I will transmit our verified Luzhou and Shanshui ledgers to the central joint task force tonight. By midnight, Beijing will have every link in front of them."
