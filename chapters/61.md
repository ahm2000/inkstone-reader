# Chapter 61: Eight Yuan a Day

At five thirty that evening, Chen Xuan stepped off the number four bus at the western edge of Yunxi County, carrying a plain red cloth bag with walnut powder and red dates. Nothing expensive, just a practical gift an ordinary clerk could bring without making an old friend uncomfortable.

Wang Lei's delivery yard sat behind a faded blue iron gate, barely forty paces wide, with crushed stone and a low corrugated-iron shed.

Parked neatly along the wall were two aging white micro-vans with clean windshields and swept cargo beds, beside the battered green electric tricycle from Old Street. Three drivers in their forties sat on plastic crates near a charcoal stove, eating hot noodles.

Seeing Chen Xuan push open the gate, Wang Lei dropped his clipboard onto a crate and hurried over, wiping his palms on his canvas trousers.

"Chen Xuan! You actually came all the way out here?" Wang Lei looked surprised, then slightly self-conscious as he glanced back at the cramped yard. "The place is rough, do not laugh at it."

"It is clean and orderly," Chen Xuan said sincerely, eyeing the stacked wooden pallets and the hand-ruled chalkboard listing every departure time. "Cleaner than half the big depots I see at work."

From the small brick room beside the shed, a thin woman in her fifties stepped out in a quilted cotton vest. Her face was pale from years of clinic visits, yet her eyes were warm.

"Is that Chen Xuan?" Wang Lei's mother asked, smiling as she came forward. "You have grown taller since high school. Still so polite."

"Good evening, Auntie," Chen Xuan said warmly, handing her the cloth bag with both hands. "Just walnut powder and dates for the cold weather. Wang Lei told me you are resting better."

"Much better," Wang Lei's mother said, gently patting his wrist before waving them toward the shed. "Come inside out of the wind. Wang Lei, pour your classmate hot water."

While Wang Lei's mother checked her kettle, Chen Xuan stepped beside the wooden desk inside the shed. A thick spiral notebook lay open on the table. Even a single glance showed neat columns of fuel receipts, tire repairs, and three names marked *Paid in full, fifteenth of the month*.

At the bottom of the page, two large wholesale-market routes had been crossed out in red ink.

Wang Lei noticed Chen Xuan's gaze and rubbed the back of his neck with a wry laugh. "Big freight bosses from the east district moved into the vegetable market last month. They offered forty percent below cost to push small vans out, and they make drivers wait ninety days for settlement. My three drivers have kids in school. I cannot make them wait ninety days for rice money."

"So you let those two routes go?" Chen Xuan asked quietly.

"Had to," Wang Lei said, pouring boiling water into two chipped enamel mugs. "We still have grocery runs and hardware deliveries. It is tight, and I take the four-in-the-morning tricycle shifts myself to save fuel. As long as the three brothers get paid on time and Mom's medicine never runs short, we will hold on."

There was no self-pity in Wang Lei's voice. No hint of asking for a loan or charity. He carried the same stubborn decency from senior year, when he pressed eight yuan into a hungry classmate's palm by the stairwell and walked away before anyone could thank him.

Chen Xuan drank the hot water slowly, chatted with Wang Lei's mother for twenty minutes, and shook hands with the three drivers before heading back toward the bus stop at dusk.

* * *

Walking down the quiet gravel shoulder toward the main road, Chen Xuan took out his phone and dialed Qin Yuhan.

She answered on the second ring. "Chen Xuan? I thought you would already be at dinner."

"Just walking back from the west edge of town," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Miss Qin, I have a small question about Haiyue Hospitality."

"Go ahead," Qin Yuhan replied, attentive at once.

"Your county hotel and the reservoir guesthouse both run daily linen, toiletries, and dry-goods transfers from the main warehouse, right?" Chen Xuan asked. "How is the delivery contractor working out?"

On the other end of the line, Qin Yuhan paused for half a second. She knew Chen Xuan well enough to read every quiet word he left unsaid. He never asked favors for himself, and he never tossed cash at people he respected.

"As a matter of fact," Qin Yuhan said smoothly, a soft smile in her voice, "our procurement manager complained on Monday that the current vendor was late three times this month and mixes up clean linen with kitchen crates. We were already planning to replace them with a local short-haul team."

"I have a high-school classmate named Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said evenly. "He runs a small yard on the west edge of Yunxi County. Two micro-vans, three honest drivers, spotless cargo beds, and hand-written timestamps. He does not want charity, Miss Qin. Just a fair look if your team is actually replacing the old vendor."

"Understood," Qin Yuhan said softly. "Strict standards, fair market rate, clean contract, nothing that would embarrass him. Leave it to me, Chen Xuan."

"Thank you, Miss Qin."

Chen Xuan slipped the phone into his gray jacket pocket and boarded the evening bus as its doors hissed open.

* * *

Two days later, a silver sedan from Haiyue Hospitality turned through the faded blue iron gate. A middle-aged procurement supervisor stepped out with a leather folio, never mentioning Chen Xuan's name once. He inspected both micro-vans, checked the clean cargo floors, examined Wang Lei's logs, and interviewed all three drivers.

One hour later, seated inside the corrugated shed, the supervisor laid a crisp one-year regional linen, amenity, and dry-goods delivery contract before Wang Lei. It covered Haiyue Hospitality's Yunxi County hotel, the Qingshan Reservoir guesthouse, and two neighboring branches at full market rates, settled every fifteen days by bank transfer.

Wang Lei stared at the stamped contract for nearly a minute, his calloused fingers trembling slightly on the edge of the paper.

That evening, as Chen Xuan was rinsing rice in the kitchen of Room 601, his phone rang on the counter.

"Chen Xuan!" Wang Lei's voice came through the speaker, thick with disbelief and honest pride. "Haiyue Hospitality came to my yard today! They inspected our vans, checked our logs, and signed us for a full year! Fair market rates, paid every fifteen days! The supervisor said someone in town mentioned our team was punctual and clean!"

Chen Xuan turned off the tap and smiled softly. "They are a strict company, Wang Lei. If they signed you, it is because your vans were clean and your drivers earned it."

"We will not drop a single box," Wang Lei swore earnestly. "Not one minute late, even in snow. My three brothers are already talking about matching uniforms, and I can finally get Mom the specialist medicine without cutting corners. Chen Xuan, let me buy you dinner this weekend. A real dinner, with braised pork."

"Deal," Chen Xuan said gently. "Just make sure it costs more than eight yuan."

Across the line, Wang Lei went quiet for two seconds, then let out a warm laugh that sounded just like seven years ago on the high-school stairwell. "Not a chance I forget that, Chen Xuan. Not a chance."
