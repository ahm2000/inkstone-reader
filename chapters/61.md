# Chapter 61: National People's Congress: Stripping the Shields

At eight eighteen on Monday morning, the mahogany doors of the National People's Congress Standing Committee inner hall locked from outside. Ten directors sat around the oval table, thick cigarette smoke curling under the ceiling lamps.

Deputy Secretary-General Fan Jianguo walked in from the cross-department war room, his collar damp and a crimson-sealed folder under his arm. He dropped the folder squarely in the center of the table, rattling three porcelain cups.

"Nobody leaves until noon," Fan Jianguo said, voice hoarse from war-room smoke. "Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua have authorized Track One tribunals and the Track Two sweep in Yunxi County. Our job is to strip thirty legislative shields before the courts open their dockets."

Credential Review Committee Director Song Wenming flipped the folder open first. His breath caught.

"Thirty?" Song Wenming muttered, face draining of color. "Seven national deputies and twenty-three provincial deputies. Fan Jianguo, did Discipline Inspection verify every seal here?"

"Verified against twenty-year offshore clearing ledgers from Zurich, Singapore, and Luxembourg," Fan Jianguo replied coldly, lighting a fresh cigarette. "Shengyuan Capital did not drain over 100 trillion yuan in compounded sovereign wealth with street thugs alone. Fang Qiming bought legislative armor in fourteen provinces."

Copies passed hand to hand around the table. Within thirty seconds, the rustle of paper died into a suffocating silence broken only by burning tobacco.

Constitutional and Law Committee Director Zheng Baoguo removed his glasses and rubbed his nose, jaw working tightly. "Look at line four. Chairman of the Southern Coastal Energy Subcommittee. He sat beside me three months ago. Look at line eleven. Provincial Standing Committee Deputy Director who sponsored three mineral-statute amendments."

"Sponsored them for Vice Governor Lin Zhengde and Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang," Supervisory and Judicial Affairs Director Ma Liancheng snapped, tapping ash into his tray. "Every time our auditors tried to close rare-earth transfer loopholes, those exact deputies stood up and cited regional stability. Now we know who paid for their speeches."

Provincial Liaison and Immunity Director Wei Donghai leaned back, forehead glistening, and exhaled a long plume of smoke. "Hold on. Do not lose your heads. Under the Deputy Law, a sitting national deputy cannot face arrest or criminal trial without formal Standing Committee approval, or an emergency Chairmen's Council determination followed by ratification. Push thirty revocations through in one morning without full committee verification, and every legal scholar will call it a procedural breach."

"A procedural breach?" Ma Liancheng barked, slamming his palm onto the table. "Wei Donghai, three hundred and eighteen principals were detained before sunrise! Nine years of coastal radar schedules walked out of military vaults! You want to lecture us about committee calendars while traitors hide behind deputy immunity badges?"

"I am not protecting traitors, Ma Liancheng!" Wei Donghai shot back, neck flushing crimson as his hand trembled around his cigarette. "I am protecting the statute book! Chief Justice Wu Cheng and Procuratorate Director Pei Hong need airtight convictions that survive history, not rushed scraps signed in a fever!"

"He is right on one point," Legislative Drafting and Emergency Motions Director Deng Shuhua cut in sharply, opening her statutory manual. "One flawed citation lets defense counsel in the Track Two cases stall for months. We draft the emergency suspension and revocation motions cleanly under Article Thirty-Two and the national-security emergency clause, citing verified financial-treason evidence from Gao Zhengyi's commission."

Financial and Economic Affairs Director Qiu Jianming had not spoken since receiving his copy. His cigarette burned down to a long column of gray ash that broke across his cuff. He did not brush it away. His eyes stayed locked on page four.

"Qiu Jianming?" Deputy Qualification Audit Director Tang Shufen asked, eyes narrowing across the table. "You have stared at line nineteen for two minutes. Do you know him?"

Qiu Jianming forced a brittle smile, his teacup clinking against his trembling fingers. "Line nineteen is a vice chairman of the provincial commerce delegation. We met twice during tax-reform consultations. I am wondering whether his signature on the Shengyuan Capital coastal-port resolution was routine endorsement rather than collusion. Perhaps we hold his revocation forty-eight hours for secondary verification."

The table went dead quiet.

Record-Filing and Review Director Lu Mingyuan turned to page four, ran a finger down the margin, and looked up with icy disbelief. "Routine endorsement? Read the footnote, Qiu Jianming. Three days after he pushed that port-customs exemption through for Customs Director Xu Wenbin, 240 million yuan in Hong Kong consulting fees cleared into his brother-in-law's Singapore trust."

Qiu Jianming's forced smile vanished. Sweat broke along his hairline as he swallowed hard. "I had not reached the footnote yet, Lu Mingyuan. Naturally, if the Singapore trust is verified, strip him immediately."

"Read every footnote before asking for forty-eight hours for anyone else," Fan Jianguo said softly, pinning Qiu Jianming to his chair until he lowered his gaze. "Remember what happened at three o'clock this morning. Nobody in this building was warned before arrests began. Anyone who tries to buy a friend two days of breathing room gets added as line thirty-one before lunch."

A cold chill swept through the smoke. Two directors stubbed out half-smoked cigarettes, hunching over their pens.

"Move," Legislative Affairs Commission Director Xu Zhihao said briskly, pulling blank red-header motion forms toward him. "Deng Shuhua and I will draft the unified emergency immunity-revocation bill for the seven national deputies under sealed national-security docket authority. Song Wenming and Tang Shufen, verify every constituency credential code right now."

"Pulling the registry codes now," Song Wenming replied, fingers flying across his terminal. "What about the twenty-three provincial deputies? Half their local standing committees were emptied overnight."

"The Chairmen's Council emergency directive overrides local quorum delays," Zheng Baoguo said firmly, sliding his glasses back on. "Lu Mingyuan and I will issue binding constitutional-supervision orders directing all fourteen provincial standing committees to ratify the twenty-three revocations by eleven thirty. No lobbying and no adjournments."

"And Yunxi County?" Wei Donghai asked quietly.

"Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming and developer Deng Yongchang hold local honorary seats, not national shields," Fan Jianguo answered, checking his watch. Eight twenty-nine. "Provincial Discipline Inspection and Public Security Minister Guo Zhengming's task force strip those local seats on the spot when they roll into Yunxi in sixty seconds. Our job is stripping the thirty shields above them so nobody in a provincial capital picks up a red phone screaming immunity."

"Sign the verification sheets," Ma Liancheng said, uncapping his pen. "Strip every shield clean before noon, and let the courts do the rest."

Through the blue-gray haze, ten pens moved in grim unison, tearing apart twenty years of legislative armor on crisp white paper.
