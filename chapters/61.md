# Chapter 61: National People's Congress: Stripping the Shields

At eight eighteen on Monday morning, the heavy wooden doors of the National People's Congress Standing Committee inner hall locked from outside. Ten directors sat around the oval table. Thick cigarette smoke curled under the ceiling lamps.

Deputy Secretary-General Fan Jianguo walked in from the joint war room. His collar was damp with sweat, and he carried a red-sealed folder under his arm. He dropped the folder right in the middle of the table, making three porcelain teacups rattle.

"Nobody leaves until noon," Fan Jianguo said, his voice hoarse from war-room smoke. "Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua have approved the Track One secret trials and the Track Two sweep in Yunxi County. Our job is to strip thirty deputy protections before the courts open."

Credential Review Committee Director Song Wenming opened the folder first. His breath caught in his throat.

"Thirty?" Song Wenming muttered, all color draining from his face. "Seven national deputies and twenty-three provincial deputies. Fan Jianguo, did Discipline Inspection check every single name here?"

"Checked against twenty years of overseas bank records from Zurich, Singapore, and Luxembourg," Fan Jianguo replied coldly, lighting a fresh cigarette. "Shengyuan Capital did not steal over 100 trillion yuan in state wealth with street thugs alone. Fang Qiming bought protection from lawmakers in fourteen provinces."

Copies passed from hand to hand around the table. Within thirty seconds, the sound of turning pages faded into a heavy silence, broken only by the soft crackle of burning tobacco.

Constitutional and Law Committee Director Zheng Baoguo took off his glasses and rubbed the bridge of his nose, his jaw tight. "Look at line four. That is the Chairman of the Southern Coastal Energy Subcommittee. He sat right beside me three months ago. Look at line eleven. That Provincial Deputy Director pushed through three mining rule changes last year."

"He pushed them through for Vice Governor Lin Zhengde and Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang," Supervisory and Judicial Affairs Director Ma Liancheng snapped, tapping ash into his tray. "Every time our auditors tried to close rare-earth loopholes, those exact deputies stood up and talked about local stability. Now we know who paid for their speeches."

Provincial Liaison and Immunity Director Wei Donghai leaned back, sweat shining on his forehead. He blew out a long cloud of smoke. "Hold on. Do not lose your heads. Under the law, a sitting national deputy cannot be arrested or put on trial without formal Standing Committee approval, or an emergency Chairmen's Council order backed up later. If we strip thirty deputies in one morning without full checks, every legal expert will say we broke the rules."

"Broke the rules?" Ma Liancheng barked, slamming his palm onto the table. "Wei Donghai, three hundred and eighteen key figures were arrested before sunrise! Nine years of coastal radar schedules walked straight out of military rooms! You want to lecture us about meeting schedules while traitors hide behind deputy badges?"

"I am not protecting traitors, Ma Liancheng!" Wei Donghai shot back. His neck flushed dark red, and his hand shook around his cigarette. "I am protecting the law! Chief Justice Wu Cheng and Procuratorate Director Pei Hong need solid convictions that will stand up forever, not sloppy papers signed in a panic!"

"He is right about one thing," Legislative Drafting and Emergency Motions Director Deng Shuhua cut in sharply, opening her law book. "One bad reference lets defense lawyers in the Track Two cases stall for months. We write the emergency removal orders cleanly under Article Thirty-Two and the national-security emergency rule. We back every single one with the financial-treason proof from Gao Zhengyi's team."

Financial and Economic Affairs Director Qiu Jianming had not said a word since taking his copy. His cigarette burned down to a long stick of gray ash that broke and fell onto his sleeve. He did not brush it off. His eyes stayed glued to page four.

"Qiu Jianming?" Deputy Qualification Audit Director Tang Shufen asked, her eyes narrowing across the table. "You have stared at line nineteen for two minutes. Do you know him?"

Qiu Jianming forced a stiff smile. His teacup clinked against his shaking fingers. "Line nineteen is a vice chairman of the provincial business group. We met twice during tax-reform talks. I am wondering if his signature on the Shengyuan Capital coastal-port paper was just routine work instead of a crime. Maybe we hold his removal for forty-eight hours to double-check."

The whole table went dead quiet.

Record-Filing and Review Director Rao Liangcai flipped to page four, ran a finger down the side, and looked up with cold disbelief. "Routine work? Read the note at the bottom, Qiu Jianming. Three days after he pushed that port-customs break through for Customs Director Xu Wenbin, 240 million yuan in Hong Kong fees landed in his brother-in-law's Singapore account."

Qiu Jianming's forced smile vanished. Sweat broke out along his hairline as he swallowed hard. "I had not read the bottom note yet, Rao Liangcai. Of course, if the Singapore account is real, strip him right away."

"Read every note before you ask for forty-eight hours for anyone else," Fan Jianguo said softly, staring straight at Qiu Jianming until the man dropped his gaze. "Remember what happened at three o'clock this morning. Nobody in this building got a warning before arrests started. Anyone who tries to buy a friend two days of breathing room gets added as line thirty-one before lunch."

A cold chill swept through the smoky room. Two directors put out their half-smoked cigarettes and hunched over their pens.

"Move," Legislative Affairs Commission Director Xu Zhihao said briskly, pulling blank red-header order forms toward him. "Deng Shuhua and I will write the emergency removal bill for the seven national deputies under sealed national-security authority. Song Wenming and Tang Shufen, check every deputy ID code right now."

"Pulling the ID codes now," Song Wenming replied, his fingers flying across his keyboard. "What about the twenty-three provincial deputies? Half their local committees were emptied overnight."

"The Chairmen's Council emergency order overrides local head-count delays," Zheng Baoguo said firmly, sliding his glasses back on. "Rao Liangcai and I will send binding orders telling all fourteen provincial committees to confirm the twenty-three removals by eleven thirty. No lobbying, and no delays."

"And Yunxi County?" Wei Donghai asked quietly.

"County Magistrate Sun Weiming and developer Deng Yongchang only hold local honorary seats, not national protection," Fan Jianguo answered, checking his watch. Eight twenty-nine. "Provincial Discipline Inspection and Public Security Minister Guo Zhengming's task force will strip those local seats on the spot when they roll into Yunxi in sixty seconds. Our job is stripping the thirty shields above them so nobody in a provincial capital picks up a red phone screaming about deputy protection."

"Sign the check sheets," Ma Liancheng said, uncapping his pen. "Strip every shield clean before noon, and let the courts handle the rest."

Through the gray smoke, ten pens moved together, tearing apart twenty years of political armor on crisp white paper.
