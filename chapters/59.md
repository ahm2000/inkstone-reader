# Chapter 59: The Verdict Hall

Monday morning arrived in Yunxi County under a clean, cold sky, yet inside the main meeting hall of the county government building, the air felt heavy enough to weigh on every shoulder in the room.

More than ninety officials filled the rows of folding wooden desks. Bureau chiefs, township heads, market regulators, and street committee directors sat with notebooks open and pens held motionless above blank pages. Not a single teacup rattled. Nobody leaned over to whisper.

On the raised platform sat County Party Secretary Fan Weiguo, County Magistrate Tao Shu, and Public Security Director Mai Zhenhua. At the center microphone sat Vice Director Gao Zhengyi of the Provincial Discipline Inspection Commission, flanked by two provincial investigators in dark coats.

Gao Zhengyi opened a thick crimson-bound dossier. He did not adjust his glasses or clear his throat for effect. When he spoke, his voice carried flatly through the hall's speakers, clear and unhurried.

"The Provincial Joint Inspection Team has concluded its special audit of Yunxi County," Gao Zhengyi announced. "All findings have been verified by the Provincial Supervisory Commission, the Public Security Department, and the Customs Anti-Smuggling Bureau. I will now read the formal disposition."

Every pen in the hall froze.

"First," Gao Zhengyi said, turning a page. "Former Deputy County Magistrate Lu Wenzhe abused his office to manipulate land-clearing bids, shield coercive commercial takeovers, and accept illegal gratuities routed through third-party accounts. He is expelled from office and formally transferred to the provincial procuratorate for criminal prosecution."

A faint, collective shift of breath moved through the back rows.

"Second. Former Old Street Police Station Director Cheng Biao falsified incident logs, interfered with lawful evidence collection, and colluded with local extortion networks. He has been stripped of rank and transferred to prosecution."

Gao Zhengyi paused only long enough to turn the next sheet.

"Third. Dong Haisheng, Shen Jinbao, and Huang Boren have been formally arrested and transferred to prosecution under the joint anti-corruption and public security sweep. Charges include organized coercion, illegal detention, fraudulent seizure of commercial leases, tax evasion, and collusion in maritime smuggling channels uncovered through Shenghua Maritime."

The hall stayed dead silent. Several bureau chiefs stared straight down at their blotters, their necks stiff. Only two weeks ago, Dong Haisheng and Huang Boren had walked through the county reception rooms as untouchable investors, while Shen Jinbao had boasted openly about owning half of Old Street. Now their names were read out like entries in a ledger of condemned goods.

"Fourth," Gao Zhengyi continued, his tone sharpening by a fraction. "All eleven forced lease-transfer agreements signed by shopkeepers on Old Street over the past four months are declared permanently void. Every coerced deposit and penalty fine has been frozen from Shenghua accounts and will be returned in full by the end of the week. No agency or developer may issue eviction notices on Old Street without direct review by the county standing committee and public hearing."

In the front row, County Party Secretary Fan Weiguo exhaled slowly through his nose, his fingers resting tightly on the edge of the table. Beside him, County Magistrate Tao Shu kept his eyes fixed on his notebook, though the skin around his knuckles had gone pale.

"Let this closing notice be archived in every department," Gao Zhengyi said, closing the crimson folder with a crisp snap. "Yunxi County is a small place, yet the law is not smaller here than it is in the provincial capital. Meeting adjourned."

* * *

That afternoon, the wind outside the County Public Security Bureau carried the faint smell of coal smoke from the residential blocks. Inside the bureau auditorium, rows of uniformed officers sat straight beneath the national emblem.

At the front stood Public Security Director Mai Zhenhua, his face composed and serious. On the third row of the guest seating sat Fan Weiguo and Tao Shu, attending the commendation assembly as county leadership.

"Today we recognize an officer who remembered what his uniform stands for when it was easiest to forget," Mai Zhenhua said into the microphone. "Officer Wei Kun, step forward."

Wei Kun rose from the front row. His dark blue uniform had been ironed twice that morning by his wife, and his shoes shone under the ceiling lamps. He walked up the three wooden steps with a steady stride and saluted cleanly.

"On the morning of the Old Street disturbance," Mai Zhenhua read aloud from the red commendation certificate, "while his superior attempted to alter the official dispatch record and bury the assault report against a law-abiding shopkeeper, Officer Wei Kun refused to falsify the log. He recorded the true arrival timestamp of seven fifty in the morning, preserved the witness statements intact, and ensured the facts reached provincial investigators uncorrupted."

Applause broke out across the auditorium, hesitant at first, then swelling into a genuine, rolling wave from the junior officers in the back rows.

Mai Zhenhua stepped forward and handed Wei Kun the red certificate, followed by a crisp white appointment order bearing the bureau seal.

"By decision of the County Party Committee and the Public Security Bureau," Mai Zhenhua announced, "Wei Kun is hereby awarded a Third-Class Merit Commendation and promoted to Director of Old Street Police Station, effective immediately."

Wei Kun took the appointment order with both hands. His jaw tightened for a brief second, his eyes bright, before he gave a sharp, clean salute. "I will not fail the people of Old Street, Director Mai."

From the third row, Fan Weiguo clapped slowly, his expression deeply sober. Beside him, Tao Shu clapped in exact rhythm, his gaze fixed on the young officer standing under the bright stage lights.

Neither man spoke until the assembly began to disperse and the auditorium thinned out.

As they walked down the side corridor toward their cars, Tao Shu finally lowered his voice. "Secretary Fan. If we had hesitated one more evening when Lu Wenzhe came asking to suppress that Old Street petition, we would not be sitting in the guest row today."

Fan Weiguo stopped by the tall corridor window, looking down at the courtyard where black sedans were pulling out into the street.

"We did not survive because we were clever, Tao Shu," Fan Weiguo said quietly. "We survived because we stopped in time and signed the right side of the ledger. Remember that morning at the reservoir? Remember who Vice Director Gao Zhengyi called before he even entered the county building?"

Tao Shu's shoulders tightened slightly. He did not need to say the name aloud. Both men saw the quiet young clerk in the plain gray jacket in their minds, sitting calmly on a ten-yuan folding stool while provincial inspectors dismantled an entire power network around him.

"Keep Old Street clean," Fan Weiguo said, turning back toward the stairs. "Keep the county offices clean. If Chen Xuan never has to look our way again, we will have done our jobs."

"Understood, Secretary Fan," Tao Shu murmured.

* * *

Three blocks away, inside the logistics office of Hengxin Logistics, Chen Xuan finished entering the morning freight manifests into his spreadsheet, saved the file, and glanced at the clock on the wall.

Four fifty-five.

His phone buzzed once on the desk. It was a short text message from his cousin, Chen Hao, containing a blurry photograph of the red public notice freshly pasted outside the Old Street committee board.

*All eleven contracts voided. Second Uncle is already buying firecrackers.*

Chen Xuan smiled faintly, shut down his computer, and zipped up his plain gray jacket. He picked up his insulated lunch box, nodded politely to Hu Dewei on the way out the glass doors, and stepped into the cool late-afternoon sunlight to walk home for dinner.
