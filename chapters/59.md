# Chapter 59: Supreme Court and Supreme Procuratorate: Building the Iron Cases

At seven forty on Monday morning, the closed-door hall linking the Supreme People's Court and the Supreme People's Procuratorate looked more like a war room than a judicial chamber. Thick cigarette smoke hung beneath the gilded emblem. Ten tribunal chiefs and prosecutorial directors faced one another across green-baize desks, binders stacked beside overflowing glass ashtrays.

Supreme People's Procuratorate First Procuratorial Hall Director Pei Hong flicked his lighter twice before the flame caught, fingers stiff from sleepless hours. "Stop pretending this is a routine indictment session. Three hundred and eighteen high-level principals are in custody across fourteen provinces, including ministerial deputies, provincial trust directors, and active officers. Every one of them has lawyers and political allies waiting to tear our indictments apart on procedure the moment dockets open."

Supreme People's Court First Criminal Tribunal Chief Justice Wu Cheng leaned forward, wire-rimmed glasses catching the glare. "Then give me an evidentiary chain nobody can break, Pei Hong. Half the digital packets on my desk lack an arrest warrant number, a seizure timestamp, or an investigative unit on the cover. If defense counsel asks how we got decrypted Zurich bank keys at three in the morning, what does my bench say?"

"Your bench says nothing about three in the morning," National Security Tribunal Chief Justice Huang Zhiping interrupted sharply, tapping long gray ash into his tray. "These three hundred and eighteen principals are not facing municipal theft hearings. Under Article One Hundred and Eleven and the State Secrets statute, treason cases proceed in closed tribunal. The intelligence source is classified above judicial review."

"Classified does not mean nonexistent!" Research and Evidentiary Standards Director Tan Xuewen shot back, face flushed with sweat. "I have sat on evidentiary panels for thirty years, Huang Zhiping. Enter raw unattributed files into a Supreme Court record and you destroy the sweep's legal credibility! Every charge must rest on independently re-verified bank logs, domestic customs manifests, and lawful secondary searches."

"We already solved that," Duty-Crimes Director Jiang Tiansheng said coldly, exhaling smoke across the baize. "Check the supplemental index Gao Zhengyi sent over ten minutes ago. Every offshore ledger line maps straight to a domestic paper contract, a domestic bank wire, and a domestic tax filing still sitting in provincial vaults. We never mention how we knew which cabinet to open. Once prosecutors walk in with a lawful warrant and pull the forged appraisal themselves, the evidence is one hundred percent homegrown and ironclad."

Judicial Supervision Chief Justice Ning Shujuan lowered her pen, expression grave. "That covers the three hundred and eighteen principals in closed tribunal. What about the public cases? The State Council directive demands an open, lawful provincial enforcement sweep this morning so the public sees ordinary anti-corruption justice rather than rumors of a midnight purge."

All eyes shifted to the thin yellow folder centered on the table: Yunxi County.

Major Crimes and Anti-Gang Director Zhuang Weiguo lifted the folder and lit a cigarette, brow furrowed. "This is the strangest file of the morning. Why does a third-tier county demolition sit beside 100-trillion-yuan national treason ring?"

"Read the provincial attachment first," Counter-Terrorism and Public Security Director Kong Xiaowei said quietly, jaw tight. "Yunxi County's Old Street redevelopment fed directly into the provincial trust fraud. Worse, look at what the local actors did over the weekend. Developer Deng Yongchang colluded with Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming and County Public Security Chief Liang Guodong to force residents off Old Street. Their street enforcers, Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao, brought controlled demolition explosives and armed thugs onto a residential block, then framed an ordinary homeowner named Chen Mingde on fabricated assault and obstruction charges to lock him inside county detention."

Enforcement Bureau Chief Justice Dong Liang's eyes widened. He set his teacup down with a sharp click. "Controlled explosives used against civilians by an organized demolition gang with police cover? That crosses straight into organized gang crime and domestic counter-terrorism statutes."

"Exactly," Kong Xiaowei replied, crushing his cigarette hard enough to bend the filter. "Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu face organized-crime leadership, illegal possession of explosives, and endangering public security. Deng Yongchang faces contract fraud, bribery of state functionaries, and financing a criminal syndicate. Sun Weiming and Liang Guodong face abuse of authority, bending the law for personal gain, and shielding a criminal organization."

"And why were those five left untouched overnight?" Case-Management Director Feng Yulan asked softly, eyes narrowing through smoke at Wu Cheng and Pei Hong. "Everyone above them in the provincial finance bureau was lifted before dawn. Yet Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Cheng Biao, and Yan Tiehu are still free."

Silence settled over the desks for three heavy seconds. A faint tremor ran through Tan Xuewen's hand as he reached for his glass.

"Because whoever ran the overnight sweep drew a hard line," Pei Hong said, voice dropping an octave. "Zero Guard never appears in any public docket, any witness statement, or any provincial arrest log. Not a syllable. Not a footnote. Yunxi County goes to the provincial joint task force in broad daylight so the arrest warrants, criminal indictments, and immediate release of Chen Mingde look completely routine, lawful, and clean."

"What about Chen Mingde himself?" Ning Shujuan asked immediately, flipping open the detainee sheet. "Liang Guodong's officers threw him into holding Saturday night on a fabricated disturbance warrant. Wait for a thirty-day prosecutorial review and an innocent man rots in a cell while the county burns."

"He does not wait thirty minutes," Zhuang Weiguo snapped. "Under Article Ninety-One of the Criminal Procedure Law, the provincial procuratorate issues an immediate non-prosecution and wrongful-detention rescission order the moment the task force takes over Yunxi County Public Security Bureau. We clear Chen Mingde's record on the spot, expunge the false filing, issue a formal written apology, and send him home cleanly. No cameras on his family. No dragging relatives down to the station."

"Agreed," Wu Cheng said firmly, capping his pen and tapping the Yunxi docket twice. "Keep both tracks sealed airtight. Track One: the three hundred and eighteen principals face closed national-security and duty-crime tribunals under re-verified domestic evidence. Track Two: Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Cheng Biao, and Yan Tiehu go straight into open provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism prosecution with Chen Mingde's full exoneration signed before nine."

Pei Hong crushed his cigarette into the overflowing tray and glanced at the wall clock. Seven fifty-four.

"Six minutes until the Cross-Department War Room convenes," Pei Hong said, gathering his folders through curling smoke. "When Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua signs the Yunxi dispatch order at eight fifteen, the provincial task force rolls into Yunxi County at eight thirty sharp. Make sure every statute is iron."

Chairs scraped back across the carpet as ten grim-faced legal chiefs strode toward the main hall.
