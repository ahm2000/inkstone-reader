# Chapter 59: Supreme Court and Supreme Procuratorate: Building the Iron Cases

At seven forty on Monday morning, the closed-door hall linking the Supreme People's Court and the Supreme People's Procuratorate looked more like a battle room than a courthouse. Thick cigarette smoke hung below the gold emblem on the wall as ten court chiefs and chief prosecutors faced one another across green-cloth desks piled with binders.

Supreme People's Procuratorate First Procuratorial Hall Director Pei Hong flicked his lighter twice before the flame caught. "Stop acting like this is a normal charging meeting. Three hundred and eighteen high-level suspects are locked up across fourteen provinces, including vice ministers, provincial fund directors, and active army officers. Every one of them has sharp defense lawyers waiting to tear our charges apart over a small rule mistake."

Supreme People's Court First Criminal Tribunal Chief Justice Wu Cheng leaned forward, wire-rimmed glasses glinting. "Then hand me an unbreakable chain of proof, Pei Hong. Half these digital files lack an arrest warrant number, seizure time, or agency name. If defense lawyers ask how we unlocked Zurich bank codes at three in the morning, what does my court say?"

"Your court says nothing about three in the morning," National Security Tribunal Chief Justice Huang Zhiping cut in sharply. "These three hundred and eighteen suspects are not facing petty town theft trials. Under the Criminal Procedure Law, cases involving state secrets are heard in closed court. The source of the tip is secret and above court review."

"Secret does not mean made up!" Research and Evidence Standards Director Tan Xuewen shot back, face flushed with sweat. "I have sat on proof-review boards for thirty years, Huang Zhiping. Put unsourced files into a Supreme Court record and you ruin the legal standing of the whole sweep! Every charge must stand on freshly checked domestic bank records, port shipping lists, and lawful new searches."

"We already solved that," Official-Crime Director Jiang Tiansheng said coldly, blowing smoke across the desk. "Look at the guide Gao Zhengyi sent ten minutes ago. Every overseas bank line matches a local paper contract, bank transfer, and tax record still sitting inside provincial filing rooms. We never mention how we knew which cabinet to open. Once prosecutors walk in with a legal warrant and pull out the fake land paper themselves, the proof is one hundred percent homegrown and rock-solid."

Judicial Supervision Chief Justice Ning Shujuan set her pen down, face serious. "That covers the three hundred and eighteen big suspects in closed court. What about the public cases? The State Council order demands an open, lawful provincial sweep this morning so ordinary people see normal anti-corruption justice instead of rumors about a midnight purge."

Every eye shifted to the thin yellow folder in the center of the table. Yunxi County.

Major Crimes and Anti-Gang Director Zhuang Weiguo picked it up and lit a cigarette, brow furrowed. "This is the strangest file of the morning. Why is a small county demolition fight sitting right beside a 1.2 trillion yuan national treason ring?"

"Read the provincial report first," Counter-Terrorism and Public Security Director Kong Xiaowei said quietly, jaw tight. "Yunxi County's Old Street rebuilding deal fed straight into the provincial loan scam. Worse, developer Deng Yongchang teamed up with County Magistrate Sun Weiming and County Public Security Chief Liang Guodong to force families off Old Street. Their street thugs, Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao, brought demolition explosives and armed men onto a home street, then framed an ordinary homeowner named Chen Mingde on fake assault and blocking-police charges to lock him in jail."

Enforcement Bureau Chief Justice Dong Liang's eyes went wide. "Demolition explosives used against ordinary families by an organized gang with police protection? That crosses straight into organized gang crime and domestic counter-terrorism laws."

"Exactly," Kong Xiaowei replied, crushing his cigarette. "Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu face charges for leading an organized gang, holding illegal explosives, and endangering public safety. Deng Yongchang faces contract fraud, bribery, and funding a criminal gang. Sun Weiming and Liang Guodong face abuse of power, twisting the law, and shielding a gang."

"And why were those five left untouched overnight?" Case-Management Director Feng Yulan asked softly, eyes narrowing at Wu Cheng and Pei Hong. "Everyone above them in the provincial finance office was grabbed before dawn, yet those five are still walking free."

Heavy silence settled over the desks. Tan Xuewen's hand shook slightly as he reached for his water glass.

"Because whoever ran the overnight sweep drew a hard line," Pei Hong said, voice dropping low. "Black Guard never shows up in any public case file, witness statement, or provincial arrest record. Not one word. Yunxi County goes to the provincial joint task force in broad daylight so the warrants, charges, and immediate release of Chen Mingde look completely normal, lawful, and clean."

"What about Chen Mingde himself?" Ning Shujuan asked at once, opening the prisoner sheet. "Liang Guodong's officers threw him into a cell Friday night on a fake disturbance order. If we wait for a thirty-day review, an innocent man sits in jail while the county burns."

"He does not wait thirty minutes," Zhuang Weiguo snapped. "The provincial prosecutors sign a written release decision the second the task force takes over Yunxi County Public Security Bureau. We clear Chen Mingde's name on the spot, wipe the fake report off the books, hand him a formal written apology, and send him home cleanly. No cameras on his family, and no dragging his relatives to the station."

"Agreed," Wu Cheng said firmly, capping his pen and tapping the Yunxi file. "Keep both tracks sealed tight. Track One: the three hundred and eighteen big suspects face closed national-security and official-crime courts backed by freshly checked domestic proof. Track Two: Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Cheng Biao, and Yan Tiehu go into open provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism trials, with Chen Mingde's cleared-name paper signed before nine o'clock."

Pei Hong crushed out his cigarette and glanced at the wall clock. Seven fifty-four.

"Six minutes until the Cross-Department War Room opens," Pei Hong said, gathering his folders. "When Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua signs the Yunxi order at eight fourteen, the provincial task force rolls into Yunxi County at eight twenty. Make sure every charge is airtight."

Chairs scraped back as ten grim-faced legal chiefs strode toward the main hall.
