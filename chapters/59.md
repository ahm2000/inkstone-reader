# Chapter 59: Supreme Court and Supreme Procuratorate: Building the Iron Cases

At seven forty on Monday morning, the closed-door hall linking the Supreme People's Court and the Supreme People's Procuratorate looked more like a battle room than a courthouse. Thick cigarette smoke hung below the gold emblem on the wall. Ten court chiefs and chief prosecutors sat facing one another across green-cloth desks, thick binders piled beside overflowing glass ashtrays.

Supreme People's Procuratorate First Procuratorial Hall Director Pei Hong flicked his lighter twice before the flame caught, his fingers stiff from a sleepless night. "Stop acting like this is a normal charging meeting. Three hundred and eighteen high-level suspects are locked up across fourteen provinces, including vice ministers, provincial fund directors, and active army officers. Every single one of them has sharp defense lawyers and powerful friends waiting to tear our charges apart over a small rule mistake the second these cases open."

Supreme People's Court First Criminal Tribunal Chief Justice Wu Cheng leaned forward, his wire-rimmed glasses catching the overhead light. "Then hand me a chain of proof nobody can break, Pei Hong. Half the digital files on my desk do not have an arrest warrant number, a time of seizure, or an agency name on the cover. If defense lawyers ask how we got unlocked Zurich bank codes at three in the morning, what is my court supposed to say?"

"Your court says nothing at all about three in the morning," National Security Tribunal Chief Justice Huang Zhiping cut in sharply, tapping long gray ash into his tray. "These three hundred and eighteen suspects are not facing petty town theft trials. Under Article One Hundred and Eleven and the State Secrets Law, treason cases go straight to a closed court. Where the tip came from is secret and above court review."

"Secret does not mean made up!" Research and Evidence Standards Director Tan Xuewen shot back, his face flushed with sweat. "I have sat on proof-review boards for thirty years, Huang Zhiping. Put unsourced files into a Supreme Court record and you ruin the legal standing of the whole sweep! Every single charge has to stand on freshly checked domestic bank records, local port shipping lists, and lawful new searches."

"We already solved that," Official-Crime Director Jiang Tiansheng said coldly, blowing smoke across the desk. "Look at the extra guide Gao Zhengyi sent over ten minutes ago. Every overseas bank line matches straight to a local paper contract, a local bank transfer, and a local tax record still sitting inside provincial filing rooms. We never mention how we knew which cabinet to open. Once prosecutors walk in with a legal search warrant and pull out the fake land paper themselves, the proof is one hundred percent homegrown and rock-solid."

Judicial Supervision Chief Justice Ning Shujuan set her pen down, her face serious. "That covers the three hundred and eighteen big suspects in closed court. What about the public cases? The State Council order demands an open, lawful provincial sweep this morning so ordinary people see normal anti-corruption justice instead of rumors about a midnight purge."

Every eye shifted to the thin yellow folder sitting right in the center of the table. Yunxi County.

Major Crimes and Anti-Gang Director Zhuang Weiguo picked up the folder and lit a cigarette, his brow furrowed. "This is the strangest file of the morning. Why is a small county demolition fight sitting right beside a 100 trillion yuan national treason ring?"

"Read the provincial report first," Counter-Terrorism and Public Security Director Kong Xiaowei said quietly, his jaw tight. "Yunxi County's Old Street rebuilding deal fed straight into the provincial loan scam. Worse, look at what the local men did over the weekend. Developer Deng Yongchang teamed up with County Magistrate Sun Weiming and County Public Security Chief Liang Guodong to force families off Old Street. Their street thugs, Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao, brought demolition explosives and armed men onto a home street, then framed an ordinary homeowner named Chen Mingde on fake assault and blocking-police charges just to lock him in the county jail."

Enforcement Bureau Chief Justice Dong Liang's eyes went wide. He set his teacup down with a sharp click. "Demolition explosives used against ordinary families by an organized gang with police protection? That crosses straight into organized gang crime and domestic counter-terrorism laws."

"Exactly," Kong Xiaowei replied, crushing his cigarette hard enough to bend the filter. "Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu face charges for leading an organized gang, holding illegal explosives, and putting public safety in danger. Deng Yongchang faces contract fraud, bribing officials, and paying for a criminal gang. Sun Weiming and Liang Guodong face abuse of power, twisting the law for personal gain, and shielding a criminal gang."

"And why were those five left untouched overnight?" Case-Management Director Feng Yulan asked softly, her eyes narrowing through the smoke at Wu Cheng and Pei Hong. "Everyone above them in the provincial finance office was grabbed before dawn. Yet Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Cheng Biao, and Yan Tiehu are still walking free."

Heavy silence settled over the desks for three long seconds. A slight shake ran through Tan Xuewen's hand as he reached for his water glass.

"Because whoever ran the overnight sweep drew a hard line," Pei Hong said, his voice dropping low. "Zero Guard never shows up in any public case file, any witness statement, or any provincial arrest record. Not one word. Not one note. Yunxi County goes to the provincial joint task force in broad daylight so the arrest warrants, criminal charges, and immediate release of Chen Mingde look completely normal, lawful, and clean."

"What about Chen Mingde himself?" Ning Shujuan asked at once, flipping open the prisoner sheet. "Liang Guodong's officers threw him into a cell Friday night on a fake disturbance order. If we wait for a thirty-day legal review, an innocent man sits in jail while the county burns."

"He does not wait thirty minutes," Zhuang Weiguo snapped. "Under Article Ninety-One of the Criminal Procedure Law, the provincial prosecutors issue an immediate release and dropped-charges order the second the task force takes over Yunxi County Public Security Bureau. We clear Chen Mingde's name on the spot, wipe the fake report off the books, hand him a formal written apology, and send him home cleanly. No cameras on his family. No dragging his relatives down to the police station."

"Agreed," Wu Cheng said firmly, capping his pen and tapping the Yunxi file twice. "Keep both tracks sealed tight. Track One: the three hundred and eighteen big suspects face closed national-security and official-crime courts backed by freshly checked domestic proof. Track Two: Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Cheng Biao, and Yan Tiehu go straight into open provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism trials, with Chen Mingde's cleared-name paper signed before nine o'clock."

Pei Hong crushed his cigarette into the overflowing tray and glanced up at the wall clock. Seven fifty-four.

"Six minutes until the Cross-Department War Room opens," Pei Hong said, gathering his folders through the curling smoke. "When Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua signs the Yunxi order at eight fifteen, the provincial task force rolls into Yunxi County at eight thirty sharp. Make sure every single charge is airtight."

Chairs scraped back across the carpet as ten grim-faced legal chiefs strode toward the main hall.
