# Chapter 91: The Ones Who Stepped In

Shortly after 9:00 PM, Liu Meifang shook an empty glass bottle over the kitchen counter in Fuxing New Village and clicked her tongue.

"Xuan, did you finish the dark soy sauce at dinner?" she called out, tilting the bottle toward the bright ceiling light. "If I do not soak the pork belly tonight, it will not have any flavor tomorrow morning."

Chen Xuan set down his teacup on the dining table, stood up, and zipped his light grey jacket. "Red-label dark soy sauce. Anything else, Mom?"

"Just the big bottle from Auntie Zhou's corner shop," Liu Meifang said, wiping her hands on her apron. "Put on your scarf. The autumn wind drops fast after dark."

Chen Xuan pocketed his single smartphone, slipped his keys into his jacket, and headed down the six flights of stairs.

Outside Building 2, the night air was crisp and clean. Earlier in the day, he had felt the quiet, distant presence of the Black Guard keeping watch around the neighborhood. Now, with the 9:00 PM withdrawal order completed across Beijing and Handong, every last Black Guard unit had pulled out cleanly. The lane belonged entirely to the evening breeze and the warm glow of yellow streetlamps.

Yet as Chen Xuan walked toward the corner convenience store, the quiet residential street felt surprisingly lively for a cool Monday night.

Beneath the tall plane tree by the gate, two middle-aged uncles in padded vests sat over a folding chess table, arguing good-naturedly over a rook move while a third uncle leaned on his bicycle handlebar and laughed at both of them. A few steps ahead, two college students in sneakers and backpacks strolled past with cups of warm milk tea, joking about a difficult math exam. Outside the fruit stall, a delivery rider sat on his electric scooter humming a pop tune while peeling a tangerine.

Nobody wore a mask. Nobody stood stiffly in dark clothes or scanned the rooftops with sharp, watchful eyes. They looked and sounded exactly like the neighbors who had lived in Yunxi County for twenty years.

Chen Xuan bought the big red-label bottle of dark soy sauce from Auntie Zhou, paid with a quick scan on his phone, and stepped back out onto the brick sidewalk.

Right beside the glass door, a cheerful young man around Chen Xuan's own age in a faded grey hoodie was holding a warm plastic bag of steamed pork buns. He glanced over, broke into an easy, relaxed grin like an old classmate running into a friend, and fell into step beside Chen Xuan.

"Hey, big brother," the young man said in a low, friendly voice that carried zero stiffness or pretense. "Do not look too surprised, just keep walking like we are chatting about the weather."

Chen Xuan slowed half a step, blinking in mild curiosity. "Do I know you?"

"Zero Guard," the young man murmured with a lighthearted smile, taking a casual bite of a warm bun. "Batch Seven. We activated automatically ten minutes ago when the Black Guard pulled out."

Chen Xuan stopped dead on the sidewalk, the soy sauce bottle nearly tilting in his hand. Genuine astonishment flickered across his calm eyes.

"Wait," Chen Xuan said softly, looking closely at the relaxed young man in the faded hoodie. "Zero Guard? I never gave an activation order."

"You did not have to," the young man chuckled easily, gesturing loosely with his paper bag as they resumed walking down the lane. "The old standing protocol triggered on its own. Tonight's sweep crossed into foreign financial channels in Zurich and touched the highest levels in Beijing. Once the Black Guard withdrew, the automatic threshold tripped, so our batch stepped in."

Chen Xuan looked around the quiet street with fresh eyes.

As they passed the fruit stall, a round-faced auntie carrying a woven basket of fresh green onions smiled warmly at Chen Xuan. "Young man, zip your collar up properly," she scolded gently like a real neighborhood elder. "Autumn wind gets right into the throat at night."

"Thank you, Auntie, I will," Chen Xuan replied automatically, half amused and half stunned.

He looked back at the two uncles arguing over chess, the college students laughing over milk tea, the office clerk strolling home with a briefcase, and the delivery rider tapping his foot to music.

"How many of you are out here?" Chen Xuan asked quietly.

"A few hundred folded into the city and surrounding blocks," the young man said cheerfully, talking as casually as a neighbor sharing local gossip. "Uncles, aunties, students, shop clerks, drivers. Not just around you, either. Your parents, Second Uncle's shop, your cousins, and anyone close to you. We stay on 24/7 permanently unless you personally tell us to stand down."

Chen Xuan let out a quiet breath, shaking his head with a helpless smile. "No wonder nobody looked tense. You really do not act like bodyguards."

"Why act stiff and scare the neighbors?" the young man grinned. "We are supposed to live normal lives around you, not crouch in bushes with earpieces. Go on home before your mom wonders where her soy sauce went. See you around, brother."

With a casual wave, the young man crossed the street toward the bus stop, munching his second steamed bun.

Two blocks away inside a quiet noodle shop, a White Guard spotter sitting by the window frowned at his tablet screen. Across the intersection, a departing Black Guard scout paused briefly by a parked sedan, checking the exact same street camera feed on his handheld display.

Both of them had noticed the sudden, lively mix of friendly uncles, aunties, students, and clerks drifting near Fuxing New Village. Quietly and independently, both ran full background checks on six different people from that lane.

Every single profile came back spotless. Real birth records, real neighborhood addresses, real tax filings, real school IDs, and completely logical daily routines going back years.

The White Guard spotter rubbed his chin with a faint flicker of doubt, found zero proof of anything unusual, and closed his tablet. Down the block, the Black Guard scout stared at the clean screens for three silent seconds, shook his head, and drove away into the night.

Neither side had the slightest clue that the Zero Guard had already taken over the street right under their noses.
