# Chapter 91: One Team That Did Not Leave

At nine fifteen on Monday night, Liu Meifang shook an empty glass bottle over the kitchen counter in Fuxing New Village and clicked her tongue.

"Chen Xuan, did you finish the dark soy sauce last night?" Liu Meifang asked, tilting the bottle toward the ceiling bulb. "I wanted to simmer the pork belly before you leave tomorrow."

Chen Jianguo looked up from the folding table where he was stacking receipts. "Leave it until morning. The corner store shuts at nine thirty."

"Nine thirty is fifteen minutes away," Liu Meifang said, wiping her hands on her apron. "If I do not marinate the meat tonight, it will taste flat by dawn. Chen Xuan, put on your jacket and run down the lane. Get the red-label bottle, not the sweet one."

Chen Xuan stood up from his stool and zipped his dark gray jacket. "Red-label dark soy sauce. Anything else?"

"A small packet of coarse salt," Liu Meifang called out as he reached the door. "Come straight back."

"Ten minutes," Chen Xuan said, stepping into the stairwell.

The lane outside Fuxing New Village was quiet under two yellowing streetlamps. Chen Xuan walked at an unhurried pace with his hands in his pockets. Halfway to the corner store, the secondary phone in his inner pocket vibrated twice with the short, urgent pulse reserved for perimeter alerts.

Chen Xuan slowed his stride, took out the matte-black handset, and answered as he turned the bend.

"Speak," Chen Xuan said quietly.

On the other end, Number 7's breathing was tight and uneven, far from his usual clipped composure. "It is nine sixteen. I have an anomaly in Yunxi County."

"Go ahead."

"At nine o'clock sharp, every Zero Guard sentry nationwide withdrew simultaneously," Number 7 said, his voice dropping to a strained murmur. "Capital rings, provincial compounds, Lanshui airports, Qingyan arterial roads. Every single unit cleared our passive grids within ninety seconds. Number 1 confirmed nationwide withdrawal three minutes ago."

Chen Xuan kept walking toward the corner store's yellow awning. "That was expected."

"There is a problem," Number 7 said. A faint metallic tap sounded over the line, as if his knuckles were striking a console. "Black Pine's passive relays around Fuxing New Village never cleared. Two heat signatures remain active inside your fifty-meter perimeter."

Chen Xuan stopped three paces short of the corner store doorway. "Identify them."

"Zero Guard," Number 7 said, the two words rigid with quiet alarm. "Same thermal dampening profile, same unrecorded tactical spacing, zero radio emissions. They are standing motionless forty meters behind you, near the locust tree at the bend of the lane. There was no standing order. Number 1 sent three dead-drop queries and got silence. Even Number 1 has no authority over them."

A tense pause held on the line. Number 7 exhaled slowly, fighting to steady his cadence.

"They are forty meters from you with zero explanation and zero chain of command," Number 7 pressed, alarm leaking through his discipline. "Give the word and I will move a Black Pine containment team into Yunxi County within twenty minutes to put a hard buffer between them and your building."

"Hold the line," Chen Xuan said calmly.

He lowered the phone and stepped into the corner store. The sleepy shopkeeper blinked from behind the counter.

"Late night, young man," the shopkeeper murmured. "Just pulling the shutter."

"One bottle of red-label dark soy sauce and a small bag of coarse salt," Chen Xuan said, setting a twenty-yuan bill on the glass.

"Heading back to Qingyan tomorrow?" the shopkeeper asked, handing over the plastic bag and loose change.

"Early bus," Chen Xuan replied. "Thank you."

He stepped back onto the lane, raised the phone to his ear, and looked down the bend he had just walked past.

Forty meters away beneath the locust tree stood two tall figures in long dark coats. Their collars were raised, their masks featureless in the dim light, their posture completely still. Neither shifted as a distant scooter rattled past two blocks away. Neither glanced toward the apartment windows. They faced outward, watching the lane approaches with cold, unblinking stillness.

Chen Xuan watched them for three seconds, the soy sauce bottle hanging loosely from his left hand.

"Are you still there?" Number 7 asked, his voice sharper now. "Our south relay confirms they have not moved a single centimeter since nine o'clock. Without an order, we cannot guarantee their intent."

"Leave them alone," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Do not interfere."

Number 7 drew a sharp breath. "You want us to leave an unrecorded Zero Guard team standing forty meters from your family's apartment?"

"Yes."

"Number 1 will demand a risk assessment," Number 7 said, his tone tightening as discipline wrestled with unease. "We have no liaison channel and no rules of engagement. If a neighbor walks past them, or if a county patrol car turns down that lane, we cannot signal them to stand down."

"You do not need to signal them," Chen Xuan replied, starting back toward Fuxing New Village at the same measured pace. "They withdrew everywhere else at nine because their mandate ended. Those two stayed by their own decision."

"By their own decision?" Number 7 echoed, disbelief cracking his flat voice. "Zero Guard does not improvise."

"Then stop trying to read them through Black Pine's ledger," Chen Xuan said, his voice carrying no anger, only quiet finality. "Pull your passive relays outside two hundred meters. Do not deploy a containment unit. Do not attempt contact. Tell Number 1 to stop sending queries."

Silence stretched for four full seconds. Chen Xuan could almost hear Number 7's jaw locking as he forced himself to accept the boundary.

"Understood," Number 7 said tightly. "Passive observation outside two hundred meters. No interference. Should I log them in your standing security file?"

"Do not log them at all," Chen Xuan said. "If they wanted to be on paper, they would carry real numbers."

"Understood," Number 7 replied, his breath easing slightly. "Your uncle's hardware shop is quiet. Number 23 confirmed the street is clear."

"Good. Get some rest."

Chen Xuan ended the call and slipped the handset back into his inner pocket.

As he neared the locust tree, an electric scooter turned into the lane, its headlight sweeping across the damp bricks. Before the beam reached the trunk, the two dark-coated figures shifted half a step into a recessed storage doorway. The motion was so silent and smooth that the rider rolled straight past without turning his head.

Chen Xuan walked past the locust tree without slowing, carrying his soy sauce and salt back to the stairwell.

When he opened the apartment door, Liu Meifang took the bag and checked the red label with a satisfied nod. "Good. Wash your hands. Your father wants to walk over to Mingde Hardware once the ribs are simmering so we can help your uncle finish stacking the shelves."

"I am ready whenever he is," Chen Xuan said, hanging his jacket neatly by the door.
