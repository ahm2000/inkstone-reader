# Chapter 91: One Team That Did Not Leave

Later that evening in Fuxing New Village, the warm yellow light of the kitchen bulb shone over a wooden counter lined with bowls, scallions, and a thick slab of fresh pork belly. Liu Meifang held an empty glass bottle up to the light, shook it twice, and clicked her tongue.

"Chen Xuan, did you use the last of the dark soy sauce yesterday?" Liu Meifang asked, tapping the rim against her palm. "I wanted to marinate the pork tonight so I can simmer it early before your flight tomorrow."

At the folding table by the window, Chen Jianguo looked up from neatly stacking utility receipts. "Leave it until morning. Uncle Wu closes the corner store at 9:30 PM."

"That still gives him plenty of time," Liu Meifang said, wiping her hands on her cotton apron. "If the meat does not soak overnight, the flavor stays thin. Chen Xuan, grab your jacket and run down the lane. Make sure you get the red-label dark soy sauce, not the sweet cooking kind."

Chen Xuan stood up from his stool and reached for his worn dark gray jacket hanging behind the door. "Red-label dark soy sauce. Do you need anything else, Mom?"

"A small bag of coarse salt," Liu Meifang called as he laced his sneakers. "And tell Uncle Wu not to give you the damp bag from the bottom shelf."

"Got it." Chen Xuan tucked a twenty-yuan note into his pocket, opened the apartment door, and headed down the concrete stairwell.

The night air in Fuxing New Village felt crisp and clean. Earlier in the week, a heavy tension had hung over every alley in Yunxi County, keeping shutters drawn and voices low. Tonight, the neighborhood felt like itself again. Television dramas murmured behind lit windows, laundry swayed gently on upstairs balconies, and the sweet smell of roasted sweet potatoes drifted from the main lane.

Uncle Wu's tiny corner store still had its warm fluorescent tube glowing over the narrow doorway. The old shopkeeper sat behind the glass counter fanning himself with a folded newspaper, smiling as Chen Xuan stepped inside.

"Out late, Chen Xuan?" Uncle Wu asked warmly, reaching up to the top shelf before Chen Xuan even finished asking. "Your mother needs the red-label soy sauce again, right? She always cooks pork belly the night before you head back to Shanghai."

"You know her better than I do, Uncle Wu," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile. "One bottle of the red label and a dry bag of coarse salt."

Uncle Wu bagged both items carefully and counted out the change. "Tell your uncle Mingde I am glad his hardware shop is busy again. Everything feels lighter tonight. Even the patrol cars on the main road look friendlier."

"I will tell him," Chen Xuan said. "Good night, Uncle Wu."

He stepped back out into the narrow brick lane, swinging the light plastic bag at his side. The streetlamp at the mouth of the alley hummed softly, casting long pools of amber light over the damp flagstones.

Halfway back toward his parents' building, near the old elm tree where the lane curved toward the courtyard gate, Chen Xuan slowed his pace.

Nothing looked out of place to an ordinary eye. A parked bicycle leaned against the brick wall. A stray orange cat sat grooming its paw on top of a concrete meter box.

Yet Chen Xuan's senses caught a subtle break in the night rhythm. Fifty meters behind him, where the shadow of the outer wall met the mouth of the alley, the air held a familiar, disciplined stillness. Two faint silhouettes blended seamlessly into the dark angle between the brick pillars.

Plain black gear. Plain black masks. No insignia, no numbers, no sound at all.

Chen Xuan stopped for a brief heartbeat beneath the elm tree, pretending to adjust the strap of the plastic bag around his fingers.

The central stand-down order had gone out at 9:00 PM. Every official Black Guard unit across Beijing and Handong had withdrawn on the exact second.

Yet this single team had not left.

They were off the books, unrecorded on any dispatch log, staying quietly at a respectful 50-meter perimeter around Fuxing New Village. They did not step closer to the apartment stairwell, did not intrude on the family's evening, and did not seek acknowledgment. They simply remained on watch so that Chen Xuan's parents could sleep without a shadow touching their door.

A quiet warmth settled in Chen Xuan's chest. Even without an order from him, they understood what mattered most to him. Not titles, not power, not fear in the capital, just the quiet safety of an ordinary apartment where his mother worried about marinating pork belly.

Chen Xuan did not call out. He did not walk back or break their cover. He turned his head just slightly, letting his gaze rest for two calm seconds on the dark bend 50 meters back, and gave one small, steady nod.

From the deep shadow by the alley wall, a single black-masked figure inclined its head a fraction of an inch in silent answer. Then the silhouettes melted back into the stillness, completely invisible once more.

Chen Xuan smiled faintly, shifted the soy sauce bottle in his hand, and walked up the stairs of his building.

When he pushed open the apartment door, the warm scent of ginger, star anise, and boiling water greeted him at once. Liu Meifang took the bag from his hand and checked the red label with a satisfied nod.

"Good, you made it before he locked up," she said brightly. "Go wash your hands. Your father wants to walk over to Old Street and check on your uncle's shop before bed."

"Coming, Mom," Chen Xuan said, hanging his jacket back on the hook as ordinary family laughter filled the little kitchen.
