# Chapter 91: One Team That Did Not Leave

At nine-fifteen on Monday night, Liu Meifang shook an empty glass bottle over the kitchen counter in Fuxing New Village and clicked her tongue.

"Chen Xuan, did you finish the dark soy sauce last night?" Liu Meifang asked, tilting the bottle toward the ceiling bulb. "I wanted to slow-cook the pork belly before you leave tomorrow."

Chen Jianguo looked up from the folding table where he was stacking receipts. "Leave it until morning. The corner store closes at nine-thirty."

"Nine-thirty is fifteen minutes away," Liu Meifang said, wiping her hands on her apron. "If I do not soak the meat tonight, it will have no flavor by morning. Chen Xuan, put on your jacket and run down the lane. Get the red-label bottle, not the sweet kind."

Chen Xuan stood up from his stool and zipped his dark gray jacket. "Red-label dark soy sauce. Anything else?"

"A small bag of coarse salt," Liu Meifang called out as he reached the door. "Come straight back."

"Ten minutes," Chen Xuan said, stepping out into the stairwell.

The lane outside Fuxing New Village was quiet under two pale yellow streetlamps. Chen Xuan walked at an easy pace with his hands in his pockets. Halfway to the corner store, the second phone in his inner pocket buzzed twice. It was the short, sharp buzz used only for security alerts.

Chen Xuan slowed his steps, pulled out the plain black phone, and answered as he turned the corner.

"Speak," Chen Xuan said quietly.

On the other end, Number 7's breathing sounded tight and uneven. He had lost his usual calm tone. "It is nine-sixteen. We have a problem in Yunxi County."

"Go ahead."

"At nine o'clock sharp, every Black Guard watcher across the country pulled out at the same time," Number 7 said, his voice dropping to a tense murmur. "The capital rings, provincial offices, Sha City airports, and Shanghai's main roads. Every single unit vanished from our sensors within ninety seconds. Number 1 confirmed the full pullout three minutes ago."

Chen Xuan kept walking toward the corner store's yellow sign. "That was expected."

"There is an issue," Number 7 said. A faint metal tap sounded over the line, as if his knuckles were hitting a desk. "White Guard's sensors around Fuxing New Village never cleared. Two heat readings are still active within fifty meters of you."

Chen Xuan stopped three steps short of the corner store door. "Who are they?"

"Black Guard," Number 7 said, the two words stiff with quiet alarm. "Same hidden heat pattern, same strange spacing, no radio signals at all. They are standing still forty meters behind you, near the locust tree at the bend in the lane. There was no order for them to stay. Number 1 sent three secret check-in messages and got complete silence. Even Number 1 has no power over them."

A tense pause held on the line. Number 7 let out a slow breath, fighting to keep his voice steady.

"They are forty meters from you with no reason and no chain of command," Number 7 pressed, worry breaking through his training. "Give the word and I will move a White Guard guard team into Yunxi County in twenty minutes. We will put a solid wall between them and your building."

"Hold the line," Chen Xuan said calmly.

He lowered the phone and walked into the corner store. The sleepy shopkeeper blinked from behind the counter.

"Late night, young man," the shopkeeper murmured. "I was just closing the shutter."

"One bottle of red-label dark soy sauce and a small bag of coarse salt," Chen Xuan said, setting a twenty-yuan bill on the glass counter.

"Heading back to Shanghai tomorrow?" the shopkeeper asked, handing over the plastic bag and loose change.

"Early bus," Chen Xuan replied. "Thank you."

He stepped back into the lane, raised the phone to his ear, and looked down the bend he had just passed.

Forty meters away under the locust tree stood two tall figures in long dark coats. Their collars were turned up, their masks blank in the dim light, and their bodies completely still. Neither moved as a distant scooter rattled past two blocks away. Neither glanced toward the apartment windows. They faced outward, watching the lane with cold, silent focus.

Chen Xuan looked at them for three seconds, the soy sauce bottle hanging lightly from his left hand.

"Are you still there?" Number 7 asked, his voice sharper now. "Our south sensor confirms they have not moved a single inch since nine o'clock. Without an order, we cannot be sure what they plan to do."

"Leave them alone," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Do not get in their way."

Number 7 drew a sharp breath. "You want us to leave an unlisted Black Guard team standing forty meters from your family's apartment?"

"Yes."

"Number 1 will demand a risk report," Number 7 said, his voice tight as duty clashed with unease. "We have no way to talk to them and no rules for dealing with them. If a neighbor walks past them, or if a county police car turns down that lane, we cannot tell them to stand down."

"You do not need to tell them anything," Chen Xuan replied, starting back toward Fuxing New Village at the same steady pace. "They left everywhere else at nine because their job ended. Those two stayed by their own choice."

"By their own choice?" Number 7 repeated, shock cracking his flat voice. "Black Guard does not act on its own."

"Then stop trying to judge them by White Guard's rules," Chen Xuan said. His voice held no anger, only quiet firmness. "Pull your sensors outside two hundred meters. Do not send a guard team. Do not try to contact them. Tell Number 1 to stop sending messages."

Silence stretched for four full seconds. Chen Xuan could almost hear Number 7 clenching his jaw as he forced himself to accept the order.

"Understood," Number 7 said tightly. "Watch from outside two hundred meters. No interference. Should I record them in your regular security file?"

"Do not write them down at all," Chen Xuan said. "If they wanted to be on paper, they would carry real numbers."

"Understood," Number 7 replied, his breath easing a little. "Your uncle's hardware shop is quiet. Number 23 confirmed the street is clear."

"Good. Get some rest."

Chen Xuan ended the call and slipped the phone back into his inner pocket.

As he neared the locust tree, an electric scooter turned into the lane, its headlight sweeping across the damp bricks. Before the light reached the tree trunk, the two dark-coated figures shifted half a step into a deep storage doorway. They moved so smoothly and silently that the rider rolled straight past without turning his head.

Chen Xuan walked past the locust tree without slowing, carrying his soy sauce and salt back to the stairwell.

When he opened the apartment door, Liu Meifang took the bag and checked the red label with a pleased nod. "Good. Wash your hands. Your father wants to walk over to Mingde Hardware once the ribs are simmering so we can help your uncle finish stacking the shelves."

"I am ready whenever he is," Chen Xuan said, hanging his jacket neatly by the door.
