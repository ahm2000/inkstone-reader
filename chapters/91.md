# Chapter 91: One Team That Did Not Leave

At nine-fifteen on Monday night, Liu Meifang shook an empty glass bottle over the kitchen counter in Fuxing New Village and clicked her tongue.

"Chen Xuan, did you finish the dark soy sauce last night?" Liu Meifang asked, tilting the bottle toward the bulb. "I wanted to slow-cook the pork belly before you leave tomorrow."

Chen Jianguo looked up from stacking receipts at the folding table. "Leave it until morning. The corner store closes at nine-thirty."

"Nine-thirty is fifteen minutes away," Liu Meifang said, wiping her hands on her apron. "If I do not soak the meat tonight, it will have no flavor by morning. Chen Xuan, put on your jacket and run down the lane. Get the red-label bottle, not the sweet kind."

Chen Xuan stood up and zipped his dark gray jacket. "Red-label dark soy sauce. Anything else?"

"A small bag of coarse salt," Liu Meifang called out as he reached the door. "Come straight back."

"Ten minutes," Chen Xuan said, stepping into the stairwell.

The lane outside Fuxing New Village lay quiet under two yellow streetlamps. Chen Xuan walked at an easy pace, hands in his pockets. Halfway to the corner store, the second phone in his inner pocket buzzed twice, the short, sharp alert used only for security notices.

Chen Xuan slowed, pulled out the plain black phone, and answered as he turned the corner.

"Speak," Chen Xuan said quietly.

On the other end, Number 7's breathing sounded tight and uneven, his usual calm gone. "It is nine-sixteen. We have a problem in Yunxi County."

"Go ahead."

"At nine o'clock sharp, every Black Guard watcher across the country pulled out together," Number 7 said, his voice a tense murmur. "The capital rings, provincial offices, Jingzhou City airports, and Shanghai's main roads. Every unit vanished from our sensors within ninety seconds. Number 1 confirmed the full pullout three minutes ago."

Chen Xuan kept walking toward the corner store's yellow sign. "That was expected."

"There is an issue," Number 7 said, knuckles tapping a desk over the line. "White Guard's sensors around Fuxing New Village never cleared. Two heat readings are still active within fifty meters of you."

Chen Xuan stopped three steps short of the store door. "Who are they?"

"Black Guard," Number 7 said, stiff with quiet alarm. "Same hidden heat pattern, same spacing, no radio signals at all. They are standing still forty meters behind you, near the locust tree at the bend in the lane. There was no order for them to stay. Number 1 sent three secret check-in messages and got complete silence. Even Number 1 has no power over them."

A tense pause held on the line. Number 7 let out a slow breath, fighting to stay steady.

"They are forty meters from you with no reason and no chain of command," Number 7 pressed, worry breaking through his training. "Give the word and I will move a White Guard team into Yunxi County in twenty minutes. We will put a solid wall between them and your building."

"Hold the line," Chen Xuan said calmly.

He lowered the phone and walked into the store. The sleepy shopkeeper blinked behind the counter.

"Late night, young man," the shopkeeper murmured. "I was just closing the shutter."

"One bottle of red-label dark soy sauce and a small bag of coarse salt," Chen Xuan said, setting a twenty-yuan bill on the counter.

"Heading back to Shanghai tomorrow?" the shopkeeper asked, handing over the bag and change.

"Early bus," Chen Xuan replied. "Thank you."

He stepped back into the lane, raised the phone, and looked down the bend he had just passed.

Forty meters away under the locust tree stood two tall figures in long dark coats, collars turned up, blank masks in the dim light, bodies completely still. Neither moved as a distant scooter rattled past two blocks away. Neither glanced toward the apartment windows. They faced outward, watching the lane in cold silence.

Chen Xuan looked at them for three seconds, the soy sauce bottle hanging lightly from his left hand.

"Are you still there?" Number 7 asked, sharper now. "Our south sensor confirms they have not moved an inch since nine o'clock. Without an order, we cannot be sure what they plan."

"Leave them alone," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Do not get in their way."

Number 7 drew a sharp breath. "You want us to leave an unlisted Black Guard team standing forty meters from your family's apartment?"

"Yes."

"Number 1 will demand a risk report," Number 7 said, voice tight as duty clashed with unease. "We have no way to talk to them and no rules for handling them. If a neighbor walks past, or a county police car turns down that lane, we cannot tell them to stand down."

"You do not need to tell them anything," Chen Xuan replied, starting back toward Fuxing New Village at the same steady pace. "They left everywhere else at nine because their job ended. Those two stayed by their own choice."

"By their own choice?" Number 7 repeated, shock cracking his flat voice. "Black Guard does not act on its own."

"Then stop judging them by White Guard's rules," Chen Xuan said, no anger in his voice, only quiet firmness. "Pull your sensors outside two hundred meters. Do not send a guard team. Do not try to contact them. Tell Number 1 to stop sending messages."

Silence stretched for four seconds. Chen Xuan could almost hear Number 7 clenching his jaw.

"Understood," Number 7 said tightly. "Watch from outside two hundred meters. No interference. Should I record them in your regular security file?"

"Do not write them down at all," Chen Xuan said. "If they wanted to be on paper, they would carry real numbers."

"Understood," Number 7 replied, his breath easing slightly. "Your uncle's hardware shop is quiet. Number 23 confirmed the street is clear."

"Good. Get some rest."

Chen Xuan ended the call and pocketed the phone.

As he neared the locust tree, an electric scooter turned into the lane, its headlight sweeping the damp bricks. Before the beam reached the trunk, the two dark-coated figures shifted half a step into a deep storage doorway so silently that the rider rolled straight past without turning his head.

Chen Xuan walked past the locust tree without slowing, carrying his soy sauce and salt back to the stairwell.

When he opened the apartment door, Liu Meifang checked the red label with a pleased nod. "Good. Wash your hands. Your father wants to walk over to Mingde Hardware once the ribs are simmering so we can help your uncle finish stacking the shelves."

"I am ready whenever he is," Chen Xuan said, hanging his jacket neatly by the door.
