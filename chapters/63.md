# Chapter 63: Organization Department: The Empty Cadre Files

The third-floor hall of the Central Organization Department hung thick with blue cigarette smoke.

At the inner table sat Executive Deputy Minister Liu Zhengwei beside two men whose presence chilled every spine in Beijing: Elder Qin of the Qin Family, retired senior military patriarch, and Elder Ye of the Ye Family, retired Discipline and Judicial patriarch. At a side desk, two clerks recorded every word and personnel ruling into the formal department minutes.

Elder Qin wore a plain dark jacket, back straight as a rifle barrel.

"Keep your pens ready," Liu Zhengwei said evenly, laying three yellow cadre folders flat. "No excuses today. Deputy Director Bai Yunfeng, deliver the audit report."

At the briefing desk, Personnel Discipline Liaison Bai Yunfeng stood crisply and opened a blue ledger.

"First, the empty files," Bai Yunfeng reported flatly. "We pulled twenty years of annual reviews for three detained officials: Sha Province Vice Governor Lin Zhengde, Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang, and Customs Director Xu Wenbin. Every review carries top marks, zero warnings, and zero overseas family listed. Yet Lin Zhengde's son has owned three villas in Zurich and a shipping firm in Singapore for nine years, while four mining complaints against He Guoliang vanished from our fourth-floor archive."

In the outer ring, Cadre Background Audit Chief Yao Minghui turned gray as ash, knuckles white around his pen.

"Second, the archive breach," Bai Yunfeng continued. "Before dawn, Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi's team arrested our fourth-floor archive deputy and two clerks. For fourteen years, whenever auditors planned a surprise check on Xu Wenbin or Lin Zhengde, that deputy sent a coded weather note to Fang Qiming's contact at Shengyuan Capital seventy-two hours early. They hid overseas shares in shell firms and swapped real complaints for glowing praise."

"Third, vacancies and the sidelined roster," Bai Yunfeng finished cleanly. "Seventeen provincial heads, twelve city leaders, and four vice ministers were detained overnight across fourteen provinces, leaving thirty-three seats empty alongside Yunxi County in Sha Province. Meanwhile, Grassroots Cadre Chief Meng Changqing compiled our sidelined pile: forty-eight veteran provincial directors and dozens of honest officers who refused Shengyuan Capital's dirty deals over fifteen years and were buried in remote archives. Among them is Senior Customs Inspector Zhou Ming, exiled to the northern hills three years ago after blocking fourteen unmarked ore barges for Xu Wenbin. Report complete."

Bai Yunfeng closed his ledger and returned silently to the outer ring.

Heavy silence crushed the room, broken only by the clerks' keyboards locking every ugly fact into the minutes. Cadre Supervision Chief Hou Jianjun stared at his trembling hands as ash fell from his forgotten cigarette onto his trousers.

Neither man dared to speak.

"Fourteen years," Elder Qin spoke at last, his voice low like distant thunder. "Four clean stamps on Lin Zhengde in twelve years. A vice governor pushed through in six days. Do not blame provincial seals. Every man in this outer ring knows how many private calls came from coastal factions, old classmates, and retired uncles asking you to look away."

A cold chill swept the hall.

Elder Ye exhaled gray smoke and tapped his ash. "Family circles and quiet favors," Elder Ye murmured softly. "That is the real rot here, Liu Zhengwei. You pass my coastal nephew today, and I pass your mining brother-in-law tomorrow. This department stopped picking servants for the people and became a private trading club for factions."

Elder Ye's dark eyes sliced across the outer ring. "This morning in the main crisis hall, an unseen private citizen gave this country 300 trillion yuan for free, with up to 500 trillion yuan ready behind it, simply so the nation would not bleed while we clean house. One ordinary citizen gave away clean wealth without asking for a favor, while men in this building sold the people's trust for a classmate's nod."

Shame and dread settled over every shoulder.

"Under emergency authority from the Premier and the National People's Congress, we settle four lines of personnel action right now," Liu Zhengwei announced coldly. "Clerks, record every name, rank, and transfer in the sealed minutes."

The clerks' fingers flew across their keys.

"Line one: criminal punishments," Liu Zhengwei stated. "The arrested archive deputy, the two leaking clerks, and any personnel officer who took a single yuan from Shengyuan Capital or Deng Yongchang are expelled immediately and handed straight to Gao Zhengyi and the closed national security courts."

"Line two: withheld ranks and frozen promotions," Liu Zhengwei continued, eyeing the outer ring. "Cadre Background Audit Chief Yao Minghui and Cadre Supervision Chief Hou Jianjun failed to verify overseas assets and let complaints vanish under their noses. Both men have their ranks frozen, upcoming promotions permanently withheld, and disciplinary warnings logged in their permanent files pending Gao Zhengyi's questioning of the archive deputy."

Yao Minghui closed his eyes, shivering quietly.

"Line three: transfers out of real power," Elder Ye cut in smoothly, voice colder than ice. "Three faction-protected officials do not have hands dirty enough for prison, yet their ties to old provincial circles make them too compromised to keep authority: Second Cadre Bureau Chief Peng Zhiqiang, and coastal provincial deputy heads Sun Hongtao and Wu Dechang."

Peng Zhiqiang's breath caught.

"On paper, we move all three upward into prestigious honorary advisory posts by noon," Elder Ye said without warmth. "Peng Zhiqiang becomes Senior Honorary Counselor of the National Historical Literature Office. Sun Hongtao moves to Vice Chairman of the Coastal Policy Research Association. Wu Dechang becomes Deputy Director of the Retired Cadre Advisory Board. Give them grand titles and bright offices, and strip away every seal, budget line, and personnel signature."

An icy hush gripped the room. Every veteran official felt the razor edge of exile by promotion: a higher-sounding chair with zero real power, turning a faction's fixer into a ghost overnight. Peng Zhiqiang stared blankly as the clerks typed his new title into the sealed minutes.

"And line four," Elder Qin said, a rare warmth softening his stern face. "Immediate awards and promotions for people with iron backbones."

Liu Zhengwei signed the sidelined roster with a bold stroke. "Pull Senior Customs Inspector Zhou Ming out of the northern hills today as Acting Customs Inspection Director with a state commendation. Lift all forty-eight sidelined provincial directors into acting leadership across the fourteen provinces. For Yunxi County in Sha Province, approve the two veteran inspection officers whose family trees Bai Yunfeng verified clean through Discipline Inspection: zero overseas accounts, zero private shares, and zero ties to Shengyuan Capital or Deng Yongchang."

"Check every spouse, child, and in-law on the remaining thirty-three picks anyway," Elder Ye warned sharply. "No surname in Beijing buys a free pass today."

"Already ordered," Liu Zhengwei replied, tapping the roster. "The minutes are sealed. Outer-ring directors, draft every order before noon. Today we burn old favors and hand the keys back to the people who refused to bend."

The silent outer ring stood as one, bowed deeply, and moved to carry out the cleanest purge the department had ever seen.
