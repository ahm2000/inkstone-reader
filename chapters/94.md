# Chapter 94: The Four-Star Salute

Half an hour passed as quietly as the drift of willow leaves across Lanshui Lake.

Inside the cedar pavilion, the charcoal in the small clay brazier had burned down to a gentle red ember, and the brown earthenware teapot was nearly empty.

Not wanting to tire a ninety-year-old man who had traveled all the way from the Western Hills, Chen Xuan rose smoothly from his wooden stool. He lifted the clay pot one last time and poured the final half-cup of warm tea for the elder so the old man would not have to strain his wrist.

"The sun is high enough now, Elder," Chen Xuan said gently. "You should rest before the drive back, and I ought to head into town before the morning market packs up."

The founding Supreme Elder looked up at him with eyes clear and restful, as though ten years of quiet worry had lifted from his narrow shoulders.

"Good," the elder said softly, nodding his silver head. "A sensible young man knows when to sit and when to leave. Walk safely, and do not forget to eat lunch on time."

"Take care of your knees, Elder," Chen Xuan replied with a warm, unhurried bow of his head.

He turned, stepped calmly across the weathered cedar threshold, and retrieved his scratched green metal thermos from the wooden rail.

Outside on the lakeside landing, General Qin Zhengguo had stood in patient, unmoving vigilance for the entire half hour. Not once had he checked his watch. Not once had he paced or glanced impatiently toward the doorway.

Yet as Chen Xuan stepped onto the wooden landing, General Qin Zhengguo caught sight of the ninety-year-old founding Supreme Elder through the open lattice window.

General Qin Zhengguo had escorted the Old Elder for more than fifteen years. He knew every subtle line of fatigue around the old man's eyes, every quiet sigh he hid from the ministers in the capital. Today, the elder sat smiling peacefully over his warm teacup, his posture light, his expression utterly serene.

Whatever had passed inside that modest pavilion had given the founding elder a peace that no state banquet or military parade had ever managed to bring.

Chen Xuan descended the three low cedar steps and stepped onto the narrow stone path winding back toward the willow trees.

As he approached, General Qin Zhengguo took one deliberate step off the stone flagstones onto the damp lakeside grass, yielding the entire path without hesitation.

Then, beneath the clear autumn sky, the highest serving four-star commander in the country brought his heels neatly together.

He straightened his broad shoulders until his spine stood straight as tempered steel.

Slowly, silently, and with profound gravity, General Qin Zhengguo bent deeply from the waist.

He wore no olive uniform today and no gleaming gold stars upon his collar, so he did not throw a theatrical military salute beside a public lake. Yet every inch of that deep, motionless bow carried the full weight of a four-star salute, offered in pure gratitude and humbled awe to an ordinary-looking office worker carrying a scratched green thermos.

Thirty paces away beneath the weeping willow, Number One watched in quiet stillness, his eyes reflecting deep, unspoken understanding.

Chen Xuan paused on the stone path.

He did not step back in awkward panic, nor did he look down with pride or satisfaction. His face remained mild, calm, and completely ordinary.

He shifted his green thermos smoothly to his left hand and offered General Qin Zhengguo a polite, natural nod in return.

"There is no need for that," Chen Xuan said mildly, his voice gentle against the soft lap of the lake water. "It is a nice morning for a walk."

General Qin Zhengguo held the bow for one full heartbeat longer before straightening slowly, his weathered face filled with quiet, unshakable respect.

"Please make sure the elder wraps his wool shawl around his collar before he walks out to the car," Chen Xuan added casually, glancing back once toward the pavilion. "The breeze off the water still has a little autumn chill in it."

"I will see to it personally, sir," General Qin Zhengguo answered, his deep baritone steady and soft. "Our car is just behind the bamboo grove. Let me have the driver take you back into Lanshui City."

Chen Xuan smiled faintly and shook his head.

"No need to trouble your driver," Chen Xuan said easily. "The Route 18 suburban bus stops up on the main road in about ten minutes, and the walk up the willow lane is good for stretching my legs."

General Qin Zhengguo opened his mouth for a fraction of a second, then closed it gently, realizing that insisting would only break the quiet simplicity Chen Xuan valued most.

"Then have a safe journey home, sir," General Qin Zhengguo said softly.

Chen Xuan gave him one last polite nod, offered a brief, familiar glance to Number One beneath the willow tree, and resumed his unhurried walk up the sunlit stone path.

His faded navy windbreaker swayed lightly in the breeze. His scratched green thermos swung quietly at his side.

As he moved up the willow lane, fifty meters out along the pine ridge and the silent reed beds, the invisible pressure that General Qin Zhengguo had sensed earlier lifted seamlessly from the cove. Without a single footstep, without a rustle of leaves or a glint of matte-black gear, the permanent Zero Guard team shifted forward in perfect, silent unison, keeping their fifty-meter circle unbroken around the young man walking toward a suburban bus stop.

General Qin Zhengguo remained standing on the damp grass beside the stone path long after Chen Xuan's figure rounded the bend of the willows and vanished toward the main road.

Only then did Number One step quietly out from beneath the willow shade to stand three paces from the General.

General Qin Zhengguo let out a long, quiet breath, shaking his head in stunned, humbled respect.

"One hundred trillion yuan dismantled in forty-eight hours," General Qin Zhengguo murmured softly, staring down the empty stone path. "Four tier-one foreign killers neutralized without waking a single neighbor. And he still walks ten minutes up a dirt lane to catch the Route 18 public bus."

Number One looked down the sunlit path where Chen Xuan had gone, a faint, proud smile touching his eyes.

"That is the Boss," Number One replied simply. "He never wanted the world to kneel. He only wanted it to stay quiet enough for a decent morning walk."

Inside the cedar pavilion, the ninety-year-old founding Supreme Elder watched from the sunlit window, resting his scarred hand warmly against his porcelain teacup.

"Come, Qin Zhengguo," the old man called gently through the open doorway. "Bring my shawl. I am ready to go back to the Western Hills. Today was a very good morning."

General Qin Zhengguo straightened his shoulders, his heart fuller and steadier than it had been in twenty years, and stepped back up the wooden stairs to escort the elder home.
