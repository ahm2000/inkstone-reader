# Chapter 94: Zurich Receives Silence

Seven time zones west of Jingzhou City, rain ran down the glass wall of a forty-second-floor private bank tower in Zurich. Inside the soundproof control room of the Northern Ledger, nobody looked outside.

Across the stone wall, sixteen secure tracking screens glowed flat red. Shanghai: offline. Shanghai: offline. Jingzhou City: offline. The capital link: offline.

Marc Keller stood beside the main desk with his silk tie pulled loose. A muscle twitched in his clenched jaw. "Send the backup signal through Singapore again. If Shanghai lost a security key, Hong Kong should still reply."

Behind the glass table, Sylvie Marchal did not touch her keyboard. Her face was pale, and her fingers stayed locked around a cold coffee cup. "I ran Singapore, Hong Kong, and Frankfurt three times in twenty minutes, Marc Keller. There is no lost key. Those lines are not blocking our codes. They are dead."

"Dead does not happen across four regions at once," Marc Keller snapped, slapping the edge of the table. "Fourteen years of money channels. Forty-seven middlemen. Three hundred and eighteen key contacts across banking, shipping, and port deals. You do not wipe out fourteen years of work in one night without a single leak or a single warning."

At the head of the table, Senior Partner Henrik Lindholm had not raised his voice. He sat back in his dark wool suit with his fingertips pressed together under his chin. Only the cold stillness in his gray eyes showed the shock behind his calm face.

"Stop yelling at the screens, Marc Keller," Henrik Lindholm said quietly. "Sylvie Marchal, give me the exact order of the shutdown."

Sylvie Marchal let out a slow breath and tapped open her black tablet. "Every link went silent within six hours between Sunday night and Monday evening local time. Our shipping finance office in Shanghai went dark at two in the morning. Two hours later, our capital contact missed three check-ins. By Monday afternoon, every city contact in Shanghai and Jingzhou City was arrested or cut off, and every overseas account tied to our forty-seven middlemen was frozen at the same time."

Marc Keller sank into a leather chair and wiped sweat from his forehead. "Those contacts. Gone. Not a single warning from our paid sources in Beijing. How does a sweep this big move without setting off even one paid tip-off?"

"Because whoever ordered it went around every office we pay," Henrik Lindholm said, his voice razor-cold. "Sylvie Marchal, check the earliest time mark. Which point broke first?"

Sylvie Marchal frowned at the system log. "That is what makes no sense, Henrik Lindholm. It did not start in Shanghai or the capital. The first strange move began three days ago in a small county called Yunxi, under Handong Province."

Marc Keller stared in disbelief. "Yunxi? I have never heard of it."

"You should have," Sylvie Marchal shot back. "Our Shanghai contact, Deng Yongchang, used a local loan ring in Yunxi County to clean local money. On Saturday night, Deng Yongchang's thugs leaned on an ordinary hardware shop there. Within a day, Deng Yongchang's whole network in Shanghai was ripped out by the roots. Six hours later, Jingzhou City's backers fell. By Monday morning, the freeze jumped straight to Shanghai and the capital, burning every middleman who touched that chain."

Silence froze the room.

Marc Keller let out a dry, shocked laugh that died in his throat. "Are you telling me the Northern Ledger lost fourteen years of money channels and three hundred and eighteen key contacts over a county hardware shop?"

"Nobody burns that many contacts over a county hardware shop," Henrik Lindholm said softly, his gray eyes narrowing. "Unless Deng Yongchang's fools stepped straight on someone invisible."

Henrik Lindholm pressed the brass intercom button on the table. "Send in Marcus Vance."

Thirty seconds later, the frosted glass doors opened.

A tall man in his late thirties stepped inside. He carried a dark coat folded over one arm, and his tailored charcoal suit was crisp and neat. His features were sharp, calm, and East Asian. On his European passport he was Marcus Vance. In the Northern Ledger's secret list, his real name was Li Weiming.

"Good afternoon, Henrik Lindholm," Li Weiming said, his English smooth and unhurried. He glanced once at the dead red screens, his face barely changing. "That looks expensive."

"It is a disaster," Marc Keller muttered darkly.

Henrik Lindholm slid a slim gray folder across the polished table. "Three hundred and eighteen key contacts across China went silent overnight, Li Weiming. Fourteen years of channels and forty-seven middlemen, gone without a single warning. Right now Zurich is blind."

Li Weiming picked up the folder and flipped calmly through the first three pages. "Shanghai, Jingzhou City, Shenzhen, and the capital. All cut off cleanly. No news leaks, no panic selling in Hong Kong. Whoever hit you wanted a clean cut, not headlines."

"Which is why I called you," Henrik Lindholm said, leaning forward. "We cannot rebuild a single channel until we know who swung the knife. Was this a state money sweep, a rival group, or one hidden hand woken up in Jingzhou City?"

"Page four," Sylvie Marchal cut in, watching Li Weiming closely. "Deng Yongchang in Shanghai was the first business link to fall, right after an incident in Yunxi County. Every local official who touched that incident was taken into custody before noon today."

Li Weiming's dark eyes lingered on the map of Jingzhou City and Shanghai. A faint, unreadable smile touched his mouth.

"A county fight brings down a fourteen-year channel all the way to Zurich," Li Weiming murmured. "That is not a normal check. Someone very quiet lives near that line, or someone very powerful had a personal reason to sweep that street clean."

"Take the night flight to Hong Kong, then enter Shanghai under your business advisor cover," Henrik Lindholm ordered, his voice cold and firm. "Carry no Northern Ledger accounts, no coded gear tied to Zurich, and make zero contact with any surviving middlemen. Do not anger local officials. Find out what set off the collapse in Jingzhou City and Shanghai, and tell me whose hand is behind it."

"And if I find a name?" Li Weiming asked calmly, slipping the folder into his briefcase.

"Do not touch them," Henrik Lindholm warned, his gaze heavy with rare caution. "Measure the shadow and report back to Zurich. Only a fool swings at a blade before knowing who holds the handle."

Marc Keller watched Li Weiming with tight doubt. "How long?"

"Seventy-two hours on the ground in Shanghai," Li Weiming replied smoothly, snapping the brass lock shut. "Even the cleanest storms leave tracks once the water drains."

He gave Henrik Lindholm a polite nod, turned cleanly, and walked out through the glass doors.

Behind him, sixteen secure screens stayed flat red, answering Zurich with nothing but silence.
