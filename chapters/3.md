# Chapter 3: The Booking Desk

The front hall of Old Street Police Station smelled of floor wax and cold tea.

Only one window was still lit, the one marked *Report a Case*, and behind it sat Wei Kun with a pen in his hand and no hope in his face.

He was a duty officer with twelve years on the job.

His title was grand, "deputy principal staff member," but he commanded no one and signed nothing.

Cheng Biao held the real post, so Wei Kun held the pen.

"We want to report an assault," Chen Jianguo said, and he turned his left cheek to the light.

Five red finger marks still showed on it.

"Brother Hu of Hongrui slapped me. On Old Street. About twenty-five minutes ago."

Wei Kun's pen stopped.

"Slapped you?" said a voice from the corner.

Lei Bo lay across two chairs with a bag of sunflower seeds on his chest.

He was a young formal officer with a gelled haircut, and his whole career was built on laughing at Cheng Biao's jokes one second early.

"Uncle, in this light I see a red face. Maybe you ate spicy food."

"Look at it properly!" Chen Jianguo shouted.

"Brother Hu? Never heard of him. Who is that?" Lei Bo spat a shell into a paper cup. "Did anybody see it?"

"The whole street saw it!"

"Then the whole street can write a statement. Tomorrow. In daylight." "Careful, Uncle. People who shout in here lose their shop license. Old men fall down on dark streets."

Chen Jianguo's mouth closed, and his hand went to the counter to steady himself.

"And my brother?" Chen Jianguo said, his voice cracking. "At two o'clock a young driver looked straight at Hu and threw himself into the paint shelf. Old Yu saw it. Five minutes later your men handcuffed Mingde for beating that driver!"

"Ah, the hardware case," Lei Bo said, and yawned. "A driver with a broken head. Very serious."

Wei Kun slid a blank report form under the glass, with a pen on top.

"Thanks, Officer," Chen Xuan said, and gave him a small nod.

"Wei," Lei Bo said sweetly, "did I ask you to help?"

Wei Kun's ears turned red, and he pulled the form back.

Chen Xuan had stood a half step behind his father, thermos in hand, looking at the floor tiles.

"Evening, Officer. I want to see my uncle," he said. "I brought a blanket and his heart pills."

"A suspect. Rules." Lei Bo did not look up.

"Good. I like rules," said Chen Xuan.

Outside, tires crunched.

A young auxiliary officer named Zhou Pingan, dozing by the door, jerked awake and ran to push it open.

Lei Bo shot off the chairs so fast his seeds scattered across the floor.

Wei Kun stood, and a clerk at the back stood.

"Director Cheng!" four voices said at once.

Cheng Biao stepped in wearing his black leather jacket over his uniform trousers, with the wooden toothpick rolling between his teeth.

He was second in command of the whole county police and ran this station too. People dropped the word "deputy" and just said "Director Cheng."

He walked through the hall as if the floor belonged to him, and the floor seemed to agree.

"Who are these people?"

"Family of the hardware shop case, Director!" Lei Bo said, bent halfway at the waist. "They came to cause a scene, and I held them here until you arrived."

"Wei Kun!" Cheng Biao's voice cracked across the hall like a whip. "Who let strangers in at night? Is this a tea house? You sit behind one window all day and can't keep it clean?"

"They came to report an assault, Director."

"Report what? That the sky is blue?"

Everyone in the station had learned that when Cheng Biao shouted at one man, the safe thing was to become furniture.

"And the time box on the hardware file, is it still empty?"

"Yes, Director."

"Leave it empty. I will tell you when."

Cheng Biao turned, and his eye landed on Zhou Pingan, who was still holding the door.

"You. Why did the glass open late? Did you think I enjoy waiting?"

"Sorry, Director, I just."

Slap!

The back of Cheng Biao's hand knocked the nineteen-year-old's cap off, and it skidded across the floor to Chen Xuan's shoe.

"Straighten up. An auxiliary with no manners."

Zhou Pingan's face went white, and then red.

He picked up the cap, put it on without a sound, and did not cry.

Chen Xuan looked at the boy for a long moment, and gave him a short nod.

"Director, I would like to see my uncle."

Cheng Biao looked him up and down, slowly, from the scuffed shoes to the green thermos.

"You are the nephew. The clerk from Qingyan."

"Yes."

"Visiting hours are over, clerk."

"The sign says nine o'clock," Chen Xuan said flatly.

"The sign is old. I decide the hours in my station."

"Director, by law the family should be told the reason."

"Within twenty-four hours. You'll get it when it's ready." The toothpick flicked to the other side of his mouth. "Don't teach me the law, clerk. I was reading it when you were learning to hold chopsticks." He leaned closer. "Your sister teaches at the junior high, I hear. Teachers' files cross my desk for background checks. A family name in a case sticks to a file for a very long time."

Chen Jianguo went gray.

Chen Xuan only looked at him.

"Remember tonight's hour, Director," he said. "You will be asked for it."

No bow, no shouting, nothing Cheng Biao could hit.

For one second the toothpick stopped moving, and then it dropped from his lip and rolled across the tiles.

He did not pick it up, and he could not say why.

It felt like pressing a fist into a pillow, and the pillow had pressed back.

"Lei Bo, see them out. And Brother Hu? Never heard the name," he added loudly. "Complaints against Hongrui go to the Hongrui office, in daytime."

"Yes, Director! Very wise, Director!" Lei Bo hurried to hold the door.

Chen Xuan laid a hand on his father's arm and steered him out into the night.

Wei Kun sat back down and looked at the empty box on the form.

He felt as if his chair were made of ice. A bead of cold sweat slid down his back.

* * *

Outside, the street lamps buzzed over an empty bus stop.

Chen Jianguo sat down hard on the bench, and his glasses fogged.

"Your sister's school file," he said. "That man said it to my face, and you did not even shout."

"Dad, sit. Breathe."

Chen Xuan sat beside him, set the blanket on his knees, and took a second phone from the bottom of his bag.

It was an old gray phone with one number in it, and he typed one line.

*Chief An. Record everything on Cheng Biao and Hongrui. Touch nothing yet.*

Then he put the phone away and unwrapped the other half of his bun.

"Who are you texting at a time like this?" Chen Jianguo said. "Your uncle is in a cold room."

"Work."

His father stared at him. Then, with nothing else to do, he laughed once, bitterly, and wiped his eyes.

* * *

At the end of the back corridor was a small room full of paper. In it sat a round-shouldered man in gray sleeve covers.

He was the records clerk, and everyone called him Old Ding.

Nobody in the building knew he belonged to the Detail.

Through a gap in the door he had watched five red finger marks on the Boss's father, and a boy's cap on the floor, and his pen had snapped in half in his fist.

Now he pressed a recorder into "save" and typed on a phone no bigger than a matchbox.

*Hu slapped the Boss's father. Cheng refused the visit, struck an auxiliary, threatened the sister's school file. All recorded.*

The answer came in nine seconds.

*Recording. Awaiting the next order.*

Out in the hall, Lei Bo dragged a chair to the front computer and typed the clerk's name.

Old Ding, filing papers at the door, kept his face blank as a plate.

"Chen Xuan, 29, born in Yunxi. Ordinary family. Clerk at a logistics firm. Clean record. Simple, honest. No car, no house, no friends worth a call."

"Boring," Cheng Biao yawned from the doorway.

"Good. Boring ones are easy."

Old Ding said nothing. The other file does not live in this building.

The door opened, and the night driver, a big man named Mu, leaned in.

"Tell me I can flatten his tires."

"No order." Old Ding dropped the broken pen into the bin. "The Boss said record. So we record."

"How long?"

Old Ding looked at the screen, and for the first time in six years, he did not know the answer.
