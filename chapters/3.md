# Chapter 3: The Black Fleet at the Side Gate

Two blocks south of Hengxin Tower, the rush of Binjiang Avenue faded behind Qingyan South Park. Few commuters walked here after dark, hurrying instead toward the metro lines and the bus terminal three miles east.

Chen Xuan had no intention of going near the bus terminal.

He walked at an unhurried pace with his canvas bag slung over one shoulder and his green thermos tucked inside. Four minutes after sending his three-word message, he turned into the narrow service lane behind the park's west gate.

Halfway down the lane waited three matte-black executive sedans parked in a tight column, bearing ordinary regional plates and no emblems. Their engines idled in a low, barely audible hum.

As Chen Xuan came within twenty paces, the front passenger door of the middle sedan opened cleanly. A tall man in a dark wool overcoat stepped out, wearing no earpiece and no insignia, his posture holding the coiled stillness of a blade inside its sheath. He pulled open the rear right door with one gloved hand and inclined his head slightly.

"Everything is ready," the man said in a low, steady voice.

"Number Seven," Chen Xuan replied with a faint nod, stepping into the rear seat.

Number Seven shut the heavy door with a muted thud, rounded the bumper, and took the front passenger seat. Before his belt clicked, the lead sedan eased forward, the middle car glided off the curb, and the rear escort fell seamlessly into place. In five seconds, the lane was empty again.

Inside the soundproofed cabin, Qingyan's traffic noise vanished. Chen Xuan set his canvas bag on the seat beside him and checked that his green thermos lid was tight, moving with the plain economy of a man who wasted neither breath nor motion.

Up front, Number Seven kept his eyes forward on the expressway ramp while the driver neither spoke nor glanced into the rearview mirror. That silence was the bedrock of the Black Pine Guard. Across the entire organization, from coastal watchers to specialists in mountain counties, there were no surnames, no given names, and no personal histories. Every operative existed solely as a number: Number One, Number Seven, Number Nine, Number Eleven, and down through the quiet ranks. No member knew another's real name or saw another's unguarded face outside strict operational necessity. Even Number One, coordinating the network behind unbroken encryption, held no roster of real identities. Every thread of loyalty terminated at a single point: Chen Xuan.

"Report," Chen Xuan said softly, resting his forearm along the armrest.

"Three minutes ago, the Cloud Gate protocol cleared Qingyan Executive Aviation Terminal," Number Seven said, crisp and measured. "Your aircraft has finished pre-flight checks, fuel is topped for a direct run with reserve, and the crew is seated on Runway Three. Number Nine's team has cleared the southern service gate. We reach the apron in nineteen minutes."

Chen Xuan glanced briefly at the dark window, his expression unreadable. "And Yunxi?"

Number Seven's shoulders stayed straight, his voice dropping half a shade in focus. "The moment your dispatch hit the relay, the Yunxi regional station entered silent alert. Two minutes ago, our local watcher confirmed Chen Mingde was brought into Old Street Police Station at two thirteen this afternoon. The intake log lists preliminary detention for intentional assault against a site supervisor employed by Hongrui Real Estate."

Chen Xuan's eyes did not blink. "Where is he right now?"

"Still inside the rear interrogation wing of Old Street Station," Number Seven answered. "No formal prosecutor's warrant has been filed. The detention slip carries only the deputy director's signature."

"Have they touched him?" Chen Xuan asked. His tone stayed completely level, yet the cabin air seemed to cool by several degrees.

Number Seven answered without hesitation. "Our watcher confirmed Chen Mingde walked through the side corridor under his own strength at two twenty, handcuffed and wearing only his shop vest. No medical unit has entered the station yard since. Number One has already ordered live telemetry and audio confirmation inside the holding rooms."

Chen Xuan held his silence for two long seconds, his thumb resting still on the armrest. "And his blood pressure medication? My mother said my father went to the gate at three."

"Confirmed," Number Seven said. "Chen Jianguo arrived at three ten carrying a pill bottle. The duty officer refused it and ordered him away. Chen Jianguo returned to the courtyard at three forty and remains indoors with Liu Meifang and Wu Lanying."

A faint, razor-thin line appeared between Chen Xuan's brows, then smoothed away. "Turning away blood pressure medicine for a fifty-four-year-old detainee is not standard procedure, even in a county station."

"It is deliberate pressure," Number Seven agreed quietly. "They want the family frightened enough to sign the relocation slip before morning."

"Keep a quiet eye on my parents' courtyard from outside the lane," Chen Xuan said. "Do not let anyone from Hongrui Real Estate or Old Street Station step near their gate tonight. If a single thug knocks on that door, stop him before his knuckles touch wood."

"Already positioned," Number Seven replied promptly. "Two watchers from the Lanshui-Yunxi circuit are parked two streets west of the courtyard. Nothing moves down that lane unseen."

Chen Xuan leaned back and closed his eyes as the sedan accelerated down the open inner lane. Ahead, the lead car never tapped its brakes; every intersection between the park and the expressway had flipped green three seconds before the convoy reached the white line, cleared silently through municipal relays.

"Who owns Hongrui Real Estate?" Chen Xuan asked without opening his eyes.

"On public registries, a local developer named Deng Yongchang holds sixty percent of the shares," Number Seven said evenly. "Site enforcement along Old Street is handled by Yan Tiehu, a former quarry contractor with two old assault records sealed five years ago."

Chen Xuan's eyes opened slightly. "A county developer and a quarry thug do not get a police deputy director to ignore medication and sign an irregular detention slip on a Friday afternoon without backing. What else did the initial scan pull?"

"Our automated trace flagged three unusual capital transfers into Hongrui Real Estate over the past eighteen months," Number Seven replied, his tone sharpening. "None of the funds originated in Yunxi County. They routed through layered shell accounts in Lanshui and two coastal free-trade zones."

Chen Xuan turned his gaze toward the tinted glass, his reflection cold and clear as winter water. "Coastal shell accounts feeding a demolition outfit in a quiet mountain county."

"Yes, Sir," Number Seven said quietly. "The volume is far too large for an ordinary street renovation. The Lanshui-Yunxi data desk is pulling the full four-year tender history now."

"Have the preliminary file printed before we board," Chen Xuan said. "I want to read it on the plane."

"It is already printing in the cabin," Number Seven answered.

Chen Xuan gave a slight nod and settled back into the stillness as the three black sedans sped toward Qingyan Airport.
