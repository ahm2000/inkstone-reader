# Chapter 3: The Booking Desk

The front hall of Old Street Police Station smelled of floor wax and cold tea.

Only one window was still lit, the one marked *Report a Case*. Behind it sat Wei Kun, the duty officer, with a pen in his hand and a tired look on his face.

His title, "deputy principal staff member," sounded grand. It was a fancy rank with no real authority. He gave orders to no one and signed nothing, while Cheng Biao held all the power in this building.

"I'm here to report a crime!" Chen Jianguo slammed his hand on the counter and turned his left cheek to the light.

Five red finger marks still showed on it.

"Yan Tiehu, that thug from Hongrui, just hit me in the middle of Old Street!"

Wei Kun's pen stopped.

"Slapped you?" said a voice from the corner.

Lei Bo lay across two chairs with a bag of sunflower seeds on his chest. He was a young officer whose whole career was built on laughing at Cheng Biao's jokes one second early.

"Yan Tiehu? Never heard of him." Lei Bo spat a shell into a paper cup. "Did anybody see it?"

"The whole street saw it!"

"Then bring the whole street tomorrow, in daylight. And save your breath, Uncle. Your brother already has an assault case open here. If you make a scene, I can lock you up right next to him until morning."

Chen Jianguo bit back the rest of his words.

"And what about my brother?" His voice cracked. "Right after two, one of Yan Tiehu's drivers glanced over at his boss and threw himself straight into the paint shelf! Nobody laid a finger on him! Yu Changshun saw the whole thing, and two minutes later your men put Chen Mingde in handcuffs!"

Lei Bo yawned. "Oh, the hardware shop case. The driver split his head open. Open and shut."

Behind the glass, Wei Kun quietly slid a blank report form through the slot.

"Thank you, Officer," Chen Xuan said, giving him a small nod.

"Wei Kun," Lei Bo drawled with a smug grin. "Did anybody ask you to stick your nose in?"

Wei Kun's ears flushed red, and he silently pulled the form back.

Chen Xuan stepped up beside his father, keeping his voice calm even though his eyes were red.

"Officer, I just want to see my uncle for five minutes. He's fifty-two and he has a weak heart. I brought his medicine and a blanket."

"He's a suspect," Lei Bo said without even looking up. "No visitors. Those are the rules."

"Alright," Chen Xuan said quietly. "Then let's go by the rules."

Zhou Pingan, a nineteen-year-old auxiliary officer without a full police rank, had been dozing by the entrance. He jerked awake and hurried to push the glass door open.

Lei Bo shot off the chairs so fast that his sunflower seeds scattered across the floor. Wei Kun stood up at once.

"Director Cheng!" four voices called out together.

Cheng Biao walked in wearing a black leather jacket over his uniform trousers, a wooden toothpick rolling between his teeth.

He was Deputy Director of the county police bureau, and he ran this station too. Everyone dropped the word "deputy" and called him Director Cheng.

"Who are these people?"

Lei Bo bent halfway at the waist. "Family of the hardware shop suspect, Director! They came in here raising a fuss, so I kept them at the counter."

"Wei Kun!" Cheng Biao's voice snapped across the hall. "Who let them stay past hours?"

"They came to report an assault, Director."

"A street argument? At this hour?" Cheng Biao did not even glance at Chen Jianguo's swollen cheek.

He leaned toward the glass window and lowered his voice. "The hardware file. The time box. Is it still blank?"

"Yes, Director."

The time box was the space on a detention form where the hour of the official decision to hold a man had to be written. Cheng Biao wanted it filled in later, with whatever hour suited him.

"Leave it blank. I will tell you when."

Cheng Biao turned, and his eye landed on Zhou Pingan, who was still holding the door.

"You. Why were you so slow opening the door?"

"Sorry, Director, I didn't hear your car."

Slap!

The back of Cheng Biao's hand knocked the boy's cap off. It skidded across the floor and stopped beside Chen Xuan's shoe.

"Stand straight. An auxiliary with no manners."

Zhou Pingan's face went white, then red. He picked up his cap and put it back on without a sound.

Chen Xuan watched the boy, his jaw tightening for half a second, before he gave Zhou Pingan a quiet nod.

"Director Cheng, I would like to see my uncle, Chen Mingde," Chen Xuan said politely.

Cheng Biao looked him up and down, from his plain sneakers to the scratched green thermos in his hand.

"You're the nephew. The office clerk from Qingyan."

"Yes."

"Visiting hours are over."

"The sign on the wall says nine o'clock," Chen Xuan said evenly. "It isn't nine yet."

"That sign is old. I set the hours in this station."

"Director Cheng, by law the family is supposed to receive written notice of why he was detained."

"Within twenty-four hours. Not one minute earlier." Cheng Biao rolled the toothpick to the other side of his mouth. "Don't quote the law at me, young man."

He stepped closer and lowered his voice so only the two of them heard.

"Your sister, Chen Yuan, teaches at the junior high, doesn't she? Her contract comes up for review in June. A family member tangled up in an assault case looks very ugly on a teacher's background check. Think about your sister before you keep making noise in my lobby."

Chen Jianguo went pale and gripped the edge of the counter.

Chen Xuan stood still for a beat. He did not argue, and he did not raise his voice. He lowered his eyes like an ordinary clerk who had just realized he was out of his depth.

"Understood, Director Cheng," Chen Xuan said quietly. "We won't disturb you tonight. We'll come back during daytime hours tomorrow for the written notice."

Cheng Biao smiled, satisfied that the young man knew his place.

"Good. Glad you have some sense. Lei Bo, show them out. As for whatever street quarrel your father is complaining about, bring your witnesses during daytime hours and we'll look into it."

"Yes, Director! Right away, Director!" Lei Bo hurried over to hold the door open.

Chen Xuan took his father's arm and led him quietly out into the night.

Behind the glass, Wei Kun sat back down and stared at the blank time box on the form. His chair felt like ice, and cold sweat slid down his back.

* * *

Outside, the street lamps buzzed over an empty bus stop.

Chen Jianguo sat down hard on the bench, his glasses fogging over.

"He threatened Chen Yuan's job right to my face," Chen Jianguo muttered, his voice shaking. "And you just stood there and agreed with him. You didn't even raise your voice!"

"Shouting inside his own station wouldn't have helped Second Uncle, Dad," Chen Xuan said softly, setting the folded blanket across his father's knees. "It would only have given him an excuse to hold you too. Does your cheek still hurt?"

Chen Jianguo let out a long, bitter breath and looked away.

Chen Xuan reached into the bottom of his canvas bag and pulled out a second phone. It was an old gray handset with only one number saved in it, belonging to Number One, the head of the Black Pine Guard.

He typed a single line with his thumb.

*Number One. Record everything on Cheng Biao and Hongrui. Touch nothing yet.*

He slipped the phone back into his bag and unwrapped the other half of his red-bean bun.

"Who are you texting at a time like this?" Chen Jianguo asked. "Your uncle is sitting in a cold cell."

"Work, Dad. Sorry. It couldn't wait."

Chen Jianguo stared at him, shook his head, and wiped his eyes with the back of his hand.

* * *

At the end of the back corridor sat a small room stacked with paper files. Inside was Number Thirty-four, a round-shouldered Black Pine Guard operative working under cover as the station's records clerk.

Nobody in the building knew Number Thirty-four belonged to the Black Pine Guard, Chen Xuan's secret guard.

Through the gap in the door, Number Thirty-four had seen the red finger marks on Chen Jianguo's cheek and Zhou Pingan's cap skidding across the floor. His pen had snapped in half in his fist.

Now he pressed save on a small recorder and typed on a phone no bigger than a matchbox.

*Yan Tiehu slapped the Boss's father. Cheng Biao refused the visit, struck an auxiliary officer, and threatened Chen Yuan's teaching job. All recorded.*

The reply came back in nine seconds.

*Recording. Awaiting the next order.*

Out in the front hall, Lei Bo typed Chen Xuan's name into the station computer. Number Thirty-four, filing papers near the door, kept his face blank.

Lei Bo read the screen aloud. "Chen Xuan, twenty-nine, born in Yunxi. Ordinary family. Logistics clerk in Qingyan. Clean record. No car, no property, no connections worth a phone call."

"Boring," Cheng Biao yawned from the doorway. "Good. Boring ones are easy to handle."

Number Thirty-four said nothing. Chen Xuan's real file was not kept in this building, and no terminal here could open it.

The back door cracked open, and Number Forty-two, a broad-shouldered operative posing as the station's night driver, leaned inside.

"Tell me I can at least flatten Cheng Biao's tires."

"No order," Number Thirty-four said, dropping the broken pen into the trash bin. "The Boss said record. So we record."

"For how long?"

Number Thirty-four looked back at the glowing screen. For the first time in six years, he did not know the answer.
