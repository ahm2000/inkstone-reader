# Chapter 3: Old Street

Old Street ran two hundred meters from a stone arch to the river, and half of it was dark tonight.

On the dark walls, someone had painted a red circle around one big word, "chai." It meant "to be torn down." A circle went up only after the owner signed.

Eleven shops wore one. Chen Jianguo walked past them with his eyes on the ground.

Everyone knew why. Hongrui Real Estate wanted to tear down Old Street and the three blocks behind it, for a riverside district. It offered owners 3,800 yuan per square meter, when the market price was 8,000. So its men pushed every owner to sign.

Mingde Hardware, on the corner, had none yet. Its shutter hung crooked from one bolt.

Inside, the glass counter lay in pieces. Every drawer on the back wall had been pulled out, and hundreds of screws covered the floor. Today's police search had torn it up a second time, and a toppled shelf of paint cans had left a white puddle by the wall.

"Thursday afternoon." Chen Jianguo's voice was hoarse. "Eight men came in with sticks. They didn't steal anything. They smashed everything, to scare him. Your uncle swept up for three hours. Then he told your aunt a cat did it, so she wouldn't worry."

Chen Xuan knelt and picked up the screws. He dropped them into the drawers one by one, reading the labels softly, the way he had at seven.

"M4. M5. Flat head. Brass."

His hands were shaking, and he could not make them stop.

His father stood in the doorway and looked away. A man does not like to cry in front of his son.

Headlights swept the wall, and a black van stopped at the curb. Five men climbed out, and the first carried a bucket of red paint.

"Brother Tie," somebody whispered.

Yan Tiehu, called Brother Tie on Old Street ("Tie" means iron), led Hongrui's "security team." That was a polite name for eight men who pushed out stubborn owners and broke things. He had a shaved head and a towel on one shoulder.

He dipped a brush in the bucket and walked to the corner wall.

"You can't paint there." Chen Jianguo strode forward. "My brother hasn't signed. This is his wall!"

"Old man, by Monday this wall is gravel."

Chen Jianguo grabbed the brush.

The slap rang out. His glasses flew off. He hit the shutter shoulder first and slid down it. On his left cheek, a red hand rose slowly, five fingers wide.

Chen Jianguo had never been slapped in his life. Shame burned hotter than the cheek.

Yu Changshun, the old tailor everyone called Old Yu, stood at the tailor's door. When the family had walked past earlier, he had whispered, "I saw it all today, Jianguo." Now he clenched his fists and did not move.

Yan Tiehu shook out his hand and raised it again. "Want the other cheek, old man?"

The hand never came down. Chen Xuan had caught it in the air.

He did not remember crossing the shop. Something hot had gone through him, and his fingers closed on Yan Tiehu's thick wrist and held.

Yan Tiehu pulled. The wrist did not move.

Then Chen Xuan slapped him.

It was not a fighter's blow. It was an open hand with everything a son has in it. The crack ran down Old Street like a firecracker, and Yan Tiehu's head snapped sideways.

Nobody breathed.

"That was for my father's face." Chen Xuan's hand was shaking, and his eyes were wet. "If you touch him again, I will break that hand."

Yan Tiehu put a hand to his cheek. Red fingers were coming up on his shaved head.

"Get him," he said through his teeth.

Four men started forward.

Across the lane, the door of Gong's Noodles opened. Six big men in work clothes walked out with their bowls in their hands. They said nothing. Behind them the quiet noodle-shop owner, Number Twenty-three of the Black Pine Guard, stood in the doorway wiping his hands. He did not even look at the van.

At the tailor's door, Yu Changshun picked up his heavy pressing iron. Doorways filled with old men and women.

Yan Tiehu counted the street twice. Then he spat on the ground.

"Not tonight." Yan Tiehu rubbed his cheek. He was not laughing now, and that was worse. "Twelve years I've worked this street, clerk. You're the first man who ever hit my face. I don't forget a first." He leaned close and spoke low. "Building 2, sixth floor. Your mother goes down for vegetables at six. Your sister takes the number 9 bus. Your little brother walks home alone after evening class. Think about that tonight."

Chen Xuan looked at him. He did not shout. He took out his phone and photographed Yan Tiehu's face, then the van's license plate.

"You know a lot about my family," he said. "Now I know about you." His voice was flat and quiet. "If my mother comes home with one scratch, you won't have a tomorrow to worry about."

Yan Tiehu laughed. It came a second too late.

He slapped a wet red circle on the corner wall, and the character ran down the bricks like blood.

At the van door he shouted back. "You want to complain? Go to the station and ask for Cheng Biao. He drinks with me every week."

The van roared away, and the six big men went back to their soup. Chen Xuan did not notice. He was looking at his father.

Chen Jianguo got to his feet, shaking all over. "Xuan! You hit him. Are you out of your mind? They'll come for us."

Chen Xuan picked up his father's glasses and held them out in both hands. His voice shook.

"Dad. Where is the police station?"
