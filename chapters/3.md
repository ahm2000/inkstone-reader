# Chapter 3: The White Fleet at the Side Gate

Two blocks south of Hengxin Tower, the noise of Binjiang Avenue faded behind Shanghai Century Park. Few workers walked here after dark. Most hurried toward the subway or the bus station five kilometers east.

Chen Xuan had no plans to go near the bus station.

He walked at an easy pace with his canvas bag over one shoulder and his green thermos inside. Four minutes after sending his three-word message, he turned into the narrow service lane behind the park's west gate.

Halfway down the lane waited three flat-black sedans parked in a neat line. They had ordinary local plates and no logos. Their engines ran in a low, soft hum.

When Chen Xuan came within twenty steps, the front passenger door of the middle car opened smoothly. A tall man in a dark wool coat stepped out. He wore no earpiece and no badge, and he stood with his hands at his sides. He pulled open the back right door with one gloved hand and bowed his head slightly.

"Everything is ready," the man said in a low, steady voice.

"Number 7," Chen Xuan replied with a slight nod, stepping into the back seat.

Number 7 shut the heavy door with a soft thud, walked around the front, and took the passenger seat. Before his seat belt clicked, the lead car moved forward, the middle car pulled off the curb, and the back car followed in tight order. Five seconds later, the lane was empty again.

Inside the quiet cabin, Shanghai's street noise disappeared. Chen Xuan placed his canvas bag on the seat beside him and checked that his green thermos lid was screwed tight.
Up front, Number 7 kept his eyes straight ahead on the highway ramp. The driver never spoke and never looked into the rearview mirror. That silence was the core rule of the White Guard. Across the whole group, from coastal watchers to men in quiet mountain counties, there were no family names, no first names, and no personal pasts. Every member lived only as a number: Number 1, Number 7, Number 9, Number 11, and down through the quiet ranks. No member knew another's real name or saw another's face unless a job required it. Even Number 1, who ran the network through locked channels, kept no list of real names. Every line of loyalty ended at one person: Chen Xuan.

"Report," Chen Xuan said softly, resting his arm on the armrest.

"Three minutes ago, the Cloud Gate plan cleared Shanghai Private Flight Terminal," Number 7 said in a crisp, calm voice. "Your plane has finished its safety checks, fuel is full for a nonstop flight with spare, and the crew is waiting on Runway Three. Number 9's team has cleared the south service gate. We will reach the plane in nineteen minutes."

Chen Xuan looked briefly at the dark window, his face unreadable. "And Yunxi?"

Number 7 kept his back straight, his voice focusing even tighter. "The moment your message hit our line, the Yunxi team went on silent alert. Two minutes ago, our local watcher confirmed Chen Mingde was brought into Old Street Police Station at 2:13 this afternoon. The log lists him as held for beating a site foreman who works for Hongrui Real Estate."

Chen Xuan did not blink. "Where is he right now?"

"Still inside the back questioning wing of Old Street Station," Number 7 answered. "No prosecutor approval has been filed. The hold slip only has the deputy station chief's name on it."

"Have they hit him?" Chen Xuan asked. His voice stayed completely flat, yet the air inside the car seemed to drop several degrees.

Number 7 answered at once. "Our watcher saw Chen Mingde walk down the side hall on his own two feet at 2:20, in handcuffs and wearing only his shop vest. No ambulance or doctor has entered the station yard since then. Number 1 has already turned on live cameras and sound inside the holding rooms."

Chen Xuan sat silent for two long seconds, his thumb resting still on the armrest. "And his blood pressure pills? My mother said my father went to the gate at three."

"Confirmed," Number 7 said. "Chen Jianguo arrived at 3:10 with a pill bottle, tea, and a sweater. The officer on duty refused to take it and ordered him to leave. Chen Jianguo went back home at 3:40 and is still inside with Liu Meifang and Wu Lanying."

A faint, sharp line appeared between Chen Xuan's brows, then smoothed away. "Turning away blood pressure medicine for a fifty-four-year-old man is not normal police work, even in a small county."

"It is done on purpose to scare them," Number 7 agreed quietly. "They want the family frightened enough to sign the move-out paper before morning."

"Keep a quiet watch on my parents' yard from outside the lane," Chen Xuan said. "Do not let anyone from Hongrui Real Estate or Old Street Station come near their gate tonight. If a single thug tries to knock on that door, stop him before his hand touches the wood."

"Already set," Number 7 replied right away. "Two watchers from the Jingzhou City-Yunxi team are parked two streets west of the yard. Nothing moves down that lane without us seeing it."

Chen Xuan leaned back and closed his eyes as the car sped down the open inner lane. Ahead, the lead car never hit its brakes. Every traffic light between the park and the highway turned green three seconds before the cars reached the white line, locked by a traffic engineer on Number 9's team.

"Who owns Hongrui Real Estate?" Chen Xuan asked without opening his eyes.

"On public papers, a local developer named Deng Yongchang owns sixty percent of the company," Number 7 said evenly. "Street work along Old Street is run by Yan Tiehu, a former quarry contractor with two old assault cases that were sealed five years ago."

Chen Xuan's eyes opened slightly. "A small-town builder and a quarry thug do not make a police deputy chief block medicine and sign a crooked hold slip on a Friday afternoon without someone backing them. What else did the first check find?"

"Our quick check found three strange money transfers into Hongrui Real Estate over the past eighteen months," Number 7 replied, his tone turning sharper. "None of the money came from Yunxi County. It passed through hidden shell accounts in Jingzhou City and two coastal trade zones."

Chen Xuan turned his eyes toward the dark glass, his reflection cold and clear. "Coastal shell accounts feeding a tear-down crew in a quiet mountain county."

"Yes, Sir," Number 7 said quietly. "The amount is way too big for a simple street rebuild. The Jingzhou City-Yunxi data team is pulling the full four-year bidding history right now."

"Have the first report printed before we get on the plane," Chen Xuan said. "I want to read it in the air."

"It is already printing in the cabin," Number 7 answered.

Chen Xuan gave a slight nod and settled back into the quiet as the three black cars raced toward Shanghai Hongqiao Airport.
