# Chapter 3: The Booking Desk

Yunxi County, Old Street Police Station. Friday, 2:40 p.m.

Wei Kun sat behind the booking desk, staring down at his intake form.

Old Street Station had thirty-six staff on the roster, though only ten were formal police officers. The other twenty-six were auxiliary officers on one-year contracts who carried batons instead of guns and could never be promoted.

The station's official chief was Cheng Biao, who also held the higher post of Deputy Director at the county police bureau, so he rarely sat in this small building. Normally, the Political Instructor, Qiu Guoqiang, the steady second-in-command who actually kept the station running by the book, handled daily work. Conveniently, two weeks ago, both Qiu Guoqiang and the station's deputy chief had been "sent to a training course," leaving the keys and all the dirty files dumped on duty officer Wei Kun.

In twelve years behind this desk, Wei Kun had booked over three hundred suspects. This thin, gray-haired shopkeeper wouldn't make trouble. He kept his eyes on the floor and answered quietly. Under Attitude, Wei Kun wrote: Calm.

Then he flipped to the attached evidence, and his pen stopped dead!

Clipped to the file was a private clinic's injury note for Boss Dong's driver, stamped 1:50 p.m.

Wei Kun's neck went ice-cold!

The arrest had happened at 2:05 p.m. The doctor's injury note had been signed fifteen minutes before the assault even happened!

It was fake, prepared in advance. And Wei Kun knew perfectly well whose signature sat on the detention order.

Should he speak up? To whom?

After twelve years on the force, Wei Kun understood the bitter math of government rank. To put it nicely, he held a senior pay grade on paper, deputy principal staff member; to put it bluntly, it was an empty title with zero real power! Nobody worked under him, and he couldn't sign a single binding order. Cheng Biao, on the other hand, held a real leadership post as Deputy Director of the county police bureau and head of this station. One real step separated the man who gave orders from the man who filled out forms.

And Wei Kun still had a 2,800-yuan monthly mortgage hanging over his head, plus a wife seven months pregnant who could barely tie her own shoes.

Cheng Biao's warning at the morning meeting still rang in his ears: "You fill in the forms, Wei. Thinking is my job."

Wei Kun swallowed hard and kept writing until he reached the box marked Time of Decision, where police had to record when they legally decided to detain a suspect. His fingers refused to move. He left it blank.

"Don't fill that box in yet."

Cheng Biao had strolled in quietly, wearing a black leather jacket over his uniform trousers, a wooden toothpick rolling between his teeth. "We'll write the time in later."

"Yes, Director Cheng," Wei Kun said stiffly. In government offices, lower officers always dropped the word "deputy" and called a boss by his highest title, especially a man whose signature could lock anyone in Yunxi behind bars for fifteen days.

Cheng Biao perched casually on the corner of the desk, smiling down at Chen Mingde like a friendly uncle at a family dinner.

"Old Chen, let's be honest," he said softly. "Assaulting a man in public means ten days in detention, maybe fifteen, plus a police record. Your daughter's in college, right? Think about how she'll have to explain her father's record at every job interview. Now, it doesn't have to be ugly. Sign the buyout contract, Hongrui drops the complaint, and you're home for dinner in ten minutes."

Silence hung over the desk.

"Director Cheng," Chen Mingde said quietly, not looking up. "I want to see the written detention order first."

Cheng Biao blinked. "What?"

"The official paper saying why you're holding me, and when it was signed. I want to see it first."

The toothpick between Cheng Biao's lips stopped moving!

Eleven Old Street owners had sat in that chair over the past two months. Eleven times, they'd broken down and signed. Why was this twelfth man, a nobody with a tiny shop and zero connections, calmly asking for official paperwork?

Was he stubborn, stupid, or did he know someone?

Unease prickled down Cheng Biao's neck. Too much rode on this corner shop: his brother-in-law's 38-million-yuan demolition contract, the villa his wife wanted, and next Wednesday, when the provincial inspection team arrived in Yunxi County! Those were senior anti-corruption investigators sent straight from the provincial capital to hunt for scandals and hear citizen complaints. One dirty land grab exposed to them could end a local official's career in a single afternoon!

Wednesday sat on Cheng Biao's chest like a heavy stone.

"Fine, Old Chen," he snapped, his friendly mask vanishing. "Wait for your paper, then. Lock him up, Wei. A cold night in the cell will clear his head!"
