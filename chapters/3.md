# Chapter 3: The Black Fleet at the Side Gate

Two blocks south of Hengxin Tower, the neon glare of Binjiang Avenue gave way to the quiet streets bordering Qingyan South Park. Tall London plane trees arched overhead, their dry autumn leaves whispering against the iron park railings while the wind carried the faint scent of pond water and wet moss. Few commuters walked this stretch after dark, preferring the brightly lit avenues leading toward the metro lines and the intercity bus terminal three miles east.

Chen Xuan had no intention of going anywhere near the bus terminal.

He walked along the damp stone pavement at the same unhurried pace he used when strolling to the corner bakery, his scratched green thermos tucked inside his canvas bag. Four minutes after sending the three-word message, he turned into the narrow, tree-lined service lane behind the park's west gate.

Halfway down the lane, parked in an orderly column beneath overhanging branches, waited three matte-black executive sedans.

Their exterior paint carried no showroom shine, absorbing the pale sodium streetlamps until the vehicles seemed carved from dusk itself. No chrome emblems gleamed on their grilles. Their license plates were ordinary regional numbers registered to quiet provincial shell entities that drew zero attention from traffic cameras. Only someone listening closely would notice the deep, virtually silent purr of modified twin-turbo engines idling in unison, or the subtle thickness of ballistic glass behind dark privacy tint.

As Chen Xuan came within twenty paces, the front passenger door of the middle sedan opened without a creak.

A tall man in a plain dark wool overcoat stepped onto the pavement. His collar was turned up against the autumn breeze, and his posture held the coiled stillness of a blade resting inside an oiled scabbard. He wore no visible earpiece, no insignia, and no readable expression. He stepped cleanly to the rear right door, pulled it open with one gloved hand, and inclined his head slightly.

"The Boss," the man said in a low, steady voice.

"Number Seven," Chen Xuan replied with a faint nod, stepping smoothly into the leather-lined rear cabin.

Number Seven closed the heavy door with a muted, airtight thud, walked around the bumper, and took the front passenger seat. Before his seatbelt clicked, the lead sedan eased forward, the middle car glided off the curb without a jolt, and the rear escort pulled seamlessly into place behind them. In five seconds, the dark lane behind Qingyan South Park was empty again, leaving only drifting leaves swirling in their wake.

Inside the middle sedan, soundproofing erased the rush-hour clamor of Qingyan completely. The cabin smelled faintly of cedarwood, clean leather, and chilled mineral water. A soft reading lamp cast a muted amber glow over the folding walnut console.

Chen Xuan set his canvas messenger bag on the empty seat beside him and checked that the lid of his green thermos was tight. Even in an armored executive sedan, his movements carried the calm economy of a man who wasted neither breath nor gesture.

In the front passenger seat, Number Seven kept his eyes forward on the windscreen as the convoy merged cleanly onto the airport expressway ramp. The driver at the wheel did not speak, turn his head, or glance into the rearview mirror.

That silence was built into the bedrock of the Black Pine Guard.

Across the entire organization, from coastal watchers to field specialists in mountain counties, there were no surnames, no given names, and no personal histories. Every member existed solely as a number: Number One, Number Seven, Number Nine, Number Eleven, and down through the quiet ranks. No member of the Black Pine Guard knew another's real name, nor did any member see another's unguarded face outside strict operational necessity. Even Number One, coordinating the network behind unbroken encryption, held no roster of real identities. Every thread of loyalty terminated at a single point: the Boss.

"Report," Chen Xuan said softly, resting his forearm along the armrest.

"Three minutes ago, the Cloud Gate protocol cleared Qingyan Executive Aviation Terminal," Number Seven said, crisp and measured. "Your aircraft has completed pre-flight checks, fuel load is topped for a direct run with reserve, and the crew is seated on Runway Three. Advance escort from Number Nine's team has cleared the southern service gate. We will reach the apron in nineteen minutes."

Chen Xuan looked out the tinted window as Qingyan's glowing skyline slid backward into the night. "And Yunxi?"

"The moment your three-word dispatch hit the relay, the Yunxi regional station entered silent alert," Number Seven answered. "Two minutes ago, our local watcher confirmed Chen Mingde was brought into Old Street Police Station at two thirteen this afternoon. The intake log lists preliminary detention for intentional assault against a site supervisor employed by Hongrui Real Estate."

Chen Xuan's eyes did not blink. "Where is Chen Mingde right now?"

"Still held in the rear interrogation wing of Old Street Station," Number Seven replied. "No formal prosecutor's warrant has been filed; the detention slip carries only the deputy director's signature. Chen Jianguo attempted to deliver blood pressure medication at three ten and was turned away at the outer gate. He returned to the family courtyard at three forty and remains indoors with Liu Meifang and Wu Lanying."

"Good," Chen Xuan murmured, the slight tightness around his jaw easing marginally. "Keep a quiet eye on the courtyard from outside the lane. Do not let anyone from Hongrui Real Estate or Old Street Station approach my parents' gate tonight."

"Already positioned, the Boss," Number Seven said. "Two watchers from the Lanshui-Yunxi circuit are parked two streets west of the courtyard. Nothing moves down that lane unseen."

Chen Xuan leaned back against the headrest, closing his eyes as the sedan accelerated smoothly down the open inner lane. Ahead, the lead car's brake lights never flashed; every traffic intersection between Qingyan South Park and the expressway had turned green three seconds before the convoy reached the white line, cleared quietly by municipal timing relays no ordinary commuter would notice.

"Who owns Hongrui Real Estate?" Chen Xuan asked without opening his eyes.

"On public registries, a local developer named Deng Yongchang holds sixty percent of the shares, with site enforcement on Old Street handled by former quarry contractor Yan Tiehu," Number Seven replied evenly. "However, our initial trace flagged three unusual capital transfers into Hongrui Real Estate over the past eighteen months. The funds did not originate in Yunxi County. They moved through shell accounts in Lanshui and two coastal free-trade zones."

Chen Xuan opened his eyes. In the tinted glass reflection, his gaze was cold and clear as winter water.

"Have the preliminary file printed before we board," Chen Xuan said quietly. "I want to read it on the plane."

"It is already printing in the cabin, the Boss," Number Seven answered.

Outside, the amber expressway lights stretched ahead in unbroken ribbons, guiding the three black sedans swiftly toward the dark horizon of Qingyan Airport.
