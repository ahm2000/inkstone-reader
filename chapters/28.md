# Chapter 28: The Leaderboard

Chen Xuan wanted two things from this Monday at Hengxin Logistics, which were a quiet desk and a hot lunch.

Instead, he found half the department crowded around a brand-new board beside the printer.

Every name on the floor was on it, each with a score and a colored bar.

General Manager Sun Haoran had invented the contest himself and called it Employee of the Quarter.

The score counted on-time deliveries, fewer errors and extra hours, and on the last Friday of the month, colleagues would add their votes on paper slips.

The Wednesday meeting with Sun, the one Hu Dewei had made sound so grim, had turned out to be only a request. Haiyue Hospitality wanted a logistics supervisor to walk a visitor through the cargo flow next month, and Chen Xuan had said yes and forgotten it by lunch.

"Zhang Pengfei, first place!" Sister Pan read out loudly, tapping the longest green bar. "Ninety-two points!"

"Qian Ying from Billing is second!"

"And third is..." Sister Pan squinted and almost dropped her tea.

"Supervisor Chen?!"

"Third place?!"

The whole crowd turned to look at Chen Xuan, who was holding his scratched green thermos and hoping the printer would finish soon.

"He leaves at five minutes to five every day!"

"He has zero extra hours! Zero!"

"How is a man with zero extra hours only seven points behind a man with sixty-one?"

Somebody did that math in his head and felt a little dizzy.

"Must be nice," Lin Dong muttered from two desks over, not quite under his breath, "showing up at nine and strolling out at five and still beating people who actually try."

Nobody answered him, and nobody needed to.

Sister Pan, who ranked eleventh and called it "a clerical error," clapped her hands and pulled a sheet of paper out of her drawer.

"Place your bets, everybody! One bowl of noodles per bet, and the winner takes all the bowls! Who gets first place at the end of the month?"

Hands shot up all over the floor.

"Zhang Pengfei, obviously!"

"I will take Qian Ying, she never makes a mistake!"

"Chen Xuan," said two people who liked a gamble.

When the sheet came back, most names stood under Zhang Pengfei, a few under Qian Ying, and exactly two under Chen Xuan.

Zhang Pengfei stood beside the board in his crisp suit, and he did not gloat, which was the surprising part.

He stared at the third bar. Zero extra hours, and only seven points behind him? Did someone upstairs favor Chen Xuan? Or did that quiet man know a trick that nobody else knew?

Whatever the answer was, Zhang Pengfei would not slow down!

To be honest, he was not a bad man, and he really did stay late. Last Thursday he had stayed to teach a nervous intern how to build a spreadsheet table, and then he had paid for the intern's dinner.

When he was a boy, his mother had told him again and again that a person with no uncle in the city must arrive earlier and leave later than everybody else. Sixty-one extra hours was not greed. It was a rule his mother had drilled into him.

He walked up to Chen Xuan with a small, stiff smile.

"Supervisor Chen, bet with me. Whoever ranks lower on the board buys the other one lunch."

"Oh, nice offer," Chen Xuan said kindly. "But I have a thermos."

Silence.

"What does a thermos have to do with it?!" Sister Pan cried.

"Hot water, tea, and lunch at noon," Chen Xuan said. "Same as always. I'm fine."

Zhang Pengfei blinked. He could not tell whether he had been refused or insulted, and he went back to his desk still unsure.

---

Mid-morning, a junior clerk named Little Zhou was turning pale at his screen.

A full container of frozen dumplings was stuck at Qingyan Port, and if it sat there too long, Hengxin would pay for every melted dumpling!

"The agent says the customs code doesn't match!" he whispered. "I have checked it twice!"

Chen Xuan was walking past with a refill of hot water.

"Hmm?" He looked at the screen. "Line three. The code has one digit swapped."

Click.

He typed the correct digit, attached the corrected form, and sent a short, polite note to the port agent: *Sorry for the trouble. Corrected form attached. Please release when convenient.* The whole thing took about two minutes.

At twenty-six minutes past ten, the container released.

Little Zhou stared at the screen and decided the system had fixed itself, and Chen Xuan was already back at his desk, drinking his tea.

Ten minutes later, Department Manager Hu Dewei found a file on his desk, with a yellow note stuck on the top page.

In neat handwriting it said: *Container released. The error was in line three. A second check of code lines on Mondays would help.* There was no name.

Hu Dewei read it three times, and his hands began to shake.

"Who is writing these?" he whispered to the empty office.

The notes had appeared in his tray for months, and every single one had been correct.

Each time, he sat up a little straighter afterward, and he could not say why. He put the file in his drawer, then took it out again and held it with both hands.

At one in the afternoon, Chen Xuan stood by the printer with his thermos, studying the board and thinking about noodles.

Auntie Lu came down the hallway pushing her cleaning cart. She was sixty-one, small and tidy, and the little radio on her cart never seemed to play anything.

"Don't stand under that vent, Supervisor Chen," she said, not looking up. "It drips on Mondays."

"Oh. Thank you, Auntie."

He took one step to the left.

Two seconds later, a single fat drop fell from the vent and splashed on the carpet exactly where his shoes had been.

Chen Xuan looked at the wet spot, then at the dry ceiling, which had never dripped before today.

"Hmm," he said.

Auntie Lu pushed her cart on toward the pantry.

Late that afternoon, every phone on the floor buzzed at once.

*Reminder from the General Manager: Voting for Employee of the Quarter will take place on the last Friday of the month. The winner will have lunch with our guest from Haiyue Hospitality. Please do your best to make a good impression. Sun Haoran.*

"A guest from Haiyue!" Sister Pan cried. "What guest? Who's the guest?!"

Nobody knew. Zhang Pengfei straightened in his chair, and across the room, Hu Dewei had gone gray.

At five minutes to five, Chen Xuan put on his coat.

"Lunch with a stranger," he thought, a little worried. "That sounds like a very long lunch."

He walked out with his thermos. Behind him, Zhang Pengfei opened his report to check it a fourth time, and nobody on the floor could answer the question on every mind: who was coming from Haiyue?
