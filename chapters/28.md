# Chapter 28: The Unthinkable Ledger

Under the cold moonlight washing across the rooftop of Building 2, Chen Xuan slipped a matte-black bone-conduction earpiece out of the tablet's side slot and fitted it behind his left ear. He tapped the encrypted audio icon once.

A soft, clean click sounded in his ear, followed immediately by Number One's voice.

"Boss," Number One said. The tone was measured and disciplined, as it had been for seven years, yet beneath the professionalism lay a tight, grim gravity that Chen Xuan had never heard before. "I apologize for triggering the priority channel at this hour. Once you see the fourth folder, you will understand why we could not wait until dawn."

"Walk me through it from the start," Chen Xuan replied quietly, his thumb already sliding upward across the cold glass. "Do not skip a link."

"Yes, Boss," Number One answered. "Folder One covers Second Uncle Chen Mingde's detention and the Old Street demolition order. On Friday afternoon, Yan Tiehu and seven hired men smashed Chen Mingde's hardware shop under direct instructions from Cheng Biao, the nominal head of Hongrui Real Estate. Within forty minutes, officers from Old Street Station arrived on a prearranged call, ignored three eyewitnesses, and detained Chen Mingde on a fabricated assault charge. The paperwork was green-lit by County Deputy Deng Yongchang, with quiet sign-offs from Zhou Quan, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong."

Chen Xuan scrolled slowly through high-resolution photographs of the forged incident reports, call-tower logs, and a scanned transfer slip showing cash payments routed to Yan Tiehu and Ma Er through a defunct timber company.

"That part is ordinary county rot," Chen Xuan observed calmly. "Why did they want his specific shop so urgently that they risked a weekday daytime arrest?"

"Because Hongrui Real Estate never cared about the street frontage," Number One said. "Open Folder Two, Boss."

Chen Xuan tapped the second tab. The screen shifted to a cross-sectional acoustic and ground-penetrating radar map rendered in pale blue and amber lines. Directly beneath the footprint of Chen Mingde's hardware shop sat a capped, steel-reinforced ventilation shaft built in the late nineteen-seventies.

"Number Fourteen and Number Nineteen ran thermal and seismic sweeps along Old Street between two and four o'clock this morning," Number One continued. "Old Street sits directly over a decommissioned municipal civil-defense tunnel network. On paper, that tunnel was sealed with poured concrete twenty-two years ago. In reality, someone excavated the collapse walls, reinforced the ceiling arches with industrial steel ribs, and laid a narrow-gauge electric rail track running three point four kilometers due east."

Chen Xuan's thumb moved along the glowing map line, following the subterranean corridor past the edge of town until it terminated beneath a corrugated warehouse directly adjacent to Pier Four.

"The night barges," Chen Xuan murmured, glancing up for half a second toward the distant white floodlights burning along the eastern curve of the Yunxi River.

"Yes, Boss," Number One confirmed. "Three nights a week, municipal dredging barges moor at Pier Four under environmental maintenance permits signed by provincial transport officials. Our infrared drones caught the loading chutes opening beneath the waterline at one-fifteen in the morning. They are not moving river silt. Number Twenty-three pulled metallurgical samples from the runoff sludge and matched the manifest codes. They are moving refined heavy rare-earth oxides stripped off the books from state strategic mines in three inland provinces, barged downriver to coastal transfer ships without a single customs log."

The wind swept across the concrete roof, rustling the collar of Chen Xuan's dark windbreaker. His expression did not change. His thumb tapped Folder Three.

At once, the screen unfolded into a dense, branching web of corporate trusts, clearing accounts, and provincial ledger entries that stretched across the display like dark roots under dead soil.

"Hongrui Real Estate is only the lowest brick in the wall," Number One said, his voice dropping half a register. "Behind Cheng Biao and Deng Yongchang sit seven layers of shell trusts registered through provincial asset-management firms. Every single one terminates at Shengyuan Capital. Once our financial audit cells broke Shengyuan Capital's shadow clearing ledger on Saturday night, we realized Yunxi County was only one minor transit valve."

Chen Xuan scrolled down page after page of verified clearinghouse reconciliations, stamped land-reserve swaps, state infrastructure bond diversions, and offshore settlement receipts. Fourteen provincial seals appeared in the margins, one after another, stretching across twenty unbroken years.

"State the verified total," Chen Xuan said softly.

"Across fourteen provinces, over twenty years of continuous extraction through rigged infrastructure debt, off-book rare-earth diversion, energy-grid procurement markups, and shadow banking channels," Number One spoke slowly, enunciating each word with cold precision, "the cross-matched sovereign drain confirmed by our audit cells stands at one hundred and four point six trillion yuan."

Even in the open night air, the number felt monstrous in its sheer, bottomless scale. One hundred trillion yuan was not a corrupt mayor pocketing kickbacks on a bridge or a county deputy selling a demolition zone. It was the systematic hollowing out of a nation's treasury, bled drop by drop across two decades until the vessels themselves were brittle.

Yet Chen Xuan did not raise his voice. He did not curse, did not pace, did not even tighten his grip on the matte-black frame of the tablet. His dark eyes simply moved downward to the bottom of the menu, where a fourth folder sat bordered in thin, stark red.

"Open Folder Four," Chen Xuan said.

On the line, Number One hesitated for half a second before releasing the final encryption layer. "Boss, Number Thirty-four and Number Forty-two intercepted the courier relay linked to Shengyuan Capital's offshore clearing hub six hours ago. Moving over one hundred trillion yuan and thousands of tons of strategic rare earths out of the country requires offshore banking immunity and foreign political shelter. Money alone was no longer enough to buy that protection."

The red-bordered folder opened across the screen.

Inside were high-resolution copies of classified technical schematics, cryptographic packet headers, and dated handoff receipts bearing the digital watermarks of senior defense and energy institutions.

Chen Xuan scrolled through them one by one, unhurried, reading every line.

Phased-array naval radar frequency-hopping calibration tables. Regional ultra-high-voltage power-grid transformer load vulnerabilities mapped down to individual relay substations. Coastal garrison rotation schedules and littoral blind-spot charts, packaged into encrypted maritime relay bursts and routed to foreign intelligence handlers through offshore shell brokerages and diplomatic pouches.

Beside each transfer sat a payment clearance code linking straight back to Shengyuan Capital's overseas trust accounts.

Chen Xuan's expression never turned frantic or loud. Not a single muscle in his jaw twitched. He stood completely still beneath the cold moonlight, his gaze resting quietly on the naval radar calibration sheet and the neat rows of countersigned authorization numbers underneath it.

Yet ten paces away, standing motionless in the dark shadow of the stairwell housing, Number Seven felt the air on the concrete rooftop suddenly grow sharp and thin, as though the temperature had dropped ten degrees in a single breath.

Number Seven had served in the Black Pine Guard for nine years. He had seen gunfights, border ambushes, and corporate wars that toppled listed conglomerates overnight. Nothing he had ever witnessed carried the quiet, suffocating weight of the stillness now radiating from the man in the plain dark windbreaker standing beside a scratched green thermos.

Above them, a thin cloud drifted clear of the moon. Its pale light fell across Chen Xuan's calm, unreadable eyes as he looked down at the price a handful of traitors had placed on the country's throat.
