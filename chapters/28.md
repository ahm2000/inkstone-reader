# Chapter 28: The Unthinkable Ledger

Chen Xuan slipped the matte-black bone-conduction earpiece from the tablet's side slot, fitted it behind his left ear, and tapped the encrypted audio icon once.

A soft click sounded against his temple, followed at once by Number 1's voice.

"I apologize for triggering the priority channel at this hour," Number 1 said. His voice stayed disciplined, yet beneath the precision lay a grim, tight gravity Chen Xuan had never heard before. "Once you open the fourth folder, you will see why we could not wait until dawn."

"Walk me through it from the start," Chen Xuan replied quietly, sliding his thumb across the glass. "Do not skip a link."

"Folder One covers Second Uncle Chen Mingde's detention and the Old Street demolition order," Number 1 began. "On Friday afternoon, Yan Tiehu and seven hired men smashed Chen Mingde's hardware shop on instructions from Cheng Biao, nominal head of Hongrui Real Estate. Forty minutes later, Old Street Station officers arrived on a prearranged call, ignored three eyewitnesses, and booked Chen Mingde on a fabricated assault charge. County Deputy Deng Yongchang cleared the paperwork, with quiet sign-offs from Zhou Quan, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong."

Chen Xuan scrolled past forged incident reports, call-tower logs, and a transfer slip routing cash to Yan Tiehu and Ma Er through a defunct timber company.

"That much is ordinary county rot," Chen Xuan observed flatly. "Why risk a weekday daytime arrest over one small shop?"

"Because Hongrui Real Estate never cared about the street frontage," Number 1 said. "Open Folder Two."

Chen Xuan tapped the second tab. A ground-penetrating radar map appeared in blue and amber lines. Directly beneath Chen Mingde's shop sat a capped, steel-reinforced ventilation shaft built in the late nineteen-seventies.

"Number 14 and Number 19 ran thermal and seismic sweeps along Old Street between two and four o'clock this morning," Number 1 continued. "Old Street sits over a decommissioned municipal civil-defense tunnel. On paper, it was sealed with concrete twenty-two years ago. In reality, someone cleared the collapse walls, braced the arches with steel ribs, and laid a narrow-gauge electric rail running three point four kilometers due east."

Chen Xuan traced the glowing corridor past the edge of town until it ended beneath a corrugated warehouse beside Pier Four.

"The night barges," Chen Xuan murmured, glancing briefly toward the white floodlights along the river bend.

"Yes," Number 1 confirmed. "Three nights a week, municipal dredging barges moor at Pier Four under environmental permits signed by provincial transport officials. Our infrared drones caught underwater loading chutes opening at one-fifteen in the morning. They are not hauling river silt. Number 23 pulled metallurgical samples from the runoff sludge and matched the manifest codes. They are moving refined heavy rare-earth oxides stripped off the books from state strategic mines in three inland provinces, barged downriver to coastal transfer ships with zero customs logs."

"How long has that rail been running?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"At least nine years based on rail wear," Number 1 answered, his tone hardening. "Two weeks ago, a drainage leak above the ventilation shaft forced them to dig from street level. Chen Mingde's shop sat directly over the primary access cap. When he refused to sell, Deng Yongchang ordered Cheng Biao to clear him out before the next barge window."

Chen Xuan's face did not shift. He tapped Folder Three.

The screen unfolded into a branching web of corporate trusts, clearing accounts, and provincial ledger entries.

"Hongrui Real Estate is only the bottom brick," Number 1 said, his voice dropping half a register. "Behind Cheng Biao and Deng Yongchang sit seven layers of shell trusts terminating at Shengyuan Capital. When our audit cells cracked Shengyuan Capital's shadow clearing ledger on Saturday night, we realized Yunxi County was only one minor transit valve."

Chen Xuan scrolled past verified clearinghouse reconciliations, land-reserve swaps, bond diversions, and offshore settlement receipts stamped with fourteen provincial seals across twenty unbroken years.

"State the verified total," Chen Xuan said quietly.

Number 1 paused for a heavy breath before enunciating each word with cold precision.

"Across fourteen provinces, over twenty years of continuous extraction through rigged infrastructure debt, off-book rare-earth diversion, energy-grid procurement markups, and shadow banking, the confirmed sovereign drain stands at 104.6 trillion yuan."

The number hung in the midnight air, too vast for ordinary anger. It was not a corrupt official skimming a bridge contract. It was the systematic hollowing out of a nation's treasury across two decades.

Yet Chen Xuan did not raise his voice, pace, or tighten his grip on the tablet. His dark eyes simply dropped to the bottom of the menu, where a fourth folder sat bordered in stark red.

"Open Folder Four," Chen Xuan said.

Number 1 hesitated half a second before releasing the final lock. "Number 34 and Number 42 intercepted the courier relay tied to Shengyuan Capital's offshore clearing hub six hours ago. Moving over 100 trillion yuan and strategic rare earths out of the country requires offshore banking immunity and foreign political shelter. Money alone stopped being enough."

The red-bordered folder opened.

Inside sat classified technical schematics, cryptographic packet headers, and dated handoff receipts bearing the digital watermarks of senior defense and energy institutions.

Chen Xuan read every line without hurrying.

Phased-array naval radar frequency-hopping calibration tables. Regional ultra-high-voltage power-grid transformer load vulnerabilities mapped to individual relay substations. Coastal garrison rotation schedules and littoral blind-spot charts, routed to foreign intelligence handlers through offshore shell brokerages and diplomatic pouches. Beside every transfer sat a payment code linking straight back to Shengyuan Capital's overseas trust accounts.

"Are the institutional watermarks genuine?" Chen Xuan asked, his voice chillingly level.

"Triple-verified by Number 42," Number 1 replied, suppressed rage threading his clipped cadence. "They are not leaks from low-level clerks. Every packet carries live senior authorization hashes. Two more batches are queued for coastal transmission before Monday dawn."

Chen Xuan did not speak. Not a muscle in his jaw twitched. He stood utterly motionless, staring at the naval radar calibration sheet and the neat rows of countersigned authorization numbers underneath.

Ten paces away in the stairwell shadow, Number 7 could hear nothing from the earpiece, yet he suddenly felt the rooftop air turn thin and sharp, as though the temperature had dropped ten degrees in a single breath.

Number 7's spine locked straight without his permission, his gloved fingers curling tight against his palms. In nine years with Black Pine Guard, he had walked through gunfights, border ambushes, and corporate wars. Nothing he had ever faced carried the suffocating stillness radiating from the man in the plain dark windbreaker beside a scratched green thermos.

Under the pale moon, Chen Xuan's eyes stayed calm and unreadable as he looked down at the price a handful of traitors had placed on the country's throat.
