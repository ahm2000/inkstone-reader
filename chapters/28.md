# Chapter 28: The Unthinkable Ledger

Chen Xuan slipped the matte-black earpiece from the tablet's side slot, fitted it behind his left ear, and tapped the secure voice icon once.

A soft click sounded against his temple, followed at once by Number 1's voice.

"I am sorry for using the emergency line at this hour," Number 1 said. His voice stayed disciplined, yet beneath the calm lay a grim, heavy weight Chen Xuan had never heard before. "Once you open the fourth folder, you will see why we could not wait until dawn."

"Walk me through it from the start," Chen Xuan replied quietly, sliding his thumb across the glass. "Do not skip a single link."

"Folder One covers Second Uncle Chen Mingde's arrest and the Old Street demolition order," Number 1 began. "On Friday afternoon, Yan Tiehu and seven hired men smashed Chen Mingde's hardware shop on orders from Cheng Biao, the named head of Hongrui Real Estate. Forty minutes later, Old Street Station officers arrived on a planned call, ignored three witnesses, and locked Chen Mingde up on a fake assault charge. County Deputy Deng Yongchang cleared the papers, with quiet sign-offs from Zhou Quan, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong."

Chen Xuan scrolled past fake police reports, phone tower logs, and a bank slip paying Yan Tiehu and Ma Er through a closed timber company.

"That much is ordinary county rot," Chen Xuan observed flatly. "Why risk a daytime arrest over one small shop?"

"Because Hongrui Real Estate never cared about the shop front," Number 1 said. "Open Folder Two."

Chen Xuan tapped the second tab. An underground scan map appeared in blue and yellow lines. Right beneath Chen Mingde's shop sat a capped, steel-lined air shaft built in the late 1970s.

"Number 14 and Number 19 ran heat and ground scans along Old Street between two and four o'clock this morning," Number 1 continued. "Old Street sits over an old 1970s air-raid tunnel. On paper, it was sealed with concrete twenty-two years ago. In truth, someone cleared the rubble, braced the rock tunnel with steel ribs, and laid a small electric rail line running three point four kilometers due east."

Chen Xuan traced the glowing path past the edge of town until it ended beneath a metal warehouse beside Pier Four.

"The night barges," Chen Xuan murmured, glancing briefly toward the white floodlights along the river bend.

"Yes," Number 1 confirmed. "Three nights a week, river dredging barges dock at Pier Four under permits signed by provincial transport officials. Our night drones caught underwater loading chutes opening at one-fifteen in the morning. They are not hauling river mud. Number 23 tested runoff samples and matched the cargo codes. They are moving refined rare-earth metals taken off the books from state mines in three inland provinces, barged downriver to coastal transfer ships with zero customs records."

"How long has that rail been running?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"At least nine years based on rail wear," Number 1 answered, his tone hardening. "Two weeks ago, a water leak above the air shaft forced them to dig from street level. Chen Mingde's shop sat right over the main lid. When he refused to sell, Deng Yongchang ordered Cheng Biao to clear him out before the next barge window."

Chen Xuan's face did not change. He tapped Folder Three.

The screen opened into a branching web of shell companies, hidden accounts, and provincial ledger entries.

"Hongrui Real Estate is only the bottom brick," Number 1 said, his voice dropping lower. "Behind Cheng Biao and Deng Yongchang sit seven layers of shell companies ending at Shengyuan Capital. When our audit teams cracked Shengyuan Capital's secret books on Saturday night, we realized Yunxi County was only one tiny valve."

Chen Xuan scrolled past verified bank records, land swaps, diverted loans, and overseas payout slips stamped with fourteen provincial seals across twenty straight years.

"Give me the verified total," Chen Xuan said quietly.

Number 1 paused for a heavy breath before speaking each word with cold precision.

"Across fourteen provinces, over twenty years of steady theft through rigged building debt, off-the-books rare-earth smuggling, inflated power-grid supply bills, and hidden banking, the confirmed loss of state money stands at 104.6 trillion yuan."

The number hung in the midnight air, too huge for ordinary anger. This was not a greedy official skimming a bridge contract. It was the steady hollowing out of a nation's treasury over two full decades.

Yet Chen Xuan did not raise his voice, pace, or tighten his grip on the tablet. His dark eyes simply dropped to the bottom of the menu, where a fourth folder sat bordered in stark red.

"Open Folder Four," Chen Xuan said.

Number 1 paused half a second before releasing the final lock. "Number 34 and Number 42 caught the courier link tied to Shengyuan Capital's overseas hub six hours ago. Moving over 100 trillion yuan and state rare earths out of the country requires overseas banking shelter and foreign political protection. Money alone stopped being enough."

The red-bordered folder opened.

Inside sat top-secret blueprints, coded file headers, and dated handoff receipts bearing the official stamps of senior defense and energy offices.

Chen Xuan read every line without hurrying.

Warship radar frequency-switching charts. Regional high-voltage power-grid weak points mapped down to individual power stations. Coastal troop shift schedules and blind-spot maps, sent to foreign intelligence handlers through overseas shell brokers and diplomatic bags. Beside every transfer sat a payment code linking straight back to Shengyuan Capital's overseas accounts.

"Are the official stamps real?" Chen Xuan asked, his voice chillingly level.

"Triple-checked by Number 42," Number 1 replied, held-back anger threading his clipped voice. "They are not leaks from low-level clerks. Every file carries live senior signature records. Two more batches are lined up for coastal transmission before Monday dawn."

Chen Xuan did not speak. Not a muscle in his jaw twitched. He stood completely still, staring at the warship radar chart and the neat rows of countersigned approval numbers underneath.

Ten paces away in the stairwell shadow, Number 7 could hear nothing from the earpiece. Yet he suddenly felt the rooftop air turn thin and sharp, as though the temperature had dropped ten degrees in a single breath.

Number 7's spine locked straight without his permission, his gloved fingers curling tight against his palms. In nine years with Black Pine Guard, he had walked through gunfights, border ambushes, and corporate wars. Nothing he had ever faced carried the suffocating stillness radiating from the man in the plain dark jacket beside a scratched green thermos.

Under the pale moon, Chen Xuan's eyes stayed calm and unreadable as he looked down at the price a handful of traitors had placed on the country's throat.
