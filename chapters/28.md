# Chapter 28: Seating Order

At twenty past five, Fan Weiguo was still signing the directive to seal Lu Wenzhe's office. His secretary waited beside the desk with the official stamp.

"Faster."

Then Fan Weiguo's phone buzzed with an unfamiliar number.

"Secretary Fan." The voice on the line was young, polite, and entirely unhurried. "A retired leader is fishing at Qingshan Reservoir. Do not approach."

"Who is this? Which leader?"

The line clicked dead. The caller was Liang Yuhang, the retired leader's aide, who knew his guards would arrest anyone who came near. Fan Weiguo knew only that the accent was not the province's.

Ten minutes later, the provincial General Office called. The inspection team would arrive tomorrow, Monday, at nine, not Wednesday. No explanation was offered.

Fan Weiguo set the receiver down gently. A retired senior leader at the reservoir, followed by an inspection moved up two full days. For the first time in thirty years, his own county felt like someone else's house.

He thought of the black card in his bottom drawer. The last time an order had come with no name, two masked men had stood by his wall all night. He did not open the drawer. He finished signing, and his hand shook on the last stroke.

* * *

Chen Xuan sat on the front step with a steamed bun in one hand and his phone in the other.

*Mom: Your uncle slept nine hours and asked for more soup. Wear your thick jacket tomorrow. Did you eat any vegetables today? Don't lie to me.*

His expression softened.

He typed back, *Tell Second Uncle I'm glad he's eating well.* Then he added, *I had cabbage today,* and took a bite of his bun. It was stuffed with cabbage, so technically it was the truth.

His grandmother, Zhou Guilan, stepped outside carrying a quilt. "Thanks, Grandma," he mumbled, and within seconds he had drifted off sitting upright, crumbs still clinging to his chin.

She draped the quilt gently over his shoulders. "Too thin, Chen Xuan," she whispered. Beside the step, the family cat quietly licked up the fallen crumbs.

* * *

Dong Haisheng, head of Hongrui Real Estate, sat in his office with his collar unbuttoned, staring at a cup of tea that had long since gone cold.

His phone buzzed, and a hushed voice came through. "Dong Haisheng, it's Gu Min. Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu Wenzhe is telling everyone he's never set foot in your office."

"Never set foot in my office? He sat at my table last Thursday and ate my steamed crab."

The line went dead.

Before Dong Haisheng could hurl the phone across the room, it rang again. Cold sweat soaked through the back of his shirt.

"Dong Haisheng." Huang Boren, the financier everyone called Chairman Huang, always spoke softly, like a man addressing a sleeping child. "The provincial inspection has been moved up to tomorrow at nine. Is the Old Street mess settled?"

"The old shopkeeper is out, Chairman. He went home this morning."

"Which records hurt us most?"

"The Ankang ledgers. The clinic's fake injury slips, and every payment we wired for them."

"Then they must not exist by morning. Handle it tonight, Dong Haisheng. A fire in a storage room is just a fire. And collect the debt book your bookkeeper carries. My name is inside it."

"Xie Boyuan called me from a noodle shop. He's terrified."

"Frightened men do what they're told. Send him in with the kerosene bucket, and go watch to make sure it's done."

* * *

The night cleaner at Ankang Private Clinic had worked there for four months. He hummed quietly to himself as he swung his mop. He was one of the Black Pine Guard's men.

Two figures hurried down the corridor carrying a red plastic bucket. It sloshed heavily, reeking of kerosene.

One was the clinic's night manager. The other was Xie Boyuan, the bookkeeper, whom Number One had let slip away on Saturday night to see where he would run. A small brass abacus hung from his belt, and he clutched a black debt ledger tightly against his chest.

Xie Boyuan nudged the storage room door open with his shoulder.

Sitting on a metal stool in the center of the room was Mai Zhenhua, Director of the County Police Bureau. Four city inspectors stood behind him, and a bright camera light clicked on.

Mai Zhenhua's officers had seized the ledgers at ten o'clock that morning through the door the night cleaner had left unlocked. Dong Haisheng did not yet know. They had simply waited to see who would show up to burn them.

The city had given him one order that week: take the ledgers, and touch nothing else in Yunxi. Mai Zhenhua obeyed it exactly. Since Friday night, when masked men had held his SWAT team still in a hallway, he checked every door twice before he walked through it.

Mai Zhenhua nodded, keeping his face calm. "Good evening. You're late. The ledgers have been waiting."

The bucket slipped from their hands. Kerosene splashed across the concrete floor.

Mai Zhenhua opened the top ledger and traced a line with his finger.

"*Ankang Private Clinic to Hongrui Real Estate: eleven injury slips, three thousand yuan each.*" He flipped a page. "*Lu Wenzhe, consulting fee, eight hundred thousand per quarter, six quarters.*"

A young inspector tapped the figures into his phone and whispered, "Four point eight million. That's eighty-six years of a duty officer's salary."

In the doorway, Dong Haisheng stood frozen, his unlit cigarette dangling from his fingers. He had come to watch. Mai Zhenhua lifted his gaze to meet him.

"It was Dong Haisheng!" Xie Boyuan shrieked at once, pointing with the black debt ledger. "He ordered the fire! I told him the bucket was too heavy!"

"After everything I've paid you, you dirty rat," Dong Haisheng hissed. His open palm cracked across Xie Boyuan's cheek hard enough to make the brass abacus chime. Leaning in close, he dropped his voice to a venomous whisper. "Your mother lives alone on Willow Lane, third floor, no elevator. She goes out at seven every morning. Say one more word to them, and my men visit her door."

Every inspector in the room heard it, and every single one understood what it meant.

Xie Boyuan clutched his stinging cheek, shaking with fury and terror. "Paid me? You threw me eight hundred yuan a night and never once said thank you!"

"Dong Haisheng," Mai Zhenhua said evenly. "You aren't under arrest tonight, but the city commission requires you to remain in Yunxi. I'll be taking your passport."

Dong Haisheng surrendered his passport along with the Singapore plane ticket tucked inside it, moving slowly, like a man having a tooth pulled.

Back in his car, he dialed with fingers that would barely close around the phone.

"Chairman, they have the ledgers. And my passport."

"I see."

A brief silence followed, broken by the faint clink of porcelain against a saucer.

"Then I will send a man to your house tonight," Huang Boren said gently. "He will sit in the room with you. He will stay there until you can never again speak to an inspector. How long that takes is entirely up to you."

Dong Haisheng understood immediately. Either he stayed silent, or the man would make him silent.

The phone slipped from his damp hand.

Before the phone even finished sliding from his hand, the gentle voice on the other end cut off with the sharp crash of splintering oak and heavy boots pouring into a villa across Qingyan City.

"National Security and Joint Anti-Corruption Task Force! Hands on the floor! Huang Boren, you are under arrest!"

The line went dead.

Outside Dong Haisheng's sedan, three unmarked black vehicles boxed the alley shut before he could even turn his ignition key. Armed joint-task-force officers pulled his door open and snapped steel handcuffs around his wrists. Every enforcer, every corrupt banker at Huaxin Bank, and every hidden backer in Huang Boren's network was swept up in the exact same minute. Not a single hand was left free anywhere in the province to touch Chen Xuan, his family, or Old Street.
