# Chapter 171: Dinner at the Harbor Villa

At half past seven on Wednesday evening, a plain blue-and-white Shenzhen taxi turned off the coastal road and rolled up to the tall iron gates of Shen Wanshan's private harbor villa.

Across the dark water of Shenzhen Bay, the glass towers of Shenzhen Bay No. 1 and the tall spire of Ping An Finance Center glowed against the night sky. Warm salt air drifted in from Shekou, carrying the distant hum of cargo ships heading toward Hong Kong.

At the villa's stone archway, four uniformed guards straightened up, expecting a black Mercedes or a Rolls-Royce. When an ordinary metered taxi braked softly beside the fountain, the lead guard blinked in disbelief.

The rear doors opened. Qin Yuhan stepped out first in a crisp dark-navy tailored suit, her heels clicking cleanly on the wet stone. Right behind her stepped Chen Xuan in a plain white cotton shirt and dark trousers, paying the driver in cash.

"Wait two blocks down by the banyan trees if you do not mind," Chen Xuan told the driver with an easy nod, handing over an extra 200 yuan note. "Dinner should not take long."

"No problem at all, sir!" the driver grinned, pulling away down the palm-lined lane.

Two blocks back in the deep shadows beneath those same banyan trees, twelve matte-white sedans sat parked in a neat, silent line with their headlights dark. None of the villa's gate guards noticed them.

Even closer, within fifty meters of Chen Xuan, two silent figures in plain matte-black gear and full black masks stood motionless along the low sea wall behind the garden hedge. A Black Guard member shifted slightly to track the upper balcony, while the second guard watched the side courtyard gate without making a sound.

The lead villa guard hurried forward, his polite smile turning stiff as he eyed Chen Xuan's simple clothes.

"Good evening, President Qin," the guard said, bowing to Qin Yuhan before glancing doubtfully at Chen Xuan. "Master Shen is waiting in the waterside hall. And this gentleman is?"

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan replied evenly, her chin high. "He is with me."

"Please follow me," the guard murmured, leading them through the lantern-lit garden.

Wide stone corridors ran beside a koi pond, opening directly into a grand waterside dining hall with floor-to-ceiling windows facing the bay.

At the head of a massive round rosewood table sat a silver-haired man in his late sixties wearing a dark silk Tang jacket. Shen Wanshan, patriarch of the Shen Family and head of a 140 billion yuan empire, sat calmly rolling two polished walnuts in his left palm.

To his right sat Shen Jingzhou, his eldest grandson, wearing a wine-red blazer and staring at Chen Xuan with cold, undisguised venom.

To Shen Wanshan's left sat a lean, polished man in his late forties wearing a tailored charcoal suit and thin gold-rimmed glasses. Koo Tianming looked like a university professor from Hong Kong, though his eyes were sharp and cold as winter steel.

"President Qin!" Shen Wanshan rose with a broad, welcoming smile. "Forgive an old man for asking you to come out to the bay on a Wednesday night. Shenzhen's sea breeze is much kinder here than down in Futian CBD."

"Chairman Shen is too polite," Qin Yuhan answered smoothly, taking the seat opposite him without a trace of nerves. "You invited me to talk business, so I came."

Chen Xuan pulled out the chair beside Qin Yuhan and sat down calmly, looking around the grand dining hall with the mild curiosity of a tourist visiting a nice hotel.

Shen Jingzhou let out a quiet snort. "Grandfather, did I not tell you? President Qin really brings her shipping clerk everywhere. Next time she visits MixC Mall, he will probably carry her handbag too."

"Jingzhou, mind your manners," Shen Wanshan said mildly, though there was zero real scolding in his tone. He turned his gaze onto Chen Xuan, his smile thinning as he studied the young man's ordinary face. "So this is Mister Chen Xuan from Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai. I have heard quite a bit about you since Monday afternoon at Shenzhen Pier Nine."

"Nothing worth mentioning," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Just trying to keep six containers on schedule so I do not lose my monthly bonus."

A quiet chuckle came from beside Shen Wanshan. Koo Tianming lifted a small clay teapot and poured pale amber tea into two porcelain cups, sliding them toward Qin Yuhan and Chen Xuan.

"A shipping clerk who cares deeply about his monthly bonus," Koo Tianming said in smooth Mandarin with a slight Hong Kong accent. "Yet when Shenzhen Pier Nine closed its gates, the port inspection office cleared the Haiyue Star before five o'clock that very evening. Tell me, Mister Chen, how does a clerk from a small Shanghai office make Shenzhen officials move so fast?"

The dining hall went very still. Outside the glass doors, waves lapped softly against the stone pier.

Qin Yuhan did not touch her tea. She kept her eyes steady on Koo Tianming, watching how cleanly the man tried to set verbal traps.

Chen Xuan picked up his porcelain cup, sniffed the warm steam, and took an unhurried sip.

"Good tea," Chen Xuan remarked simply. "As for Pier Nine, the ship passed her safety check in Shanghai four days earlier. When inspectors follow the law, ships move on time. There is no mystery in that."

Koo Tianming smiled faintly, tapping one finger against his cup. "A neat answer. Still, I asked a few friends in Shanghai about Hengxin Logistics. Nice little company near the old docks, nowhere near Lujiazui or Jin Mao Tower. Yet your name has no family background on paper, no shares in any listed firm, and no relatives in Beijing. Tell me honestly, Mister Chen. Which elder in Shanghai or Beijing sent you down to Shenzhen to back Haiyue Group?"

Shen Wanshan leaned forward, resting his forearms on the polished rosewood. His eyes narrowed like an old hawk watching a rabbit.

"Speak plainly, young man," Shen Wanshan said, pulling a fine cigarette from a jade box and lighting it with a silver lighter. Gray smoke curled between them. "In Shenzhen, everyone respects real backing. If you speak for a big house in Shanghai or a minister in Beijing, put the name on the table. Old Shen knows how to show respect."

Shen Jingzhou leaned in eagerly, waiting for Chen Xuan to drop some grand name or start stammering in fear.

Chen Xuan set his teacup down gently and looked straight back at Shen Wanshan through the curling smoke.

"Nobody sent me," Chen Xuan said in a plain, relaxed voice. "I rent a small apartment in Shanghai, eat noodles downstairs when I get off work, and take the bus when it rains. You invited President Qin for dinner, Chairman Shen. Are we going to eat, or are we going to spend all evening talking about my resume?"

For two full seconds, nobody spoke.

Shen Jingzhou's face reddened with sudden anger. "You arrogant little clerk, who do you think you are talking to?"

"Enough, Jingzhou," Koo Tianming interrupted softly, raising one slender hand. His smile stayed on his lips, yet the warmth vanished completely from behind his gold-rimmed glasses. "Mister Chen is right. We should not let the dishes grow cold."

Koo Tianming glanced down at the gold watch on his wrist. The minute hand clicked onto eight o'clock.

"Bring out the wine," Koo Tianming called lightly toward the side corridor. "Tonight is a special night for both Shanghai and Shenzhen. Let us share a toast that President Qin will never forget."
