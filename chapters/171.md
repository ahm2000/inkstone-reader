# Chapter 171: Dinner at the Harbor Villa

A crisp late November breeze swept across the dark bay, carrying the salt tang of the harbor along the cliff road. Inside the quiet sedan, Qin Yuhan checked her wristwatch in the amber dashboard glow before glancing at the passenger seat.

Chen Xuan leaned back with his eyes half closed, holding his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos between his palms. He took a slow sip of warm chrysanthemum tea and sighed in comfort.

"You are coming to a formal shipping banquet, not a neighborhood chess club," Qin Yuhan said, though her lips curved slightly. "Leave the green thermos in the car, Xuan. If Shen Wanshan sees it, he will spend all evening mocking Haiyue Group."

"Then it stays right here," Chen Xuan replied, tucking the thermos into the door pocket and checking that his single smartphone was in his charcoal jacket. "Still, you owe me a quiet Friday night at Emerald Riverside Villa. I had fresh pork ribs marinating, and now I get two hours of polite smiles and lukewarm soup."

"I will make it up to you," Qin Yuhan said softly as she guided the sedan toward the iron gates of Harbor Villa. "Shen Wanshan insisted on a family dinner and asked that I bring my husband. He is clearly planning something, and I feel steadier with you beside me."

In her tailored camel coat and dark silk dress, she carried the poised dignity of Haiyue Group's president from the 38th floor of Haiyue Tower, yet tonight her fingers gripped the wheel a shade tighter.

"Consider my overtime paid," Chen Xuan said with an easy grin. "Just eat well and let the old man talk."

At the stone gatehouse, an older gatekeeper in a thick knitted vest stepped out with a warm, crinkly smile, leaning toward Chen Xuan's window like a familiar neighborhood uncle.

"Chilly wind off the water tonight, young fellow," the older man said cheerfully. "Keep your wife warm on the steps. The kitchen steamed fresh sea bass an hour ago, so do not skip the fish."

"Thanks, Uncle," Chen Xuan replied naturally. "Stay warm out here."

Qin Yuhan smiled as she drove up the gravel sweep toward the sprawling stone villa perched above the black water. "Even the gatekeeper here likes you. You really have a way of chatting with everyone."

"People are easy to get along with when you do not put on airs," Chen Xuan said casually, adjusting his collar as she parked beside a row of black luxury sedans.

A pair of stone lions guarded the broad front steps, illuminated by brass lanterns flickering in the cool autumn draft. Before Qin Yuhan could reach for her own door handle, Chen Xuan stepped around the hood, opened the door for her, and offered his arm with an easy smile. She tucked her hand into the crook of his elbow, feeling the steady warmth through his wool sleeve.

The warmth vanished the moment they stepped inside the grand dining hall. Tall arched windows overlooked the black bay, and a bright crystal chandelier lit a long rosewood table laid with fine porcelain and untouched platters of coastal seafood. At its head sat Shen Wanshan in a burgundy silk jacket with a heavy gold watch at his wrist. Beside him sat a lean, cold-eyed man in a bespoke charcoal suit.

"President Qin, right on time," Shen Wanshan said with a rehearsed smile, his gaze sliding past her to Chen Xuan's simple jacket with open amusement. "And your husband, the logistics clerk from Hengxin. It is good for a young man to see how real business works."

"Good evening, Chairman Shen," Qin Yuhan answered evenly, keeping her arm linked with Chen Xuan's.

"This is Mr. Kwok Wing-fai," Shen Wanshan said, gesturing to the lean man. "He represents our Hong Kong shipping partners and offshore financing group."

Kwok gave a thin nod without standing. "Please sit, President Qin. Neither the tide nor the market waits."

They had barely sat down when a heavy thud shook the marble floor. The tall oak double doors had been swung shut and latched from the inside. From behind the stone pillars and side alcoves, a dozen burly private guards in dark suits stepped out, silently ringing the hall.

Qin Yuhan straightened immediately, her voice turning icy. "Chairman Shen, is this how you host dinner guests?"

Shen Wanshan chuckled, dropping all pretense. He slid a thick navy legal folder across the polished rosewood until it stopped directly before Qin Yuhan.

"Let us skip the small talk," Shen Wanshan said, leaning forward. "Haiyue Group holds three deep-water berth rights and four prime southern coastal shipping routes. Inside that folder is a transfer agreement. My family and Mr. Kwok's consortium will pay you 150 million yuan for the full package."

Qin Yuhan stared at him in sharp disbelief. "150 million yuan? Those routes and berths are worth over 2.4 billion yuan. That is daylight robbery."

"Call it risk management," Kwok said smoothly in accented Mandarin. "Starting tomorrow morning, Haiyue's southern cargo vessels will face endless port delays, missing insurers, and sudden customs holds. Within sixty days, penalty clauses will bleed Haiyue dry. Sign tonight and walk away intact."

"You are threatening me in your own house," Qin Yuhan said, rising halfway. "I will not sign a single page."

Two guards behind her stepped closer. Shen Wanshan calmly lifted a crystal glass of aged baijiu toward the chandelier.

"Sit down, President Qin," Shen Wanshan said coldly. "Nobody leaves Harbor Villa until the ink dries. And your quiet little clerk should stay seated if he wants to go home in one piece."

Qin Yuhan's fingers tightened on the table edge, worried Chen Xuan might panic.

Instead, Chen Xuan remained completely relaxed. He picked up a plump steamed prawn with his chopsticks and placed it neatly onto her plate. Beneath the tablecloth, his left thumb tapped his single smartphone screen once.

"Eat a few shrimp while they are hot, Yuhan," Chen Xuan said calmly. "I have a feeling our host is about to lose his appetite."
