# Chapter 171: Dinner at the Harbor Villa

By the following evening, a crisp late November breeze swept across the dark bay, carrying the salt tang of the harbor along the winding coastal cliff road. Inside the quiet sedan, Qin Yuhan checked her wristwatch in the amber glow of the dashboard before glancing toward the passenger seat.

Chen Xuan leaned back with his eyes half closed, cradling his green metal thermos between his palms. He took a slow sip of warm chrysanthemum tea and let out a contented sigh.

"You are coming to a formal shipping dinner, not a neighborhood chess club," Qin Yuhan said, though the corner of her mouth curved upward. "Leave the thermos in the car, Xuan. If Shen Wanshan sees it, he will spend the entire evening mocking Haiyue Group."

"Then it stays right here," Chen Xuan replied, tucking the thermos into the door pocket and patting his charcoal jacket to make sure his phone was inside. "Still, you owe me a quiet weekend once we fly back to Emerald Riverside Villa. I was looking forward to slow-braising duck with taro in our own kitchen, and instead we are stuck in Shenzhen for two hours of polite smiles and lukewarm soup."

"I will make it up to you as soon as we get home to Shanghai," Qin Yuhan said softly as she guided the sedan toward the tall iron gates of Harbor Villa. "Shen Wanshan insisted on a family dinner and specifically asked that I bring my husband. He is clearly planning something, and I feel much steadier with you beside me."

In her tailored camel coat and dark silk dress, she carried all the poised dignity of Haiyue Group's president, yet tonight her fingers gripped the steering wheel a shade tighter than usual.

"Consider my overtime paid," Chen Xuan said with an easy grin. "Just eat well and let the old man talk."

At the stone gatehouse, an older gatekeeper in a thick knitted vest stepped out with a warm, crinkly smile, leaning toward Chen Xuan's window like a familiar neighborhood uncle.

"Chilly wind off the water tonight, young fellow," the older man said cheerfully. "Keep your wife warm on those front steps. The kitchen has fresh steamed grouper ready for the table tonight, cooked just right, so do not skip the fish."

"Thanks, Uncle," Chen Xuan replied naturally. "Stay warm out here."

Qin Yuhan smiled as she drove up the gravel sweep toward the sprawling stone villa perched above the black water. "Even the gatekeeper here takes an instant liking to you. You really have a way of getting along with everyone."

"People are easy to chat with when you do not put on airs," Chen Xuan said casually, smoothing his collar as she parked beside a row of sleek black luxury sedans.

A pair of carved stone lions guarded the broad front steps, illuminated by brass lanterns flickering in the cool autumn draft. Before Qin Yuhan could reach for her own door handle, Chen Xuan stepped around the hood, opened the passenger side for her, and offered his arm with an unhurried smile. She tucked her hand into the crook of his elbow, drawing quiet reassurance from the steady warmth through his wool sleeve.

That warmth vanished the moment they crossed the threshold into the grand dining hall. Tall arched windows overlooked the dark bay, and a bright crystal chandelier lit a long rosewood table laid with fine porcelain and untouched platters of coastal seafood. At its head sat Shen Wanshan in a burgundy silk jacket with a heavy gold watch resting on his wrist. Beside him sat a lean, cold-eyed man in a bespoke charcoal suit.

"President Qin, right on time," Shen Wanshan said with a rehearsed smile, his gaze sliding past her to Chen Xuan's plain jacket with open amusement. "And your husband, the logistics clerk from Hengxin. It is good for a young man to see how real coastal business is conducted."

"Good evening, Chairman Shen," Qin Yuhan answered evenly, keeping her arm linked with Chen Xuan's.

"Allow me to introduce Mr. Kwok Wing-fai," Shen Wanshan said, gesturing toward the lean man beside him. "He represents our Hong Kong shipping partners and offshore financing group."

Kwok offered a thin nod without bothering to stand. "Please sit, President Qin. Neither the tide nor the market waits for anyone."

They had barely taken their seats when a heavy thud vibrated through the marble floor. The tall oak double doors at the far end of the hall had been swung shut and latched from the inside. From behind the stone pillars and side alcoves, a dozen burly private guards in dark suits stepped out, silently ringing the room.

Qin Yuhan straightened at once, her voice turning icy. "Chairman Shen, is this how you host dinner guests?"

Shen Wanshan chuckled, dropping every shred of courtesy. He slid a thick navy legal folder across the polished rosewood until it stopped directly in front of Qin Yuhan.

"Let us skip the polite small talk," Shen Wanshan said, leaning forward on his elbows. "Haiyue Group holds three deep-water berth rights and four prime southern coastal shipping routes. Inside that folder is a transfer agreement. My family and Mr. Kwok's consortium will pay you 150 million yuan for the full package."

Qin Yuhan stared at him in sharp disbelief. "150 million yuan? Those routes and berths are worth over 2.4 billion yuan. That is daylight robbery."

"Think of it as risk management," Kwok said smoothly in accented Mandarin. "Starting tomorrow morning, Haiyue's southern cargo vessels will run into endless port delays, vanishing insurers, and sudden customs holds. Within sixty days, penalty clauses will bleed Haiyue dry. Sign tonight and walk away intact."

"You are threatening me in your own house," Qin Yuhan said, rising halfway from her chair. "I will not sign a single page."

Two guards standing behind her stepped closer. Shen Wanshan calmly lifted a crystal glass of aged baijiu toward the chandelier.

"Sit down, President Qin," Shen Wanshan said coldly. "Nobody leaves Harbor Villa until the ink dries. And your mild-mannered husband should stay right in his chair if he wants to walk out in one piece."

Qin Yuhan's fingers tightened on the edge of the table, worried that Chen Xuan might panic.

Instead, Chen Xuan remained completely relaxed. He picked up a plump steamed prawn with his chopsticks and placed it neatly onto her porcelain plate. Beneath the tablecloth, his left thumb tapped his phone screen once.

"Eat a few shrimp while they are hot, Yuhan," Chen Xuan said calmly. "I have a feeling our host is about to lose his appetite."
