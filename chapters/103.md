# Chapter 103: Grocery Shopping at IFC Mall

Ten minutes later, in the bright underground gourmet supermarket inside IFC Mall in Lujiazui, Chen Xuan pushed a silver shopping cart past the fresh produce displays while Qin Yuhan walked beside him with her ivory blazer draped lightly over one arm.

Several shoppers in the aisle glanced twice at the striking pair. Even without her corporate jacket, Qin Yuhan looked like a magazine cover in her pale silk blouse and tailored trousers, yet she was currently leaning over a chilled wooden bin of winter melons with furrowed brows.

"Hold on," Qin Yuhan said, lifting a thin pre-sliced wedge of winter melon wrapped in clear film and reading the printed label. "38 yuan for one small slice of winter melon? Did they water this melon with French perfume?"

Chen Xuan chuckled, taking the pricey wedge out of her hand and setting it neatly back into the imported organic bin. He stepped two paces to the left, picked up a much larger, heavier slice from the local farm section, and held it out for her inspection.

"Look at the white flesh and the tight seeds," Chen Xuan explained calmly. "This one is from a farm outside Suzhou. Freshly cut this morning, twice the weight, and 9 yuan. For soup, freshness beats a fancy sticker every time."

Qin Yuhan inspected the price tag, then grinned and placed the 9-yuan wedge proudly into the cart. "You really know how to run a kitchen. If I shopped alone, I would probably spend 500 yuan just to make one pot of soup."

"That is why you keep me around," Chen Xuan teased, steering the cart toward the spice shelves.

They moved unhurriedly down the aisles, choosing fresh ginger, fragrant star anise, crisp green scallions, and a small punnet of bright autumn strawberries. When Qin Yuhan reached out to slip a tall Italian bottle of truffle olive oil into the cart, Chen Xuan smoothly lifted it back onto the shelf and replaced it with a reliable bottle of pressed peanut oil.

"Braised pork ribs want honest aroma, not fancy truffles," he told her with a wink. "Trust the chef."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, relinquishing the bottle without a fight. At the fresh meat counter, Chen Xuan asked the butcher for two pounds of center-cut pork ribs with clean white bone and thin, even marbling, checking the color with a practiced glance before thanking the man and laying the neat parcel beside the winter melon.

Just as they turned the corner toward the beverage and wine aisle, a bright, overly familiar voice called out from a few steps away.

"Yuhan? My goodness, is that really you?"

Qin Yuhan paused and turned. Walking toward them was Zhou Qian, the daughter of a mid-sized textile family she had met at two autumn charity galas. Dressed in a loud designer tweed jacket, Zhou Qian clung to the arm of a tall young man with gelled hair and a heavy gold watch glinting under the supermarket lights.

"Zhou Qian," Qin Yuhan greeted her politely, her tone courteous but cool.

"I almost did not recognize you pushing a grocery cart," Zhou Qian said, her eyes darting quickly from Qin Yuhan's silk blouse to Chen Xuan's plain gray windbreaker, his canvas bag, and the 9-yuan winter melon resting in the cart. A faint, patronizing smile curled her lips. "And who is this? Did Haiyue Group hire a new household driver to help you carry bags?"

Beside her, the young man chuckled smugly and adjusted his watch cuff. "Honey, let me introduce myself. I am Sun Ming. I just picked up an imported 800k coupe downstairs and thought we would grab a nice bottle from the wine cellar here." He lifted a sleek black gift box in his hand. "A decent Bordeaux, only 12k a bottle. Nothing too wild for a Friday night."

Zhou Qian smiled sweetly at Qin Yuhan, though her gaze lingered mockingly on Chen Xuan. "Yuhan, with your status as president of Haiyue Group, you really should not settle for standing around meat counters. A woman like you deserves a partner who takes you to Michelin dining rooms, not someone counting pennies over local vegetables."

Chen Xuan stood completely relaxed with one hand resting on the cart handle, looking as unbothered as if someone were discussing the weather.

Qin Yuhan's cool gaze turned sharp as cold jade. Before Chen Xuan could even speak, she stepped half a pace closer, slipped her slender fingers firmly between his, and lifted their joined hands slightly so the matching wedding bands caught the bright overhead light.

"You misunderstood, Zhou Qian," Qin Yuhan said, her voice clear, steady, and effortlessly commanding. "This is my husband, Chen Xuan. And for the record, his braised pork ribs and winter melon soup are worth more to me than any 12k bottle of wine or Michelin table in Shanghai."

Zhou Qian's painted smile stiffened on her face. Sun Ming blinked in disbelief, looking Chen Xuan up and down as if trying to figure out what hidden trick the man in the plain windbreaker had pulled to marry the proudest heiress in Pudong.

"Husband?" Sun Ming muttered, forcing an awkward laugh. "Well, everyone has different tastes, I suppose. Still, if you ever want to try real vintage wine instead of supermarket soup, President Qin, you know where to find us."

Chen Xuan finally smiled, his tone mild and entirely polite. "Actually, Mr. Sun, you might want to check the harvest year on that box before you pay. That estate had a wet autumn harvest that year, and the cellar here marks it up by nearly half. For 12k, you are mostly buying the black wooden box."

Sun Ming's face flushed a dull red. He stared down at the box in his hand, suddenly unsure whether to argue or hide the label, while Zhou Qian tugged sharply at his sleeve in embarrassment.

Chen Xuan turned back to his wife as if the two of them had already faded into the background. "Did you want extra fresh coriander for the soup tonight, or just green scallions?"

"Just scallions," Qin Yuhan said, her eyes sparkling with quiet amusement as she squeezed his hand. "Let us check out. I am starving."

Leaving Zhou Qian and Sun Ming frozen beside the wine display, Chen Xuan pushed the cart smoothly toward the checkout counter. Five minutes later, carrying two neat paper bags of fresh groceries, they walked out into the crisp autumn evening toward home.
