# Chapter 103: State Security Maps the Zurich Web

Three floors beneath State Security headquarters in the capital, Interview Room Four sat cool and bright under white lights, a steel table bolted to its center.

Three foreign nationals sat along one side in plain navy tracksuits, their watches, rings, and silk ties locked upstairs. On the left sat Viktor Kroll, a silver-haired European in his late fifties trying to keep a senior banker's ease. In the middle sat Claire Moreau, a sharp-featured Frenchwoman with her arms folded tight. On the right sat Kenji Takahashi, a thin man in wire-rimmed glasses who had not touched his water cup in two hours.

The steel door clicked open. Minister Ding Guoping entered with a rolled paper map and a red marker. Senior Investigator Lu Chengwei followed with two gray binders and took a chair in the corner.

Ding Guoping unrolled the map across the steel table, weighted the corners, and met the three faces across from him.

"You have held us for thirty-six hours without a single call to our embassies," Claire Moreau snapped. "I have told your officers ten times. We run a registered private advisory office in Shanghai. Holding European and Japanese citizens in a basement will be on every front page in London and Paris by Friday."

Viktor Kroll leaned back with a thin, practiced smile. "She is right, Minister Ding. Whoever fed your office a bad tip about our Shanghai firm made an expensive mistake. You want foreign banks to trust Shanghai, do you not? Let us call our consulates, and we can treat this as a misunderstanding."

Ding Guoping sat opposite them and uncapped the red marker.

"Your embassies received formal notice at seven this morning," Ding Guoping said evenly. "They asked to see the charge sheets. We sent twelve pages. Neither Paris, nor Bern, nor Tokyo asked a second question."

Claire Moreau's confident look froze. "That is a bluff. You cannot charge private consultants with espionage just because we met local businessmen in Shanghai."

"You did not meet local businessmen," Ding Guoping said calmly, tapping Shanghai with the red marker. "For eight years, your office on Nanjing West Road was the East Asia relay desk for the Zurich Northern Ledger. You paid local informants, moved hidden cash through Hong Kong shell firms, and collected coastal defense files and state-bank loan sheets for Zurich."

Viktor Kroll laughed softly, though his eyes stayed hard. "Even if two or three careless local traders in Shanghai dropped our name to save their necks, that is nothing. Big international firms have hundreds of local contacts. You caught a few small fish and now pretend you caught a whale."

Kenji Takahashi had not spoken. His eyes dropped from Ding Guoping's face to the map.

Ding Guoping noticed. Slowly, he drew a clean red line from Shanghai to Hong Kong, out to Singapore, across to Tokyo, up to Seoul, and straight west to Zurich.

"Let us talk about those small fish, Viktor Kroll," Ding Guoping said quietly. "Two nights ago at two in the morning, we did not just knock on your Shanghai door. Let me read from your own internal books."

He nodded to Lu Chengwei, who opened the first binder.

"Desk Code Iron Pine, in Hong Kong, handling 1.6 billion yuan in coastal shipping bribes," Ding Guoping read flatly. "Desk Code Cold Crane, in Shenzhen, running offline bank cards for state energy officials, totaling 2.1 billion yuan. Desk Code Silver Bridge, in Tokyo and Osaka, buying port patrol schedules from Southern Theater supply officers for 85 million yuan."

Every muscle in Kenji Takahashi's face went rigid. His fingertips pressed hard into the steel edge.

"Impossible," Kenji Takahashi whispered tightly. "Nobody in Shanghai had the code names for the Tokyo and Shenzhen desks. Those desks never spoke to each other. Even I only knew my own route."

"Yet I am reading them straight off the page," Ding Guoping replied, tapping Zurich. "Altogether, your Asian web moved 4.8 billion yuan in dirty cash over four of its fourteen years. Every courier from Shanghai to Shenzhen was picked up the same night. Every relay account in Hong Kong and Singapore was frozen before sunrise."

Claire Moreau stared at the red lines crossing the map. Arrogance drained from her face, leaving pale shock. "How? The master desk keys never leave Zurich. Only three managing partners in Switzerland hold the full Asia list. You could not get that from a raid in Shanghai."

Ding Guoping leaned back and folded his hands.

"You still think this was a local police raid," Ding Guoping said, his tone turning ice-cold. "Look at the map again, Claire Moreau. We did not cut off one branch. In one night, we tore out the entire Asian root system of the Zurich Northern Ledger. Every desk is dark. Every courier is in handcuffs. Every safe house is empty."

Viktor Kroll's banker's mask cracked wide open. His right hand trembled on the table. He stared at the red circle around Zurich, then raised his eyes to Ding Guoping.

"If Zurich's master list is already in Beijing," Viktor Kroll said hoarsely, "then the partners in Switzerland know their Asian network is gone."

"They knew by four in the morning two days ago," Ding Guoping confirmed. "Which brings us to your hope of a rescue. Your partners in Zurich are not calling your embassies to save you. They are shredding their own paper trails, closing old holding rooms, and praying you do not start naming names in Beijing."

Silence crashed down over Interview Room Four.

Claire Moreau uncrossed her arms and pressed both palms flat on the steel, her breathing quick and shallow. She glanced at Viktor Kroll, hoping for his steady smile, and saw only a gray-faced man staring at a dead map.

Kenji Takahashi removed his glasses and rubbed his eyes. Every trace of his earlier calm was gone.

"Minister Ding," Kenji Takahashi said quietly, his voice shaking. "If I give you the real names of the two Swiss partners who signed off on the Southern Theater payments, will that be noted on my file?"

"Kenji, stop!" Viktor Kroll snapped weakly, with no force left.

"Why should I stop, Viktor?" Kenji Takahashi turned on him sharply. "Look at the table! Zurich already buried us. They are not coming for us."

Ding Guoping capped his red marker with a neat click and stood.

"Senior Investigator Lu Chengwei will take your statements one by one," Ding Guoping said evenly. "Do not leave out a single name. The Zurich Northern Ledger lost Asia in one night, and we are not finished cleaning up what they left behind."

Without waiting for an answer, Ding Guoping walked out and pulled the steel door shut behind him.
