# Chapter 115: Chen Xuan's Quiet Friday

While the Harbor View Hotel lobby turned quiet and tense downtown, Chen Xuan was already high above the evening clouds on his way home.

He had walked back from the riverside park to Hengxin Logistics at four-forty, rinsed out his green thermos in the break room, and wiped down his desk with a dry cloth.

Down at the corner of the next block, an old woman in a knitted vest sat behind a wooden cart piled high with bright orange fruit. A cardboard sign propped against a crate read sweet southern oranges, five yuan a pound.

Chen Xuan stopped by the cart and picked up one of the oranges to check the skin.

"Just came off the morning truck, young man," the old woman said cheerfully. "Peel one and taste it. Sweet as honey."

"No need to peel one, auntie," Chen Xuan smiled. "I can tell by the weight. Give me two big bags, about ten pounds total."

"Ten pounds? Bringing them home for the family?"

"My mother loves these after dinner," Chen Xuan said, helping her hold open the thin red plastic bags while she weighed the fruit on a hanging scale. He checked a few oranges lightly to make sure none were bruised, handed her a crisp fifty-yuan note, and told her to keep the small change.

A plain dark sedan sat parked near the curb with its headlights off and its engine humming softly. As soon as Chen Xuan stepped onto the narrow sidewalk, the driver's door opened and Number 7 stepped out in a neat dark jacket.

Number 7 walked around the rear bumper, opened the back door, and reached out smoothly for the two red plastic bags.

"Oranges?" Number 7 asked with a faint, easy smile.

"Careful with the bottom ones," Chen Xuan said as he ducked into the warm leather seat. "They are ripe. If they roll around on the turn, my mother will blame me for buying bruised fruit."

"I will wedge them beside the front console," Number 7 said, setting both bags securely on the front passenger floor before getting back behind the wheel. "Good week at the office?"

"Pretty quiet," Chen Xuan replied, resting his cloth bag on his knee. "Finished the shipping logs early today and got an hour of reading down by the river. Have you eaten yet, Number 7?"

"Had a bowl of beef noodles around four," Number 7 said, pulling smoothly out onto the main avenue toward the small private airfield outside the city. "Though those oranges smell pretty good."

Chen Xuan reached forward, pulled a bright orange from the top of the nearest bag, and set it in the cup holder beside the gear stick. "Eat one when we get to the gate. Do not skip dinner just to wait on my flight."

"Thank you," Number 7 said quietly, his eyes steady on the road ahead.

Twenty-five minutes later, the sedan rolled straight through a quiet side gate at the Shanghai executive airfield and stopped beside a sleek white private jet parked on the far edge of the tarmac.

Chen Xuan stepped aboard with his cloth bag, and Number 7 followed right behind, setting the two bags of oranges neatly inside the small galley cabinet so they would stay upright during takeoff.

Within six minutes, the engines hummed to life, the jet rolled down the clear runway, and the lights of Shanghai shrank into tiny golden dots beneath the wing.

Once the cabin leveled out smoothly above the clouds, Chen Xuan poured warm water from the galley pot into his green thermos, dropped in a pinch of jasmine leaves, and leaned back in the wide cream leather seat with his history book open across his lap.

Ten minutes into the flight, the small black phone built into the armrest gave two soft rings.

Number 7 stepped forward from the front seat, picked up the handset, and listened for two seconds.

"It is Number 1," Number 7 said calmly, handing the receiver across.

Chen Xuan set his bookmark carefully between the pages and took the phone. "Speak."

On the other end of the line, Number 1's voice came through clear and steady. "Two things to report from Shanghai. First, State Security just took custody of two armed foreign nationals at their northern drop point. Both men came from Europe on short visas. They went to Yunxi County this morning, found a recent Hengxin Logistics port receipt near Pier Four, and followed you from your office to the riverside park this afternoon."

Chen Xuan paused with his thermos half raised. "Followed me to the park?"

"Yes," Number 1 said. "Our White Guard team never had to move."

"Black Guard?" Chen Xuan asked calmly.

"Black Guard," Number 1 confirmed. "The two foreigners stepped behind the cedar trees and crossed the fifty-meter line. Black Guard's close-protection team dropped both men in six seconds without making a sound, took their pistols and phones, and delivered them straight to State Security's drop point. Nobody in the park saw a thing."

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of warm jasmine tea. "And the second thing?"

"Their employer is an overseas fixer named Li Weiming, traveling under the name Marcus Vance," Number 1 said. "He flew in from Zurich yesterday to figure out why Qi Tongwei, Shanshui Group, and the Northern Ledger's network collapsed on Tuesday night. He hired those two trackers to dig into Yunxi Old Street. State Security already froze his hotel room at the Harbor View and missed him in the lobby five minutes ago."

"Did anyone bother Old Street or my parents?" Chen Xuan asked, his tone still even, though his eyes sharpened slightly.

"No," Number 1 replied immediately. "The two trackers only asked a boatman and a tea seller a few casual questions near Pier Four before finding that stray shipping receipt. They never went near your parents' house, and neither Chen Jianguo nor Liu Meifang noticed anything unusual. Yunxi County is completely quiet."

"Good," Chen Xuan said, relaxing back into his seat. "Let State Security handle Li Weiming and his two trackers by the book. No need for White Guard to waste extra effort on them."

"Understood. Should we send any message to Black Guard?"

"Leave them alone," Chen Xuan said with a faint shake of his head. "They do what they want anyway. As long as they do not disturb my family, I will not interfere."

"Understood. Enjoy your weekend in Yunxi."

"You too, Number 1. Get some rest."

The plane would land at the county airstrip in twenty minutes, where a car waited. By seven, he would be walking down Old Street with two bags of sweet oranges, right in time for his mother's braised pork and hot rice.

He opened his history book back to the marked page and read peacefully until the wheels touched down.
