# Chapter 80: The Quiet Watch Across the Street

When Chen Xuan carried the two red bags of apples and pears back into the sixth-floor apartment at quarter past twelve, the living room was loud with the warm clatter of lunch.

Wu Lanying had set soy-braised river fish in the center of the folding table, beside stir-fried spinach, pickled radish, and ginger rib soup. Chen Mingde sat at the head with color back in his cheeks, laughing as Chen Hao and Chen Yuan argued over the crispiest fish tail.

"Where did all this fruit come from?" Liu Meifang asked, wiping her hands on her apron as she took the bags. "These apples are heavy! Did you spend half your pocket money at the corner stand?"

"Wang Lei brought them," Chen Xuan said easily, setting the dark vinegar beside the soy sauce. "He heard Second Uncle was home and pedaled straight over on his delivery tricycle."

Chen Mingde paused with his chopsticks in midair, his face softening. "That good boy Wang Lei! Why did you not pull him upstairs for lunch? Last week, when Yan Tiehu's thugs blocked my doorway, Wang Lei was the only person on Old Street who walked past their iron pipes to buy a hinge so I would not stand alone!"

"I asked him twice, Second Uncle," Chen Xuan replied, smiling faintly. "He said his shirt smelled like grease and did not want to interrupt our meal. He promised to come drink tea once Mingde Hardware reopens."

"Next time he delivers near Old Street, I am dragging his tricycle inside and feeding him three bowls of dumplings," Wu Lanying declared, washing two bright apples at the sink. "That boy has a better heart than half the big shots in Yunxi County."

"Speaking of big shots," Chen Jianguo piped up, lifting his teacup proudly. "Old Zhao from the third floor shouted up five minutes ago that the county radio just announced an emergency leadership change! County Magistrate Sun Weiming and Chief Liang Guodong are officially kicked out of office and facing provincial charges! As the old saying goes, a fat termite can chew through oak all winter, until the carpenter brings a steel hammer in spring!"

Chen Yuan nearly choked on his rice, slapping the table as he laughed. "Dad, stop! That does not even rhyme! Carpenters do not hammer termites!"

"They do if the termites wear county badges!" Chen Hao roared back, tapping his soup spoon against Chen Jianguo's cup. "Here is to Second Uncle being home and Hongrui swept off Old Street!"

"Sit down and eat before the braised fish gets cold, Xuan!" Liu Meifang called warmly, scooping fish belly into his bowl. "Do not stand by the doorway like a guest!"

"Give me one minute, Mom," Chen Xuan said mildly, picking up his green thermos. "I want a breath of fresh air first."

He slid open the narrow balcony door and stepped out into the crisp noon air, pulling the glass partway shut so the laughter and clinking chopsticks hummed behind him.

Resting his arms on the concrete railing, Chen Xuan looked down six floors toward the courtyard gate of Fuxing New Village.

Just beyond the iron gate, Wang Lei's blue delivery tricycle merged cleanly into midday traffic. Wang Lei stood to pedal three hard strokes to catch the green light before turning toward the shipping yard and vanishing from sight.

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of hot tea from his green thermos. His gaze drifted across the two-lane street to the shaded bus stop opposite Building 2.

His hand stopped an inch from the railing.

Standing beside the blue bus sign were two plain figures in long dark coats. Neither wore a badge, hat, or earpiece, nor held a phone or bag. To anyone leaving the noodle shop ten steps away, they looked like two quiet riders waiting in the shade.

Yet while Chen Xuan watched, the Route 3 county bus hissed to a stop, opened its doors, and pulled away. Neither figure moved. Two minutes later, the Route 7 bus stopped, picked up an old woman, and rolled off.

The two dark-coated figures did not board. They did not look at each other or shift their feet. Their posture held a cold, razor-sharp stillness no regular rider, provincial officer, or normal bodyguard could hold for ten seconds, let alone half an hour in broad daylight.

Chen Xuan judged the distance with one calm glance.

Fifty meters from Building 2's stairwell. Clear view of both ends of the street, the gate, and the sixth-floor balcony.

They were not White Guard. Number 1 had ordered every White Guard unit to stand down after last night's cleanup, and even on duty White Guard agents blended in like normal people, buying cigarettes or leaning on scooters.

These two did not pretend to be normal. They stood completely outside ordinary life, silent and absolute.

Black Guard.

Even Number 1 had zero power over them. They answered to no provincial office, no ministry line, and no chain of command Chen Xuan had ever asked for. After the capital's overnight thunder, they had not faded back into the shadows of the vault. On their own silent, secret order, a close-watch team had planted itself right at the fifty-meter line outside his family's building.

As if sensing Chen Xuan's gaze across six floors, the figure on the left tilted its head up less than half an inch.

Its high collar hid its jaw in shadow, and its eyes rose straight through the bright midday light to lock squarely onto Chen Xuan's on the sixth-floor balcony.

For half a second, neither moved.

There was no salute, no nod, no hand signal, and no words across the fifty meters between them. Only a cold, clean, silent understanding passing between the man holding a faded green thermos on an apartment balcony and the quiet watch motionless across the road.

Then the dark-coated figure lowered its eyes back to the street as a delivery van rolled between them.

Behind Chen Xuan, the balcony door slid open and Chen Yuan stuck his head out, waving his chopsticks.

"Xuan! Are you coming or what? Chen Hao is eyeing the last piece of braised fish belly, and Dad is about to make up a fourth saying if you do not save us!"

Chen Xuan closed the lid of his green thermos with a soft click. A calm, warm smile returned to his face as he turned his back on the bus stop.

"Tell Chen Hao to leave that piece alone," Chen Xuan said, stepping inside and sliding the door shut. "I am coming."
