# Chapter 80: The Quiet Watch Across the Street

When Chen Xuan carried the two red bags of apples and pears back into the sixth-floor apartment at quarter past twelve, the living room was loud with the warm clatter of lunch.

Wu Lanying had set a steaming platter of soy-braised river fish in the center of the folding table, flanked by stir-fried spinach, pickled radish, and ginger rib soup. Chen Mingde sat at the head of the table with color back in his cheeks, laughing as Chen Hao and Chen Yuan argued over who got the crispest fish tail.

"Where did all this fruit come from?" Liu Meifang asked, wiping her hands on her apron as she took the bags from Chen Xuan and peered inside. "These apples are crisp and heavy! Did you spend half your pocket money at the corner stall?"

"Wang Lei brought them," Chen Xuan said easily, setting the dark vinegar beside the soy sauce. "He heard Second Uncle was home and pedaled straight over on his delivery tricycle."

Chen Mingde paused with his chopsticks midair, his expression softening. "That good boy Wang Lei! Why did you not pull him upstairs for lunch? Last week, when Yan Tiehu's thugs blocked my doorway, Wang Lei was the only soul on Old Street who walked past their iron pipes to buy a hinge so I would not stand alone!"

"I asked him twice, Second Uncle," Chen Xuan replied, smiling faintly. "He said his shirt smelled like axle grease and did not want to interrupt our meal. He promised to come drink tea once Mingde Hardware reopens."

"Next time he delivers near Old Street, I am dragging his tricycle into the shop and feeding him three bowls of dumplings," Wu Lanying declared, washing two bright apples at the sink. "That boy has a better heart than half the big shots in Yunxi County."

"Speaking of big shots," Chen Jianguo piped up, lifting his teacup with theatrical satisfaction. "Old Zhao from the third floor shouted up five minutes ago that the county broadcast station just announced an emergency leadership reshuffle! Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming and Chief Liang Guodong are officially removed from office pending provincial prosecution! As the old proverb says, a fat termite can chew through oak all winter, until the carpenter brings a steel hammer in spring!"

Chen Yuan nearly choked on his rice, slapping the table as he laughed. "Dad, stop! That does not even rhyme! Carpenters do not hammer termites!"

"They do if the termites wear county badges!" Chen Hao roared back, clinking his soup spoon against Chen Jianguo's cup. "Here is to Second Uncle being home and Hongrui swept off Old Street!"

"Sit down and eat before the braised fish goes cold, Xuan!" Liu Meifang called warmly, scooping fish belly into Chen Xuan's bowl. "Do not stand by the doorway like a guest!"

"Give me one minute, Mom," Chen Xuan said mildly, picking up his green insulated thermos. "I want a breath of fresh air before I sit down."

He slid open the narrow balcony door and stepped outside into the crisp noon air, pulling the glass partway shut so the laughter, clinking chopsticks, and Chen Jianguo's indignant defense of his proverbs hummed warmly behind him.

Resting his forearms on the concrete railing, Chen Xuan looked down six stories toward the courtyard gate of Fuxing New Village.

Just beyond the iron archway, Wang Lei's battered blue delivery tricycle merged cleanly into midday traffic. Wang Lei pedaled standing up for three strokes to catch the green light, his thin shirt fluttering behind him, before turning toward the freight depot and disappearing from sight.

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of hot tea from his green thermos, letting his gaze drift across the two-lane street to the shaded bus stop directly opposite Building 2.

His hand paused a fraction of an inch from the railing.

Standing beside the blue metal route sign were two unremarkable figures in long dark coats. Neither wore a badge, a hat, or an earpiece. Neither held a phone or shopping bag. To any passerby leaving the noodle shop ten paces away, they were simply two quiet commuters waiting in the shade.

Yet while Chen Xuan watched, the Route 3 county bus hissed to a stop, opened its doors for twenty seconds, and pulled away. Neither figure moved a millimeter. Two minutes later, the Route 7 shuttle stopped at the same sign, picked up an elderly woman, and rumbled off down the street.

The two dark-coated figures did not board. They did not turn toward each other. They did not shift their weight. Their posture held a cold, razor-flat stillness that no civilian commuter, no provincial inspector, and no ordinary bodyguard could sustain for ten seconds, let alone half an hour in broad daylight.

Chen Xuan measured the distance automatically with one calm glance.

Fifty meters from Building 2's stairwell. Clear sightline to both ends of the street, the courtyard gate, and the sixth-floor balcony.

They were not Black Pine Guard. Number One had locked every Black Pine unit into full stand-down after the overnight reckoning, and even on active duty, Black Pine operatives blended through calculated civilian rhythm, buying cigarettes or leaning on scooters.

These two did not pretend to be ordinary. They stood outside ordinary life altogether, silent and absolute.

Zero Guard.

Even Number One held zero authority over them. They answered to no provincial dispatch, no ministry line, and no human chain of command Chen Xuan had ever requested. After the capital's overnight thunder, they had not vanished back into the shadows of the vault. By their own unexplained, unrecorded directive, a silent close-protection watch had anchored itself at the exact fifty-meter perimeter of his family's building.

As if sensing the shift in Chen Xuan's focus across six stories of noon air, the figure on the left tilted its head upward by less than half an inch.

Its high collar shadowed its jawline, and its gaze rose straight through the midday glare to lock squarely onto Chen Xuan's eyes on the sixth-floor balcony.

For half a second, neither moved.

There was no salute. No nod of submission. No signal tapped against a cuff. No words crossed the fifty meters between them.

Only a chill, clean, silent acknowledgment passing between the man holding a faded green thermos on an apartment balcony and the quiet watch standing motionless across the road.

Then the dark-coated figure lowered its gaze back to the intersection, returning to absolute stillness as a delivery van rolled between them.

Behind Chen Xuan, the balcony door slid open an inch and Chen Yuan stuck his head out, waving his chopsticks.

"Xuan! Are you coming or what? Chen Hao is eyeing the last piece of braised fish belly, and Dad is about to invent a fourth proverb if you do not save us!"

Chen Xuan closed the lid of his green thermos with a soft click. A calm, genuine smile returned to his face as he turned his back on the bus stop across the street.

"Tell Chen Hao to leave that piece alone," Chen Xuan said, stepping back inside and sliding the balcony door shut behind him. "I am coming."
