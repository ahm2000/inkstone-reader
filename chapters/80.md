# Chapter 80: Grandma's Eighty-First Birthday

Bright autumn sunshine spilled over the red brick courtyard of Hongshi Village, warming the old persimmon tree whose branches still held glowing orange fruit.

Today was Grandmother Zhou Guilan's eighty-first birthday, and the entire Chen family had gathered before nine. Fresh red paper cuttings adorned the wooden window lattices, and rich aromas of braised pork belly, gingered chicken soup, and steamed buns drifted from the kitchen.

Inside the kitchen, Liu Meifang and Second Aunt Wu Lanying moved in practiced rhythm beside the iron stove. Out in the sunny yard, Chen Jianguo and Second Uncle Chen Mingde carried two round wooden tables beneath the persimmon tree, while Chen Yuan and Chen Hao arranged red stools and wiped porcelain bowls.

Beside the doorstep sat Grandfather Chen Fugen and Grandmother Zhou Guilan on their cane chairs. Zhou Guilan wore a newly pressed burgundy quilted jacket, her silver hair combed neatly back, her face glowing with quiet joy.

Sitting on a low wooden stool right at Zhou Guilan's knees was Tang Xiaoyu. She wore her bright red knitted sweater with neat black trousers, her hair braided tidily with red yarn. With careful fingers, she was shelling roasted peanuts into a small blue bowl for Zhou Guilan.

"Look at our Tang Xiaoyu," Zhou Guilan chuckled fondly, patting the girl's head. "Not a single broken peanut skin in the bowl. She is more patient than Chen Xuan was at her age."

"Grandma Zhou Guilan, you say that every time I visit," Chen Xuan laughed softly, walking over from the porch with a folded box and a thermos of jasmine tea.

He knelt briefly beside Zhou Guilan's chair and draped a soft camel-colored cashmere shawl gently around her shoulders.

"Happy eighty-first birthday, Grandma," Chen Xuan said warmly. "The mornings in Hongshi Village turn chilly fast. Keep this around your shoulders outside."

Zhou Guilan stroked the soft wool, her eyes shining. "You always spend your hard-earned wages on your old grandmother. Save your money for yourself, child."

"I still have plenty left for dinner, Grandma," Chen Xuan smiled, handing Tang Xiaoyu a paper-wrapped sesame pastry from Lanshui before stepping back to help Chen Jianguo set out the teacups.

* * *

Just past ten o'clock, the wooden courtyard gate swung open with a loud creak, and a booming voice cut across the peaceful morning.

"Eldest Sister-in-law! Happy birthday! We barely squeezed out of city traffic this morning!"

Third Uncle-in-law Liu Guoliang strode into the yard first, wearing an overly glossy black leather jacket despite the mild sun. A thick gold-plated watch glinted on his wrist as he swung a cheap, brightly colored box of low-grade liquor. Behind him walked his wife, glancing critically around the modest brick walls and wooden tables.

Chen Fugen and Zhou Guilan rose politely to welcome their distant relatives, and Chen Jianguo hurried forward to offer warm chairs and fresh tea.

Liu Guoliang barely sat down before launching into his favorite subject.

"I told my wife we had to drive out early today," Liu Guoliang declared loudly so the whole yard could hear. "Our son just got promoted to regional supervisor at a trading company in the city. Twenty thousand yuan a month, plus quarterly commission, and he just financed a brand-new black sedan! He wanted to drive us himself, but big executives are always stuck in meetings with important people."

Second Uncle Chen Mingde nodded politely without commenting, while Liu Meifang exchanged a quiet, resigned glance with Wu Lanying from the kitchen door.

Liu Guoliang took a loud slurp of jasmine tea, set the cup down with a clack, and turned his squinting eyes toward Chen Xuan, who was wiping porcelain spoons near the table.

"Speaking of city jobs," Liu Guoliang drawled, a smug smirk curling his mouth. "Chen Xuan, I heard you are still sitting at that little desk in a small freight office in Qingyan City. What do they pay you, twelve thousand yuan a month?"

"Yes, Third Uncle-in-law Liu Guoliang," Chen Xuan replied evenly, his voice mild and unhurried. "Twelve thousand a month, with regular hours and weekends off. It is steady work."

Liu Guoliang let out a dry, theatrical laugh, slapping his knee. "Twelve thousand! A young man in his twenties bragging about twelve thousand yuan is like bragging about owning a bicycle! No wonder Chen Jianguo still lives in that old sixth-floor walk-up. Without connections or ambition, you will sit at the bottom until your hair turns white."

Chen Yuan's hands froze over the bowls, her eyebrows drawing together sharply. Chen Jianguo's face darkened as he opened his mouth to speak.

Before Chen Jianguo could say a word, Liu Guoliang's gaze drifted down to Tang Xiaoyu, who sat quietly beside Zhou Guilan in her red knitted sweater, her small fingers tightening around the blue peanut bowl.

* * *

"And wait a minute," Liu Guoliang said, pointing his chin rudely toward Tang Xiaoyu. "Is this that orphan girl from Yunxi County? Chen Jianguo, are you out of your mind? Your own son makes a clerk's pittance, your house is barely big enough for yourselves, and you go and take in an unrelated girl with no parents to feed and put through school?"

The warm air in the courtyard froze instantly.

Tang Xiaoyu lowered her head, her small shoulders drawing inward as her cheeks flushed with quiet hurt. Beside her, Zhou Guilan's smile vanished, her fingers gripping her cane chair tightly.

"Liu Guoliang!" Chen Jianguo snapped, his voice low and stern. "Mind your tongue in my mother's courtyard. Tang Xiaoyu is family."

"I am just speaking practical truth!" Liu Guoliang scoffed, waving his ringed hand. "Charity sounds nice until the tuition bills pile up and nobody thanks you. A small household has to know its limits."

Chen Hao stepped forward with clenched fists, and Chen Yuan looked ready to throw the tea tray across the yard.

Then a calm, warm hand rested gently on Chen Jianguo's shoulder.

Chen Xuan stepped past his father without a trace of anger. He lifted the clay teapot, poured a fresh, steaming cup of jasmine tea for Grandmother Zhou Guilan, and leaned down to place two shelled peanuts gently into Tang Xiaoyu's palm.

"Tang Xiaoyu is the sharpest student in her class, Third Uncle-in-law Liu Guoliang," Chen Xuan said softly, his voice completely steady and polite. "Having her at our table is our family's good fortune, not a burden. Today is Grandma Zhou Guilan's eighty-first birthday, so let us keep the yard warm and cheerful for her."

He straightened up and met Liu Guoliang's eyes with an unruffled, quiet gaze that carried no shouting and no heat, yet made the older man swallow his next sneer and look away toward his teacup.

"Drink some tea while it is hot, Third Uncle-in-law," Chen Xuan added mildly. "Lunch will be ready before noon."

Liu Guoliang muttered under his breath, annoyed that Chen Xuan refused to take the bait, while Zhou Guilan pulled Tang Xiaoyu warmly against her side and whispered softly that nobody was allowed to spoil their birthday morning.
