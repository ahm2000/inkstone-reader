# Chapter 50: The Footnote from Yunxi

At four forty-five in the morning, the briefing hall hung thick with gray cigarette smoke. Nobody opened a window or called for tea.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua sat hunched over Case One, flipping through the final pages. He had spent ten minutes tracing stolen-money rings from coastal ports to provincial finance offices, listing the emergency bank freezes he had to sign before markets opened at nine thirty.

At the very back of the binder, tucked behind military treason reports and overseas bank charts, his thumb caught on a single thin sheet of plain white paper.

He paused, his brow furrowing, and read the title twice.

"Wait a minute," Zhou Zhenhua said, breaking the silence. "What is this doing at the back of Case One?"

Gu Chengshan rubbed his temples beside the ashtray and looked over. "What is it? Another port chief?"

"No," Zhou Zhenhua replied, tapping the page with a frown. "It is a county-level note. Yunxi County. An Old Street demolition fight, a local developer named Deng Yongchang, two street thugs named Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao, a county official named Sun Weiming, and a local police official named Liang Guodong. At the bottom sits a wrongful arrest note about a framed hardware shopkeeper named Chen Mingde."

Gao Zhengyi leaned forward, squinting through the smoke. "A county demolition fight?" Gao Zhengyi asked in disbelief. "Why in hell is a local hardware shopkeeper from Yunxi County sitting at the bottom of a 1.2 trillion yuan treason file?"

"That is what I am asking," Zhou Zhenhua said, turning the sheet over to show its blank back. "Look at the order note in the corner. Black Guard held Deng Yongchang, Yan Tiehu, Cheng Biao, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong inside Yunxi County on purpose so provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terror police can arrest them cleanly at dawn. They left them untouched."

"Is this Chen Mingde a secret informer?" Gao Zhengyi pressed, his eyes narrowing. "Did he stumble onto a smuggling book in his hardware shop, or is he related to a retired general?"

Gu Chengshan reached across, scanned the crisp lines on the note, and shook his head. "Chen Mingde," Gu Chengshan murmured. "Fifty-four years old. Runs a small hardware shop on Old Street in Yunxi County. No party rank. No army record. No central ties. Not a single line of intelligence value."

"Then why is his name inside Case One?" Zhou Zhenhua asked, looking sharply at all three men. "We have three hundred and eighteen top officials arrested across forty ministries, forty-seven foreign spies in custody, navy radar charts, southern grid keys, and 1.2 trillion yuan in stolen state money. How does a tiny county hardware shopkeeper end up at the back of the master treason vault?"

Across the table, General Qin Zhengguo had not moved since Zhou Zhenhua first said Yunxi County.

"Hand me that sheet," Qin Zhengguo said quietly.

Zhou Zhenhua slid the white page across the table. Qin Zhengguo picked it up by the edges, his eyes moving slowly down every line: Yunxi County, Old Street, Deng Yongchang, Yan Tiehu, Cheng Biao, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Chen Mingde. Beneath it sat one stark order: Lawful local sweep only. Clear Chen Mingde through normal county legal review. Zero central inquiry.

A sudden, chilling clarity washed across Qin Zhengguo's weathered face. His jaw set hard as stone. His eyes lingered on the exact time of Chen Mingde's wrongful arrest, then shifted back to the exact time of Black Guard's nationwide launch.

He did not know the name of Chen Mingde's nephew. Nobody in the capital knew Chen Xuan's face, his desk, or his family. Yet Qin Zhengguo had spent forty years studying how landslides begin.

Slowly, Qin Zhengguo laid the white sheet inside the back cover of Case One, closed the binder with a dull thump, and rested his palm on the blank red wax seal.

"Qin Zhengguo?" Gu Chengshan asked, his voice dropping at the stillness in the general's eyes. "What did you just see?"

Qin Zhengguo looked across the smoke-filled table from Gu Chengshan to Zhou Zhenhua, then rested his gaze on Gao Zhengyi.

"Listen to me carefully," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice cold enough to freeze the air. "Someone kicked a stone in Yunxi, and the whole mountain fell down. Do not dig into who owns the stone. Clean the mountain, and free that shopkeeper cleanly by the book."

The words hung in the gray smoke like a drawn blade.

Zhou Zhenhua's mouth parted. A chill ran down his spine as the meaning hit him, and his eyes dropped to the closed cover of Case One. "You mean," Zhou Zhenhua began, then swallowed hard. "All of tonight, all forty ministries, all three theater commands, all forty-two black cases, started because someone in Yunxi County tried to crush an ordinary shopkeeper?"

"I told you not to dig," Qin Zhengguo snapped, eyes flashing with cold warning. "Do not ask who Chen Mingde is to anyone. Do not send a central officer to his door. Do not send a provincial leader to visit his shop, do not offer him a medal, and do not put a watch on his street. Let provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terror teams sweep up Deng Yongchang, Yan Tiehu, Cheng Biao, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong strictly under local law. Let the county prosecutor drop the false charge against Chen Mingde cleanly by the book, and let that man walk home in peace."

Gao Zhengyi stared at the closed folder, the blister on his burned fingers throbbing. Cold sweat broke out along his neck as the terrifying scale of what he had almost poked into settled in his chest. "Understood," Gao Zhengyi said hoarsely. "Strictly provincial authority. Zero central footprints. Zero questions asked."

"And the other forty-one cases?" Zhou Zhenhua asked, his voice barely steady as he glanced down the row of black cases.

Gu Chengshan stood straight, buttoning his jacket with steady hands. Shock had burned away, leaving only grim resolve.

"At six o'clock, the State Council, the Military Commission, and the CCDI open all forty-two cases together," Premier Gu Chengshan ordered. "Every one of those three hundred and eighteen top officials goes into formal custody. Every one of those forty-seven foreign spies goes to State Security. By seven, the provincial task force lands in Yunxi County to clean out Deng Yongchang and his four partners by the book."

Gu Chengshan looked one last time at General Qin Zhengguo.

"We clean the mountain," Gu Chengshan said quietly. "And nobody in this capital ever speaks a word about the stone."

At four fifty in the morning, outside the sealed double doors of the briefing hall, the sky over the capital's rooftops was still pitch black.
