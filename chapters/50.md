# Chapter 50: The Footnote from Yunxi

At four forty-five in the morning, the briefing hall hung thick with gray cigarette smoke. Nobody opened a window. Nobody called for tea.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua sat hunched over the open main folder from Case One, his fingers stiff as he flipped through the final pages. He had spent ten minutes tracing the stolen-money rings from coastal ports to provincial finance offices, listing the emergency bank freezes he would have to sign before markets opened at nine thirty.

At the very back of the thick binder, tucked behind the military treason reports and overseas bank charts, Zhou Zhenhua's thumb caught on a single thin sheet of plain white paper.

He paused. His brow furrowed deeply. He pulled the sheet closer under the dim light and read the title twice.

"Wait a minute," Zhou Zhenhua said, breaking the heavy silence. "What is this doing at the back of Case One?"

Gu Chengshan, rubbing his temples beside the ashtray, looked over. "What is it? Another port chief?"

"No," Zhou Zhenhua replied, tapping the thin page with a confused frown. "It is a county-level note. Yunxi County. An Old Street demolition fight, a local developer named Deng Yongchang, two street thugs named Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao, a county official named Sun Weiming, and a local police official named Liang Guodong. Right at the bottom sits a wrongful arrest note about a framed hardware shopkeeper named Chen Mingde."

Gao Zhengyi leaned forward at once, squinting through the drifting smoke. "A county demolition fight?" Gao Zhengyi asked, sharp with disbelief. "Why in hell is a local hardware shopkeeper from Yunxi County sitting at the bottom of a 100 trillion yuan treason file?"

"That is what I am asking," Zhou Zhenhua said, turning the sheet over to show its blank back. "Look at the order note in the corner. Black Guard held Deng Yongchang, Yan Tiehu, Cheng Biao, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong inside Yunxi County on purpose so provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terror police can arrest them cleanly at dawn. Zero did not touch them."

"Is this Chen Mingde a secret informer?" Gao Zhengyi pressed, his eyes narrowing with habit. "Did he stumble onto a smuggling book in his hardware shop? Or is he related to a retired general?"

Gu Chengshan reached across, scanned the crisp lines on the note, and slowly shook his head. "Chen Mingde," Gu Chengshan murmured quietly. "Fifty-two years old. Runs a small hardware shop on Old Street in Yunxi County. Zero party rank. Zero army record. Zero central ties. Not a single line of intelligence value."

"Then why is his name inside Case One?" Zhou Zhenhua asked, looking sharply at all three men. "We have three hundred and eighteen top officials arrested across forty ministries, forty-seven foreign spies in custody, navy radar charts, southern grid keys, and 104 trillion yuan in stolen state money. How on earth does a tiny county hardware shopkeeper end up at the back of the master treason vault?"

Across the table, General Qin Zhengguo had not moved a muscle since Zhou Zhenhua first said Yunxi County.

"Hand me that sheet," Qin Zhengguo said quietly.

Zhou Zhenhua slid the white page across the wood table. Qin Zhengguo picked it up by the edges, his eyes moving slowly down every line: Yunxi County, Old Street, Deng Yongchang, Yan Tiehu, Cheng Biao, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Chen Mingde. Beneath it sat one stark order: Lawful local sweep only. Clear Chen Mingde through normal county legal review. Zero central inquiry.

As Qin Zhengguo read, a sudden, chilling clarity washed across his weathered face. His jaw set hard as stone. His eyes lingered for three full seconds on the exact time of Chen Mingde's wrongful arrest, then shifted back to the exact time of Black Guard's nationwide launch.

He did not know the name of Chen Mingde's nephew. Nobody in the capital knew Chen Xuan's face, his office desk, or his private family life. Yet Qin Zhengguo had spent forty years studying how landslides begin.

Slowly, carefully, Qin Zhengguo laid the white sheet inside the back cover of Case One. He closed the thick folder with a dull thump and rested his flat palm on the blank red wax seal.

"Qin Zhengguo?" Gu Chengshan asked, his voice dropping as he saw the stillness in the general's eyes. "What did you just see?"

Qin Zhengguo looked across the smoke-filled table. His gaze moved from Gu Chengshan to Zhou Zhenhua, then rested on Gao Zhengyi.

"Listen to me very carefully," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice cold enough to freeze the air. "Someone kicked a stone in Yunxi, and the whole mountain fell down. Do not dig into who owns the stone. Clean the mountain, and free that shopkeeper cleanly by the book."

The words hung in the gray smoke like a drawn blade.

Zhou Zhenhua's mouth parted. A sudden chill ran down his spine as the meaning hit him. His eyes widened and dropped to the closed cover of Case One. "You mean," Zhou Zhenhua began, then caught himself and swallowed hard. "You mean all of tonight, all forty ministries, all three theater commands, all forty-two black cases, started because someone in Yunxi County tried to crush an ordinary shopkeeper?"

"I told you not to dig," Qin Zhengguo snapped, eyes flashing with cold warning. "Do not ask who Chen Mingde is to anyone. Do not send a central officer to his door. Do not send a provincial leader to visit his shop, do not offer him a medal, and do not put a watch on his street. Let provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terror teams sweep up Deng Yongchang, Yan Tiehu, Cheng Biao, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong strictly under local law. Let the county prosecutor drop the false charge against Chen Mingde cleanly by the book, and let that man walk home in peace."

Gao Zhengyi stared at the closed folder, the blister on his burned fingers throbbing faintly. Cold sweat broke out along his neck as the terrifying scale of what he had almost poked into settled in his chest. "Understood," Gao Zhengyi said hoarsely. "Strictly provincial authority. Zero central footprints. Zero questions asked."

"And the other forty-one cases?" Zhou Zhenhua asked, his voice barely steady as he glanced down the long line of black cases.

Gu Chengshan stood straight, buttoning his jacket with steady hands. The shock had burned away, leaving only grim resolve in his eyes.

"At six o'clock, the State Council, the Military Commission, and the CCDI open all forty-two cases together," Premier Gu Chengshan ordered, his voice clear through the smoke. "Every one of those three hundred and eighteen top officials goes into formal custody. Every one of those forty-seven foreign spies goes to State Security. By seven, the provincial task force lands in Yunxi County to clean out Deng Yongchang and his four partners by the book."

Gu Chengshan looked one last time across the table at General Qin Zhengguo.

"We clean the mountain," Gu Chengshan said quietly. "And nobody in this capital ever speaks a word about the stone."

At four fifty in the morning, outside the sealed double doors of the briefing hall, the first gray of Monday dawn touched the rooftops of the capital.
