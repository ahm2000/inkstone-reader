# Chapter 121: Li Weiming's Next Move

The private tea room on the top floor of the Wanfeng Freight building smelled of roasted oolong and cigarette smoke.

Li Weiming sat by the low mahogany table, his gray suit wrinkled from the four-hour drive north out of Shanghai.

Across the table, Song Baisheng leaned back in his wooden chair and tapped a cigarette against his lighter.

"You look like you ran all the way from Shanghai on foot, Old Li," Song Baisheng said with a dry chuckle. He lit his cigarette, blew gray smoke toward the ceiling, and pushed a porcelain teacup across the table. "Drink first. Your hands are still stiff."

Li Weiming ignored the tea.

"Shanghai is finished," Li Weiming said, his voice tight. "The port customs sweep cleaned out every broker we paid for six years. Two of my local managers are already in detention, and the three crews I sent to watch the docks disappeared off the street without leaving a whisper."

Song Baisheng's smile cooled. He leaned forward, resting his forearms on the table. "Disappeared? You mean the police picked them up?"

"If the police had picked them up, my lawyers would have a detention slip by now," Li Weiming muttered, tapping ash sharply into the glass tray. "Nobody knows who took them. One minute they were parked outside a shipping office in Shanghai. The next minute their phones went dead and their vans were parked neatly at a scrapyard with the keys still in the ignition."

For a few seconds, only the low hum of the air conditioner filled the room.

"Then why are you sitting in my office in Sha City?" Song Baisheng asked flatly. "If you brought heat north with you, Old Li, finish your tea and take the highway right back out of town. Wanfeng Freight has forty-two warehouses and three hundred trucks running clean schedules. I am not burning my company down for your overseas friends."

"Nobody followed me," Li Weiming said sharply. "I switched cars twice at service stations and threw my old phones into a river. And I am not here to hide, Song Baisheng. I am here because Zurich just approved 15 million yuan in clean consulting fees for the man who gives our remaining port-data servers a legal roof."

Song Baisheng stopped mid-motion, his cigarette hovering over the ashtray. His eyes narrowed with sudden calculation.

"15 million," Song Baisheng repeated slowly. "In real money, or in overseas paper I can never spend?"

"5 million wired to your Hong Kong trading account the day the contract is signed," Li Weiming replied without blinking. "Another 10 million in three monthly payments once the servers are online inside your network. Plus a four-percent share of every priority cargo clearance fee that passes through."

Song Baisheng sat back and let out a long breath of smoke. The fear in his eyes had already given way to the itch of easy money.

"Tell me what is left of your setup," Song Baisheng said quietly. "If those servers are marked by the Shanghai sweep, putting them in my basement is the same as hanging a target over my head."

"The three backup servers were never in Shanghai," Li Weiming said, leaning closer across the table. "We kept them offline in a bonded storage room outside Sha City for emergencies. The hardware is clean. No customs officer has ever seen the serial numbers. What we lost in Shanghai was our legal excuse to connect them to the coastal port schedules."

Song Baisheng nodded slowly, tapping his fingers on the wooden armrest. "And you want to use next week's Sha Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum."

"You are the chairman of the provincial freight association," Li Weiming said, a cold smile touching his mouth. "Every major shipping company in the province will sit in that convention hall next week, along with two deputy governors and the provincial commerce office. If Wanfeng Freight introduces a shared smart-routing and cold-chain alliance on stage, twenty local freight companies will rush to sign just to get their photo in the provincial paper."

"And inside the technical pages of that alliance contract," Song Baisheng said softly, "my company becomes the official data hub for the whole province."

"Exactly," Li Weiming whispered. "Once the provincial commerce office stamps that agreement, our three backup servers plug straight into the port and highway schedules under Wanfeng Freight's legal license. Nobody from Shanghai or the capital will question a provincial logistics platform approved in front of two hundred cameras."

Song Baisheng crushed his cigarette into the ashtray and poured himself a fresh cup of oolong.

"There is one problem, Old Li," Song Baisheng said carefully. "The provincial commerce office invited a handful of clean freight companies from Shanghai as model guests. If someone from Shanghai reads the technical pages too closely, they might ask why a local alliance needs overseas routing servers."

Li Weiming waved a hand dismissively. "Who from Shanghai is coming? Small shipping clerks and frightened managers who barely survived last week's sweep. They will spend three days clapping at speeches, eating free banquet shrimp, and begging you for a highway contract. None of them will read a forty-page software annex."

"What about Haiyue Group?" Song Baisheng asked, raising a thick eyebrow. "I heard Qin Zhengming's daughter, Qin Yuhan, is on the guest list."

Li Weiming's jaw tightened before he forced a cold laugh. "Qin Yuhan is a young woman trying to look tough for her father's board. We tuck the technical annex behind the main partnership pages and hand out the folders ten minutes before the stage photo. Even if she squints at it, she will not stall a provincial ceremony in front of the deputy governor."

Song Baisheng thought for several seconds, turning his teacup between his thick fingers before looking straight into Li Weiming's bloodshot eyes.

"Make the upfront payment 6 million instead of 5 million," Song Baisheng said smoothly. "I have to grease two clerks in the forum committee to push my speech into the main morning slot and make sure nobody edits the contract beforehand."

Li Weiming stared at him for a beat, then nodded sharply. "6 million on signing day. 9 million after the servers go live. Same 15 million total, and I will sit at a side table under another name, in glasses, away from the cameras, to watch the contract get stamped."

Song Baisheng grinned and lifted his cup across the table.

"Then welcome to Sha City, Old Li," Song Baisheng said smoothly. "Let the inspectors in Shanghai chase empty vans all week. By the time the forum closes, we will own the cleanest shipping highway in the province."

Li Weiming lifted his lukewarm teacup and clinked it lightly against Song Baisheng's rim. For the first time since fleeing Shanghai, the tight knot in his chest began to loosen.

Neither man noticed the quiet maintenance van parked fifty meters down the street outside the Wanfeng Freight gate, or the faint click of a camera lens behind its dark front glass.
