# Chapter 121: Lazy Mornings at Emerald Riverside Villa

After a final morning rematch with Elder Qin and an unhurried flight back to Shanghai, life at Emerald Riverside Villa returned to its quiet rhythm. Through the tall glass windows, the Huangpu River glinted past the riverside lawn toward the towers of Lujiazui.

Inside the open kitchen, the rich aroma of toasted sesame oil and chopped green onions drifted across the warm wooden floor.

Chen Xuan stood in front of the stove wearing a light blue cotton apron over his white dress shirt. A pair of bamboo chopsticks rested loosely in his right hand as he flipped a golden, flaky scallion pancake in a cast-iron skillet. The oil sizzled softly, and the edges of the dough puffed up into crisp layers. On the back burner, a clay pot of yellow millet porridge bubbled gently.

Beside the stove, his dented green metal thermos sat open on the marble counter, steaming with newly brewed Yunnan red tea and three red wolfberries floating near the rim.

Quiet footsteps clicked down the wooden staircase. A moment later, Qin Yuhan walked into the dining area in a cream silk blouse and charcoal tailored trousers. Her dark hair was tied back in a neat low bun, and the cool autumn morning light caught the soft curve of her smile as she breathed in the smell of breakfast.

"You only wake up before sunrise when you feel like making scallion pancakes," Qin Yuhan teased, pulling out a dining chair.

"A wise husband knows that good food keeps the peace at home," Chen Xuan replied without turning around. He slid the crispy pancake onto a warm white plate, sliced it into four neat triangles, and set it in front of her beside a bowl of golden millet porridge. "Besides, you have a long week at Haiyue Tower. If I send you to the 38th floor on an empty stomach, your board members will think I am mistreating you."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft laugh and picked up her chopsticks. The first bite crunched lightly, warm and savory with scallion and sea salt. "Grandpa called last night while you were doing the dishes. He asked when you are coming back for a rematch on the chessboard. He still refuses to believe you beat him twice in a row."

"Tell Elder Qin I am always ready," Chen Xuan said, untying his apron and sitting across from her. He took a slow sip from his dented 15-yuan thermos and sighed happily. "As long as Auntie Lin makes those braised lotus roots again, I will even let him take back two moves."

"You are shameless," Qin Yuhan murmured, though her eyes curved with warmth. After a few bites of porridge, her brow furrowed slightly. "This week really is going to be tiring. Director Zhao and my cousin Qin Haoran have been pushing a new port warehouse acquisition all month. They want to bring it to a vote tomorrow or Wednesday morning. On top of that, I have to attend the Shanghai Chamber of Commerce Charity Gala at the Waldorf on the Bund on Wednesday evening."

"That sounds like a lot of talking," Chen Xuan said calmly. He reached across the table and placed a small glass container of peeled autumn pears beside her handbag. "Do not let a few noisy old men ruin your appetite, Wife. Eat your breakfast first. Whatever proposal they bring up, read the fine print twice and do not rush."

Qin Yuhan looked at the neatly sliced pears and felt the tightness in her shoulders melt away. Everyone at Haiyue Financial Center expected her to be sharp, tireless, and untouchable. Only here, across a sunlit breakfast table from her lazy junior supervisor of a husband, did she feel completely at ease.

"Thank you, Chen Xuan," she said softly. "Are you sure you do not want me to drop you right at the gate of Hengxin Logistics?"

"Definitely not," Chen Xuan said at once, picking up his canvas satchel and his green thermos. "If my coworkers see me stepping out of your silver sedan, Supervisor Hu will start asking me to pay for everyone's afternoon milk tea. Two blocks away is perfect. The autumn air is good for my digestion."

Half an hour later, Qin Yuhan pulled over beside a row of golden plane trees near the Pudong business district. Chen Xuan stepped out, waved her off with an easy grin, and strolled the remaining two blocks at a leisurely pace.

He took the elevator up to the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics, tapped his employee badge at the front desk, and settled into his favorite corner workstation by the window. Around him, keyboards clattered and phones rang as the Monday rush began.

Supervisor Hu Dewei hurried past carrying a stack of shipping manifests, his forehead already shiny with stress. "Chen Xuan! Stop daydreaming and double-check the eastern freight rates before noon. General Manager Sun is in a terrible mood today. He wants our route binders spotless before the end of the week."

"Got it, Supervisor Hu," Chen Xuan replied mildly. He opened his spreadsheet, checked twelve columns of freight tariffs in less than ten minutes, fixed two arithmetic errors left by another clerk, and hit save.

With his morning work cleanly finished, he leaned back in his creaky swivel chair and watched white clouds drift past the glass towers outside. Beneath his desk, his matte-phone vibrated once.

Chen Xuan pulled it out and glanced at the brief text message from Number 1.

"Young Master, the Shanghai Municipal Planning Office will publish the eastern riverside greenbelt zoning notice tomorrow afternoon as scheduled. No interference detected. Do you require any adjustment?"

Chen Xuan's thumb moved lightly over the screen. He typed three words and sent them back.

"Let it run."

He locked the phone, slipped it back into his pocket, and took another slow, contented sip of warm Yunnan red tea. The autumn sun warmed his desk, and the rest of his Monday looked wonderfully quiet.
