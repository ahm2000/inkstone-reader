# Chapter 121: Lazy Mornings at Emerald Riverside Villa

After a final Sunday morning chess rematch with Elder Qin in Beijing and an unhurried afternoon flight back to Shanghai, life at Emerald Riverside Villa settled right back into its quiet rhythm. By Monday morning, crisp autumn light slanted through the tall glass windows, turning the surface of the Huangpu River silver beyond the riverside lawn and the distant towers of Lujiazui.

Inside the open kitchen, the warm aroma of toasted sesame oil and finely chopped green onions drifted across the wooden floor.

Chen Xuan stood before the stove wearing a light blue cotton apron over his white dress shirt. A pair of bamboo chopsticks rested loosely in his right hand as he flipped a golden, flaky scallion pancake in a cast-iron skillet. The oil sizzled softly, and the edges of the dough puffed into crisp, layered rings. On the back burner, a small clay pot of yellow millet porridge simmered with a gentle rhythm.

Beside the stove, his dented green thermos sat open on the kitchen counter, steaming with newly brewed Yunnan red tea and three plump red wolfberries floating near the rim.

Quiet footsteps clicked down the wooden staircase. A moment later, Qin Yuhan walked into the dining area in a cream silk blouse and charcoal tailored trousers. Her dark hair was gathered in a neat low bun, and the cool morning light caught the soft curve of her smile as she breathed in the smell of breakfast.

"You only wake up before sunrise when you feel like making scallion pancakes," Qin Yuhan teased, pulling out a chair at the dining table.

"A wise husband knows that hot breakfast keeps the peace at home," Chen Xuan replied without turning around. He slid the crispy pancake onto a warm white plate, cut it into four neat triangles, and set it before her beside a bowl of golden millet porridge. "Besides, you have a packed week at Haiyue Tower. If I send you into Monday meetings on an empty stomach, your directors will think I am mistreating you."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft laugh and picked up her chopsticks. The first bite crunched lightly, warm and savory with scallion and sea salt. "Grandpa called last night while you were washing the dishes. He asked when you are coming back to Beijing for another rematch. He still refuses to believe you beat him twice in a row."

"Tell Grandpa I am ready whenever he wants," Chen Xuan said, untying his apron and sitting across from her. He took a slow sip from his green thermos and sighed with easy contentment. "As long as your mom makes those braised lotus roots again, I will even let him take back two moves."

"You are shameless," Qin Yuhan murmured, though her eyes curved with warmth. After a few spoonfuls of porridge, her brow knitted slightly. "This week really is going to be exhausting. Director Zhao and my cousin Qin Haoran have been pushing a northern dock warehouse purchase all month. They want to force a board vote tomorrow or Wednesday morning. On top of that, I have to attend the Shanghai Chamber of Commerce charity dinner at the Waldorf on the Bund on Wednesday evening."

"That sounds like a lot of talking," Chen Xuan said calmly. He reached across the table and placed a small glass container of freshly peeled, sliced autumn pears beside her handbag. "Do not let a few noisy people ruin your appetite, Wife. Finish your porridge first. Whatever contract they slide across the table, read the fine print twice and do not let them rush you."

Qin Yuhan looked at the neatly sliced pears and felt the knot between her shoulders loosen. Everyone at Haiyue Financial Center expected her to stay sharp, tireless, and untouchable from dawn until night. Only here, across a sunlit breakfast table from her unhurried logistics clerk of a husband, could she let her guard down completely.

"Thank you, Chen Xuan," she said softly. "Are you sure you do not want me to drop you right at the gate of Hengxin Logistics?"

"Definitely not," Chen Xuan answered at once, picking up his canvas satchel and his green thermos. "If my coworkers see me stepping out of your silver sedan, Supervisor Hu will start expecting me to buy everyone's afternoon milk tea. Two blocks away is plenty. The autumn breeze is good for digestion."

Half an hour later, Qin Yuhan pulled over beside a row of golden plane trees near the Pudong business district. Chen Xuan stepped out, waved her off with a relaxed grin, and strolled the remaining two blocks at an unhurried pace.

He rode the elevator up to the Hengxin Logistics office, tapped his employee badge at the front desk, and settled into his favorite corner desk by the window. Around him, keyboards clattered and desk phones chimed as the Monday rush picked up speed.

Supervisor Hu Dewei hurried past carrying a stack of shipping manifests, his forehead already damp with stress. "Chen Xuan! Stop staring out the window and double-check the eastern freight tariffs before noon. General Manager Sun is in a foul mood today. He wants our route binders spotless before the end of the week."

"Got it, Supervisor Hu," Chen Xuan replied mildly. He opened his spreadsheet, scanned twelve columns of freight rates in less than ten minutes, corrected two arithmetic slips left by another clerk, and hit save.

With his morning work cleanly finished, he leaned back in his creaky swivel chair and watched white clouds drift past the glass towers outside. Inside his pocket, his phone vibrated once.

Chen Xuan pulled it out and glanced at the brief message from Number 1.

"Young Master, the Shanghai Municipal Planning Office will publish the northern dock riverside greenbelt zoning notice tomorrow afternoon as scheduled. No interference detected. Do you require any adjustment?"

Chen Xuan's thumb moved lightly across the glass. He typed three words and sent them back.

"Let it run."

He locked his phone, slipped it back into his pocket, and took another slow, contented sip of warm Yunnan red tea. Autumn sunlight warmed the edge of his desk, and the rest of his Monday looked wonderfully peaceful.
