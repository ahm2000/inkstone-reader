# Chapter 121: Lazy Mornings at Emerald Riverside Villa

Monday morning sunlight poured through the tall glass windows of Emerald Riverside Villa in Pudong, shining across the wooden floor of the open kitchen. Outside, the Huangpu River sparkled in the breeze, and the Oriental Pearl Tower stood clear against the blue Shanghai sky above Lujiazui.

Inside the kitchen, the rich aroma of toasted sesame oil and chopped green onions filled the air.

Chen Xuan stood in front of the stove wearing a light blue cotton apron over his white shirt. A pair of bamboo chopsticks rested loosely in his right hand as he flipped a golden, flaky scallion pancake in a cast-iron skillet. The oil sizzled softly, and the edges of the dough puffed up into crispy layers.

Beside the stove, his scratched green enamel thermos sat open on the marble counter, steaming with newly brewed Yunnan red tea and three red wolfberries floating on top.

Quiet footsteps clicked down the spiral staircase. Qin Yuhan walked into the dining room wearing a cream-colored business suit, her dark hair pinned up neatly for her morning board meeting at Haiyue Tower. She looked every inch the cool president of Haiyue Group who had graduated from Fudan University.

Yet the moment she smelled the fried scallions, her cool expression melted. She set her briefcase down, walked into the kitchen on tiptoe, and reached toward the plate where two finished scallion pancakes were cooling.

Tap.

Before her fingers could pinch a crispy corner, the blunt end of a pair of bamboo chopsticks tapped the back of her hand gently.

"President Qin," Chen Xuan said without even turning his head from the skillet. "Did you wash your hands after buckling your watch and checking your morning emails?"

Qin Yuhan pulled her hand back and glared at the back of his blue apron, though amusement danced in her bright eyes. "Chen Xuan, this is my kitchen, and I bought that flour at the supermarket near Century Avenue on Sunday afternoon."

"You bought the flour, I rolled the dough, and kitchen law says the cook makes the rules," Chen Xuan chuckled, sliding the third golden pancake onto the porcelain plate and turning off the stove. He picked up a sharp knife, sliced the warm pancakes into neat triangular wedges, and held a crispy piece right up to her lips with his chopsticks. "Open up. Be careful, the inside is still hot."

Qin Yuhan leaned forward and bit into the pancake. The outer crust shattered with a light crunch, while the inside was soft, savory, and fragrant with fresh green onions and toasted white sesame seeds. Her eyes curved into happy crescents as she chewed.

"How do you always get so many flaky layers inside?" Qin Yuhan asked after swallowing, reaching for a warm glass of soy milk on the counter. "When our weekend housekeeper Auntie Zhang makes them, they are never this crispy."

"The secret is rolling the oiled dough three extra times and letting it rest while the tea steeps," Chen Xuan said proudly, untying his blue apron and hanging it on the brass hook by the pantry door. He carried the plate of pancakes, two bowls of millet porridge, and a small dish of sweet pickled radish over to the sunlit dining table. "Sit down and eat a proper breakfast before you go run your meetings in Lujiazui. You barely ate dinner on the flight back from Beijing yesterday."

Qin Yuhan sat down across from him, picked up her chopsticks, and watched him settle lazily into his chair.

Looking at his relaxed posture, she thought back to their weekend in Beijing for Elder Qin Zhengguo's seventy-eighth birthday. Neither she, nor her parents Qin Zhengming and Lin Shuyin, nor her retired general grandfather had any idea that the man who had just fried scallion pancakes for her was the true master of the White Order, the Black Order, and the 300 trillion yuan sovereign reserve stored across China Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China.

To Qin Yuhan, he was simply her warm-hearted husband who earned twelve thousand yuan a month at Hengxin Logistics.

"By the way," Qin Yuhan said, taking another triangle of scallion pancake. "On the plane home from Beijing yesterday, my mother sent me three voice messages. Even after Grandfather told her to stop nagging us, she still hopes you will transfer to Haiyue Group. Our supply chain department has a deputy director opening this month with an annual salary of 600k yuan, only two floors below my office in Haiyue Tower."

Chen Xuan blew on his hot millet porridge and shook his head immediately. "Not interested. Thank Mother Lin for me, though."

"Why not?" Qin Yuhan teased, propping her chin on one hand as she smiled at him. "If you come work at Haiyue Group as a deputy director, nobody in my family can complain about your twelve-thousand-yuan clerk job anymore. Plus, you would get to see your wife during work hours."

"President Qin, do not try to tempt me with your beauty," Chen Xuan laughed, pointing his spoon at her. "I know how executives live at Haiyue Group. You hold meetings at eight in the morning, eat cold sandwiches at noon, review contracts until eight at night, and answer phone calls on Saturday. Why would I trade my peaceful desk at Hengxin Logistics for that?"

"Is a 600k yuan salary worse than twelve thousand yuan?" Qin Yuhan asked, holding back a laugh.

"Money cannot buy my three o'clock afternoon tea break," Chen Xuan replied seriously, tapping his green enamel thermos. "At Hengxin Logistics, I check twenty shipping forms in the morning, water the lucky bamboo on my windowsill, drink hot Yunnan red tea in the afternoon, and pack my bag at four fifty-five sharp so I can buy fresh groceries and cook dinner for my wife. If I become an executive, who is going to make you crispy scallion pancakes in the morning?"

Qin Yuhan looked at the warm plate of golden pancakes and felt a soft warmth spread through her chest.

"Fine, stay a lazy clerk then," Qin Yuhan smiled, leaning across the table to steal the crispest piece of pancake right off his plate. "Since you refuse to earn 600k yuan at my company, you owe me braised spare ribs and lotus root soup for dinner tonight."

"Deal," Chen Xuan grinned, watching her finish her soy milk.

As they walked out to the villa driveway ten minutes later, fifty meters down the riverside street, a plain black sedan belonging to the Black Order quietly kept watch as the ordinary logistics clerk waved goodbye to his wife and strolled toward the subway station with his green thermos tucked under his arm.
