# Chapter 207: Lunch at Haiyue Tower

At 11:55 AM, Chen Xuan pushed through the revolving glass doors of Haiyue Tower, bringing a brief gust of crisp December air into the warm granite lobby. He wore a simple dark down jacket over a soft gray knit sweater, carrying his dented green metal thermos in his left hand and a three-tier stainless steel insulated lunchbox wrapped in a clean checkered cloth in his right.

The lobby smelled faintly of fresh espresso and polished stone. A young receptionist behind the marble counter spotted him immediately and offered a bright, polite nod. Everyone at the front desk knew he had a private executive card for the top floors, though they still had no idea why such an unhurried, friendly young man always carried homemade soup into the heart of Pudong's financial district.

Chen Xuan stepped into the high-speed elevator alongside two young analysts from the finance division who were chatting excitedly about December year-end bonuses and the new northern shipping routes. He leaned comfortably against the brushed brass rail, listening with a faint smile as the digital floor numbers climbed smoothly past the thirties.

When the doors slid open at Haiyue Tower, the hallway was quiet and softly lit. Thick woven carpets muffled his footsteps as he walked past the glass conference rooms toward the corner executive suite.

Secretary Lin Wei looked up from her desk outside the walnut double doors and broke into a warm, relieved smile. "Mr. Chen, you arrived right on time. President Qin finished the coastal route briefing twenty minutes ago, and she barely touched her breakfast this morning."

"I figured as much," Chen Xuan said softly, lifting the cloth-wrapped lunchbox. "December mornings always keep her glued to her desk. Is she alone right now?"

"Yes, she cleared the next hour completely," Lin Wei said, stepping aside to tap the door gently before opening it a crack. "Go right in."

Chen Xuan knocked twice with his knuckles and pushed the heavy walnut door open.

The 38th-floor executive office was spacious and bathed in pale winter sunlight. Floor-to-ceiling windows framed the gray-blue ribbon of the Huangpu River far below, with cargo barges gliding slowly through the cold water. Behind the wide mahogany desk, Qin Yuhan was leaning over a shipping chart, her dark hair pinned up neatly, a slight crease of fatigue between her eyebrows.

Under the desk, she had already kicked off her black leather heels to give her ankles a break after three hours of meetings.

The moment the rich, savory scent of slow-braised beef brisket and sweet winter radish drifted across the room, Qin Yuhan looked up. Her cool corporate expression melted in an instant.

"Xuan!" she called out brightly.

Without bothering to hunt for her shoes, she hurried around the corner of the desk in her stockinged feet, crossing the thick wool rug to meet him. She wrapped her arms briefly around his waist, resting her cheek against his cool down jacket for a second before pulling back with shining eyes.

"You brought the winter radish soup," she said happily. "I smelled it the moment the door opened."

Chen Xuan chuckled, setting the lunchbox on the low coffee table before gently catching her elbow. "Look at you, running around in stockinged feet in December. Even with the floor heating on, you will catch a chill."

He reached beside her desk, picked up the soft fleece indoor slippers she kept tucked behind the cabinet, and set them neatly right in front of her feet.

Qin Yuhan laughed softly and slipped her feet into them without arguing. "You sound just like Grandpa Qin when I forget my coat. Come sit down before the soup cools off."

They settled side by side on the wide cream leather sofa near the window. Chen Xuan shrugged off his down jacket, untied the checkered cloth, and unlatched the three stainless steel tiers one by one.

Warm steam curled immediately into the sunlit air. The bottom tier held tender chunks of beef brisket braised with star anise, ginger, and sweet orange carrots until the sauce shone like dark amber. The middle tier held steaming winter radish and pork rib soup, clear and fragrant with a scatter of bright green scallions floating on top. The top tier carried stir-fried winter greens and fluffy white rice.

Chen Xuan ladled a generous bowl of hot soup first and handed it to her along with a porcelain spoon.

Qin Yuhan blew gently across the surface and took her first sip. Her shoulders dropped almost an inch as the warmth spread straight down to her stomach. "That is wonderful," she murmured. "Every time I eat lunch from the building canteen, I feel hungry again by 3:00 PM. One spoonful of your soup fixes my whole morning."

"I simmered the brisket last night after you fell asleep and heated the radish fresh before I left the house," Chen Xuan said, picking up his own chopsticks and placing a tender piece of beef onto her rice. "You have been working too hard this week. The northern routes are not going to sail away if you take a proper lunch break."

Qin Yuhan smiled warmly, chewing slowly as she studied his relaxed face. "Speaking of work, how did you sneak out of Hengxin Logistics so early? Did your strict department manager let you go without a lecture?"

"Manager Hu is not even on the 12th floor today," Chen Xuan replied easily, taking a sip of warm green tea from his dented green thermos. "He spent the whole morning ironing his best suit and left around 10:00 AM with General Manager Sun. Word around the office is they are chasing a massive client in Pudong."

Qin Yuhan nearly choked on her laugh, quickly covering her mouth with a napkin as a mischievous sparkle danced in her eyes.

"A massive client in Pudong, huh?" she teased softly. "Must be a very important company."

"Sounds like it," Chen Xuan said with a casual shrug, entirely unbothered. "Good for them. As long as the 12th floor stays quiet and I can clock out at 4:55 PM to pick up groceries for dinner, they can chase whatever client they like."

Qin Yuhan shook her head affectionately, leaning closer so her shoulder brushed his. "Well, Mr. Logistics Clerk, finish that piece of beef first. Because it turns out I actually need your professional opinion on something before my afternoon guests come upstairs."
