# Chapter 135: Shoulder Rubs and Sealed Minutes

Soft golden light from the floor lamp filled the living room of Emerald Riverside Villa that Sunday night, casting a cozy glow over the wide beige sofa.

They had returned from Shanghai Hongqiao Station just over an hour earlier. After a long hot shower that washed away the dust of the train journey, Qin Yuhan curled up sideways on the plush couch in a loose cream knit sweater. A soft cashmere blanket rested over her knees, and a glass bowl of freshly washed green grapes sat within easy reach on the coffee table.

Chen Xuan sat right behind her on the sofa cushion, his gray cotton sleeves rolled neatly up to his elbows.

He pressed his thumbs steadily into the tight muscles along the top of her shoulders, working in slow, warm circles from the base of her neck out toward her collarbones.

"Right there," Qin Yuhan let out a long, blissful sigh, her head drooping forward as every bit of stiffness from the high-speed train melted under his warm hands. "Ah, that feels amazing. Honestly, Chen Xuan, where on earth did a logistics clerk learn to give shoulder rubs like this?"

"Years of lifting heavy shipping binders at the office," Chen Xuan joked lightly, easing his grip into a gentle, steady rhythm along her upper back. "Builds very dependable thumbs."

"Nonsense," she giggled softly, reaching back without looking to press a crisp green grape right against his lips. "Your excuses get lazier every week. Open up."

Chen Xuan caught the sweet grape cleanly, smiling as he chewed. "Careful, President Qin. If you spoil me like this, I might refuse to get out of bed tomorrow morning."

"You have to get up," Qin Yuhan teased, turning her head slightly so her warm cheek brushed his forearm. "You promised to clock in at Hengxin Logistics on time, clock out at 4:55 PM sharp, and stop by the corner market for a fresh sea bass."

"A promise is a promise," Chen Xuan murmured warmly. "Steamed sea bass with ginger and a pot of lotus root soup tomorrow night. Deal?"

"Deal," she whispered, her eyelids already growing heavy from the warmth of the room and the steady comfort of his hands.

Within ten minutes, her breathing slowed into a soft, even rhythm. Her head tilted gently back against his chest, fast asleep.

Chen Xuan smiled faintly. Moving slowly so he would not wake her, he pulled the cashmere blanket up over her shoulders and rested one arm securely around her.

On the glass coffee table, his phone lit up silently for two seconds before dimming again.

Chen Xuan glanced over at the brief message glowing on the screen. It was from Number 1.

"Young Master, the Jingzhou harbor files are officially closed, and Ding Yizhen's entire network has been handed over to the provincial discipline team. All quiet on our end."

Chen Xuan reached out with his free hand, tapped the screen once to clear the notification, and placed the phone face down beside the bowl of grapes. His expression remained completely calm. The storm in Handong was over, and inside this quiet Pudong living room, there was only the soft breathing of his sleeping wife.

Over a thousand kilometers north in Beijing, the late-autumn night was colder and far quieter.

Inside a warm, wood-paneled study in the western compound, Premier Gu Chengshan sat across a low mahogany table from ninety-year-old Elder Song. Thin wisps of steam rose from two white porcelain teacups between them.

Lying open on the polished desk was a thick red-bordered dossier: the complete minutes of the Handong shipping forum, the full confession of Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen, and the frozen ledgers of the Zurich holding company.

Premier Gu turned the final page slowly and closed the stiff cover.

"Sha Ruijin and Li Dakang handled the public cleanup cleanly," Premier Gu said in a low, steady voice. "Four coastal logistics hubs are back under honest management, and nearly 800 million in public port funds were recovered before a single yuan crossed the border, covering the 300 million in bidding deposits and another 500 million in hidden harbor rebates."

Elder Song lifted his teacup with calm, practiced hands, taking a small sip. A faint, knowing smile crinkled the corners of his old eyes.

"And the anonymous tip that guided Hou Liangping straight to the Zurich transfer records?" Elder Song asked mildly.

"Clean as fresh snow," Premier Gu replied, shaking his head in quiet admiration. "Not a single fingerprint, not a single phone trace, and not one request for credit. That precision can only belong to the holder of the Black Order."

Elder Song set his teacup down gently on the porcelain saucer.

"Most men who move pieces on a board this large want their names carved into history," the old elder murmured softly. "Our quiet benefactor sweeps away the rot, protects the people doing honest work, and steps straight back into a peaceful life without even looking over his shoulder."

Premier Gu looked at the closed dossier for a long moment. He reached into the brass tray beside his elbow, lifted a heavy red seal, and pressed it firmly onto the top corner of the folder.

Crisp red characters appeared across the cover: TOP SECRET: SEALED.

He rose, carried the dossier to the steel archive cabinet behind his desk, and locked it inside with a sharp click.

Across the entire file, across every briefing room and sealed vault in Beijing, the name of the man who had quietly tipped the scales did not appear even once. Neither Premier Gu nor Elder Song knew his name, his address, or the quiet desk he worked at in Shanghai. To the highest halls of the capital, he remained only a shadow who guarded the country from afar.

Back in Pudong, the living room clock ticked softly.

Chen Xuan bent down, scooped his sleeping wife gently into his arms, and carried her unhurriedly up the wooden stairs of Emerald Riverside Villa. With a quiet tap of his elbow, he clicked off the downstairs lamp, letting the peaceful late-autumn night settle over their home.
