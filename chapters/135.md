# Chapter 135: Shoulder Rubs and Sealed Minutes

By nine thirty that night, the living room lamp of Emerald Riverside Villa cast a soft golden glow across the beige sofa.

They had returned from Shanghai Hongqiao Station an hour earlier. After a hot shower, Qin Yuhan curled up sideways on the long fabric couch in her loose cream home sweater, a light cashmere blanket draped over her knees while a bowl of freshly washed green grapes sat on the glass coffee table.

Behind her on the sofa cushion sat Chen Xuan.

With his sleeves rolled up to his elbows, Chen Xuan pressed his thumbs gently into the tight muscles along the top of her shoulders, kneading in slow, warm circles from the base of her neck out toward her collarbones.

"Right there," Qin Yuhan let out a long, blissful hum, her head drooping forward as all the stiffness from the high-speed train ride melted away under his fingers. "A little bit more on the left side. Oh, that feels amazing."

"You carried that heavy leather briefcase all the way through the station lobby before you let me take it," Chen Xuan scolded lightly, keeping his thumb pressure steady and warm. "No wonder your left shoulder is tied up like a little walnut."

"I did not want the newly stamped Handong warehouse permits to get crumpled," Qin Yuhan murmured sleepily, her eyes half closed as she leaned back into his hands. "Do you know how long Vice President Liu has been trying to get those four coastal licenses approved? Six months. And we brought all four back in one weekend without spending a single extra dime."

"That is because President Qin has sharp eyes and a loud microphone," Chen Xuan chuckled, reaching over with one hand to pop a cold, sweet green grape right between her lips.

Qin Yuhan chewed the sweet grape slowly and smiled without opening her eyes.

"My mother called while you were boiling water in the kitchen," Qin Yuhan said softly. "Father already told her and Grandfather in Beijing all about how I read Article 14 on stage and how you stood right in front of Ding Yizhen in the hallway."

"Oh?" Chen Xuan raised an eyebrow, moving his thumbs smoothly down to her upper back. "What did Mother-in-law say? Did she finally decide I am worth more than my twelve-thousand-yuan paycheck?"

"She said you were still a bit lazy," Qin Yuhan giggled softly, reaching back with one hand to pat his knee. "Wait until you hear what Grandfather said on the phone from the Beijing courtyard. He laughed for a whole minute and said a young man who does not flinch when a deputy mayor barks at his wife has good steady bones, even if he only works in the logistics basement."

"Grandfather has the wisdom of a retired four-star general," Chen Xuan said cheerfully. "Next time we go to Beijing, I will bring him an extra tin of West Lake Dragon Well tea from my desk drawer."

Qin Yuhan turned her head slightly on the cushion, looking up at his relaxed, handsome face under the warm living room lamp.

Nobody in her entire family had the faintest idea who Chen Xuan really was. To her, to her father Qin Zhengming, to her mother Lin Shuyin, and even to her retired military grandfather Elder Qin Zhengguo in Beijing, Chen Xuan was simply her carefree, lucky husband who clocked out at four fifty-five every afternoon and loved cooking her dinner.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan whispered, her voice warm and thick with sleep as she caught his hand and tucked it under her cheek like a pillow. "Marrying a lazy logistics clerk was the best business decision I ever made in my whole life."

"Glad to hear the return on investment meets Lujiazui standards, wife," Chen Xuan smiled softly, leaning down to kiss her forehead. "Rest for five minutes. I will go bring your warm milk."

At that exact moment, seven hundred miles north in Beijing, the heavy red oak doors of a small confidential conference room remained tightly sealed.

Around the long oval table sat Premier Gu Chengshan, Elder Song, and the twenty-four half-premier council members. In front of every seat lay a two-page sealed summary from Handong Province, detailing the morning arrest of Deputy Mayor Ding Yizhen, Cai Chenggong, and overseas broker Li Weiming, along with the thirty-five million yuan Zurich trust and the hidden fiber line to Northern Ledger Limited.

Nobody in the room spoke for a full minute. Even the steam rising from the porcelain teacups seemed still as the senior leaders read the line explaining how every bank receipt and server log had appeared in a plain brown folder on Sha Ruijin's and Ding Guoping's desks at four in the morning, and how four armed thugs had been neutralized on the fourteenth floor of Shanshui Villa in eight silent seconds without waking a single guest.

Premier Gu Chengshan slowly closed the red-stamped folder and looked around the quiet table. "Eighty billion yuan of coastal freight data stopped eighteen hours before the cables went live," Gu Chengshan said in a low, solemn voice. "Just like the four national banks, ICBC, China Construction Bank, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank, someone handed the state the complete knife and let our own laws make the cut."

At the head of the table, Elder Song let out a quiet breath, feeling the same deep chill and unspoken gratitude that weighed on all twenty-four half-premier council members. "The unseen citizen behind the 300 trillion yuan shield is watching the coastlines just as closely as the treasury," Elder Song said quietly, locking the minutes inside the red dispatch box. "Do not pry into who passed the folder in Jingzhou City. Let Handong finish cleaning its harbors according to the law, and remember the debt we owe to that quiet hand."

Back in the sunny kitchen of Emerald Riverside Villa in Shanghai, the person who held that 300 trillion yuan shield in his palm was busy pouring warm milk into a cartoon cat mug.

Chen Xuan carried the mug back into the living room, only to find that Qin Yuhan had already fallen fast asleep on the sofa with a gentle smile on her face.

Shaking his head with a soft chuckle, Chen Xuan set the mug on the coaster, scooped his wife effortlessly up into his arms without waking her, and carried her upstairs to bed.
