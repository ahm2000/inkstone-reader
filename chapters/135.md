# Chapter 135: The Breakfast Arrest

At eight o'clock the next morning, warm sunlight poured through the tall glass windows of the Shanshui Villa breakfast hall.

Long tables held bowls of steaming rice congee, bamboo baskets of pork buns, and pots of hot soy milk.

At a quiet table by the window, Chen Xuan sat calmly in a clean light-blue shirt.

Beside him, Sun Haoran looked as if he had not slept a wink all night.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran murmured in a dazed voice. "I lay awake until four staring at the ceiling. First Haiyue Group's president backs you in front of three hundred chairmen. Then Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin shuts down Deputy Mayor Ding Yizhen in the hallway and nods to you like an honored guest. Tell me the truth. Did you save Secretary Sha's life when you were a boy?"

Chen Xuan smiled lightly, tapped one warm boiled egg gently against his saucer, and began peeling the shell in neat pieces.

"Nothing that dramatic, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Eat your bun before it gets cold. Today is going to be an interesting morning."

Sun Haoran sighed helplessly and finally took a small bite, still shaking his head.

Across the center of the breakfast hall, at a large round table for senior leaders, the mood was heavy as lead.

Deputy Mayor Ding Yizhen sat stiffly with a dark, grim face, barely touching his tea.

"Still nothing?" Cai Chenggong whispered hoarsely under his napkin.

"My four men never called back after midnight," Li Weiming muttered, his voice tight and dry, sweat beading along his hairline. "Their phones are all off. Something went wrong on the eighth floor."

"You idiot!" Ding Yizhen hissed under his breath, setting his spoon down hard. "I told you to keep quiet after Secretary Sha spoke to me! Why did you touch that clerk at midnight?"

"I had no choice!" Li Weiming snapped back quietly, adjusting his silver-rimmed glasses with shaking fingers. "My passport is flagged! If we do not find out who gave Haiyue Group that Zurich registration number, none of us will leave Jingzhou City free men!"

"Wait," Cai Chenggong whispered suddenly, his face losing all color. "Look at the window table."

Ding Yizhen and Li Weiming turned sharply.

Across the sunlit room, Chen Xuan sat calmly beside Sun Haoran, taking a spoonful of plain congee as if nothing had happened at midnight.

A cold terror crept up Li Weiming's throat.

At five minutes past eight, the double glass doors of the breakfast hall swung wide open.

Two straight, brisk lines of officers walked directly into the dining room. In front marched four investigators from the Provincial CCDI, the Party's anti-corruption body, wearing crisp dark jackets and carrying red-stamped folders. Right behind them walked six plainclothes State Security officers, led by the sharp-eyed captain from Corridor Eight.

Every conversation in the breakfast hall died instantly.

Spoons froze halfway to mouths. More than a hundred port directors and chairmen turned their heads as the officers strode straight across the carpet toward the center round table.

The lead Provincial CCDI investigator stopped beside Ding Yizhen's chair, opened his badge case cleanly, and spoke in a clear, steady voice that carried across the silent room.

"Ding Yizhen," the investigator announced formally. "By order of the Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection, you are under formal investigation for serious violations of discipline and law, including taking illegal favors tied to the Provincial Freight Alliance. Stand up and come with us now."

Ding Yizhen's hand jerked violently. His porcelain teacup slid off the saucer and shattered on the marble floor, splashing hot tea across his trousers.

"Wait! There is a misunderstanding!" Ding Yizhen cried, his face going chalk-white as he scrambled half out of his chair. "I already wrote my report for Secretary Sha! I am a Deputy Governor of this province, you cannot take me out of a public dining hall!"

"You can speak at the review office, Ding Yizhen," the CCDI investigator replied coldly. "Do not make this harder on yourself."

Two officers stepped to either side of Ding Yizhen, took his arms firmly, and lifted him from his chair.

At the same second, the State Security captain stepped behind Cai Chenggong and Li Weiming, holding up two detention notices stamped in bright red ink.

"Cai Chenggong. Li Weiming," the captain declared clearly. "You are both detained by State Security for illegal transfer of national coastal shipping data, secret overseas clearing deals tied to Alpine Marine Data Services, and unlawful gains totaling more than 15 million yuan."

Cai Chenggong slumped backward, his mouth opening and closing weakly. "No! I was tricked! Li Weiming drafted Article 14! It was all Li Weiming's idea!"

The State Security captain ignored him and fixed his ice-cold eyes straight onto Li Weiming.

"And Li Weiming," the captain added quietly, his voice cutting through the dead-silent hall. "You are also accused of paying 500k yuan in cash to four armed men for an attempted midnight kidnapping on the eighth floor. All four are in custody and have been taken in for questioning."

The silver-rimmed glasses slipped clean off Li Weiming's sweaty nose and clattered onto his plate.

His legs gave out completely. As two officers snapped steel handcuffs around his wrists and hauled him upright, Li Weiming's terrified eyes shot one last time across the silent dining hall.

They landed straight on the sunlit window table.

Chen Xuan had not even turned his chair around. He simply sat quietly beside his green thermos, peeling the last piece of shell from his boiled egg.

Li Weiming's face twisted in freezing dread as the truth finally hit him. That clerk was never an ordinary office worker. Before he could choke out a word, the officers marched him, a sobbing Cai Chenggong, and a pale, broken Ding Yizhen straight out through the glass doors into the waiting black vans outside.

For ten long seconds after the doors closed, nobody dared breathe. Then a wave of stunned whispers broke out across every table.

Beside the window, Sun Haoran sat frozen like stone, half a pork bun dropping from his chopsticks onto his plate.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran whispered, his voice barely a squeak. "They just took away a Deputy Governor, the head of Wanfeng Freight, and that Zurich advisor right in front of us."

"Good," Chen Xuan said mildly, dipping his clean peeled egg into a pinch of salt. "Now the provincial ports can go back to doing honest business."

Two tables away, Qin Yuhan sat gracefully with a cup of hot black tea. She watched the glass doors swing shut behind the last officer, then slowly turned her gaze toward the window table.

Across the quiet space, Chen Xuan looked up and caught her eye.

He gave her a calm, warm smile, then lowered his gaze back to his breakfast, took a bite of his boiled egg, and washed it down with a spoonful of warm white congee.
