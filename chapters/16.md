# Chapter 16: Saturday Morning at Fuxing New Village

Dawn reached the sixth floor of Fuxing New Village before the county road woke. Chen Xuan stepped out of his old bedroom, easing the latch shut so it would not click. Across the hall, Chen Jianguo breathed evenly inside. After two sleepless nights over Chen Mingde, his father had finally rested once Chen Xuan gave his calm, immovable word that the matter was already handled and no one in the family would step near the county police station.

In the kitchen, Chen Xuan rinsed two cups of millet, added sweet potato and red dates to his mother's clay pot, and set the flame low. Twenty minutes later, Liu Meifang shuffled into the doorway, pulling a cardigan over her shoulders.

"You are up already?" she asked softly, eyes catching the faint shadow under his lashes. "You traveled yesterday. You should have slept longer."

"I wake early in Qingyan," Chen Xuan said calmly, skimming the rim of the pot. "Let Dad sleep another hour. Have a bowl first, then I will walk with you to the morning market."

Liu Meifang hesitated, fingers bunching her cardigan. "You do not need to come, Xuan. I am only buying greens and a little pork."

"Someone has to carry the basket," Chen Xuan replied evenly. "And I want the walk."

Her shoulders dropped half an inch, quiet relief softening her eyes. Since Thursday, when Hongrui Real Estate's men shouted outside Mingde Hardware and word of Chen Mingde's detention spread, every trip downstairs had felt like walking through needles.

"Your father still wakes up thinking about Mingde," she murmured over her porridge, stirring without eating. "He trusts you, and I do too. It is just hard when neighbors stare like our house is on fire."

"Let them stare," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Second Uncle Chen Mingde will be home soon. Eat well, and do not answer anyone asking questions they have no business asking."

Liu Meifang studied his composed face, nodded slowly, and lifted her spoon.

After breakfast, Chen Xuan took the woven basket and a thermos of hot jasmine tea, walking behind his mother down six flights of stairs. At the ground landing, Auntie Zhou from the third floor stopped sweeping the instant she saw them, eyes sharpening with eager curiosity.

"Oh, Meifang, heading to the market?" Auntie Zhou called out, stepping closer. "I heard Hongrui Real Estate brought the police straight to your brother-in-law's shop. Did your husband go back to the station to plead?"

Liu Meifang stiffened, hand tightening on her purse. Before she could force a polite evasion, Chen Xuan stepped level with her and held Auntie Zhou with a calm, unreadable gaze.

For two seconds, Auntie Zhou's smile froze. The gossip died in her throat, her eyes darting down to the basket and back up, flustered.

"Morning, Auntie Zhou," Chen Xuan said politely. "Nothing for anyone to worry about."

"Oh, morning, Chen Xuan," Auntie Zhou stammered, stepping quickly aside. "Good, good, I will not keep you."

She lowered her head and swept furiously at clean concrete. Outside, Liu Meifang let out a long breath, a startled smile tugging at her mouth. "She usually corners me for ten minutes. You barely said a word."

"People only talk when they think you are afraid," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

At the market, Liu Meifang walked noticeably lighter, choosing spinach, white radishes, and pork ribs while Chen Xuan paid before she could open her purse. At the fork near the old grain warehouse, he paused.

"Go on up and rest," he told her. "I will stop by the alley behind the middle school for fifteen minutes. Wang Lei should be open."

Liu Meifang smiled warmly. "Give him my regards. Tell him to come for dinner once his mother's legs ease up."

Chen Xuan watched his mother enter Fuxing New Village before walking down the lane to a low courtyard with a flaking green gate. Inside, Wang Lei squatted beside his battered electric delivery tricycle, grease on his knuckles, prying a punctured tire off a bent rim.

"That rim is warped on the left edge," Chen Xuan said quietly from the gate. "Pry from there and you will pinch the tube again."

Wang Lei froze, then looked up with a wide, tired grin. "Chen Xuan? Nobody told me you were back." He started to stand, knees popping. "Wait, let me wash my hands and grab a stool."

"Finish the wheel first," Chen Xuan said. Setting the basket on a crate, he squatted opposite Wang Lei and braced the tire bead firm while Wang Lei slid the iron underneath.

Together they found the pinhole in soapy water. While the rubber cement dried, Chen Xuan poured steaming jasmine tea into the thermos cap and set it beside Wang Lei's elbow.

Wang Lei laughed softly, picking up the warm cap with his cleaner hand. "Still carrying jasmine tea in the same old thermos." He sipped, and his jaw tightened. "Tell me straight, Chen Xuan. How is Second Uncle Chen Mingde? Hongrui Real Estate's loudmouths were bragging near the freight depot yesterday. I wanted to come by last night, but my south-township run went past ten."

"Second Uncle Chen Mingde will be home soon," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Do not worry about the noise on Old Street."

Wang Lei searched his face, finding a quiet steadiness that made panic seem foolish. His brow eased. "Good. If your family needs to haul stock out of Mingde Hardware, call me. My tricycle looks like scrap, but it still climbs hills."

"I know," Chen Xuan replied, refilling the cap. "How are your routes this month? And how is your mother's knee in this damp weather?"

Wang Lei pressed the patch flat and tapped it with a wrench. "Steady enough. Groceries in the morning, hardware cartons after lunch. Thirty or forty yuan a trip when it stays dry. My mother's knee aches when river fog drops, so I bought herbal plasters on West Street. Nothing we cannot handle."

"Take Sunday afternoon off," Chen Xuan said quietly. "A knee does not heal if you are carrying her up the steps half the week."

Wang Lei gave a short, stubborn laugh. "If I stop running, the depot hands my morning route away. We are fine, Chen Xuan. Worry about your uncle, not me."

Watching Wang Lei's scarred knuckles work the pump, Chen Xuan remembered high school twelve years earlier. During a lean winter when Chen Xuan lived on steamed buns and boiled water, Wang Lei never offered pity. Every noon for a semester, Wang Lei dropped two meat buns and a salted egg onto Chen Xuan's desk, claiming his mother packed too much. Eight yuan a day out of thin pocket money, given so casually that a proud teenager could accept it without feeling small.

Chen Xuan now held wealth that could buy Yunxi County ten times over before breakfast. Yet flaunting money would only bruise something honest between them. A quiet debt deserved quiet respect.

Once the wheel was bolted true, Chen Xuan stood, lifted a parcel holding half the pork ribs and spinach, and hung it on Wang Lei's handlebar.

"Mom bought too much," Chen Xuan said matter-of-factly. "She said it will sit until Monday. Stew it with ginger for your mother tonight."

Wang Lei looked at the bag, then at Chen Xuan, old recognition warming his eyes. The refusal died gently on his tongue. "Tell Auntie Liu Meifang thank you. Stay sharp while you are home, Chen Xuan. Those Hongrui Real Estate people have no bottom line."

"They will find one soon enough," Chen Xuan said calmly, and walked back toward Fuxing New Village.
