# Chapter 16: Saturday Morning at Fuxing New Village

Dawn reached the sixth floor of Fuxing New Village before the county road even woke up. Chen Xuan stepped out of his old bedroom, gently easing the door shut so it would not click. Across the hall, Chen Jianguo breathed evenly in his sleep. After two sleepless nights worrying about Chen Mingde, his father had finally rested once Chen Xuan gave his calm, firm promise that everything was already handled and nobody in the family would go near the county police station.

In the kitchen, Chen Xuan washed two cups of millet, added sweet potato and red dates to his mother's clay pot, and turned the stove flame down low. Twenty minutes later, Liu Meifang shuffled into the doorway, pulling a sweater over her shoulders.

"You are up already?" she asked softly, her eyes catching the faint tiredness under his lashes. "You traveled all day yesterday. You should have slept longer."

"I always wake up early in Shanghai," Chen Xuan said calmly, skimming the top of the pot. "Let Dad sleep another hour. Have a bowl first, and then I will walk with you to the morning market."

Liu Meifang hesitated, her fingers twisting the edge of her sweater. "You do not need to come, Xuan. I am only buying greens and a little pork."

"Someone has to carry the basket," Chen Xuan replied evenly. "Besides, I want the walk."

Her shoulders dropped a little, and quiet relief softened her eyes. Since Thursday, when Hongrui Real Estate's men shouted outside Mingde Hardware and word of Chen Mingde's arrest spread, every trip downstairs had felt like walking over hot coals.

"Your father still wakes up thinking about Mingde," she murmured over her porridge, stirring it without eating. "He trusts you, and I do too. It is just hard when the neighbors stare like our house is on fire."

"Let them stare," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Second Uncle Chen Mingde will be home soon. Eat well, and do not answer anyone asking questions that are none of their business."

Liu Meifang studied his calm face, nodded slowly, and picked up her spoon.

After breakfast, Chen Xuan grabbed the woven market basket and a thermos of hot jasmine tea. He walked behind his mother down six flights of stairs. At the ground floor landing, Auntie Zhou from the third floor stopped sweeping the instant she saw them, her eyes lighting up with eager curiosity.

"Oh, Meifang, heading to the market?" Auntie Zhou called out, stepping closer. "I heard Hongrui Real Estate brought the police straight to your brother-in-law's shop. Did your husband go back to the station to beg them?"

Liu Meifang stiffened, her hand tightening on her purse. Before she could force out a polite excuse, Chen Xuan stepped up beside her and met Auntie Zhou with a calm, steady stare.

For two seconds, Auntie Zhou's smile froze. The gossip died in her throat. Her eyes darted down to the basket and back up, flustered.

"Morning, Auntie Zhou," Chen Xuan said politely. "Nothing for anyone to worry about."

"Oh, morning, Chen Xuan," Auntie Zhou stammered, stepping quickly out of the way. "Good, good, I will not hold you up."

She dropped her head and swept furiously at clean concrete. Outside the building, Liu Meifang let out a long breath, a surprised smile tugging at her mouth. "She usually traps me for ten minutes. You barely said a word."

"People only push when they think you are scared," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

At the market, Liu Meifang walked with a much lighter step. She picked out fresh spinach, white radishes, and pork ribs while Chen Xuan paid before she could even open her purse. At the fork near the old grain warehouse, he paused.

"Go on up and rest," he told her. "I will stop by the alley behind the middle school for fifteen minutes. Wang Lei should be open."

Liu Meifang smiled warmly. "Tell him I said hello. Tell him to come over for dinner once his mother's legs feel better."

Chen Xuan watched his mother walk safely into Fuxing New Village before turning down the side lane to a low courtyard with a peeling green gate. Inside, Wang Lei squatted beside his beat-up electric delivery cart, grease on his knuckles, prying a flat tire off a bent wheel rim.

"That rim is bent on the left edge," Chen Xuan said quietly from the gate. "Pry from there and you will pinch the tube again."

Wang Lei froze, then looked up with a wide, tired grin. "Chen Xuan? Nobody told me you were back." He started to stand up, his knees popping. "Wait, let me wash my hands and grab you a stool."

"Finish the wheel first," Chen Xuan said. Setting the basket on a wooden crate, he squatted across from Wang Lei and held the rubber edge steady while Wang Lei slid the iron bar underneath.

Together they found the tiny hole in a pan of soapy water. While the rubber glue dried, Chen Xuan poured steaming jasmine tea into the thermos cap and set it beside Wang Lei's elbow.

Wang Lei laughed softly, picking up the warm cap with his cleaner hand. "Still carrying jasmine tea in the same old thermos." He took a sip, and his jaw tightened. "Tell me straight, Chen Xuan. How is Second Uncle Chen Mingde? Hongrui Real Estate's loudmouths were bragging near the freight yard yesterday. I wanted to come by last night, but my south-township delivery ran past ten."

"Second Uncle Chen Mingde will be home soon," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Do not worry about the noise on Old Street."

Wang Lei searched his face and saw a quiet steadiness that made panic feel silly. His brow relaxed. "Good. If your family needs to haul goods out of Mingde Hardware, call me. My cart looks like scrap metal, but it still climbs hills."

"I know," Chen Xuan replied, refilling the cap. "How are your routes this month? And how is your mother's knee in this damp weather?"

Wang Lei pressed the rubber patch flat and tapped it with a wrench. "Steady enough. Groceries in the morning, hardware boxes after lunch. Thirty or forty yuan a trip when it stays dry. My mother's knee aches when the river fog rolls in, so I bought herbal patches on West Street. Nothing we cannot handle."

"Take Sunday afternoon off," Chen Xuan said quietly. "A knee does not heal if you are carrying her up the stairs half the week."

Wang Lei let out a short, stubborn laugh. "If I stop running, the depot gives my morning route to someone else. We are fine, Chen Xuan. Worry about your uncle, not me."

Watching Wang Lei's scarred hands work the air pump, Chen Xuan remembered high school twelve years ago. During a lean winter when Chen Xuan lived on plain steamed buns and hot water, Wang Lei never showed pity. Every single noon for a whole term, Wang Lei dropped two meat buns and a salted egg onto Chen Xuan's desk, claiming his mother packed too much. Eight yuan a day out of thin pocket money, given so casually that a proud teenager could accept it without feeling small.

Chen Xuan now owned enough wealth to buy Yunxi County ten times over before breakfast. Yet flashing money would only hurt something honest between them. A quiet kindness deserved quiet respect.

Once the wheel was bolted back on straight, Chen Xuan stood up, lifted a bag holding half the pork ribs and spinach, and hung it on Wang Lei's handlebar.

"Mom bought too much," Chen Xuan said casually. "She said it will just sit until Monday. Stew it with ginger for your mother tonight."

Wang Lei looked at the bag, then up at Chen Xuan, old understanding warming his eyes. His refusal died gently on his tongue. "Tell Auntie Liu Meifang thank you. Stay careful while you are home, Chen Xuan. Those Hongrui Real Estate people have no shame at all."

"They will hit a wall soon enough," Chen Xuan said calmly, and walked back toward Fuxing New Village.
