# Chapter 16: Saturday Morning at Fuxing New Village

Dawn reached the sixth floor of Fuxing New Village before the county road woke. Chen Xuan eased his old bedroom door shut. Across the hall, Chen Jianguo breathed evenly. After a sleepless night worrying about Chen Mingde, his father had finally rested once Chen Xuan promised everything was handled and nobody in the family would go near the police station.

In the kitchen, Chen Xuan sat with a glass of water. Twenty minutes later, Liu Meifang shuffled in, pulling a sweater over her shoulders and reaching for the millet, sweet potato, and red dates.

"You are up already?" she asked softly, noticing the tiredness under his eyes. "You traveled all day yesterday. You should have slept longer."

"I always wake early in Shanghai," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Let Dad sleep. Eat first, then I will walk with you to the morning market."

Liu Meifang hesitated, twisting her sweater. "You do not need to come, Xuan. I am only buying greens and pork."

"Someone has to carry the basket," Chen Xuan replied. "Besides, I want the walk."

Quiet relief softened her eyes. Since Friday afternoon, when word of Chen Mingde's arrest spread, every trip downstairs felt like walking over hot coals.

"Your father still wakes thinking about Mingde," she murmured, stirring her porridge. "He trusts you, and I do too. It is just hard when neighbors stare like our house is on fire."

"Let them stare," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Second Uncle Chen Mingde will be home soon. Eat well, and ignore anyone asking questions."

Liu Meifang studied his calm face, nodded, and picked up her spoon.

After breakfast, Chen Xuan took the woven basket and a thermos of hot jasmine tea, walking behind his mother down six flights. On the ground landing, Auntie Zhou from the third floor stopped sweeping the instant she saw them, eyes bright with curiosity.

"Oh, Meifang, heading to the market?" Auntie Zhou called, stepping closer. "I heard Hongrui Real Estate brought police to your brother-in-law's shop. Did your husband go back to the station to beg?"

Liu Meifang stiffened, gripping her purse. Chen Xuan stepped beside her and met Auntie Zhou with a calm, steady stare.

Auntie Zhou's smile froze. The gossip died in her throat, and her eyes darted down, flustered.

"Morning, Auntie Zhou," Chen Xuan said politely. "Nothing for anyone to worry about."

"Oh, morning, Chen Xuan," Auntie Zhou stammered, stepping aside quickly. "Good, good, I will not hold you up."

She dropped her head and swept furiously at clean concrete. Outside, Liu Meifang let out a long breath, smiling in surprise. "She usually traps me for ten minutes. You barely spoke."

"People only push when they think you are scared," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

At the market, Liu Meifang walked with a lighter step, picking spinach, white radishes, and pork ribs while Chen Xuan paid before she could open her purse. Near the old grain warehouse, he paused.

"Go on up and rest," he told her. "I will stop by the alley behind the middle school for fifteen minutes. Wang Lei should be open."

Liu Meifang smiled warmly. "Tell him hello, and invite him for dinner once his mother's legs feel better."

Chen Xuan watched his mother enter Fuxing New Village before turning down a side lane to a courtyard with a peeling green gate. Inside, Wang Lei squatted beside his beat-up electric delivery cart, prying a flat tire off a bent rim.

"That rim is bent on the left," Chen Xuan said quietly from the gate. "Pry there and you will pinch the tube again."

Wang Lei froze, then looked up with a wide, tired grin. "Chen Xuan? Nobody told me you were back. Wait, let me wash my hands and grab a stool."

"Finish the wheel first," Chen Xuan said. Setting the basket on a crate, he squatted across from Wang Lei and held the rubber steady while Wang Lei slid the iron bar under.

They found the pinhole in soapy water. While the glue dried, Chen Xuan poured steaming jasmine tea into the thermos cap and set it by Wang Lei's elbow.

Wang Lei laughed softly, picking it up with his cleaner hand. "Still carrying jasmine tea in the same thermos." He sipped, jaw tightening. "Tell me straight, Chen Xuan. How is Second Uncle Chen Mingde? Hongrui Real Estate's loudmouths were bragging near the freight yard yesterday. I wanted to come by last night, but my south-township run went past ten."

"Second Uncle Chen Mingde will be home soon," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Do not worry about the noise on Old Street."

Wang Lei searched his face and saw a quiet steadiness that made panic feel silly. His brow relaxed. "Good. If your family needs to haul goods out of Mingde Hardware, call me. My cart looks like scrap metal, but it climbs hills."

"I know," Chen Xuan replied, refilling the cap. "How are your routes? And how is your mother's knee in this damp weather?"

Wang Lei tapped the patch flat with a wrench. "Steady enough. Groceries mornings, hardware boxes afternoons. Thirty or forty yuan a trip when dry. My mother's knee aches in river fog, so I bought herbal patches on West Street. Nothing we cannot handle."

"Take Sunday afternoon off," Chen Xuan said quietly. "A knee does not heal if you carry her up stairs half the week."

Wang Lei gave a stubborn laugh. "If I stop, the depot gives my morning route away. We are fine, Chen Xuan. Worry about your uncle, not me."

Watching Wang Lei's scarred hands work the pump, Chen Xuan remembered high school twelve years ago. During a lean winter when he lived on plain buns and hot water, Wang Lei never showed pity. Every noon for a full term, he dropped two meat buns and a salted egg onto Chen Xuan's desk, claiming his mother packed too much. Eight yuan a day from thin pocket money, given so casually a proud teenager could accept without feeling small.

Chen Xuan could pay off every Old Street buyout bill many times over. Yet flashing money would only hurt something honest between them. Quiet kindness deserved quiet respect.

Once the wheel was bolted on, Chen Xuan hung a bag holding half the pork ribs and spinach on Wang Lei's handlebar.

"Mom bought too much," Chen Xuan said casually. "It will sit until Monday. Stew it with ginger for your mother tonight."

Wang Lei looked at the bag, then at Chen Xuan, old understanding warming his eyes. His refusal faded. "Tell Auntie Liu Meifang thank you. Stay careful, Chen Xuan. Those Hongrui Real Estate people have no shame."

"They will hit a wall soon enough," Chen Xuan said calmly, and walked back toward Fuxing New Village.
