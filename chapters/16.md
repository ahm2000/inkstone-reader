# Chapter 16: Nine O'Clock Monday

Party Secretary Fan Weiguo touched his tie. It was flat. He touched it again.

Behind him, twenty-one officials stood in two rows, placed by rank. Every pair of eyes was on the back of his collar.

Fan Weiguo stood in front, because he was the top man in Yunxi. County Magistrate Tao Shu waited half a step to the left, with a warm, neat smile. It was the smile of a man who had never once looked at Fan Weiguo's chair.

Next came Kang Guangming of the Discipline Commission. Then came Shao Wenhua of the Organization Department, then the bureau heads.

After Shao Wenhua, the row had a gap the width of one man. Nobody looked at it.

It was where Lu Wenzhe belonged. Now he sat in his own office. Two Discipline Commission officers stood outside his door.

Ma Baoguo, Chief of the Housing Bureau, had called Lu Wenzhe "Elder Brother" over wine. Now he kept far away from the gap, like a man walking around a puddle.

A red banner stretched over the gate: *Warmly Welcome the Provincial Inspection Team to Yunxi.* Under it sat a fruit basket as big as a washtub.

The provincial inspection team was a group the province sent to read a county's files and question its officials. Its report could lift one man up and drop another. It could send a third out of the building between two silent men in dark jackets.

"Secretary." His young aide breathed the word. "Your hands."

Fan Weiguo pressed them flat against his trousers.

* * *

Across the road, a Golden Wheat Bakery truck stood at the curb with its back doors open and steam pouring out.

Shan Yucai was a baker of the Detail's bread trucks. For nine months he had sold buns to the gate guards every morning. He wiped his floury arms and watched the road.

Pang Jinquan, the gate guard everyone called Old Pang, took two buns. "You smell trouble, Brother Shan?"

"I smell bread, Uncle Pang."

Under the counter, his thumb tapped a phone no bigger than a matchbox. He wrote to An Zhiyuan, Chief of the Detail.

*Three black cars, no flags, turning in. Eighteen minutes early. No one here knows the Boss's name.*

An Zhiyuan's answer came dry and quick.

*Good. The Boss is on the early bus from Yunxi and knows nothing about this. Keep it so.*

Shan Yucai set a bun over the phone and smiled at the next customer.

* * *

On the early bus from Yunxi, Chen Xuan slept against the window with his thermos in his lap and his mouth open.

His phone buzzed twice on his knee. On the third try he answered with his eyes shut.

"Brother!" His sister Chen Yuan's voice came out of the speaker like a boiling kettle. "Did you eat? Are you asleep right now?"

"Morning, Yuan. No. Okay, a little."

"You're asleep! Grandma says you left your jacket on the porch. The cat sat on it all night! Big Brother, you're hopeless! Mom says wear it or she'll come to your office with the ladle!"

"Not the ladle." Chen Xuan sat up with a small groan. "Yuan, please. I'll wear it, I swear. I ate, too. Half a bun."

"Half a bun is not eating!"

"Then tell the cat I'm sorry. And I want my jacket back." He laughed softly. "Tell Mom I love her. Don't tell her I said it. She'll cry."

She hung up on him, trying not to laugh. The old woman in the next seat had heard every word. She passed him half an orange.

"Thanks, Auntie." He ate it, smiling, with his eyes shut.

* * *

At the gate, the three cars slid in without a sound. The two rows stiffened as one.

The middle car stopped, but no one got out. Behind the glass, Fan Weiguo saw a man alone in the back seat. It was Vice Director Gao Zhengyi, head of the team.

Gao Zhengyi smoothed his coat and pulled his tie straight. He sat up like a schoolboy waiting for his headmaster.

Fan Weiguo forgot to breathe.

He had watched many leaders get out of many cars. Not one had ever fixed his tie for a county. A man did that only for someone he feared or respected. Was it the retired leader from the reservoir? Or someone else in Yunxi?

His secretary leaned in with a phone. "Secretary, I ran every name in the Old Street complaint. Chen Xuan is the shop owner's nephew. A logistics clerk. The file says simple, honest, nothing. Not even a parking ticket."

"Nothing?" Fan Weiguo whispered. A man with no past made no sense.

The car door opened. Fan Weiguo hurried up with both hands out, half bowing.

"Vice Director Gao! Welcome! The county leaders have stood here since dawn!"

"Secretary Fan. Magistrate Tao." Gao Zhengyi's voice was quiet and tired, which was worse than a shout. "Please take down the banner."

Fan Weiguo's smile slipped. An aide ran off to find a ladder.

"The fruit too. The team accepts nothing, not even an apple. Give it to your gate guards' families."

Pang Jinquan came back with a bun in his mouth. He stopped in front of the basket and chewed slowly.

"For my wife?" he asked around the bun.

"And your grandchildren," said Gao Zhengyi kindly. "And cancel the lunch. We'll eat in your canteen and pay what everyone pays."

Ma Baoguo jumped forward for the briefcase. "Vice Director, let me!"

"I have two hands, Chief Ma." Gao Zhengyi kept hold of it. "The team carries its own papers. Nobody can say we took them from you."

Then Tao Shu stepped up, helpful.

"Vice Director, your team must not lose even one day. My county self-check group will have every Old Street file ready by tonight."

Gao Zhengyi nodded as if accepting a cup of tea.

"That's generous, Magistrate Tao. The team will read for itself. A man who offers to tidy a file before the inspector comes has just told me which file to open first."

A muscle in Tao Shu's cheek jumped. Fan Weiguo saw it. A small mean joy flickered up in him, then went out. If Gao Zhengyi would not take Tao Shu's files, whose would he take?

Gao Zhengyi looked across the two rows, and every face dropped. At the end his eyes stopped.

"Director Mai."

Police Director Mai Zhenhua stepped out on stiff legs.

"Old Street Station has a duty officer who refused to write a false hour on a detention notice." Gao Zhengyi spoke lightly, as if talking about rain. "Wei Kun, I'm told. We'll go there first. I want to shake his hand. And Director Mai, bring the original detention notice, not a copy. If its hour was changed, I will ask who held the pen."

Mai Zhenhua did not blink.

Behind him the bureau chiefs murmured.

"I always said Wei Kun was a fine officer," said Ma Baoguo.

"You've never met him," hissed the man beside him.

Mai Zhenhua had read leaders' signals for thirty years. He read this one twice. Someone very high had noticed little Wei Kun by name.

Tao Shu murmured at Fan Weiguo's left ear, still smiling. "The vice director goes home on Friday, Secretary. We stay. The Old Street approvals went out under your Party Committee seal. My name is on none of them."

Who in Yunxi was big enough to make a provincial vice director fix his tie?

* * *

That noon, in a provincial office, Pu Jian, an officer of the Provincial Organization Department, got a message from his old classmate Tao Shu. *Quietly check Chen Mingde's nephew. Chen Xuan. Tell no one.*

Pu Jian typed the name. The screen answered at once. *Chen Xuan, 29. Born Yunxi County. Ordinary family. Simple, honest. Nothing.*

Too clean. Tao Shu had asked for more. So Pu Jian typed his own senior code to open a deeper, sealed file.

The screen went red. PERMISSION DENIED. Under it was a second line: *This attempt has been reported.*

Cold sweat ran down his neck. He had opened files on governors. He had never seen that screen.

At six that evening, two men from the top-level review team walked into his office and took him to a closed room. That was shuanggui. Party discipline officers kept a man there for weeks, with no phone and no visitors, until he explained himself. Nobody told him why.

Tao Shu heard it at eight, at his window. His face went pale.

"Gu Min." His voice was thin as a thread. "Nobody asks about that boy again. Not me, not you, not anyone."

Gu Min had served Lu Wenzhe and now carried Tao Shu's briefcase. He wrote nothing down. It was the first order Tao Shu had ever given that he did not want on paper.
