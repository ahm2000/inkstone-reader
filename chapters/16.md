# Chapter 16: Nine O'Clock Monday

Party Secretary Fan Weiguo touched his tie, found it flat, and touched it again.

Behind him, twenty-one officials stood in two rows, placed by rank. Every pair of eyes sat on the back of his collar.

Fan stood in front, because he was the top man in Yunxi. County Magistrate Tao Shu waited a half step to the left, wearing a warm, tidy smile, the smile of a man who had never once looked at Fan's chair.

Next came Kang of the Discipline Commission, then Shao of the Organization Department, then the bureau heads, each trying to stand a little lighter than his neighbor.

After Shao, the row had a hole in it the width of one man. Nobody looked at it.

It was where Lu Wenzhe belonged. Now Lu sat in his own office, and two Discipline Commission officers stood outside his door.

Housing Bureau Chief Ma had called Lu "Elder Brother" over wine on Friday. Now he kept a careful body's length from the hole, like a man walking around a puddle.

A red banner stretched over the gate: *Warmly Welcome the Provincial Inspection Team to Yunxi.* Beneath it sat a fruit basket big enough to bathe a child in.

Every man there knew what a provincial inspection team was. The province sends investigators to read a county's files and question its officials. Their report can lift one man, sink another, and send a third out of the building between two silent men in dark jackets.

Each of them was privately counting which one he would be.

Fan had faced four teams in thirty years. This was the first that made his knees feel thin.

It had been due on Wednesday. Someone far above him had moved it up, and no one would tell him who.

"Secretary," his young aide breathed. "Your hands."

Fan flattened them against his trousers.

* * *

Across the road, a Golden Wheat Bakery truck stood at the curb with its back doors open and steam pouring out.

Baker Shan had sold buns to the gate guards every morning for nine months. He wiped his floury arms and watched the road.

"You smell trouble, Brother Shan?" asked old Pang, the gate guard, taking two buns.

"I smell bread, Uncle Pang."

Under the counter, his thumb tapped a phone no bigger than a matchbox.

*Three black cars, no flags, turning in. Eighteen minutes early. No one here has the Boss's name.*

Chief An's reply came dry and quick.

*Good. The Boss is on the early bus from Yunxi and knows nothing about this. Keep it so.*

Baker Shan set a bun over the phone and smiled at the next customer.

* * *

On the early bus from Yunxi, Chen Xuan slept against the window with his thermos in his lap and his mouth a little open.

His phone buzzed twice on his knee. On the third try he answered with his eyes shut.

"Brother!" Chen Yuan's voice came out of the speaker like a kettle. "Did you eat? Did you call Mom? Are you asleep right now?"

"Morning, Yuan. No."

"You're asleep! Grandma says you left your jacket on the porch, and the cat sat on it all night! You're a grown man, brother!"

"I ate," he murmured. "Tell the cat I want my jacket back."

She hung up on him in despair. The old woman in the next seat had heard every word, and she quietly passed him half an orange.

"Thanks, Auntie," he said, and ate it with his eyes shut.

* * *

At the gate, the three cars slid in without a sound, and the two rows stiffened as one.

The middle car stopped, but no one got out. Behind the glass, Fan saw a man alone in the back seat: Vice Director Gao Zhengyi, head of the team.

Gao smoothed his coat, then pulled his tie square. He lifted his chin and sat straight as a schoolboy waiting for his headmaster.

Fan forgot to breathe.

He had watched many leaders get out of many cars, and not one had ever fixed his tie for a county. A man does that only for someone he fears or someone he respects.

Who here was that man?

His secretary leaned in with a phone. "Secretary, I ran the name in the Old Street complaint. Chen Xuan, the shop owner's nephew, a logistics clerk. The file says simple, honest, nothing. Not even a parking ticket."

"Nothing?" Fan whispered. A man with no past made no sense at all, and he felt more lost than before.

The door swung open, and Fan rushed up with both hands out, half bowing.

"Vice Director Gao! Welcome! The county leaders have stood here since dawn!"

"Secretary Fan. Magistrate Tao." Gao's voice was quiet and tired, which was worse than a shout. "Please take down the banner."

Fan's smile slipped sideways. An aide ran off to find a ladder.

"The fruit too. The team accepts nothing, not even an apple. Give it to your gate guards' families."

Old Pang came back with a bun in his mouth and stopped, chewing slowly, in front of the basket.

"For my wife?" he asked around the bun.

"And your grandchildren," said Gao kindly. "And cancel the lunch. We'll eat in your canteen and pay what everyone pays."

Somewhere inside Fan, a twelve-seat banquet table he had reserved lay down and died.

Chief Ma lunged for the briefcase. "Vice Director, allow me!"

"I have two hands, Chief Ma," Gao said, and kept hold of it. "I'd hate to see you drop this."

Ma backed away and stepped on the shoe of the man behind him.

Then Tao stepped up, bright and helpful.

"Vice Director, your team must not lose even one day. My county self-inspection group will have every Old Street file ready by tonight."

Gao nodded as if accepting a cup of tea.

"That's generous, Magistrate Tao. The team will read for itself. A man who offers to tidy a file before the inspector comes has just told me which file to open first."

A muscle in Tao's cheek jumped. Fan saw it, and a small mean joy flickered up in him. It went out almost at once. If Gao would not take Tao's files, whose would he take?

"Secretary Kang, may the team use your third floor?"

"Everything is ready, Vice Director."

Gao's gaze crossed the two rows, and each face dropped. At the very end it stopped.

"Director Mai."

Police Director Mai Zhenhua stepped out on stiff legs, with wet palms.

"Old Street Station has a duty officer who refused to write a false hour on a detention notice," said Gao lightly, as though talking about rain. "Wei Kun, I'm told. We'll go there first. I want to shake his hand. And Director Mai, bring the original detention notice, not a copy. I'm sure it's where you left it."

Mai did not blink. Who had carried a duty officer's name to the provincial capital in one night?

Behind him the bureau chiefs began to murmur.

"Shake hands with a duty officer?"

"I always said Wei Kun was a fine officer," said Chief Ma.

"You've never met him," hissed the man beside him.

Mai had read leaders' signals for thirty years, and he read this one twice. Someone very high had noticed little Wei Kun by name.

"Vice directors go home to the capital by evening, Secretary," Tao murmured at Fan's left ear, still smiling. "You and I don't."

Fan watched the black cars roll away. He kept one question and could find no answer.

Who in Yunxi was big enough to make a provincial vice director fix his tie?

* * *

That noon, in a provincial office, Wu Jian of the Organization Department got a message from his old classmate Tao Shu. *Quietly check Chen Mingde's nephew. Chen Xuan. Tell no one.*

Wu typed the name. The screen answered at once. *Chen Xuan, 29. Born Yunxi County. Ordinary family. Simple, honest. Nothing.*

Too clean. Tao had asked for more, so Wu typed his own senior code to open the layer below.

The screen went red. PERMISSION DENIED. Under it, a second line: *This attempt has been reported.*

Cold sweat ran down his neck. He had opened files on governors, and he had never seen that screen.

At six that evening, two men from the top-level review team walked into his office and took him to a closed room. That is shuanggui: Party discipline officers hold a man there, with no phone and no visitors, for weeks, until he explains himself.

Tao heard it at eight, at his window. He went pale.

"Gu Min." His voice was a thread. "Nobody asks about that boy again. Not me, not you, not anyone."

Gu Min, who now carried Tao's briefcase, wrote nothing down. It was the first order Tao had ever given that he did not want on paper.
