# Chapter 16: The Cell Door

Qian Yu stamped the receipt at 11:44 and woke the night duty chief. The chief began reading the nineteen pages in his slippers and was wide awake by page four.

At three in the morning, he woke Mai Zhenhua, Police Director of Yunxi, because the folder named one of Mai Zhenhua's own men. Mai Zhenhua said "Yes" eleven times in a row, then sat on the edge of his bed with his feet gone numb.

At midnight, Number Twenty-three had delivered beef noodles with an extra egg and a gray blanket to the family window. Chen Mingde had eaten every last strand, his eyes brimming quietly as he finished the egg.

Two streets away, Number Ninety-five, under cover as head nurse, happened to run into Su Min, Wei Kun's wife. By dawn, a free hospital bed was waiting for her.

At five in the morning, Chen Xuan walked to the police station alone with his thermos and sat on the front steps. He could not sleep, and the steps felt closer to his uncle than his bed. His mother would arrive at 6:35 with soup.

* * *

Inside, Lei Bo slept on the reception bench with his cap pulled over his face.

Wei Kun had not slept at all. He sat behind the glass with a cold cup of tea and one honest detention notice, waiting for the storm to break.

A plain gray sedan pulled up to the steps with no flag and no siren.

The man who stepped out was short and stocky, wearing a dark jacket.

He pushed open the glass door. From his stool in the corner, Number Thirty-four, the records clerk, looked up, and his stapler froze mid-press.

"Director Mai," Number Thirty-four said softly.

The words landed like a fire alarm.

Lei Bo tumbled off the bench and scrambled upright into a salute.

"Where is Director Cheng?"

Cheng Biao hurried out of the back corridor in his shirtsleeves, pulling on his jacket with a toothpick still between his teeth.

"Director Mai! You're here so early! If I'd known, I would have waited at the gate!"

"Is the Chen Mingde case yours?"

"Just a minor scuffle, Director. A driver, an old shopkeeper, a few bruises."

Mai Zhenhua pulled a slip of paper from his pocket.

"The driver's injury note is from Ankang Private Clinic. It's stamped 1:50. The street camera shows he fell at 2:03. Tell me, Director Cheng, how does a man get treated thirteen minutes before he falls?"

The lobby went so quiet that the hum of the vending machine sounded rude.

Cheng Biao swallowed. "Maybe the clocks didn't match, Director."

"The county Party Committee and the city Discipline Commission have made their decision. You are suspended from every post, effective immediately. Hand over your ID and your keys."

The toothpick dropped from Cheng Biao's mouth and rolled to the toe of Mai Zhenhua's shoe.

Cheng Biao turned hoarsely. "Lei Bo. Tell the Director who runs this station."

Lei Bo stared up at the ceiling, then down at his own cap.

"Director Cheng?" Lei Bo looked genuinely puzzled. "I hardly know him!"

Cheng Biao dropped his keys into Mai Zhenhua's palm. Two city officers stepped forward and escorted him toward the door.

Outside on the front steps sat a young man in worn sneakers, holding a green thermos on his knees.

Chen Xuan stood up politely and stepped aside to let the officers pass. He gave a quiet nod. "Morning, Director Cheng."

Cheng Biao's footsteps faltered. He stared at the ordinary clerk standing calmly in the early light, a sudden cold suspicion flaring in his chest.

The officers nudged him forward. Passing Chen Xuan, Cheng Biao leaned in and hissed under his breath, "Don't celebrate too soon, clerk. Your whole family is in Dong Haisheng's book. I'm not the one you should be worried about."

Chen Xuan only offered a faint, polite nod and stepped back to wait for his uncle.

Inside, Mai Zhenhua called out, "Wei Kun."

Wei Kun stepped out from behind the glass and stood at attention.

"Yes, Director."

"Bring me the key to the detention cell."

Wei Kun's knees nearly gave out. He had braced himself for punishment, not trust.

* * *

In the detention corridor, Chen Mingde sat on the concrete bench, folding and unfolding a paper cup.

When the steel door swung open, he rose out of habit, the same way he stood to greet customers at his shop.

"Mr. Chen Mingde." Mai Zhenhua took the old man's hand in both of his and bowed.

"We held you wrongly and damaged your good name. I apologize to you, your wife, and your daughter in Wuhan."

Chen Mingde shook his head gently. "Director, I'm only a hardware shopkeeper. I'm nobody important."

"You are to me." Mai Zhenhua turned to the officers lined along the wall, keeping his voice level. "Every officer who worked this corridor since Friday will submit a written statement by noon detailing who gave which order. Anyone who lies is finished. The next time a citizen is held like this, the officer who signed the order sits on that bench within the hour, and I will lock the door myself."

Once Mai Zhenhua stepped ahead to escort Chen Mingde toward the front lobby, Zhong Hai caught Wei Kun alone at the mouth of the corridor, his hands twitching at his sides.

"Wei Kun," he whispered, pale to the lips. "Before anyone else asks, I have to tell you myself. Yesterday I sent Lei Bo the timestamp you wrote. Seven-fifty. They offered sixty thousand yuan for Zhong Tiantian's heart surgery."

Wei Kun's face went through shock, then a deep, quiet hurt. His right hand clenched into a fist, and Zhong Hai squeezed his eyes shut and waited.

The fist trembled, then slowly opened.

"Does Zhong Tiantian go into surgery on Monday?"

"Monday," Zhong Hai whispered.

"Then go sit with him," Wei Kun said roughly. "And don't speak to me today."

Zhong Hai bowed his head low, turned, and hurried down the hall.

* * *

At 6:20, the glass doors opened and Chen Mingde stepped out into the morning air with a gray blanket draped over his shoulders.

Chen Xuan stood up so fast his thermos rolled down a step.

"Second Uncle." His voice was tight with relief.

"Brother!" Chen Jianguo shouted from the curb. He ran faster than he had in twenty years, catching his brother in a fierce embrace.

Wu Lanying wept silently, pressing both hands over her mouth.

Chen Xuan stepped in and wrapped his arms around his uncle, his eyes reddening as he held on tightly.

"I'm sorry we kept you waiting so long, Second Uncle. I'm just glad you're out."

"Chen Xuan." Chen Mingde's big, rough hand patted the back of his shoulder. "You came. That was all I needed."

Wu Lanying pulled them all together, and for a long minute nobody spoke.

Chen Jianguo's voice shook with relief. "Heaven has eyes."

Chen Mingde shook his head softly. "It was luck. A good director, an early car. I didn't do anything at all."

Wu Lanying dabbed her eyes and touched Chen Xuan's cool cheek. "Chen Xuan, you've been sitting out here since five."

"I couldn't sleep anyway," Chen Xuan said with a warm, tired smile. "Wanted to be the first one to greet him."

At 6:35, Liu Meifang hurried up the street carrying a steel pot of soup and pressed a lid into Chen Mingde's hands.

"Drink! It's still hot! And if anyone says one more word against my brother-in-law, I brought a ladle."

She pressed a second lid into Chen Xuan's hands. "Drink, you too! Look how thin your wrists are."

He took a sip. Seeing Mai Zhenhua standing at the top of the steps, Chen Xuan set the lid down and offered a polite nod.

"Thank you for handling this promptly, Director Mai."

Mai Zhenhua looked at the calm, ordinary young man beside Chen Mingde. Just a logistics clerk, he told himself, yet someone had filed nineteen pages at 11:44 last night.

"It was our duty," Mai Zhenhua replied stiffly. Right then, his phone buzzed in his coat pocket. One glance at the screen drained the color from his face.

*General Office, Provincial Department of Public Security.*

Ever since the mysterious top-level lockdown had chilled the county Friday night, every senior official in Yunxi had been walking on eggshells. Mai Zhenhua turned away from the steps to answer. "Yes, sir."

"Yes. He has been released, sir. He's with his family right now. Soup, sir. His sister-in-law brought him soup."

At the glass doors, Wei Kun stood holding his cap. Chen Xuan caught his eye and gave him a respectful nod.

"Thank you for the hot water, Officer Wei. My uncle told me. It meant a great deal."

Wei Kun flushed to his ears and found himself smiling.

Back inside his sedan, Mai Zhenhua typed the young man's name into the police terminal.

Chen Xuan, twenty-nine, born in Yunxi. Honest, quiet, spotless record. Ordinary in every way.

A slow chill crept down Mai Zhenhua's spine. That calm manner. The timing. A call from the provincial office before breakfast, all over a hardware shopkeeper.

His thumb hovered over a gray button marked *Restricted*. After a long beat, he locked the screen.

A wise man does not open a door when he cannot bear to see what stands behind it.
