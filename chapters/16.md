# Chapter 16: The Red-Walled Table

Inside the deepest, windowless wood-paneled hall of the red-walled compound at the heart of the national capital, the air was so still that the ticking of a single antique wall clock sounded loud.

Seven men sat around a modest round table. Above the Vice Premier, above every ministry, and above every theater command, this was the highest decision-making table in the nation.

The Senior Vice Premier and four-star General Qin Zhengguo stood side by side at the foot of the table, laying the complete matte-black Zero Guard dossier and the joint cleanup plan in front of the presiding elder.

"Speak plainly," the presiding elder said, his voice calm, deep, and weathered by decades of storms.

For twenty minutes, the Senior Vice Premier laid out every finding from the internal and cross-department meetings without softening a single word: the over one hundred trillion yuan in twenty-year sovereign asset theft, the nineteen compromised regional bank desks, the foreign espionage cells inside core ministries, the stolen naval propulsion and missile-guidance files, the national power-grid override keys, the agricultural seed-bank leaks, the dead overseas intelligence officers, and the Zero Guard's silent pre-dawn handoff of every captured traitor and seized server drive to the national agencies for cleanup.

Then General Qin Zhengguo stepped forward half a pace.

"Elders," the four-star commander said gravely, "every theater command held full combat readiness through the night. Our electronic warfare teams confirmed that three live overseas data bursts were severed inside the eastern relay grid between two and four in the morning by the Zero Guard's jammers. Had the hundred-kilometer shell and the silent ministerial watches not gone up last night, half the traitors sitting in our custody right now would have burned their servers and vanished onto private jets to Zurich and Singapore before sunrise."

Around the round table, not one of the seven highest leaders interrupted. A silver-haired elder on the left held an unlit cigarette between his fingers and never struck a match.

"Tell me again how this entire web was uncovered," the elder asked softly, his voice heavy with disbelief.

The Senior Vice Premier swallowed hard. "In a small county called Yunxi, in Lanshui Province. A local hardware shopkeeper refused to sell his corner store for half price to a corrupt developer. A street thug slapped the shopkeeper's older brother in public, and later left red demolition paint on his family's front door. That man's son happened to be the one who holds the Absolute Order."

Silence fell over the highest room in the nation.

Not a single person smiled. Instead, a deep, bone-chilling awe settled around the round table.

"A street thug slapped his father over a sixty-square-meter hardware shop," another senior elder murmured, shaking his head slowly. "He woke the Zero Guard to protect his mother's door and root out who stood behind the thug, and in one night they pulled out a cancer that had been rotting inside our ministries and banks for twenty years and handed every traitor straight to our agencies for cleanup."

"One hundred trillion yuan," a third leader said quietly, tapping the thick black cover with one knuckle. "At any ordinary meeting, that number alone would shake the country. Looking at the defense blueprints, the grid keys, and the blood of our overseas agents in the back half of this binder, even a hundred trillion yuan looks like the smallest thing on this table."

The presiding elder closed the matte-black dossier slowly and folded his hands over it.

"Hear my ruling," he said, his voice carrying absolute, unshakable weight.

Every man in the room straightened at once.

"First. Approve the joint cleanup plan. Every national traitor, compromised official, and foreign asset handed over by the Zero Guard goes immediately under sealed military and state-security tribunal. No leniency, no political shelter, and no leaks to foreign press. Second. Every offshore channel, bank desk, and shadow trust tied to the hundred-trillion-yuan pipeline stays frozen under central audit until every recoverable yuan is clawed back into the state treasury. Third. Close the local Lanshui and Yunxi cases publicly under a clean anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism security sweep so not a shred of panic reaches ordinary citizens."

He paused, his gaze turning toward the map of Lanshui Province.

"Fourth. As for Yunxi County, and the young man living there. Nobody in this hall, nobody in any ministry, and nobody in any province asks his name, opens his file, or approaches his family. Let him handle his county matter in his own way. Last night, without asking for a single medal or a single word of thanks, he saved this country from a knife aimed straight at its throat. We owe him a debt no title can repay."

"Understood," the Senior Vice Premier and General Qin Zhengguo answered together, bowing deeply.

* * *

Hundreds of miles south, pale morning sunlight spilled through the curtain of Room 601 in Building 2.

Chen Xuan sat up in bed, rubbed his eyes, and let out a quiet yawn.

Outside, the streets of Yunxi County sounded completely ordinary. Bikes rattled past the compound gate, a vegetable vendor called out fresh greens, and not a single citizen had any idea that overnight, stealth fighter jets had circled above the clouds while the highest leaders in the national capital stared in trembling silence at a hundred-trillion-yuan treason dossier.

In the kitchen, Liu Meifang clattered a soup pot and called out loudly. "Chen Xuan! Are you up yet? You slept like a log! Wash your face and come eat before your father leaves for the station!"

"Coming, Mom," Chen Xuan called back mildly.

He pulled on his plain gray jacket, picked up his scratched green thermos, and stepped out into the smell of warm pork-rib soup and steamed red-bean buns.
