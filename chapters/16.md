# Chapter 16: The Old Gentleman

Up in the provincial capital, Vice Director Gao Zhengyi of the Discipline Inspection Commission was zipping his leather briefcase for a quick lunch when his private phone rang on the desk.

He led the teams that checked counties for corruption, so important calls reached him every day, and the Commission itself was the Party's anti-corruption watchdog for the whole province.

But the number glowing on the screen had not called him in three full years.

Gao stood up behind his desk before he pressed the button, even though he was alone in the room.

"Hello, sir!"

"Gao," said a dry, calm, old voice. "I am down in Yunxi County, fishing at Qingshan Reservoir, and a young man beside me has caught nothing in two hours and does not look the least bit impatient. It has been a long time since I sat next to a man like that."

"It is wonderful to hear you are resting well, sir," Gao said, and his throat tightened.

"Your team comes to Yunxi on Wednesday, doesn't it?" The old man paused, and water lapped faintly through the phone. "Move it up and come tomorrow morning. I want to see a county that has not had two extra days to tidy its books. And Gao, do everything by the book, with no fuss."

"Yes, sir! Tomorrow at nine sharp, strictly by the rules!"

The line clicked dead.

Gao stood still for ten long seconds with the silent phone in his hand.

The reason was simple. That old gentleman had retired from the very top of Lanshui Province eleven years ago, and for ten years before that, every city and county leader had trembled at one margin note in his handwriting. If such a man picked a small county reservoir for a three-week fishing stay and then called a team in two days early, the sky over Yunxi was about to fall on somebody's head!

Gao grabbed the office landline and began to rewrite his whole team's schedule.

"Clear every meeting on the list! We drive tonight and reach Yunxi by morning!"

His assistant had already canceled her own lunch and stood at the door with a fresh pot of tea, ready to be seen noticing trouble before anyone asked her to.

"Your tea, Vice Director," she said softly. "I will also book the rooms in Yunxi."

"Yes, and quietly!"

---

Down in Yunxi County, in the Party Secretary's office, Fan Weiguo had not slept for thirty hours.

Under his pressed jacket his white shirt clung damply to his back, and he sat behind the wide desk with both palms flat on the glass top so that nobody walking in could see his fingers tremble.

First the midnight call from the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission had jolted him out of bed; then, early that morning, the City Party Secretary, his direct superior and the top leader of the whole city, had called him in person and delivered the news like three thunderclaps. Deputy Police Director Cheng Biao was suspended. Every Old Street buyout deadline was frozen, along with the county's 640-million-yuan relocation fund, and all eleven signed contracts would go to independent legal review. Three investigators from the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission already sat in Yunxi, and a fourth was on the highway.

"Weiguo, I trust you will cooperate with the investigators," the City Party Secretary had ended, in a voice Fan had never heard from him. "Fully."

This was not how the week was supposed to go!

Somebody had walked into the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission at midnight with a clinic timestamp, video and bank records, and Fan wanted that name!

Every road led back to a hardware shopkeeper and his clerk nephew, and that made no sense at all!

Was it the secret complaint letter that someone had sent anonymously to the Governor's office about Old Street, a leak from his own staff, or Tao Shu, who dodged Friday's dinner with a fake cold and had finally opened his drawer?

Brrring!

The red landline cut through the room, and Fan's shoulders jumped. He drew a sharp breath, steadied his voice and lifted the receiver. "Fan Weiguo speaking."

"Secretary Fan?" The caller sounded crisp, polite and unmistakably official. "This is the General Office of the Provincial Party Committee. Please take no notes. A retired senior leader is visiting Qingshan Reservoir in your county today and tomorrow, privately, to fish. There is to be no welcome, no escort, no ID check, no photograph and no question, and nobody from Yunxi may approach him, ask his name or say that we called."

Fan's breath caught, because the General Office was the nerve center of the province's highest leaders, and it called only when something large was moving.

"I understand completely," he said carefully. "May I ask which leader?"

"Secretary Fan," the caller cut in smoothly, "when the City Party Secretary walks into your office, how do you stand?"

"I stand at once, of course. Straight up."

"For this gentleman, the Provincial Party Secretary stands straight." There was one heavy beat on the line. "If anything spoils his weekend, do not trouble to come to work on Monday. That is all."

Click.

Fan sat frozen with the dead phone against his ear, and he felt the blood drain out of his face.

The top leader of the entire province stood at attention for this man. How many living people fit that? Four, five at most, all retired, all famous, and one of them, Fan remembered with a sick drop in his stomach, was known to love quiet reservoir fishing.

At Qingshan Reservoir. This weekend. Hours after Old Street exploded.

Could that be chance, or had the old man heard about Old Street? Did Chen Mingde's family have a secret backer in the provincial capital?

Fan cut that thought off, because even finishing the guess felt dangerous!

Just then the door burst open without a knock, and his young aide rushed in, white-faced and gasping.

"Secretary Fan, bad news! The Provincial Inspection Team has canceled Wednesday! They arrive tomorrow morning at nine sharp!"

Fan did not even blink, and his aide waited, shaking, for an answer.

"Secretary Fan, what do we tell the others?"

"Nothing yet." Fan put the phone down slowly. He stared at the open doorway like a man waiting for the ceiling to come down, because after thirty years in government he knew the storm had already reached the shore.

"Notify every member of the County Standing Committee," he ordered, hoarse but steady. "Emergency session in Conference Room Three at two-thirty sharp, and tell them nobody gets a cold today."

Outside, the whispers ran down the corridor.

"A retired leader is fishing at our reservoir?"

"Nobody may ask his name."

"Then who is he, and why is he here?"

---

In the eastern courtyard, Chief An Zhiyuan read the message his provincial contacts had just sent, then read it again.

*The inspection team has moved its Yunxi arrival up from Wednesday to nine tomorrow morning. And the Provincial Party Committee's General Office has quietly warned every office in the county that a retired senior leader is fishing in private at Qingshan Reservoir.*

Very slowly, An set the note flat on the wooden desk.

At this exact minute the Boss sat beside that reservoir fishing with his grandfather, and An's team had not made one call to the provincial capital. Nobody had touched the inspection schedule, and Attorney Bai had simply filed one clean complaint at the city level, strictly on Chen Xuan's order.

So who had pushed a provincial team two full days ahead with a single casual call?

An had spent forty years watching the highest levels of government, and only a tiny handful of retired elders held that kind of quiet power, and nearly every one of them preferred plain clothes and an afternoon with a fishing rod.

He reached for his encrypted handset and pressed Nine's channel, and for the first time in years his steady voice shook.

"Nine, where is the Boss sitting right now? And who is sitting next to him?"
