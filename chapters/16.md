# Chapter 16: Saturday Morning at Fuxing New Village

Dawn reached the sixth floor of Fuxing New Village before the sun cleared the eastern ridge of Yunxi County. Pale gray light filtered through the faded blue curtains of the living room, settling over the scuffed linoleum floor and the low wooden table. Outside, thin river mist clung to the neighboring concrete balconies, muffling the first distant sputter of motorbikes along the county road.

Chen Xuan stepped out of his old bedroom in a washed cotton shirt and dark trousers, closing the door softly so the latch would not click. Across the narrow hallway, his parents' door stood half an inch ajar. He heard the slow, even breathing of Chen Jianguo. After two sleepless nights worrying over Chen Mingde, his father had finally sunk into real rest once Chen Xuan returned home and gave his calm, immovable word that the matter was already being handled and that nobody in the family needed to step near the county police station.

In the small kitchen, Chen Xuan scooped two cups of golden millet, rinsed the grains until the water turned faintly cloudy, and poured them into the blackened clay pot his mother had used for twenty years. He added cold water, four peeled chunks of sweet potato, and three red dates, then turned the gas burner to its lowest blue ring.

Twenty minutes later, the nutty aroma of simmering millet filled the apartment. Soft footsteps shuffled down the hall, and Liu Meifang appeared in the doorway, pulling a knitted cardigan over her shoulders.

"You are up already?" Liu Meifang asked in a hushed voice. "You traveled back yesterday. You should have slept longer."

"I wake early in Qingyan," Chen Xuan said calmly, stirring the thick golden film gathering along the rim. "Let Dad sleep a bit more. Once you have had a bowl, I will walk with you to the morning market."

Liu Meifang hesitated, her fingers tightening on the hem of her cardigan. "You do not need to come. The market is damp, and there is nothing heavy to buy."

"I want to walk," Chen Xuan replied evenly. "If we are making pork rib soup for lunch, someone has to carry the basket."

He did not mention the whispers. Since Thursday, when Hongrui Real Estate's men shouted outside Mingde Hardware on Old Street and news of Chen Mingde's detention spread, Fuxing New Village had hummed with sidelong glances. Liu Meifang had dreaded stepping into the stairwell under prying eyes.

After two warm bowls of porridge with pickled radish, Chen Xuan picked up the woven basket and a stainless-steel thermos of hot jasmine tea, walking half a step behind Liu Meifang down the six flights of stairs.

At the ground floor, Auntie Zhou from the third floor paused her sweeping, mouth opening as if to ask about Chen Mingde. Then she met Chen Xuan's serene, composed gaze. The rehearsed curiosity died in her throat.

"Morning," Chen Xuan said politely.

"Morning, Chen Xuan," Auntie Zhou answered, flustered, and resumed sweeping.

Out on the market lane, Liu Meifang let out a slow breath, her shoulders easing. Beneath the iron roof of the morning market, steam rose from bun baskets beside buckets of silver carp. Liu Meifang picked fresh spinach, white radishes, and two strips of meaty pork ribs. Chen Xuan paid before she could open her purse and carried the heavy bags easily.

"Go on up and rest before you start the soup," Chen Xuan said when they reached the fork near the old grain warehouse. "I will stop by the alley behind the middle school for fifteen minutes. Wang Lei should be open."

Liu Meifang nodded warmly. "Give him my regards. Tell him to come for dinner when his mother's legs are better."

Chen Xuan watched his mother turn safely into Fuxing New Village before walking down the narrow cobblestone lane that smelled of damp brick and rubber cement. Halfway down stood a low courtyard with a flaking green iron gate. Inside, under a sagging tarpaulin, Wang Lei squatted beside his battered electric delivery tricycle, grease on his knuckles, prying a punctured front tire from a bent steel rim.

"That rim is warped on the left edge," Chen Xuan said quietly from the gate. "Pry from there and you will pinch the tube again."

Wang Lei looked up and broke into a wide, tired grin. "Chen Xuan? Nobody told me you were back." He started to stand, his knees popping. "Wait, let me wash my hands and fetch a stool."

"Finish the wheel first," Chen Xuan said. Setting the market bags on a wooden crate, he squatted opposite Wang Lei and held the opposite tire bead firm while Wang Lei slid the iron underneath.

Within a minute, they found the pinhole in a chipped enamel basin of soapy water. While the rubber cement dried, Chen Xuan unscrewed his thermos and poured steaming jasmine tea into the metal cap, setting it on the crate beside Wang Lei's elbow.

Wang Lei laughed softly, picking up the warm cap with his cleaner left hand. "Still carrying jasmine tea in the same kind of thermos." He took a slow sip and sighed. "How is Second Uncle Chen Mingde? I heard Hongrui Real Estate's loudmouths bragging near the freight depot yesterday. I wanted to come by Fuxing New Village last night, but my south-township run went past ten."

"Second Uncle Chen Mingde will be home soon," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Do not worry about the noise on Old Street."

Wang Lei studied Chen Xuan's face, finding a quiet steadiness that made panic seem foolish. "Good. If your family needs a hand moving anything at Mingde Hardware, call me. My tricycle is ugly, but it still climbs hills."

"I know," Chen Xuan replied, refilling the cap. "How are your delivery routes this month? And how is your mother's knee in this damp weather?"

Wang Lei pressed the patch down and tapped it with a wrench. "Steady enough. Groceries in the morning, hardware cartons in the afternoon. Thirty or forty yuan a trip when it stays dry. My mother's knee aches when river fog drops, so I bought herbal plasters on West Street. Nothing we cannot handle."

Watching Wang Lei's scarred hands work the tire pump, Chen Xuan remembered Yunxi County High School twelve years earlier. During a lean winter when Chen Xuan lived on plain steamed buns and boiled water, Wang Lei had never pitied him or made a speech. Every noon for an entire semester, Wang Lei had dropped two meat buns and a salted egg onto Chen Xuan's desk, claiming his mother packed too much. Eight yuan a day out of Wang Lei's own meager pocket money, given with such casual kindness that a proud teenager could accept it without feeling small.

Chen Xuan now held wealth that could buy Yunxi County ten times over before breakfast. Yet he understood Wang Lei's pride. Flaunting money would only break something honest between them. A quiet debt deserved quiet respect.

Once the wheel was bolted back true, Chen Xuan stood, split off a clean wrapped parcel holding half the fresh pork ribs and spinach, and hung it on Wang Lei's handlebar.

"Mom bought too much," Chen Xuan said matter-of-factly. "She said it will sit in the fridge until Monday. Stew it with ginger for your mother tonight."

Wang Lei looked at the bag, then at Chen Xuan, old recognition warming his eyes. He did not argue. "Tell Auntie Liu Meifang thank you. Stay safe while you are home, Chen Xuan. Those Hongrui Real Estate people have no bottom line."

"They will find one soon enough," Chen Xuan said calmly, and walked back toward Fuxing New Village as pale gold sunlight broke over the rooftops.
