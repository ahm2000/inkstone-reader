# Chapter 84: Military and State Security Clash

By half past three on Monday afternoon, the joint Central Military Commission and Ministry of State Security war room smelled like scorched tobacco and gun oil. Only four people stood inside the soundproofed chamber: General Qin Zhengguo, State Security Minister Ding Guoping, and two silent senior duty officers pinned against the far wall, neither daring to breathe loudly while two of the republic's heaviest hammers squared off across a steel map table.

Between them lay two thick blue dossiers. One bore the crimson stamp of Southern Theater Counterintelligence. The other carried the black seal of the Shanghai Municipal State Security Bureau, listing forty-seven captured foreign cutouts detained in a synchronized sweep before dawn.

General Qin Zhengguo did not sit. He braced both knuckles against the edge of the steel table, his uniform tunic crisp, a lit cigarette clamped between the fingers of his right hand, its ash growing long and forgotten.

"Sign the transfer order, Ding Guoping," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice a low rumble that rattled the teacup saucers. "Every single one of those forty-seven Shanghai cutouts was moving metallurgical telemetry and radar-alloy specifications leaked out of Southern Theater logistics depots. Under wartime counter-espionage statutes, they belong in Military Intelligence custody by five o'clock."

Ding Guoping sat opposite him, legs crossed, a half-smoked cigarette resting on the rim of a tin ashtray. His face remained calm, almost scholarly, yet his eyes were hard as flint.

"I am not signing that transfer order, Qin Zhengguo," Ding Guoping replied evenly. "They were captured on civilian soil in Shanghai, operating under commercial shipping and maritime-arbitration cover. State Security built the net that caught them, and State Security holds custody."

Qin Zhengguo's jaw clenched so hard a muscle knotted beneath his ear. He slapped the Southern Theater dossier flat onto the map table.

"Three senior colonels and eleven depot quartermasters in the Southern Theater took offshore retainers from that Shanghai cell!" Qin Zhengguo barked, smoke bursting from his nostrils. "Men wearing the uniform of the People's Liberation Army sold alloy dispatch logs to foreign handlers disguised as demolition engineers! You want me to sit quietly while your ministry plays chess in the dark? I am convening closed military court-martials tonight. Every traitor wearing shoulder boards gets stripped, tried, and sentenced before the Southern Theater command loses its spine!"

Ding Guoping did not flinch at the roar. He picked up his cigarette, took a measured drag, and exhaled slowly through his nose.

"Try your own officers all you want, Qin Zhengguo," Ding Guoping said coldly. "Court-martial the three colonels. Shoot them behind closed walls if the Military Commission signs the warrant. I will not interfere with uniform discipline. What you will not do is drag forty-seven foreign cutouts into a military tribunal and broadcast to Zurich that their entire Shanghai relay went dark overnight."

"Broadcast?" Qin Zhengguo stepped around the corner of the table, looming over Ding Guoping. "A closed military court-martial does not invite reporters, Ding Guoping!"

"It invites military prosecutors, theater defense clerks, logistics witnesses, and forty-seven consular protests the second a single foreign passport hits a military detention docket!" Ding Guoping shot back, his calm cracking for the first time as he leaned forward, eyes blazing. "Use your head, Qin Zhengguo! Right now, Zurich's Northern Ledger headquarters does not know whether their Shanghai cell was swept by us, flipped by a rival syndicate, or simply went silent for a routine security blackout. Two of those forty-seven cutouts still have live scheduled burst transmissions set for midnight tonight."

Qin Zhengguo stopped mid-stride. His eyes narrowed into dangerous slits. "You are planning to run a live playback on their transmitters."

"Yes," Ding Guoping snapped. "If we keep all forty-seven under silent civilian detention inside State Security black sites, my signals division can feed controlled telemetry through those two transmitters for seventy-two hours. That is all we need to trace the return acknowledgment ping straight past Lukas Weber in Geneva and into the actual Northern Ledger handler desks in Zurich, Frankfurt, and Singapore. Hand those cutouts to a military tribunal today, and Zurich burns every switchboard in Europe before dinner. We catch forty-seven errands runners and let the architects walk away clean."

Silence fell heavy across the table. Along the far wall, the two duty officers stared rigidly at the floor, sweat beading along their collars.

Qin Zhengguo stared down at Ding Guoping for ten long seconds. Slowly, he crushed his cigarette out in the tin ashtray, grinding the filter until it tore apart.

"You intelligence bureaucrats always think the whole world is a chessboard," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice dropping to a razor-edged whisper. "You want seventy-two hours of clean signal traces. Do you know what seventy-two hours of silence looks like inside a theater command when rumors start leaking that depot officers vanished overnight? Morale rots from the barracks up. If the troops think the high command is hiding traitors to protect some spy game in Switzerland, the damage to the army is worse than the stolen alloys."

Ding Guoping met his glare without blinking. "And if we let Northern Ledger rebuild a fresh relay in Shenzhen or Dalian six months from now because we never mapped their European command nodes, your Southern Theater depots will be bleeding again next winter. You want to punish the rot inside the barracks, Qin Zhengguo. I want to rip out the root in Zurich so it never grows back."

Qin Zhengguo reached into his breast pocket, pulled out a fresh cigarette, and flicked his steel lighter twice before the flame caught. His hand was steady, though his eyes burned with suppressed fury.

"Forty-eight hours," Qin Zhengguo said abruptly.

Ding Guoping arched an eyebrow. "Seventy-two."

"Forty-eight," Qin Zhengguo repeated, his tone brooking zero negotiation. "And one condition. Two of my Southern Theater Counterintelligence interrogators sit inside every single debriefing room in Shanghai with full audio feed straight to my desk. They do not touch your transmitters, and they do not file military dockets until Wednesday at four o'clock. In return, the second your signals boys map the Zurich acknowledgment nodes, every cutout who touched military-alloy specifications is handed over to my custody without a single word of protest from your ministry."

Ding Guoping studied Qin Zhengguo's hard, weathered face through the drifting haze of smoke. Slowly, the State Security Minister reached across the table, pulled the transfer order toward himself, and crossed out the timestamp with a black fountain pen, writing in Wednesday, 16:00, and signing his name beside it.

"Forty-eight hours," Ding Guoping agreed, sliding the amended paper back. "Your two officers fly to Shanghai on my plane in thirty minutes. Tell them if they spook a single cutout before midnight's burst transmission, I will have them court-martialed myself."

Qin Zhengguo let out a short, humorless bark of laughter and pocketed the paper. "Worry about your own clerks, Ding Guoping. If Zurich slips through your fingers after forty-eight hours, I am coming back into this room without knocking."

He turned sharply on his heel and strode out the steel door, leaving Ding Guoping alone in the thick smoke, already reaching for the red telephone to call Shanghai.
