# Chapter 84: Military and State Security Clash

By half past three on Monday afternoon, the joint military and State Security war room smelled of burnt tobacco and gun oil. Only four people stood inside the soundproof room: General Qin Zhengguo, State Security Minister Ding Guoping, and two silent duty officers standing stiffly against the far wall. Neither officer dared to breathe too loudly while two of the country's heaviest figures squared off across a steel map table.

Between them lay two thick blue folders. One bore the red stamp of Southern Theater Counterintelligence. The other carried the black seal of the Shanghai State Security office, listing forty-seven foreign agents caught in a quiet dawn raid.

General Qin Zhengguo did not sit. He planted both knuckles on the edge of the steel table, his uniform crisp, a lit cigarette held between the fingers of his right hand with its ash growing long.

"Sign the transfer order, Ding Guoping," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice a low rumble that rattled the teacups. "Every single one of those forty-seven Shanghai agents was moving metal test data and radar-metal plans leaked out of Southern Theater supply bases. Under the military secrets law, they belong in Military Intelligence hands by five o'clock."

Ding Guoping sat across from him with his legs crossed and a half-smoked cigarette resting on a tin ashtray. His face stayed calm, almost like a teacher's, yet his eyes were hard as flint.

"I am not signing that transfer order, Qin Zhengguo," Ding Guoping replied evenly. "They were caught on civilian ground in Shanghai, working under fake shipping and trade jobs. State Security built the net that caught them, and State Security keeps them."

Qin Zhengguo's jaw locked so hard a muscle jumped under his ear. He slapped the Southern Theater folder flat onto the steel table.

"Three senior colonels and eleven supply officers in the Southern Theater took foreign payoffs from that Shanghai ring!" Qin Zhengguo barked. "Men wearing army uniforms sold metal shipping logs to foreign handlers dressed as building engineers! You want me to sit quietly while your office plays chess in the dark? I am calling closed military trials tonight. Every traitor wearing army stars gets stripped, tried, and punished before the Southern Theater loses its spine!"

Ding Guoping did not flinch at the roar. He picked up his cigarette and took a calm puff.

"Try your own officers all you want, Qin Zhengguo," Ding Guoping said coldly. "Put the three colonels on trial. Shoot them behind closed walls if the Military Commission signs the order. I will not touch army discipline. What you will not do is drag forty-seven foreign agents into a military court and tell Zurich that their whole Shanghai link went dark overnight."

"Tell them?" Qin Zhengguo stepped around the corner of the table, looming over Ding Guoping. "A closed military trial does not invite reporters, Ding Guoping!"

"It brings in military lawyers, clerks, supply witnesses, and forty-seven foreign embassy protests the second a single foreign passport hits a military jail list!" Ding Guoping shot back. His calm cracked for the first time as he leaned forward, eyes burning. "Use your head, Qin Zhengguo! Right now, Zurich's Northern Ledger headquarters does not know whether their Shanghai ring was caught by us, hit by a rival gang, or just went quiet for safety. Two of those forty-seven agents still have timed radio bursts set for midnight tonight."

Qin Zhengguo stopped mid-step. His eyes narrowed into hard slits. "You plan to send fake messages on their radios."

"Yes," Ding Guoping snapped. "If we keep all forty-seven locked up quietly inside State Security sites, my radio team can feed controlled data through those two sets for seventy-two hours. That is all we need to trace the reply signal straight past Lukas Weber in Geneva and right into the real Northern Ledger desks in Zurich, Frankfurt, and Singapore. Hand those agents to a military court today, and Zurich burns every phone line in Europe before dinner. We catch forty-seven errand runners and let the real leaders walk away clean."

Heavy silence fell across the room. Along the far wall, the two duty officers stared stiffly at the floor, sweat forming on their collars.

Qin Zhengguo stared down at Ding Guoping for ten long seconds. Slowly, he crushed his cigarette into the tin ashtray, grinding the filter until it tore apart.

"You spy chiefs always think the whole world is a chessboard," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice dropping to a sharp whisper. "You want seventy-two hours of clean signal traces. Do you know what seventy-two hours of silence looks like inside an army command when rumors leak that supply officers vanished overnight? Trust rots from the barracks up. If the troops think high command is hiding traitors to protect some spy game in Switzerland, the damage to the army is worse than the stolen metal."

Ding Guoping met his glare without blinking. "And if we let Northern Ledger build a new relay in Shenzhen or Dalian six months from now because we never found their European command hubs, your Southern Theater bases will bleed again next winter. You want to punish the rot inside the barracks, Qin Zhengguo. I want to rip out the root in Zurich so it never grows back."

Qin Zhengguo reached into his breast pocket, pulled out a fresh cigarette, and flicked his steel lighter twice before it caught. His hand was steady, though his eyes burned with held-back rage.

"Forty-eight hours," Qin Zhengguo said abruptly.

Ding Guoping raised an eyebrow. "Seventy-two."

"Forty-eight," Qin Zhengguo repeated, leaving zero room to argue. "And one condition. Two of my Southern Theater counter-spy officers sit inside every questioning room in Shanghai with a live sound feed straight to my desk. They do not touch your radios, and they do not file military charges until Wednesday at four o'clock. In return, the second your radio team maps the Zurich hubs, every agent who touched army metal plans is handed to me without a single word of protest from your office."

Ding Guoping studied Qin Zhengguo's hard, weathered face through the drifting smoke. Slowly, the State Security Minister reached across the table and pulled the transfer order toward him. He crossed out the time with a black pen, wrote in Wednesday at four o'clock, and signed his name beside it.

"Forty-eight hours," Ding Guoping agreed, sliding the changed paper back. "Your two officers fly to Shanghai on my plane in thirty minutes. Tell them if they spook a single agent before midnight's radio burst, I will have them put on trial myself."

Qin Zhengguo let out a short, cold bark of laughter and tucked the paper into his pocket. "Worry about your own clerks, Ding Guoping. If Zurich slips through your fingers after forty-eight hours, I am coming back into this room without knocking."

He spun on his heel and strode out the steel door, leaving Ding Guoping alone in the thick smoke, already reaching for the red phone to call Shanghai.
