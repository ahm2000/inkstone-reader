# Chapter 84: Military and State Security Clash

By late Monday afternoon, the soundproof joint briefing room in Beijing felt tighter than a submarine hull. Cold air poured steadily from overhead vents, carrying the faint scent of oiled leather and freshly printed ink. Across the center of the room stood a brushed steel map table, its surface lit by the sharp red glow of a digital sector overlay.

Four senior men occupied the room, along with two silent duty officers standing rigid against the far wall. Neither duty officer dared to shift his weight while two of the nation's sharpest operational commanders squared off across the metal table.

Between them lay two thick folders. One bore the crimson stamp of Southern Theater Counterintelligence, headed by Major General Lu Zhen. The other carried the black seal of the Ministry of State Security, placed there by Deputy Minister Shen Kuo, down from his seventh-floor office without a single aide. At the head of the table sat retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo, flanked by Director Fang of the Joint Security Coordination Office.

Major General Lu Zhen broke the silence first, sliding his crimson folder six inches forward until it tapped the edge of the map display.

"Three coastal ports, two railway hubs in Handong, and four key intersections in Beijing," Lu Zhen said, his voice hard as iron. "Over the last seventy-two hours, unrecorded personnel moved through every one of those zones. No military serial numbers. No police credentials. No highway toll logs. Even our theater satellite sweeps picked up nothing but blank traffic intervals."

He looked straight across the table at Shen Kuo. "Your ministry has domestic counter-espionage authority, Deputy Minister. Yet you are telling me you still do not have a single name, unit code, or chain of command for these people?"

Deputy Minister Shen Kuo did not flinch. He opened his black-sealed folder with calm precision and laid three clean satellite prints beside the glowing map.

"We tracked their footprint from the moment the Handong arrests began, General Lu," Shen Kuo replied evenly. "They are known in our restricted brief as the Black Guard. Our analysts cross-checked every active military roster, retired special forces file, and domestic security database. None of them exist on paper."

"That is precisely my point!" Lu Zhen snapped, planting both palms on the steel table. "An unrecorded force with that level of discipline and reach is operating inside our borders right now. My duty is to protect military command lines and regional security. I am requesting immediate authorization to deploy two counterintelligence intercept teams to Handong and the capital perimeter to identify and detain them for questioning."

Director Fang stiffened in his chair, glancing uneasily toward Elder Qin Zhengguo.

Shen Kuo shook his head once, his expression cool and analytical. "If you send armed intercept teams after them, General, you will create a crisis where none exists. Look at what they actually did over the last three days. Did they approach any military base? No. Did they interfere with Sha Ruijin, Li Dakang, or Hou Liangping's arrest teams? No."

Shen Kuo tapped the second print on the table. "What they did do was cut off two encrypted satellite uplinks that Zhao Ruilong's coastal brokers were using to tip off overseas short-sellers. They also detained three foreign intelligence couriers trying to smuggle forged bank ledgers out of a Jingzhou dock and left them neatly tied outside the port police gate with every piece of evidence intact."

"Helpful actions do not erase a breach of protocol," Lu Zhen argued sharply. "Today they help plug a leak. What if tomorrow their unknown commander gives a different order? A state cannot tolerate an armed shadow operating outside the law and outside our chain of command."

"And what happens if your intercept team corners them in a crowded city street and someone panics and fires a shot?" Shen Kuo countered, his voice dropping an octave. "We just stabilized a 5 trillion yuan liquidity crisis this morning. Our clearing lines held, our markets stayed calm, and the Handong corruption case is moving smoothly through the Procuratorate. Starting a covert firefight inside Beijing or Jingzhou would hand foreign intelligence agencies the exact chaos they failed to buy on the stock exchange."

Silence stretched across the steel table. Both men had solid professional grounds. Lu Zhen spoke from military doctrine, while Shen Kuo spoke from intelligence reality.

At the head of the table, Elder Qin Zhengguo finally lifted his gaze from the glowing red overlay. He placed one weathered hand flat on the metal surface, and the tension in the room immediately dropped several degrees.

"Both of you are doing your jobs," Elder Qin said, his tone steady and unhurried. "Lu Zhen is right to guard the boundary of military discipline. Shen Kuo is right to look at the actual threat on the ground. Now listen to the facts."

Both commanders straightened instinctively.

"First," Elder Qin continued, "the Black Guard is operating under a fixed withdrawal deadline. Once the Handong sweep and financial stabilization are locked in, they pull back completely on schedule. They are not here to stay, and they are not contesting state authority."

He looked directly at Major General Lu Zhen. "Second, your counterintelligence teams are needed elsewhere. Zhao Dehan and Gao Yuliang did not build a twenty-year network alone. Foreign financial groups and corrupt insiders are still trying to move dirty money through coastal ports and erase digital trails. Put your people on those border leaks and port registries. Do not waste one hour chasing ghosts who are already preparing to leave."

Lu Zhen held Elder Qin's gaze for three long seconds, weighing the old general's calm certainty. Slowly, he closed his crimson folder and stood to salute. "Understood, Elder Qin. Southern Theater will focus strictly on port lockdowns and cross-border intelligence leaks."

Elder Qin nodded, then turned to Shen Kuo. "State Security will keep passive monitoring on foreign actors only. No active pursuit of the Black Guard. Make sure our anti-corruption prosecutors in Handong have clean communications and full protection against outside sabotage."

"Already underway, Elder Qin," Shen Kuo replied, closing his black-sealed folder.

"Good," Elder Qin said, rising from his chair with a quiet sigh. "The real battle is inside our own house, cleaning out the rot with real evidence and real law. Do not lose sight of that."

As the two commanders filed out of the briefing room, the cold air finally felt breathable again, though every man in the room knew the calm would last only as long as their discipline held.
