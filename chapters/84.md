# Chapter 84: The Fourth Chair Is Filled

Dusk settled gently over Yunxi Old Street, bathing the newly restored bluestone paving in the soft amber glow of paper lanterns swaying beneath dark timber eaves.

After finishing dinner and helping Chen Jianguo wash the dishes in Room 601, Chen Xuan told his parents he was going out for a short evening walk to digest his meal. He slipped on his washed gray jacket, tucked his hands comfortably into his pockets, and strolled unhurriedly down the lively lane.

Old Street felt completely reborn.

The bulldozers, thugs, and threatening eviction notices were nothing more than distant dust now. Beside the stone arch, a grandfather roasted sweet chestnuts over black gravel, filling the cool autumn air with nutty warmth. Two doors down, the tailor's sewing machine hummed behind bright windows while children chased each other around the glowing lamp posts with paper pinwheels.

Forty meters behind Chen Xuan, high along the shadowed tile ridges and dark stone alleys bordering Old Street, the permanent Zero Guard team moved with soundless precision. Masked and clad in featureless matte-black gear, they remained completely unseen by the strolling townsfolk, guarding their silent fifty-meter circle without casting even a shadow onto the warm yellow lanterns below.

Chen Xuan slowed his pace as he reached the middle bend of the lane, where a simple weathered wooden signboard hung straight and clean above a brightly lit glass storefront.

*Gong's Noodles.*

Rich, savory steam drifted out through the half-open transom window, carrying the unmistakable fragrance of slow-simmered beef marrow, star anise, ginger, and fresh hand-pulled wheat dough.

* * *

Through the clear windowpane, Chen Xuan looked quietly into the spotless little noodle shop.

Three ordinary regulars from the neighborhood sat near the back tables, chatting softly over half-finished bowls while wiping the sweat from their foreheads. Behind the stainless-steel counter stood a broad-shouldered, middle-aged shopkeeper wearing a faded blue apron and a white cotton cloth draped over one shoulder.

Number Twenty-three moved with practiced, unhurried discipline, skimming foam from the heavy iron soup cauldron and rinsing ceramic bowls in steaming hot water.

Beside the front window stood the small square timber table with four worn wooden chairs. Three of the seats were bare.

On the fourth side of the table, facing the lantern-lit street, sat a spotless white porcelain bowl turned neatly upright, flanked by a pair of clean bamboo chopsticks laid perfectly parallel on a small ceramic rest.

For three long years, ever since Chen Xuan had walked away from the peak of the world to live an ordinary life in Qingyan City, Number Twenty-three had set that fourth place every single evening at dusk. Rain or snow, busy night or quiet winter, that bowl had never been touched by another customer, and the fourth chair had remained empty, waiting in silent loyalty for one guest who might never walk through the door.

The wooden bell strung above the doorframe chimed softly as Chen Xuan pushed the door open and stepped inside.

Behind the steaming counter, Number Twenty-three looked up automatically to greet the incoming customer.

The instant his eyes met Chen Xuan's calm, familiar gaze, Number Twenty-three froze completely motionless. His weathered fingers gripped the long wooden soup ladle so tightly his knuckles turned pale. For a brief, breathless heartbeat, the quiet steam rising from the iron cauldron seemed to pause in midair.

Neither man spoke a secret code, and neither man broke the ordinary peace of the noodle shop.

Chen Xuan simply walked across the clean tiled floor, pulled out the fourth wooden chair beside the window, and sat down comfortably.

For the first time in three years, the fourth chair was filled.

Chen Xuan picked up the parallel bamboo chopsticks, looked toward the counter with a warm, gentle smile, and spoke in an easy, everyday tone.

"Boss, one large bowl of braised beef noodles, please," Chen Xuan said softly. "Extra cilantro and a spoonful of your house chili oil."

Number Twenty-three lowered his head sharply for half a second to steady the sudden fierce moisture gathering behind his eyes. When he looked back up, his expression was filled with deep, unspoken pride.

"Right away, guest," Number Twenty-three answered, his deep voice just a fraction hoarser than usual. "Sit comfortably. The broth just hit its best hour."

* * *

Behind the counter, Number Twenty-three worked with a reverence usually reserved for sacred rituals.

His calloused hands, steady enough to handle steel blades in the darkest nights of his past, trembled ever so slightly as he dropped a fresh handful of hand-pulled noodles into the rolling cauldron. He lifted them out at the exact second they turned springy and tender, shook them cleanly dry, and ladled rich, amber beef marrow broth over the steaming white strands.

With careful precision, he sliced five thick, glistening pieces of tender braised beef shank, scattered bright green scallions and fresh cilantro across the top, added a gleaming spoonful of fragrant red chili oil, and then paused. Reaching gently into the simmering soy pot beside the stove, Number Twenty-three lifted out a glossy, perfectly marinated braised egg and tucked it neatly onto the side of the bowl.

Carrying the heavy porcelain bowl with both hands as though it were forged from jade, Number Twenty-three walked out from behind the counter and set it gently down in front of Chen Xuan.

"Careful, the bowl is hot," Number Twenty-three murmured quietly, stepping back half a pace with his hands clasped respectfully in front of his faded blue apron.

Chen Xuan lifted his ceramic spoon, blew gently across the fragrant golden surface, and took his first slow sip of the broth.

Warmth bloomed cleanly from his throat straight down to his chest. Every hour of slow fire, every careful measure of spice, and three years of silent devotion lived inside that single spoonful.

Chen Xuan nodded appreciatively, picked up the bamboo chopsticks, and began to eat without hurrying.

He did not leave a single strand of noodle behind. He ate the tender braised beef, savored the marinated egg, and tilted the white porcelain bowl gently at the end to finish the very last warm spoonful of rich bone broth.

Setting his chopsticks neatly parallel across the empty bowl, Chen Xuan wiped his mouth with a paper napkin and reached into his gray jacket pocket. He placed twenty-five yuan in crisp, ordinary bills flat on the wooden table beside the bowl, the exact menu price down to the last yuan.

Chen Xuan stood up, meeting Number Twenty-three's eyes with genuine, quiet warmth.

"That broth is the best on Old Street," Chen Xuan said softly, his smile sincere and unhurried. "Thank you for the extra egg. Keep the pot warm, because I will come back again next week."

Number Twenty-three straightened his back beneath the faded apron, offering a deep, silent bow of his head as his weathered eyes shone with quiet fulfillment.

"The fourth chair will always be waiting for you," Number Twenty-three whispered softly.

The wooden bell chimed gently as Chen Xuan stepped back out into the cool autumn evening, disappearing naturally into the warm lantern light of Old Street.

Inside Gong's Noodles, Number Twenty-three stood beside the window for a long, quiet moment, a single warm tear sliding down his weathered cheek before he wiped it away with the back of his sleeve. With steady, proud hands, he finally lifted the empty porcelain bowl from the fourth chair and carried it back toward the steaming kitchen, his heart lighter than it had been in three whole years.
