# Chapter 84: Military and State Security Clash

By late Monday afternoon, the soundproof joint briefing room in Beijing felt tighter than a submarine hull. Cold air poured steadily from overhead vents, carrying the faint scent of oiled leather and freshly printed ink. Across the center of the room stood a brushed steel map table, its surface lit by the sharp red glow of a digital sector overlay.

Four men occupied the room, along with two silent duty officers standing rigid against the far wall. Neither duty officer dared to shift his weight while two of the nation's sharpest operational commanders squared off across the metal table.

Between them lay two thick folders. One bore the crimson stamp of Southern Theater Counterintelligence. The other carried the black seal of the Ministry of State Security, documenting forty-seven foreign espionage agents neutralized in a silent dawn sweep before domestic officers even reached their targets.

General Ma Zhenhai, the active Southern Theater Operations Commander, did not sit. He planted both knuckles on the edge of the steel table, his uniform crisp and his jaw clenched tight.

"Explain this to me, Minister Ding," General Ma Zhenhai snapped, jabbing a finger at the red dead-zone circle glowing on the map. "Three hours ago, my forward relay detachment ran a routine signal check near the Yunxi sector. Every military satellite uplink, every encrypted tactical radio, and every drone telemetry link inside a fifty-meter radius went completely dark for eighteen minutes. Not a single shot was fired. Not a single jamming truck was detected on radar. Then my men caught two seconds of thermal silhouettes on the rooftops, wearing plain black gear and masks with zero unit markings!"

Across the table, State Security Minister Ding Guoping kept his face cold and still, though a vein pulsed faintly at his temple.

"Do not point your finger at my ministry, General Ma," Ding Guoping replied sharply. "If you had bothered to read my folder instead of shouting, you would see that my own counterintelligence team hit the exact same wall thirty minutes earlier. Our encrypted handsets were wiped clean. Our street cameras looped twenty minutes of empty pavement. And when my strike team kicked in the doors of four foreign safe houses at dawn, forty-seven enemy agents were already bound on the floor with their cipher books stacked neatly on the coffee tables."

General Ma Zhenhai stared at him in disbelief. "You expect me to believe State Security does not own that shadow unit?"

"I am telling you no registered branch of this government owns them!" Ding Guoping shot back, slapping the black-sealed folder onto the steel map. "They wear plain black gear with no numbers, leave no digital footprint, and shut down both military and security comms without drawing a weapon. If Southern Theater is not running an unauthorized black-ops battalion behind Beijing's back, then we have an unknown armed force operating inside our own borders!"

"Then we settle it tonight," General Ma Zhenhai barked, turning toward the communications console. "I am scrambling two high-altitude recon drones and moving a rapid-response brigade to cordon off that fifty-meter perimeter around the Yunxi neighborhood. Whoever is hiding behind those black masks comes out in handcuffs before sundown!"

"Agreed," Ding Guoping said grimly, reaching for his secure phone. "I will authorize a full State Security sweep to lock down every street corner and force them into the light."

Clink.

The soft sound of a porcelain teacup setting down onto a wooden saucer cut cleanly through the rising heat in the room.

At the far end of the steel table sat retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo, attending the briefing as a senior veteran advisor. He wore no medals and no combat uniform, only a plain dark Zhongshan jacket buttoned to the collar, yet the moment he lifted his eyes, both General Ma and Minister Ding froze with their hands in midair.

"Put the phone down, Ding Guoping," Elder Qin Zhengguo said quietly. "And step away from that console, Ma Zhenhai. Neither of you understands how close you just came to triggering a disaster."

General Ma Zhenhai swallowed hard, straightening his posture out of lifelong respect for the legendary general. "Elder Qin, with respect, an unrecorded armed force disabled active military communications inside domestic territory. If we do not identify them and secure the perimeter, we are failing our oath."

Elder Qin Zhengguo rose slowly from his chair and walked to the glowing map table. He reached down and tapped the fifty-meter circle with one weathered knuckle.

"They are not an enemy unit," Elder Qin Zhengguo said, his voice low and heavy with decades of buried history. "Those men in plain black gear belong to the Black Guard. They answer only to the holder of the Black Order."

The room went completely silent. Even the two duty officers by the wall seemed to stop breathing.

"The Black Order," Ding Guoping whispered, his sharp composure finally slipping. "Elder Qin, I thought those archives were just old wartime myths."

"They are not myths," Elder Qin Zhengguo replied calmly. "Think about what happened today, both of you. They handed State Security forty-seven foreign spies without killing a single domestic officer. They shut down Southern Theater's radios without firing a shot. That was not an act of war. That was a polite warning telling you to stay outside the fifty-meter line."

General Ma Zhenhai's throat went dry. "And if we had sent a brigade across that line?"

Elder Qin Zhengguo looked him straight in the eye. "Then you would not be standing here tomorrow, and neither would the men you sent. The untouchable Yunxi household is off-limits to every military theater and every intelligence bureau in this country. That order comes from the very top."

Slowly, Ding Guoping lowered his secure phone onto the table. General Ma Zhenhai stepped back from the console and let out a long, tight breath.

"Issue a full 5:00 PM stand-down order," Elder Qin Zhengguo instructed, turning toward the door. "Recall every drone, erase every probe log, and never look inside that fifty-meter circle again."
