# Chapter 84: Military and State Security Clash

By late Monday afternoon, the soundproof joint briefing room in Beijing felt tighter than a submarine hull. Cold air poured steadily from overhead vents, carrying the faint scent of oiled leather and freshly printed ink. Across the center of the room stood a brushed steel map table, its surface lit by the sharp red glow of a digital sector overlay.

Four senior men occupied the room, along with two silent duty officers standing rigid against the far wall. Neither duty officer dared to shift his weight while two of the nation's sharpest operational commanders squared off across the metal table.

Between them lay two thick folders. One bore the crimson stamp of Southern Theater Counterintelligence, headed by Major General Lu Zhen. The other carried the black seal of the Ministry of State Security, placed there by Deputy Minister Shen Kuo, documenting forty-seven foreign espionage agents neutralized in a coordinated dawn sweep across coastal ports and Handong.

At the head of the table sat Retired Four-Star General Elder Qin Zhengguo, his wool military coat draped neatly over the back of his chair, listening without interrupting beside a senior Central Military Commission operations director.

"Forty-seven foreign intelligence assets captured in four hours should have been a clean operational triumph," Deputy Minister Shen Kuo said, slapping his open palm onto the black folder. "Instead, my cyber division watched fourteen encrypted relay nodes go completely dark right in the middle of our intercept window. Our technical teams traced the cutoff to an unrecorded unit operating under the Black Guard designation. No ministry authorization, no joint-command docket, no clearance signatures. They cut our active wiretaps in ninety seconds flat."

Major General Lu Zhen leaned forward over the glowing map, his jaw tight. "State Security is not the only department with a grievance, Deputy Minister. My theater radar and municipal substation logs picked up unlogged perimeter teams securing three trunk switching stations outside Jingzhou and two satellite uplinks near Beijing between 1:00 AM and 4:00 AM. Not a single movement order passed through Central Military Commission dispatch. Under defense law, an armed, unrecorded force operating inside domestic security sectors is an unacceptable breach of command."

Shen Kuo pointed sharply across the table. "On that point, General Lu and I agree completely. My ministry has already drafted a joint tracking request. Give us seventy-two hours of shared satellite imagery and signal logs, and we will map every safe house, communication frequency, and command node used by this Black Guard."

"And what exactly do you plan to do if you find one of their staging points, Deputy Minister?" Elder Qin Zhengguo asked quietly.

The room went still. Both commanders turned toward the white-haired general at the head of the table.

Elder Qin picked up a red grease pencil and tapped three blinking amber dots on the digital sector overlay. "Before either of you signs a single tracking warrant, look closely at your own forensic timestamps from 1:40 AM."

Lu Zhen frowned, leaning closer to the screen. "Those are the three trunk switching stations outside Jingzhou."

"Correct," Elder Qin said, his voice calm yet carrying the unmistakable weight of forty years in command. "Now read page twelve of your own counterintelligence appendix, General Lu. Two of those three switching stations were compromised by technicians on Shanshui Group's payroll. They were actively feeding real-time provincial banking schedules and police movement routes to offshore short-selling desks tied to the 1.2 trillion yuan capital-flight attack."

Lu Zhen's expression stiffened as he flipped open his crimson folder and scanned the page. His silence confirmed every word.

Elder Qin shifted his steady gaze to Shen Kuo. "And Deputy Minister, your own technical report admits that the fourteen encrypted relays the Black Guard severed were already leaking live intercept telemetry back to foreign handlers. Had State Security waited for standard inter-agency sign-off to isolate those lines, the forty-seven foreign agents you just arrested would have burned their cipher books and vanished before sunrise. Worse, the leaked clearing data would have triggered a national banking panic before the 8:30 AM market open."

Shen Kuo pressed his lips together, his knuckles whitening against the edge of the steel table. "Even if their timing prevented a leak, Elder Qin, institutions cannot function on mystery. We do not know who commands them, we cannot audit their roster, and they left zero operational trace."

"Look at the actual record in front of you," Elder Qin countered firmly. "Did the Black Guard fire a single unauthorized shot? No. Did they seize a single military depot, disrupt a single civilian power grid, or detain a single innocent citizen? No. Every captured espionage dossier and compromised relay log was left cleanly inside your own secure drop channels so your prosecutors could build airtight criminal cases."

The senior Central Military Commission director beside Elder Qin nodded slowly. "The operational discipline was flawless. Whoever directed that sweep knew our institutional vulnerabilities better than our own internal auditors."

"That is the exact point both of you need to absorb," Elder Qin said, sliding a single sealed directive bearing the Capital Top Council's joint stamp across the brushed steel table. "The Black Guard answers to the holder of the Black Order, the same sovereign shield that just placed a 300 trillion yuan reserve behind our national banks to stop a foreign financial assault. Furthermore, their emergency deployment is temporary and scheduled to withdraw cleanly once the financial firebreak stabilizes."

Elder Qin stood, placing both hands firmly on the table edge. "Let me put this in plain military terms. If Counterintelligence and State Security spend the next seventy-two hours hunting an allied sovereign shield instead of purging the internal leaks that nearly cost this country 1.2 trillion yuan, you will not be defending national security. You will be provoking the one force that just prevented a national financial collapse."

Neither commander spoke. The logic was airtight, and the Top Council directive left zero room for turf battles.

"Plug your compromised relay nodes," Elder Qin ordered crisply. "Interrogate the forty-seven foreign assets, trace every official who sold them access, and cancel all tracking operations against the Black Guard immediately."

Major General Lu Zhen closed his crimson folder and snapped to attention. "Clear, Elder Qin. Southern Theater will focus entirely on internal security audits."

Deputy Minister Shen Kuo exhaled slowly, closed the black file, and inclined his head. "Understood, Elder Qin. State Security will stand down on the tracking request."

"Good," Elder Qin said, lifting his wool coat. "Now get back to work."
