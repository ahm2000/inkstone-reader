# Chapter 192: The Kill Order

By six o'clock on Friday evening, dusk had settled over Victoria Harbour, and neon signs along Kowloon glowed red and gold across the dark water.

Inside the Central penthouse, the mahogany dining table lay buried under tall stacks of leaked hotel logs, conference guest lists, customs entry sheets, and grainy port security photos.

A blue haze of cigar and cigarette smoke hung over the papers.

Marcus Vance rubbed his temples, tossing aside a list of bankers from Futian CBD. "This is hopeless, Victor. I checked forty bank directors, twenty shipping executives, and every senior official who attended the Sha City Economic Forum. Not one of them visited both Shanghai Port and Shenzhen Pier Nine on the days we were hit."

"Because you only look at the people sitting in the front row," Hao Zhenhai muttered.

He had tossed off his suit jacket and rolled up his white sleeves as ash spilled across his cuffs. For forty minutes, Hao Zhenhai had ignored the VIP rosters completely, checking ordinary hotel sheets, economy flight lists, and gate visitor logs instead.

Suddenly, Hao Zhenhai's fingers froze on a printed sheet from a modest business hotel near the Sha City Economic Forum.

"Wait," Hao Zhenhai whispered, leaning close. "Simon! Hand me the Shenzhen Pier Nine gate log from Wednesday afternoon. And pull the Shanghai Port customs visitor sheet from three weeks ago!"

Simon Kwok hurried around the table, dropping both folders beside Hao Zhenhai's elbow.

Hao Zhenhai flipped rapidly through the pages, his breathing growing faster.

"Look at this!" Hao Zhenhai snapped, his voice shaking with shock and fury.

Victor Leung leaned forward, cigar clamped between his teeth, while Marcus Vance squinted down at the three red circles.

"Chen Xuan?" Marcus Vance read aloud, blinking in disbelief. He picked up the company profile Simon Kwok had printed from a Shanghai registry. "Twenty-five years old. Junior clerk at Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai. Monthly pay under ten thousand yuan. No car, rents an old apartment, carries a green thermos."

Marcus Vance let out a loud, incredulous laugh and tossed the paper back. "Have you lost your mind from too many cigarettes, Zhenhai? We lost 140 billion yuan in Zurich and 12 billion yuan in Shenzhen, and you blame a plain Shanghai desk clerk?"

"Shut up and look at the dates!" Hao Zhenhai barked, slapping his palm down so hard the crystal ashtray rattled. "Shanghai Port, Terminal Four. Our night supervisor gets arrested at nine in the evening. Who signed in at the customs office two hours earlier for a cargo delay? Chen Xuan!"

He jabbed his finger onto the second sheet.

"Sha City Economic Forum. The exact afternoon our provincial fixer gets dragged out in handcuffs, who is checked into the hotel across the street and spotted walking down the side hall? Chen Xuan!"

He slapped down the third page beside a grainy camera print from Shenzhen Pier Nine.

"And Wednesday afternoon in Shenzhen!" Hao Zhenhai's eyes were bloodshot and fierce. "Twenty minutes before the Shen Family collapsed and Pier Nine was sealed, look at the gate camera! Same faded dark-blue windbreaker, same cheap sneakers, same green thermos! Standing fifty paces from Old Man Shen's private yacht!"

The mocking smile vanished from Marcus Vance's face.

"Three cities," Marcus Vance whispered, his throat dry. "Three disasters. One clerk."

Victor Leung sat silent for ten full seconds.

"Think about how smart men hide," Victor Leung said softly, his voice colder than ice. "If a billionaire or a Beijing official walked into Shanghai Port, Sha City, and Shenzhen Pier Nine, our lookouts would flag them in five minutes. Nobody watches a plain clerk from a small logistics firm. Nobody checks his pockets. Nobody asks why he carries a green thermos."

"Whether he gives the orders or carries the real keys for those white-coated scouts," Hao Zhenhai hissed, crushing his cigarette out with shaking fingers, "he is the thread linking every blow we took. If those scouts are already on Changbai Mountain, Chen Xuan is heading north next!"

Victor Leung exhaled a slow plume of cigar smoke and looked at Simon Kwok.

"Is Kieran Cole still in Hong Kong?"

"Yes, Mr. Leung," Simon Kwok replied at once. "He and his three men are staying in Tsim Sha Tsui on our monthly retainer."

"Bring Kieran Cole up here now," Victor Leung ordered. "And book four first-class seats to Shanghai Hongqiao Airport on the next flight."

Twenty minutes later, the heavy oak doors opened again.

Kieran Cole strode into the smoky penthouse with an arrogant swagger. A white scar crossed his right knuckles, and his gray eyes carried the flat, empty look of a man who had spent fifteen years taking dirty contracts across Asia and Eastern Europe.

"Mr. Leung," Kieran Cole said with a crooked smile, dropping uninvited into a leather chair. "Your aide sounded like the building was burning. Who needs to disappear?"

"A Shanghai office clerk?" Cole raised an eyebrow. "You pulled my four-man squad off standby for a kid holding a tea bottle? Does he have armored cars? Armed guards?"

"No visible guards," Victor Leung said evenly. "He walks alone. He rides the subway. He shops near Nanjing Road."

"Give me a knife and two hours, and I can handle him alone," Cole smirked, spinning the photo between scarred fingers.

"Do not get careless, Cole," Hao Zhenhai warned sharply through cigarette smoke. "This clerk stood at the center of three hits that cost us over 150 billion yuan. Take your full team. Bring Viktor Orlov, Tomasz Novak, and Rico Alvarez. Ask no questions, leave him cold, and make sure he never boards a flight out of Shanghai."

Victor Leung leaned forward, folding his hands on the table.

"8 million yuan," Victor Leung stated flatly. "4 million wired to your Macau account when you board the plane. The other 4 million the moment you confirm Chen Xuan is dead in Shanghai tonight."

Kieran Cole's smirk widened into a hungry grin. 8 million yuan for one unarmed clerk was the easiest payday he had seen in ten years.

"Consider him gone before midnight," Kieran Cole said, slipping the photo into his jacket as he stood. "My boys already have clean foreign passports and contacts waiting in Shanghai with fresh gear. By breakfast tomorrow, Mr. Leung, your little clerk problem will be cold in an alley."

Marcus Vance exhaled nervously, swirling the last drops of whiskey in his glass. "Are you sure about sending foreign killers onto Nanjing Road, Victor? If State Security catches wind of it, Beijing will tear Hong Kong apart."

"They will not catch wind of anything," Victor Leung replied smoothly, lighting a fresh Cuban cigar as he looked over Victoria Harbour. "Kieran Cole has worked six countries without leaving a fingerprint. Tonight that clerk dies in the rain. Tomorrow we seal Changbai Mountain and take back everything we lost."

Neither man realized they had just sent four wolves straight toward a perimeter no predator on earth had ever walked back out of.
