# Chapter 192: The Kill Order

By 12:00 PM in Hong Kong (+8:00 GMT), the winter rain had thickened into a steady silver sheet over Repulse Bay. High up on the hillside, a private dining club sat tucked behind tall iron gates and wet banyan trees. No tourists came here, and the staff knew better than to linger once tea was poured.

Inside a quiet timber-paneled room overlooking the misty cove, Victor Leung sat across from a lean man in a dark charcoal rain jacket. Kieran Cole had a pale scar cutting across his left jaw and the still, watchful eyes of someone who had spent fifteen years taking high-risk contracts across three continents.

Kou Zhenshan stood near the sideboard, watching the door. Three other men waited just outside in the covered hallway, wearing plain winter coats that made them look like foreign shipping consultants on a layover.

"Thank you for coming on short notice, Mr. Cole," Victor Leung said, gesturing to the teapot.

"Skip the tea," Kieran Cole said in flat, clipped English. "Your Macau broker said 8 million yuan. Half upfront, half on proof. That kind of money means either the target is guarded by a platoon or you want it done tonight."

"Both parts of your guess are half right," Victor Leung replied smoothly. "I want it done tonight, in Shanghai. As for guards, there are none."

He slid a sealed manila envelope across the table. Kieran Cole opened it with one hand and spread five high-resolution photographs across the linen tablecloth.

Every photo showed the same young man in Shanghai. In one frame, he was stepping off a subway escalator in a gray winter coat. In another, he was standing near a street stall holding a dented green metal thermos. A third showed him walking down a quiet Pudong sidewalk with a canvas grocery tote.

Across the top of the summary sheet sat a single typed line: Target Alpha, Shanghai Freight Liaison.

"No name?" Cole asked, looking up.

"Our mainland informants hit a wall when they pulled his deeper records," Victor Leung admitted without flinching. "On paper, he works a minor logistics desk at Hengxin Logistics and clocks out at 4:55 PM. In reality, he is linked to Haiyue Group and appears to be the quiet courier for a major European private fund that has been bleeding my consortium dry."

Kieran Cole studied the target's face for ten silent seconds. "No name. Blank registry. Tied to a major European fund. And you claim he walks around Shanghai with zero protection."

"We had two spotters watch him for seventy-two hours," Kou Zhenshan stepped forward to say. "No armored sedans. No suits trailing him. No security team watching his street. He takes the metro, buys vegetables, and goes home to his wife. He hides by playing a lazy desk clerk."

Cole's eyes stayed cold and skeptical. "Amateurs mistake quiet for helpless. If he is a courier for serious money, he might still carry a tracker or have a quick panic line on his phone."

"Then take his phone after you drop him," Victor Leung said evenly. "Keep it intact if you can. If not, destroy it and send me a photo confirming the job is finished. Right now, 4 million yuan is already sitting in your Macau escrow account. The other 4 million releases the moment my terminal receives confirmation."

Kieran Cole thought for a brief moment, weighing the payout against the risk. A fee of 8 million yuan for a single night's work on an unguarded civilian target was more than generous, and Shanghai's cold December rain would keep street crowds thin and cameras blurred.

"Fine," Cole said. He reached back and tapped twice on the wooden door frame.

Three men stepped quietly into the room. Rico Alvarez was compact and wiry, with dark eyes that darted instantly to every exit. Tomasz Novak stood a head taller than everyone else, broad across the shoulders like a former heavyweight boxer. Dmitri Sokol was thin, pale, and silent, his hands resting casually in his coat pockets.

"Briefing takes three minutes," Cole told them, pointing to the photos on the table. "Target is in Shanghai. Mid-to-late twenties, gray coat, usually carries a battered green thermos. We cross to Shenzhen by car in twenty minutes. Our private charter to Shanghai Hongqiao is already filed under a logistics inspection permit. We land at 5:30 PM."

Rico Alvarez picked up one of the photos and smirked. "Looks like a college kid who sleeps through morning lectures."

"Do not underestimate him," Cole warned sharply. "We pick him up outside his office or along his evening route. No gunfire on a crowded street. We herd him into a side alley or a quiet parking lot, finish it in under thirty seconds, grab his phone, and vanish. Novak takes the heavy block. Rico closes from the flank. Dmitri runs overwatch with a suppressed pistol in case anything unexpected walks into the lane."

Dmitri Sokol gave a single stiff nod. "What is our exit window?"

"A fast boat out of the Yangtze estuary at 2:00 AM, or an early morning transfer through Ningbo," Kou Zhenshan answered. "My people have already cleared both routes. Just make sure there is no noise before you leave the city."

Kieran Cole tucked the five photographs into his inner jacket pocket and zipped it shut. "Confirm the Macau escrow transfer."

Victor Leung nodded to Kou Zhenshan. Kou tapped his tablet twice, and a soft chime sounded on Cole's encrypted handset. Cole glanced at the screen, saw the 4 million yuan lock into place, and nodded once.

"You will have your photo before midnight," Cole said.

He turned and led Rico Alvarez, Tomasz Novak, and Dmitri Sokol out into the wet corridor. Moments later, the muted rumble of a dark van faded down the hillside driveway.

Kou Zhenshan exhaled slowly, pouring himself a cup of tea. "Cole has never failed a contract in ten years. By tomorrow morning, Shanghai will be scrambling in the dark, and we will already be halfway to Changbai Mountain."

Victor Leung looked out at the cold rain sweeping across Repulse Bay. "Make sure the northern vault is sealed tight, Zhenshan. Once Shanghai bleeds tonight, we finish moving the accounts and never look back."
