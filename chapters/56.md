# Chapter 56: Zhou Guodong's Phone

High up on the thirty-eighth floor of Shenghua Maritime's glass tower, morning sun poured warmly across the marble floor.

Zhou Guodong had just lifted his dark clay teapot when his personal phone vibrated across the mahogany desk. Seeing his son's name on the screen, he answered with an indulgent chuckle.

"Well? Did Sun Haoran bring his knees to Berth Seven on time?"

Instead of a smug laugh, a frantic shriek exploded through the speaker, drowned out by marine sirens and shouting voices.

"Dad! They cut the seals! The central anti-smuggling bureau is on Berth Seven! They handcuffed Wang Zhiming and opened all six blue containers! Dad, they found the cars and the crates!"

A crisp, unfamiliar voice cut across the line. "Put the phone down, Zhou Mingcheng. Step away from the vehicle."

Then the line went dead.

The clay teapot slipped in Zhou Guodong's hand, splashing hot pu'er tea across his silk sleeve. He did not even feel the scald. His face flushed dark purple, then drained swiftly to chalk.

"Six containers," Zhou Guodong breathed, slamming the teapot down and grabbing his private silver phone. "Impossible. Nobody knew about Bonded Annex East except Wang Zhiming and my warehouse chief. Who tipped them off?"

He stabbed the speed-dial key for Deputy Director Lin Haibo at the Qingyan Port Authority, a man whose daughter's overseas tuition Zhou Guodong had paid for four straight years.

The line rang twice. Then a flat mechanical tone announced that the subscriber had switched off his phone.

Zhou Guodong cursed and punched in the number of Section Chief Ma Guoping at Qingyan Customs. It rang seven times, then clicked straight to voicemail.

Sweat broke out across Zhou Guodong's bald crown. He grabbed the desk landline with his left hand while dialing his silver mobile with his right, working rapidly down the private list of municipal protectors, harbor supervisors, and legal fixers he had fed with envelopes and banquet tables for a decade.

On the fourth call, Vice Director Qian Wenbin of the Harbor Commerce Office finally picked up.

"Qian Wenbin!" Zhou Guodong shouted, his composure cracking wide open. "What is happening at Berth Seven? Send someone from the city committee down to the dock right now! Tell them it is a clerical error!"

On the other end, Qian Wenbin's voice sounded like a man standing on a trapdoor.

"Zhou Guodong, are you insane?" Qian Wenbin hissed in sheer terror. "Never call this number again! A direct strike team from the General Administration of Customs Anti-Smuggling Bureau flew in overnight and bypassed the entire city! They have dual-use smuggling manifests with your company seal on them! Keep my name out of your mouth!"

*Click.*

The dial tone hummed coldly against Zhou Guodong's ear.

He tried a fifth number. Blocked. He tried a sixth, a senior lawyer with ties to the provincial courts. The secretary hung up without a word.

Every bridge Zhou Guodong had built over ten years had turned to ash in four minutes. His right hand shook so badly that the silver mobile bounced off the blotter. Slowly, Zhou Guodong collapsed into his leather chair, staring at the ceiling with hollow eyes.

Outside, hurried footsteps echoed across reception, followed by his secretary's terrified gasp.

The double doors swung open. Four prosecutors in dark coats walked in, flanked by six uniformed officers from the Anti-Smuggling Bureau.

"Zhou Guodong, Chairman of Shenghua Maritime Group," the lead prosecutor said evenly, placing a red-stamped warrant over the spilled tea. "You are under arrest for organized smuggling of luxury vehicles and restricted dual-use equipment, and bribery of public officials. Stand up."

Zhou Guodong could not even summon the breath to argue. Two officers lifted him by the elbows and led him out of his glass tower forever.

* * *

Down at Berth Seven, the morning breeze turned clear and bright.

While forensic officers cataloged Shenghua Maritime's seized cargo, an acting Inspector from the central team reviewed Hengxin Logistics' three orange containers, verifying Monday's green clearance and Wang Zhiming's forged Code 704-B override.

"Clear these three boxes immediately," the Inspector told the yard supervisor. "Move them to the front of the *Eastern Crane* queue. Issue a formal written apology under Qingyan Port Customs seal and waive every surcharge."

By 9:20 AM, with forty minutes to spare before cutoff, the gantry crane lowered all three containers cleanly into the *Eastern Crane*.

* * *

Back on the twelfth floor of the Dongfang Building, the dispatch monitor chimed brightly at 9:35 AM.

Pan Xiulan stared at the green clearance banner, blinked twice, and let out a breathless cheer that rattled the coffee mugs in the pantry.

"General Manager Sun! They cleared! All three containers are aboard the *Eastern Crane*! Zero penalty! And Qingyan Port Customs emailed us a stamped official apology letter!"

General Manager Sun Haoran burst from his glass office, practically snatching the printed sheet from Pan Xiulan's hand. His fingers trembled as he read the red customs seal twice from top to bottom.

Before anyone could finish cheering, Manager Hu Dewei's phone buzzed with a breaking harbor news alert. Hu Dewei glanced down, and every drop of color vanished from his cheeks.

"General Manager Sun," Hu Dewei whispered, his voice shaky. "Look at the news. Shenghua Maritime Group just got raided at Berth Seven by a central anti-smuggling task force from Beijing. Deputy Director Wang Zhiming was arrested on the dock. Zhou Mingcheng was detained at the harbor. And prosecutors just arrested Chairman Zhou Guodong in his office."

A sudden, profound silence settled across the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics. Twenty employees stood completely still beside their desks.

Three days ago, Shenghua Maritime Group had been an untouchable mountain over Qingyan Port. On Tuesday morning, Zhou Guodong had sentenced Hengxin Logistics to death. And on Tuesday morning, sitting calmly in the back corner by the window, Chen Xuan had taken one quiet look at the hold notice and told General Manager Sun not to cancel the booking.

Slowly, in unison, General Manager Sun Haoran and Manager Hu Dewei turned their heads toward the corner desk by the window.

Chen Xuan sat calmly in his neat gray shirt, his green thermos resting beside his keyboard. In his hands, he held a small bright orange that Liu Meifang had tucked into his coat pocket that morning.

Unbothered by the stares, Chen Xuan peeled the skin away in one neat spiral, laid the peel on a square of paper towel, and divided the orange into two tidy halves.

"General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said mildly, glancing up. "Now that the *Eastern Crane* has loaded, should I send the confirmation to Miss Qin Yuhan's office at Haiyue Hotel Group?"

Sun Haoran swallowed hard, straightening his jacket as if addressing a visiting board chairman. "Yes. Yes, Chen Xuan, please do. Whenever it is convenient for you."

Beside him, Manager Hu Dewei rubbed his damp palms against his trousers, pale with awe and lingering terror at how close he had come to ordering Chen Xuan to beg Zhou Mingcheng on Tuesday.

"Chen Xuan," Hu Dewei said in a small, overly polite voice. "May I refill your thermos with fresh hot water?"

"No need, Manager Hu, it is still warm," Chen Xuan replied politely, popping a sweet slice of orange into his mouth.

Across the aisle, Zhang Pengfei sat behind his monitor with a quiet, knowing smile. He did not know who Chen Xuan really was, and he never intended to ask. He only knew that on the twelfth floor of the Dongfang Building, an ordinary office worker was eating an orange while the biggest shipping family in Qingyan Port turned to dust.
