# Chapter 56: Central Military Commission: Closed-Door Storm

At six forty-five on Monday morning, the underground meeting room beneath the Central Military Commission smelled of burnt tobacco and cold sweat. Ten senior department heads sat around the dark wooden table. Their uniform collars were buttoned tight despite the stuffy air, and the ashtrays were already half full.

General Qin Zhengguo stood at the head of the table, a cigarette burning forgotten between two thick fingers. When he slammed the decoded navy radar files and border troop move records onto the wood, the crack sounded like a rifle shot. Two white teacups jumped in their saucers.

"Nine years," General Qin Zhengguo said, his voice raw. "Nine full years of troop shift lists, gaps in our coastal radar, and weapons shipping schedules walking straight out of our vaults. Not one single warning ever reached my desk."

He flicked ash into a glass tray, his eyes locked onto the left side of the table. Logistics Chief Lieutenant General Kang Weidong froze with his lighter halfway to his mouth. Beside him, Political Work Director Lieutenant General Shen Mingyuan set his pen down, his knuckles bone-white.

"General Qin Zhengguo, we need to check who handled these files before we jump to conclusions," Lieutenant General Kang Weidong said, forcing his voice to stay calm while a vein hammered at his temple. "Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu held top clearance. His supply reviews passed three separate checks every few months."

"Three checks reporting straight to your office, Kang Weidong," snapped Military Discipline Chief General Du Changhe. He crushed his cigarette out hard and shook another one from his pack. "Are you telling us your inspectors missed nine years of fake double shipping lists by accident?"

"Watch what you are saying, Du Changhe," Kang Weidong shot back, his chair scraping backward. "Logistics handles forty thousand orders a month across five military zones. If a senior colonel changes a secret list using a real security key, that is a spy-hunting failure, not a supply mistake."

Naval Intelligence Director Rear Admiral Xue Biao blew smoke through his nose and leaned forward. "Do not dump your leaking office onto my navy, Kang Weidong. Look at page fourteen. Those radar repair gaps off the east coast were not spotted by foreign satellites. They matched your fuel-boat orders to the exact minute. Someone inside your building handed Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu the master schedule."

"That is a lie!" Kang Weidong barked, slapping his palm down on the folder. "My office does not keep navy radar schedules!"

"No," Joint Staff Deputy Chief General Lei Zhenshan cut in coldly, tapping a stained finger on a yellow page tab. "Your office kept the joint drill timetables. And Political Work renewed Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's security clearance every eighteen months without questioning a single junior officer under him."

Every head snapped toward Lieutenant General Shen Mingyuan. He had not even lit his cigarette. His shaking fingers tore the paper seam until dry tobacco spilled across his desk pad.

"Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu had a spotless record," Shen Mingyuan said, his voice cracking before he cleared his throat. "He won two medals. No family overseas. No gambling debts. No unexplained houses or money. His file was cleaner than half the officers in this building."

"That should have scared you," Equipment Development Director General Yao Guangming muttered through gray smoke. "Nobody stays that spotless for twenty years unless someone higher up is scrubbing the dirt off his name. Who signed off on his move to the central schedule room in 2017?"

Silence dropped over the room like wet iron. The air vents hummed loudly overhead.

General Qin Zhengguo took a slow drag before speaking. "Your deputy signed it, Shen Mingyuan. Your old division chief signed beside him, Kang Weidong. Both of them were pulled out of their apartments at three in the morning by people you do not have the rank to question."

Three regional army officers traded pale, stiff looks. Eastern Theater Representative Major General Gao Feng swallowed hard. "General Qin Zhengguo, forgive me for speaking bluntly. The overnight list names three hundred and eighteen major suspects nationwide, including seventeen active officers above regiment rank. Who made those arrests? Military Discipline did not even hear about it until five thirty."

"Not us," General Du Changhe confirmed grimly, staring down at his scarred hands. "When my teams reached Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's home, the front door was unlocked, the safes stood open, and a printed list of proof sat on his dining table. Every hard drive was already copied and sealed."

Rocket Force Security Chief Major General Bai Lixin leaned forward, his jaw tight. "I have to ask what everyone here is choking on, General Qin Zhengguo. If an outside team walked past three rings of outer guards and grabbed a senior colonel without setting off one alarm, our base security is a joke. Was it Black Pine Guard?"

Several men drew sharp breaths. A cigarette rolled off an ashtray and burned against the dark wood until Southern Theater Representative Major General Huo Jian slapped it out with his bare thumb, barely flinching.

General Qin Zhengguo's eyes went completely flat. "Black Pine Guard did not touch Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu."

"Then who did?" Northern Theater Representative Major General Wen Bo demanded, his voice rising. "Base commanders are calling in panic because their contacts vanished before sunrise! Who has the authority to pull active officers out of bed without a Central Military Commission warrant?"

"Zero Guard," General Qin Zhengguo said quietly.

Those two words sucked all the air out of the room. General Lei Zhenshan froze with his teacup inches from his lips. General Du Changhe stared blankly as gray ash fell onto his knee.

"Impossible," Lei Zhenshan whispered. "Zero Guard has not moved since the founding era. Even Black Pine Guard cannot call them out. I dialed the vault line at five o'clock and got a dead tone."

"Because Black Pine Guard is locked down and under monitoring," General Qin Zhengguo said, crushing his cigarette into the tray. "I had to wake retired Founding Elder Song at four in the morning for the old override code just so the vault would confirm our chain of command is still alive. The people who dropped these files on us did not ask our permission. They handed us a mop and told us to clean up our own floor before the roof falls in."

Kang Weidong's face had turned the color of wet chalk. His hand shook as he reached for his water glass. "General Qin Zhengguo, I swear on forty years in uniform, I never took a single cent from Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu. I will turn myself over to Military Discipline today."

"You will," General Qin Zhengguo replied coldly. "Every man at this table hands over his personal files, staff lists, and family asset sheets to General Du Changhe before noon. Nobody leaves this compound without a signed pass."

"What about the regional bases?" Major General Gao Feng asked quickly, sweat shining along his hairline. "Once foreign spy stations realize their mole is gone, they will shut down their coastal links before lunch."

"Let them try," Rear Admiral Xue Biao snapped, stubbing out his cigarette and stabbing a finger at the coastal maps. "This file lists every leaked signal and dirty dock contractor. Give me ninety minutes and Naval Intelligence will lock down every shipyard from Qingdao to Zhanjiang."

"You have sixty," General Qin Zhengguo ordered, his eyes sweeping across all ten men. "At eight o'clock, Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua open the Cross-Department War Room. I will not walk in carrying army excuses. Kang Weidong, Shen Mingyuan, write down every name that touched Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's file since 2017. Lei Zhenshan, set up emergency officer swaps for all three military zones. Remove anyone who pushes back."

He tapped a fresh cigarette against his thumb and met their eyes one by one.

"Someone did our job in forty-eight hours and left the knife sitting on our table. Either we cut the rot out tonight, or we explain why the Central Military Commission slept for nine years. Move."

Chairs scraped back in ragged unison through swirling smoke. Nobody looked at Kang Weidong or Shen Mingyuan as the heavy folders slammed shut.
