# Chapter 56: Central Military Commission: Closed-Door Storm

At six forty-five on Monday morning, the subterranean conference room beneath the Central Military Commission reeked of scorched tobacco and cold sweat. Ten senior department heads sat around the walnut table, collars buttoned tight despite the stifling air, ashtrays already half full.

General Qin Zhengguo stood at the head of the table, a cigarette burning forgotten between two thick fingers. When he slammed the decrypted naval radar logs and border-garrison transfer files onto the wood, the crack sounded like a rifle bolt. Two porcelain teacups jumped in their saucers.

"Nine years," General Qin Zhengguo said, his voice scraped raw. "Nine full years of rotated garrison rosters, coastal radar blind windows, and munitions transit schedules walking straight out of our archives. Not one red flag reached my desk."

He flicked ash into a glass tray, eyes locked on the left side of the table. Logistics Chief Lieutenant General Kang Weidong froze with his lighter halfway to his mouth. Beside him, Political Work Director Lieutenant General Shen Mingyuan lowered his pen, knuckles bone-white.

"General Qin Zhengguo, we must verify the chain of custody before drawing conclusions," Lieutenant General Kang Weidong said, forcing his tone level while a pulse hammered in his temple. "Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu held top internal clearance. His quarterly logistics audits passed three separate panels."

"Three panels reporting directly to your office, Kang Weidong," snapped Military Discipline Chief General Du Changhe. He crushed his cigarette out violently and shook another from his pack. "Are you telling us your auditors missed nine years of duplicate manifests by accident?"

"Watch your implication, Du Changhe," Kang Weidong shot back, his chair scraping backward. "Logistics processes forty thousand orders a month across five theaters. If a senior colonel alters an encrypted manifest with a valid clearance key, that is a counter-intelligence failure, not a supply oversight."

Naval Intelligence Director Rear Admiral Xue Biao exhaled smoke through his nose and leaned forward. "Do not dump your leaking warehouse on my fleet, Kang Weidong. Look at page fourteen. Those radar maintenance windows off the eastern shelf were not stolen by satellites. They matched your fuel-barge requisitions to the minute. Someone inside your building handed Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu the master calendar."

"That is a lie!" Kang Weidong barked, slapping his palm on the folder. "My office does not hold naval radar timetables!"

"No," Joint Staff Deputy Chief General Lei Zhenshan cut in coldly, tapping a stained finger on a yellow tab. "Your office held the joint exercise tables. And Political Work renewed Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's clearance every eighteen months without interviewing one junior officer."

Every head snapped toward Lieutenant General Shen Mingyuan. He had not lit his cigarette; his trembling fingers shredded its seam until dry tobacco spilled onto his blotter.

"Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu had an immaculate record," Shen Mingyuan said, his voice cracking before he cleared his throat. "Decorated twice. No overseas relatives. No gambling debts. No unexplained property. His file was cleaner than half the officers in this building."

"That should have terrified you," Equipment Development Director General Yao Guangming muttered through gray smoke. "Nobody stays that clean for twenty years unless someone above is scrubbing the dirt off him. Who signed his transfer to the central scheduling vault in 2017?"

Silence dropped like wet iron. The air vents hummed loudly.

General Qin Zhengguo took a slow drag before speaking. "Your deputy signed it, Shen Mingyuan. Your former division chief countersigned it, Kang Weidong. Both were pulled out of their apartments at three in the morning by people you lack the clearance to question."

Three theater representatives exchanged pale, rigid glances. Eastern Theater Representative Major General Gao Feng swallowed hard. "General Qin Zhengguo, forgive my bluntness. The overnight list cites three hundred and eighteen principals nationwide, including seventeen active-duty officers above regiment rank. Who executed the extraction? Military Discipline was not briefed until five thirty."

"Not us," General Du Changhe confirmed grimly, staring at his scarred hands. "My teams reached Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's compound to find the door unlocked, safes open, and a printed evidence index on his dining table. Every encrypted drive was already imaged and sealed."

Rocket Force Security Chief Major General Bai Lixin leaned forward, jaw clenched tight. "I have to ask what everyone here is choking on, General Qin Zhengguo. If an outside unit walked past three layers of perimeter guards and extracted a senior colonel without tripping one alarm, our command security is a joke. Was it Black Pine Guard?"

Several men drew sharp breaths. A cigarette rolled off an ashtray and smoldered against the walnut until Southern Theater Representative Major General Huo Jian slapped it dead with his bare thumb, barely flinching.

General Qin Zhengguo's eyes went flat. "Black Pine Guard did not touch Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu."

"Then who did?" Northern Theater Representative Major General Wen Bo demanded, voice rising. "Garrison commanders are calling in panic because their liaisons vanished before dawn! Who has authority to pull active officers out of bed without a Central Military Commission warrant?"

"Zero Guard," General Qin Zhengguo said quietly.

The two words drained the oxygen from the room. General Lei Zhenshan froze with his teacup inches from his lips. General Du Changhe stared blankly as ash collapsed onto his knee.

"Impossible," Lei Zhenshan whispered. "Zero Guard has not activated since the founding era. Even Black Pine Guard cannot summon them. I called the vault line at five and got a dead tone."

"Because Black Pine Guard is locked down and under monitoring," General Qin Zhengguo said, crushing his butt in the tray. "I woke retired Founding Elder Song at four in the morning for the old override phrase just so the vault would acknowledge our chain of command is still breathing. The people who handed us these files did not ask permission. They handed us a mop and told us to clean our own floor before the roof comes down."

Kang Weidong's face had turned the color of damp chalk. His hand shook as he reached for his glass. "General Qin Zhengguo, I swear on forty years in uniform, I never took a single fen from Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu. I will submit to Military Discipline today."

"You will," General Qin Zhengguo replied coldly. "Every man here submits his personal archive, staff rosters, and family disclosures to General Du Changhe before noon. Nobody leaves this compound without countersigned clearance."

"What about the theater garrisons?" Major General Gao Feng asked quickly, sweat beading along his hairline. "Once foreign stations realize their mole vanished, they will burn their coastal relays before lunch."

"Let them try," Rear Admiral Xue Biao snapped, stubbing out his cigarette and stabbing a finger at the radar maps. "The packet lists every compromised transponder and dirty dock contractor. Give me ninety minutes and Naval Intelligence locks down every dry dock from Qingdao to Zhanjiang."

"You have sixty," General Qin Zhengguo ordered, sweeping his gaze across all ten men. "At eight o'clock, Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua open the Cross-Department War Room. I will not walk in carrying military excuses. Kang Weidong, Shen Mingyuan, write down every name that touched Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's file since 2017. Lei Zhenshan, draft emergency rotations for all three theaters. Relieve any officer who resists."

He tapped a fresh cigarette against his thumbnail and met their eyes one by one.

"Someone did our job in forty-eight hours and left the knife on our table. Either we cut the rot out tonight, or we explain why the Central Military Commission slept for nine years. Move."

Chairs scraped back in ragged unison through swirling smoke. Nobody looked at Kang Weidong or Shen Mingyuan as the folders slammed shut.
