# Chapter 1: Leave on Time

At 4:47 on Friday afternoon, the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai had stopped pretending to work. Everyone was watching the clock.

Chen Xuan sat at desk fourteen in a clean gray jacket and dark trousers. Beside his mouse pad sat a scratched green thermos of jasmine tea and half a cold red-bean bun. On paper, he was a junior operations supervisor earning twelve thousand yuan a month before tax. For three years, he arrived at 8:55, handled spreadsheets quietly, and packed up at 4:55.

Two desks over, Pan Xiulan snapped her notebook shut. "Eight minutes," she muttered. "If I catch the elevator before five, I can make the 5:30 bus on Binjiang Avenue."

Across the aisle, Zheng Qian groaned. "Sister Pan, you say that every Friday, and something always drops at 4:50. Do not jinx our row."

Pan Xiulan gave a tired glare, her mouth twitching. "Blame the port desk. Look at Chen Xuan. His screen is already clean."

Zheng Qian grinned toward desk fourteen. "He never chats and never misses a number. Are you taking that half bun home again?"

Chen Xuan looked up mildly. "It is still good," he said evenly. "Wasting food is a bad habit."

Before Zheng Qian could reply, the corner office door banged open. Department Manager Hu Dewei marched out, face red, clutching a thick red folder. Smiles vanished at once. Zheng Qian pretended to type. Pan Xiulan froze with one hand in her drawer.

Hu Dewei slammed the folder onto an empty desk. "Nobody turns off their computer," he barked. "General Manager Sun Haoran just came back from the port meeting. Three cold containers from the southeast dock have wrong customs weights. The client wants a checked report before midnight. Everyone stays until it is done."

Pan Xiulan's shoulders sagged. Zheng Qian stared at his screen, jaw tight with quiet anger.

Only Zhang Pengfei stood up right away from the front row, smoothing his striped tie with an eager smile. "Manager Hu, do not worry," Zhang Pengfei said loudly. "General Manager Sun Haoran trusted our team for a reason. I already canceled dinner. Working together, we can finish in three or four hours."

Hu Dewei's shoulders eased. He nodded at Zhang Pengfei, then swept a hard stare across the room. "At least someone knows what duty means." His eyes locked onto desk fourteen.

Chen Xuan had not touched his keyboard. He was capping his thermos and placing the red-bean bun in his canvas bag.

Hu Dewei's face darkened. He strode down the aisle and slapped the desk wall. "Chen Xuan. Did you hear me?"

Chen Xuan zipped his bag calmly and met Hu Dewei's glare. "Manager Hu, I heard you clearly. I am leaving at 4:55."

Every keyboard went silent. Zheng Qian froze, eyes wide. Pan Xiulan shot Chen Xuan a desperate warning look behind her monitor.

Zhang Pengfei stepped behind Hu Dewei with a sharp laugh. "Chen Xuan, have you lost your mind? Manager Hu is taking heat from General Manager Sun Haoran, and we are giving up Friday night. Who do you think you are to walk out on the clock?"

"Stay out of it, Zhang Pengfei," Chen Xuan said calmly. "This has nothing to do with overtime."

"Then what is it about?" Hu Dewei snapped, jabbing a finger at him. "You coasted on twelve thousand a month for three years because I was too soft. Walk out at 4:55 and do not come back Monday. I will have human resources move you to the north-suburb cold warehouse. You can count boxes until your fingers freeze."

Pan Xiulan gasped and gripped her desk. Zheng Qian winced. Zhang Pengfei folded his arms, his smug smile widening.

Chen Xuan did not blink. He tapped his spacebar to wake the screen and turned the monitor toward Hu Dewei.

"The three cold containers from the southeast dock," Chen Xuan said calmly. "HXL-409, HXL-412, and HXL-418. I uploaded the fixed weight report to the shared folder at 11:20 this morning and sent a copy to General Manager Sun Haoran's assistant before lunch."

Hu Dewei's raised finger froze. "What did you say?"

"The wrong weight was a copying mistake from Tuesday's boat transfer," Chen Xuan continued evenly. "While checking the bills, I also found a fourteen-thousand-yuan extra storage fee charged Wednesday afternoon. The approval stamp has the initials Z.P."

Color drained from Zhang Pengfei's face. His arms dropped stiffly, his smile twisting into a grimace. "That is nonsense!" Zhang Pengfei blurted, voice cracking. "Manager Hu, the dock clerk mixed up the papers! I never approved double storage!"

"The scanned bill with the time stamp is on page four," Chen Xuan said, clicking his monitor off. "General Manager Sun Haoran's assistant replied at 2:15 that the port accepted the fix. The only open problem in that red folder is the fourteen-thousand-yuan charge under Zhang Pengfei's initials."

Zheng Qian's mouth fell open at Zhang Pengfei's sweating forehead. Pan Xiulan exhaled slowly, eyes bright with stunned respect.

Hu Dewei stared at the blank screen, then opened the red folder with fumbling fingers and read the front note he had never checked. His red cheeks turned an embarrassed purple.

"Manager Hu, I can explain," Zhang Pengfei stammered with a forced laugh. "It was busy, and the shipping agent swore the second bill was for cold storage."

"Shut up!" Hu Dewei roared, snapping the folder shut and spinning on him. "Everyone else, go home! Zhang Pengfei, get into my office right now and explain why my name sits above fourteen thousand yuan of fake storage fees!"

Zhang Pengfei flinched and hurried after Hu Dewei. The door slammed, muting the start of Hu Dewei's shouting.

Relief swept the room. Zheng Qian gave Chen Xuan a wide-eyed thumbs-up under his desk. Pan Xiulan picked up her purse and whispered gratefully.

"You just saved us four hours. Will you be alright Monday? Manager Hu hates losing face."

Chen Xuan picked up his thermos and slung his bag over his shoulder. "He has bigger numbers to explain to General Manager Sun Haoran on Monday. He will not have time for desk fourteen."

The wall clock clicked to 4:55.

"Have a good weekend, Sister Pan," Chen Xuan said politely. "Have a good weekend, Zheng Qian."

He walked unhurriedly to the elevator hall. At 4:55 on Friday, Hengxin Tower's three elevators were always stuck upstairs, yet Chen Xuan never touched the call button. As he entered, the middle elevator chimed, opening an empty, spotless cab waiting on the twelfth floor just for him.

Chen Xuan stepped in, pressed ground, and closed his eyes for six quiet seconds as it descended.
