# Chapter 1: Leave on Time

At four forty-seven on a Friday afternoon in late autumn, the western sun slanted across the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics in Qingyan, turning the dust motes above the cubicles into drifting amber. The central air conditioning hummed with the tired rhythm of a machine promised maintenance three quarters ago. Along the glass wall facing the elevated ring road, rows of gray monitors cast a pale glow over faces that had stopped pretending to be busy twenty minutes earlier.

Chen Xuan sat at desk fourteen, near the fire exit where the fluorescent tube flickered once every six minutes. He wore a clean gray jacket from a discount rack and dark trousers pressed just well enough to pass inspection. Beside his mouse pad rested a scratched green metal thermos filled with morning jasmine tea, and beside it sat a plastic wrapper holding half of a cold red-bean bun from the corner bakery downstairs.

On paper, Chen Xuan was a junior operations supervisor earning twelve thousand yuan a month before tax. He arrived at eight fifty-five, ate in the canteen at twelve fifteen, answered every spreadsheet inquiry without raising his voice, and packed his bag at four fifty-five. Three years in the same corner desk had taught his colleagues to regard him like the potted ficus near the printer: dependable, quiet, and entirely unremarkable.

Two desks to his left, Pan Xiulan closed her manifest ledger with a soft sigh. "Eight minutes left," she murmured, glancing at the wall clock. "If traffic on Binjiang Avenue holds, I might catch the five-thirty bus before the rain starts."

Across the aisle, Zheng Qian leaned back in his squeaking swivel chair and stretched his arms. "Sister Pan, you say that every Friday, and every Friday something drops on our heads at four fifty. Do not jinx the whole department."

Before Pan Xiulan could answer, the frosted glass door of the corner office swung open hard enough to rattle the metal blinds. Department Manager Hu Dewei marched out, his round face flushed beneath a receding hairline, clutching a thick red folder against his chest like a warrant. The chatter across the twelfth floor died instantly, replaced by a frantic chorus of empty keyboard clicking.

Hu Dewei slapped the red folder onto the nearest empty desk. "Nobody turns off a computer," he announced, his voice carrying to the pantry. "General Manager Sun Haoran just returned from the port authority meeting. Three refrigerated containers from the southeast terminal have mismatched customs weights, and the client wants a reconciled audit before midnight. Everyone in operations stays tonight until the variance is cleared."

A quiet groan rippled through the cubicles and died before becoming audible. Pan Xiulan's shoulders dropped. Zheng Qian stared miserably at his reflection in the dark edge of his screen.

Only Zhang Pengfei stood up promptly from the front row, smoothing his striped tie with an eager smile. "Manager Hu, do not worry," Zhang Pengfei said loudly. "General Manager Sun Haoran trusted our department for a reason. I already cancelled my dinner plans. If we pull together, we can trace the logs in three or four hours."

Hu Dewei's expression softened slightly as he nodded at Zhang Pengfei. "At least someone here understands responsibility," Hu Dewei said. His gaze swept the desks for an example to make and landed squarely on desk fourteen.

Chen Xuan had not touched his keyboard. He was calmly screwing the steel cap back onto his green thermos and tucking the half red-bean bun into the side pocket of his canvas bag.

"Chen Xuan," Hu Dewei snapped, stepping down the aisle. "Did you not hear me? Put that bag down. Are your ears stuffed with cotton?"

Chen Xuan zipped the side pocket with unhurried precision and looked up. His dark eyes were calm and devoid of heat. "Manager Hu, I heard you clearly," Chen Xuan said evenly. "I am leaving at four fifty-five."

The silence tightened until the breakroom refrigerator sounded loud. Zheng Qian froze with his hand on his mouse. Pan Xiulan shot Chen Xuan a desperate warning glance from behind her monitor.

Zhang Pengfei let out a sharp laugh and stepped behind Hu Dewei. "Chen Xuan, what kind of attitude is that? Manager Hu is carrying pressure from General Manager Sun Haoran himself, and the rest of us are staying for a port emergency. Who do you think you are to walk out on the clock?"

Hu Dewei's jaw clenched, veins standing out along his collar. "You have been coasting at twelve thousand a month for three years because I had a soft heart," Hu Dewei barked, pointing a stubby finger at Chen Xuan. "If you step out that door at four fifty-five, do not bother returning on Monday. I will have personnel transfer you to the north-suburb cold warehouse to count pallets until your fingers freeze."

Chen Xuan did not flinch. He tapped the spacebar with two fingers to wake his screen and turned the monitor toward Hu Dewei.

"The three refrigerated containers from the southeast terminal," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Containers HXL-409, HXL-412, and HXL-418. The reconciled weight audit was uploaded to the shared folder at eleven twenty this morning. I copied General Manager Sun Haoran's assistant on the summary before lunch."

Hu Dewei blinked, his raised finger hovering. "What did you say?"

"The mismatch was a clerical carry-over from Tuesday's barge transfer," Chen Xuan continued, neither smug nor rushed. "While reconciling the invoices, I also flagged a fourteen-thousand-yuan double-storage overcharge billed to the company on Wednesday afternoon. The approval stamp on the duplicate voucher carries the initials Z.P."

Color drained from Zhang Pengfei's face in a single heartbeat. "That is impossible," Zhang Pengfei stammered, his smile collapsing. "The terminal clerk must have misfiled the sheet! I never signed off on double storage!"

"The scanned voucher with its timestamp is on page four," Chen Xuan said, shutting down his monitor with a soft click. "General Manager Sun Haoran's assistant replied at two fifteen confirming the port authority accepted the correction. The only open item left in that red folder is the fourteen-thousand-yuan discrepancy under Zhang Pengfei's initials."

Hu Dewei stared at the red folder, then flipped open the brass fastener. His eyes darted across the printed cover memo he had never bothered to read before storming out of his office. His face turned from crimson to blotchy, humiliated purple.

"You," Hu Dewei muttered, snapping the folder shut with trembling fingers. He glared at Zhang Pengfei. "Everyone else can go home! Zhang Pengfei, get into my office right now and explain why I am signing off on fourteen thousand yuan of ghost storage fees!"

Pan Xiulan exhaled softly and gathered her purse. Zheng Qian gave Chen Xuan a wide-eyed thumbs-up beneath his desk.

Chen Xuan picked up his scratched green thermos, slung the canvas strap over his shoulder, and glanced at the wall clock. The minute hand clicked cleanly onto four fifty-five.

"Have a good weekend, Manager Hu," Chen Xuan said politely. "Have a good weekend, Sister Pan."

He walked unhurriedly through the glass entrance doors into the marble elevator lobby. On a Friday at five minutes to five, Hengxin Tower's three elevators were normally jammed on the upper floors, forcing clerks to wait several minutes. Chen Xuan never reached the call button. As his shoes crossed the threshold, the central elevator chimed softly, its brushed-steel doors parting smoothly on an empty, spotless cab that seemed to have been held waiting on the twelfth floor for him alone.

Chen Xuan stepped inside, pressed the ground floor, and leaned lightly against the cool steel rail while the doors slid shut. Outside, Qingyan's autumn dusk was settling over the glass towers, and the ordinary office worker closed his eyes for six quiet seconds as the cab descended.
