# Chapter 1: Leave on Time

On the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics, the air conditioner hummed, and the whole floor smelled of instant noodles and tired people.

Chen Xuan looked at the clock on his screen and felt warm inside.

Fifteen minutes.

At 4:55 he would stand up, and at 6:10 the express bus to Yunxi County would pull out of the station.

By eight o'clock he would be at his mother's table, being scolded for looking too thin while she filled his bowl with pork-rib soup.

That was his whole plan for the weekend. He thought it was perfect.

At Hengxin Logistics, Chen Xuan was an operations supervisor, paid 12,000 yuan a month.

Politely, it was a middle job.

Honestly, he was the man who checked everyone's numbers while nobody noticed him.

He never chased bonuses, never went to the boss's drinking dinners, and never stayed late.

His method was simple: finish the day's work before lunch, then drink hot tea all afternoon.

"Chen Xuan! Eyes off that clock!" Sister Pan called from the next cubicle.

"Every Friday, same thing. His body is here, but his soul left at lunch."

"Some people get paid and feel no shame," Zhang Pengfei added without looking up.

"Mm," said Chen Xuan, and drank some tea.

Then the glass door at the end of the floor swung open.

Thirty backs straightened at once.

It always happened when Department Manager Hu Dewei came out of his office.

Hu Dewei was a thin man in his forties. He carried a mug as big as a flowerpot, and he walked like a man with bad news.

He was heading straight for Chen Xuan's desk.

Chen Xuan stood up, because sitting would only make the talk longer.

"Manager."

"Supervisor Chen, do you have a minute?" Hu Dewei said, tugging at his tie.

"Sure."

"It's the port bills."

Hu Dewei lowered his voice, but he did not lower it enough, and every keyboard on the floor stopped clicking.

"Three containers cleared customs this month. Every invoice must be checked against the shipping records. General Manager Sun wants the audit on his desk Monday at nine."

"I know it's Friday. I hate to ask. But somebody has to stay tonight, or I've got nothing to show him."

Nobody breathed.

General Manager Sun Haoran came down to the twelfth floor about twice a year. Both times, somebody cleaned out a desk the next day.

"I'll stay, Manager Hu!"

Zhang Pengfei was on his feet in three seconds.

He wore a pressed suit, arrived first, left last, and wanted a promotion more than sleep.

"This is my nineteenth late night this quarter, and I don't mind a twentieth! A real worker finishes the job. He doesn't run off the second the clock says five."

"Pengfei is the hardest worker on this floor," Sister Pan agreed loudly.

"Thanks, Pengfei. That's good of you," Chen Xuan said mildly. "Manager, the audit is already done."

Silence.

"The file went into the shared folder at 11:20 this morning. I was going to mention it, but you looked busy."

Hu Dewei's mouth opened.

"All three containers?"

"Yes. And the second container has a mistake. The port agent charged us storage fees twice. I marked the line in yellow."

Simply put, a box left at the port costs a fee each day. The agent had charged that fee twice.

Hu Dewei practically ran to the nearest computer.

At the bottom, in neat yellow letters, sat one note: *Overcharged 14,000 yuan for duplicate storage. Request refund.*

"Fourteen thousand," Hu Dewei whispered, and his big flowerpot mug trembled against the desk.

That was more than Chen Xuan's pay for a month. He had found it before lunch and told nobody.

"Eleven-twenty? But he went to eat at noon!"

"At two o'clock I saw him asleep on his arms!"

"He found fourteen thousand yuan while napping?"

Sister Pan, who had praised Zhang Pengfei ten seconds earlier, turned to the girl beside her with a straight face.

"I have always said the quiet ones get things done."

Zhang Pengfei stood in the middle of the room, and his smile slid off his face.

This was not in his script!

For three weeks he had waited for Chen Xuan to slip up even once.

Tonight he was supposed to be the hero who gave up his Friday while everyone watched.

Instead the job had been finished since morning, and Chen Xuan was peeling the paper off a red-bean bun like a man at a picnic!

Did Chen Xuan have someone behind him?

Who finds fourteen thousand yuan and doesn't even send an email to take the credit?

His ears burned red.

He sat down hard and made himself a promise.

Next week he would check every report four times.

Hu Dewei turned around, standing a little too straight, and spoke a little too carefully.

"Supervisor Chen. You can go home, of course. And thank you."

Since March, he had felt odd whenever Chen Xuan walked past.

He couldn't say why, and that scared him more.

"Thanks, Manager. Good weekend. Night, everyone."

At 4:55 sharp, Chen Xuan put the bun into his canvas bag, picked up his scratched green thermos and walked out while thirty pairs of eyes followed his back.

In the corridor, the elevator was already waiting with its doors open.

In three years that had never happened on a Friday.

"Lucky," Chen Xuan thought, and stepped inside.

* * *

The waiting hall of Qingyan Express Bus Station smelled of fried dough and diesel.

Chen Xuan bought his sixty-eight-yuan ticket and took his usual spot at Gate Six, behind a pillar, out of the draft.

Then his phone buzzed with a voice message from his mother.

Liu Meifang had the loudest voice in the family, and this time it was shaking.

"Xuan, listen and don't panic! The police took your Second Uncle away this afternoon! At two o'clock, out of his own shop, in handcuffs, in front of the whole street! Your father ran to the station and they wouldn't even let him in! Your Second Aunt has been on our sofa since three and hasn't touched a drop of water! They say he beat somebody up! Beat somebody up! His hands shake when he scales a fish!"

Her voice broke.

"Come home as fast as you can!"

Chen Xuan stood very still, and he played it a second time.

His Second Uncle, Chen Mingde, had run a small hardware shop on Yunxi's Old Street for over twenty years.

He was soft-spoken, and he apologized to chairs when he bumped them.

When Chen Xuan was seven, this uncle had put a fishing rod in his hand for the first time, then sat beside him for six hours without once complaining that nothing was biting.

Now people were saying that man had beaten someone in broad daylight.

Chen Xuan's throat tightened.

Something cold and steady settled in his chest. It felt heavier than anger.

His family teased him that his calm was just laziness.

It was not.

It was his grandfather's rule: listen to everyone once, then decide. Never explain yourself afterward.

He typed with his thumb: *I'm on the 6:10 bus. Don't worry.*

His mother skipped meals whenever she was frightened, so he added one more line: *Mom, eat first.*

Soon the gate clicked open, and he took a window seat on the right.

A plain middle-aged man in a faded gray jacket boarded just before the doors hissed shut.

He carried a small dark canvas bag, sat three rows back, and closed his eyes like any tired worker.

Chen Xuan ate his bun and watched the lights of Qingyan slide past, thinking about his uncle's hands.

Three rows behind him, the gray jacket opened one sharp eye and tapped three words into a phone.

Somewhere ahead of the bus, inside a windowless van painted like a flower-delivery truck, a young captain read them off his screen.

*The Boss is on the bus.*

Above his head hung a wall map, and on it glowed two hundred and fourteen green dots.

They sat in vans, rooftops, a bakery and a quiet airfield, all inside a circle one hundred kilometers wide, and all of them were waiting for one man.

That man was eating a red-bean bun.

"Any order from the Boss?" the captain's deputy asked.

"None."

"His uncle was arrested four hours ago, Captain. If he says one word,"

"Then nothing in this province will stand still by morning," the captain said quietly. He did not look away from the green dots.

"So pray he never gets angry."
