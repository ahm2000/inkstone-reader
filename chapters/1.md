# Chapter 1: Leave on Time

Chen Xuan looked at the clock in the corner of his monitor at Hengxin Logistics and smiled.

Fifteen more minutes. Then he could pack up without anyone staring, and the 6:10 express bus to Yunxi County would be waiting. If he caught it, he would sit at his mother's table before eight, with a bowl of pork-rib soup in front of him.

That was his whole plan for the weekend. He thought it was a very good plan.

At Hengxin Logistics, Chen Xuan was an operations supervisor, paid 12,000 yuan a month. Politely speaking, that was a middle post. Honestly speaking, he was the man who checked other people's numbers while nobody noticed him. The pay was enough for a small rented room in Qingyan and some money for his parents, and he asked nothing more of it. He never chased bonuses, never went to the boss's drinking dinners, never stayed late. His method was simple: finish all the work before lunch, then drink hot tea all afternoon.

"Supervisor Chen, do you have a minute?"

A nervous voice pulled his eyes off the clock.

Department Manager Hu Dewei was standing beside his desk. He held a mug as big as a flowerpot, and his other hand kept tugging at his tie. He always looked like a man who had just heard bad news.

"Sure, Manager. What is it?"

"The port bills!" Hu Dewei coughed and looked away. "Three containers cleared customs this month, and every invoice has to be checked against the shipping records. General Manager Sun wants the audit on his desk Monday at nine sharp. I know it is Friday, and I hate to ask, but somebody must stay tonight and do it. Otherwise I have nothing to hand him!"

The whole office went quiet.

Everybody stared at their keyboards.

Nobody wanted to be picked.

"I can stay, Manager!"

Before three seconds had passed, Zhang Pengfei was on his feet. He wore a crisp suit every day, arrived first, left last, and wanted the next promotion more than he wanted sleep.

"I have already worked nineteen late nights this quarter! One more Friday is nothing! I never leave my work half done, not like some people who vanish the second the clock hits five!"

On the words "some people," his chin pointed straight at Chen Xuan.

"Pengfei really is the hardest worker on this floor!" Sister Pan called out from the next cubicle. She was the oldest clerk in the department, with sharp eyes and a loud opinion on everyone's business.

Chen Xuan did not look bothered. He nudged his mouse and spoke in an easy voice.

"Manager, the audit is finished. The file went into the shared folder at 11:20 this morning."

Silence.

"Finished?" Hu Dewei blinked twice. "All three containers?"

"Yes," Chen Xuan said calmly. "And the second container has an error. The port agent charged us twice for storage. I marked the line in yellow."

The reason that mattered was simple. When a container sits at the port past its free days, the port charges a fee for every extra day. The agent had billed that fee two times for the same container, and a person reading fast would never see it.

Hu Dewei hurried to the nearest computer and opened the folder.

He scrolled down, and the doubt on his face turned into shock.

Every invoice matched, line by line. At the bottom, in neat yellow, sat one note: *Overcharged 14,000 yuan for duplicate storage. Please request a refund.*

Fourteen thousand yuan!

That was more than Chen Xuan's whole month of pay, saved before lunch, and he had not said one word about it.

"Eleven-twenty?"

"But he went down to eat at noon!"

"At two o'clock he was asleep on his arms!"

"And he found fourteen thousand?"

Whispers ran across the floor. Sister Pan, who ten minutes earlier had praised Zhang Pengfei, turned to the girl beside her. "I always say the quiet ones get things done," she said, loud enough for the whole row, as if she had said it all along.

Zhang Pengfei stood in the middle of the room with his mouth half open.

Not in his script!

For three weeks he had waited for Chen Xuan to make one careless mistake. Tonight he was supposed to be the hero who gave up his Friday while everyone watched. Now the job had been done since morning, and Chen Xuan was sitting there peeling the paper off a red-bean bun!

Was it a trick? Did he have someone behind him? Why would a man find fourteen thousand yuan and not even send an email to take the credit?

His face burned red. He sank into his chair and clenched his jaw. Next week, he swore, he would check every report four times!

"Well, then, Supervisor Chen, you may go home," Hu Dewei said. His voice sounded dry.

Ever since March, Hu Dewei had felt nervous whenever Chen Xuan stood near him, and he could not say why. Chen Xuan never shouted and never acted important. So why did Hu Dewei want to straighten his back every time he walked past?

He had no answer.

Not knowing only made his palms sweat.

"Thank you, Manager. Have a good weekend."

At 4:55 sharp, Chen Xuan put the red-bean bun in his canvas bag, picked up his scratched green thermos and walked out while the whole floor watched his back.

In the hallway, the elevator was already waiting on the twelfth floor.

Its doors were open.

In three years in this building, that had never happened on a Friday afternoon.

"Lucky," Chen Xuan thought, and stepped inside.

---

The waiting hall at Qingyan Express Bus Station was packed and loud, and it smelled of fried dough and diesel.

Chen Xuan bought a 68-yuan ticket and took his usual spot at Gate Six, behind a pillar, out of the draft. He had stood in this spot almost every Friday for three years.

Then his phone buzzed.

It was a voice message from his mother, Liu Meifang. She had the loudest voice in the family, and even when she cried, she did not turn it down.

"Xuan, listen, don't panic! The police took your Second Uncle away this afternoon! At two o'clock, out of his own shop, in handcuffs, in front of the whole street! Your father ran to the station and they would not let him in! Your Second Aunt has been on our sofa since three and has not drunk a drop of water! They say he beat somebody up. How could that be true?! His hands shake when he scales a fish! Come home as fast as you can!"

Chen Xuan stood still and played it a second time.

"Hmm?"

His Second Uncle, Chen Mingde, was his father's younger brother, a soft-spoken man who had run a small hardware shop on Yunxi's Old Street for over twenty years. When Chen Xuan was seven, Second Uncle had taught him how to fish.

A man like that, beating someone up in broad daylight?

Chen Xuan did not flare up. A quiet, steady weight settled in his chest instead. He wanted to hear his uncle's side before anyone else wrote the story for him.

He typed with his thumb: *I'm on the 6:10 bus. Don't worry. Please eat dinner first.*

His mother skipped meals whenever she worried, so he added one more line: *Mom, eat first.*

His family joked that his calm was laziness. To be honest, it was a rule from his grandfather: when something goes wrong, ask once, politely. If the answer is not right, ask again, politely.

Soon.

The gate clicked open, and he took a window seat on the right.

A plain middle-aged man in a faded gray jacket boarded just before the doors hissed shut. He held a small dark canvas bag on his lap, sat three rows behind Chen Xuan and closed his eyes at once, like any tired worker going home for the weekend.

Chen Xuan did not look back.

He ate his bun and watched the lights of Qingyan City slide past the window.

Three rows behind him, the man in the gray jacket opened one sharp eye.

Inside the half-open canvas bag, a slim black phone lit up with a single line of green text. The man read it, tapped three quick words and covered the bag again.

Who was he writing to? And why would a tired worker need to report where one quiet clerk was sitting?
