# Chapter 1: Leave on Time

Qingyan City, Hengxin Logistics Group, Operations Department.

Friday, 4:40 p.m.

Chen Xuan checked the clock in the corner of his monitor and smiled to himself.

Only fifteen minutes left before he could pack up without drawing stares, and fifty minutes until the 6:10 express bus left for Yunxi County. If he caught it, he'd be sitting at his mother's table before eight with a steaming bowl of pork-rib soup in front of him.

To be honest, that was his entire ambition for the weekend, and he thought it was a pretty good one.

At Hengxin Logistics, Chen Xuan was a mid-level operations supervisor making 12,000 yuan a month. In a provincial capital like Qingyan, that was comfortable ordinary pay, enough to rent a small place near work, eat well, and send a little money home without anyone getting jealous. He never fought for bonuses, never tagged along to the boss's drinking dinners, and never stayed a single minute late. His secret was simple: finish everything before lunch, then drink hot tea and rest all afternoon.

"Supervisor Chen, do you have a minute?"

A nervous voice pulled his eyes off the clock.

Department Manager Hu Dewei stood beside his desk, clutching a ceramic mug the size of a flowerpot while his other hand tugged at his tie. He always looked like a man who had just heard bad news.

"Sure, Manager. What's up?"

"It's the port bills." Hu Dewei coughed awkwardly and looked away. "Three cargo containers cleared customs this month, and every invoice has to be checked against the shipping records. General Manager Sun wants the audit on his desk Monday at nine sharp. I know it's Friday, and I hate to ask, but somebody has to stay late tonight."

The whole office went dead quiet!

Everyone stared down at their keyboards, praying the manager wouldn't look their way.

"I can stay, Manager!"

Before three seconds passed, Zhang Pengfei shot up from his chair. Always dressed in a crisp suit, he was the first man in every morning and the last out every night, hungry for the next promotion. "I've already worked nineteen late nights this quarter! One more Friday means nothing to me. I never leave work half-done, not like some people who vanish the second the clock hits five."

He tilted his chin straight toward Chen Xuan when he said "some people."

"Pengfei really is the hardest worker on this floor!" Sister Pan chimed in loudly from the next cubicle. She was the oldest clerk in the department, with sharp eyes and a loud opinion about everybody's business.

Several younger clerks traded uneasy glances. Everybody knew Zhang Pengfei had been trying to make Chen Xuan look lazy for weeks.

Chen Xuan didn't look bothered at all. He reached out, nudged his mouse, and spoke in an easy, mild voice.

"Wait a second, Manager. That audit is already finished. I put the file in the shared folder at 11:20 this morning."

Silence.

"Finished?" Hu Dewei blinked twice, wondering if he'd heard wrong. "All three containers?"

"Mm," Chen Xuan said, nodding lightly. "And I caught a mistake on the second container. The port agent billed us twice for storage. I marked the line in yellow."

Hu Dewei hurried to the nearest computer and clicked into the folder. As he scrolled down the columns, the doubt on his face turned into surprise, and then straight into shock!

Every invoice matched to the decimal. At the bottom, marked in neat yellow, sat a calm note: *Overcharged 14,000 yuan for duplicate storage. Please request a refund.*

Fourteen thousand yuan, more than an entire month of Chen Xuan's own salary, saved without a single word of bragging!

"Eleven-twenty?" Sister Pan blurted out, her jaw dropping. "He finished all three containers before lunch?! But he went down to eat at noon, and at two o'clock he was napping with his head on his arms!"

"He checked all three containers in one morning?"

"And caught a fourteen-thousand-yuan error?!"

"Supervisor Chen works way too fast!"

"Wait, why didn't he even tell Manager Hu he saved fourteen thousand yuan?"

Whispers rippled across the whole floor.

In the middle of the room, Zhang Pengfei stood frozen with his mouth half open, feeling as if someone had slapped him right in front of the entire department!

For three weeks he'd waited for Chen Xuan to make just one careless mistake so everyone could see who truly deserved the promotion. And now? While Zhang Pengfei was gearing up to sacrifice his Friday night like a hero, the job had been done since morning, and Chen Xuan was sitting right there, calmly peeling the wax paper off a red-bean bun!

Was it a trick? Did he copy someone's spreadsheet, or did he have a secret contact at the port? And why on earth would a man save the company fourteen thousand yuan and not even send an email to take credit?

Zhang Pengfei's face burned bright red. Humiliated and angry, he sank back into his chair and clenched his jaw. Next week, he swore to himself, he'd check every report three times, no, four times!

"Well, then, Supervisor Chen, you can head home," Hu Dewei said, his voice oddly dry.

Ever since March, Hu Dewei had felt a strange nervousness whenever Chen Xuan stood nearby, and he couldn't explain why. Chen Xuan never shouted, never broke a rule, and never acted like a big shot. So why did Hu Dewei always feel an urge to straighten his back the moment Chen Xuan walked past?

He had no answer, and not knowing only made his palms sweat more.

"Thank you, Manager. Have a good weekend."

Chen Xuan tucked the red-bean bun into his canvas bag, picked up his scratched old green thermos, and walked out at 4:55 sharp while the whole floor watched his back.

In the hallway, the elevator was already waiting on the twelfth floor, its doors wide open.

In three years of working in this building, that had never happened once on a Friday afternoon.

"Lucky," Chen Xuan thought happily, and stepped inside.

Qingyan Express Bus Station, 5:50 p.m.

The waiting hall was packed and noisy, smelling of fried dough, tea eggs, and diesel fumes, while a loudspeaker called out county names he'd never visited.

Chen Xuan bought a 68-yuan ticket for the 6:10 bus to Yunxi County and took his usual spot at Gate Six, tucked behind a pillar out of the draft. Almost every Friday for three years, he'd stood right here.

Suddenly, his phone buzzed.

It was a voice message from his mother, Liu Meifang. She had the loudest voice in the family, and even when she was crying, she didn't turn the volume down a single notch.

"Xuan, listen, don't panic! The police took your Second Uncle away this afternoon! At two o'clock, straight out of his own shop, in handcuffs, in front of the whole street! Your father ran to the police station and they wouldn't even let him inside! Your Second Aunt has been sitting on our sofa since three and hasn't drunk a drop of water! They claim he beat somebody up. How could that be true?! Your Second Uncle's hands shake when he scales a fish! Come home as fast as you can!"

Chen Xuan stood still by the pillar and played the message a second time.

"Hmm?"

His Second Uncle, Chen Mingde, was his father's younger brother, a soft-spoken man who'd run a tiny hardware shop on Yunxi's Old Street for over twenty years. When Chen Xuan was seven, Second Uncle had taught him how to fish. Every New Year, even in lean winters when the shop barely paid rent, Second Uncle still pressed a crisp red envelope into his hand.

A man like that, beating someone up in broad daylight? And the police hauling him off in handcuffs from his own counter?

Chen Xuan didn't flare up in anger. Instead, a quiet, steady weight settled in his chest, like seeing someone you love caught in the rain and knowing you have to bring them an umbrella.

He typed with his thumb: *I'm on the 6:10 bus. Don't worry. Please eat dinner first.*

Remembering how his mother skipped meals whenever she worried, he added one more line: *Mom, eat first.*

His family always joked that his calm temper was laziness. To be honest, it wasn't laziness at all. It was just a simple rule his grandfather had taught him long ago: when something goes wrong, ask once, politely. If the answer isn't right, ask again, politely.

The gate clicked open. Chen Xuan climbed aboard and took a window seat on the right.

A minute later, a plain-looking middle-aged man in a faded gray jacket boarded right before the doors hissed shut. Carrying a small dark canvas bag on his lap, he sat three rows behind Chen Xuan and closed his eyes at once, like any tired worker heading home for the weekend.

Chen Xuan didn't glance back once. He ate his red-bean bun and watched the lights of Qingyan City drift past the window, one by one.

Three rows behind him, the man in the gray jacket cracked one sharp eye open. Inside his half-zipped canvas bag, a slim black phone lit up silently with a single line of green text. He glanced down, tapped three quick words on the screen, and slid his hand smoothly back over the bag.
