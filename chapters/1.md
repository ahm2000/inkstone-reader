# Chapter 1: Leave on Time

At four forty-seven on a Friday afternoon, the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics in Qingyan had stopped pretending to work. Everyone watched the clock.

Chen Xuan sat at desk fourteen in a clean gray jacket and dark trousers, his scratched green thermos of jasmine tea and half of a cold red-bean bun beside his mouse pad. On paper, he was a junior operations supervisor earning twelve thousand yuan a month before tax. For three years he had arrived at eight fifty-five, answered spreadsheet inquiries quietly, and packed up at four fifty-five.

Two desks over, Pan Xiulan snapped her ledger shut. "Eight minutes," she muttered. "If I clear the elevator before five, I can catch the five-thirty bus on Binjiang Avenue."

Across the aisle, Zheng Qian groaned. "Sister Pan, you say that every Friday, and something always drops at four fifty. Do not jinx the row."

Pan Xiulan glared tiredly, though her mouth twitched. "Blame the port desk. Look at Chen Xuan, his screen is already clean."

Zheng Qian grinned at desk fourteen. "Because he never chats and never misses a decimal. Taking that half bun home again?"

Chen Xuan looked up mildly. "It is still good," he said evenly. "Wasting food is a bad habit."

Before Zheng Qian could answer, the corner office door banged open. Department Manager Hu Dewei marched out, face flushed dark red, clutching a thick red folder. Every smile vanished. Zheng Qian snapped upright and tapped dead keys. Pan Xiulan froze with one hand in her drawer.

Hu Dewei slammed the folder onto an empty desk. "Nobody shuts down a computer," he barked. "General Manager Sun Haoran just returned from the port authority meeting. Three refrigerated containers from the southeast terminal have mismatched customs weights, and the client wants a reconciled audit before midnight. Everyone stays until it is cleared."

Pan Xiulan's shoulders dropped, her fingers slipping off her purse strap. Zheng Qian stared blankly at his monitor, his jaw working in silent frustration.

Only Zhang Pengfei rose promptly from the front row, smoothing his striped tie with an eager smile. "Manager Hu, do not stress yourself," Zhang Pengfei said loudly. "General Manager Sun Haoran trusted our department for a reason. I already cancelled dinner. If everyone pulls together, we can trace the logs in three or four hours."

Hu Dewei's stiff shoulders eased slightly. He nodded at Zhang Pengfei, then swept a hard stare across the desks. "At least someone here understands responsibility." His eyes locked onto desk fourteen.

Chen Xuan had not touched his keyboard. He was screwing the cap onto his green thermos and tucking the red-bean bun into his canvas bag.

Hu Dewei's face darkened. He strode down the aisle and planted a palm on the partition. "Chen Xuan. Did you hear me?"

Chen Xuan zipped the side pocket with unhurried care and met Hu Dewei's glare, his dark eyes flat and calm. "Manager Hu, I heard you clearly," he said. "I am leaving at four fifty-five."

Every keyboard stopped dead. Zheng Qian's hand froze on his mouse, eyes wide. Pan Xiulan leaned behind her monitor and shot Chen Xuan a desperate warning look.

Zhang Pengfei stepped behind Hu Dewei with a sharp laugh. "Chen Xuan, have you lost your mind? Manager Hu is carrying pressure from General Manager Sun Haoran, and the rest of us are giving up Friday night for a port emergency. Who do you think you are to walk out on the clock?"

"Stay out of it, Zhang Pengfei," Chen Xuan said without raising his voice. "This has nothing to do with overtime."

"Then what is it about?" Hu Dewei snapped, his jaw clenching until a vein stood out along his collar. He jabbed a finger at Chen Xuan. "You have coasted at twelve thousand a month for three years because I had a soft heart. Walk out at four fifty-five and do not bother returning Monday. I will have personnel transfer you to the north-suburb cold warehouse to count pallets until your fingers freeze."

Pan Xiulan drew a sharp breath, gripping her desk. Zheng Qian winced and looked at his lap. Zhang Pengfei folded his arms, his smug smile widening.

Chen Xuan did not blink. He tapped his spacebar with two fingers to wake the screen and turned the monitor toward Hu Dewei.

"The three refrigerated containers from the southeast terminal," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Containers HXL-409, HXL-412, and HXL-418. The reconciled weight audit was uploaded to the shared folder at eleven twenty this morning. I copied General Manager Sun Haoran's assistant before lunch."

Hu Dewei's raised finger stopped in midair. His brow knotted. "What did you say?"

"The mismatch was a clerical carry-over from Tuesday's barge transfer," Chen Xuan continued, neither smug nor rushed. "While reconciling the invoices, I also flagged a fourteen-thousand-yuan double-storage overcharge billed to the company on Wednesday afternoon. The approval stamp on the duplicate voucher carries the initials Z.P."

Color drained from Zhang Pengfei's face in a single heartbeat. His arms dropped stiffly, his smile collapsing into a twitching grimace. "That is nonsense!" Zhang Pengfei blurted out, voice cracking high. "Manager Hu, the terminal clerk must have misfiled the sheet! I never signed off on double storage!"

"The scanned voucher with its timestamp is on page four," Chen Xuan said evenly, clicking his monitor off. "General Manager Sun Haoran's assistant replied at two fifteen confirming the port authority accepted the correction. The only open item left in that red folder is the fourteen-thousand-yuan discrepancy under Zhang Pengfei's initials."

Zheng Qian's mouth fell open as he stared at Zhang Pengfei's sweating forehead. Pan Xiulan exhaled slowly, her eyes bright with stunned respect.

Hu Dewei stared at the blank screen, then jerked his eyes to the red folder. With stiff, fumbling fingers he flipped open the fastener and read the cover memo he had never checked before storming out. His crimson cheeks turned a blotchy, humiliated purple.

"Manager Hu, I can explain," Zhang Pengfei stammered with a forced laugh. "It was a rushed afternoon, and the customs broker swore the second invoice was for cold storage."

"Shut up!" Hu Dewei roared, snapping the folder shut. His eyes burned as he wheeled on Zhang Pengfei. "Everyone else, go home! Zhang Pengfei, get into my office right now and explain why my signature sits above fourteen thousand yuan of ghost storage fees!"

Zhang Pengfei flinched and hurried behind Hu Dewei. The corner office door slammed shut, muffling the start of Hu Dewei's shouting.

A collective exhale swept the cubicles. Zheng Qian gave Chen Xuan a wide-eyed thumbs-up beneath his desk. Pan Xiulan gathered her purse and leaned over with a grateful whisper.

"You just saved us four hours," she said. "Will you be alright Monday? Manager Hu hates losing face."

Chen Xuan picked up his green thermos and slung his canvas bag over his shoulder. "He has bigger numbers to explain to General Manager Sun Haoran on Monday," he replied calmly. "He will not have time for desk fourteen."

The wall clock clicked onto four fifty-five.

"Have a good weekend, Sister Pan," Chen Xuan said politely. "Have a good weekend, Zheng Qian."

He walked unhurriedly into the elevator lobby. On a Friday at five to five, Hengxin Tower's three elevators were always jammed upstairs, yet Chen Xuan never reached the call button. As he crossed the threshold, the central elevator chimed softly, its doors sliding open onto an empty, spotless cab waiting on the twelfth floor for him alone.

Chen Xuan stepped inside, pressed the ground floor, and closed his eyes for six quiet seconds while the cab descended.
