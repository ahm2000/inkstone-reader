# Chapter 1: Leave on Time

On the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics, the air conditioner hummed. The whole floor smelled of instant noodles and tired people.

Chen Xuan watched the clock on his screen and felt happy.

He had only fifteen minutes left.

At 4:55 he would stand up. At 6:10 the express bus to Yunxi County would leave. By eight he would sit at his mother's table. She would say he was too thin and fill his bowl with pork-rib soup.

That was his whole weekend plan, and it was perfect.

He was an operations supervisor, paid 12,000 yuan a month.

That sounded important. It was not. A supervisor was one step above a clerk, with nobody to boss. The manager wrote his yearly review, and the review decided his bonus.

His method was simple. He finished all his work before lunch. Then he drank hot tea all afternoon.

"Chen Xuan! Stop looking at the clock!" Pan Xiulan, the coworker everyone called Sister Pan, leaned over from the next desk. "His body is here. His mind went home at lunch."

Zhang Pengfei, a coworker in a pressed suit, did not look up. "Some people get paid and feel no shame."

Chen Xuan drank some tea. "My mind comes back every morning, Sister Pan. It just needs a cup of tea first."

Bang!

The glass door at the end of the floor flew open, and thirty backs went straight.

Hu Dewei, the department manager, walked out with a huge mug in one hand and a red folder in the other. His face was gray. Everyone knew why. Sun Haoran, General Manager of Hengxin Logistics, had shouted in his office all afternoon. Now the anger needed somewhere to go.

"Listen up!" Hu Dewei slapped the folder on the nearest desk. "The port bills. Three containers, every invoice checked against the shipping records. General Manager Sun wants the audit on his desk Monday at nine. So nobody leaves tonight until it is done."

A groan rolled across the floor.

"Anyone who leaves early loses the Friday bonus, three hundred yuan, and I'll write it in your review. Phones in drawers. Questions?"

Nobody had questions.

Hu Dewei's eyes moved across the room and stopped.

"Chen Xuan."

Chen Xuan stood up. "Manager."

"Oh, you must be so tired from watching the clock. Every Friday at five, you disappear. Supervisor." He said the word like a joke. "A clerk with a nicer card. The transfer to the north warehouse needs only my signature. I could sign it Monday, and nobody would ask why."

"I'll stay, Manager Hu!" Zhang Pengfei jumped up and gave a quick little bow. "This is my nineteenth late night this quarter. A real worker finishes the job. He doesn't run away when the clock says five."

Hu Dewei nodded at Zhang Pengfei. "You see that, Chen Xuan? Learn from Pengfei. That's attitude."

"Hardest worker on this floor," Pan Xiulan agreed, loud enough for the manager. Then she whispered to Zheng Qian, the young clerk beside her. "Look at Chen Xuan's face. He is not worried at all."

Chen Xuan did not look at any of them. He looked at the red folder.

"Manager, the audit is done."

The whole floor went dead silent.

"It went into the shared folder at 11:20 this morning."

"Don't joke with me, Chen. Not today."

"I don't joke before five, Manager. I save it for after."

"Open the folder."

Hu Dewei's jaw went tight. He walked fast to the nearest computer and clicked. He scrolled. His mouth fell open.

Every invoice matched, line by line. At the bottom, in yellow, sat one note: *Port charged storage twice on container two. Overcharged 14,000 yuan. Request refund.*

The port charged a fee for every extra day a box sat there. The agent had billed the same fee two times.

"Fourteen thousand." Hu Dewei said it in a whisper. His big mug shook in his hand.

That was more than Chen Xuan earned in a month.

Pan Xiulan turned to Zheng Qian with a straight face. "I always said the quiet ones get things done."

Hu Dewei's ears went red. He had just shouted at this man in front of thirty people.

"Why didn't you report it?" He said it too loudly. "Hiding results from your manager is a serious problem."

"I put it in the folder. You didn't ask. You looked so busy."

Someone snorted and turned it into a cough.

Zhang Pengfei froze in the middle of the floor.

For three weeks he had waited for Chen Xuan to make a mistake. Tonight he was supposed to be the hero who gave up his Friday. Instead the job had been done since morning, and Chen Xuan was peeling a red-bean bun like a man at a picnic.

Hu Dewei coughed. "Everyone goes home!" he told the room. "Except Pengfei. The audit is done, but someone should check it again. Since you love staying so much."

Zhang Pengfei opened his mouth, shut it, and sat down.

"And Chen." Hu Dewei tried to find his old angry voice, but it came out thin. "Don't think this saves you. We will talk Monday about your attitude."

Since March a rumor had gone around the floor: a black car had waited outside twice for Chen Xuan. Hu Dewei did not believe it. But he felt strange whenever Chen Xuan walked past, and that scared him more than any shouting could fix.

"Sure, Manager. Have a good weekend." Chen Xuan gave a half smile. "Night, Pengfei. Don't work too hard."

At 4:55 sharp Chen Xuan put the bun in his canvas bag and picked up his scratched green thermos. Then he walked out. Thirty pairs of eyes followed him.

In the hall, the elevator was already waiting with its doors open. In three years that had never happened on a Friday.

"Lucky day." Chen Xuan stepped inside.

* * *

The bus station smelled of fried dough and diesel.

Chen Xuan bought a thirty-two-yuan ticket and stood at Gate Six, out of the wind.

Then his phone buzzed with a voice message from his mother.

Liu Meifang, his mother, had the loudest voice in the family. This time it was shaking.

"Xuan, listen, and don't panic! The police took your Second Uncle away this afternoon! At two o'clock! Out of his own shop! In handcuffs, in front of the whole street! Your father ran to the station and they would not let him in! They say he beat somebody up! Your uncle would not hurt a fly!"

Her voice broke.

"Come home as fast as you can!"

Chen Xuan's heart dropped. The phone almost slid out of his cold fingers.

He played it again.

His Second Uncle, Chen Mingde, had run a small hardware shop on Yunxi's Old Street for over twenty years. He said sorry to chairs when he bumped them. When Chen Xuan was seven, this uncle had sat beside him for six hours with a fishing rod and never once said that nothing was biting.

Now people said that man had beaten someone in the middle of the day.

His throat tightened and his eyes went hot. Under the fear, something cold and steady settled in his chest. It felt heavier than anger.

He typed slowly with his thumb, because his hand was not steady. *Mom, I'm coming. I'm on the 6:10 bus. Please don't cry. Eat something. I'll be there soon.*

On the bus he took a window seat on the right. A plain middle-aged man in a faded gray jacket got on last, sat three rows back, and closed his eyes like any tired worker.

Chen Xuan ate his bun and watched the city lights slide past. He thought about his uncle.

Three rows behind him, the gray jacket opened one sharp eye. The man was Mo Lin, an operative of the Black Pine Guard. He tapped three words into a phone.

Somewhere ahead, inside a windowless van painted like a flower-delivery truck, Captain Tang Liqun read them off his screen. *The Boss is on the bus.*

The Boss was Chen Xuan. The Black Pine Guard was a secret guard that watched over him day and night. The van, the gray jacket, even the open elevator at work belonged to them.

Above the captain hung a wall map glowing with two hundred and fourteen green dots. Each dot was one of their people, in vans, on rooftops, in a bakery, on a quiet airfield. Chen Xuan knew a few of them and had no idea how many there were. They moved only when he gave an order. He had given none.

Every one of them was waiting for one man, and that man was eating a red-bean bun.

"Any order from the Boss?" his deputy asked.

"None."

"His uncle was arrested four hours ago, Captain. If he says one word, what happens?"

"Then every dot on this map moves, and nothing in this province sleeps tonight." Tang Liqun did not look away.

"So pray he stays calm."
