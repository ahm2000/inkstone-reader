# Chapter 1: Leave on Time

At 4:47 on a Friday afternoon, the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai had stopped pretending to work. Everyone was watching the clock.

Chen Xuan sat at desk fourteen in a clean gray jacket and dark trousers. Beside his mouse pad sat a scratched green thermos of jasmine tea and half of a cold red-bean bun. On paper, he was a junior operations supervisor earning twelve thousand yuan a month before tax. For three years, he had arrived at 8:55, answered spreadsheet questions quietly, and packed up at 4:55.

Two desks over, Pan Xiulan snapped her notebook shut. "Eight minutes," she muttered. "If I catch the elevator before five, I can make the 5:30 bus on Binjiang Avenue."

Across the aisle, Zheng Qian groaned. "Sister Pan, you say that every Friday, and something always drops at 4:50. Do not jinx our row."

Pan Xiulan gave him a tired glare, though the corner of her mouth twitched. "Blame the port desk. Look at Chen Xuan. His screen is already clean."

Zheng Qian grinned toward desk fourteen. "That is because he never chats and never misses a number. Are you taking that half bun home again?"

Chen Xuan looked up mildly. "It is still good," he said in an even voice. "Wasting food is a bad habit."

Before Zheng Qian could reply, the corner office door banged open. Department Manager Hu Dewei marched out, his face dark red, clutching a thick red folder. Every smile disappeared at once. Zheng Qian sat straight up and pretended to type. Pan Xiulan froze with one hand inside her desk drawer.

Hu Dewei slammed the folder onto an empty desk. "Nobody turns off their computer," he barked. "General Manager Sun Haoran just came back from the port meeting. Three cold containers from the southeast dock have the wrong customs weights. The client wants a full checked report before midnight. Everyone stays until it is done."

Pan Xiulan's shoulders sagged, and her fingers slipped off her purse strap. Zheng Qian stared blankly at his screen, his jaw tight with quiet anger.

Only Zhang Pengfei stood up right away from the front row. He smoothed his striped tie and gave an eager smile. "Manager Hu, do not worry yourself," Zhang Pengfei said loudly. "General Manager Sun Haoran trusted our team for a reason. I already canceled my dinner plans. If everyone works together, we can check the files in three or four hours."

Hu Dewei's stiff shoulders relaxed a little. He nodded at Zhang Pengfei, then swept a hard stare across the room. "At least someone here knows what duty means." His eyes locked onto desk fourteen.

Chen Xuan had not touched his keyboard. He was screwing the cap onto his green thermos and putting the red-bean bun into his canvas bag.

Hu Dewei's face turned dark. He walked quickly down the aisle and slapped his palm onto the desk wall. "Chen Xuan. Did you hear me?"

Chen Xuan zipped the side pocket of his bag without rushing. He met Hu Dewei's glare with flat, calm dark eyes. "Manager Hu, I heard you clearly," he said. "I am leaving at 4:55."

Every keyboard in the room went dead silent. Zheng Qian's hand froze on his mouse, his eyes wide. Pan Xiulan leaned behind her monitor and shot Chen Xuan a desperate warning look.

Zhang Pengfei stepped up behind Hu Dewei with a sharp laugh. "Chen Xuan, have you lost your mind? Manager Hu is taking heat from General Manager Sun Haoran, and the rest of us are giving up Friday night for a port problem. Who do you think you are to walk out right on the clock?"

"Stay out of it, Zhang Pengfei," Chen Xuan said without raising his voice. "This has nothing to do with overtime."

"Then what is it about?" Hu Dewei snapped. His jaw clenched so hard a vein stood out above his collar, and he jabbed a finger at Chen Xuan. "You have coasted on twelve thousand a month for three years because I was too soft on you. Walk out at 4:55 and do not bother coming back Monday. I will have human resources move you to the north-suburb cold warehouse. You can count boxes until your fingers freeze."

Pan Xiulan drew a sharp breath and gripped the edge of her desk. Zheng Qian winced and looked down at his lap. Zhang Pengfei folded his arms, his smug smile growing wider.

Chen Xuan did not blink. He tapped his spacebar with two fingers to wake the screen, then turned the monitor toward Hu Dewei.

"The three cold containers from the southeast dock," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Containers HXL-409, HXL-412, and HXL-418. I uploaded the fixed weight report to the shared folder at 11:20 this morning. I also sent a copy to General Manager Sun Haoran's assistant before lunch."

Hu Dewei's raised finger stopped in midair. His brows knitted together. "What did you say?"

"The wrong weight was a simple copying mistake from Tuesday's boat transfer," Chen Xuan went on, neither smug nor rushed. "While I was checking the bills, I also spotted a fourteen-thousand-yuan extra storage fee charged to the company on Wednesday afternoon. The approval stamp on that second bill has the initials Z.P."

All color drained from Zhang Pengfei's face in a single heartbeat. His arms dropped stiffly to his sides, and his smile twisted into a nervous grimace. "That is nonsense!" Zhang Pengfei blurted out, his voice cracking high. "Manager Hu, the dock clerk must have mixed up the papers! I never approved double storage!"

"The scanned bill with the time stamp is on page four," Chen Xuan said evenly, clicking his monitor off. "General Manager Sun Haoran's assistant replied at 2:15 to say the port accepted the fix. The only open problem left in that red folder is the fourteen-thousand-yuan extra charge under Zhang Pengfei's initials."

Zheng Qian's mouth fell open as he stared at Zhang Pengfei's sweating forehead. Pan Xiulan let out a slow breath, her eyes bright with stunned respect.

Hu Dewei stared at the blank screen, then jerked his eyes down to the red folder. With stiff, fumbling fingers, he opened the clip and read the front note he had never checked before storming out. His red cheeks turned a blotchy, embarrassed purple.

"Manager Hu, I can explain," Zhang Pengfei stammered with a forced laugh. "It was a busy afternoon, and the shipping agent swore the second bill was for cold storage."

"Shut up!" Hu Dewei roared, snapping the folder shut. His eyes burned as he spun around to face Zhang Pengfei. "Everyone else, go home! Zhang Pengfei, get into my office right now and explain why my name sits above fourteen thousand yuan of fake storage fees!"

Zhang Pengfei flinched and hurried after Hu Dewei. The corner office door slammed shut, muting the start of Hu Dewei's shouting.

A quiet wave of relief swept across the desks. Zheng Qian gave Chen Xuan a wide-eyed thumbs-up under his desk. Pan Xiulan picked up her purse and leaned over with a grateful whisper.

"You just saved us four hours," she said. "Will you be alright on Monday? Manager Hu hates losing face."

Chen Xuan picked up his green thermos and slung his canvas bag over his shoulder. "He has bigger numbers to explain to General Manager Sun Haoran on Monday," he replied calmly. "He will not have time for desk fourteen."

The wall clock clicked to 4:55.

"Have a good weekend, Sister Pan," Chen Xuan said politely. "Have a good weekend, Zheng Qian."

He walked unhurriedly into the elevator hall. On a Friday at five minutes to five, Hengxin Tower's three elevators were always stuck on the upper floors, yet Chen Xuan never even reached the call button. As he stepped into the hall, the middle elevator chimed softly. Its doors slid open to show an empty, spotless cab waiting on the twelfth floor just for him.

Chen Xuan stepped inside, pressed the ground floor button, and closed his eyes for six quiet seconds as the elevator went down.
