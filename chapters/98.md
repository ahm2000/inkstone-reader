# Chapter 98: The Coffee Shop Below Jin Mao Tower

The bright early autumn sun shimmered off the tall glass spires of Jin Mao Tower and the Oriental Pearl Tower down the avenue, while a light breeze sent golden ginkgo leaves tumbling across the Lujiazui plaza.

Inside Cloud Corner Café on the ground floor of the plaza, soft piano music drifted over polished marble tables. At Table Six by the floor-to-ceiling window sat Qin Yuhan.

She wore a tailored cream-colored blazer over a white silk blouse, her dark hair pinned neatly back with a silver clip. As the 26-year-old president of Haiyue Group, whose executive suite occupied the thirty-eighth floor of Haiyue Tower, and the granddaughter of retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo in Beijing, Qin Yuhan was used to sitting calmly across from senior directors from ICBC and China Construction Bank.

Right now, however, she was rubbing her temples and staring out the window with a quiet sigh.

Her status-conscious mother in Beijing, Lin Shuyin, had called her four times that morning trying to force her to have lunch with Zhou Mingcheng, a flashy Lujiazui fund manager whose family loved boasting about their connections. Worse still, her grandfather Elder Qin Zhengguo had phoned after breakfast, his voice warm yet unmistakably worried, asking when his stubborn granddaughter would finally find a steady, honest husband instead of spending every evening alone with financial reports.

Qin Yuhan did not want a rich heir who treated marriage like a corporate merger. She simply wanted a calm, simple, decent man who would not chase her family name or play power games.

The midday lunch rush had packed every table in the café. Just as Qin Yuhan reached for her lukewarm black coffee, a pleasant, unhurried voice spoke beside her table.

"Excuse me. Everywhere else is full. Would you mind if I shared the empty seat across from you for a few minutes?"

Qin Yuhan looked up. Standing beside Table Six was a tall young man in a clean 20-yuan white cotton shirt and simple sneakers. In his right hand he held a dented 15-yuan green metal thermos, and in his left he carried a small brown paper bag that gave off the warm, comforting scent of freshly baked walnut cookies.

There was no polished arrogance in his posture and no calculating glance at her designer handbag or watch. His eyes were clear, relaxed, and genuinely friendly.

"Please, go ahead," Qin Yuhan said, her tense shoulders loosening slightly. "I am not expecting anyone."

"Thank you," Chen Xuan replied with an easy smile. He sat down, set his scratched green thermos neatly beside the sugar bowl, and flagged down a passing waiter to order a simple pot of hot green tea.

Then he opened his brown paper bag, picked up a clean napkin, and set two golden-brown walnut cookies on it before sliding them halfway across the marble table.

"I bought these from an old bakery two streets over," Chen Xuan said casually. "The baker only pulls them out of the oven right before noon, and they taste terrible once they go cold. You look like you have been staring at spreadsheets all morning. Have one while it is still warm."

Qin Yuhan blinked in mild surprise. In Lujiazui, men who approached her table usually offered business cards, stock tips, or loud boasts about their yachts. Nobody had ever slid a warm bakery cookie across the marble with zero expectations.

"Thank you," she said softly, picking up a cookie and taking a small bite. The crisp pastry crumbled gently, rich with roasted walnuts and a light touch of brown sugar. It was genuinely delicious, and for the first time all morning, her headache faded.

"You work nearby?" she asked, curiously eyeing the green metal thermos. "I am Qin Yuhan, by the way. I work across the avenue at Haiyue Group."

"Chen Xuan," he answered with an easy smile as the waiter poured his steaming green tea. "I am just over at Hengxin Logistics down the street. I handle shipping schedules and paperwork. Good hours, no overtime, and plenty of time to cook dinner."

"You cook?" Qin Yuhan asked, a genuine smile touching her lips.

"Braised pork ribs, steamed sea bass, and tomato egg soup are my specialties," Chen Xuan said, lifting his teacup. "People in Lujiazui spend too much money on cold salads and bitter coffee. A proper hot meal fixes half of life's problems."

Qin Yuhan looked across the table at him. He spoke with complete ease, neither intimidated by the upscale café nor trying to impress her. His clothes were cheap, his thermos was battered, yet he carried an odd, steady warmth that made the noisy plaza outside feel miles away.

A sudden, bold, surprisingly practical thought crossed her mind. Her mother would never stop pushing arrogant suitors like Zhou Mingcheng until she was married. And her grandfather only wanted her settled with someone kind and grounded.

She set her coffee cup down and folded her hands neatly on the marble table.

"Mr. Chen," Qin Yuhan said quietly, her dark eyes steady and serious, "I know this will sound sudden, and you are free to laugh and walk away. Are you single?"

Chen Xuan paused with his teacup halfway to his lips, amused. "Completely single. My mother reminds me of that exact fact every time I visit home."

Qin Yuhan drew a calm breath. "My family has been pressuring me nonstop to marry a man I cannot stand. I run my own company, I have my own home, and I do not need anyone's money. What I need is a calm, respectful marriage with an honest, simple man who wants a peaceful life. Would you ever consider a straightforward agreement like that?"

Chen Xuan studied her earnest face for a quiet moment, seeing the genuine exhaustion tucked behind her composed poise. Before he could answer, Qin Yuhan reached hesitantly toward the second walnut cookie, waiting to see if he thought she had lost her mind.
