# Chapter 98: Qin Yuhan's Visit to Room 601

Sunday afternoon brought a pale, gentle sun over Yunxi County.

A modest silver sedan turned smoothly into the narrow courtyard off Willow Lane and parked neatly beside the bicycle shed. There were no escort vehicles, no luxury emblems, and no chauffeur hurrying to open the door.

Qin Yuhan stepped out from the driver's seat herself, wearing a cream cashmere sweater under a simple camel wool coat. From the passenger seat she lifted a bamboo basket brimming with glossy winter oranges.

She looked up at the weathered concrete of Building 2, smiled quietly, and walked into the stairwell.

Building 2 had no elevator. The concrete steps were worn smooth by thirty years of footsteps, and the hallway smelled of pickled radish, laundry soap, and simmering ginger.

Qin Yuhan climbed all six flights at an easy pace. By the time she reached the green iron security door of Room 601, her cheeks held a healthy rosy flush.

Before she could knock a second time, the wooden inner door swung wide.

"Miss Qin!" Liu Meifang's face lit up like the morning sun. She wiped her hands on her floral apron and hurried to unlock the iron gate. "Chen Jianguo, put down your newspaper! Miss Qin came all the way from Qingyan!"

"Auntie Liu," Qin Yuhan greeted warmly, offering the basket with both hands. "I picked up some sweet winter oranges. I hope I am not interrupting your Sunday."

"Interrupting? You are making my whole week!" Liu Meifang took the basket and ushered Qin Yuhan inside, pulling out a brand-new pair of pink cotton slippers from the shoe cabinet. "The stairs are so tall, your legs must be tired. Do not think about leaving before dinner. I have pork-rib soup with lotus root simmering on the stove right now!"

"Auntie Liu, I really should not put you to trouble," Qin Yuhan began politely.

"Putting one more bowl on the table is not trouble, it is good luck!" Liu Meifang declared firmly. "Chen Xuan! Pour Miss Qin some hot tea!"

Chen Xuan walked over from the living room in a soft gray knit sweater, carrying a porcelain cup of freshly steeped jasmine tea.

"You found the parking spot," Chen Xuan said with a quiet smile, handing her the cup.

"You told me to avoid the corner under the sycamore tree because the birds have terrible aim," Qin Yuhan replied, her eyes crinkling. "I followed your instructions."

Behind Chen Xuan, Chen Yuan leaned casually against the bedroom doorframe with a teasing grin.

"Welcome, Miss Qin," Chen Yuan said warmly, then lowered his voice just enough for his younger brother to hear. "Driving sixty kilometers on a Sunday with winter oranges. Look at Mom's face, Chen Xuan. She is already picking out a tablecloth for New Year."

Chen Xuan did not even blink. "Big Brother, Mom asked you to peel the garlic twenty minutes ago."

Chen Yuan chuckled, raised both hands in surrender, and retreated toward the kitchen.

* * *

In the living room, Tang Xiaoyu jumped off her small wooden stool the moment Qin Yuhan sat down on the sofa, clutching a thick blue exercise book to her chest.

"Sister Qin!" Tang Xiaoyu called out happily, her braids bouncing.

"Hello, Tang Xiaoyu," Qin Yuhan said, her voice softening at once. "How has school been?"

"Look!" Tang Xiaoyu stepped right up and opened the blue notebook proudly across Qin Yuhan's knees.

Every page held neat, disciplined rows of arithmetic problems. At the top of the latest test sheet sat a bright red "100" circled twice, with a teacher's note in red ink: *First place in the grade. Keep up the wonderful focus.*

"Uncle Chen Xuan helped me check my fractions every Saturday morning," Tang Xiaoyu announced, beaming. "Teacher Wang said nobody else in the whole fourth grade solved the bonus question without a mistake."

Qin Yuhan looked down at the crisp handwriting, then glanced sideways at Chen Xuan, who was quietly peeling a winter orange at the edge of the table.

"That is remarkable, Tang Xiaoyu," Qin Yuhan praised sincerely. "Even university students rush past tricky word problems. You have real patience."

Tang Xiaoyu giggled. "Uncle Chen Xuan says rushing only makes you redo the work twice. If you finish cleanly the first time, you get to eat dinner earlier."

"A very wise philosophy," Qin Yuhan murmured, her gaze lingering warmly on Chen Xuan.

Chen Xuan placed half a peeled orange into Tang Xiaoyu's palm and handed the other half to Qin Yuhan. "Wash your hands before eating, Tang Xiaoyu. Soup is almost ready."

* * *

Half an hour later, the round folding table in the living room was crowded with steaming dishes.

In the center sat a wide clay pot of milky-white pork-rib soup, fragrant with lotus root, red dates, and sliced ginger. Around it stood braised tofu, stir-fried bok choy, steamed perch with scallions, and Liu Meifang's homemade pickled radish.

Chen Jianguo sat at the head of the table in his clean plaid shirt, pouring a small cup of warm rice wine for himself and setting hot orange-peel tea beside Qin Yuhan's plate.

"Miss Qin, do not be shy in this house," Chen Jianguo said heartily, lifting his chopsticks. "Our home is small, and the stairs are steep, but the soup is honest. As the old saying goes, when good company climbs six flights of stairs, even plain winter broth tastes sweeter than palace wine."

Across the table, Chen Yuan coughed lightly into his napkin to hide a laugh. "Dad, which book did that old saying come from?"

Chen Jianguo lifted his chin with solemn dignity. "It comes from my own mouth, two seconds ago. Every proverb has to start somewhere, Chen Yuan."

Liu Meifang laughed out loud and tapped her husband's arm with her ladle. "Stop inventing proverbs and eat your perch before it gets cold!"

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, a clear, unguarded sound that rarely echoed inside the glass conference rooms of Qingyan. She lifted her spoon and tasted the broth.

It was rich, clean, and deeply comforting, warming her down to her fingertips.

"Uncle Chen is right," Qin Yuhan said genuinely. "I have eaten at many grand hotels, and none of them have soup half as good as Auntie Liu's."

Liu Meifang beamed. "Hear that, Chen Xuan? Miss Qin knows good food! Eat more, Miss Qin, you are far too thin from working in the city."

While Liu Meifang heaped tender ribs onto Qin Yuhan's plate and Tang Xiaoyu chattered about school, Chen Xuan said very little.

He moved with quiet, effortless grace. He picked the small bones cleanly out of steamed perch for Tang Xiaoyu. He topped up Chen Jianguo's cup before it fell below half. When Qin Yuhan's bowl emptied, he stood calmly, ladled fresh hot broth and two slices of soft lotus root, and set it back beside her elbow.

Their fingers brushed briefly against the warm porcelain rim.

Qin Yuhan looked up and met his calm, dark eyes.

Under the bright ring light of Room 601, surrounded by laughter, clinking chopsticks, and steam rising from a humble clay pot, the man who could move mountains with a single phone call looked utterly, completely at home.
