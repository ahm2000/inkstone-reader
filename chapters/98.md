# Chapter 98: The Coffee Shop Below Jin Mao Tower

At twelve-twelve, the noon sun shimmered off the tall glass spires of Jin Mao Tower and the Oriental Pearl Tower down the avenue.

Inside Cloud Corner Café on the ground floor of an upscale Lujiazui plaza, soft piano music drifted over polished marble tables. At Table Six by the floor-to-ceiling window sat Qin Yuhan.

She wore a cream-colored blazer over a silk blouse, her dark hair pinned back with a silver clip. As the twenty-six-year-old president of Haiyue Group and the granddaughter of retired Beijing military general Elder Qin Zhengguo, Qin Yuhan was used to sitting in boardrooms across from bank directors from ICBC and China Construction Bank.

Right now, however, she was rubbing her temples and staring out the window with a quiet sigh.

Her status-conscious mother in Beijing, Lin Shuyin, had called her four times that morning trying to force her to have lunch with Zhou Mingcheng, a flashy Lujiazui fund manager whose family had connections in Beijing. Even her father, Qin Zhengming, had sent a text telling her not to make her mother angry. To avoid Zhou Mingcheng, Qin Yuhan had agreed to fill in for her assistant's cousin on a low-key blind date at this quiet coffee shop so she would have a real excuse to turn her mother down.

"Just thirty minutes," Qin Yuhan murmured to herself, checking her slim gold wristwatch. "Drink one cup of lemon water, exchange polite small talk, and go back to Haiyue Group."

Just then, the glass door of the café swung open.

A tall young man in a clean white casual shirt and comfortable dark trousers walked in. He had warm, clear eyes, broad shoulders, and an easygoing smile that made him look completely out of place among the stressed Lujiazui bankers rushing past outside. In his right hand, he carried a scratched dark green steel thermos.

He glanced around the room, spotted the cream-colored blazer at Table Six, and walked over.

"Excuse me," Chen Xuan said with a friendly smile. "Are you the friend Auntie Wang's niece introduced?"

Qin Yuhan looked up. For a second, she was surprised by how handsome and clean-cut he was, and then her gaze dropped to the chipped green thermos in his hand. A genuine trace of amusement flickered in her cool, bright eyes.

"Yes, I am Qin Yuhan," she said politely, gesturing to the chair across from her. "You must be Chen Xuan. Please sit."

"Nice to meet you, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan said, pulling out the chair and sitting down comfortably. He set his green thermos upright right next to the fine bone-china sugar bowl.

When a waiter in a crisp vest stepped over with two leather menus, Chen Xuan waved a hand politely. "Just a plate of walnut cookies and a cup of hot water for her, plus a free hot water refill for my thermos if you do not mind. Thanks."

The waiter blinked at the scratched green thermos, glanced at Qin Yuhan's nod, and hurried off.

Qin Yuhan rested her chin lightly on her fingers, watching Chen Xuan unscrew his thermos cap to let the steam out. "Mr. Chen, I have lived and worked in Lujiazui for four years, and I think you are the very first person I have ever seen bring an old green thermos into Cloud Corner Café."

"The coffee here costs sixty-eight yuan a cup and tastes like burnt medicine," Chen Xuan replied honestly, pushing the plate of walnut cookies toward her when the waiter set it down. "My jasmine tea costs forty yuan a pound back home in Yunxi County, and this green thermos keeps it hot from nine in the morning until five in the afternoon. Why pay sixty-eight yuan to give yourself a stomachache?"

Qin Yuhan let out a soft, clear laugh that made several nearby businessmen turn their heads. At Haiyue Group, her executives were always stiff and careful around her, trying to impress her with big financial words. Even back home in Beijing, every young man her mother Lin Shuyin introduced spent the entire meal bragging about his family or his stock returns.

"You have a very good point," Qin Yuhan said, picking up a walnut cookie. "Auntie Wang's niece did not tell me much before I came. What do you do here in Lujiazui, Chen Xuan?"

"I work on the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics two blocks away," Chen Xuan answered easily, pouring a little hot jasmine tea into the metal cap of his thermos to cool. "I am an ordinary shipping clerk. I check container forms, make sure the numbers match, and earn twelve thousand yuan a month."

Qin Yuhan raised her eyebrows slightly. She waited for him to add that he was planning to start a big company, or that he had secret investments, or that he was studying at Fudan University. Every man in Lujiazui tried to make himself sound ten times bigger than he really was.

Instead, Chen Xuan took a happy sip of his tea and stopped right there.

"That is all?" Qin Yuhan asked playfully, leaning forward a bit. "Usually, when men sit down at a blind date in Lujiazui, they spend twenty minutes telling me about their five-year career plan, how they work until midnight, and how they plan to buy a flat on the Bund before they turn thirty."

"Working until midnight sounds terrible," Chen Xuan said, shaking his head with genuine sympathy. "What is the point of buying a big flat on the Bund if you only sleep in it for four hours a night? My biggest life goal is much simpler."

"Oh? What is your biggest life goal?" Qin Yuhan asked, smiling with real curiosity.

"Never working a single minute of overtime, clocking out at four fifty-five every afternoon, eating three warm meals a day, and sleeping eight full hours every night," Chen Xuan said proudly, holding up four fingers. "If I have enough money left over to buy fresh groceries and keep my tea jar full, I consider my life a complete success."

Qin Yuhan stared at him for a moment, and then her eyes curved into bright crescents as she laughed again. The tight headache that had bothered her all morning from her mother Lin Shuyin's phone calls seemed to melt right away.

"Never working overtime and sleeping eight hours a night," Qin Yuhan repeated wistfully. "Do you know how many company presidents on this street would trade half their shares just to have your schedule for one week?"

"They do not need to trade their shares," Chen Xuan said with a warm grin. "They just need to buy a green thermos and learn how to turn off their phones at five o'clock."
