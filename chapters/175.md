# Chapter 175: Collapse by the Bay

"Lies!" Shen Wanshan roared, his voice cracking with panic as he pointed a trembling finger at Deputy Director Zhao Jianming. "Smuggling? Financial fraud? Shenhai Shipping has operated cleanly out of Shenzhen for twenty years! You have no proof! My lawyers in Hong Kong and Beijing will tear your warrants to shreds by sunrise!"

"Really?" Deputy Director Zhao Jianming asked evenly.

He glanced toward the right side of the dining hall.

Number 19 stepped smoothly forward, holding a thick, white-sealed evidence case. With a crisp click, Number 19 opened the case on the edge of the rosewood table and placed two heavy, neatly indexed stacks of documents in front of Zhao Jianming and Ma Weidong.

"Take a good look, Shen Wanshan," Zhao Jianming said, lifting the top file of the Beijing task-force package that reached us this afternoon and opening it toward the silver-haired patriarch. "Every single cargo manifest from Yantian Port Berth Four over the past thirty-six months. Seven hundred and forty undeclared night containers carrying untaxed luxury electronics, restricted industrial metals, and grey-market goods routed between Hong Kong and Shenzhen under fake agricultural codes."

Shen Wanshan's mouth fell open.

"How did you get those?" Shen Wanshan whispered, his knees going weak. "Those ledgers were kept inside a private vault in Shekou! Only three people alive knew the code!"

"And here is the second stack," Zhao Jianming continued without pause, turning his sharp gaze squarely onto Koo Tianming. "Koo Tianming's complete Seven-Harbor files. Every private transfer route linking Hong Kong, Macau, Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Xiamen, Ningbo, and Shanghai. Every offshore payout sent to corrupt dock inspectors, including the 4 million yuan wired to Chief Inspector Lu Guobin's brother-in-law last autumn."

The last drop of color drained from Koo Tianming's face.

"Wait!" Koo Tianming blurted out, his calm Hong Kong accent breaking into desperation. "Deputy Director Zhao, listen to me! I am only an outside consultant! Shen Wanshan owns the ships! Shen Wanshan signed the Yantian Port manifests! He forced me to handle the Hong Kong accounts!"

"You traitorous dog!" Shen Wanshan screamed, lunging half a step toward Koo Tianming before two Anti-Smuggling officers caught his shoulders. "You brought those Seven-Harbor routes to my table twelve years ago! You told me nobody in Shenzhen or Beijing could ever trace them!"

"Stop fighting!" Shen Jingzhou whimpered, tears of terror spilling down his pale cheeks as he looked wildly toward the open terrace doors. "Grandfather, I did not sign any of those Yantian Port papers! I do not even know what Seven-Harbor means! Let me go!"

Before anyone could answer, Shen Jingzhou shoved his fallen chair toward an officer and bolted frantically toward the dark terrace beside the koi pond, hoping to reach the family's speedboat docked twenty meters away.

He took exactly three strides.

Number 88 did not even run.

"Going somewhere, Young Master Shen?" Ma Weidong asked drily, snapping cold steel handcuffs around Shen Jingzhou's wrists.

Click.

"Grandfather! Save me!" Shen Jingzhou sobbed, his arrogant sneer from Shenzhen Pier Nine completely gone as two officers marched him toward the courtyard.

Beside the rosewood table, Senior Inspector Ma Weidong stepped smoothly over to Koo Tianming and locked steel handcuffs tightly around his wrists. He merely stared once more at the plain matte-white phone beside Chen Xuan's teacup, his shoulders sagging in total defeat.

"Who are you?" Koo Tianming whispered hoarsely as officers led him past Chen Xuan's chair. "Even in Hong Kong, only the highest houses ever hear of a White Order."

Chen Xuan did not answer.

Across the table, Shen Wanshan stared down at the Yantian Port smuggling logs, the four red-stamped recall notices from China Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China, and the court warrants freezing every brick of his 140 billion yuan empire.

All at once, the strength left his body.

Shen Wanshan's broad shoulders caved inward. The proud patriarch who had commanded Shenzhen's southern docks for two decades slumped heavily back into his rosewood chair, his silver head dropping forward as if he had aged twenty years in a single breath.

"Finished," Shen Wanshan muttered weakly, staring at his empty, shaking palms. "Twenty years of building, all gone in one night."

Deputy Director Zhao Jianming nodded once to two officers. They stepped forward, lifted Shen Wanshan by the elbows, and locked the final pair of steel handcuffs onto his wrists.

"Shen Wanshan, you are under criminal detention," Zhao Jianming announced clearly. "Prosecutors will decide on formal arrest within days. Take them all to the vans. Seal the villa and secure the Yantian Port files."

Within ten minutes, the dining hall emptied out.

The Macau enforcers, Liang Biao among them, were loaded into Public Security vans. Koo Tianming, Shen Wanshan, and Shen Jingzhou were led out through the lantern-lit garden, their heads bowed under the headlights.

Before leaving, the four Shenzhen regional chiefs of China Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China bowed briefly to Chen Xuan and wished Qin Yuhan a pleasant evening.

Number 7 gave Chen Xuan a crisp nod. All forty White Guard members filed silently out of the courtyard. Within sixty seconds, the twelve matte-white sedans glided back onto the coastal road and vanished into the night.

Only the soft lap of Shenzhen Bay waves remained.

Chen Xuan checked his watch. It was barely half past eight.

"The night is still early," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Shall we head back? The taxi driver is probably getting bored waiting by the banyan trees."

Qin Yuhan stood up slowly, looking straight at Chen Xuan under the warm light. Behind him, the skyline of Shenzhen Bay No. 1 and Ping An Finance Center sparkled across the dark water. Yet right in front of her stood the same quiet man who ate ten-yuan noodles near Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai and worried about working overtime.

She walked beside him out through the koi garden and past the open gates. Two shadows in plain matte-black gear shifted silently along the sea wall, staying within fifty meters as they walked down the lane.

Under the yellow streetlamp two blocks down, the plain blue-and-white taxi sat idling beneath the banyan trees.

Qin Yuhan slowed her steps just before reaching the car. She turned to face Chen Xuan, the sea breeze lifting dark hair across her cheek. Her eyes were bright and shining with unmistakable realization.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan said softly, a warm smile touching her lips. "You really are not just an ordinary shipping clerk from Hengxin Logistics, are you?"

Chen Xuan paused with his hand on the taxi door handle and smiled back with easy warmth.

"My pay slip still says Hengxin Logistics," Chen Xuan replied gently, opening the rear door for her. "And my bus card still only works on Shanghai Line 2. Come on, President Qin. If we do not get back soon, we will miss the wonton shop across from the hotel."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft laugh, the tension of the evening melting away. She stepped into the plain taxi, looking up at him with bright, admiring eyes as he climbed in beside her and closed the door.
