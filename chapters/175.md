# Chapter 175: Collapse by the Bay

With the Four National Banks locking down every account and Director Zhou's team finishing their verification of the sealed audit dossier, the final curtain fell on the Shen Family.

Director Zhou closed the thick kraft portfolio, walked smoothly around the rosewood table, and stopped directly in front of Shen Wanshan. His voice was calm, steady, and completely devoid of sympathy.

"Shen Wanshan and Kwok Wing-fai," Director Zhou announced clearly. "Based on verified customs seizures, offshore financial records, and witness testimony regarding tonight's unlawful detention and commercial coercion, you are both formally under arrest. Your charges include organized maritime smuggling, financial fraud, tax evasion, and illegal cross-border capital transfers."

Two senior officers stepped forward. The sharp metallic click of steel handcuffs echoed across the silent dining hall.

The sound seemed to sever the last thread holding Shen Wanshan upright. All the pride, wealth, and coastal influence he had built over thirty years evaporated in a single heartbeat. His knees buckled completely, and he collapsed heavily onto the cold marble floor right beside the shattered remains of his baijiu glass.

His fine burgundy silk jacket soaked up the spilled liquor and fine dust. Trembling from head to toe, Shen Wanshan raised his bloodshot eyes toward Qin Yuhan and Chen Xuan.

"How?" Shen Wanshan whispered hoarsely, his voice breaking like dry twigs. "It was supposed to be a quiet Thursday dinner. Nobody outside this room knew what was in that folder. How did everything collapse in a single hour?"

Director Zhou did not waste a breath answering him. "Take them both to the transport vans," he ordered crisply. "Seal the study and log the unsigned transfer contract as primary evidence of extortion."

Officers lifted the limp, muttering Shen Wanshan from the floor and led him out toward the flashing blue lights in the courtyard, followed closely by a pale, weeping Kwok Wing-fai. The hired guards who had surrounded the table less than an hour ago were already sitting lined up along the entrance wall, waiting to have their identities recorded.

Director Zhou turned toward Qin Yuhan and offered a polite, professional nod. "President Qin, your witness statement is complete for tonight. Haiyue Group's coastal shipping routes and berth rights are entirely secure, and our office will send a formal clearance notice to your legal department tomorrow morning. You and your husband are free to leave whenever you wish."

"Thank you, Director Zhou," Qin Yuhan said, her voice still carrying a faint trace of awe. "We truly appreciate how quickly your team acted."

"Just doing our duty," Director Zhou replied evenly, never once glancing curiously at the quiet man standing beside her.

Together, Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan walked out through the tall stone doorway of Harbor Villa into the crisp late November night. A cool sea breeze brushed their faces, carrying away the heavy smell of spilled baijiu and tense panic from inside the hall. Below the cliff, the dark waters of the bay rolled gently beneath the amber glow of distant cargo ships.

Qin Yuhan paused at the top of the stone steps and let out a long, deep breath that she felt like she had been holding since they first drove through the gates. Slowly, she reached over, slipped her chilled fingers into Chen Xuan's warm hand, and squeezed tightly.

"I still cannot believe what just happened," Qin Yuhan murmured, turning her bright eyes up toward him. "An hour ago, Shen Wanshan had twelve guards blocking the doors and tried to force me to sign away 2.4 billion yuan in coastal routes. Now he is sitting in the back of a federal van, and all four national banks froze his entire empire."

"Bad luck catches up fast when people get too greedy," Chen Xuan said mildly, gently rubbing the back of her hand with his thumb to warm her up.

Qin Yuhan let out a soft, breathless laugh, leaning closer against his arm as they walked toward their parked sedan. "Honestly, Xuan, I think marrying you might have brought me the best luck of my life. Every time trouble corners me, it somehow blows up right in the other person's face before they can even lay a finger on us."

"Well, a calm husband is good for the family fortune," Chen Xuan teased lightly.

"Wait until I tell Grandpa tomorrow," Qin Yuhan added with a bright smile. "He is going to laugh for a whole week when he hears Shen Wanshan's trap collapsed before he could even finish his toast. He will probably insist on playing three extra games of chess with you this weekend just to celebrate."

"Tell him I will bring good tea, then," Chen Xuan said, opening the passenger door for her before walking around to the driver's seat so she would not have to drive after such a tense evening. "And once we get back to the hotel suite, I will order two bowls of hot wonton noodle soup before bed. We barely touched dinner in there, and we have a flight back to Shanghai tomorrow."

"That sounds much better than a fancy banquet," Qin Yuhan said warmly, fastening her seatbelt and noticing his green metal thermos still resting safely in the door pocket. She smiled to herself, feeling completely safe and grounded.

As the sedan rolled smoothly back down the gravel drive, the friendly older gatekeeper in the thick knitted vest stepped out of the stone gatehouse and gave them a warm, cheerful wave, calling out for them to drive carefully on the coastal road. Chen Xuan tapped the horn softly in reply and gave the older man a relaxed nod.

Soon, the flashing blue lights of Harbor Villa faded behind the dark headland, leaving only the quiet hum of the road and the gentle shimmer of harbor lights along the bay. Qin Yuhan rested her head lightly against the seatback, glancing fondly at her husband's calm profile as he hummed a quiet tune.

She had no idea at all that the smiling, unhurried husband sitting beside her had orchestrated the entire collapse of the Shen Family without even letting his tea go cold.
