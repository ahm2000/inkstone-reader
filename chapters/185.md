# Chapter 185: Elder Qin Zhengguo's Warning

The man who walked into the briefing room wore no medals, no rank insignia, and no dress uniform. He was dressed in a plain dark wool jacket over a charcoal knit sweater, looking at first glance like any stern retired grandfather out for an evening walk. Yet his back was straight as a northern pine, and the sharp authority in his gaze instantly quieted the room.

It was retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo, summoned by Elder Song as a veteran military advisor who had spent four decades commanding troops across the northern frontier.

Premier Gu Chengshan stood slightly in clear respect. "Elder Qin, thank you for coming out so late on a Sunday night."

"No need for formality, Premier," Elder Qin Zhengguo replied in a deep, gravelly voice. He nodded warmly to Elder Song, then walked straight past the chairs to the illuminated topographic table showing the Changbai Mountain valley. He leaned forward, planting both hands on the table's edge, and studied the satellite thermal scan for ten silent seconds.

Then he lifted his eyes and looked straight at ICBC President Han Guoliang.

"I caught the tail end of your brilliant plan from the doorway, President Han," Elder Qin said bluntly, his tone dry as winter iron. "Tell me something. Have you ever flown a transport helicopter into a narrow Changbai canyon in late November crosswinds?"

Han Guoliang stiffened, his ears turning red. "Elder Qin, I only meant that 300 tons of gold is at stake. If we delay, the smugglers could move it across the border."

"Move 300 tons of solid gold overnight on mountain roads?" Elder Qin snorted sharply, tapping the granite ridge on the map. "Three hundred tons takes fifteen heavy cargo trucks running nose to tail through icy switchbacks. They are not slipping that into a backpack before breakfast. What they can do in three seconds, however, is press a detonator."

The four bank presidents fell completely silent. Elder Qin pointed a firm finger at the heat signature tucked inside the western cliff face.

"Look at that layout," Elder Qin continued coldly. "That refinery sits inside an old reinforced mine shaft carved straight into solid rock. The joint reconnaissance brief Elder Song sent me an hour ago already flagged roughly sixty armed foreign mercenaries rotating along that perimeter. Those men are not street thugs who panic and drop their guns when they see uniforms. They are paid professionals working for international syndicates."

He fixed Han Guoliang with a piercing stare. "The moment your dawn helicopters roar over that outer ridge, the rotor wash will echo five kilometers down the valley. Those mercenaries will not even bother fighting a losing battle against our troops. They will blow the entrance charges and the interior support pillars, drop ten thousand tons of frozen granite onto the gold, and scatter into the forest trails. You will lose brave young soldiers in the blast, bury the reserves under half a mountain, and spend six months digging through rubble while the people behind this laugh from overseas."

Han Guoliang opened his mouth, found zero defense left to stand on, and slowly sat back down in his chair, his face burning with embarrassment. Beside him, Fang Zhengming, Liang Qingshan, and Tang Wenhao exchanged sober glances, quietly relieved that Elder Song had brought a real soldier into the room before they signed off on a disaster.

Premier Gu Chengshan leaned forward intently. "Elder Qin, what is the right way to take that valley?"

Elder Qin Zhengguo straightened his shoulders, his expression calm and decisive. "No loud helicopters, no local police mobilizations that could leak word up the mountain, and no premature roadblocks. You send a small, elite silent team in on foot over the northern ridge under cover of night, moving against the freezing wind shear where thermal sensors struggle to separate human heat from cold pine cover. They clear the outer watchposts quietly, cut the wiring on the demolition charges, and secure the tunnel mouth before a single alarm sounds. Once the tunnel is safe and the mercenaries are neutralized, then the main forces roll in to lock down every bar of gold."

At the head of the table, Elder Song nodded with calm, unhurried approval. "Exactly right," Elder Song said softly. "Strike quietly, protect our soldiers, and leave the syndicates no chance to destroy the evidence."

"Agreed," Premier Gu Chengshan said firmly, looking around the table. "We follow Elder Qin's tactical blueprint to the letter. Joint military and security command will coordinate the silent clearing operation immediately, while the four banks prepare forensic teams to receive and audit the bullion the moment the valley is declared secure."

With the strategy settled, the tension in the briefing room finally gave way to focused action. Half an hour later, as the bank presidents hurried out to their respective headquarters, Elder Qin Zhengguo picked up his dark wool cap and walked toward the corridor beside Elder Song.

"Bankers ought to stick to ledgers and leave the mountains to soldiers," Elder Qin grumbled lightly, though the stern lines around his eyes softened. "Good thing my granddaughter Yuhan is happily settled down in Shanghai with a quiet, reliable husband who cooks a fine pot of braised pork ribs. Now that I do not have to worry about her, this old man finally has spare time on a Sunday night to keep Beijing from doing something foolish."

Elder Song chuckled warmly, walking beside his old comrade without a trace of surprise in his steady gaze. "A peaceful home is the greatest blessing a family can have, Old Qin. Come, let me walk you to your car."
