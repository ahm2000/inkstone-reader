# Chapter 185: General Qin Zhengguo's Warning

Four-star General Qin Zhengguo walked into the smoky Beijing briefing room without knocking, his dark-green dress uniform buttoned neatly to the collar and four gold stars glinting calmly on each shoulder.

He closed the walnut doors firmly behind him, leaving his aide in the hallway.

General Qin Zhengguo wasted no time on small talk.

Click.

A worn brass lighter flared in his rough hand.

"President Han Guoliang," General Qin Zhengguo said in a deep, iron-steady voice, fixing his sharp eyes on the ICBC president. "I heard your last sentence through the door. Armored trucks and helicopters into Changbai Mountain before sunrise?"

Han Guoliang cleared his throat, sitting straighter under the four-star general's gaze. "General Qin Zhengguo, if 6 billion yuan in national rare metals and the Seven-Harbor Consortium's paper ledgers are sitting in that abandoned mine, we cannot let them slip away!"

General Qin Zhengguo pulled the Changbai Mountain contour map closer and tapped the narrow frozen valley ringed in red.

"Nobody wants them to slip away," General Qin Zhengguo said bluntly, looking around the table at the bank presidents, Ding Guoping, and Zhou Zhenhua. "The Seven-Harbor Consortium has foreign armed contractors guarding that Changbai Mountain valley. If regular troops march in loudly, they will blow the tunnels and slip across the border."

Dead silence fell over the smoky room.

Fang Zhengming of China Construction Bank leaned forward slightly, frowning. "General Qin, surely a single mountain valley cannot stop our northern military zone? How many foreign fighters can they have?"

"Between seventy and ninety," General Qin Zhengguo replied flatly, tapping ash into a glass tray. "Military intelligence finished checking Bank of China's Hong Kong transfer names an hour ago. Those men are not local smugglers with hunting rifles. They are former foreign special-forces contractors who fought in three overseas wars. And they do not care about winning a gunfight against our regular battalions."

"Then what do they care about?" Tang Wenhao of Bank of China asked quietly.

"Destroying the evidence and staying alive to collect their pay," General Qin Zhengguo answered coldly. "Look at the map. That abandoned mine sits less than twenty-eight kilometers from the northeast border. In November, the air in Changbai Mountain is thin and freezing. You can hear heavy helicopter blades or armored engines from twelve kilometers away."

He pointed his cigarette at two steep white ridges flanking the single snow-covered logging road.

"High-altitude checks confirmed heat cameras and buried sensors along both ridges," General Qin Zhengguo continued evenly. "Inside the main refinery tunnel, they have stored industrial mining explosives beside the support pillars. The moment a regular convoy turns onto that forest road, those foreign guards have fifteen clear minutes. Five minutes to trigger the charges and bury 6 billion yuan of rare metals and every paper ledger under a million tons of frozen granite. Ten minutes to mount high-speed snowmobiles, cross the frozen river ridge, and vanish across the border."

Han Guoliang's face paled slightly. He leaned back slowly, seeing how close his rushed idea would have come to causing a disaster. "If the tunnels collapse, we lose the rare metals, we lose the master ledger, and we never learn who their overseas buyers or domestic protectors are."

"Worse," State Security Minister Ding Guoping added darkly, rubbing his temples. "If a loud gunfight breaks out thirty kilometers from the border with foreign contractors blowing up a mountain valley, foreign intelligence agencies will know within an hour. Every domestic accomplice in Shanghai, Shenzhen, and Hong Kong will burn their files before lunchtime."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua lit a fresh cigarette, his expression calm and deeply thoughtful as smoke curled around his glasses.

"Old Qin," Zhou Zhenhua spoke quietly, using his familiar title for the general. "What if you send a small, silent team from the northern special unit on foot through the pine forests at night? No helicopters, no trucks."

General Qin Zhengguo took a slow drag and shook his head bluntly.

"I already checked with the Northern Theater commander before walking in here," Qin Zhengguo replied honestly. "The snow up in Changbai Mountain is waist-deep this week, and it drops to twenty-five below zero after midnight. Even my best twenty-man squad needs six hours to cross those ridges on skis. They can slip past the road sensors and take out the outer sentries. Yet the moment the first guard misses a ten-minute radio check inside the heated tunnel, the commander inside hits the detonator button. My men will still be four hundred meters outside the steel blast doors."

Nobody argued.

Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank hesitated, glancing briefly at Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua before lowering his eyes back to his folder.

Zhou Zhenhua caught that glance.

"Gentlemen," Zhou Zhenhua said calmly to the four national bank presidents. "Your four banks have done outstanding work today. Keep every account tied to the Changbai Mountain forestry loans, power bills, and Hong Kong gold shells quietly under watch, but freeze nothing further until we give the signal. Do not alert the mountain valley. You may return to your headquarters now."

Fang Zhengming, Han Guoliang, Tang Wenhao, and Liang Qingshan understood immediately that the talk was moving above their chairs. All four rose neatly, gathered their folders, offered respectful nods, and filed quietly out through the walnut doors.

The moment the latch clicked shut, the smoky room grew even quieter. Only Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, Minister Ding Guoping, and General Qin Zhengguo remained.

Ding Guoping pushed Liang Qingshan's handwritten note about Shenzhen Bay No. 1 and the matte-white card at Shanghai's Plaza 66 silently across the wood toward General Qin Zhengguo.

General Qin Zhengguo read the three lines. His eyes narrowed slightly, and a muscle jumped once along his tight jaw. He still remembered standing behind the glass doors of his Western Hills study not long ago, watching two silent figures vanish from a locked military yard without tripping a single heat sensor.

"He is in Shanghai," General Qin Zhengguo murmured, his voice dropping low. "And he handed us Koo Tianming's banking trail before sunrise this morning."

"He did not just hand us the trail," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said softly, looking down at the red circle around Changbai Mountain. "He left the biggest nest untouched in the snow so we could see how deep the rot really goes."

"If regular troops cannot reach those steel doors before the detonator clicks," Ding Guoping said quietly, meeting both men's eyes, "only one group in this world can walk straight past mountain sensors and eighty foreign contractors in total silence."

General Qin Zhengguo stared at the glowing orange tip of his cigarette for three long seconds. Then he crushed it slowly into the glass ashtray and stood up.

"I will order the northern border regiments to seal the outer crossings quietly from forty kilometers back," General Qin Zhengguo said evenly. "Not a single truck or helicopter goes near Changbai Mountain yet. Keep the border locked and wait to see which way the wind blows from Shanghai."
