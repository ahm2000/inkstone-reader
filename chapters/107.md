# Chapter 107: The Woman in the Gray Coat

Beside the side glass partition of the Gate Seven Clearance Hall sat a young woman in a dove-gray wool coat.

Her dark hair was tied in a neat low knot, framing a composed face with sharp eyes. On her lap rested a black leather folder stamped in tiny silver letters with the emblem of Haiyue Group.

Her name was Qin Yuhan. At twenty-seven, she ran Haiyue Group's coastal supply division in Shanghai, and she had come to Gate Seven herself because a container of imported surgical scanners had been stuck since Tuesday night. Almost nobody in the hall knew she was the granddaughter of four-star General Qin Zhengguo, and she liked it that way. She preferred watching how a place worked when nobody thought anyone important was looking. Right now, what she saw at Counter Three made her eyes turn cool.

Zhou Mingcheng had just finished driving Lin Xiaodong back toward the benches when he spotted the quiet woman by the glass. His gaze caught the silver Haiyue Group emblem and her tailored coat.

His sneer vanished in half a second. He stubbed out his cigarette, smoothed his navy jacket, and walked over with a bright, practiced smile.

"Excuse me, Miss," Zhou Mingcheng said warmly. "Are you with Haiyue Group? I am Zhou Mingcheng, head of Mingcheng Freight Services. We handle priority clearance at Gate Seven."

Qin Yuhan looked up briefly, polite and distant. "Qin Yuhan, supply division. I am waiting for ticket forty-nine."

"Director Qin, you should not sit on a plastic bench in a noisy hall like this," Zhou Mingcheng said smoothly. "Haiyue Group is one of Shanghai's finest firms. Give me your container number and manifest. I will walk it behind the window right now, no queue and no fee. Just a favor between friends."

"No need, Manager Zhou," Qin Yuhan replied evenly, closing her folder. "Haiyue Group follows the standard queue. Every company here has drivers waiting."

Zhou Mingcheng's smile twitched, though he forced a light laugh. "Director Qin is too modest. Still, if the line drags, just nod my way. My uncle Zhou Desheng oversees gate operations here, so one word from me saves you two days."

A dull chime sounded above Counter Three. The red display blinked to forty-eight.

Chen Xuan capped his scratched green thermos, tucked it into his canvas satchel, and walked calmly to Counter Three. He placed Hengxin Logistics' stamped manila folder on the marble ledge.

"Good morning," Chen Xuan said steadily. "Number forty-eight. Hengxin Logistics. Precision valve shipment, pre-inspection already cleared. I need the gate release slip for Lane Two."

Before the clerk could reach for it, Zhou Mingcheng stepped over, his bruised pride looking for an easy target. He sneered at Chen Xuan's faded windbreaker, worn sneakers, and chipped green thermos.

"Hold on," Zhou Mingcheng said, slapping his palm flat on Chen Xuan's folder. "Who told you to walk up here while I am speaking with Director Qin from Haiyue Group?"

Chen Xuan did not flinch or pull back. He glanced at Zhou Mingcheng's gold-watched hand, then lifted his calm eyes.

"The screen called forty-eight," Chen Xuan said mildly. "Director Qin holds forty-nine. And you were already finished speaking with her."

A few clerks on the benches let out nervous breaths. Behind the glass, the young clerk shrank back in his chair.

Zhou Mingcheng let out a harsh laugh, glancing toward Qin Yuhan as if expecting her to join in. "Look at this guy. Wearing a fifty-yuan windbreaker, carrying an old farmer's thermos, working for a tiny outfit like Hengxin Logistics, and lecturing me about queue numbers."

He tapped the folder with two fingers. "Listen carefully, little clerk. Counter Three is handling priority brokerage accounts this morning. If Hengxin Logistics wants that truck out before noon, pay the 200k quarterly fast-lane fee, or wait until Friday. Take your folder off the marble."

Chen Xuan tilted his head, his voice still quiet. "Manager Zhou, are you an employee of the Shanghai Port Authority?"

Zhou Mingcheng scoffed. "Everyone at Gate Seven knows who I am."

"That is not a yes," Chen Xuan said politely, turning to the nervous clerk behind the glass. "Clerk twelve, may I ask you a question?"

The clerk swallowed hard. "Yes?"

"Look at the laminated notice beside your water cooler," Chen Xuan said clearly, pointing inside the booth. "Shanghai Customs and Port Authority Joint Notice Nineteen, issued Monday morning. Article Four states that any container with a green pre-inspection code must receive a gate release slip within fifteen minutes, at zero extra cost. Article Seven states that private brokerage agents are forbidden from standing behind public counters or holding up non-client manifests."

The hall went dead quiet. Lin Xiaodong stopped rubbing his shoe and stared in disbelief.

The window clerk turned pale and glanced at the laminated sheet. Every word Chen Xuan quoted was printed there in black ink.

"If you refuse to stamp a cleared manifest because a private broker put his hand on my folder," Chen Xuan added gently, "the camera above Counter Three records both time and sound. When the customs review team checks this week's logs, it will be your employee badge on the screen, not Manager Zhou's."

The clerk's hand shook as he looked from Chen Xuan's calm face to Zhou Mingcheng's dark glare.

"You little nobody, who the hell do you think you are?" Zhou Mingcheng snapped, dropping all politeness. "Quoting a wall notice to threaten port staff? Do you know what happens to smart-mouthed clerks who act tough at Gate Seven?"

Chen Xuan slid his folder smoothly from under Zhou Mingcheng's palm and placed it inside the glass slot.

"I am just an ordinary clerk from Hengxin Logistics," Chen Xuan answered calmly. "I read the notice board while waiting in line. If your brokerage is legal, Manager Zhou, there is no reason to be upset about a public rule."

Beside the glass partition, Qin Yuhan set her pen down, real interest lighting her sharp eyes. Across three provinces she had met presidents and negotiators who shouted to hide fear or bowed to survive. This plain man had not raised his voice once, yet he had taken apart a local bully in three clean sentences while carrying an old green thermos.

Behind the glass, the clerk hesitated two seconds, swallowed hard, and stamped Chen Xuan's release slip with a sharp thud.

Zhou Mingcheng stared at the red stamp, his face flushing dark red in front of Qin Yuhan and forty watching clerks.

"Fine," Zhou Mingcheng hissed. "Take your piece of paper. Let us see if your truck makes it past the Gate Seven scale."
