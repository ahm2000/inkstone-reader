# Chapter 107: The Woman in the Gray Coat

Beside the side glass partition of the Gate Seven Clearance Hall sat a young woman in a clean dove-gray wool coat.

Her dark hair was tied back in a neat low knot, framing a calm, composed face with sharp eyes. On her lap rested a slim black leather folder stamped in tiny silver letters with the emblem of Haiyue Group.

Her name was Qin Yuhan. At twenty-seven, she ran the coastal supply division of Haiyue Group in Qingyan, and she had come to Gate Seven herself because a container of imported surgical scanners had been held up since Tuesday night. Almost nobody in the port hall knew her family background, and she liked it that way. If anyone had known she was the granddaughter of four-star General Qin Zhengguo, half the port office would have rushed outside in the rain to open her car door.

Qin Yuhan preferred watching how a place worked when nobody thought anyone important was looking. Right now, what she saw at Counter Three made her eyes turn cool.

Zhou Mingcheng had just finished driving Lin Xiaodong back toward the benches when he turned and spotted the quiet woman by the glass partition. His gaze caught the silver Haiyue Group emblem on her folder, then moved up to her tailored gray coat.

The sneer vanished from his face in half a second.

He stubbed his cigarette out in the metal bin, smoothed his navy jacket, and walked over with a bright, practiced smile.

"Excuse me, Miss," Zhou Mingcheng said warmly. "Are you with Haiyue Group? I am Zhou Mingcheng, head of Mingcheng Freight Services. We handle priority clearance here at Gate Seven."

Qin Yuhan looked up briefly, polite and distant. "Qin Yuhan, supply division. I am just waiting for ticket forty-nine."

"Director Qin, you should not be sitting on a plastic bench in a noisy hall like this," Zhou Mingcheng said smoothly. "Haiyue Group is one of Qingyan's finest firms. Give me your container number and manifest. I will walk it straight behind the window right now, no queue and no fee. Just a small favor between friends."

"No need, Manager Zhou," Qin Yuhan replied evenly, closing her folder. "Haiyue Group follows the standard queue. Every company here has drivers waiting."

Zhou Mingcheng's smile twitched, though he forced a light laugh. "Director Qin is too modest. Still, if the line drags, just nod my way. My uncle Zhou Desheng oversees gate operations here, so one word from me saves you two days."

Just then, a dull chime sounded above Counter Three. The red display blinked from forty-seven to forty-eight.

Chen Xuan capped his scratched green thermos, tucked it into the side pocket of his canvas satchel, and walked calmly up to Counter Three. He placed Hengxin Logistics' stamped manila folder neatly on the marble ledge.

"Good morning," Chen Xuan said in a steady voice. "Number forty-eight. Hengxin Logistics. Precision valve shipment, pre-inspection already cleared. I need the gate release slip for Lane Two."

Before the window clerk could reach for the folder, Zhou Mingcheng stepped back over, his bruised pride looking for an easy target. He looked Chen Xuan up and down, sneering at the faded dark windbreaker, worn sneakers, and chipped green thermos peeking from the canvas bag.

"Hold on," Zhou Mingcheng said, slapping his palm flat on top of Chen Xuan's folder. "Who told you to walk up here while I am speaking with Director Qin from Haiyue Group?"

Chen Xuan did not flinch or pull the folder back. He glanced at Zhou Mingcheng's gold-watched hand, then lifted his calm eyes to the man's face.

"The screen called forty-eight," Chen Xuan said mildly. "Director Qin holds forty-nine. And you were already finished speaking with her."

A few clerks on the benches let out nervous breaths. Behind the glass, the young window clerk shrank back in his swivel chair.

Zhou Mingcheng let out a harsh laugh, glancing back toward Qin Yuhan as if expecting her to laugh too. "Look at this guy. Wearing a fifty-yuan windbreaker, carrying an old farmer's thermos, working for a tiny outfit like Hengxin Logistics, and lecturing me about queue numbers."

He tapped Chen Xuan's folder with two fingers. "Listen carefully, little clerk. Counter Three is handling priority brokerage accounts this morning. If Hengxin Logistics wants that truck out before noon, pay the 200k quarterly fast-lane fee, or step back and wait until Friday. Take your folder off the marble."

Chen Xuan tilted his head slightly, his voice still quiet. "Manager Zhou, are you an employee of the Qingyan Port Authority?"

Zhou Mingcheng scoffed. "Everyone at Gate Seven knows who I am."

"That is not a yes," Chen Xuan said politely. He turned to the nervous clerk behind the glass. "Clerk twelve, may I ask you a question?"

The window clerk swallowed hard. "Yes?"

"Look at the laminated notice pinned beside your water cooler," Chen Xuan said clearly, pointing one steady finger toward the wall inside the booth. "Qingyan Customs and Port Authority Joint Notice Nineteen, issued Monday morning. Article Four states that any container with a green pre-inspection code must receive a gate release slip within fifteen minutes, at zero extra cost. Article Seven states that private brokerage agents are forbidden from standing behind public counters or holding up non-client manifests."

The entire hall went dead quiet.

Lin Xiaodong stopped rubbing his shoe and stared at Chen Xuan in disbelief.

The window clerk turned pale and glanced toward the laminated sheet beside the water cooler. Every word Chen Xuan had quoted was printed there in black ink.

"If you refuse to stamp a cleared manifest because a private broker put his hand on my folder," Chen Xuan added gently, "the camera above Counter Three records both the time and the sound. When the customs review team checks this week's logs, it will be your employee badge on the screen, not Manager Zhou's."

The window clerk's hand shook as he looked from Chen Xuan's calm face to Zhou Mingcheng's dark glare.

"You little nobody, who the hell do you think you are?" Zhou Mingcheng snapped, dropping all trace of politeness. "Quoting a wall notice to threaten port staff? Do you know what happens to smart-mouthed clerks who act tough at Gate Seven?"

Chen Xuan slid his folder smoothly out from under Zhou Mingcheng's palm without roughness and placed it flat inside the glass slot.

"I am just an ordinary clerk from Hengxin Logistics," Chen Xuan answered calmly. "I read the notice board while waiting in line. If your brokerage is legal, Manager Zhou, there is no reason to be upset about a public rule."

Beside the glass partition, Qin Yuhan slowly set her pen down on her leather folder.

For the first time all morning, real interest lit her sharp eyes. She had met company presidents and tough negotiators across three provinces. Most people shouted loudly to hide fear, or bowed their heads to survive. This plain man in a faded windbreaker had not raised his voice once, and he had taken apart a local bully in three clean sentences while carrying an old green thermos.

Behind the glass, the window clerk hesitated for two seconds, swallowed hard, and stamped Chen Xuan's release slip with a sharp thud.

Zhou Mingcheng stared at the red ink stamp, his face flushing dark red in front of Qin Yuhan and forty watching clerks.

"Fine," Zhou Mingcheng hissed through his teeth. "Take your piece of paper. Let us see if your truck makes it past the Gate Seven scale."
