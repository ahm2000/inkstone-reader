# Chapter 68: Southern Theater Command: The Purge Begins

At seven fifty on Monday morning, the steel doors of Southern Theater Command's military court room locked from inside. Ten theater commanders and political commissars sat along the table, olive uniforms crisp and shoulders stiff. Thick cigarette smoke already hung low over the brass ashtrays.

At the far end, the secure wall screen flickered and locked onto a live feed from Beijing.

General Qin Zhengguo sat squarely in the frame, three-star shoulder boards gleaming under bright light. His lined face showed zero warmth, straight and hard as concrete.

All ten officers snapped to attention together.

"Sit," General Qin Zhengguo ordered, voice booming like gunfire. "Keep your pens ready. Nobody leaves this room until every signature is on the purge order."

The commanders sat heavily. Near the middle, Theater Deputy Commander Major General Kong Weiming glanced at the empty chair at the lower right. That seat belonged to the Coastal Logistics Division. An hour earlier, military police had marched Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's aide out in handcuffs.

"Four hours ago," General Qin Zhengguo began without wasting a word, "Central Military Commission discipline officers arrested Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu at his home. Inside his locked tablet and safe, investigators found fourteen fake military fuel slips, three orders sending coastal patrols away, and bank transfer records linking his brother-in-law's shipping agency to Chairman Deng Yongchang's business group."

A sharp gasp cut through the smoky silence.

Major General Kong Weiming slammed his palm onto the table, rattling two ashtrays, his neck turning dark red. "Fuel slips and patrol orders? Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu sold military sea clearances to civilian smugglers?"

"Worse than smugglers, Kong Weiming," General Qin Zhengguo replied coldly. "Over nine years, Ma Zhenyu used Southern Theater's coastal radar repair schedule to open forty-minute blind spots along the Shanghai Port and Yunxi County sea lane. Unregistered cargo barges belonging to Yan Tiehu and Deng Yongchang sailed straight through those blind spots under military signal cover."

Political Commissar Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang swore under his breath. His eyes snapped to Coastal Defense Chief Major General Shao Biao.

"Shao Biao!" Zheng Haiyang's voice cracked like a whip. "Every coastal radar shutdown longer than fifteen minutes needs two signatures from your Coastal Defense office! How did Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu open forty-minute blind spots three times in six months without your desk catching it?"

Major General Shao Biao's face turned blotchy red, then drained pale. Sweat soaked his uniform collar as he gripped the table edge.

"Commissar Zheng Haiyang, General Qin Zhengguo, hear me out!" Shao Biao stammered. "Ma Zhenyu sent tuning requests saying saltwater rust hit the Shanghai Sector Three radar dish! His engineers signed off on the error codes! My duty officers treated them like routine repairs!"

"Routine repairs at two fifteen in the morning on three moonless Tuesdays?" General Qin Zhengguo cut him off coldly, holding a printed record to the camera. "Do not lie to a military court, Shao Biao. On the second shutdown, Radar Station Four's watch captain logged an unscheduled boat crossing the Yunxi channel and called your deputy right away. Your deputy told him to erase the blip and wrote him up for a bad reading."

Shao Biao's mouth fell open. His hands dropped into his lap, fingers clenching white. "My deputy, Colonel Feng Deming, handled night alerts! I swear on my service record I never took a single cent from Ma Zhenyu or Deng Yongchang!"

"Whether you took money or slept while thieves blinded your radar line," Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang barked, jamming his cigarette into the tray until sparks flew, "your carelessness turned Southern Theater's coastal shield into a private smuggling gate! You are suspended from command right now, Shao Biao! Hand your gun and badge to the guard officer after this briefing!"

Shao Biao's shoulders slumped. He stared down at his shaking fingers and said nothing.

Across the table, Theater Chief of Staff Major General Lin Zhigang leaned forward, gray ash falling from his forgotten cigarette onto his map folder. "General Qin Zhengguo, how wide is the purge order? How deep does the rot go?"

"We treat the whole chain as dirty until checked clean," General Qin Zhengguo said flatly. "Carry out four orders now. First, strip Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's entire coastal supply chain down to platoon level. Twelve base supply officers and four fuel dispatch captains named in your sealed file go into military jail within thirty minutes."

Major General Kong Weiming wrote fast, jaw clenched. "Military police battalions One and Three are waiting at the truck yard, General Qin Zhengguo. We lock every depot gate before eight twenty."

"Second," General Qin Zhengguo went on, "lock down all seven coastal radar stations from Shanghai Port south past Yunxi County. No outgoing calls, no erased files, and nobody moves without orders. Seize every tape and digital log from the past twenty-four months."

Naval Coastal Liaison Senior Captain He Jianping cleared his throat, eyes wide behind his glasses. "General Qin Zhengguo, if we lock down all seven stations at once, who keeps live sea watch over the southern lanes?"

"Third order," General Qin Zhengguo answered without pausing. "Swap out every tainted officer and watch crew within two hours. Replacement radar technicians and supply commanders from the inland reserve brigade are already in the air on transport helicopters heading for your runway. They land at eight forty. The handover finishes by ten o'clock sharp, with zero gap in coverage."

A tense quiet settled over the table. Commanders traded grim looks. Putting inland reserve crews in the air before the meeting started meant Beijing had timed this strike to the minute while they were still asleep.

"Fourth order," General Qin Zhengguo added, leaning closer so his stare pinned every officer. "Give zero-leak outer security for central customs and naval security teams taking Pier Seven at Shanghai Port. Any Southern Theater officer who makes an unauthorized call to Shanghai city officials, Jingzhou City officials, or private shipping firms will face trial under wartime military rules."

Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang stood slowly. "General Qin Zhengguo, Southern Theater Command takes full blame. By ten o'clock today, Ma Zhenyu's supply chain will be in handcuffs, every radar station will have clean reserve crews, and every record will be sealed for the Central Military Commission."

"Make sure it is," General Qin Zhengguo replied coldly. "The Central Military Commission audit team's plane is already coming down into Southern Theater airspace. Do not leave a single loose end when their wheels hit the runway. Move out."

The screen snapped black. For three seconds, nobody breathed. Then Major General Kong Weiming threw his pen down and barked at the waiting military police colonel outside to roll the armored trucks, while Major General Shao Biao sat frozen in the smoke, watching his career turn to ash before eight o'clock.
