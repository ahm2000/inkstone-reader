# Chapter 68: The Reception Hall

The grand lobby of the Lanshui International Convention Hotel gleamed with Italian marble, crystal chandeliers, and tall arrangements of white lilies.

Outside the revolving glass doors, black executive sedans discharged provincial business magnates, port executives, and municipal delegations in tailored wool suits.

General Manager Sun Haoran paused at the threshold, smoothing his navy jacket. Beside him, Manager Hu Dewei stared at the three-story ceiling, clutching his briefcase tightly. Chen Xuan walked calmly behind them, his backpack checked at the concierge desk, carrying only a notebook and a cheap blue ballpoint pen.

At the velvet-draped registration desk, three staffers checked credentials with brisk efficiency. When Sun Haoran presented Hengxin Logistics' cream-colored invitation, the lead coordinator glanced at the company name and gave his modest suit a polite, practiced chill.

"Hengxin Logistics from Qingyan City," the coordinator murmured without smiling. "Regional county fleet. Your badges are here, General Manager Sun Haoran. You are assigned to Section D, Table Twenty-four."

"Table Twenty-four?" Hu Dewei asked. "Excuse me, miss, where is Section D?"

"Far rear left corner near the service doors," she replied smoothly, already gesturing to the next guest. "It is labeled Small County Logistics. Please follow the signs inside Grand Ballroom A."

Sun Haoran's cheeks reddened faintly, and his jaw tightened, though he collected the three plastic lanyards with quiet dignity.

"Small County Logistics," Hu Dewei muttered bitterly as they entered the reception hall. "They send a gold-embossed invitation, then shove us beside the kitchen swing doors like extra chairs."

"Do not take it to heart, Hu Dewei," Sun Haoran said quietly, forcing a wry smile. "At least we are in the room. Let us find Table Twenty-four."

Chen Xuan followed them across the thick carpet, completely unbothered. Reaching the corner table, he noticed something far more practical than the seating chart.

"Look on the bright side, General Manager Sun Haoran," Chen Xuan said mildly, gesturing ten paces left. "Being near the service doors puts us closest to the refreshment counter. The almond pastries just came out of the oven."

Sun Haoran blinked, looked at the silver trays of warm pastries, and let out a genuine chuckle. "Leave it to you to find the silver lining, Chen Xuan. Go ahead and grab something before the panel starts."

* * *

Chen Xuan strolled to the buffet table, placed two flaky almond pastries and a cup of jasmine tea on a small plate, and stood calmly beside a marble pillar, watching provincial executives trade business cards.

Fifty paces away, near the center aisle, a tall man in a tailored charcoal double-breasted suit stopped mid-sentence. His gold-rimmed badge read *Marcus Vance, Senior Partner, Nordic-Pacific Capital*.

Li Weiming's dark eyes narrowed by a fraction of a millimeter as they locked onto the young man in the washed gray jacket standing peacefully beside the pastry trays. In the last photographs his vanished trackers had transmitted from Qingyan City before going silent, their target had worn that exact same jacket.

Li Weiming excused himself from two bank vice presidents with a charming laugh, picked up a glass of sparkling water, and walked unhurriedly toward the refreshment corner.

"You have excellent taste," Li Weiming said smoothly in lightly accented Mandarin, stopping two paces from Chen Xuan with a disarming smile. "The chef trained in Lyon. Most guests rush for smoked salmon and miss the almond pastries."

Chen Xuan swallowed neatly, dabbed his mouth with a napkin, and turned with polite friendliness.

"They really are good," Chen Xuan replied mildly. "And they are warm. At company meetings we usually only get packaged biscuits."

Li Weiming's smile deepened as his sharp gaze studied Chen Xuan's face, searching for any flicker of guardedness or hidden sharpness. He found none. Chen Xuan looked like a contented junior clerk enjoying a rare business trip.

"Allow me to introduce myself," Li Weiming said, extending a manicured hand. "Marcus Vance, though my Chinese name is Li Weiming. Senior Partner with Nordic-Pacific Capital, advising on infrastructure and freight across Asia. And you are?"

"Chen Xuan," Chen Xuan answered politely, shaking his hand briefly. "Just an administrative clerk from Hengxin Logistics in Qingyan City. Nice to meet you, Mr. Li Weiming."

"Qingyan City," Li Weiming repeated softly. "What a coincidence. My European partners read an economic brief about your region last week. Yunxi County is right next door, is it not?"

Chen Xuan nodded simply, sipping his jasmine tea. "Yes, Yunxi County is across the ridge. Forty minutes by highway."

"Fascinating place," Li Weiming murmured, watching Chen Xuan's eyes closely. "I heard there was a dramatic financial shakeup around Yunxi Old Street recently. Something involving Huaxin Bank's regional branch and a sudden audit. A few international funds lost their bearings following the news. Did Hengxin Logistics feel any ripples?"

The probe was velvet wrapped around a surgical needle. Anyone tied to Huaxin Bank's collapse or the vanishing of two foreign operatives on East Avenue would have tensed or offered a rehearsed denial.

Chen Xuan merely blinked once, looking mildly thoughtful as he picked up his second pastry.

"I do not really follow banking news, Mr. Li Weiming," Chen Xuan replied with disarming sincerity. "Huaxin Bank is way above my pay grade. All I know is that Yunxi Old Street reopened cleanly, road repairs finished on schedule, and our drivers no longer take the bumpy dirt detour. That saved our fleet a lot of fuel."

Li Weiming held his smooth smile for three seconds, though a flicker of genuine confusion stirred behind his eyes.

"Just fuel savings?" Li Weiming asked lightly. "You never wondered how a quiet county street drew such high-level attention overnight?"

"Not really," Chen Xuan said gently, gesturing toward the buffet. "When you earn five thousand yuan a month sorting freight manifests, you mostly worry about whether the warehouse roof leaks and whether the hotel buffet refills the roast duck at lunch. Speaking of which, do you know if lunch is served here in Grand Ballroom A, Mr. Li Weiming?"

For half a heartbeat, Li Weiming's poise nearly slipped. He stared at the ordinary clerk holding a half-eaten pastry, searching for irony or mockery, and saw only calm warmth.

"It is," Li Weiming said smoothly, recovering with a soft chuckle. "At twelve thirty sharp. It is refreshing to meet someone here who is not trying to sell me a ten-billion-yuan dream."

"I would not even know how many zeroes are in ten billion," Chen Xuan replied politely. "Excuse me, Mr. Li Weiming, my general manager is waving me back."

"Enjoy the opening panel, Mr. Chen Xuan," Li Weiming said graciously. "I am sure we will cross paths again."

Chen Xuan nodded and strolled back to Table Twenty-four.

Alone beside the pillar, Li Weiming's warm smile cooled into a thin, calculating line. Either this clerk from Hengxin Logistics was truly a harmless nobody swept up by coincidence, or he was the most terrifyingly composed actor Li Weiming had met in twenty years.

Either way, today's opening panel would force every small fish in Lanshui Province into the net.
