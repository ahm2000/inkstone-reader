# Chapter 68: Southern Theater Command: The Purge Begins

At seven fifty on Monday morning, the steel doors of Southern Theater Command's closed-door military court room locked from inside. Ten theater commanders and political commissars sat along the long table, olive uniforms crisp and shoulders stiff. Thick gray cigarette smoke already hung low over the brass ashtrays, stinging their eyes.

At the far end of the table, the secure wall screen flickered once and locked onto a live video feed from Beijing.

General Qin Zhengguo sat squarely in the frame, three-star shoulder boards gleaming under bright white light. His lined face showed zero warmth. He sat as straight and hard as concrete.

All ten officers pushed back their chairs and snapped to attention together.

"Sit," General Qin Zhengguo ordered, his voice booming through the speakers like gunfire. "Keep your pens ready. Nobody leaves this room until every signature is on the purge order."

The commanders sat heavily. Near the middle, Theater Deputy Commander Major General Kong Weiguo glanced at the empty chair at the lower right end. That seat belonged to the Coastal Logistics Division. An hour earlier, military police had marched Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's aide out in steel handcuffs.

"Four hours ago," General Qin Zhengguo began without wasting a word, "Central Military Commission discipline officers arrested Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu at his coastal base quarters. Inside his locked tablet and private safe, investigators found fourteen fake military fuel slips, three orders sending coastal patrols away, and bank transfer records linking his brother-in-law's shipping agency to Chairman Deng Yongchang's business group."

A sharp gasp cut through the smoky silence.

Major General Kong Weiguo slammed his palm onto the table, rattling two brass ashtrays. His neck turned dark red. "Fuel slips and patrol orders? Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu sold military sea clearances to civilian smugglers?"

"Worse than smugglers, Kong Weiguo," General Qin Zhengguo replied coldly. "Over nineteen months, Ma Zhenyu used Southern Theater's coastal radar repair schedule to open forty-minute blind spots along the Shanghai Port and Yunxi County sea lane. Unregistered cargo barges belonging to Yan Tiehu and Deng Yongchang sailed straight through those blind spots under military signal cover."

Political Commissar Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang reached for his cigarette pack with visibly shaking fingers. He dropped the first cigarette onto his notepad, swore under his breath, and lit a second one, pulling hard until the tip glowed bright orange. His eyes snapped across the table to Coastal Defense Chief Major General Shao Biao.

"Shao Biao!" Zheng Haiyang's voice cracked like a whip. "Every coastal radar shutdown longer than fifteen minutes needs two signatures from your Coastal Defense office! How on earth did Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu open forty-minute blind spots three times in six months without your desk catching it?"

Major General Shao Biao's face turned blotchy red, then drained pale. Sweat soaked into his stiff uniform collar as both hands gripped the edge of the table.

"Commissar Zheng Haiyang, General Qin Zhengguo, please hear me out!" Shao Biao stammered, rushing to defend himself. "Ma Zhenyu sent in tuning requests saying saltwater rust had hit the Shanghai Sector Three radar dish! His engineers signed off on the error codes! My duty officers treated them like routine repairs!"

"Routine repairs at two fifteen in the morning on three moonless Tuesdays?" General Qin Zhengguo cut him off, his tone freezing cold. He held a printed record up to the camera. "Do not lie to a military court, Shao Biao. On the second shutdown, Radar Station Four's watch captain logged an unscheduled boat crossing the Yunxi channel and called your deputy straight away. Your deputy told him to erase the blip and wrote him up for a bad reading."

Shao Biao's mouth fell open. His hands dropped into his lap, his fingers clenching white against his knee. "My deputy, Colonel Feng Deming, handled night alerts! I swear on my service record I never took a single cent from Ma Zhenyu or Deng Yongchang!"

"Whether you took money or slept while thieves blinded your radar line," Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang barked, jamming his cigarette into the brass tray until sparks flew, "your carelessness turned Southern Theater's coastal shield into a private smuggling gate! You are suspended from command right this minute, Shao Biao! Hand your gun and badge to the guard officer after this briefing!"

Shao Biao's shoulders slumped under an invisible weight. He stared down at his shaking fingers and said nothing.

Across the table, Theater Chief of Staff Major General Lin Zhigang leaned forward. His cigarette burned forgotten between his fingers while gray ash fell onto his map folder. "General Qin Zhengguo, how wide is the purge order? How deep does the rot go?"

"We treat the whole chain as dirty until it is checked clean," General Qin Zhengguo said flatly. "Carry out four orders right now. First, strip Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's entire coastal supply chain down to platoon level. Twelve base supply officers and four fuel dispatch captains named in your sealed file go into military jail within thirty minutes."

Major General Kong Weiguo wrote fast, his jaw clenched. "Military police battalions One and Three are waiting at the truck yard, General Qin Zhengguo. We lock every depot gate before eight twenty."

"Second," General Qin Zhengguo went on, "lock down all seven coastal radar stations from Shanghai Port south past Yunxi County. No outgoing calls, no erased files, and nobody moves in or out without orders. Seize every tape and digital log from the past twenty-four months."

Naval Coastal Liaison Senior Captain He Jianping cleared his throat, his eyes wide behind his glasses. "General Qin Zhengguo, if we lock down all seven stations at once, who keeps live sea watch over the southern lanes?"

"Third order," General Qin Zhengguo answered without pausing. "Swap out every tainted officer and watch crew within two hours. Replacement radar technicians and supply commanders from the inland reserve brigade are already in the air on transport helicopters heading for your runway. They land at eight forty. The handover finishes by ten o'clock sharp, with zero gap in coverage."

A tense quiet settled over the table. Commanders traded grim looks. Putting inland reserve crews in the air before the meeting even started meant Beijing had timed this strike to the minute while they were still asleep.

"Fourth order," General Qin Zhengguo added, leaning closer so his stare pinned every officer to his chair. "Give zero-leak outer security for central customs and naval security teams taking Pier Seven at Shanghai Port. Any Southern Theater officer who makes an unauthorized call to Shanghai city officials, Sha City officials, or private shipping firms will face trial under wartime military rules."

Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang stood up slowly, crushing a second cigarette into the ashtray. "General Qin Zhengguo, Southern Theater Command takes full blame. By ten o'clock today, Ma Zhenyu's supply chain will be in handcuffs, every radar station will have clean reserve crews, and every record will be sealed for the Central Military Commission."

"Make sure it is," General Qin Zhengguo replied coldly. "The Central Military Commission audit team's plane is already coming down into Southern Theater airspace. Do not leave a single loose end when their wheels hit the runway. Move out."

The screen snapped black. For three seconds, nobody breathed. Then Major General Kong Weiguo threw his pen down and barked at the waiting military police colonel outside to roll the armored trucks, while Major General Shao Biao sat frozen in the thick smoke, watching his career turn to ash before eight o'clock.
