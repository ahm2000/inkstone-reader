# Chapter 68: Southern Theater Command: The Purge Begins

At seven fifty on Monday morning, the steel doors of Southern Theater Command's closed-door military tribunal room were bolted from inside. Ten theater commanders and political commissars sat along the conference table, olive uniforms crisp, shoulders rigid. Thick gray cigarette smoke already hung low over the brass ashtrays, stinging the eyes.

At the far end of the table, the encrypted wall screen flickered once and locked onto a live feed from Beijing.

General Qin Zhengguo sat squarely in the frame, three-star shoulder boards gleaming under stark white light. His weathered face showed zero warmth, his posture unyielding as concrete.

All ten officers pushed back their chairs and snapped to attention in unison.

"Sit," General Qin Zhengguo ordered, his voice booming through the speakers with the flat crack of artillery. "Keep your pens ready. Nobody leaves this room until every signature is on the purge order."

The commanders sat heavily. Near the middle, Theater Deputy Commander Major General Kong Weiguo glanced toward the vacant chair at the lower right end. That seat belonged to the Coastal Logistics Division, and an hour earlier military police had marched Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's aide out in steel handcuffs.

"Four hours ago," General Qin Zhengguo began without preamble, "Central Military Commission discipline officers arrested Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu at his coastal depot quarters. Inside his encrypted tablet and private safe, investigators recovered fourteen forged military fuel vouchers, three coastal patrol diversion orders, and bank wire confirmations linking his brother-in-law's shipping agency to Chairman Deng Yongchang's corporate group."

A sharp intake of breath cut through the smoky silence.

Major General Kong Weiguo slammed his palm onto the table, rattling two brass ashtrays. His neck flushed dark red. "Fuel vouchers and patrol diversions? Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu sold coastal military clearances to civilian smugglers?"

"Worse than smugglers, Kong Weiguo," General Qin Zhengguo replied coldly. "Over nineteen months, Ma Zhenyu used Southern Theater's coastal radar maintenance schedule to open forty-minute blind windows along the Qingyan Port and Yunxi County corridor. Unregistered cargo barges belonging to Yan Tiehu and Deng Yongchang moved straight through those blind corridors under military transponder cover."

Political Commissar Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang reached for his cigarette pack with visibly trembling fingers. He dropped the first cigarette onto his notepad, swore under his breath, and lit a second one, inhaling until the paper glowed orange. His eyes snapped across the table to Coastal Defense Chief Major General Shao Biao.

"Shao Biao!" Zheng Haiyang's voice cracked like a whip. "Every coastal radar blackout longer than fifteen minutes requires a dual signature from your Coastal Defense Directorate! How in hell did Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu open forty-minute blind windows three times in six months without your desk flagging it?"

Major General Shao Biao's face flushed blotchy red, then drained pale. Sweat soaked into his stiff uniform collar as both hands gripped the table edge.

"Commissar Zheng Haiyang, General Qin Zhengguo, please hear me out!" Shao Biao stammered defensively. "Ma Zhenyu submitted calibration requests citing saltwater corrosion on the Qingyan Sector Three relay dish! His engineers certified the fault codes! My duty officers processed them as routine maintenance!"

"Routine maintenance at two fifteen in the morning on three moonless Tuesdays?" General Qin Zhengguo cut him off, tone dropping below freezing. He held a printed transcript up to the camera. "Do not lie to a military tribunal, Shao Biao. On the second blackout, Radar Station Four's watch captain logged an unscheduled vessel crossing the Yunxi channel and called your deputy directly. Your deputy ordered him to delete the ping and wrote him up for miscalibration."

Shao Biao's mouth fell open. His hands slid into his lap, fingers clenching white against his knee. "My deputy, Colonel Feng Deming, handled night escalations! I swear on my service record I never took a single fen from Ma Zhenyu or Deng Yongchang!"

"Whether you took money or slept while thieves blinded your radar grid," Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang barked, crushing his cigarette into the brass tray until sparks scattered, "your negligence turned Southern Theater's coastal shield into a private smuggling gate! You are suspended from command effective this minute, Shao Biao! Surrender your sidearm and badge to the marshal after this briefing!"

Shao Biao's shoulders slumped under an invisible weight. He stared at his trembling fingers and said nothing.

Across the table, Theater Chief of Staff Major General Lin Zhigang leaned forward, his cigarette burning forgotten between his knuckles while gray ash dropped onto his map folder. "General Qin Zhengguo, what is the full scope of the purge order? How deep does the rot run?"

"We assume the entire chain is compromised until audited clean," General Qin Zhengguo stated flatly. "Execute four directives immediately. First, strip Senior Colonel Ma Zhenyu's entire coastal logistics chain down to platoon level. Twelve depot supply officers and four fuel dispatch captains named in your encrypted packet go under military tribunal detention within thirty minutes."

Major General Kong Weiguo scribbled furiously, jaw clenched. "Military police battalions One and Three are standing by at the motor pool, General Qin Zhengguo. We seal every depot gate before eight twenty."

"Second," General Qin Zhengguo continued, "lock down all seven coastal radar stations from Qingyan Port south past Yunxi County. No outgoing calls, no data wipes, no unauthorized movement across any perimeter. Seize every magnetic tape and digital log from the past twenty-four months."

Naval Coastal Liaison Senior Captain He Jianping cleared his throat, eyes wide behind his spectacles. "General Qin Zhengguo, if we lock down all seven stations at once, who holds active maritime watch over the southern lanes?"

"Third directive," General Qin Zhengguo answered without missing a beat. "Rotate every compromised officer and watch crew out of post within two hours. Replacement radar technicians and logistics commanders from the inland reserve brigade are already aboard transport helicopters en route to your airstrip. They land at eight forty. Handover completes by ten o'clock sharp, zero coverage gap."

A tense hush settled over the table. Commanders traded sobering looks. Putting inland reserve crews in the air before the briefing even began meant Beijing had planned this strike to the minute while they were still asleep.

"Fourth directive," General Qin Zhengguo added, leaning closer so his gaze pinned every officer to his chair. "Provide zero-leak perimeter support for central customs and naval security teams seizing Pier Seven at Qingyan Port. Any Southern Theater officer who places an unauthorized call to Qingyan municipal officials, Lanshui officials, or civilian shipping firms will be tried under wartime discipline articles."

Lieutenant General Zheng Haiyang rose slowly, crushing a second cigarette into the ashtray. "General Qin Zhengguo, Southern Theater Command accepts full responsibility. By ten o'clock today, Ma Zhenyu's logistics chain will be in handcuffs, every radar station will be under clean reserve crews, and every ledger will be sealed for the Central Military Commission."

"Make sure it is," General Qin Zhengguo replied coldly. "The Central Military Commission audit team's transport plane is already descending into Southern Theater airspace. Do not leave a single loose thread when their wheels hit tarmac. Execute."

The screen blinked black. For three seconds, nobody breathed. Then Major General Kong Weiguo threw his pen down and barked at the waiting military police colonel outside to roll the armored trucks, while Major General Shao Biao sat frozen in the thick smoke, watching his career turn to ash before eight o'clock.
