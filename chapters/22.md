# Chapter 22: Cheng Biao's Bad Afternoon

By half past three on Sunday afternoon, the director's office at Old Street Station smelled strongly of stale smoke. Station Director Cheng Biao crushed a cigarette into a full ashtray, his jaw clenched tight.

For fifteen years, he had run Old Street Station like his own private kingdom. Never once had a family stayed as stubbornly quiet as the Chen household. Whenever Hongrui Real Estate needed a tough shopkeeper broken, Cheng Biao locked up the head of the house for disturbing public order and simply waited. Within six hours, relatives always came running with red envelopes, tears, and signed transfer papers for Zhou Quan.

Yet Chen Mingde had been locked up since Friday night. Chen Jianguo had come to the front desk once on Saturday morning before his son returned to Yunxi County, only to be brushed off. Since Saturday afternoon, nobody from Building 2 of Fuxing New Village had shown up, called, or sent a neighbor to beg.

Worse, Cheng Biao's private phone had rung twice since noon. First Zhou Quan called, sharp and impatient about the slipping demolition schedule. Then Deng Yongchang called in a cold temper, pointing out that Yan Tiehu's street thugs had gone strangely quiet since Saturday night while heavy trucks were waiting to roll beneath the quarry road after dark.

"Stubborn old mules," Cheng Biao muttered. He walked to his desk, pulled a questioning form pad from the drawer, and wrote Chen Jianguo's name and address across the lines. Without logging a case number in the city record book, he slammed the red station stamp onto the corner.

He jabbed the intercom button. "Zhao Kai. Xu Wenbin. Up here."

A minute later, two junior officers stepped inside. Zhao Kai wore a lazy smirk. Xu Wenbin stood quietly behind him.

Cheng Biao slid the stamped slip across the glass desk. "Take the white patrol car to Building 2 of Fuxing New Village. Bring Chen Jianguo here to answer questions about Chen Mingde's case."

Zhao Kai glanced at the blank case-number line and grinned. "What if the old man argues at the door?"

"Remind him that refusing to come makes things much harder for his younger brother inside," Cheng Biao snapped, his eyes bloodshot.

"Understood, Director Cheng Biao," Zhao Kai said smoothly, slipping the paper into his pocket. "Should we bring his son along too if he talks back?"

"Bring whoever gets in the way," Cheng Biao barked. "I want them sitting downstairs before five."

Xu Wenbin hesitated, eyeing the slip. "Director Cheng Biao, should we log it at the front desk first?"

Cheng Biao glared at him. "Since when do you teach me how to run Old Street Station? Move."

Xu Wenbin stiffened and lowered his eyes. "Yes, Director Cheng Biao."

Twenty minutes later, the white patrol car parked crookedly outside the rusted iron gate of Building 2 at Fuxing New Village. Zhao Kai left the engine running as he and Xu Wenbin strode into the courtyard.

"Watch the old man's knees shake the second he sees the red stamp," Zhao Kai chuckled loudly.

"Keep your voice down," Xu Wenbin muttered, glancing up at the balconies. "Neighbors are watching."

"Let them watch."

Before Zhao Kai's shoe touched the first stair, a matte-black city car with provincial-city plates glided to a stop behind their patrol car, blocking it in.

Zhao Kai frowned and spun around. "Hey! You are blocking a police car. Move it!"

The back doors opened. Three men in plain dark gray jackets stepped out. The lead man, silver at his temples with eyes as calm as still water, carried a slim black folder. His two assistants held tablets.

"Stop right where you are," the silver-haired man said. His voice was unhurried, yet it carried unmistakable weight.

Zhao Kai's smirk faltered, though he still puffed out his chest. "Who are you? We are on official duty from Old Street Station."

The man walked to the gate and opened a dark red leather ID booklet stamped in gold. "Shen Zhigang. City Discipline Inspection Commission, Third Supervision Office. Show me your papers for this address."

Xu Wenbin went stiff, all color draining from his face. Zhao Kai's bold act collapsed in a heartbeat. He swallowed hard and handed over the hastily stamped slip with unsteady fingers.

Shen Zhigang scanned it for three seconds, his voice turning cold as ice. "A questioning form for an ordinary citizen, written on a Sunday afternoon, with no city case number, no legal approval, and no real link to an open criminal case."

"Inspector Shen Zhigang, Director Cheng Biao just wanted to ask a few routine questions," Zhao Kai stammered, sweat beading on his forehead. "Just a friendly talk."

"A friendly talk that threatens a prisoner's family?" Shen Zhigang asked flatly. "Our records show Chen Mingde's detention report was never sent up to the provincial system. Who approved holding a citizen for forty-eight hours off the books?"

Zhao Kai's mouth opened and shut without a sound. Beside him, Xu Wenbin stared hard at the concrete, his jaw locked.

Behind Shen Zhigang, an assistant took photos of the bad form and wrote down the patrol car's plate number.

"Officer Zhao Kai, Officer Xu Wenbin," Shen Zhigang said evenly, handing back the sheet. "This order is not valid and is stopped on the spot. You will not climb those stairs. You will not contact Chen Jianguo or anyone in his home. Go back to Old Street Station and tell Station Director Cheng Biao that our office has opened a surprise review of every detention and demolition record signed in the past three years. Have those files on his desk by eight tomorrow morning."

Zhao Kai's face turned gray. His fingers shook as he took the slip. "Yes, Inspector Shen Zhigang. We are leaving right now."

Neither officer looked back. They hurried to their car, waited with burning ears while the black car backed out of the way, and sped down the lane. Shen Zhigang nodded to his assistants and stepped back into his car without knocking on a single door. The whole stop took less than four minutes, set in motion quietly by a clean rule-breaking report that Black Pine Guard's team had dropped into the inspection channel two hours earlier.

Upstairs, behind the parted lace curtain of the third-floor window, Chen Xuan stood holding a warm mug of water. He watched the black car glide out of the lane.

In the kitchen behind him, Liu Meifang peeled apples. At the dining table, Chen Jianguo read the county paper in total peace, never once having walked near the gate or the police station.

"Chen Xuan," Liu Meifang called gently. "What are you looking at down there? Come eat some fruit."

"Just a car turning around, Mom," Chen Xuan replied, letting the curtain fall back. "Coming."

Two kilometers away at Old Street Station, the phone receiver shook in Cheng Biao's damp hand as Zhao Kai's breathless voice finished telling him what had happened.

"Say that name again," Cheng Biao rasped, his chest tightening like a vise.

"Shen Zhigang, Director," Zhao Kai whispered in panic. "City Discipline Inspection, Third Supervision Office. He took photos of the form and ordered three years of detention and demolition files on your desk by eight tomorrow."

"Three years of files?" Cheng Biao's voice cracked. "How did Shen Zhigang know you were at Fuxing New Village before you even touched the stairs? Did someone here leak it?"

"I swear nobody knew, Director," Zhao Kai stammered, breathing hard. "He was already waiting. He even knew Chen Mingde's detention was never uploaded."

Cheng Biao's throat went bone dry. He slammed the receiver down so hard the plastic cracked right across the middle. His shaking palm stayed pressed against the broken phone. For the first time in fifteen years of ruling Old Street, Cheng Biao felt an invisible, airtight wall closing around him from every side, and he could not see the hands pushing it inward.
