# Chapter 22: Thirty-Yuan Leftovers

Chen Xuan had ridden in on the early bus from Yunxi, still half asleep. All he wanted was hot tea and a quiet Monday.

He got a warm tea egg instead. Ge Qinghe, the sixty-one-year-old cleaner, pressed it into his hand right by the elevator.

"Thanks, Ge Qinghe." His face brightened. "You're the nicest person on this floor. Don't tell the others."

Pan Xiulan stood at the whiteboard with a marker clenched in her fist, looking like a judge who had waited all year to bring down the gavel.

"Third quarter scores, everyone! First place, Chen Xuan, 93.6. Second place, Zhang Pengfei, 93.4!"

For one second, nobody breathed. Then thirty heads swiveled toward the same corner desk.

"Only two-tenths of a point? Zhang Pengfei worked nineteen late nights!"

"And Chen Xuan leaves at 4:55 every single day. I've seen him practically sprint for the elevator!"

Zhang Pengfei stood in front of the board in his neatly pressed suit. His face went pale, then pink, then red as a boiled shrimp. He had logged nineteen late nights, only to lose by two-tenths of a point to a man who ate steamed buns at his desk.

"Quiet down, please." Hu Dewei, the department manager, held his ceramic mug. "Only one name from our department goes up to General Manager Sun as Employee of the Quarter. That person moves to the top of the year-end promotion list. Since the gap is narrow, Zhang Pengfei requested a recount tomorrow at nine."

Chen Xuan lifted his thermos an inch. "Morning, Manager Hu. A recount is fine with me. I like numbers. They're nice and quiet."

"Of course, of course," Pan Xiulan said quickly. An hour ago she had called Chen Xuan a sleepwalker. Now she stared intently at the ceiling.

Zhang Pengfei straightened his tie, then pulled back his left cuff until a silver wristwatch caught the fluorescent light.

"Don't take it too hard, Supervisor Chen. A promotion should go to someone who earns it, not to some junior clerk who gets by on luck. By the way, my wife's uncle at the Port Authority picked out this Longines for me. Nine thousand eight hundred yuan. He says a future department head needs to look the part."

A low, envious murmur rippled across the office. The Port Authority decided which ships unloaded first, meaning his uncle could make or break a freight company.

Nine thousand eight hundred yuan was nearly a full month of Chen Xuan's salary. The message was unmistakable: my family has a harbor, and you have a steamed bun.

Chen Xuan glanced at the watch for half a second.

"Nine thousand eight hundred?" He kept his expression completely level. "It suits you. Wear it to the recount."

Zhang Pengfei waited for envy, anger, or even a flicker of embarrassment. Instead, he got a polite nod and a yawn.

"Chen Xuan!" Pan Xiulan snapped, switching sides in a single breath. "A winner should at least look concerned! Look at him, he's yawning!"

"Sorry, Pan Xiulan." Chen Xuan rubbed his eyes with a rueful half-smile. "The bus. I was up at five."

Zhang Pengfei leaned in close so only Chen Xuan could hear, keeping a pleasant smile on his face for the rest of the room.

"Tomorrow at nine, Supervisor, I'm going to show everyone what you really are. My wife's uncle decides which of Hengxin's ships unload first. One phone call, and your name goes straight onto the transfer list for the Harbor Road warehouse, four hours north. It's a three-year posting."

Chen Xuan looked straight at him for the first time that morning. His gaze was as calm and unbothered as a man checking the time.

"Zhang Pengfei," he said quietly. "The second container's invoice is sitting on my desk. Your initials are on it, and it bills us for storage twice. The Port's own stamp approved both charges. When the audit opens that file, someone is going to ask whose uncle works there."

Zhang Pengfei's smile froze on his lips as the color drained from his cheeks. Pan Xiulan's marker slipped from her fingers and hit the floor. Nobody bent to pick it up.

Zhang Pengfei's mouth had gone bone dry. How could an ordinary clerk know what was inside the Port's own books?

* * *

When hot water hit the tea leaves, a rich aroma drifted out over the cubicles. It was deep, sweet, and clean, like rain falling over a mountain ridge.

Pan Xiulan stopped mid-sentence.

Hu Dewei lifted his head, sniffed once, and followed the scent straight to Chen Xuan's desk.

"Supervisor Chen. May I?"

"Sure, Manager Hu. It's hot, so go slow."

Chen Xuan poured a small paper cup. Hu Dewei took one careful sip and closed his eyes. A heartbeat later, his eyes snapped wide open.

He had tasted this exact tea only once before, at the birthday dinner of Sun Haoran, General Manager of Hengxin Logistics. Sun Haoran had unlocked a small wooden box and measured out a single pinch for each guest, treating it like gold dust.

"What tea is this?" The paper cup trembled in Hu Dewei's fingers.

"Just green tea. Thirty yuan a bag, from Hao Jingshan's stall on Binjiang Road." Chen Xuan blew gently across his thermos lid. "He said it was last year's leftovers and nobody wanted it, so he gave me a good price. I know how that feels."

"Thirty yuan?"

Pan Xiulan snatched the cup and took one sniff. She shrieked so loudly that the entire floor jumped.

"My brother-in-law runs a tea shop! This is Cloud Peak! The real thing! Eighty thousand yuan a jin!"

A jin was half a kilogram. The office did the math faster than it had ever run a spreadsheet. That was nearly seven months of Chen Xuan's salary sitting inside a single paper bag.

"My brother-in-law says one half-jin gift box got a man his license renewal pushed through in a week!" Pan Xiulan's voice dropped into a greedy hush. "No forms. No waiting. Just one box!"

"Wait a second." Lin Dong, the youngest clerk, tapped furiously at his phone calculator. "Five grams to fill a thermos. That's eight hundred yuan every morning!"

"Eight hundred?"

"Two days of his pay, steeped in a scratched thermos!"

"Then Hao Jingshan must be a fool."

"Or a saint."

"Or Chen Xuan's family is so rich they practically feed this stuff to the dog."

Out of the thirty people on the floor, nineteen decided Chen Xuan was secretly wealthy. Six decided Hao Jingshan was a fool. Five decided the whole thing had to be a trick.

Chen Xuan listened to the commotion and scratched his head. What was so remarkable about a cup of tea?

Zhang Pengfei slowly tugged his sleeve back down over the Longines. A minute ago it had felt like a crown. Now it felt like a trinket from a gift shop.

He had just sneered at Chen Xuan for being a low-level clerk. Meanwhile, Chen Xuan was drinking the price of that watch in tea every twelve days.

"Show-off," Zhang Pengfei muttered, his ears burning. "Playing humble, what a joke. It's a trick. Tomorrow at nine I'll prove it right in front of General Manager Sun."

"Supervisor Chen." Hu Dewei wiped the rim of the cup with his sleeve. "If you ever want the desk by the window, it's yours."

"Thanks, Manager Hu, but I like my corner. The morning sun hits my bun just right."

"Of course you do," Hu Dewei said very softly.

* * *

That evening, inside the supply closet, Ge Qinghe stood between two mop buckets with a black phone pressed to her ear. There was no trace of the gentle cleaner's smile on her face now.

She was Number Fourteen of the Black Pine Guard, a member of its thirty-person Inner Ring. She had nearly dropped her mop that morning the instant the scent of that tea drifted into the hallway.

"Number One, I recognize that batch," she said in a low voice. "It's from Hao Jingshan's private shelf. A hundred grams of it sat on your study table last spring."

On the other end of the line, Number One, head of the Black Pine Guard, let out a long breath.

Hao Jingshan belonged to the Black Pine Guard as well, holding a rank far higher than his modest tea stall suggested. He served the Boss in his own quiet way, selling him his finest leaves for thirty yuan and calling them leftovers.

"Go on."

"Eleven of our people were in the building today. They sent me forty-one messages. One of them asked: *Number Fourteen, does the tea put his cover at risk?*"

"What did you tell them?"

"That the tea is only tea. The real risk is an entire office floor gossiping about him."

"Does he realize?"

"He thanked me for a tea egg, Chief. And Manager Hu stayed on the phone for nine minutes after the Boss left. He called General Manager Sun."

"What did Sun Haoran say?"

"He's coming down to the twelfth floor in person tomorrow at nine for the recount."

Number One went silent. A cold, uneasy thought settled in Ge Qinghe's chest.

Nobody in the world was supposed to know who Chen Xuan really was. Yet one scratched thermos and a thirty-yuan paper bag had set a whole department arguing about him. And now the head of the entire company was coming down to the twelfth floor to taste the tea himself.

"Number One," she said after a beat. "What does a man like Sun Haoran want from a cup of that tea?"
