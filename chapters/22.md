# Chapter 22: Cheng Biao's Bad Afternoon

By half past three Sunday afternoon, the director's office at Old Street Station reeked of stale smoke. Station Director Cheng Biao crushed a cigarette into a full ashtray, jaw clenched tight.

For fifteen years, he had run Old Street Station like his own kingdom. Never had a family stayed as stubbornly quiet as the Chen household. Whenever Hongrui Real Estate needed a shopkeeper broken, Cheng Biao locked up the head of the house for disturbing public order and waited. Within six hours, relatives came running with red envelopes, tears, and signed transfer papers for Zhou Quan.

Yet Chen Mingde had been locked up since Friday night. Chen Jianguo came once Saturday morning before his son returned to Yunxi County, only to be brushed off. Since Saturday afternoon, nobody from Building 2 of Fuxing New Village had shown up, called, or sent a neighbor to beg.

Worse, Cheng Biao's phone had rung twice since noon. First Zhou Quan called, impatient about the slipping demolition schedule. Then Deng Yongchang called in a cold temper, pointing out that Yan Tiehu's thugs had gone strangely quiet since Saturday night while heavy trucks waited beneath the quarry road.

"Stubborn old mules," Cheng Biao muttered. He pulled a questioning form from his drawer and wrote Chen Jianguo's name and address. Without logging a case number, he slammed the red station stamp onto the corner.

He jabbed the intercom. "Zhao Kai. Tian Jiahao. Up here."

A minute later, two junior officers entered. Zhao Kai wore a lazy smirk; Tian Jiahao stood quietly behind him.

Cheng Biao slid the slip across his desk. "Take the patrol car to Building 2 of Fuxing New Village. Bring Chen Jianguo here to answer questions about Chen Mingde's case."

Zhao Kai glanced at the blank case-number line and grinned. "What if the old man argues?"

"Remind him that refusing makes things much harder for his younger brother inside," Cheng Biao snapped, eyes bloodshot.

"Understood, Director Cheng Biao," Zhao Kai said smoothly, pocketing the paper. "Bring his son too if he talks back?"

"Bring whoever gets in the way," Cheng Biao barked. "I want them downstairs before five."

Tian Jiahao hesitated. "Director Cheng Biao, should we log it at the front desk first?"

Cheng Biao glared. "Since when do you teach me how to run Old Street Station? Move."

Tian Jiahao lowered his eyes. "Yes, Director Cheng Biao."

Twenty minutes later, the white patrol car parked crookedly outside Building 2 at Fuxing New Village. Zhao Kai left the engine running as they strode into the courtyard.

"Watch the old man's knees shake when he sees the red stamp," Zhao Kai chuckled.

"Keep your voice down," Tian Jiahao muttered, eyeing the balconies. "Neighbors are watching."

"Let them watch."

Before Zhao Kai reached the first stair, a matte-black car with provincial-city plates stopped behind their patrol car, blocking it in.

Zhao Kai spun around. "Hey! You are blocking a police car. Move it!"

Three men in plain dark gray jackets stepped out. The silver-haired lead man, eyes calm as still water, carried a slim black folder while his two assistants held tablets.

"Stop right where you are," the silver-haired man said, voice unhurried yet heavy with authority.

Zhao Kai's smirk faltered, though he puffed out his chest. "Who are you? We are on official duty from Old Street Station."

The man walked to the gate and opened a dark red leather ID stamped in gold. "Shen Zhigang. City Discipline Inspection Commission, Third Supervision Office. Show me your papers."

Tian Jiahao went stiff, all color draining from his face. Zhao Kai's bold act collapsed instantly. Swallowing hard, he handed over the stamped slip with shaky fingers.

Shen Zhigang scanned it, his voice turning to ice. "A questioning form for an ordinary citizen on a Sunday afternoon, with no city case number, no legal approval, and no link to an open criminal case."

"Inspector Shen Zhigang, Director Cheng Biao just wanted routine questions," Zhao Kai stammered, sweating. "Just a friendly talk."

"A friendly talk that threatens a prisoner's family?" Shen Zhigang asked flatly. "Records show Chen Mingde's detention was never sent to the provincial system. Who approved holding a citizen for forty-eight hours off the books?"

Zhao Kai's mouth opened without a sound. Tian Jiahao stared at the concrete, jaw locked.

An assistant photographed the bad form and noted the patrol car's plate.

"Officer Zhao Kai, Officer Tian Jiahao," Shen Zhigang said evenly, handing the sheet back. "This order is invalid and stopped on the spot. You will not climb those stairs or contact Chen Jianguo's family. Tell Station Director Cheng Biao our office has opened a surprise review of every detention and demolition record from the past three years. Have those files on his desk by eight tomorrow."

Zhao Kai turned gray, taking the slip with shaking hands. "Yes, Inspector Shen Zhigang. We are leaving now."

Neither officer looked back. They hurried to their car, waited with burning ears while the black car backed aside, and sped away. Shen Zhigang returned to his car without knocking on a single door. The stop took under four minutes, triggered quietly by a rule-breaking report White Guard dropped into the inspection channel two hours earlier.

Upstairs, behind the third-floor lace curtain, Chen Xuan held a warm mug of water and watched the black car glide away.

In the kitchen, Liu Meifang peeled apples. At the table, Chen Jianguo read the county paper in peace, never having gone near the gate or police station.

"Chen Xuan," Liu Meifang called gently. "What are you watching? Come eat fruit."

"Just a car turning around, Mom," Chen Xuan replied, dropping the curtain. "Coming."

Two kilometers away at Old Street Station, the receiver shook in Cheng Biao's damp hand as Zhao Kai finished reporting.

"Say that name again," Cheng Biao rasped, chest tightening like a vise.

"Shen Zhigang, Director," Zhao Kai whispered in panic. "City Discipline Inspection, Third Supervision Office. He photographed the form and ordered three years of detention and demolition files by eight tomorrow."

"Three years of files?" Cheng Biao's voice cracked. "How did Shen Zhigang know you were at Fuxing New Village before you touched the stairs? Who leaked it?"

"Nobody knew, Director," Zhao Kai stammered. "He was already waiting. He even knew Chen Mingde's detention was never uploaded."

Cheng Biao's throat went bone dry. He slammed the receiver down hard enough to crack the plastic. For the first time in fifteen years of ruling Old Street, Cheng Biao felt an airtight wall closing around him, and he could not see the hands pushing it inward.
