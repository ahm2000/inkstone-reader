# Chapter 22: Cheng Biao's Bad Afternoon

By half past three on Sunday afternoon, the director's office at Old Street Station reeked of stale tobacco. Station Director Cheng Biao crushed a cigarette into an overflowing ashtray, his jaw clenched tight.

For fifteen years, he had run Old Street Station like a private kingdom, and never once had a family stayed as infuriatingly silent as the Chen household. Whenever Hongrui Real Estate needed a stubborn shopkeeper broken, Cheng Biao detained the household head for obstructing public order and waited. Within six hours, relatives always came running with red envelopes, tears, and signed transfer papers for Zhou Quan.

Yet Chen Mingde had been locked up since Friday night. Chen Jianguo had come to the front desk once Saturday morning before his son returned to Yunxi County, only to be turned away. Since Saturday afternoon, nobody from Building 2 of Fuxing New Village had appeared, called, or sent a neighbor to beg.

Worse, Cheng Biao's private phone had rung twice since noon. First Zhou Quan called, sharp with impatience over the slipping demolition schedule. Then Deng Yongchang called with cold irritation, noting that Yan Tiehu's street enforcers had gone strangely quiet since Saturday night while heavy trucks waited to move beneath the quarry road after dark.

"Stubborn old mules," Cheng Biao muttered. He strode to his desk, pulled an informal inquiry summons pad from the drawer, and scrawled Chen Jianguo's name and address across the lines. Without logging a case number in the municipal registry, he slammed the station stamp onto the corner.

He jabbed the intercom. "Zhao Kai. Xu Wenbin. Up here."

A minute later, two junior officers stepped inside. Zhao Kai wore a lazy smirk; Xu Wenbin stood quietly behind him.

Cheng Biao slid the stamped slip across the glass. "Take the white sedan to Building 2 of Fuxing New Village. Invite Chen Jianguo here to assist with an inquiry into Chen Mingde's case."

Zhao Kai glanced at the blank registry line and grinned. "What if the old man argues at the door?"

"Remind him that refusing a summons makes things much harder for his younger brother inside," Cheng Biao snapped, eyes bloodshot.

"Understood, Director Cheng Biao," Zhao Kai said smoothly, pocketing the slip. "Should we bring his son along too if he talks back?"

"Bring whoever gets in the way," Cheng Biao barked. "I want them sitting downstairs before five."

Xu Wenbin hesitated, eyeing the slip. "Director Cheng Biao, should we log it at the duty desk first?"

Cheng Biao glared at him. "Since when do you teach me how to run Old Street Station? Move."

Xu Wenbin stiffened and lowered his eyes. "Yes, Director Cheng Biao."

Twenty minutes later, the white patrol sedan parked crookedly outside the rusted iron gate of Building 2 at Fuxing New Village. Zhao Kai left the engine idling as he and Xu Wenbin strode into the courtyard.

"Watch the old man's knees buckle the second he sees the red stamp," Zhao Kai chuckled loudly.

"Keep your voice down," Xu Wenbin muttered, glancing up at the balconies. "Neighbors are watching."

"Let them watch."

Before Zhao Kai's shoe touched the first stairwell step, a matte-black municipal sedan with provincial-city plates glided to a stop behind their patrol car, blocking its exit.

Zhao Kai frowned and spun around. "Hey! You are blocking a police vehicle. Move it!"

The rear doors opened. Three men in plain dark gray jackets stepped out. The lead man, silver at his temples and eyes calm as still water, carried a slim black portfolio while his two companions held recording tablets.

"Stop where you are," the silver-haired man said, his voice unhurried yet carrying unmistakable institutional weight.

Zhao Kai's smirk faltered, though he puffed out his chest. "Who are you? We are on official duty from Old Street Station."

The man walked to the gate and opened a dark red leather credential booklet stamped in gold. "Shen Zhigang. Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission, Third Supervision Office. Show me your authorization paperwork for this residence."

Xu Wenbin went rigid, color draining from his face. Zhao Kai's bravado collapsed in a heartbeat. He swallowed hard and handed over the hastily stamped slip with unsteady fingers.

Shen Zhigang scanned it for three seconds, his tone turning crisp as frost. "An informal inquiry summons for a civilian witness, issued on a Sunday afternoon, lacking a municipal registry number, prosecutorial authorization, or any documented nexus to an approved criminal file."

"Inspector Shen Zhigang, Director Cheng Biao just wanted a few routine questions," Zhao Kai stammered, sweat beading on his forehead. "Only a friendly inquiry."

"A friendly inquiry threatening a detainee's family?" Shen Zhigang asked flatly. "Our audit logs show Chen Mingde's administrative detention review was never uploaded to the provincial supervision network. Who authorized holding a civilian forty-eight hours off the ledger?"

Zhao Kai's mouth opened and shut without a sound. Beside him, Xu Wenbin stared fixedly at the concrete, his jaw locked.

Behind Shen Zhigang, an assistant photographed the defective summons and logged the patrol car's plate.

"Officer Zhao Kai, Officer Xu Wenbin," Shen Zhigang said evenly, returning the sheet. "This summons is procedurally invalid and is halted on the spot. You will not climb those stairs. You will not contact Chen Jianguo or any member of his household. Return to Old Street Station and inform Station Director Cheng Biao that our office has opened an unscheduled compliance audit covering every administrative detention and demolition-related ledger signed over the past three years. Have those files on his desk by eight tomorrow morning."

Zhao Kai's face turned wet ash. His fingers shook as he took the slip. "Yes, Inspector Shen Zhigang. We are leaving right now."

Neither officer looked back. They hurried to their sedan, waited with burning ears while the black car backed up, and fled down the lane. Shen Zhigang nodded to his assistants and stepped back into his sedan without knocking on a single door. The entire intervention took under four minutes, triggered silently through a verified procedural-violation filing dropped into the Discipline Inspection backchannel by Black Pine Guard's compliance unit two hours earlier.

Upstairs, behind the parted lace curtain of the third-floor window, Chen Xuan stood holding a warm mug of water, watching the black sedan glide out of the lane.

In the kitchen behind him, Liu Meifang peeled apples. At the dining table, Chen Jianguo read the county newspaper in complete peace, never once having stepped near the gate or the police station.

"Chen Xuan," Liu Meifang called gently. "What are you watching down there? Come eat some fruit."

"Just a car turning around, Mom," Chen Xuan replied, letting the curtain fall back. "Coming."

Two kilometers away at Old Street Station, the receiver shook in Cheng Biao's damp palm as Zhao Kai's breathless voice finished recounting what had happened.

"Say that name again," Cheng Biao rasped, his chest tightening like a vise.

"Shen Zhigang, Director," Zhao Kai whispered frantically. "Municipal Discipline Inspection, Third Supervision Office. He photographed the summons and ordered three years of detention and demolition ledgers on your desk by eight tomorrow."

"Three years of ledgers?" Cheng Biao's voice cracked. "How did Shen Zhigang know you were at Fuxing New Village before you even touched the stairs? Did someone here leak it?"

"I swear nobody knew, Director," Zhao Kai stammered, breathing raggedly. "He was already waiting. He even knew Chen Mingde's detention was never uploaded."

Cheng Biao's throat went bone dry. He slammed the receiver onto the cradle so hard the plastic cracked across the center. His trembling palm stayed pressed against the broken phone. For the first time in fifteen years of ruling Old Street, Cheng Biao felt an invisible, airtight wall closing around him from every direction, and he could not see the hands pushing it inward.
