# Chapter 22: The Call at Night

Late that evening, a phone began to ring on the folding table beside a basket of hot steamed buns in Room 601 of Building 2, Fuxing New Village. It belonged to Wu Lanying, Chen Mingde's wife. Everyone around the table froze.

The screen showed Dong Haisheng's name.

"Don't answer that crook!" Chen Jianguo barked.

Wu Lanying answered anyway, her fingers trembling. Her thumb accidentally brushed the speaker icon, and a smooth voice filled the room.

"Wu Lanying. Good evening. Having dinner? Good, good. I'm only calling to help."

Behind the voice came a thick laugh Chen Jianguo recognized immediately. It belonged to Cheng Biao.

Dong Haisheng, chairman of Hongrui Real Estate, spoke in a gentle, unhurried tone. "Your husband is in a tough spot. Ten days, maybe fifteen. Your daughter in Wuhan will have to carry that police record into every job interview for the rest of her life. Lucky for you, I have friends. Sign the hardware shop over to Hongrui tonight for two hundred twenty-eight thousand yuan, and Chen Mingde eats breakfast at your table tomorrow morning. Until then, he's our guest. Guests get looked after, if you catch my meaning."

Wu Lanying's face went pale. Under the table, she clutched Chen Xuan's hand.

His hand was warm and steady as he closed his fingers around hers.

That hardware shop was the family's entire livelihood, and Dong Haisheng was offering less than half of what it was worth.

"One more thing, Wu Lanying." Dong Haisheng's voice dropped, almost affectionate. "Your brother-in-law lives on the sixth floor of Building 2. No elevator, and the stairwell light has been broken since spring. His wife goes out for vegetables at six every morning. I even know which bus your nephew takes to work. I'm not threatening anyone. I'm just saying a family with so many people out on the road ought to want things settled cleanly. Sign by nine tomorrow morning. After nine, the offer disappears, and your husband's file goes to the prosecutor as a repeat offense."

Liu Meifang's soup ladle clattered to the floor.

Chen Xuan's younger brother, Chen Hao, shot to his feet so fast his chair tipped backward.

A cold weight settled in Chen Xuan's chest, though his expression stayed completely calm.

"Who's there?" Dong Haisheng asked.

"Chen Xuan. His nephew."

"Ah, the office clerk. Young man, you strike me as sensible. Tell your aunt to sign."

Chen Xuan's voice came out small and shaky, like a scared nephew's. "If anyone in my family gets so much as a scratch, you won't have a tomorrow to worry about."

Dong Haisheng gave a short, amused laugh. "Brave boy. Nine o'clock sharp." The line went dead.

Chen Hao gave a tight, angry laugh. "Big Brother, your voice was shaking. He laughed at you."

"Big Brother." Chen Yuan was not joking. Her hand trembled slightly against the tabletop. "He knew what time Mom buys vegetables. He knew about the stairs."

"I know," Chen Xuan said softly, placing a reassuring hand on her shoulder. "Don't worry, we'll handle it. Dad, let's finish dinner before the soup gets cold."

"This is grown-up business," Chen Jianguo muttered, though his own voice was unsteady. "Just stay out of it and eat."

Liu Meifang bent down and snatched up her ladle. "Let him try coming up our stairs." She brandished it once.

Chen Xuan's phone buzzed on the table. The caller ID read only: Work.

"Work," Chen Xuan said, stepping out onto the balcony and sliding the glass door shut behind him.

"At this hour? Eat your dinner first." Liu Meifang called after him.

"Boss."

It was Number One, and for the first time in his life he sounded close to shouting.

"I heard every word of that call. And Niu Dali's crew has left their van with iron pipes, heading straight for the tailor's shop. Two hundred and fourteen of us are already in position, Boss, and the Special Police are on call. Give me an order, Boss."

"Number One." Chen Xuan's voice dropped low and flat. "Three things. First, that crew does not lay a finger on Yu Changshun or his granddaughter tonight. Second, starting right now, put one of our men on my parents' stairwell, one near my aunt's door, and one near my cousin in Wuhan. Quietly, around the clock. Third, archive that call recording. Dong Haisheng just named my father's building, my mother's morning routine, and my cousin's city. Put it straight into his file."

"Yes, Boss."

When Chen Xuan stepped back inside, Chen Hao was glued to the peephole.

"Big Brother, the stairwell outside just got so bright I need sunglasses. And there's a guy in a property-management vest sitting on a folding stool with a thermos of tea. He says nobody's slipping on his watch."

"Really?" Chen Xuan said mildly. "That's lucky."

"The nicest repairman in the whole county." Liu Meifang declared. "Chen Xuan, you're too skinny. Drink your soup."

Chen Yuan studied her brother for a long moment. He simply picked up his spoon and drank.

* * *

Two black Special Police vans idled at the edge of the parking lot behind People's Hospital. Eighteen officers in tactical gear leaned against the doors.

They belonged to the Qingyan City Special Police Brigade, just returning from a joint drill, tired and hungry. Number One had asked for them before Dong Haisheng ever called, so they were already waiting. Their commander, Team Leader Bao Zhen, was forty-one, with a jaw like a brick. He was not a man who took orders from strangers in gray jackets.

Number Nine, the man in the gray jacket from the bus, walked calmly out of the shadows toward him. Number Nine was a Black Pine Guard operative.

"Team Leader Bao?"

"Who are you?"

"A traveler," Number Nine said. It was the code phrase. "Number Nine."

He said it as casually as someone giving his name at a bus stop.

Then Number Nine pulled something no larger than a matchbox from his pocket and placed it in Bao Zhen's palm.

It was a heavy rectangle of black metal. A slender pine branch was engraved along the edge, and a plain number 9 was stamped across its face.

Bao Zhen had only heard rumors of it twice in twenty years, whispered in barracks at two in the morning to spook new recruits. The Black Pine Badge, the highest authority of a hidden guard. Any elite unit shown that badge was bound to obey its bearer immediately, without question, even above its own commanding officers.

Bao Zhen froze. Without a conscious thought, his right hand snapped up into a rigid salute.

"Yes, sir," he said, his voice hoarse.

His deputy, Fu Jian, stared at the salute and whispered, "Team Leader? Who is that?"

Bao Zhen murmured out of the corner of his mouth, arm still locked in place. "Follow every order he gives. Ask nothing."

Number Nine spoke so mildly it almost sounded polite. "Team Leader, I need a sweep. Anti-gang, standard and unannounced. Hongrui's security crew on Old Street, all eight men and whatever van they use. Move in twenty minutes. Bring them in clean. No broken bones unless they swing first. No phone calls tonight. Appreciate it."

"Understood."

Number Nine turned and vanished back into the dark. Bao Zhen lowered his hand and realized his palm was slick with sweat.

"Gear up. We're running a random sweep."

Twenty minutes later on Old Street, Niu Dali had one fist twisted in Han Guifu's collar and the other cocked back to strike.

Then the entire lane flooded with blinding white headlights from both ends at once.

Black vans skidded to a halt over the flagstones, and a dozen officers in helmets poured out.

"City Special Police! Hands against the wall!"

Niu Dali instantly released Han Guifu, who dropped heavily onto his own doorstep with his mouth hanging open.

"What the hell is this?" Niu Dali barked.

A Special Police officer pinned him face-first against the tailor's brick wall and kicked his boots apart.

"Do you know who I am?" Niu Dali yelled into the masonry, panic cracking his voice. "I'm with Hongrui. Director Cheng Biao of this station is my boss. Call him!"

"No phone calls." Bao Zhen walked past him without even glancing over.

Yu Changshun stood frozen in his doorway with Yu Ning's face buried against his hip, unable to close his mouth.

"Is this a raid?" he whispered.

A Special Police officer glanced at the splintered wooden signboard and answered gently, "Just a routine check, sir."
