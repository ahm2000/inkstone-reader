# Chapter 22: Cheng Biao's Bad Afternoon

By half past three on Sunday afternoon, the second-floor director's office at Old Street Station hung thick with stale tobacco smoke. Station Director Cheng Biao stood beside his filing cabinet, crushing a cigarette into an overflowing ashtray. Outside, the concrete courtyard lay quiet under the slanting sun.

Cheng Biao's jaw was clenched tight. For fifteen years, he had run Old Street Station like a private fiefdom, and never once had he dealt with a family as infuriatingly silent as the Chen family.

Whenever Hongrui Real Estate needed a stubborn shopkeeper softened up, Cheng Biao's officers detained the household head for obstructing public order and waited. Within six hours, relatives always came running with red envelopes, tearful pleas, and signed transfer papers for Zhou Quan.

Yet Chen Mingde had been locked inside since Friday night. Chen Jianguo had come to the front desk once on Saturday morning before his son returned to Yunxi County, only to be turned away by the duty clerk. Since Saturday afternoon, nobody from Building 2 of Fuxing New Village had appeared at the gate, called the station landline, or sent a neighbor to negotiate.

Worse still, Cheng Biao's private phone had rung twice since noon. First Zhou Quan from Hongrui Real Estate called, impatient over the slipping demolition schedule. Then Deng Yongchang called with cold irritation, noting that Yan Tiehu's street enforcers had gone strangely quiet since the previous night while heavy trucks waited to move beneath the quarry road after dark.

"Stubborn old mules," Cheng Biao muttered. He strode to his wooden desk, pulled an informal inquiry summons pad from the drawer, and scrawled Chen Jianguo's name and address across the blank lines. He pressed the station rubber stamp onto the bottom corner without logging a case number into the municipal electronic registry.

He tapped the intercom button. "Zhao Kai. Xu Wenbin. Come up here."

A minute later, two junior officers knocked and stepped inside. Zhao Kai wore a lazy smirk born of easy afternoons collecting favors along Old Street, while Xu Wenbin stood quietly behind him.

Cheng Biao slid the stamped slip across the glass desktop. "Take the white sedan to Building 2 of Fuxing New Village. Invite Chen Jianguo to the station to assist with an inquiry into Chen Mingde's case. If he argues, remind him that refusing a summons makes things much harder for his younger brother inside."

"Understood, Director Cheng Biao," Zhao Kai said smoothly, tucking the paper into his breast pocket. "Should we bring his son along too if he talks back?"

"Bring whoever gets in the way," Cheng Biao snapped. "I want them sitting downstairs before five."

Twenty minutes later, the white patrol sedan turned off the county avenue into the narrow lane leading to Fuxing New Village. Old red-brick apartment blocks stood quietly in the late afternoon sun. Zhao Kai parked crookedly outside the rusted iron courtyard gate of Building 2, leaving the engine ticking as he and Xu Wenbin stepped out.

They adjusted their caps and strode through the open iron gate toward the concrete stairwell of the eastern unit, not bothering to lower their voices.

"Watch the old man fold the second he sees the stamp," Zhao Kai grinned.

Before Zhao Kai's shoe touched the first concrete step, the quiet hum of a sedan sounded from the lane behind them. A matte-black municipal car with clean provincial-city plates glided to a stop directly behind the patrol vehicle, blocking its exit.

Zhao Kai frowned and turned. "Who is that? Tell them to move."

The rear doors opened together. Three men in plain dark gray jackets stepped onto the asphalt. The lead man, in his late forties with silver at his temples and eyes calm as still water, carried a slim black leather portfolio. His two companions held compact recording tablets and credential folders.

"Stop where you are," the silver-haired man said, his voice unhurried yet carrying unmistakable institutional weight.

Zhao Kai's smirk faltered. "Who are you? We are on official duty from Old Street Station."

The man walked steadily to the courtyard gate and opened a dark red leather credential booklet embossed in gold. "Shen Zhigang. Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission, Third Supervision Office. Show me your authorization paperwork for the action you are attempting at this residence."

Xu Wenbin stiffened immediately, his hand dropping from his belt. Zhao Kai swallowed hard, pulled the hastily stamped slip from his pocket, and handed it over with unsteady fingers.

Shen Zhigang glanced at the sheet for three seconds, his tone turning crisp as frost. "An informal inquiry summons for a civilian witness, issued on a Sunday afternoon, lacking a municipal registry filing number, prosecutorial authorization, or any documented nexus to an approved criminal file. Furthermore, our audit logs show Chen Mingde's administrative detention review at Old Street Station was never uploaded to the provincial supervision network."

Behind Shen Zhigang, an assistant photographed the defective summons and logged the patrol car's plate into his tablet.

"Officer Zhao Kai, Officer Xu Wenbin," Shen Zhigang said evenly, returning the sheet. "This summons is procedurally invalid and is halted on the spot. You will not climb those stairs. You will not contact Chen Jianguo or any member of his household. Return to Old Street Station immediately and inform Station Director Cheng Biao that our office has opened an unscheduled compliance audit covering every administrative detention and demolition-related ledger signed at Old Street Station over the past three years. Have those files on his desk by eight tomorrow morning."

Zhao Kai's face turned the color of wet ash. "Yes, Inspector Shen Zhigang. We are leaving right now."

Neither officer glanced back at the stairwell. They hurried to their sedan, waited with burning ears while the black municipal car backed up to let them out, and fled down the lane. Shen Zhigang watched them turn the corner, nodded to his assistants, and stepped back into his sedan without knocking on a single door. The entire intervention lasted less than four minutes, triggered silently through a verified procedural-violation filing dropped into the municipal Discipline Inspection legal-audit backchannel by Black Pine Guard's compliance unit two hours earlier.

Upstairs, behind the parted lace curtain of the third-floor window, Chen Xuan stood holding a warm mug of water, watching the black sedan glide out of the lane until Sunday stillness returned.

Behind him in the kitchen, Liu Meifang peeled apples into a ceramic bowl. At the dining table, Chen Jianguo sat wearing his reading glasses, turning the pages of the county newspaper in complete peace, never once having stepped near the gate or the police station.

"Chen Xuan," Liu Meifang called gently. "Come eat some fruit before dinner."

"Coming, Mom," Chen Xuan replied, letting the curtain fall back into place.

Two kilometers away at Old Street Station, the landline receiver shook in Cheng Biao's damp palm as Zhao Kai's trembling voice finished recounting what had happened at Building 2.

"Municipal Discipline Inspection?" Cheng Biao rasped, his chest tightening like an iron vise. "Three years of case ledgers? How did Shen Zhigang know you were at Fuxing New Village before you even climbed the stairs?"

Zhao Kai had no answer, only ragged breathing.

Cheng Biao slammed the receiver down onto the cradle so hard the plastic housing cracked across the center. His hand stayed pressed against the broken phone, trembling slightly as the dropping sun threw long dark bars across his desk. For the first time in fifteen years of ruling Old Street, Cheng Biao felt an invisible, airtight wall closing around him from every direction, and he could not even see the hands pushing it inward.
