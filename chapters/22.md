# Chapter 22: The Midnight Filing

In the marble lobby of the Municipal Discipline Commission, a lone night clerk named Qian Yu sat behind a long desk with a cup of instant noodles and a crossword puzzle. For two years of night shifts, he had dropped every late complaint into a drawer to wait for Monday morning.

A man in a tailored gray suit crossed the lobby with unhurried grace. Qian Yu shot to his feet so quickly that broth splashed onto his tie.

Number Eleven spoke pleasantly. "Please sit and finish your dinner. I'd hate to ruin your tie."

"No, no, sir. How can I help you?"

"I'm here to file a complaint."

Number Eleven set a thin black folder on the counter with both hands and slid it forward with a polite nod.

"Nineteen pages, all in order. I'll need a signed receipt, an official stamp, and the exact time."

Qian Yu stopped mid-chew as his eyes fell on the names circled in red. Three companies, followed by two officials.

Cheng Biao, head of public order for the county police.

Lu Wenzhe, Executive Deputy Magistrate of Yunxi County.

Qian Yu's voice dropped to a whisper. "Sir, is this real?"

"I'm afraid it's entirely real."

Number Eleven offered a polite smile that made the young clerk's stomach drop.

"If I were you, I'd wake someone up."

Qian Yu glanced at the wall clock: 11:44 p.m.

He reached for the phone.

Whose door was he about to knock on at midnight?

* * *

Qian Yu stamped the receipt at 11:44 and woke the night duty chief. The chief began reading the nineteen pages in his slippers and was wide awake by page four.

At three in the morning, he woke Mai Zhenhua, Police Director of Yunxi, because the folder named one of Mai Zhenhua's own men. Mai Zhenhua said "Yes" eleven times in a row, then sat on the edge of his bed with his feet gone numb.

At midnight, Number Twenty-three had delivered beef noodles with an extra egg and a gray blanket to the family window. Chen Mingde had eaten every last strand, his eyes brimming quietly as he finished the egg.

Two streets away, Number Ninety-five, under cover as head nurse, happened to run into Su Min, Wei Kun's wife. By dawn, a free hospital bed was waiting for her.

At five in the morning, Chen Xuan walked to the police station alone with his thermos and sat on the front steps. He could not sleep, and the steps felt closer to his uncle than his bed. His mother would arrive at 6:35 with soup.

* * *

Inside, Lei Bo slept on the reception bench with his cap pulled over his face.

Wei Kun had not slept at all. He sat behind the glass with a cold cup of tea and one honest detention notice, waiting for the storm to break.

A plain gray sedan pulled up to the steps with no flag and no siren.

The man who stepped out was short and stocky, wearing a dark jacket.

He pushed open the glass door. From his stool in the corner, Number Thirty-four, the records clerk, looked up, and his stapler froze mid-press.

"Director Mai," Number Thirty-four said softly.

The words landed like a fire alarm.

Lei Bo tumbled off the bench and scrambled upright into a salute.

"Where is Director Cheng?"

Cheng Biao hurried out of the back corridor in his shirtsleeves, pulling on his jacket with a toothpick still between his teeth.

"Director Mai! You're here so early! If I'd known, I would have waited at the gate!"

"Is the Chen Mingde case yours?"

"Just a minor scuffle, Director. A driver, an old shopkeeper, a few bruises."

Mai Zhenhua pulled a slip of paper from his pocket.

"The driver's injury note is from Ankang Private Clinic. It's stamped 1:50. The street camera shows he fell at 2:03. Tell me, Director Cheng, how does a man get treated thirteen minutes before he falls?"

The lobby went so quiet that the hum of the vending machine sounded rude.

Cheng Biao swallowed. "Maybe the clocks didn't match, Director."

"The county Party Committee and the city Discipline Commission have made their decision. You are suspended from every post, effective immediately. Hand over your ID and your keys."

The toothpick dropped from Cheng Biao's mouth and rolled to the toe of Mai Zhenhua's shoe.

Cheng Biao turned hoarsely. "Lei Bo. Tell the Director who runs this station."

Lei Bo stared up at the ceiling, then down at his own cap.

"Director Cheng?" Lei Bo looked genuinely puzzled. "I hardly know him!"

Cheng Biao dropped his keys into Mai Zhenhua's palm. Two city officers stepped forward and escorted him toward the door.

Outside on the front steps sat a young man in worn sneakers, holding a green thermos on his knees.

Chen Xuan stood up politely and stepped aside to let the officers pass. He gave a quiet nod. "Morning, Director Cheng."

Cheng Biao's footsteps faltered. He stared at the ordinary clerk standing calmly in the early light, a sudden cold suspicion flaring in his chest.

The officers nudged him forward. Passing Chen Xuan, Cheng Biao leaned in and hissed under his breath, "Don't celebrate too soon, clerk. Your whole family is in Dong Haisheng's book. I'm not the one you should be worried about."

Chen Xuan only offered a faint, polite nod and stepped back to wait for his uncle.

Inside, Mai Zhenhua called out, "Wei Kun."

Wei Kun stepped out from behind the glass and stood at attention.

"Yes, Director."

"Bring me the key to the detention cell."

Wei Kun's knees nearly gave out. He had braced himself for punishment, not trust.
