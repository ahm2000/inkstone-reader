# Chapter 81: The Interrogation of Vice Minister He Guoliang

Blue cigarette smoke hung in layered sheets beneath the low fluorescent tube of the sealed Central Commission for Discipline Inspection interrogation room in the capital. By Monday afternoon, the ashtray between Gao Zhengyi and Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang already held six crushed filters.

He Guoliang sat upright in the bolted steel chair, his collar damp with sweat despite the cold hum of the vent. He clasped his hands on the table as if presiding over a grid-capacity review. Only the faint tremor in his left thumb betrayed him.

"Gao Zhengyi," He Guoliang said, forcing a thin, practiced smile, "I have served the Energy Administration for thirty-one years. If there are irregularities in provincial substation procurement, my ministry will cooperate with an audit. Locking a vice minister in a basement room over unverified rumors from Lanshui is irregular."

Gao Zhengyi did not look up from the manila folder. He tapped a fresh cigarette against the metal table, lit it with a dull steel lighter, and exhaled a long plume straight across the gap between them.

"Irregular," Gao Zhengyi repeated, his voice flat. "You think you are sitting here because of a provincial substation audit."

He Guoliang's jaw tightened. "Lin Zhengde in Lanshui has always been reckless. If he used my name to push through a land variance for Shengyuan Capital, that is his crime, not mine. I signed framework approvals for grid upgrades. Every vice minister in the capital signs fifty of those a quarter."

"I have not asked you about Lin Zhengde yet," Gao Zhengyi said. He finally raised his bloodshot, razor-sharp eyes. "I am asking you about Zurich."

For half a second, He Guoliang froze. His pupils shrank to pinpricks, and his clasped fingers pressed hard enough to turn his knuckles white.

"Zurich?" He Guoliang laughed, a dry, brittle sound that died halfway down his throat. "Gao Zhengyi, I have not stepped foot in Switzerland since the 2016 energy forum. Pull my diplomatic passport from Foreign Affairs right now."

"I do not care where your shoes walked," Gao Zhengyi said. He turned the folder around and slid a single printed sheet across the scratched laminate. "Read line four."

He Guoliang stared at Gao Zhengyi's face, searching for a bluff. Finding only cold patience, he lowered his gaze to the paper.

There were no photographs, no bank logos, and no memos. Only twelve lines of forty-character alphanumeric strings, paired with date stamps stretching back nineteen years, bearer-bond tranche identifiers, and settlement custody codes.

The color drained from He Guoliang's face in a single rush. His breath caught audibly in his chest.

"Where," he whispered, his voice cracking, "where did your commission get this?"

"Answer the question, He Guoliang," Gao Zhengyi said softly, taking another slow drag. "Line four. 740 million Swiss francs in unregistered bearer paper, issued through a blind custodial trust in Zurich in November 2011. Three days after you approved the sole-source turbine tender for the northern corridor."

He Guoliang pulled his hands off the table and hid them in his lap, where they began to shake violently. "This is fabricated," he said, though his voice had lost all volume. "Those strings do not exist in any domestic database. Even the Swiss clearing desk splits the custody hash across three offline ledgers. Nobody in the capital has the decryption key to match those tranche numbers to domestic tenders."

"You just confirmed the structure of the custody hash," Gao Zhengyi noted without blinking. "Keep talking."

Sweat rolled from He Guoliang's forehead into his eyebrows. He wiped it away with a trembling wrist, his eyes darting wildly toward the blank gray wall behind Gao Zhengyi, as if trying to see through the concrete to whoever had handed over an impossible dossier.

"It was not your people," He Guoliang muttered, terror overtaking calculation. "State Security could not touch those vaults. Finance could not touch them. Only one archive on earth could pull raw bearer-bond strings from Zurich without tripping the clearinghouse alarm."

Gao Zhengyi leaned forward an inch, resting his forearms on the table edge. "You have thirty seconds before I close this folder and sign the referral for treason alongside corruption. Lines nine through twelve match every offshore disbursement to a rare-earth quota override or a high-voltage transformer contract bearing your chop. Your son's accounts in Singapore were frozen at eleven this morning. Decide what you want left of your name."

He Guoliang's chest heaved. A ragged sob tore out of his throat, and he slumped forward, his palms slapping flat against the cold laminate.

"I did not build the channel!" He Guoliang choked out, spittle flying from his lips as his composure disintegrated completely. "I was only a vice director in 2007 when they brought me in! They swore the offshore custody chain sat behind three layers of European sovereign immunity!"

"Who brought you in?" Gao Zhengyi asked, his tone dropping an octave. "Full names. Titles. Dates."

He Guoliang swallowed hard, his Adam's apple bobbing frantically. "Cao Wenhui," he gasped. "Former president of Huaxia Development Bank before he retired in 2018. He controlled the cross-border letters of credit that masked the turbine rebates as equipment-leasing deposits."

Gao Zhengyi's pen moved steadily across a lined pad. "Who else?"

"Zheng Rongkui," He Guoliang blurted, words spilling in a panicked torrent. "Retired president of the National Agricultural Credit Bank. And Pan Desheng, who retired from the Industrial Clearing Bank four years ago. The three of them sat on the legacy credit committee together. Whenever a provincial grid project or mining concession needed to move 10 billion yuan off the domestic balance sheet without central-bank flags, Cao Wenhui wrote the guarantee, Zheng Rongkui routed settlement through regional rural-credit shells, and Pan Desheng cleared the foreign-exchange swap."

Gao Zhengyi tapped his cigarette ash into the overflowing tray. "Three retired state-bank presidents," he said evenly. "And who held the other end in Switzerland?"

He Guoliang shuddered, hugging his own ribs. "A man in Geneva. Lukas Weber. He works out of an unmarked office near Rue du Rhone as a maritime-insurance arbitrator. We never spoke to the Northern Ledger principals directly. Everything went through Lukas Weber. He received domestic swap confirmations from Pan Desheng's clerks, converted them into physical bearer tranches in Zurich, and returned the forty-character receipt hashes."

"How did Lukas Weber verify your identity?" Gao Zhengyi pressed.

"Encrypted satellite line once a quarter," He Guoliang whispered, staring blankly at the sheet that had ended his life. "I read him the first eight characters of my old ministry badge and the invoice code from the latest grid tender. He never raised his voice. He just told me which provincial company to clear next, and which vice governor to protect."

"Which vice governors?" Gao Zhengyi demanded.

"Lin Zhengde in Lanshui," He Guoliang said, tears of dread blurring his eyes. "And four others across the southern and western corridors. I will write every name, every account, every trust. Just tell me who opened the Zurich ledger, Gao Zhengyi. If Lukas Weber learns those strings are on your desk, the retired bank presidents will not be the only ones who vanish before sunrise."

Gao Zhengyi crushed his cigarette out, stood up, and snapped the folder shut.

"Lukas Weber will not be making any calls tonight, He Guoliang," Gao Zhengyi said coldly. "Write down every name. Leave out a single clerk, and I will come back with the second folder."

He walked out the steel door, leaving He Guoliang hunched over the yellow notepad, his shaking hand smearing ink across the first line as the room filled with the quiet sound of a broken man's weeping.
