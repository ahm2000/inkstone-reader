# Chapter 81: The Interrogation of Vice Minister He Guoliang

Blue cigarette smoke hung low under the bright ceiling light of the basement questioning room in the capital. By Monday afternoon, the ashtray between Gao Zhengyi and Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang already held six crushed cigarette butts.

He Guoliang sat up straight in the bolted steel chair. His shirt collar was damp with sweat even though cold air hummed from the vent. He folded his hands on the table as if he were running a routine power grid meeting. Only a faint shake in his left thumb gave away his fear.

"Gao Zhengyi," He Guoliang said, forcing a thin, practiced smile. "I have worked at the Energy Administration for thirty-one years. If there are problems with provincial power station deals, my office will help with a full check. Locking a vice minister in a basement room over wild rumors from Lanshui is completely out of line."

Gao Zhengyi did not look up from the brown folder in front of him. He tapped a fresh cigarette on the metal table, lit it with a dull steel lighter, and blew a long cloud of smoke straight across the gap between them.

"Out of line," Gao Zhengyi repeated, his voice flat. "You think you are sitting here over a local power station check."

He Guoliang's jaw tightened. "Lin Zhengde in Lanshui has always been reckless. If he used my name to push through a special land permit for Shengyuan Capital, that is his crime, not mine. I signed routine power grid approvals. Every vice minister in the capital signs fifty of those every three months."

"I have not asked you about Lin Zhengde yet," Gao Zhengyi said. He finally lifted his tired, sharp eyes. "I am asking you about Zurich."

For half a second, He Guoliang froze. His eyes shrank tight, and he pressed his folded hands together so hard his knuckles turned white.

"Zurich?" He Guoliang let out a dry, brittle laugh that died in his throat. "Gao Zhengyi, I have not set foot in Switzerland since the 2016 energy summit. Pull my official passport from Foreign Affairs right now if you want proof."

"I do not care where your shoes walked," Gao Zhengyi said. He turned the folder around and slid a single printed page across the scratched table. "Read line four."

He Guoliang stared at Gao Zhengyi's face, looking for a bluff. He found only cold patience. Slowly, he looked down at the paper.

There were no photos, no bank logos, and no letters. There were only twelve lines of forty-character codes made of letters and numbers. Next to each code sat dates going back nineteen years, secret bank bond numbers, and foreign vault storage tags.

All the color drained from He Guoliang's face at once. A sharp gasp caught in his chest.

"Where," he whispered, his voice cracking, "where did your office get this?"

"Answer the question, He Guoliang," Gao Zhengyi said softly, taking another slow puff. "Line four. 740 million Swiss francs in secret bank bonds, set up through a hidden trust in Zurich in November 2011. Three days after you approved the no-bid power plant deal for the northern line."

He Guoliang pulled his hands off the table and hid them in his lap, where they began to shake badly. "This is fake," he said, though his voice was barely a whisper. "Those codes do not exist in any computer in this country. Even the Swiss bank splits the bank proof across three offline record books. Nobody in the capital has the secret key to match those bond numbers to our local deals."

"You just confirmed how the bank proof is split," Gao Zhengyi noted without blinking. "Keep talking."

Sweat rolled down He Guoliang's forehead into his eyebrows. He wiped it away with a shaking wrist. His eyes darted wildly toward the blank gray wall behind Gao Zhengyi, as if trying to see through the concrete to whoever had handed over this impossible file.

"It was not your people," He Guoliang muttered, pure terror replacing his calm mask. "State Security could not touch those vaults. Finance could not touch them. Only one hidden archive on earth could pull raw bond codes from Zurich without setting off the bank alarm."

Gao Zhengyi leaned forward an inch, resting his arms on the table edge. "You have thirty seconds before I close this folder and add treason to your corruption charges. Lines nine through twelve match every foreign payout to a rare-earth limit change or a heavy power gear deal with your stamp on it. Your son's bank accounts in Singapore were frozen at eleven this morning. Decide what you want left of your name."

He Guoliang's chest heaved. A ragged sob broke from his throat, and he slumped forward, slapping both palms flat on the cold table.

"I did not build the channel!" He Guoliang choked out, spittle flying from his lips as his control snapped completely. "I was only a vice director in 2007 when they pulled me in! They swore the foreign account chain sat behind three layers of European legal protection!"

"Who pulled you in?" Gao Zhengyi asked, his voice dropping lower. "Full names. Titles. Dates."

He Guoliang swallowed hard, his throat working fast. "Cao Wenhui," he gasped. "Former president of Huaxia Development Bank before he retired in 2018. He controlled the foreign bank papers that hid the power plant kickbacks as equipment rental deposits."

Gao Zhengyi's pen moved steadily across a lined pad. "Who else?"

"Zheng Rongkui," He Guoliang blurted out, words spilling in a panicked rush. "Retired president of the National Agricultural Credit Bank. And Pan Desheng, who retired from the Industrial Clearing Bank four years ago. The three of them sat on the old loan committee together. Whenever a provincial power job or mining deal needed to move 10 billion yuan off the official books without central bank alerts, Cao Wenhui wrote the backing paper, Zheng Rongkui ran the money through local shell companies, and Pan Desheng handled the foreign money trade."

Gao Zhengyi tapped his cigarette ash into the overflowing tray. "Three retired state bank presidents," he said evenly. "And who ran the other end in Switzerland?"

He Guoliang shivered and hugged his own ribs. "A man in Geneva. Lukas Weber. He works out of an unmarked office near Rue du Rhone as a shipping insurance fixer. We never spoke to the Northern Ledger leaders in Zurich directly. Everything went through Lukas Weber. He got the local trade confirmations from Pan Desheng's clerks, turned them into paper bonds in Zurich, and sent back the forty-character receipt codes."

"How did Lukas Weber know it was really you?" Gao Zhengyi pressed.

"A secret satellite phone once every three months," He Guoliang whispered, staring blankly at the sheet that had ended his life. "I read him the first eight numbers of my old ministry badge and the bill code from the latest power deal. He never raised his voice. He just told me which provincial company to clear next, and which vice governor to protect."

"Which vice governors?" Gao Zhengyi demanded.

"Lin Zhengde in Lanshui," He Guoliang said, tears of fear blurring his eyes. "And four others across the southern and western lines. I will write down every name, every account, every hidden trust. Just tell me who opened the Zurich books, Gao Zhengyi. If Lukas Weber learns those codes are on your desk, the retired bank presidents will not be the only ones who vanish before sunrise."

Gao Zhengyi crushed out his cigarette, stood up, and snapped the folder shut.

"Lukas Weber will not be making any calls tonight, He Guoliang," Gao Zhengyi said coldly. "Write down every name. Leave out a single clerk, and I will come back with the second folder."

He walked out the steel door, leaving He Guoliang hunched over the yellow pad. The broken man's shaking hand smeared ink across the first line as quiet sobs filled the cold room.
