# Chapter 81: The Interrogation of Vice Minister He Guoliang

Blue cigarette smoke hung low in the basement questioning room in the capital. By Monday afternoon, the ashtray between Gao Zhengyi and Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang already held six crushed butts.

He Guoliang sat upright in the bolted steel chair, his collar damp with sweat despite the cold vent air. He folded his hands as if running a power grid meeting, though a faint tremor in his left thumb betrayed his fear.

"Gao Zhengyi," He Guoliang said behind a thin smile. "I have worked at the Energy Administration for thirty-one years. If there are issues with provincial power deals, my office will assist a full check. Locking a vice minister in a basement over wild rumors from Sha City is completely out of line."

Gao Zhengyi did not look up from the brown folder.
"Out of line," Gao Zhengyi repeated flatly. "You think you are sitting here over a local power station check."

He Guoliang's jaw tightened. "Lin Zhengde in Sha City has always been reckless. If he used my name to push a special land permit for Shengyuan Capital, that is his crime, not mine. I signed routine power grid approvals. Every vice minister signs fifty of those every quarter."

"I have not asked you about Lin Zhengde yet," Gao Zhengyi said, lifting tired, sharp eyes. "I am asking you about Zurich."

For half a second, He Guoliang froze. His eyes shrank tight, and his knuckles turned white.

"Zurich?" He Guoliang let out a dry laugh that died in his throat. "Gao Zhengyi, I have not set foot in Switzerland since the 2016 energy summit. Pull my official passport from Foreign Affairs if you want proof."

"I do not care where your shoes walked," Gao Zhengyi said, sliding a single printed page across the table. "Read line four."

He Guoliang searched Gao Zhengyi's face for a bluff, found only cold patience, and looked down.

There were no photos, logos, or letters, only twelve lines of forty-character codes beside dates going back nineteen years, secret bank bond numbers, and foreign vault storage tags.

Color drained from He Guoliang's face at once. A sharp gasp caught in his chest.

"Where," he whispered, voice cracking, "where did your office get this?"

"Answer the question, He Guoliang," Gao Zhengyi said softly. "Line four. 740 million Swiss francs in secret bank bonds, set up through a hidden trust in Zurich in November 2011. Three days after you approved the no-bid power plant deal for the northern line."

He Guoliang pulled his shaking hands into his lap. "This is fake," he said, barely above a whisper. "Those codes do not exist in any computer in this country. Even the Swiss bank splits the proof across three offline record books. Nobody in the capital has the secret key to match those bond numbers to our local deals."

"You just confirmed how the bank proof is split," Gao Zhengyi noted without blinking. "Keep talking."

Sweat rolled into He Guoliang's eyebrows. He wiped it away with a shaking wrist, eyes darting toward the blank gray wall behind Gao Zhengyi as if trying to see whoever had handed over this impossible file.

"It was not your people," He Guoliang muttered, pure terror breaking his mask. "State Security could not touch those vaults. Finance could not touch them. Only one hidden archive on earth could pull raw bond codes from Zurich without tripping the bank alarm."

Gao Zhengyi leaned forward an inch. "You have thirty seconds before I close this folder and send this file to the Procuratorate with a treason charge. Lines nine through twelve match every foreign payout to a rare-earth limit change or heavy power gear deal carrying your stamp. Your son's Singapore accounts were flagged this morning, and a freeze request has gone to the Singapore bank. Decide what you want left of your name."

He Guoliang's chest heaved. A ragged sob broke from his throat, and he slapped both palms flat on the table.

"I did not build the channel!" He Guoliang choked out as his control snapped. "I was only a vice director in 2007 when they pulled me in! They swore the foreign account chain sat behind three layers of European legal protection!"

"Who pulled you in?" Gao Zhengyi asked quietly. "Full names. Titles. Dates."

He Guoliang swallowed hard. "Cao Wenhui," he gasped. "Former president of Huaxia Development Bank before he retired in 2018. He controlled the foreign bank papers hiding power plant kickbacks as equipment rental deposits."

Gao Zhengyi's pen moved steadily across a lined pad. "Who else?"

"Zheng Rongkui," He Guoliang blurted in a panicked rush. "Retired president of the National Agricultural Credit Bank. And Pan Desheng, who retired from the Industrial Clearing Bank four years ago. The three sat on the old loan committee together. Whenever a provincial power job or mining deal needed to move 10 billion yuan off official books without central bank alerts, Cao Wenhui wrote the backing paper, Zheng Rongkui ran the money through local shell firms, and Pan Desheng handled the foreign money trade."

Gao Zhengyi tapped ash into the tray. "Three retired state bank presidents," he said evenly. "And who ran the other end in Switzerland?"

He Guoliang shivered and hugged his ribs. "A man in Geneva. Lukas Weber. He works out of an unmarked office near Rue du Rhone as a shipping insurance fixer. We never spoke to the Northern Ledger leaders in Zurich directly. Everything went through Lukas Weber. He got local trade confirmations from Pan Desheng's clerks, turned them into paper bonds in Zurich, and sent back the forty-character receipt codes."

"How did Lukas Weber know it was really you?" Gao Zhengyi pressed.

"A secret satellite phone once every three months," He Guoliang whispered, staring blankly at the sheet that had ended his life. "I read him the first eight numbers of my old ministry badge and the bill code from the latest power deal. He never raised his voice. He just told me which provincial company to clear next, and which vice governor to protect."

"Which vice governors?" Gao Zhengyi demanded.

"Lin Zhengde in Sha City," He Guoliang said, tears blurring his eyes. "And four others across the southern and western lines. I will write down every name, account, and hidden trust. Just tell me who opened the Zurich books, Gao Zhengyi. If Lukas Weber learns those codes are on your desk, the retired bank presidents will not be the only ones who vanish before sunrise."

Gao Zhengyi crushed out his cigarette, stood up, and snapped the folder shut.

"Lukas Weber will not be making any calls tonight, He Guoliang," Gao Zhengyi said coldly. "Write down every name. Leave out a single clerk, and I will come back with the second folder."

He walked out the steel door, leaving He Guoliang hunched over the yellow pad, his shaking hand smearing ink across the first line as quiet sobs filled the room.
