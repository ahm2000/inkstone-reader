# Chapter 21: The Forgotten Witness

Sunday afternoon on Old Street in Yunxi County was unusually quiet. Wooden shutters hung half-drawn across most storefronts, and faded red demolition notices peeled along the brick walls.

Chen Xuan walked slowly down the lane in his dark cotton jacket, carrying his dented green metal thermos. He never glanced toward rooftops or alleys, yet he noticed every latched door and every wary pair of eyes behind dusty glass.

Forty paces back inside Gong's Noodles, a solitary customer in a charcoal jacket ate clear-broth noodles. Number Twenty-three kept his eyes loosely on a folded newspaper while tracking the entire lane in his peripheral vision. He never looked directly at Chen Xuan, and Chen Xuan never looked toward the noodle shop.

Near the southern bend, Chen Xuan stopped outside a narrow tailor shop. Old tailor Yu Changshun sat on a low stool by the threshold, gray trousers across his knees and a threaded needle still between his fingers.

"Uncle Yu Changshun," Chen Xuan said softly.

Yu Changshun blinked behind wire-framed spectacles. Recognizing Chen Xuan, the tight knot between his eyebrows eased slightly, though unease lingered in his eyes. "Chen Xuan. You came back from Qingyan."

"Yesterday," Chen Xuan replied. He pulled a spare stool from beside the doorframe, wiped the dust off, and sat down. "Do you have a few minutes?"

"Sit, sit," Yu Changshun muttered, setting the trousers aside. He poured hot water from a chipped kettle into two mugs of coarse green tea, his wrist trembling slightly as the stream hit the porcelain. "Nobody gets trousers hemmed when half the block is waiting for bulldozers."

Chen Xuan set his thermos by his shoe and accepted the warm mug. "Your hands are stiff today, Uncle Yu Changshun."

Yu Changshun gave a dry laugh that never reached his eyes. "It is not the cold, boy. It is the air on this street." He studied Chen Xuan's calm face. "How is your father holding up? Chen Jianguo has always been proud. What they did to Chen Mingde on Friday night was shameless. Everyone knows Chen Mingde spent thirty years fixing neighbors' fuses and drains without taking a fen."

"My father is resting at home," Chen Xuan said evenly. "We are handling it."

Yu Changshun leaned forward, his spectacles slipping down his nose. "Be careful, Chen Xuan. You work an ordinary office job in Qingyan, so you only see the polite face of Yunxi County on holidays. This place is not what it was when you were a boy."

"How has it changed?" Chen Xuan asked mildly, taking a slow sip.

"People do not argue anymore," Yu Changshun said, his voice tightening. "They bolt their doors and pretend they cannot hear."

Chen Xuan set his mug down. "Hongrui Real Estate has only pushed this demolition for a year. Compensation fights happen everywhere. People yell and bargain. Why is everyone on Old Street too frightened to even open their shutters?"

Yu Changshun froze. His eyes darted toward the northern corner, then across to a shuttered hardware shop two doors down. For three seconds his throat worked in silence. Finally, he pulled his stool closer and dropped his voice to a hoarse whisper.

"Because this is not the first time Hongrui Real Estate wanted the southern end of Old Street," Yu Changshun murmured. "Three years ago, before those red notices ever went up, they tried to buy the four shops closest to the old stone drainage culvert. Three owners took the low price and moved across the river. One man refused."

"Who was he?" Chen Xuan asked quietly.

"Lin Guoliang," Yu Changshun said, the name heavy on his tongue. "He ran the small hardware shop past Chen Mingde's store. A stubborn, honest man. His wife died years ago, and he raised his daughter, Lin Xiaoyu, alone behind that counter. When Zhou Quan sent men offering half the market value, Lin Guoliang threw their contract back into the street."

"Was it only about the price?" Chen Xuan asked.

Yu Changshun's knuckles turned pale around his mug. He stared down into his tea, his jaw tight with old grief. "At first we thought so. Then one night he sat right where you are sitting and told me the truth. His back storeroom sat directly above the old masonry culvert leading toward the river quarry. For months, between two and four in the morning, he felt low vibrations through his concrete floor, like heavy wheeled carts rolling underground."

Chen Xuan's gaze stayed locked on the old tailor's face. "Did he look?"

"He pried up a loose stone slab near his backyard drain," Yu Changshun whispered, his shoulders hunching. "He said the smell hit him first: wet clay and diesel exhaust rising from a tunnel that had no business being there. There is no subway in Yunxi County, Chen Xuan, and no mine under our houses."

"What did Lin Guoliang do?" Chen Xuan asked steadily.

"What an honest man thinks he should do," Yu Changshun said bitterly, his eyes reddening. "He photographed the opening and wrote to the county construction bureau. Four days later, Yan Tiehu brought six men into his shop and smashed every shelf. Lin Guoliang swept up the glass and still refused to sign."

Yu Changshun drew a shaky breath. "Two weeks later, at eleven at night, he rode his bicycle down the empty road east of the bridge to buy stomach medicine. A flatbed truck struck him from behind and crushed him against the guardrail."

"What did the station say?" Chen Xuan asked, his tone flat and quiet.

"Old Street Station closed the file in forty-eight hours," Yu Changshun rasped. "Station Director Cheng Biao signed the report himself, calling it a drunk-driving crash because Lin Guoliang swerved. Everyone here knew Lin Guoliang had not touched alcohol in twenty years because of his ulcer. The driver surrendered, paid a small sum through a broker, and vanished."

"And his daughter?" Chen Xuan asked.

Yu Changshun shut his eyes, a tremor passing through his thin shoulders. "Lin Xiaoyu was nineteen, studying accounting at a college in Lanshui. She came home for the funeral and petitioned the county office. Within a month, Yan Tiehu's men showed up at her dormitory with forged debt notes claiming her father owed Hongrui Real Estate three hundred thousand yuan."

"They went all the way to Lanshui?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"They waited outside her classes," Yu Changshun said, his voice cracking. "They harassed her roommates and threatened administrators until the college forced her out. Last I heard, she fled the province to stay alive. Nobody on Old Street dares say the name Lin Guoliang anymore."

Silence settled over the table. Chen Xuan sat completely still, his face ordinary, his fingers steady against his green thermos. He did not frown or raise his voice. Yet looking into those calm, bottomless eyes, Yu Changshun felt an inexplicable chill prickle along his neck and pulled his jacket tighter.

"Thank you for the tea, Uncle Yu Changshun," Chen Xuan said gently. He set a small tin of Longjing tea on the table and stood. "Keep your door locked at night, and do not sign anything."

Yu Changshun gripped the table edge, his fingers trembling. "Chen Xuan, do not provoke Yan Tiehu or Cheng Biao. They have backers in the county and in Lanshui. You cannot fight them."

"I know," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

He lifted his thermos and walked slowly back up Old Street. Inside Gong's Noodles, Number Twenty-three set down his chopsticks, tucked a banknote under the saucer, and tapped a coded sequence into a dark terminal beneath the table. Within three seconds, Lin Guoliang and Lin Xiaoyu were logged at top priority inside the Black Pine Guard archive, bound directly to the tunnel beneath Old Street.
