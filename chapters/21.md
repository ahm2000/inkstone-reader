# Chapter 21: The Forgotten Witness

Sunday afternoon on Old Street in Yunxi County was unusually quiet. Wooden shutters hung half-closed over most shop fronts, and faded red demolition notices peeled off the brick walls.

Chen Xuan walked slowly down the lane in his dark cotton jacket, carrying his dented green metal thermos. He never glanced up at the rooftops or into the side alleys. Still, he noticed every locked door and every nervous pair of eyes watching from behind dusty glass.

Forty steps behind him, inside Gong's Noodles, a lone customer in a charcoal jacket ate a bowl of clear-broth noodles. Number 23 kept his eyes loosely on a folded newspaper while watching the whole lane from the corner of his eye. He never looked straight at Chen Xuan, and Chen Xuan never looked toward the noodle shop.

Near the southern bend, Chen Xuan stopped outside a narrow tailor shop. Old tailor Yu Changshun sat on a low stool in the doorway, gray trousers laid across his knees and a threaded needle still resting between his fingers.

"Uncle Yu Changshun," Chen Xuan said softly.

Yu Changshun blinked behind his wire-framed glasses. Once he recognized Chen Xuan, the tight frown between his brows eased a little, though worry still lingered in his eyes. "Chen Xuan. You came back from Qingyan."

"Yesterday," Chen Xuan replied. He pulled a spare stool from beside the door, wiped the dust off it, and sat down. "Do you have a few minutes?"

"Sit, sit," Yu Changshun muttered, setting the trousers aside. He poured hot water from a chipped kettle into two mugs of rough green tea. His wrist trembled slightly as the hot stream hit the porcelain. "Nobody brings trousers to be hemmed when half the block is waiting for bulldozers."

Chen Xuan set his thermos beside his shoe and took the warm mug. "Your hands are stiff today, Uncle Yu Changshun."

Yu Changshun let out a dry laugh that never reached his eyes. "It is not the cold, boy. It is the air on this street." He studied Chen Xuan's calm face. "How is your father holding up? Chen Jianguo has always been a proud man. What they did to Chen Mingde on Friday night was shameless. Everyone here knows Chen Mingde spent thirty years fixing neighbors' fuses and drains without taking a single cent."

"My father is resting at home," Chen Xuan said evenly. "We are handling it."

Yu Changshun leaned forward, his glasses slipping down his nose. "Be careful, Chen Xuan. You work an ordinary office job in Qingyan, so you only see the polite face of Yunxi County on holidays. This town is not what it was when you were a boy."

"How has it changed?" Chen Xuan asked mildly, taking a slow sip of tea.

"People do not argue anymore," Yu Changshun said, his voice tightening. "They bolt their doors and pretend they cannot hear."

Chen Xuan set his mug down. "Hongrui Real Estate has only pushed this demolition for a year. Fights over buyout money happen everywhere. People usually shout and bargain. Why is everyone on Old Street too scared to even open their shutters?"

Yu Changshun froze. His eyes darted toward the northern corner, then across the lane to a closed hardware shop two doors down. For three seconds his throat worked in silence. At last, he pulled his stool closer and dropped his voice to a rough whisper.

"Because this is not the first time Hongrui Real Estate wanted the southern end of Old Street," Yu Changshun murmured. "Three years ago, before those red notices ever went up, they tried to buy the four shops closest to the old stone drain. Three owners took the cheap offer and moved across the river. One man refused."

"Who was he?" Chen Xuan asked quietly.

"Lin Guoliang," Yu Changshun said, the name heavy on his tongue. "He ran the small hardware shop just past Chen Mingde's store. A stubborn, honest man. His wife died years ago, so he raised his daughter, Lin Xiaoyu, all by himself behind that counter. When Zhou Quan sent men offering half what the shop was worth, Lin Guoliang threw their contract right back into the street."

"Was it only about the price?" Chen Xuan asked.

Yu Changshun's knuckles turned pale around his mug. He stared down into his tea, his jaw tight with old grief. "At first we all thought so. Then one night he sat right where you are sitting and told me the truth. His back storage room sat directly above the old stone drain leading toward the river quarry. For months, between two and four in the morning, he felt low shaking through his concrete floor, like heavy carts rolling underground."

Chen Xuan kept his gaze steady on the old tailor's face. "Did he look?"

"He pried up a loose stone slab near his backyard drain," Yu Changshun whispered, hunching his shoulders. "He said the smell hit him first. Wet clay and diesel smoke rising out of a tunnel that had no business being there. There is no subway in Yunxi County, Chen Xuan, and there is no mine under our houses."

"What did Lin Guoliang do?" Chen Xuan asked steadily.

"What an honest man thinks he should do," Yu Changshun said bitterly, his eyes reddening. "He took photos of the opening and wrote to the county building office. Four days later, Yan Tiehu brought six men into his shop and smashed every shelf. Lin Guoliang swept up the broken glass and still refused to sign."

Yu Changshun drew a shaky breath. "Two weeks later, at eleven at night, he rode his bicycle down the empty road east of the bridge to buy stomach medicine. A flatbed truck hit him from behind and crushed him against the guardrail."

"What did the police station say?" Chen Xuan asked, his tone flat and quiet.

"Old Street Station closed the case in forty-eight hours," Yu Changshun rasped. "Station Director Cheng Biao signed the report himself. He called it a drunk crash and claimed Lin Guoliang swerved. Everyone on this street knew Lin Guoliang had not touched a drop of alcohol in twenty years because of his bad stomach. The driver turned himself in, paid a small sum through a middleman, and disappeared."

"And his daughter?" Chen Xuan asked.

Yu Changshun shut his eyes, a shiver running through his thin shoulders. "Lin Xiaoyu was nineteen, studying accounting at a college in Lanshui. She came home for the funeral and filed complaints at the county office. Within a month, Yan Tiehu's men showed up at her school dorm with fake debt papers claiming her father owed Hongrui Real Estate 300k yuan."

"They went all the way to Lanshui?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"They waited outside her classes," Yu Changshun said, his voice cracking. "They harassed her roommates and threatened the school staff until the college forced her out. Last I heard, she fled the province just to stay alive. Nobody on Old Street dares say the name Lin Guoliang anymore."

Silence settled over the small table. Chen Xuan sat completely still, his face plain and calm, his fingers steady against his green thermos. He did not frown or raise his voice. Yet looking into those calm, bottomless eyes, Yu Changshun felt a sudden chill crawl up his neck and pulled his jacket tighter around himself.

"Thank you for the tea, Uncle Yu Changshun," Chen Xuan said gently. He set a small tin of Longjing tea on the table and stood up. "Keep your door locked at night, and do not sign anything."

Yu Changshun gripped the edge of the table, his fingers trembling. "Chen Xuan, do not anger Yan Tiehu or Cheng Biao. They have powerful backers in the county and in Lanshui. You cannot fight them."

"I know," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

He picked up his thermos and walked slowly back up Old Street. Inside Gong's Noodles, Number 23 set down his chopsticks, tucked a bill under his saucer, and tapped a short code into a dark device hidden beneath the table. Within three seconds, Lin Guoliang and Lin Xiaoyu were entered at top priority in the Black Pine Guard records, linked directly to the tunnel beneath Old Street.
