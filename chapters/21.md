# Chapter 21: Eleven Contracts

In the tenth-floor office of Hongrui Real Estate, Dong Haisheng's finance director burst in without knocking, with a bank slip shaking in his hand.

"Boss Dong, every company account is frozen! I tried to pay the workers this week, and the screen only said 'Restricted, under review'!"

Dong stared at him, and his heavy gold chain slid down against his shirt.

"Frozen?"

"Every single one, Boss!"

"Frozen by who?"

"The city review team, Boss! It is the order about the eleven contracts!"

Then his lawyer came in, pale, holding a one-page note from the city review team.

"Can they really do this?" Dong said, and he read the note twice, and each time it got worse.

"They can, Boss. It is all written down."

*All eleven contracts were signed within twelve hours of the owner leaving the police station. No price evaluation report exists for any of them. Treat all eleven as signed under pressure.*

That meant the eleven shops he had bought at half their value were no longer his!

And while the accounts stayed frozen, he could not pay one worker or one supplier, so how could he start building the mall before the bank's review on the thirtieth, and how could he hold on for three more weeks?

At nine o'clock sharp, his private phone rang, and the screen showed only one word: *Huang.*

Chairman Huang was the private lender who had secretly lent Dong three hundred million yuan.

Dong's hand went cold, and he answered on the third ring.

"Boss Dong." The voice was soft, patient and never in a hurry. "On Friday I asked whether the corner was finished. Today is Tuesday, and I see a hardware shop that is still open, and eleven shops you may have to give back."

"Chairman Huang, please, give me three days! I will fix the accounts, I will speak to the county..."

"I am not angry," Chairman Huang said. "I am never angry. I lent you three hundred million yuan because I believed in a corner, and I don't see a corner. So I will not ask you for money today. I will send someone to sit with you."

"Sit with me?" Dong's voice cracked. "What does that mean?"

"He is a very patient man. He will arrive this afternoon."

Click.

Dong understood at once.

"He is sending a man to watch me," he told the lawyer hoarsely.

The man would sit beside him at his desk, at lunch and in the car, and he would never leave until three hundred million yuan came back.

Where could Dong find that much, and who would lend him a single yuan now?

Only one name came to him, so he grabbed his car keys and ran!

By mid-morning, he was at the County Magistrate's office, pounding on a different door.

"I am sorry, Boss Dong, the Magistrate is in a meeting," said Tao Shu's secretary, Ji Yang, politely.

Ji was twenty-nine, careful, and good at saying no with a smile, and behind him the big wooden door stayed shut.

"Then I will wait!" Dong said. "Tell him it's urgent! Tell him it's Hongrui!"

Ji went in.

Behind the door, County Magistrate Tao Shu sat at a clean desk, reading a report on road repairs as if nobody in the world were waiting for him.

"Tell him I have a cold," Tao said, without looking up.

"Magistrate, you had a cold on Friday, too."

"It came back. Colds do."

Tao turned a page.

The reason was simple, and Tao knew it well: he had never taken one yuan from Dong Haisheng, and he had made sure no camera ever caught the two of them in one frame. His hands were clean, and that was the whole point of eight careful years, because a man with clean hands does not let a friend in trouble come near him, in case the trouble sticks.

Besides, in his bottom drawer lay a thick folder with one name on its tab: *Fan.*

Tao did not need Dong, because he only needed to wait!

In the lobby, Ji came back out with a sad little bow.

"I am very sorry. The Magistrate has a cold."

"AGAIN?!" Dong roared, and a guard stepped closer. "He had a cold on Friday! Does the man ever get well?!"

"Quiet, sir," the guard said.

Ji kept his face perfectly serious, though privately he thought the cold was the most reliable member of the whole county government.

Dong walked out slowly, and nobody stopped him, and nobody called after him.

Everyone he had ever dined with was suddenly in a meeting.

---

Two mornings later, in a Qingyan City hotel, a cleaner opened a room and found a thick gold chain on the table beside a cold cup of tea. The bed had not been slept in, there was no guest, and nobody knew where Dong Haisheng was.

---

Back in Yunxi County, the Standing Committee was holding its regular meeting in Conference Room Three. The next item was road repair in Dongqiao Town, and Fan Weiguo, in Seat One with his pen in his hand, heard none of it.

He was looking out the window.

Across the courtyard, in the building where Lu Wenzhe's office was, a door opened, and three people stepped out.

Two were officers from the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission, and one came from Kang Guangming's county office. They carried no handcuffs, and they walked politely on either side of a man in a gray suit, who held his jacket over his arm as if they were all going to lunch.

The man was Lu Wenzhe.

Seat Five.

He glanced up at the window, and for one second his eyes met Fan's, and then the officers led him into a plain car, and it rolled out of the gate.

This was called "double designation," and it let investigators keep an official in a quiet room for questioning, away from work and family, for weeks if needed, with no phone and no visits. Whatever the name, a man who went in did not come out the same.

Seat Five was empty now.

Nobody said a word out loud, but a few whispers ran along the back row.

"That was Lu!"

"In daylight, with his jacket on his arm..."

"Quiet, the Secretary is looking."

"I was never close to Lu Wenzhe," Shao Wenhua, in Seat Four, had already told two people that morning, which was true the way most convenient things are true, technically and only since yesterday.

Fan had expected to feel victory, because Lu was the man who signed every payment from that six-hundred-forty-million-yuan fund.

Instead, a cold weight settled in his stomach. If they could take Lu at lunchtime, politely, in daylight, then who was next? Lu had signed the payments, but Fan had signed every approval!

He turned his head and saw Tao Shu reading the road report. Only the corner of Tao's mouth moved, once, small and gentle, like a man who had remembered something pleasant.

Shao Wenhua wrote one line in his notebook and closed it.

At twelve-forty, the meeting ended, and chairs scraped back.

Fan was pushing his papers into his red folder when Kang Guangming stopped beside him, his face as stony as ever.

"Secretary Fan," Kang said quietly. "Tomorrow they want to see you. Nine o'clock."
