# Chapter 21: Eleven Contracts

Tuesday morning, in Hongrui Real Estate's tenth-floor office, Dong Haisheng's finance director burst in without knocking, a bank slip shaking in his hand.

"Boss Dong! Every company account is frozen! Every single one! I tried to pay this week's wages for the workers, and the screen only said 'Restricted, under review'!"

Dong stared at him. His heavy gold chain slid down against his shirt. "Frozen by who?"

"The city review team. It's the order about the eleven contracts."

Then his lawyer arrived, pale, with a one-page note from the city review team. Dong read it twice, and each time it got worse.

*All eleven contracts were signed within twelve hours of the owner leaving the police station. No price evaluation report exists for any of them. Treat all eleven as signed under pressure.*

That meant the eleven shops he had bought at half their value were no longer his. And while the accounts stayed frozen, he couldn't pay a single worker or a single supplier. How could he possibly start building the mall before the bank's review on the thirtieth? How could he hold on for three more weeks?!

At nine o'clock sharp, his private phone rang. The screen said only: *Huang.* Chairman Huang was the private lender who had secretly lent him 300 million yuan.

Dong's hand went cold. He answered on the third ring.

"Boss Dong." The voice was soft, patient, and never in a hurry. "On Friday I asked you whether the corner was finished. Today is Tuesday. I see a hardware shop that is still open, and eleven shops you may have to give back."

"Chairman Huang, please, give me three days! I'll fix the accounts, I'll speak to the county..."

"I'm not angry," Chairman Huang said. "I'm never angry. I lent you three hundred million yuan because I believed in a corner. I don't see a corner. So I will not ask you for money today. I will send someone to sit with you."

"Sit with me?" Dong's voice cracked. "What does that mean?"

"He's a very patient man. He will arrive this afternoon."

Click.

Dong understood. The man would sit beside him at his desk, at lunch, and in the car, and he would never leave until three hundred million yuan came back. Where could Dong find that much? Who would lend him a single yuan now?

Only one name came to him. He grabbed his car keys and ran.

By mid-morning, Dong was at the County Magistrate's office, pounding on a different door.

"I'm sorry, Boss Dong, the Magistrate is in a meeting," Tao Shu's secretary, Ji Yang, said politely.

Ji was twenty-nine, careful, and good at saying no with a smile. Behind him, the big wooden door was shut.

"Then I'll wait!" Dong said. "Tell him it's urgent! Tell him it's Hongrui!"

Ji went in. Behind the door, County Magistrate Tao Shu sat behind a clean desk, reading a report on road repairs as if nobody in the world was waiting.

"Tell him I have a cold," Tao said, without looking up.

"Magistrate, you had a cold on Friday, too."

"It came back. Colds do."

Tao turned a page. He hadn't taken a single yuan from Dong Haisheng, and he'd made sure that no camera had ever caught the two of them in one frame. His hands were clean, and that was the whole point of being careful for eight years. A man with clean hands doesn't let a friend in trouble come near him, because the trouble might stick to him too.

Besides, in his bottom drawer lay a thick folder with one name on its tab: *Fan.* Tao didn't need Dong. He only needed to wait.

In the lobby, Ji came back out with a sad little bow. "I'm very sorry. The Magistrate has a cold."

"AGAIN?!" Dong roared. A guard stepped closer. "He had a cold Friday! Does the man ever get well?!"

Ji kept his face perfectly serious. Privately, he thought the cold was the most reliable member of the whole county government.

Dong walked out slowly. Nobody stopped him, and nobody called after him. Everyone he'd ever dined with was suddenly in a meeting.

On Thursday morning, a hotel cleaner in Qingyan City opened a hotel room and found a thick gold chain lying on the table beside a cold cup of tea. It was Dong Haisheng's chain. The bed hadn't been slept in, there was no guest, and nobody knew where Dong Haisheng was.

At noon, in Conference Room Three, the Standing Committee was holding its regular Tuesday meeting. The next item on the agenda was road repair in Dongqiao Town.

Fan Weiguo sat in Seat One with his pen in his hand and heard none of it. He was looking out the window.

Across the courtyard, in the building where Lu Wenzhe's office was, a door opened. Three people stepped out: two officers from the city Discipline Commission, and one from Kang Guangming's county office. They didn't have handcuffs. They walked politely on either side of a man in a gray suit, carrying his jacket over his arm, as if they were going to lunch.

The man was Lu Wenzhe. Seat Five.

He glanced up at the window. For one second, his eyes met Fan's. Then the officers led him into a plain car, and it rolled out of the gate.

People called it "double designation": investigators could keep an official away from his work and his family, in a quiet room for questioning, for weeks if needed, no phone, no visits. Whatever the name, the man who went in didn't come out the same.

Seat Five was empty now. Nobody had said a word.

Shao Wenhua, in Seat Four, had already mentioned to two separate people that morning that he'd "never been close" to Lu Wenzhe, which was true the way most convenient things are true: technically, and only since yesterday.

Fan expected to feel victory. After all, Lu was the man who signed every payment from that 640-million-yuan fund. Instead, a cold weight settled in his stomach. If they could take Lu at lunchtime, politely, in daylight, then who was next? Lu had signed the payments, but Fan had signed every approval!

He turned his head, and he saw Tao Shu.

Tao was reading the road report. Only the corner of his mouth moved, once, small and gentle, as if he'd remembered something pleasant.

Shao Wenhua, in Seat Four, wrote one line in his notebook and closed it.

At twelve-forty, the meeting ended, and chairs scraped back. Fan was pushing his papers into his red folder when Kang Guangming stopped beside him, his face as stony as ever.

"Secretary Fan," Kang said quietly. "Tomorrow they want to see you. Nine o'clock."
