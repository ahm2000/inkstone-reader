# Chapter 21: The Forgotten Witness

Sunday afternoon on Old Street in Yunxi County was unusually quiet. Wooden shutters hung half-closed, and faded red demolition notices peeled off brick walls.

Chen Xuan walked slowly in his dark cotton jacket, carrying his dented green thermos. Without glancing at rooftops or alleys, he noticed every locked door and nervous pair of eyes behind dusty glass.

Forty steps back, inside Gong's Noodles, a lone customer in a charcoal jacket ate clear-broth noodles. Number 23 kept his eyes on a newspaper while watching the lane from the corner of his eye. Neither man looked at the other.

Near the southern bend, Chen Xuan stopped outside a narrow tailor shop. Old tailor Yu Changshun sat on a low stool in the doorway, gray trousers across his knees and a threaded needle in his fingers.

"Uncle Yu Changshun," Chen Xuan said softly.

Yu Changshun blinked behind his wire glasses. Recognizing Chen Xuan, his frown eased slightly, though worry stayed. "Chen Xuan. You came back from Shanghai."

"Yesterday," Chen Xuan replied, wiping dust from a spare stool and sitting. "Do you have a few minutes?"

"Sit, sit," Yu Changshun muttered, setting the trousers aside. He poured hot water into two mugs of rough green tea, his wrist trembling. "Nobody brings trousers to hem when half the block waits for bulldozers."

Chen Xuan set his thermos down and took the mug. "Your hands are stiff today, Uncle Yu Changshun."

Yu Changshun gave a dry, humorless laugh. "It is not the cold, boy. It is the air on this street." He studied Chen Xuan's calm face. "How is Chen Jianguo holding up? What they did to Chen Mingde Friday night was shameless. Everyone knows Chen Mingde spent thirty years fixing neighbors' fuses and drains for free."

"My father is resting at home," Chen Xuan said evenly. "We are handling it."

Yu Changshun leaned forward. "Be careful, Chen Xuan. You work an office job in Shanghai, so you only see Yunxi County's polite face on holidays. This town is not what it was."

"How has it changed?" Chen Xuan asked mildly, sipping tea.

"People do not argue anymore," Yu Changshun said tightly. "They bolt their doors and pretend they cannot hear."

Chen Xuan set his mug down. "Hongrui Real Estate has only pushed this demolition for a year. Buyout fights happen everywhere. Why is everyone on Old Street too scared to open their shutters?"

Yu Changshun froze, glancing nervously at a closed hardware shop two doors down. Pulling his stool closer, he dropped his voice to a rough whisper.

"Because this is not the first time Hongrui Real Estate wanted Old Street's south end," he murmured. "Three years ago, before those red notices went up, they tried buying the four shops closest to the old stone drain. Three owners took the cheap offer and moved across the river. One man refused."

"Who was he?" Chen Xuan asked quietly.

"Lin Guoliang," Yu Changshun said heavily. "He ran the small hardware shop past Chen Mingde's store. A stubborn, honest man. His wife died years ago, so he raised his daughter, Lin Xiaoyu, alone behind that counter. When Zhou Quan sent men offering half what the shop was worth, Lin Guoliang threw their contract into the street."

"Was it only about price?" Chen Xuan asked.

Yu Changshun's knuckles turned pale around his mug, jaw tight with old grief. "We thought so at first. Then one night he sat right there and told me the truth. His back storage room sat directly above the stone drain leading toward the river quarry. For months, between two and four in the morning, he felt low shaking through his concrete floor, like heavy carts rolling underground."

Chen Xuan kept his gaze steady. "Did he look?"

"He pried up a loose slab near his backyard drain," Yu Changshun whispered. "The smell hit him first. Wet clay and diesel smoke rising out of a tunnel that had no business there. There is no subway in Yunxi County, Chen Xuan, and no mine under our houses."

"What did Lin Guoliang do?" Chen Xuan asked steadily.

"What an honest man thinks he should," Yu Changshun said bitterly, eyes reddening. "He photographed the opening and wrote to the county building office. Four days later, Yan Tiehu brought six men and smashed every shelf. Lin Guoliang swept up the glass and still refused to sign."

Yu Changshun drew a shaky breath. "Two weeks later, at eleven at night, he rode his bicycle east of the bridge for stomach medicine. A flatbed truck hit him from behind and crushed him against the guardrail."

"What did the police say?" Chen Xuan asked flatly.

"Old Street Station closed the case in forty-eight hours," Yu Changshun rasped. "Station Director Cheng Biao signed it himself, calling it a drunk crash and claiming Lin Guoliang swerved. Everyone knew Lin Guoliang had not touched alcohol in twenty years because of his bad stomach. The driver turned himself in, paid a small sum through a middleman, and vanished."

"And his daughter?" Chen Xuan asked.

Yu Changshun shut his eyes, shivering. "Lin Xiaoyu was nineteen, studying accounting at a college in Sha City. She came home for the funeral and filed complaints at the county office. Within a month, Yan Tiehu's men showed up at her dorm with fake debt papers claiming her father owed Hongrui Real Estate 300k yuan."

"They went all the way to Sha City?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"They waited outside her classes," Yu Changshun said, voice cracking. "They harassed her roommates and threatened staff until the college forced her out. Last I heard, she fled the province to stay alive. Nobody on Old Street dares say Lin Guoliang's name anymore."

Silence settled over the table. Chen Xuan sat completely still, face calm, fingers steady on his thermos. He did not frown or raise his voice. Yet looking into those bottomless eyes, Yu Changshun felt a sudden chill crawl up his neck and pulled his jacket tighter.

"Thank you for the tea, Uncle Yu Changshun," Chen Xuan said gently, setting a small tin of Longjing tea on the table as he stood. "Keep your door locked at night, and sign nothing."

Yu Changshun gripped the table, trembling. "Chen Xuan, do not anger Yan Tiehu or Cheng Biao. They have powerful backers in the county and Sha City. You cannot fight them."

"I know," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

He picked up his thermos and walked slowly up Old Street. Inside Gong's Noodles, Number 23 set down his chopsticks, tucked a bill under his saucer, and tapped a short code into a hidden device. Within three seconds, Lin Guoliang and Lin Xiaoyu were entered at top priority in White Guard records, linked straight to the tunnel beneath Old Street.
