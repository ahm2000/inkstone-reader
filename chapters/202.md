# Chapter 202: Sunday Afternoon Flight

Thirty-five thousand feet above the northern coast, the sleek white jet cut smoothly southward through a pale winter sky. Far below the wing, the snow-blanketed pine ridges of Changbai Mountain had already faded behind thick banks of December cloud.

Inside the warm, quiet cabin, Chen Xuan leaned back in a wide beige leather seat. He wore a plain dark winter jacket, faded jeans, and thick wool socks, his shoes tucked neatly under the footrest. With the northern trip finished and the cabin humming softly around him, he looked less like a man returning from a high-stakes mountain storm and more like someone heading home after a long weekend visit to relatives.

Across the aisle, Number 7 stood beside the polished walnut side table, carefully tying the drawstrings on two woven cloth sacks. Dressed in a simple navy knit sweater and dark trousers, he looked like a polite travel companion rather than a guard. Next to the cloth sacks sat a neat insulated cardboard box sealed with brown tape.

"Young Master, the thin-shelled Changbai pine nuts are packed in the left bag," Number 7 said with a mild, respectful smile. "I checked them myself at the old farmer's stall before we left the valley. Every shell is cracked open cleanly so Madam will not hurt her fingernails. The sun-dried wild hazel mushrooms are in the box, wrapped in dry parchment so they stay crisp for the broth tonight, and there is a small jar of mountain linden honey tucked in the corner."

Chen Xuan turned his head from the window and grinned. "You remembered the cracked shells. Good eye. Last winter she bought a bag of sealed pine nuts from the supermarket and spent twenty minutes fighting them with a spoon before giving up."

"I made sure the farmer gave us the fresh roasted batch from yesterday afternoon, Young Master," Number 7 replied easily. He poured a fresh cup of hot jasmine tea from a glass pot and set it on the armrest tray. "Also, the final update from Hong Kong just came through."

Chen Xuan lifted the cup and blew lightly across the steam. "Go ahead."

"At 9:05 AM Hong Kong (+8:00 GMT) time, ICAC officers arrested Victor Leung in his Central penthouse," Number 7 reported in a calm, steady voice. "All Seven-Harbor clearing accounts in Singapore (+8:00 GMT) and Zurich (+1:00 GMT) are locked through Aegis Meridian's legal compliance channels. Up north, Kou Zhenshan and Viktor Orlov are already in custody and have begun cooperating with investigators. Seven-Harbor's shipping subsidiaries are suspended from trading, and the coastal freight routes into Shanghai and Handong are completely clear."

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of the warm tea and nodded once. "Clean work. Make sure the local forestry workers and truck drivers up near Changbai get their overdue wages settled through the court liquidation first. They should not lose their winter pay because of a few greedy men in Hong Kong."

"Already arranged, Young Master," Number 7 answered promptly. "The legal trustees have flagged employee payroll as the top priority claim."

"Good." Chen Xuan set the cup down and reached into his jacket pocket for his smartphone.

He carried only that one regular everyday phone. There were no backup handsets, no bulky encrypted bricks, just a slightly wornsmartphone with a clear silicone case and a photo of Qin Yuhan smiling beside a bowl of steamed crabs as the lock screen.

Three unread messages sat waiting on the chat app, all from his wife.

The first was sent at 11:15 AM: *Are you on the way back yet? Do not tell me your company's northern warehouse audit got snowed in.*

The second came at 12:30 PM: *Mom and Dad called. Grandpa is coming over for dinner tonight, and he already asked if you are making hot pot.*

The third arrived ten minutes ago: *Eat something warm before you board. Do not just drink tea on an empty stomach.*

A soft smile warmed Chen Xuan's face. His thumbs moved quickly over the screen as he typed back.

*Just headed south now. No snow delays. Tell Grandpa to sharpen his chess moves, because I brought back wild hazel mushrooms and fresh roasted pine nuts from the mountain. I will stop by the market for winter beef brisket and be home before 4:30 PM.*

Almost immediately, the typing indicator bounced at the top of the screen.

*You remembered the mushrooms! Drive safely from the airport, or take the subway if the tunnel traffic is jammed. I already put pear and ginger tea on the stove.*

Chen Xuan chuckled softly and tucked the phone back into his pocket.

"The weather in Shanghai is crisp today, Young Master," Number 7 said, folding a spare blanket neatly onto the opposite seat. "Six degrees Celsius, light north wind, clear skies before dusk. We have a sedan waiting at the private apron to take you directly to Emerald Riverside Villa."

Chen Xuan shook his head lazily. "Drop me near the Pudong Line 2 subway station instead. If I show up at the villa gate in a luxury sedan, the neighborhood aunties will ask questions, and besides, my favorite butcher stall right outside the metro exit saves the best winter beef brisket until 3:30 PM."

Number 7 did not argue or stiffen. He simply smiled and nodded. "Understood, Young Master. I will pack the pine nuts, mushrooms, and honey into your canvas tote bag so everything fits comfortably on your shoulder."

"Thanks," Chen Xuan said, leaning his head back against the soft headrest.

Outside the small oval window, the winter clouds began to break, revealing long stretches of pale brown farmland and silver rivers winding toward the Yangtze delta. Twelve hours ago, an international consortium had been dismantled across three time zones. Now, the only deadline on Chen Xuan's mind was beating the afternoon rush at the butcher stall so the beef broth would have a full two hours to simmer before dinner.
