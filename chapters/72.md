# Chapter 72: Panic at Old Street Station

At thirty-two minutes past eight, the lobby of Old Street Police Station smelled of stale cigarette smoke and fear. Station Director Cheng Biao paced behind the counter like a trapped pig, a cigarette shaking in one hand while the other hammered redial on the desk phone.

"Still dead!" Cheng Biao snarled, slamming the receiver hard enough to crack the plastic. He spun on Deputy Director Zhou Quan, face dark red. "What is wrong with the county police line? Eight calls to Chief Liang Guodong's office, six to his mobile, and not one rings through!"

Zhou Quan stood by the glass window, wiping sweaty palms on his pants. Four young patrol officers sat frozen at their desks, pretending to sort traffic tickets while stealing nervous looks at the shouting.

"Director Cheng Biao, calm down," Zhou Quan said tightly. "It is Monday morning. Chief Liang Guodong is in the eight-thirty roll call with County Magistrate Sun Weiming. His phone is probably on silent."

"And the radio operator went to roll call too?" Cheng Biao barked, pointing at the dead police radio. "What about Yan Tiehu? Why is his phone off? His crew was supposed to clear the west alley behind Mingde Hardware at eight! Nobody showed up, and Chairman Deng Yongchang's office just clicks dead!"

Zhou Quan swallowed hard. "Maybe it is that phone outage from midnight. Director Cheng Biao, should we move Chen Mingde to the county jail before nine so his family cannot make a scene outside?"

"Do not touch Chen Mingde!" Cheng Biao snapped. "Chief Liang Guodong told me to keep him in Holding Cell Two until Hongrui Real Estate's lawyer brings the move-out paper at noon. We have a signed injury report from Yunxi People's Hospital and a legal arrest warrant! The paperwork is ironclad!"

Zhou Quan shifted and glanced down the cell hallway. "Director Cheng Biao, the hospital clerk called me at seven sounding terrified. He said the X-ray computer showed someone outside checked the files last night. What if someone looks closely at that scan?"

"Who in Yunxi County dares check Chief Liang Guodong's file?" Cheng Biao sneered, taking a hard drag. "Stop shaking like a scared dog, Zhou Quan. Pour some tea and sit down."

He had barely finished when heavy tires screeched outside.

Three matte-black provincial SUVs braked hard across the driveway, blocking the gate. Doors slid open, and heavy boots hit the concrete in steady rhythm.

The glass lobby doors swung wide. Twelve officers strode inside: six in dark Provincial Anti-Corruption Office windbreakers and six in matte-black special police vests. The four young patrol officers jumped from their chairs in terror. Two officers blocked the front doors while two more guarded the gun room and cell hallway.

In the center walked a lean, sharp-eyed officer in his late forties wearing senior provincial rank badges.

Cheng Biao's cigarette dropped and hissed on the floor. His fat face went pale. With shaking hands he buttoned his wrinkled collar, hurried around the desk, and threw up a sweaty salute.

"Welcome, provincial leaders!" Cheng Biao blurted behind a frantic grin. "Old Street Station Director Cheng Biao reporting! We did not know a provincial team was coming, or we would have lined up outside! Please come into my office for tea!"

The lean officer ignored the salute. His cold eyes swept over Cheng Biao's sweaty forehead, then moved to Zhou Quan, whose knees shook behind the glass.

"I am Ji Changming, Provincial Police Discipline and Supervision Division," he said, voice flat as winter iron. "Step back from the desk, Cheng Biao. Keep your hands where I can see them."

Cheng Biao's smile cracked. "Leader Ji Changming, there must be a mistake. If this is about the Old Street demolition dispute, we only followed county orders. We answered an assault call at Mingde Hardware and locked up the suspect by the book."

Ji Changming pulled two printed sheets from his briefcase and dropped them onto the counter.

"Look at sheet one," Ji Changming ordered quietly.

Cheng Biao looked down, and his breath caught.

Printed in sharp detail was the hospital X-ray used to accuse Chen Mingde of breaking a worker's ribs. Red boxes highlighted the hidden file data: the real patient was twenty-nine years old, injured three months ago in a highway car crash. The file had been copied and renamed on Yunxi People's Hospital computer number four at eleven twenty on Friday night.

"Now look at sheet two," Ji Changming said.

Cheng Biao's hand shook against the counter. The second sheet showed the county arrest computer log. Chen Mingde's warrant had been typed under Cheng Biao's own code at eight eighteen on Friday evening, twelve full minutes before Old Street Station's patrol car even received the fake call or drove to Mingde Hardware.

"Twelve minutes before the supposed fight," Ji Changming said without blinking. "You invented a crime, pasted a three-month-old traffic X-ray onto a fifty-four-year-old man, and locked an innocent citizen in Holding Cell Two to steal his shop."

The four young patrol officers stared in horrified silence. One dropped his pen and backed against the wall.

Sweat stung Cheng Biao's eyes. Suddenly he spun and pointed a shaking finger straight at Zhou Quan.

"Leader Ji Changming, I did not know!" Cheng Biao yelled, voice shrill with panic. "Deputy Director Zhou Quan handled the whole file! He brought me the warrant to stamp and swore the scan was real! He must have taken money from Yan Tiehu behind my back!"

Zhou Quan stared for half a second, then his pale face twisted with pure rage.

"You shameless bastard, Cheng Biao!" Zhou Quan screamed, slamming his hand on the glass. "You sat in your office with Chief Liang Guodong on speakerphone! You ordered me to fake the warrant time before the patrol car even left! You took 200k yuan in cash from Chairman Deng Yongchang's driver last Friday in the back alley! I kept the tea box with your fingerprints in my locker!"

"Shut your lying mouth, Zhou Quan!" Cheng Biao roared. He lunged half a step before two special police officers locked his arms in an iron grip.

"Enough," Ji Changming said calmly. "Hou Liangping just radioed that Chief Liang Guodong and County Magistrate Sun Weiming were arrested at the county compound three minutes ago. Chairman Deng Yongchang's headquarters is being sealed right now. Finish arguing in the provincial interrogation room."

Cheng Biao went limp, eyes bulging in dread. "Liang Guodong is arrested? Already?"

"Cuff both of them," Ji Changming ordered.

Two sharp metal clicks echoed through the silent lobby. Steel cuffs bit into Cheng Biao's thick wrists and Zhou Quan's shaking hands in front of every officer and clerk. Zhou Quan sank half to his knees, sobbing that he would tell everything for a lighter sentence.

Ji Changming turned to the pale duty clerk behind the key board.

"You," Ji Changming said quietly. "Hand me the key to Holding Cell Two. Now."

Two kilometers away, Chen Xuan sat at the family table with a red-bean bun in his hand and glanced at the wall clock. Eight thirty-three. He took a bite.
