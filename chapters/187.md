# Chapter 187: Lunch Behind Jin Mao Tower

At twelve noon sharp, the glass towers of Lujiazui glittered under a pale winter sun.

Two minutes behind the towering spire of Jin Mao Tower, tucked down a quiet side lane most tourists walked right past, sat a cozy corner shop with a faded red wooden sign and fogged-up windows.

Chen Xuan pushed open the wooden door and spotted Qin Yuhan seated near the back window.

She wore a tailored cream wool coat over a dark sweater, her black hair tied back with a pearl clip.

Outside across the narrow lane, two ordinary-looking men in plain matte-black jackets sat quietly on a stone bench with paper cups of tea, keeping a relaxed watch within fifty meters of the door.

Chen Xuan unzipped his faded dark-blue windbreaker and slid onto the wooden bench across from her.

"You are fast," Chen Xuan smiled as a waiter set down two steaming bowls of braised beef noodles and a dish of chilled cucumber. "Did you already hear about my twenty thousand yuan bonus?"

Qin Yuhan rested her chin lightly on her palm, her dark eyes sparkling. "Half of Lujiazui heard Haiyue Group doubled Hengxin Logistics' annual contract before ten o'clock. And somehow, the most mysterious man in Shanghai still picks a thirty-two-yuan noodle shop over a private room at IFC Mall."

"Good broth does not care how tall the skyscraper next door is," Chen Xuan replied easily, snapping open his bamboo chopsticks and sliding the chili oil jar toward her. "Besides, your father's shipping managers would probably spill their soup if they saw me walking into their favorite steakhouse."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, a bright, genuine sound.

"Speaking of my father," Qin Yuhan said, her tone turning quieter through the rising steam. "He told me everything about our family dinner on Friday night."

Chen Xuan picked up a tender slice of beef. "Did General Qin Zhengguo enjoy the wine?"

Qin Yuhan shook her head with a dry, amazed smile. "He barely touched his glass, Chen Xuan. You should have seen my father's face when he described it to me."

She leaned forward slightly, lowering her voice under the clatter of bowls. "My uncle, General Qin Zhengguo, has spent forty years in uniform. He has stared down border standoffs and typhoons without blinking. In my whole life, I have never seen him stunned speechless."

"And at that dinner?" Chen Xuan asked lightly, his dark eyes steady on hers.

"When my father mentioned your name and what happened at Shenzhen Pier Nine," Qin Yuhan murmured, watching his reaction, "Uncle Zhengguo pieced together how fast the Seven-Harbor Consortium's southern accounts were frozen across ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank, and how cleanly their thugs vanished off the docks without a single civilian hurt. He froze with his teacup halfway to his mouth."

She let out a small, quiet laugh. "Father said Uncle sat staring at his teacup for five straight minutes without saying a word. Then he set it down, lit a cigarette at the dining table even though my aunt hates smoke indoors, and told my father one sentence."

"What sentence?" Chen Xuan asked, sipping hot soup.

"He said, 'Qin Zhengming, never mistake that young man for an ordinary clerk, and never let anyone in our family owe him a slight. Whoever stands behind him can move mountains without making a sound.'" Qin Yuhan met Chen Xuan's eyes, half teasing, half serious. "My father barely slept all weekend thinking about that."

Chen Xuan set his spoon down gently, his face calm and steady.

"General Qin Zhengguo is a sharp soldier," Chen Xuan said evenly. "He cares about order and hates seeing foreign money bully local ports. I simply did what needed to be done in Shenzhen. Your family has nothing to worry about."

Qin Yuhan studied his quiet face for a moment.

Slowly, her playful smile faded. She set her chopsticks across her bowl and folded her hands on the table.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan asked quietly, her voice earnest. "Tell me honestly, just between the two of us."

"Go ahead."

"After Shenzhen Pier Nine, and after the big banks shut down their southern credit lines," Qin Yuhan said, her brows drawing together, "is the Seven-Harbor Consortium really finished?"

Around them, the noodle shop stayed lively and warm.

Chen Xuan ate a slice of chilled cucumber unhurriedly, then rested his forearms on the wooden table. His dark eyes grew calm and cool, like winter water under thin ice.

"Tell me, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan said softly. "If you cut off a snake's tail in the south, do you turn your back and assume the head is dead?"

Qin Yuhan's breath caught slightly. "No. You watch where the head crawls."

"Exactly," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Seven-Harbor took a hard blow in Shenzhen. They lost Pier Nine, their local cover, and billions in bank credit across Guangdong and Hong Kong. Most of their junior partners are already running."

He tapped one finger lightly against the table. "But a group that spent twelve years building hidden shipping lanes from Hong Kong to the northern provinces does not vanish after one bad week. Cornered in the south, they will rush to salvage whatever valuable pieces they hid in the north before winter locks everything down."

Qin Yuhan exhaled slowly, a sharp glint lighting her eyes. "I had the exact same feeling this morning."

"Oh?" Chen Xuan raised an eyebrow.

"At breakfast, my father told me he ordered Haiyue Group's Shanghai shipping desks to flag any strange winter cargo bookings heading north," Qin Yuhan murmured. "Two of our freight brokers near Shanghai Port mentioned someone has been paying triple the normal rush fee for cold-weather flatbeds since Friday night."

Chen Xuan's eyes narrowed just a fraction, though his voice stayed relaxed. "Your father has good instincts. Paying triple for winter flatbeds in December means someone is in a big hurry."

"Be careful, Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan said softly, nudging the dish of cucumbers closer to him. "People like that are most dangerous when cornered."

"I am always careful," Chen Xuan replied, his cool look melting back into a warm smile. "Now eat your noodles before they swell up and turn soft."

Qin Yuhan shook her head with a small smile and picked up her chopsticks. For the next twenty minutes, they traded light jokes about Hu Dewei's purple face at Hengxin Logistics and Chen Hao's oath under the Oriental Pearl Tower to get into Fudan University.

Outside in the crisp lane behind Jin Mao Tower, Qin Yuhan wrapped her cream scarf around her neck beside her silver sedan.

"Call me if anything unusual turns up on your desk, alright?" she asked quietly.

"I will," Chen Xuan promised with a light wave.

As her car pulled onto the Lujiazui avenue, Chen Xuan strolled calmly back toward Hengxin Logistics. In less than three hours, the very clue they had just talked about was going to land straight on top of his wooden desk.
