# Chapter 132: Silence on the Stage

For ten full seconds, nobody in the three-hundred-seat auditorium moved a muscle.

Three hundred port directors, trucking chairmen, and local reporters stared back and forth between the plain young clerk at the corner table and the stiff man standing behind the wooden podium.

Up on stage, the bright spotlights suddenly felt as hot as a summer oven.

A bead of sweat rolled down Song Baisheng's temple into his white collar.

In the front row, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang kept his jaw tight and his eyes hard.

At the side table, Li Weiming sat frozen with his pen pressed against his notebook.

Song Baisheng forced out a harsh, dry laugh into the microphone.

"Well," Song Baisheng said, his voice pitched too loud and too fast. "It seems we have an eager young clerk from Shanghai today. Hengxin Logistics, did you say? I have been in provincial freight for twenty years, and I have never even heard of your company."

A few of Song Baisheng's close allies near the front chuckled nervously, trying to break the silence.

"Young man," Song Baisheng went on, waving one hand as if brushing away a fly. "International shipping is not the same as running three old delivery vans down a county road. Modern freight needs European clearing standards. Alpine Marine Data Services is a partner chosen by our technical team so Sha City ports can connect with Europe smoothly. As for the 15 million yuan setup fee, building a province-wide system costs real money. A small back-office clerk cannot be expected to understand international shipping."

He wiped his upper lip quickly and forced his smile back onto his face.

"Now, let us not waste the precious time of our provincial leaders on amateur guesses," Song Baisheng declared loudly, gesturing toward the waiting staff with the signing pens. "Will the twenty lead chairmen please step up to the stage?"

At the back corner table, Sun Haoran sank lower in his chair, his face pale with dread.

"Chen Xuan, stop now, please," Sun Haoran whispered urgently, gripping the tablecloth. "Look at Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang's face! Do not say another word!"

Chen Xuan did not look worried at all.

Nobody stepped toward the stage.

Instead, two rows behind Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang, a chair slid neatly back across the carpet.

Qin Yuhan stood up.

She wore a tailored charcoal blazer over a crisp blouse, her posture straight and calm.

"Director Song," Qin Yuhan said clearly. Her voice was cool, steady, and carried crisp authority across the entire hall. "Haiyue Group handles more than thirty percent of the container cargo in this province. Would you say that we also do not understand international shipping?"

Song Baisheng's smile cracked down the middle. "President Qin, of course not. Haiyue Group is a pillar of Sha City. I was only explaining to that small clerk that modern contracts are complex."

"His questions were completely accurate," Qin Yuhan cut in smoothly, looking down at the printed sheet inside her folder. "Haiyue Group checked the Swiss business records last night. Alpine Marine Data Services carries Zurich registration number CHE-419.827.603. It was opened nine months ago at a rented mailbox address on Bahnhofstrasse, with zero full-time employees."

A sharp gasp rippled through the hall. Reporters snapped their heads toward Qin Yuhan, and two television cameras swung smoothly to frame her face.

"Furthermore," Qin Yuhan continued, lifting her cool eyes back to the stage, "the single owner listed behind that mailbox firm is an overseas shell company on a Caribbean island. Under Article 14, every port in Sha City would have to send live ship schedules, fuel dock numbers, and grain warehouse stock straight to that foreign server every four hours, with zero Chinese oversight."

She closed her blue folder with a crisp snap that echoed through the speakers.

"Haiyue Group will not sign a single line of this agreement," Qin Yuhan stated firmly. "And we demand to know who hid Article 14 in a provincial contract ten minutes before the vote."

For two seconds, the hall stayed breathless.

Then the entire room erupted.

"Wait a minute!" shouted Director Liang of Dongwan Deepwater Port, slamming his leather folder shut and shooting to his feet in the fourth row. "A mailbox firm with zero employees? Song Baisheng, you swore to me last night this was a state-backed provincial platform!"

"Look at page forty-nine!" yelled another port chairman from the left aisle, waving the thick contract in the air. "If we refuse to upload our cargo lists after signing, we owe a 20 million yuan fine to Wanfeng Freight! What kind of trap is this?"

One after another, six major port directors rose angrily from their seats across the auditorium. Chairs scraped loudly against the floor as furious voices overlapped.

"Who actually owns that Zurich company?"

"Explain the 15 million yuan setup fee right now!"

"Why did the transport office hand this out ten minutes before the cameras rolled?"

Up on stage, Song Baisheng stumbled half a step backward. His face turned a sickly gray-white under the bright lights, and sweat soaked straight through his collar.

"Quiet! Please, listen to me!" Song Baisheng shouted, slapping the microphone so hard a shrill squeal tore through the speakers. "There is a printing mistake! Let our lawyers explain after we sign!"

"Nobody is signing anything!" roared a gray-haired shipping chairman from the center row, tossing his folder onto the table. "This whole thing stinks to high heaven!"

At the side table, Li Weiming's calm mask shattered. His hand jerked, knocking his silver pen onto the marble tiles. He stared wildly at the cameras, then at Qin Yuhan, and finally snapped his head toward the corner table where Chen Xuan sat quietly drinking jasmine tea.

In the front row, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang's face flushed a dark, furious red. Behind him, three provincial television cameras were still blinking red, recording every second of the collapse.

Wu Zhenbang shot to his feet, grabbed the trembling stage host by the shoulder, and barked in a low, venomous voice.

"Cut the microphones! Call a break right now! Get those cameras out of here!"

With a dull thump, the auditorium speakers went dead. A hasty voice over the wall intercom announced a thirty-minute recess.

The Provincial Freight Alliance vote had collapsed live on stage before a single pen touched paper.

Angry chairmen stood up in loud clusters, grabbing their briefcases as they streamed toward the exits. Far in the back corner, Sun Haoran sat frozen like a man who had just watched lightning strike the stage.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran whispered, swallowing hard. "You really just blew up the whole provincial forum with three questions."

Chen Xuan screwed the steel cap neatly back onto his green thermos.

"I only asked what was printed on page forty-eight, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said mildly, standing up and smoothing his plain white shirt. "Come on. Let us stretch our legs during the break."
