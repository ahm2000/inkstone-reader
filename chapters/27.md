# Chapter 27: Nine O'Clock Monday

Party Secretary Fan Weiguo adjusted his tie. It was already straight. He adjusted it again.

He had slept badly for three nights. Every time he closed his eyes, he saw a matte-black card on his desk.

Behind him, twenty-one officials stood in two rows by rank. Every pair of eyes was fixed on the back of his collar.

Fan Weiguo stood at the front, as the highest-ranking official in Yunxi. County Magistrate Tao Shu waited half a step to his left, wearing a warm, composed smile. It was the smile of a man who had never once coveted Fan Weiguo's seat.

Next came Kang Guangming of the County Discipline Inspection Commission, then Shao Wenhua of the Organization Department, then the bureau heads.

Just past Shao Wenhua, the row had a gap the width of one man. Nobody looked at it.

That spot had belonged to Lu Wenzhe. Right now, he was sitting in his own office with two Discipline Commission officers stationed outside his door.

Ma Baoguo, Chief of the Housing Bureau, had once called Lu Wenzhe "Elder Brother" over drinks. Today he stood well clear of the empty space.

A bright red banner stretched across the compound gate: *Warmly Welcome the Provincial Inspection Team to Yunxi.* Beneath it sat a huge fruit basket.

The provincial inspection team had come to audit the county's records and question its officials. Its report could lift one career, ruin another, or send a third man out of the building between two silent officers.

"Secretary," his young aide murmured. "Your hands."

Fan Weiguo quickly pressed his palms flat against his trousers.

* * *

Across the street, a Golden Wheat Bakery truck idled at the curb, its rear doors open and warm steam drifting into the morning air.

Number Seventy-three ran one of the Black Pine Guard's bakery trucks. For nine months he had sold steamed buns to the compound gate guards every morning. He wiped his flour-dusted forearms and kept an eye on the road.

Pang Jinquan, the gate guard, took two buns. "You smell trouble, Number Seventy-three?"

"I smell bread, Pang Jinquan."

Beneath the counter, Number Seventy-three's thumb tapped across a phone no larger than a matchbox. He sent a message to Number One, head of the Black Pine Guard.

*Three black sedans, no flags, turning in. Eighteen minutes early. No one here knows the Boss's name.*

Number One's reply came back brisk and dry.

*Good. The Boss is on the early bus from Yunxi and knows nothing about this. Keep it that way.*

Number Seventy-three set a steamed bun over the phone and smiled at the next customer.

* * *

On the early bus out of Yunxi, Chen Xuan leaned asleep against the window, his thermos resting in his lap and his mouth slightly open.

His phone buzzed twice against his knee. On the third ring he picked up without opening his eyes.

"Brother." His sister Chen Yuan's voice burst from the speaker like a boiling kettle. "Did you eat? Wait, are you asleep right now?"

"Morning, Chen Yuan. No. Okay, maybe a little."

"You are asleep. Grandma says you left your jacket on the porch, and the cat slept on it all night. Big Brother, you're hopeless. Mom says you'd better wear a coat, or she'll march into your office with the ladle!"

"Not the ladle." Chen Xuan sat up with a soft groan. "Chen Yuan, please. I'll wear it, I swear. And I ate, too. Half a bun."

"Half a bun is not breakfast."

"Then tell the cat I'm sorry, and that I want my jacket back." He chuckled softly. "Tell Mom I love her. Actually, don't tell her I said that. She'll cry."

Chen Yuan hung up on him, holding back a laugh. The older woman in the next seat had heard the whole call. She broke an orange in half and handed it over.

"Thanks, Auntie." Chen Xuan smiled, ate the orange, and closed his eyes again.

* * *

At the compound gate, the three black sedans glided in without a sound. Both rows of officials snapped to attention.

The middle car rolled to a stop, yet no one stepped out. Through the window, Fan Weiguo could see a single man seated in the back. It was Vice Director Gao Zhengyi, head of the inspection team.

Gao Zhengyi smoothed his jacket and straightened his tie. He sat up stiffly, like a schoolboy bracing for his headmaster. Once, his hand slid to the inside pocket of his jacket and stayed there, as if something heavy lay inside.

Fan Weiguo forgot to breathe.

He had welcomed many senior leaders. Not one had ever fixed his tie for a county government. A man did that only for someone he feared or deeply respected.

And Fan Weiguo knew that hand on the pocket. He had held his own hand over his own drawer all weekend. Had Gao Zhengyi received a card too?

Was it the retired official staying out by the reservoir? Or someone else in Yunxi?

His secretary leaned close, holding a phone. "Secretary, I checked every name tied to the Old Street complaint. Chen Xuan is the hardware shop owner's nephew. Just a logistics clerk. His file says he's plain, honest, and completely ordinary. Not even a parking ticket."

"Completely ordinary?" Fan Weiguo whispered. A background that spotless made no sense at all.

The car door opened. Fan Weiguo hurried forward with both hands extended in a half-bow.

"Vice Director Gao. Welcome. The county leadership has been waiting here since dawn!"

"Secretary Fan. Magistrate Tao." Gao Zhengyi's voice was quiet and weary, which was worse than a shout. "Please take down that banner."

Fan Weiguo's smile faltered. An aide immediately sprinted off to fetch a ladder.

"Take the fruit away as well. The team accepts nothing, not even an apple. Give it to your gate guards' families."

Pang Jinquan strolled back with a bun in his mouth and stopped in front of the basket.

"For my wife?" he asked around the bun.

"And your grandchildren," Gao Zhengyi replied mildly. "Also, cancel the banquet lunch. We'll eat in your canteen and pay the same rate as everyone else."

Ma Baoguo hurried forward to take his briefcase. "Vice Director, allow me."

"I have two hands, Chief Ma." Gao Zhengyi kept his grip on the handle. "The team carries its own documents. That way nobody can claim we took anything from you."

Tao Shu stepped forward smoothly, looking eager to help.

"Vice Director, we wouldn't want your team to lose even a single day. My county self-inspection group will have every Old Street file organized and ready for you by tonight."

Gao Zhengyi nodded as politely as if accepting a cup of tea.

"That's very considerate, Magistrate Tao. However, the team will review the records itself. Anyone who volunteers to tidy a file before the inspectors arrive has just told me which file to open first."

A muscle ticked in Tao Shu's jaw. Fan Weiguo caught it and felt a brief spark of satisfaction. If Gao Zhengyi refused Tao Shu's files, whose files would he pull instead?

Tao Shu's smile had not been quite right since Friday night, when two masked men had stood beside his window. Neither man had spoken of that night. Neither would.

Gao Zhengyi swept his gaze across the two rows of officials, and every head lowered. Kang Guangming and Shao Wenhua kept their eyes on the floor. Neither had slept. At the far end, Gao Zhengyi's eyes stopped.

"Director Mai."

Mai Zhenhua, Director of the County Police Bureau, stepped forward on stiff legs. Since Friday night, he flinched whenever a stranger in a dark suit stood close behind him.

"The complaint file I read on the road holds an honest detention notice from Old Street Station." Gao Zhengyi spoke lightly, as if commenting on the weather. "Its duty officer is Wei Kun, I'm told. We'll visit that station first. I want to shake his hand. And Director Mai, bring the original detention notice, not a photocopy. If the time has been altered, I will want to know who held the pen."

Mai Zhenhua did not blink.

Behind him, the bureau chiefs began to murmur.

"I always said Wei Kun was a fine officer," Ma Baoguo put in.

"You've never even met him," the man beside him hissed.

Mai Zhenhua had spent thirty years reading signals from his superiors. He read this one twice. Someone very high up had taken note of Wei Kun by name.

Beside Fan Weiguo, Tao Shu spoke in a low murmur, his smile still in place. "The vice director goes home on Friday, Secretary. We stay. The Old Street approvals went out under your Party Committee seal. My name isn't on a single one of them."

Fan Weiguo barely heard him. Only one question echoed in his head: who in Yunxi was powerful enough to make a provincial vice director straighten his tie?
