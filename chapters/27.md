# Chapter 27: Midnight on the Rooftop

At eleven-thirty on Sunday night, the sixth-floor apartment in Building 2 was dark and quiet. Steady breathing drifted from the main bedroom where Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang slept. Behind the closed door of the spare room, Wu Lanying and her two children had finally fallen asleep.

Chen Xuan sat up on the narrow couch without turning on a lamp. Moving quietly so not a single floorboard creaked, he slipped into a plain dark jacket and picked up his scratched green thermos from the low coffee table. Earlier that evening, he had filled it with boiling water and a pinch of cheap jasmine tea from his mother's tin.

He checked that both bedroom doors were shut, slid the brass latch back by hand so it would not click, and stepped out into the dark hallway. Pulling the door shut behind him, he walked up the half-flight of stairs to the roof landing, lifted the rusted iron bar smoothly, and stepped out onto the concrete rooftop of Building 2.

A sharp river wind met him in the dark. Below him, Yunxi County lay mostly asleep. Old Street formed a dim line of roofs to the west where Chen Mingde's hardware shop sat behind bent shutters. To the east, bright white floodlights burned over Pier Four along the river bend.

Chen Xuan leaned one elbow on the low concrete wall, unscrewed the plastic cup-lid of his green thermos, and took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea.

Two days ago, when he stepped off his private jet at the airfield outside Yunxi County, the Black Pine Guard team escorting him had already spread across town without leaving a trace. Nobody in Fuxing New Village knew they were here. Nobody at Old Street Station realized their files were being opened layer by layer while Chen Xuan stayed quietly inside his parents' home, keeping his father away from the station gate.

He had expected ordinary county rot. A greedy developer like Cheng Biao, hired thugs like Yan Tiehu, a corrupt deputy like Deng Yongchang, and a station chief eager to stamp Hongrui Real Estate's demolition order.

Then his second matte-black phone pulsed once inside his jacket pocket.

It was not the soft single buzz of a normal update. It was a sharp double beat, followed by three seconds of stillness and a second identical pulse.

In seven years of leading the Black Pine Guard across the country, Number 1 had used that emergency signal only twice. The first time was a cross-border money attack aimed at three coastal financial hubs. The second was a provincial gang plotting to kill a central inspector.

Chen Xuan's posture did not change. He did not hurry to reach into his pocket. He finished his sip of tea calmly, set the plastic lid loosely over the thermos, and turned smoothly toward the stairwell door behind him.

The heavy iron roof door swung inward without a single scrape.

Out of the dark stairwell stepped a tall, lean figure in dark weatherproof gear. A low cap brim and raised collar kept his face hidden in shadow.

The operative crossed the concrete on soundless boots and stopped three steps away, holding a sealed matte-black tablet in both gloved hands.

"From Number 1," Number 7 said quietly, bowing slightly from the waist as he held out the tablet. His voice was clipped, low, and tight with an urgency he barely kept behind his training. "The full proof chain was double-checked thirty minutes ago. Every file is signed and locked to your thumbprint. The secure voice line is live and waiting on standby."

Chen Xuan took the cold metal tablet in his left hand, his dark eyes resting steadily on Number 7's shadowed face. "Number 1 does not trigger the double-beat signal for a county demolition fight," Chen Xuan remarked softly. "How many teams did he pull off standby?"

Number 7's gloved fingers tightened slightly at his sides. "Forty-two regional audit and scouting teams across fourteen provinces, Sir," he answered without raising his gaze. "He froze all routine jobs at midnight on Saturday and took personal command of the checking desk."

"And Yunxi County?" Chen Xuan asked evenly. "Did anyone at Old Street Station or inside Deng Yongchang's circle notice the sweep?"

"None," Number 7 replied at once. "Old Street Station's night shift is asleep at the front desk. Cheng Biao is drinking in a private room at the riverside hotel. Deng Yongchang has not left his courtyard since nine o'clock. Every traffic camera, cell tower, and county record was copied without setting off a single local alarm."

Chen Xuan studied the operative for a quiet moment. Even in the dark, he could see the stiff line of Number 7's shoulders and the tight set of his jaw. Nine years of service had trained Number 7 never to show emotion during a handoff, yet the air around him felt pulled tight like wire.

"You read the cover sheet," Chen Xuan said calmly. It was not a question.

Number 7 paused for half a heartbeat, his chin dipping lower in quiet agreement. "Only the clearance label needed to carry the tablet, Sir. When Number 1 sealed the fourth folder, his hand did not shake. Still, he ordered every courier on the relay to destroy their travel passes on arrival and wait out of earshot."

"Wait out of earshot," Chen Xuan repeated quietly.

"Yes. He said what sits inside Folder Four does not belong in the ears of any operative below his desk." Number 7 paused, then added in a flatter, steadier voice, "The area around Building 2 and Fuxing New Village is completely clean. No local cars, county officers, or Hongrui Real Estate lookouts within eight hundred meters. Your family's stairwell is clear."

Chen Xuan looked down briefly at the dark glass of the tablet, then back up at the motionless figure before him. "You have been awake since Friday night, Number 7."

"Still on shift," Number 7 answered at once, his posture steady. "No relief is needed until the county is settled."

"Take your post by the stairwell," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Keep your eyes on the street."

Number 7 bowed once more, stepped backward without a sound, and slipped into the deep shadow beside the concrete stairwell wall. Within two seconds, he had vanished so completely into the dark corner that an ordinary resident coming up to check a clothesline would have sworn the rooftop stood empty.

Chen Xuan turned back toward the sleeping town below. Setting his scratched green thermos neatly against the inside of the concrete wall, he held the matte-black tablet in his left hand and pressed his right thumb against the dark glass sensor at the corner.

A pale blue glow lit his calm, unreadable face as the lock opened, and the first page of a twenty-year ledger appeared in the midnight wind.
