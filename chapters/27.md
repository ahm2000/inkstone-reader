# Chapter 27: Midnight on the Rooftop

At eleven-thirty on Sunday night, the apartment on the sixth floor of Building 2 was completely dark. Only the pale, filtered glow of the courtyard streetlamp seeped through the thin floral curtains in the living room, painting faint bars of amber across the tiled floor and the folded edge of the dining table.

From the master bedroom came the slow, even breathing of Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang. Behind the closed door of the spare room, Wu Lanying and her two children had finally fallen into an exhausted sleep.

Chen Xuan sat up quietly on the narrow couch where he had been resting fully dressed. He did not turn on the lamp. Moving with an unhurried, practiced economy that made not even the old wooden floorboards creak, he slipped his arms into a plain dark windbreaker and picked up his scratched green thermos from the low coffee table. Earlier in the evening, he had filled it with boiling water and a small pinch of inexpensive jasmine tea leaves from his mother's tin.

He paused by the entrance, checked that the bedroom doors were firmly shut, then slid the brass latch back by hand so it would not click. Stepping out into the stairwell of Building 2, he pulled the apartment door gently behind him until it rested flush against the frame without waking anyone inside.

The stairwell was cold and smelled faintly of damp lime plaster, dried garlic hung outside a fourth-floor doorway, and decades of coal dust settled into the corners of the concrete steps. No motion-sensor bulb flickered on; the sixth-floor fixture had burned out two winters ago and had never been replaced.

Chen Xuan climbed the final half-flight of stairs to the roof access landing. Above him stood a heavy sheet-iron fire door streaked with rust along its lower seam. He lifted the flat iron latch bar smoothly, pushed the door outward with his shoulder, and stepped onto the wide concrete rooftop of Building 2.

A sharp late-autumn wind met him at once, carrying the chill of the river and the distant, damp scent of wet river silt and coal smoke. Above Yunxi County, thin gray clouds drifted slowly across a pale, cold moon, washing the rooftop's cracked tar seams, rusted television aerials, and empty clotheslines in silver light.

Chen Xuan walked to the southern parapet, rested one elbow casually on the rough concrete ledge, and looked out over the sleeping county town.

From six stories up, Yunxi County looked small and quiet, almost fragile in the dark. Row after row of low tiled roofs and aging five-story residential blocks stretched toward the dark ribbon of the Yunxi River. Half a kilometer to the west, the narrow, crooked lane of Old Street lay under a handful of dim yellow streetlamps. Somewhere beneath those dark eaves stood Chen Mingde's hardware shop, its corrugated shutters still bent inward from Friday afternoon, its workbench covered in undisturbed dust.

Further east, where the river curved past the edge of town, a cluster of harsh white floodlights burned steadily against the dark water. Pier Four never truly slept, even on a Sunday night near midnight.

Chen Xuan unscrewed the plastic cup-lid of his scratched green thermos, poured a small measure of hot jasmine tea, and took a slow sip. The steam vanished instantly into the cold air, leaving a faint, clean warmth against his chest.

Two days ago, when he had stepped down from the private jet at the regional airfield outside Yunxi County, the Black Pine Guard had already fanned out across the town like ink dissolving into dark water. No one in Fuxing New Village had seen them, and no one at Old Street Station realized their records were being peeled open layer by layer while Chen Xuan stayed quietly inside his parents' sixth-floor apartment, keeping his father away from the police station.

He had expected a local land grab. He had expected a corrupt county deputy, a greedy developer like Cheng Biao, a handful of hired thugs like Yan Tiehu, and a rotten station chief protecting Hongrui Real Estate's demolition contract.

The quiet hum inside his inner jacket pocket told him it was none of those things.

His secondary matte-black phone pulsed once against his ribs.

It was not the soft, single tremor of a routine status update. It was a sharp, unmistakable double beat, followed by three seconds of silence and a second identical pulse.

In seven years of commanding the Black Pine Guard across the entire country, Number One had triggered that emergency-priority signal only twice. The first time had been during a coordinated cross-border currency attack that threatened three coastal financial hubs. The second had been when a provincial syndicate attempted to assassinate a sitting central inspector.

Chen Xuan did not stiffen. He did not rush to pull the phone out of his pocket. Instead, he took one more unhurried sip from the green plastic cap, set the lid loosely back over the mouth of the thermos, and turned calmly toward the concrete stairwell housing behind him.

Across the rooftop, the heavy rusted iron door opened without a single metallic scrape.

Whoever had come up the stairs had oiled the hinges silently beforehand or lifted the door's weight cleanly off its pins as it swung inward. Out of the pitch-black stairwell stepped a tall, lean figure dressed in dark, nondescript weatherproof clothing that absorbed the moonlight rather than reflecting it. A low cap brim and a raised collar kept the operative's face completely obscured in shadow.

Inside the Black Pine Guard, no operative knew another member's real name or face, not even Number One. They existed solely as numbers, stripped of personal history, bound by discipline and silence, answering only to the man they called the Boss.

The operative moved across the cracked concrete on soundless soles, stopping three paces in front of Chen Xuan. In both gloved hands, the figure held a sealed matte-black encrypted tablet inside a thin carbon casing.

"Boss," Number Seven said quietly, bowing slightly from the waist as he extended the tablet forward. His voice was low, clipped, and free of wasted words. "From Number One. The full forensic chain was cross-verified thirty minutes ago. Every annex is signed and locked to your biometric key. The encrypted audio channel is live and holding on standby."

Chen Xuan reached out and took the tablet. Its metal housing was cold from the night air, carrying a faint, steady weight in his palm.

"You have been awake since Friday night, Number Seven," Chen Xuan remarked softly.

"Still on shift, Boss," Number Seven answered without lifting his gaze. "Perimeters around Building 2 and Fuxing New Village remain clean. No local vehicles or county personnel within eight hundred meters."

Chen Xuan gave a slight nod. Number Seven bowed once more, stepped backward without a sound, and retreated smoothly into the deep shadow beside the concrete stairwell housing. Within two seconds, the operative blended so completely into the dark angle of the wall that an ordinary passerby could have walked across the entire rooftop and sworn they were completely alone.

Chen Xuan turned back toward the sleeping lights of Yunxi County. Resting his scratched green thermos neatly against the inside of the concrete parapet, he held the matte-black tablet in his left hand and pressed his right thumb against the dark glass sensor at its corner.

A pale, cold blue glow bloomed across his calm face as the seal unlocked, and the first page of a twenty-year ledger opened in the midnight wind.
