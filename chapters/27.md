# Chapter 27: Midnight on the Rooftop

At eleven-thirty on Sunday night, the sixth-floor apartment in Building 2 was dark and quiet. Even breathing drifted from the master bedroom where Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang slept, and behind the closed door of the spare room, Wu Lanying and her two children had finally found rest.

Chen Xuan sat up on the narrow couch without turning on a lamp. Moving with practiced quiet so not a single floorboard creaked, he slipped into a plain dark windbreaker and picked up his scratched green thermos from the low coffee table. Earlier in the evening, he had filled it with boiling water and a pinch of cheap jasmine tea from his mother's tin.

He checked that both bedroom doors were shut, slid the brass latch back by hand so it would not click, and stepped out into the dark stairwell. Pulling the door flush behind him, he climbed the half-flight to the roof landing, lifted the rusted iron latch bar smoothly, and stepped out onto the concrete rooftop of Building 2.

A sharp river wind met him in the dark. Below him, Yunxi County lay mostly asleep, Old Street a dim line of roofs to the west where Chen Mingde's hardware shop sat behind bent shutters, and Pier Four a cluster of bright white floodlights burning along the eastern bend of the river.

Chen Xuan leaned one elbow on the southern parapet, unscrewed the plastic cup-lid of his green thermos, and took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea.

Two days ago, when he had stepped off his private jet at the regional airfield outside Yunxi County, the Black Pine Guard escorting him had already dispersed across the town without leaving a ripple. No one in Fuxing New Village knew they were here. No one at Old Street Station realized their records were being peeled open layer by layer while Chen Xuan stayed quietly inside his parents' apartment, keeping his father away from the station gate.

He had expected ordinary county rot. A greedy developer like Cheng Biao, hired thugs like Yan Tiehu, a corrupt deputy like Deng Yongchang, and a station chief eager to rubber-stamp Hongrui Real Estate's demolition order.

Then his secondary matte-black phone pulsed once inside his jacket pocket.

It was not the soft single tremor of a routine status update. It was a sharp double beat, followed by three seconds of stillness and a second identical pulse.

In seven years of commanding the Black Pine Guard across the country, Number One had triggered that emergency-priority signal only twice. The first had been a coordinated cross-border currency attack aimed at three coastal financial hubs. The second had been a provincial syndicate plotting to assassinate a sitting central inspector.

Chen Xuan's posture did not change. He did not rush to reach into his pocket. He finished his sip of tea unhurriedly, set the plastic lid loosely over the thermos, and turned calmly toward the stairwell housing behind him.

The heavy iron roof door swung inward without a single metallic scrape.

Out of the black stairwell stepped a tall, lean figure in dark weatherproof gear. A low cap brim and raised collar kept the operative's face in shadow. Inside the Black Pine Guard, no member knew another's real name or face, not even Number One. They existed only as numbers, stripped of personal history, bound by discipline and silence, answering solely to Chen Xuan.

The operative crossed the concrete on soundless soles and halted three paces away, holding a sealed matte-black encrypted tablet in both gloved hands.

"From Number One," Number Seven said quietly, bowing slightly from the waist as he extended the tablet forward. His voice was clipped, low, and tight with an urgency he barely kept behind his discipline. "The full forensic chain was cross-verified thirty minutes ago. Every annex is signed and locked to your biometric key. The encrypted audio channel is live and holding on standby."

Chen Xuan took the cold metal tablet in his left hand, his dark eyes resting steadily on Number Seven's shadowed face. "Number One does not trigger the double-beat signal for a county demolition dispute," Chen Xuan remarked softly. "How many cells did he pull off standby?"

Number Seven's gloved fingers tightened by a fraction at his sides. "Forty-two regional audit and reconnaissance cells across fourteen provinces, Sir," he answered without lifting his gaze. "He froze all nonessential assignments at midnight on Saturday and took personal command of the verification desk."

"And Yunxi County?" Chen Xuan asked evenly. "Did anyone at Old Street Station or inside Deng Yongchang's circle notice the sweep?"

"None," Number Seven replied at once. "Old Street Station's night shift is asleep at the desk. Cheng Biao is drinking in a private room at the riverside hotel. Deng Yongchang has not left his courtyard since nine o'clock. Every traffic camera, relay tower, and municipal ledger within the county was mirrored without tripping a single local alert."

Chen Xuan studied the operative for a quiet moment. Even in the dark, he could see the rigid set of Number Seven's shoulders and the faint tension along his jaw. Nine years of service had trained Number Seven never to show emotion during a handoff, yet the air around him felt taut as drawn wire.

"You read the cover sheet," Chen Xuan said calmly. It was not a question.

Number Seven hesitated for half a heartbeat, his chin dipping lower in silent acknowledgment. "Only the clearance header required to transport the slate, Sir. When Number One sealed the fourth folder, his hand did not shake, yet he ordered every courier on the relay to destroy their transit credentials on arrival and wait out of earshot."

"Wait out of earshot," Chen Xuan repeated quietly.

"Yes. He said what sits inside Folder Four does not belong in the ears of any operative below his desk." Number Seven paused, then added in a flatter, steadier tone, "Perimeters around Building 2 and Fuxing New Village remain clean. No local vehicles, county officers, or Hongrui Real Estate lookouts within eight hundred meters. Your family's stairwell is clear."

Chen Xuan looked down briefly at the dark glass of the tablet, then back up at the motionless figure before him. "You have been awake since Friday night, Number Seven."

"Still on shift," Number Seven answered immediately, his posture unyielding. "No relief is needed until the county is settled."

"Take your post by the stairwell," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Keep your eyes on the street."

Number Seven bowed once more, stepped backward without making a sound, and retreated smoothly into the deep shadow beside the concrete stairwell housing. Within two seconds, he had vanished so completely into the dark angle of the wall that an ordinary resident coming up to check a clothesline would have sworn the rooftop stood empty.

Chen Xuan turned back toward the sleeping town below. Resting his scratched green thermos neatly against the inside of the concrete parapet, he held the matte-black tablet in his left hand and pressed his right thumb against the dark glass sensor at the corner.

A pale blue glow lit his calm, unreadable face as the biometric seal unlocked, and the first page of a twenty-year ledger opened in the midnight wind.
