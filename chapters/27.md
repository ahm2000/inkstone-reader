# Chapter 27: Stay

Friday dinner at Fuxing New Village was over, and the apartment was loud with it: the TV, Chen Hao grumbling that somebody had eaten the last rib, Liu Meifang insisting it went to the guest, and Chen Hao saying that was exactly his point!

Xiaoyu had lived here six days. She cleared the bowls without being asked and folded her quilt every morning, and she still had not said thank you.

Chen Xuan had arrived at seven with a tea egg in his pocket and set it beside her bowl.

"The cleaning auntie at my office gives me one every week," he had said. "She says I'm too thin, so I'm giving mine to you."

On the phone, Grandma Zhou Guilan had agreed with him at once.

After dinner he took his parents aside in the hallway and spoke a few quiet sentences. Liu Meifang put down her ladle, and Chen Jianguo sat very still on the stool by the door.

"Tonight," Chen Xuan said now, "she should hear it from me."

On the balcony, the night air smelled of rain and other people's cooking, and a line of Chen Jianguo's socks hung in the yellow light.

"Xiaoyu, I need to tell you something, and you can say stop at any time."

She nodded, her eyes on the socks.

"Your father worked at the Tiancheng yard, just as you said. About two months ago, he fell from a scaffold at a site in Dongqiao Town. He died from the fall."

Chen Xuan kept his voice slow and plain.

"The company did not report it, and nobody told you. That was wrong, and a lawyer is putting it right, by the law, one step at a time."

"The company will have to pay what it owes. That money is yours, though the claim could take months, and I will keep every paper safe for you."

For a long moment, the only sound was a scooter far below.

"I knew," said Xiaoyu.

"You knew?"

"He never missed a night, not even when he was late and covered in dust. Then the foreman said he had 'gone home,' but he had no other home. Just me and the room. So I knew."

Her hands gripped the railing. "I only wanted to stand where somebody might say his name."

"I'm sorry," said Chen Xuan. "I'm very sorry."

He did not hug her, and he did not tell her not to cry. He stood beside her, close enough to be there and far enough that she could choose.

After a while she said, "Can I wash the dishes now?"

"Yes."

She turned on the tap, and the water ran loud, and her shoulders began to shake without a sound.

Liu Meifang came to the kitchen door, then took the dishcloth right out of her hands and pressed it to her own face. She cried into it, loudly, the way she did everything, and Xiaoyu cried beside her.

What do you say to a girl who has already said it all herself? Liu Meifang had no words, and for once she did not look for any.

Chen Jianguo stood in the doorway and began, "As the old saying goes," and then he stopped.

For thirty years he had a saying for rain, for pork prices and for children, and tonight there was not one!

He closed his mouth and, very gently, turned off the TV.

In the hallway, Chen Hao sat on the floor with his back to the wall, and Chen Yuan sat down beside him.

Neither said anything.

Late that night, Chen Xuan stopped at the girls' room door.

"Xiaoyu, do you want to?" He paused. "Live here. With us."

She did not answer. She turned her face to the wall, and he said, "Okay, no hurry," and went to bed on the sofa.

---

Two mornings later.

Xiaoyu was hanging wet socks on the balcony line when Chen Xuan came and stood next to her.

"Last Monday, Ms. Ouyang at Civil Affairs wrote 'temporary family care' on your form, and I did not like that word. On Friday I asked you something, and you did not answer. I think some questions are too big to answer, so I will say it plainly."

She held a clothespin, perfectly still.

"Stay," said Chen Xuan. "There. I asked."

The clothespin snapped in her fingers. Xiaoyu looked at the two halves, then at him, and nodded hard, three times, and wiped her face with the back of her wrist.

"For how long?" she managed.

"Until you want to go. Even then, dinner is still at six."

"Six flights! My knees want soup! Too thin, I can tell from here!" a shrill voice shouted up the stairwell.

"That's Grandma," said Chen Xuan. "Brace yourself."

Grandma Zhou Guilan came through the door at full speed, carrying a jar of pickled eggs as big as her head. She took Xiaoyu's face in both tiny hands and looked at it carefully.

"Two eggs a day, no arguing," she said. "Do you like fishing? That old man sent a rod, and I told him she is a girl, and he said so what!"

"I... yes," said Xiaoyu. "I think so."

"Good! Where's my bowl?"

Late that afternoon, Chen Hao appeared at the girls' room door holding an old, scuffed school bag.

He had sulked all weekend, because first the last rib and then his long charger had gone to the guest, and he had decided the whole thing was unfair! But on Saturday he had seen the safety pin that held her backpack strap together.

"It's from ninth grade," he mumbled, looking at the wall. "Mom sewed the strap. The zipper works if you pull from the left. My name's inside, but I can cross it out."

"Leave it," said Xiaoyu, taking the bag in both hands.

"Oh, okay!" said Chen Hao, and he fled.

At six, everyone came to the table. Chen Yuan stood at the fridge with a red marker and a small whiteboard, and she wrote in square teacher's letters: *House Rules. Rule 1: Dinner is at six. Everybody comes.*

She left room beneath it for Rule 2, and she did not say a word about why.

Then she held out the marker to Xiaoyu!

Xiaoyu looked at it for a long moment, took it, and in small, careful letters wrote: *Rule 2: Say thank you.*

That night, on his way to the bus stop, Chen Xuan phoned the Civil Affairs office line and left a polite message for Ms. Ouyang, asking what form the family needed so Xiaoyu could stay and start school.

---

By Monday morning, Chen Xuan had already gone back to Qingyan on the Sunday-night bus.

Liu Meifang opened the fridge to reach for the pickled radishes, and froze. On the middle shelf sat a tea egg wrapped in plain brown paper, cold and perfectly ordinary, and nobody in the house had bought it.

"Who put an egg in my fridge?!" Liu Meifang cried. "Nobody went out last night!"

"As the old saying goes," said Chen Jianguo, finding his voice again, "a lucky house is a house where eggs appear."

"That is not a saying, Dad," said Chen Yuan.

"It is now," he said, and Xiaoyu, holding Chen Hao's old school bag, actually smiled.

---

At the front office of Yunxi No. 2 Middle School, Chen Yuan had practiced her speech all weekend: a missing file, a name nobody knew, a seat in a full class. She walked in with Xiaoyu beside her, ready to argue with every rule in the building!

The office clerk glanced up from her tea. "Teacher Chen? Is this Tang Xiaoyu?"

"You know her name?" said Chen Yuan.

"A seat in Class Seven-Three is saved in the window row. Ms. Ouyang at Civil Affairs sent over the temporary family-care form first thing this morning, and I added her name right away." The clerk shrugged and smiled. "Fast, for Civil Affairs."

Chen Yuan took the list and read it twice. The stamp at the bottom was real, and the date was today! She had a whole speech ready, and nobody needed to hear it.
