# Chapter 27: Stay

Yunxi County, Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. Friday, 7:50 p.m.

Dinner was over, and the apartment was loud with it: the TV, Chen Hao grumbling that somebody had eaten the last rib, Liu Meifang insisting it went to the guest, and Chen Hao saying that was exactly his point!

Xiaoyu had lived here six days. She cleared the bowls without being asked, she folded her quilt every morning, and she still hadn't said thank you.

Chen Xuan had arrived at seven with a tea egg in his pocket. "The cleaning auntie at my office gives me one every week," he'd said, setting it beside her bowl. "She says I'm too thin. I'm giving mine to you." On the phone, Grandma Zhou Guilan had agreed at once.

After dinner he'd taken his parents aside in the hallway and spoken a few quiet sentences. Liu Meifang had put down her ladle. Chen Jianguo had sat very still on the stool by the door.

"Tonight," Chen Xuan said now. "She should hear it from me."

On the balcony, the night air smelled of rain and other people's cooking. A line of Chen Jianguo's socks hung in the yellow light.

"Xiaoyu, I need to tell you something. You can say stop at any time."

She nodded, her eyes on the socks.

"Your father worked at the Tiancheng yard, just like you said. About two months ago, he fell from a scaffold at a site in Dongqiao Town. He died from the fall." Chen Xuan kept his voice slow and plain. "The company didn't report it. Nobody told you. That was wrong, and a lawyer is putting it right, by the law, one step at a time. The company will have to pay what it owes. That money is yours, though the claim could take months, and I will keep every paper safe for you."

For a long moment, the only sound was a scooter far below.

"I knew," said Xiaoyu.

"You knew?"

"He never missed a night. Not even when he was late and covered in dust. Then the foreman said he'd 'gone home,' but he had no other home. Just me and the room. So I knew." Her hands gripped the railing. "I only wanted to stand where somebody might say his name."

"I'm sorry," said Chen Xuan. "I'm very sorry."

He didn't hug her and didn't tell her not to cry. He stood beside her, close enough to be there, far enough that she could choose.

After a while she said, "Can I wash the dishes now?"

"Yes."

She turned on the tap, and the water ran loud, and her shoulders began to shake without a sound.

Liu Meifang came to the kitchen door, then took the dishcloth right out of her hands and pressed it to her own face. She cried into it, loudly, the way she did everything, and Xiaoyu cried beside her. What do you say to a girl who has already said it all herself? Liu Meifang had no words, and for once she didn't look for any. Chen Jianguo stood in the doorway. "As the old saying goes," he began, and stopped. Thirty years he'd had a saying for rain, for pork prices, for children! Tonight there wasn't one. He closed his mouth and, very gently, turned off the TV.

In the hallway, Chen Hao sat on the floor with his back to the wall, and Chen Yuan sat down beside him. Neither said anything.

Late that night, Chen Xuan stopped at the girls' room door.

"Xiaoyu. Do you want to?" He paused. "Live here. With us."

She didn't answer. She turned her face to the wall, and he said, "Okay. No hurry," and went to bed on the sofa.

Yunxi County, Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. Sunday, 10:30 a.m.

Two days later, Xiaoyu was hanging wet socks on the balcony line when Chen Xuan came and stood next to her.

"Last Monday, Ms. Ouyang at Civil Affairs wrote 'temporary family care' on your form," he said. "I didn't like that word. Friday I asked you something, and you didn't answer. I think maybe some questions are too big to answer. So I'll say it plainly."

She held a clothespin, perfectly still.

"Stay," said Chen Xuan. "There. I asked."

The clothespin snapped in her fingers. Xiaoyu looked at the two halves, then at him, and nodded hard, three times, and wiped her face with the back of her wrist.

"For how long?" she managed.

"Until you want to go. Even then, dinner is still at six."

A shrill voice rang up the stairwell. "Six flights! My knees want soup! Too thin, I can tell from here!"

"That's Grandma," said Chen Xuan. "Brace yourself."

Grandma Zhou Guilan came through the door like a very small storm, carrying a jar of pickled eggs as big as her head. She took Xiaoyu's face in both tiny hands, looked at it carefully, and said, "Two eggs a day, no arguing. Do you like fishing? That old man sent a rod. I said she's a girl. He said so what!"

"I... yes," said Xiaoyu. "I think so."

"Good! Where's my bowl?"

Late that afternoon, Chen Hao appeared at the girls' room door holding an old, scuffed school bag. He had sulked all weekend, because first the last rib and then his long charger had gone to the guest, and he'd decided the whole thing was unfair! But on Saturday he'd seen the safety pin that held her backpack strap together.

"It's from ninth grade," he mumbled, looking at the wall. "Mom sewed the strap. The zipper works if you pull from the left. My name's inside, but I can cross it out."

Xiaoyu took the bag in both hands. "Leave it."

"Oh. Okay!" Chen Hao fled.

At six, everyone came to the table. Chen Yuan stood at the fridge with a red marker and a small whiteboard, and wrote in square teacher's letters: *House Rules. Rule 1: Dinner is at six. Everybody comes.* She left room beneath it for Rule 2, and she didn't say a word about why. Then she held out the marker to Xiaoyu. Xiaoyu looked at it for a long moment, took it, and in small, careful letters wrote: *Rule 2: Say thank you.*

That night, on his way to the bus stop, Chen Xuan phoned the Civil Affairs office line and left a polite message for Ms. Ouyang, asking what form the family needed so Xiaoyu could stay and start school.

Yunxi County, Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. Monday, 6:50 a.m.

Chen Xuan had gone back to Qingyan on the Sunday-night bus.

Liu Meifang opened the fridge to reach for the pickled radishes, and froze. On the middle shelf sat a tea egg wrapped in plain brown paper. It was cold, and perfectly ordinary, and nobody in the house had bought it.

"Who put an egg in my fridge?!" Liu Meifang cried. "Nobody went out last night!"

"As the old saying goes," said Chen Jianguo, finding his voice again, "a lucky house is a house where eggs appear."

"That is not a saying, Dad," said Chen Yuan.

"It is now," he said, and Xiaoyu, holding Chen Hao's old school bag, actually smiled.

Yunxi No. 2 Middle School, front office. Monday, 7:40 a.m.

Chen Yuan had practiced her speech all weekend: a missing file, a name nobody knew, a seat in a full class. She walked in with Xiaoyu beside her, ready to argue with every rule in the building.

The office clerk glanced up from her tea. "Teacher Chen? Is this Tang Xiaoyu?"

"You know her name?" said Chen Yuan.

"A seat in Class Seven-Three is saved in the window row. Ms. Ouyang at Civil Affairs sent over the temporary family-care form first thing this morning, and I added her name right away." The clerk shrugged and smiled. "Fast, for Civil Affairs."

Chen Yuan took the list and read it twice. The stamp at the bottom was real, and the date was today. She had a whole speech ready, and nobody needed to hear it.
