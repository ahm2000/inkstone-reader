# Chapter 151: The Sealed Box from Zurich

A sharp late autumn wind rattled the tall glass windows of the Beijing State Security building. Inside the bright analysis room, the steady hum of cooling servers filled the quiet Friday night. Long oak tables held stacks of bound shipping records, illuminated tablets, and a heavy brushed steel box whose magnetic lock had finally been pried open an hour earlier.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua stood at the head of the table with his shirt sleeves rolled to his elbows. Beside him sat Deputy Director Zhong Xiaoai from the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and Handong Discipline Secretary Tian Guofu, each nursing a paper cup of strong green tea to fight off the fatigue of a long week.

Since the Shanghai crackdown had dismantled the northern financial network days earlier, the task force had worked around the clock tracing every offshore link. Most of the staff had expected the trail to cool down once the primary accounts were frozen. Instead, the diplomatic pouch flown in from Zurich (+1:00 GMT) that afternoon had blown the case wide open again.

"We thought freezing the Shanghai accounts cut off the main artery," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said calmly, tapping the open steel lid. "According to what came out of this Aegis Meridian vault box, we only clipped one branch."

Zhong Xiaoai leaned forward, squinting at the printed spreadsheets spread across the table. "The Swiss judicial team confirmed this box belonged to Aegis Meridian's private clearing division. Did the decryption team finish pulling the hidden partition?"

"Just finished twenty minutes ago," replied Lin Mei, the lead technical analyst, walking over from the server rack with a thick printout in her hands. "The outer drive only held dormant shell companies in Europe. The real archive was etched onto a secondary titanium chip sealed inside the lining."

Tian Guofu set down his paper cup and leaned closer. "What is on it?"

"A live southern maritime smuggling channel," Lin Mei said, laying a color coded port map across the center of the table. Red lines stretched from Zurich through offshore clearing houses in Singapore before converging sharply on a single coastal point in Guangdong. "Every single route terminates at Shenzhen Pier Nine."

Silence settled over the room for several seconds.

"Pier Nine," Zhong Xiaoai murmured, her brows knitting tightly. "That is a private deep water berth. If I remember the coastal registry correctly, the operating lease has been held by the Shen Family for nearly fifteen years."

"Correct," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, flipping open a fresh dossier. "On paper, the Shen Family runs a respectable maritime logistics empire in Shenzhen, handling bulk containers, cold chain imports, and private customs brokerage. In reality, the decoded Zurich manifests show they have been acting as Aegis Meridian's southern back door for the last six years."

Lin Mei pointed to three highlighted columns of figures. "Over the past eighteen months alone, more than 4.8 billion yuan in unregistered capital and restricted industrial components moved through Pier Nine. Whenever foreign brokers needed to slip sensitive cargo past standard port inspections, the Shen Family flagged the containers as priority transit, swapped the electronic seals after midnight, and pushed them straight onto coastal feeders."

"Even worse," Tian Guofu added grimly as he scanned the second page of the report, "look at these berth logs. They are not just smuggling for Aegis Meridian. They have been using fabricated safety holds to extort legitimate domestic shipping firms that dock near their terminal. If a rival company refuses to pay their private clearance fee, their cargo sits in port until the delivery contracts bleed out. It is the exact same predatory pattern we rooted out in Handong, only moved onto the water."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua looked out the dark window toward the quiet avenues of Beijing (+8:00 GMT). Even in late November, the capital felt tense beneath its calm surface.

"Remember how we located this Zurich box in the first place," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said softly. "The anonymous dossier that reached the central office before the Shanghai sweep gave us the exact vault serial number. Every single lead left behind by the holder of the Black Order has hit the mark without a millimeter of error."

Zhong Xiaoai exhaled slowly, tapping her pen against the folder. "We still have no trace of who that person is. Not a name, not a voice, not a single digital footprint. Yet he handed us a roadmap that would have taken three ministries five years to piece together."

"Do not waste energy trying to guess his identity," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua warned firmly. "The top leadership made the directive clear. We follow the evidence, protect the country's economic security, and leave the benefactor completely undisturbed."

Tian Guofu nodded, turning his attention back to the southern port map. "Then what is our move on the Shen Family? If we send an inspection team down to Shenzhen tomorrow morning, their local informants will smell it from ten miles away. They will dump the secondary ledgers into the harbor before our plane even lands."

"We do not kick the door in empty handed," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua decided. "The Shen Family thinks the Shanghai sweep was a northern storm that has already passed. Because they feel safe, they will keep operating as usual on Monday morning. Lin Mei, pull every vessel schedule bound for Shenzhen Pier Nine over the next seventy two hours."

"Already running the manifest cross check," Lin Mei answered, turning back to her terminal.

"Zhong Xiaoai, draft a sealed briefing for Premier Gu Chengshan's office before dawn," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua ordered. "Tian Guofu, coordinate with the coastal customs liaison and put two discreet surveillance teams on the public wharves near Pier Nine by Sunday night. Do not alert local port brokers, and do not make a move until we catch them holding a live vessel or swapping a sealed container."

Zhong Xiaoai stood up, gathering the decrypted manifests into a leather folio. "Understood. If the Shen Family stretches its claws out one more time, we will have everything we need to snap the trap shut."

Across the room, the printers whirred softly into the late autumn night, spitting out page after page of shipping records from the southern coast.
