# Chapter 120: Let the Boy Be Lazy

Later that night, the birthday guests had gone home, and the red silk lanterns in the Qin family courtyard cast a gentle golden glow across the stone flagstones and old pomegranate trees.

Out in the quiet garden, a cool autumn breeze drifted down from the northern lakes, carrying the faint scent of pine needles and fallen leaves. Chen Xuan stood beneath the lantern light by the stone chess table, holding a fresh porcelain cup of hot Yunnan red tea. Seeing Qin Yuhan rub her fingers together in her pale blue coat, Chen Xuan reached out and wrapped both of his large, warm hands around her slender fingers and the warm porcelain teacup at the same time.

"Still cold?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"A little," Qin Yuhan admitted, not pulling her hands away. Under the warm lantern glow, the cool president of Haiyue Group looked relaxed and soft, her eyes reflecting the golden light. "When Gu Mingzhe dropped his wine cup at dinner tonight, Second Aunt and Qin Wenbo did not dare say another word for the rest of the meal. Even my mother went completely quiet."

She tilted her head and looked up at him with playful curiosity. "It really was a coincidence, though. How on earth did his Section Director find out he was bragging so quickly?"

Chen Xuan shrugged lazily, taking a slow sip of tea over her shoulder. "In Beijing, walls have ears and loud mouths always trip over their own tongues. People who pretend to be big shots usually get caught sooner or later."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, leaning her cheek lightly against his sleeve. "Whatever the reason, it was the most peaceful dinner I have had in this courtyard in five years. Thank you for coming with me, Chen Xuan."

"Anytime," Chen Xuan smiled. Seeing the autumn breeze pick up, he slipped off his own dark wool jacket and draped it gently over her shoulders.

Not far away, inside the softly lit study overlooking the courtyard, Lin Shuyin stood beside the carved wooden desk, still speaking earnestly to her father-in-law.

"Dad, I admit Gu Mingzhe turned out to be a pompous fool," Lin Shuyin sighed, rubbing her temples. "I will never mention his name again. And I also admit Chen Xuan is polite, cooks well, and plays wonderful chess. Yet I still worry about Yuhan's future."

Elder Qin Zhengguo sat calmly in his rosewood armchair, nursing the last of his Yunnan red tea. "What is there to worry about, Shuyin?"

"He has no ambition at all," Lin Shuyin said honestly. "He clocks out at 4:55 PM every single afternoon and is completely happy making 12,000 yuan a month as a logistics clerk. Yuhan carries the whole weight of Haiyue Group on her shoulders at Haiyue Tower. Should I ask Zhengming to pull a few strings and find Chen Xuan a proper management post? At least then people will not gossip."

Elder Qin Zhengguo set his porcelain cup down with a soft clink. He stood up, tapped his walnut cane lightly against the floor, and walked over to the open lattice window.

"Come here, Shuyin," the retired four-star general said quietly. "Look outside and tell me what you see."

Lin Shuyin stepped beside him and looked out into the lantern-lit courtyard. Under the old pomegranate tree, Qin Yuhan was not checking her mobile phone, not frowning over corporate contracts, and not guarding her expression like a steel wall. Wrapped in Chen Xuan's jacket, she was smiling freely, her shoulders completely relaxed as she chatted with her husband over a cup of tea.

Lin Shuyin paused, her words catching in her throat. She could not remember the last time she had seen her daughter look so genuinely carefree.

"Shuyin, you have spent too many years counting titles and salaries," Elder Qin said, his voice deep and full of quiet wisdom. "I spent forty years in the army watching ambitious men climb over each other until their hearts turned cold and hard. Look at Gu Mingzhe tonight. Full of ambition, full of big words, and empty inside."

Elder Qin turned to his daughter-in-law, his gaze warm yet firm. "Yuhan already fights enough battles in the Shanghai boardrooms every week. She does not need another general or another schemer waiting for her at home. What she needs is a quiet harbor where she can take off her armor and breathe."

He glanced back out at Chen Xuan and chuckled softly. "That boy is steady, honest, and warm-hearted. He makes good tea, beats me at chess without boasting, and cooks her hot meals every night. He does not care about status or showing off, and he keeps my granddaughter smiling from the heart. If he wants to clock out on time and enjoy a slow, peaceful life, let the boy be lazy. Do not push him, and do not meddle."

Lin Shuyin looked once more at her daughter's bright, peaceful smile under the red lanterns. Slowly, the stubborn knot she had carried in her chest for months finally loosened. She let out a long breath and nodded gently. "You are right, Dad. I understand now. I will stop pushing them."

"Good," Elder Qin smiled, patting her arm. "Go get some rest. Tomorrow morning I am going to ask Chen Xuan for a rematch before they fly back to Shanghai."

Out in the quiet courtyard, the autumn wind rustled through the golden locust leaves beyond the red brick wall. Chen Xuan glanced once toward the peaceful shadow of the hutong outside, where two unseen members of the Black Order kept their silent watch in the cool night air. He smiled faintly, wrapped an arm around his wife's shoulders, and guided her back toward the warm lights of the main house.
