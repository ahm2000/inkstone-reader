# Chapter 120: Elder Song's Letter

A quiet wooden courtyard nestled beneath tall pine trees high in the Western Hills.

By late Saturday afternoon, a lone olive-green sedan rolled quietly up the gravel lane and parked outside the plain gray brick gate.

Under the stone veranda, ninety-year-old Founding Elder Song sat in a worn bamboo armchair with a dark woolen blanket folded neatly across his knees. His hair was completely white, his face lined with nine decades of storms and quiet years, yet his dark eyes were bright, clear, and calm as mountain water.

A small clay charcoal stove hissed softly beside the stone table, warming a black iron kettle.

"Sit down, Qin Zhengguo," Elder Song said warmly, his voice slightly hoarse with age yet steady and unhurried. "Do not stand at attention in my yard. You are almost seventy yourself now, and you still walk like a young lieutenant."

Qin Zhengguo smiled with genuine affection, his stiff military posture softening as he sat down on the wooden stool across from the old man. He reached out automatically, lifted the iron kettle, and poured fresh hot water into two small white porcelain cups filled with rolled green tea leaves.

"I just came straight from the Great Hall, Elder Song," Qin Zhengguo said quietly, setting the kettle back on the stove. "Premier Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua, Gao Zhengyi, and Ding Guoping finished the second-month review an hour ago. I thought you should hear the numbers directly."

"Tell me," Elder Song replied, lifting his teacup slowly with both hands to warm his thin fingers. "My ears are still good."

"46 trillion yuan in stolen state assets recovered or locked safely into state trusts," Qin Zhengguo reported calmly. "Three hundred and forty-two senior officials removed and charged. All three theater commands completely cleaned out and back on steady readiness. Public markets stayed completely calm, food prices barely moved, and ordinary towns like Yunxi County heard public court verdicts over their radios this morning."

Elder Song took a slow sip of tea and closed his eyes for a moment, letting the quiet weight of those numbers settle in the mountain air. When he opened his eyes again, a deep, peaceful light shone in them.

"46 trillion yuan," Elder Song murmured softly. "Sixty days, and not one shop closed in panic, not one soldier broke rank, and not one innocent family lost its roof. In my whole life, Qin Zhengguo, I have never seen a storm pass so cleanly."

"Neither have I," Qin Zhengguo admitted honestly. "Without the files that came through Number 1, and without the quiet hand standing behind Black Guard, cleaning out a rot that deep would have taken ten years and split half the provinces apart."

Elder Song set his teacup down gently on the stone table. For a long moment, he gazed out past the eaves at the tall pines swaying gently in the evening breeze.

"Thirty-five years ago," Elder Song said softly, as if speaking to an old memory, "when the old guard first wrote the sealed rules for Black Guard in the winter hills, we thought it was only a last shield to lock away in a dark vault and pray nobody would ever need to wake. None of us imagined that a young man of this generation would hold it with such calm hands."

Qin Zhengguo looked down at his own teacup, his expression solemn. "Minister Ding Guoping confirmed today that not a single word about Black Guard has leaked anywhere in the country or abroad. And whoever stands behind Number 1 has never once asked for money, rank, favors, or even a single line of credit."

"He does not want rank," Elder Song said with a faint, knowing smile. "A man who can move mountains without making a sound has no use for a shiny chair in the capital. He just wanted the sky to stay clean and his own quiet corner to stay untouched."

Slowly, Elder Song reached over to the small lacquered wooden box resting beside his elbow. He lifted the lid, took out a single sheet of plain cream writing paper, a matching unmarked cream envelope, and a worn black brush pen.

Qin Zhengguo watched in silence as the ninety-year-old man steadied his wrist on the stone table. Elder Song's fingers were thin and veined with age, yet when the tip of the brush touched the cream paper, his hand did not tremble even a fraction of an inch.

He wrote only one short line of neat, clean black ink across the center of the page.

He did not sign a title. He did not stamp a red seal. He did not even write a name on the outside of the envelope.

Elder Song waited a few quiet seconds for the ink to dry in the cool mountain breeze, folded the cream paper neatly into thirds, slipped it inside the unmarked envelope, and pressed the flap sealed with his thumb.

Then he slid the plain cream envelope gently across the stone table toward General Qin Zhengguo.

"Zhengguo, pass this to Number 1," Elder Song said softly, his clear old eyes resting steadily on the general's face. "Ask if the one who holds the Black Order has time for one cup of tea with an old man."

General Qin Zhengguo froze for half a second. He looked down at the plain unmarked envelope lying between the two white teacups, and then back up at Founding Elder Song.

In the entire country, there were not five people alive who would dare ask for a face-to-face meeting with the unseen master of Black Guard. Even Premier Gu Chengshan had just reminded everyone in the Great Hall never to pry and never to push. Yet Elder Song was not giving an order, and he was not prying. He was simply an old man in the Western Hills asking for one quiet cup of tea before his years ran out.

"I will deliver it to Number 1 personally tonight, Elder Song," Qin Zhengguo said gravely, picking up the unmarked cream envelope with both hands as if it weighed ten pounds. "No third hand will touch it."

"Good," Elder Song smiled gently, picking up his teacup once more. "Tell Number 1 there is no rush and no obligation. If he is busy with his own life, or if he prefers to stay out of sight, an old man understands completely. If he ever has a quiet afternoon to spare, the kettle here is always warm."

"I will pass on every word," Qin Zhengguo promised softly, tucking the sealed cream envelope safely into his inner breast pocket.

Above the gray courtyard roof, the late afternoon sun dipped slowly behind the dark ridge of the Western Hills, turning the pine tops gold. Inside the quiet yard, the iron kettle hummed softly on its bed of glowing charcoal, waiting for an answer that only one ordinary man in a small county town could give.
