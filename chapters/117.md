# Chapter 117: Dinner Behind Mingde Hardware

By six o'clock on Saturday evening, the small brick courtyard behind Mingde Hardware smelled like roasted garlic, star anise, and slow-simmered pork ribs.

Before anyone sat down, a horn honked twice out in the back lane, followed by the crunch of new tires on gravel.

"Move the stools aside, move them aside!" Wang Lei's booming voice rang out as he pushed the wooden back gate wide and stood proudly with both hands on his hips, pointing at a gleaming silver delivery van parked along the brick wall. "Uncle Mingde! Auntie Lanying! Uncle Jianguo! Come see my brand-new steel horse!"

"Look at that shine!" Chen Hao jumped up and trotted to the gate, circling the van like a kid at a car show. "Wang Lei, you really bought the long-bed model! Even the paint still smells new!"

"Of course I did!" Wang Lei puffed out his chest and patted the door, then quickly wiped his own handprint off with his sleeve. "Paid 110k yuan out of my own savings and clean freight contracts! Not one cent borrowed from a loan shark, and not one cent paid to any road bully! Starting Monday, I am running three regular routes between Yunxi County and Shanghai. Chen Mingde, whenever your shop needs steel pipes hauled from the city, call me and I will bring them straight to your gate for gas money!"

"Listen to Wang Lei talking big already," Chen Yuan laughed, carrying out a platter of sliced cucumbers and peanuts. She poked her brother Chen Hao in the arm. "Chen Hao, your friend bought a whole new van, and you still forget to oil your scooter chain."

"My scooter runs fine!" Chen Hao protested, making everyone laugh. "Wait until next year, Chen Yuan. Once factory orders pick up, I am buying a truck too!"

"Good, young people should aim high," Chen Mingde beamed, patting the van's hood before waving everyone inside. "Come eat before the ribs get cold! Wang Lei, sit next to Yu Changshun."

Within five minutes, all nine chairs around the round table were full.

Across from them sat Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang, with Chen Hao, Chen Yuan, and Wang Lei squeezed together on one side, and Yu Changshun already uncorking his second jug of yellow rice wine on the other.

"Nobody pick up chopsticks yet!" Yu Changshun stood with his cup raised high, his face already pink with cheer. "First toast goes to Old Street! After this morning's court broadcast, Deng Yongchang and his pack of wolves are behind iron bars for good, and honest folk can finally sleep in peace! Drink up!"

"To Old Street!" everyone cheered, clinking cups over the steaming clay pot.

"And second toast goes to Wang Lei's new van!" Chen Mingde added warmly. "Drive safely, earn honest money, and never speed on the county highway!"

"I promise, Uncle Mingde! Steady as a rock!" Wang Lei grinned, downing his cup in one gulp.

While the table buzzed with laughter, Chen Xuan sat quietly at the corner seat nearest the kitchen stove. He did not try to talk over anyone. Instead, he picked up the big porcelain ladle, lifted the lid off the clay pot, and began filling small bowls with rich, clear pork-rib soup, making sure each bowl held two meaty ribs and a slice of sweet white radish.

He placed the first bowl gently before Wu Lanying, the second before Liu Meifang, and passed bowls steadily around to Chen Mingde, Chen Jianguo, and Yu Changshun.

"Chen Xuan is always the most thoughtful one," Wu Lanying smiled warmly, blowing on her hot soup. "Sit down and eat too, child. Do not spend the whole meal serving us."

"Plenty left in the pot, Auntie," Chen Xuan replied with a quiet smile, ladling bowls for Chen Yuan and Chen Hao. "Drink it while it is hot."

"Look at Chen Xuan," Wang Lei pointed his chopsticks toward him with a grin. "Steady job at Hengxin Logistics, calm temper, never brags, and always pours soup for his elders. Chen Hao, learn from your brother! Last time we ate noodles, you ate all the beef before I even sat down!"

"That was because you were busy staring at the shopkeeper's daughter!" Chen Hao shot back, sending Chen Yuan and Liu Meifang into laughter.

"Enough teasing, eat your food," Liu Meifang scolded fondly, putting a thick piece of braised fish into Chen Xuan's bowl. "Chen Xuan, are they still treating you well at Hengxin Logistics?"

"Very well, Mom," Chen Xuan answered calmly. "Twelve thousand yuan a month right on time, quiet office, and Saturdays off whenever the yard is clear."

Chen Jianguo took a slow sip of yellow rice wine, set his cup down with a crisp tap, and cleared his throat importantly. Whenever he cleared his throat like that at dinner, everyone knew a speech was coming.

"You young ones listen closely," Chen Jianguo said, waving one finger slowly with deep pride. "People like Deng Yongchang and Sun Weiming built tall towers out of stolen bricks. Do you know why they fell overnight?"

"Because the court caught them?" Wang Lei guessed brightly.

"Bigger than that!" Chen Jianguo declared, sitting up straight as his eyes shone behind his glasses. "Remember my words: a crooked beam may hold the roof on a sunny day, yet one honest wind will drop the whole house before the soup even boils!"

Silence held for half a second, and then Yu Changshun burst out laughing, slapping the table.

"Ha! Listen to Chen Jianguo making up his own proverbs right at the dinner table!" Yu Changshun wheezed with laughter. "Before the soup even boils! Where did you dig up that line?"

"I thought of it myself!" Chen Jianguo insisted stoutly, though his ears turned slightly red. "Tell me if it is not true! Crooked beam, honest wind, soup boiling, every word makes perfect sense!"

"It makes wonderful sense, Dad," Chen Xuan said softly, a genuine smile curving his lips as he refilled his father's soup bowl. "A crooked beam never lasts. Only steady foundations keep a house standing."

"There! You hear that?" Chen Jianguo pointed triumphantly at Chen Xuan. "At least my own son understands good wisdom! Liu Meifang, stop laughing behind your napkin!"

"I am just amazed we have a philosopher sitting in our courtyard," Liu Meifang teased, wiping her eyes as Chen Mingde refilled the wine cups.

Warm laughter rose beneath the persimmon tree and drifted over the wall into the quiet dusk of Old Street. Chen Xuan sat back in his wooden chair, tasting his aunt's hot pork-rib soup, completely content as the ordinary office worker in the middle of his noisy, happy family.

Forty meters down the dark brick lane outside, two silent figures in plain black clothes stood motionless near the corner lamp, keeping every disturbance far from the warm light of the courtyard.
