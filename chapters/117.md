# Chapter 117: The Qin Courtyard in Beijing

The next morning, golden leaves crackled softly underfoot along the quiet red-walled hutong near the northern lakes, where tall locust trees arched over the gray stone pavement.

Down the shaded hutong outside the vermilion gates of the Qin family courtyard, two relaxed Zero Guard locals bought a bag of hot roasted chestnuts from a street vendor and chatted easily with a passing postman, blending naturally into the peaceful Beijing morning.

Chen Xuan walked slowly beside Qin Yuhan, carrying her gift bags in one hand and his own modest cloth-wrapped porcelain jar of Yunnan red tea in the other. Tucked under his arm was a small bamboo box of handmade osmanthus cakes packed fresh before they left Shanghai.

"Cold?" Chen Xuan asked, glancing at her rosy cheeks.

"A little, though the northern sun feels nice," Qin Yuhan said, adjusting her cream coat. She paused by the carved stone lions flanking the red gates and smoothed his collar with gentle fingers. "Remember what I said last night. Just stay relaxed."

"Relaxed is my specialty," Chen Xuan grinned.

Inside the sprawling traditional courtyard, red silk lanterns hung beneath the painted wooden eaves, and the air smelled of pine needles, ripe pomegranates, and freshly swept flagstones. In the center of the main hall sat Elder Qin Zhengguo, a retired four-star military commander with silver hair trimmed short, thick eyebrows, and a back as straight as an iron spear. Wearing a simple dark wool tunic coat, he rested one hand on a polished walnut walking cane while listening to his relatives chat.

Beside him sat Qin Yuhan's father, Qin Zhengming, who greeted the young couple with a warm smile, and her mother, Lin Shuyin, whose expression tightened slightly the moment she noticed Chen Xuan's plain autumn jacket and worn canvas bag.

On the other side of the hall sat Second Uncle, Second Aunt, and Qin Yuhan's cousin, Qin Wenbo. The moment Chen Xuan stepped across the wooden threshold, Second Aunt's eyes swept over him from head to toe.

"Yuhan, you finally made it!" Second Aunt called out brightly, before turning a polite, pointed smile toward Chen Xuan. "And this must be Chen Xuan. Look how casually you dress for Elder Qin's seventy-eighth birthday. I suppose logistics clerks in Shanghai do not need to wear suits very often."

Qin Wenbo chuckled softly from his armchair, adjusting the cuff of his tailored blazer. "Mom, do not tease him. A modest salary does not leave much room for bespoke wool. Look, he even brought a jar of loose tea."

Beside Qin Wenbo's elbow sat a lacquered wooden box containing a carved inkstone that Second Aunt had already announced twice cost 120,000 yuan at a Beijing auction.

Qin Yuhan's gaze turned chilly, and she stepped half a pace forward, ready to speak. Chen Xuan gently touched her elbow to calm her, then walked forward with an easy, unhurried stride and bowed respectfully to the silver-haired general.

"Happy seventy-eighth birthday, Grandpa," Chen Xuan said in a clear, warm voice. "I wish you good health, calm days, and plenty of sunshine. Yuhan picked out the jade paperweight and the cashmere coat for you. I brought a small jar of old Yunnan red tea and some osmanthus cakes I thought might go well with an afternoon pot."

The main hall grew quiet for a beat. Most young men who stood before Elder Qin Zhengguo stiffened their shoulders, stammered over their words, or tried too hard to sound important. Even senior officers felt the weight of the old general's sharp gaze.

Yet Chen Xuan stood completely at ease, his posture natural, his eyes clear and respectful without a trace of flattery or fear.

Elder Qin Zhengguo studied him for two long seconds, his thick eyebrows lifting slightly. He did not glance at the 120,000-yuan inkstone on the side table. Instead, he reached out a weathered hand and took the plain porcelain jar.

He popped the wax-sealed lid and took a slow breath. A rich, mellow fragrance of aged autumn tea leaves drifted out, warm and sweet like honey and dry pine wood.

Elder Qin's stern face broke into a genuine smile. "Now this is proper tea. Not that perfumed rubbish people pack in velvet boxes to show off. Auntie Zhou, boil a kettle of fresh mountain water from the stone jug out back. Brew a full pot right away."

Second Aunt's bright smile froze on her face. Qin Wenbo cleared his throat awkwardly, glancing down at his 120,000-yuan inkstone box that still sat untouched on the table.

"Grandpa, try the osmanthus cakes too once the tea is ready," Qin Yuhan said, her eyes sparkling as she slipped her hand quietly into Chen Xuan's palm. "Chen Xuan stood in line at the old bakery in Shanghai to buy them fresh."

"Good, very good," Elder Qin nodded, gesturing for the couple to sit right beside him. "Yuhan, your color looks much better than it did last autumn. Last time you came to Beijing, you looked thin as a bamboo pole from working too late. It seems this husband of yours actually knows how to feed you."

"He cooks every single night, Grandpa," Qin Yuhan said softly, smiling at Chen Xuan. "His braised pork ribs are better than any restaurant in Pudong."

Lin Shuyin coughed lightly and adjusted her shawl. "Dad, cooking is fine, of course, though a man should still think about his career."

Elder Qin waved his hand carelessly, his eyes twinkling with quiet amusement. "Enough about careers before lunch. First, let us drink this boy's tea and see what else he is hiding."
