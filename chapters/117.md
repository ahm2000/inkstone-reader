# Chapter 117: The Qin Courtyard in Beijing

Saturday morning in Beijing was crisp and bright, with sunlight filtering through the tall locust trees along the quiet red-walled hutong near the northern lakes.

Forty meters outside the heavy vermilion gates of the Qin family courtyard, two plainclothes members of the Black Order bought hot roasted chestnuts from a street vendor and strolled along the shaded sidewalk, staying quietly within their fifty-meter radius of Chen Xuan.

Inside the sprawling traditional courtyard, red silk lanterns hung beneath the carved wooden eaves, and the air smelled of pine needles and freshly brewed tea. In the center of the main hall sat Elder Qin Zhengguo, a retired four-star military commander with silver hair trimmed short, thick eyebrows, and a back as straight as an iron spear. Wearing a simple dark wool tunic coat, he held a walnut walking cane beside his knee while listening to his eldest son and younger relatives offer birthday greetings.

Elder Qin Zhengguo had spent his life guarding the northern border and had retired five years before the 300 trillion yuan sovereign injection quietly transformed China Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China. He had never met Chen Xuan, had never seen Chen Xuan's face or green thermos, and had zero idea that his favorite granddaughter's new husband was the unseen holder of the Black Order. Like everyone else in the Qin family, Elder Qin Zhengguo only knew what Lin Shuyin and Qin Zhengming had reported from Shanghai: that Qin Yuhan had stubbornly married a carefree, twelve-thousand-yuan logistics clerk from Yunxi County in Handong Province.

Near the carved rosewood screen by the courtyard entrance, Lin Shuyin stood stiffly in an embroidered burgundy dress beside her husband Qin Zhengming. Across from them stood Second Aunt Zhou Meilin, wearing a thick jade bracelet, and Qin Yuhan's twenty-seven-year-old cousin Qin Haoran, who wore a tailored Italian suit and a shiny gold chronograph watch.

"Sister-in-law," Second Aunt Zhou Meilin said with a sweet, mocking smile, fanning herself with a silk handkerchief. "Yuhan's plane landed last night, did it not? Why is she taking so long to walk in? Could it be that our famous Shanghai logistics clerk was intimidated by the guards at the alley entrance?"

Qin Haoran chuckled, adjusting his silk tie. "Mom, be understanding. A shipping clerk who makes twelve thousand yuan a month in Shanghai has probably never seen a private Beijing courtyard in his life. Wait until he sees the antique porcelain in Grandfather's hall. He will be too scared to put his feet down."

Lin Shuyin's face burned with embarrassment, while Qin Zhengming frowned and opened his mouth to defend his son-in-law.

Before Qin Zhengming could speak, light footsteps sounded on the stone flagstones by the moon gate.

Qin Yuhan walked in wearing a tailored pale blue cashmere coat, her dark hair pinned neatly and the 680k yuan mother-of-pearl wristwatch from Plaza 66 gleaming softly on her wrist. Right beside her walked Chen Xuan in a clean dark jacket and casual trousers, holding Qin Yuhan's left hand with one hand and carrying a small white porcelain tea jar and his scratched green enamel thermos in the other.

The chatter in the courtyard paused instantly.

Qin Haoran stared at the green enamel thermos hanging from Chen Xuan's fingers and almost laughed out loud. "Cousin Yuhan! Aunt Lin told us your husband had a quirky habit, but I did not believe he would actually carry an old factory water jug to Grandfather's seventy-eighth birthday!"

Second Aunt Zhou Meilin covered her mouth with a soft snicker. "My goodness, Yuhan, you run Haiyue Group and sit on assets near Tomson Riviera and Lujiazui, yet your husband walks into a Beijing family banquet looking like a retired park gatekeeper! Talk about hitting the jackpot without lifting a finger!"

Several younger cousins around the courtyard exchanged amused whispers, waiting to see the small-town clerk blush, bow his head, or hide the thermos behind his back.

Chen Xuan did not blush at all. He looked at his green thermos, gave Qin Haoran a pleasant, unbothered smile, and lifted the jug slightly.

"Beijing weather is dry in the morning, Cousin Haoran," Chen Xuan said cheerfully. "Airport water fountains are always lukewarm, and this old enamel cup keeps hot water warm for six hours. If your throat gets dry from talking so much before lunch, I have enough hot water left for half a cup."

Qin Yuhan's lips curved into an irrepressible smile, and a few of the courtyard servants standing by the pillars had to look down at their shoes to hide their grins.

Qin Haoran's face stiffened. "Who needs hot water from a ten-yuan tin mug? I just presented Grandfather with a pair of Qianlong-era jade paperweights worth 450k yuan! What did you bring in that little white jar? Five-yuan pickled radishes from your logistics cafeteria?"

"Just four ounces of sun-dried red tea from Yunnan," Chen Xuan said calmly, walking past a spluttering Qin Haoran with Qin Yuhan's hand still tucked warmly in his own.

Up in the main hall, Elder Qin Zhengguo tapped his walnut cane lightly against the brick floor. The sharp, resonant sound made Second Aunt Zhou Meilin and Qin Haoran shut their mouths immediately.

Elder Qin Zhengguo's keen, hawk-like eyes swept over Chen Xuan from head to toe. The old general had seen thousands of ambitious young men in Beijing who bowed too low, smiled too eagerly, or puffed out their chests to look important. Yet this young man from Shanghai stood with loose, natural shoulders, steady eyes, and not a trace of greed or fear on his face.

"Grandfather," Qin Yuhan stepped forward warmly. "Happy birthday. This is Chen Xuan."

"Happy birthday, Grandfather," Chen Xuan smiled easily, setting his green thermos on a side stool and handing over the small white porcelain jar with both hands. "Yuhan said you wake up at five every morning to walk the courtyard. Try brewing three pinches of this red tea after breakfast. It warms the stomach without making it sour."

Elder Qin Zhengguo took the plain white jar, pulled off the cork lid, and sniffed the rich, honey-like aroma of top-grade wild ancient-tree tea. The old general raised one thick silver eyebrow in mild surprise.

"Not a bad nose for tea, young man," Elder Qin Zhengguo said in a deep, gravelly voice. "Sit down and drink a cup before lunch."
