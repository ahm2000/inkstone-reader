# Chapter 23: The Girl at the Noodle Shop

The firecrackers on Old Street were finished and the eggs were eaten, and Mingde Hardware was still crowded with neighbors telling each other they had "never doubted it for one second."

Chen Xuan had been at the shop since early evening, and he had already been hugged by four people and fed by six!

"Come on, Xuan," Chen Jianguo said. "Your mother's soup won't be ready until half past eight, and as the old saying goes, an empty stomach climbs six floors twice as slowly."

"That is not an old saying," said Chen Hao, who had a math book open in one hand and had not looked up all evening.

"It is an old saying now," said Chen Jianguo.

So the three of them crossed the lane to Old Beef Noodles, the little six-table shop under the red awning, and halfway there, Chen Xuan stopped.

A girl stood at the edge of the awning, just outside the circle of yellow light.

She looked about thirteen and small for it, in a man's gray work jacket so big that the sleeves were rolled up, with short, crooked hair that looked cut in a hurry. A school backpack hung from one shoulder, and a plastic bag was twisted around her fist.

She was not looking at the noodle shop. She was watching the street, and every passerby got exactly one look before she moved on to the next, very steady, very serious.

It was a look far too tired for a girl her age!

A woman walking past clicked her tongue at the girl, the sound adults make at children they have judged to be trouble, and crossed to the other side of the lane.

"Hmm?" said Chen Xuan.

"Is she lost?" Chen Hao pulled out his earbud at last.

"Maybe." Chen Xuan walked over and stopped at a polite distance. "Good evening. Are you waiting for someone?"

"My dad," the girl said at once.

"He's coming soon," the girl added. "He's late from work."

She said it fast and flat, like a sentence she had practiced many times, and right on cue, her stomach growled so loudly that Chen Jianguo heard it from three steps away!

"I am not hungry at all," said the girl.

"Of course not," Chen Xuan said, nodding seriously. "We are about to eat, though, at the window table. If your dad is late, you are welcome to wait inside where it's warm."

He did not ask her anything more. He walked in, and his father and brother followed.

"Brother," Chen Hao whispered. "She's lying."

"Mm."

"So why didn't you tell her so?"

"If I say it, she needs a bigger lie tomorrow," Chen Xuan said mildly. "If I don't, she can tell the truth when she is ready."

Chen Hao opened his mouth, closed it, and put the earbud in his pocket for good!

"Young Chen!" The cook, Gong Yi, waved a ladle from behind the counter. "I heard your uncle's firecrackers all the way from my stove! Three bowls?"

"Three, please. And a fourth bowl for the girl outside, whenever she is ready, with an extra egg."

The first three bowls landed on the table in under a minute, each with two fried eggs that nobody had ordered.

Ten minutes later, the door opened a crack, and the girl stood there with her bag.

"I can wash dishes," she said to the floor. "For a bowl."

"The kitchen is that way," said Chen Xuan. "But your bowl is already paid for, so would you wash after you eat? Then your stomach and your hands will both be warm."

She thought about it for a long moment. "After?"

"After."

"...Okay."

She ate fast, then slowly, as if she had remembered her manners, and when the bowl was empty, she carried it to the sink and washed it herself.

Then she washed the three bowls from the window table, and then every pair of chopsticks in the box!

"So young, and so careful..." someone whispered at the next table.

"Nobody seems to know whose girl she is..."

"Brother Gong," Chen Jianguo called, "this girl washes better than your dishwasher!"

"I don't own a dishwasher, you know."

"That explains why she is so good!"

Chen Xuan waited until she had dried her hands. "What's your name?"

"Tang Xiaoyu."

"Do you live near here, Xiaoyu?"

"At my aunt's." Her eyes slid away. "It's close."

He nodded and let that one go too. "And your dad's name? Old Street is small, and somebody might know him."

"Tang Dawei," she said, so quickly that it seemed to jump out of her. "He works at the yard on Dongqiao Road."

"Which yard is that?"

She hesitated. "The one that knocks buildings down. Tiancheng."

"Hmm," said Chen Xuan softly.

Dongqiao was his grandparents' town, and he knew that road well.

Behind the counter, Gong Yi's ladle stopped over the pot.

Gong Yi was Number Twenty-three of the Detail, the hidden team of protectors who worked for Chen Xuan and acted only when he gave the signal.

The name Tiancheng meant something to Gong Yi, because it was the demolition company owned by the brother-in-law of Cheng Biao, the police deputy director who had jailed the shop owners and held the huge Old Street contract.

Last night he had handed this girl two steamed buns, as he did for any hungry kid at his door, and when she offered to wash dishes for them, he told her nobody washes dishes for two buns. Tonight she had come back to prove she meant it.

Everything in him wanted to sit her down and call every office in town before the soup cooled!

But the Boss sat six feet away, ordering noodles like any customer, and an ordinary noodle cook does not make calls about a child whose father is "late." If anyone was going to start this, it had to be the Boss.

So Gong Yi turned his back, typed one line to Chief An under the counter, and cracked another egg into the pan, because that was the only thing he was allowed to do.

At the window table, Chen Xuan frowned.

Was a father really this "late"? Why had she said the name so fast? He did not know yet, but he knew what a normal person does when a child is alone at night.

"Xiaoyu, let's ask the police to help find your dad. That's what they are for."

The girl went stiff. "I didn't take anything."

"Nobody here said that you did."

"They always say that first, before anything else."

"I will walk in with you and sit beside you the whole time," Chen Xuan said, keeping his voice very ordinary. "If it is awful, we will walk right out. Deal?"

A long pause. "Not in a cell, please."

"There is no cell there. There is only a steel bench, and it isn't even comfortable."

The corner of her mouth moved, and it was not a smile yet, but it was close.

---

At Old Street Police Station, the lobby was nearly empty, and Chen Xuan pulled a number from the ticket machine and looked at it.

Number one!

In all his visits to this lobby, he had never been number one before.

Behind the glass sat Instructor Qiu Guoqiang, who had run the station since the director was suspended, with gray at his temples and reading glasses pushed up on his forehead.

"Mr. Chen!" Qiu came out through the side door at once. "I heard your uncle's shutters went up today. I am very glad."

Chen Xuan explained in a few plain sentences, and Qiu bent down so his eyes were level with Xiaoyu's.

"Nobody here thinks you did anything wrong, little one," he said gently.

She looked at him for a long second and nodded.

He searched the missing-persons list and found no child and no Tang Dawei. The Dongqiao station was closed for the night, he said, but he would call them first thing Monday.

The station had a cot in the rest room, and Auxiliary Officer Ma, a patient woman on the night shift, would sit with her.

"She can sleep safely here tonight," said Qiu, rubbing his tired face. "But a child with no guardian is not police business after that. It belongs to Civil Affairs, the county office that looks after children who have no guardian. Take her there. It opens Monday."

Chen Xuan looked at the calendar taped to the glass.

Friday night, then Saturday, then Sunday.

And beside him, Xiaoyu's eyes had already turned to the front door, counting the steps.
