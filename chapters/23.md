# Chapter 23: The Bank Vault in Lanshui

Rain began falling over Lanshui shortly after dusk, turning the glass towers of the provincial capital's financial district into smeared columns of cold gold and wet steel. Two hundred kilometers west of Yunxi County, on the nineteenth floor of an unmarked commercial building leased through three nested holding companies, the lights were turned off except for the pale blue glow of forty matte-black monitors.

This was the regional Financial Intelligence room of Black Pine Guard. The air smelled faintly of ozone and cooled aluminum. Every workstation was separated by dark acoustic dividers, and the analysts seated at the consoles wore plain charcoal jackets with no badges, no nameplates, and no personal phones. Inside Black Pine Guard, nobody knew another member's real name or face. Even when two operatives sat three meters apart for twelve hours straight, they addressed one another strictly by number, bound together only by discipline and loyalty to the Boss.

At the center console, Number Eleven stood with one hand resting on the back of a low swivel chair, eyes fixed on a cascading wall of hexadecimal settlement packets. Seated directly in front of the primary terminal, Number Seventy-three typed with a steady, unhurried rhythm, running a custom decryption pipeline against a forty-terabyte raw disk image.

Twelve hours earlier, Black Pine field technicians had physically mirrored that image from a pair of hidden, water-cooled clearing servers housed inside a private sub-basement vault beneath a commercial bank branch in downtown Lanshui. On paper, the bank vault held routine escrow archives for regional infrastructure bonds. In reality, the servers belonged to Shengyuan Capital, the parent syndicate sitting quietly behind Hongrui Real Estate and the demolition push along Yunxi County's Old Street.

"Secondary routing key is holding at layer four," Number Seventy-three said, his voice flat and calm over the faint hum of the server rack. "They rotate the hardware handshake every six hours using a seed tied to municipal land-auction escrow timestamps."

"Use the Yunxi County auction ledger from last November," Number Eleven replied quietly. "Hongrui Real Estate won parcel seven at thirty percent below reserve price on a Friday afternoon. Their escrow deposit cleared through the Lanshui branch eleven minutes early. Check the timestamp offset."

Number Seventy-three entered the eleven-minute delta into the key-derivation script and struck the execute bar. For four seconds, the screen held frozen in stark white code. Then the authentication hash flashed green, and layer four peeled open like a steel vault door swinging off its hinges.

Lines of encrypted SWIFT mirror logs began pouring across the central display wall, automatically parsed by Black Pine's forensic ledger engine into clean relational graphs.

At first glance, the surface layer looked like standard provincial corruption. Shell contractors billed inflated concrete and steel prices on highway projects; county land bureaus signed discounted transfers to Hongrui Real Estate; and unrecorded rare-earth ore extracted from the illegal tunnel beneath Yunxi's Old Street was blended into legitimate quarry manifests to disguise its origin.

Then Number Seventy-three expanded the deep-clearing partition buried beneath the domestic escrow tables, and the quiet rhythm of keystrokes across the room came to an abrupt halt.

"Number Eleven," Number Seventy-three said, his neutral tone dropping half an octave. "This is not construction skimming."

Number Eleven leaned closer to the central screen, watching the transaction vectors bloom outward from Lanshui across the coastal provinces and straight past the national border.

"Trace the upstream origin of ledger block nine," Number Eleven ordered.

Number Seventy-three isolated the upstream feeds. Every single inflow into Shengyuan Capital's private clearing core came from Tier-One state-owned infrastructure vehicles: long-term energy supply contracts, strategic rare-earth and lithium mineral quotas, and deep-water port-concession lease revenues. Using falsified demurrage claims, rigged commodity-hedging derivatives, and phantom equipment-leasing invoices, Shengyuan Capital's hidden servers were systematically siphoning off a fixed percentage of sovereign resource cash flows before those funds ever reached provincial treasury accounts.

"Look at the conversion layer," Number Eleven murmured, his eyes tracking the glowing amber lines.

Once the siphoned yuan cleared through a labyrinth of domestic factoring trusts in Lanshui and three neighboring provincial capitals, the funds vanished into cross-border trade-settlement mirrors. Within twenty-four hours, the capital reappeared offshore as physical gold-backed bearer instruments, stripped of all domestic beneficial-ownership records and custodied inside private clearing vaults in Singapore, Zurich, and a web of Cayman Islands trusts.

"They are using regional real-estate demolitions and county-level rare-earth smuggling as physical cash-laundering ballast," Number Seventy-three said quietly. "Hongrui Real Estate in Yunxi County is just one small valve on the underside of the pipe. The real volume is moving straight out of state-owned energy, mineral, and port concessions."

"Keep drilling," Number Eleven instructed. "Gold-backed bearer instruments in Zurich and the Caymans do not sit idle for twenty years. Where do the outgoing disbursements land?"

Number Seventy-three ran a cross-correlation query matching the outbound settlement windows from the Zurich and Cayman custodians against Black Pine Guard's global counter-intelligence registry. The progress bar crawled across the bottom of the screen for eight long seconds. When the results populated in crimson ink on the right-hand monitor, even Number Eleven's breathing paused for a fraction of a second.

Every quarter, on the fifteenth day of March, June, September, and December, fixed tranches of bearer-instrument liquidity flowed directly out of the Cayman trusts and Zurich clearing accounts into fourteen seemingly unrelated offshore entities: maritime insurance brokers in Rotterdam, aerospace parts consultancies in Frankfurt, and private satellite-analytics firms registered in Delaware and Singapore.

Every single one of those fourteen recipients carried a verified Class-A red flag inside Black Pine's global threat archive. They were not luxury holding companies for greedy officials buying overseas mansions. They were documented financial cutouts operated by foreign defense-intelligence services.

"Verify the matching hashes," Number Eleven said, his voice sharp as cold steel. "Are those disbursements one-way transfers, or are they settlement payments against delivered cargo?"

Number Seventy-three opened the metadata headers attached to the last six quarterly transfers. Each disbursement contained an encrypted thirty-two-character delivery confirmation tag, paired with a courier archive reference number routed back to a local drop node right here in Lanshui.

"They are fee-for-delivery settlements," Number Seventy-three confirmed, staring at the crimson lines reflected in his dark glasses. "And the most recent delivery tag cleared four days ago, twenty-four hours before Hongrui Real Estate moved to seize Old Street in Yunxi County."

Rain drummed steadily against the nineteenth-floor windows, blurring the bright neon skyline of Lanshui into streaks of liquid light. Inside the dark room, nobody spoke. They had entered Yunxi County to trace a local demolition gang and clear an innocent uncle's name for the Boss. Instead, pulling on one loose thread at Old Street had exposed an underground sovereign siphon feeding foreign defense intelligence straight out of the nation's treasury.

Number Eleven reached for the encrypted internal intercom mounted beside the console and pressed the direct key to the regional command office.

"Number One," Number Eleven said evenly. "Financial Intelligence has breached Shengyuan Capital's Lanshui vault servers. We have verified SWIFT mirrors linking state energy, mineral, and port revenues through Singapore, Zurich, and Cayman gold-backed trusts directly into foreign defense-intelligence cutouts. We have active delivery tags terminating in Lanshui."

A brief, measured silence answered from the other end before Number One's calm, heavy voice came through the speaker. "Lock down the entire floor. Zero external transmissions. Cross-link the delivery hashes with Signals and Cyber immediately. Tell Number Thirty-four to decrypt the courier drive we lifted tonight."

"Understood," Number Eleven replied. He ended the link, looked back at the crimson lines pulsing across the glass wall, and knew that the night in Lanshui had barely begun.
