# Chapter 23: The Bank Vault in Lanshui

Two hundred kilometers west of Yunxi County, rain fell steadily over Lanshui after dark. On the nineteenth floor of an unmarked building in the business district, Black Pine Guard's regional money-tracking room sat dark except for the pale glow of forty screens.

Every analyst in the room worked in total silence, wearing plain dark jackets as numbers and bank charts rolled across the glass wall.

At the center desk, Number 11 stood behind Number 73's chair, watching lines of bank transfer code pour down the monitor. Number 73 typed at a steady pace, unlocking a massive forty-terabyte copy of a hidden drive.

Twelve hours earlier, Black Pine field agents had physically copied that drive from hidden, water-cooled servers inside a private basement vault under a bank branch in downtown Lanshui. On paper, the vault held ordinary local building-loan records. In reality, the servers belonged to Shengyuan Capital, the parent group behind Hongrui Real Estate and the demolition along Yunxi County's Old Street.

"The fourth lock is still holding," Number 73 said flatly. "They change the key every six hours using a time stamp tied to a local land auction."

"Which auction?" Number 11 asked quietly.

"Yunxi County, last autumn. Our guesses keep missing by a few minutes."

Number 11 leaned forward, his eyes sharpening. "Use the Yunxi County land auction record from last November. Hongrui Real Estate won lot seven at thirty percent below the starting price on a Friday afternoon. Their deposit cleared through the Lanshui bank eleven minutes early. Feed that eleven-minute gap into the key."

Number 73's brow twitched in quiet understanding. He typed in the number and pressed enter. For four seconds, white code froze on the screen. Then the lock flashed green, and layer four peeled open.

Secret international bank transfer logs flooded across the main display wall. Black Pine's tracking software sorted them at once into clean, easy-to-read charts.

"We are inside," Number 73 murmured.

At first glance, the top layer looked like ordinary provincial corruption. Fake building firms charged inflated prices for concrete and steel on highway jobs. County land offices signed cheap land deals over to Hongrui Real Estate. Unrecorded rare-earth ore from the illegal tunnel under Yunxi's Old Street was mixed into legal quarry slips.

"Routine county and province theft," Number 11 observed coolly. "Open the deep hidden files beneath the local accounts."

Number 73 opened the hidden section. Within two seconds, his fingers stopped dead over the keyboard. His shoulders went rigid.

"Number 11," Number 73 said, his flat voice dropping lower. "This is not construction skimming."

Number 11 stepped closer to the wall. Glowing transfer lines spread outward from Lanshui, crossing the coastal provinces and piercing straight past the national border.

"Trace where block nine's money comes from," Number 11 ordered tightly.

Number 73 pulled up the source list, his hands moving fast now. "Every single inflow into Shengyuan Capital's secret vault comes from top state companies. Long-term energy supply deals. Rare-earth and lithium quotas. Deep-water port leases."

"How are they pulling the money out before state audits catch it?" Number 11 asked.

"Fake shipping delay claims, rigged price deals, and fake machine rental bills," Number 73 replied, his jaw hardening. "Shengyuan Capital's hidden servers are skimming a fixed slice of state resource money before those funds ever reach provincial treasury accounts."

"Follow the money out," Number 11 murmured. "Where does the yuan go once it leaves Lanshui?"

Number 73 highlighted the glowing yellow lines. "Through local shell companies in Lanshui and three nearby capitals, then into cross-border trade channels. Within twenty-four hours, the money shows up overseas as gold-backed cash papers with no owner names attached. They sit in private vaults in Singapore, Zurich, and the Cayman Islands."

He let out a slow breath, staring at the wall. "County demolitions and rare-earth smuggling are just small cover to wash cash. Hongrui Real Estate in Yunxi County is one tiny valve on the bottom of a giant pipe. The real flow is draining straight out of state energy, minerals, and ports."

Number 11's hand tightened on the back of the chair. "Keep digging. Gold-backed papers in Zurich and the Caymans do not sit untouched for twenty years. Where do the outgoing payments land?"

Number 73 matched the Zurich and Cayman payout dates against Black Pine Guard's global threat list. The progress bar crept forward for eight long seconds.

When the results appeared in dark red on the right-hand screen, Number 73 froze. Beside him, even Number 11 stopped breathing for a heartbeat.

Every three months, on the fifteenth of March, June, September, and December, set sums of cash flowed from the Cayman and Zurich accounts into fourteen seemingly unrelated foreign firms. Shipping insurers in Rotterdam. Aircraft advisers in Frankfurt. Satellite data firms in Delaware and Singapore.

Every single one carried a top-level red flag in Black Pine's global threat records. They were not luxury holding firms for corrupt officials. They were known money fronts run by foreign military intelligence services.

"Check the bank proof," Number 11 said, his voice sharp as cold steel. "Are those one-way transfers, or are they payments for delivered goods?"

Number 73 opened the details on the last six quarterly payouts. A faint crease formed between his brows. "Each payment carries a secret thirty-two-character delivery proof code, paired with a courier drop record routed right back to a local pickup point here in Lanshui."

"Check the timing," Number 11 said quietly.

"They are pay-on-delivery settlements," Number 73 confirmed, red light reflecting off his dark glasses. "And the newest delivery code cleared four days ago, twenty-four hours before Hongrui Real Estate moved on Old Street in Yunxi County."

Nobody in the dark room spoke. At nearby desks, two analysts had stopped typing and were staring silently at the red lines on the wall. They had entered Yunxi County to trace a demolition gang and clear an innocent uncle's name for Chen Xuan. Instead, pulling one loose thread on Old Street had exposed a hidden pipeline draining stolen state money straight into foreign military intelligence.

Number 11 reached for the secure intercom and pressed the direct button to regional command.

"Number 1," Number 11 said evenly. "Financial Intelligence has opened Shengyuan Capital's Lanshui vault servers. We have solid bank records linking state energy, mineral, and port money through Singapore, Zurich, and Cayman gold-backed accounts into fourteen foreign military intelligence fronts. Active pay-on-delivery codes end right here in Lanshui."

A brief, heavy silence answered before Number 1's calm voice came through. "Are the codes verified?"

"Double-checked," Number 11 answered. "The latest payment cleared four days ago."

"Lock down the floor," Number 1 ordered at once. "Cut all outside phone and internet lines. Send those shipping codes to Number 34's tech team right now, and tell Number 34 to open the locked hard drive we copied tonight."

"Understood," Number 11 replied. He ended the call, looked back at the red lines burning across the glass wall, and knew the night in Lanshui had barely begun.
