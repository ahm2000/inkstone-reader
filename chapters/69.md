# Chapter 69: Shanghai Municipal Emergency Session

At eight o'clock sharp on Monday morning, the third-floor emergency room of the Shanghai Government felt like a boiler about to blow. Thick smoke hung over the long table, hiding the pale faces of twelve department heads pulled off their commute with ten minutes' warning.

At the head of the table, Shanghai Municipal Party Secretary Lin Haibo crushed his third cigarette in fifteen minutes and tapped a fourth from a crumpled pack. His collar button was undone, and his jaw clenched tight.

Beside him, Shanghai Mayor Wu Yongqiang wore a stiff, fake smile that never reached his eyes, his thumb rubbing nervously up and down his porcelain mug.

"Where is Public Security Chief Qi Tongwei?" Lin Haibo snapped, voice rough from smoke. "His deputy told my secretary at seven thirty that Qi Tongwei never made it to the office and his home phone is dead. Did nobody in Shanghai check on a bureau director before eight?"

Two seats down, Shanghai Municipal Public Security Bureau Deputy Chief Hou Zhenbang cleared his throat uneasily and slid a folded note across the table.

"Secretary Lin Haibo, Mayor Wu Yongqiang," Hou Zhenbang said tightly. "I sent two plainclothes officers past Qi Tongwei's compound at seven forty. The gate guard said three black sedans with Beijing central plates pulled in just after midnight. Men in dark suits showed central discipline badges, walked Qi Tongwei out in his coat, and drove off without telling our bureau or Jingzhou City."

Wu Yongqiang's smile vanished. His hand froze on his mug. "Midnight? Central discipline officers took Shanghai's Customs Director from his home at midnight without a single warning call to the City Party Committee?"

"That is not the worst of it, Mayor Wu Yongqiang!" Port Authority Director Qian Zhigang blurted, half-standing. His shaking elbow knocked his teacup, spilling hot tea across his folder, and he did not even wipe it. "At seven thirty, forty central customs inspectors backed by armed naval guards rolled through Shanghai Port's main truck gate! No city permits, and no call to my office!"

Lin Haibo's cigarette stopped halfway to his lips. "Armed naval guards inside a city port? What are they seizing, Qian Zhigang?"

"Pier Seven!" Qian Zhigang gasped, sweat running down his nose. "They locked down every crane and customs warehouse on Pier Seven, ordered dockworkers off the deck at gunpoint, and kicked my computer manager out of the Port Authority server room! They have taken total control of Shanghai Port's entire shipping computer room!"

Pure panic shot around the table like a live wire.

Municipal Commerce Director Feng Jianhua jerked upright. "The shipping computer room? Five years of cargo lists, customs stamps, and tax refund records sit on those machines! If central customs checks Pier Seven's records, half the shipping companies in Shanghai will freeze before noon!"

"Forget the shipping companies, Feng Jianhua!" Lin Haibo barked, slamming his lighter onto the wood. "Qi Tongwei vanishes at midnight. Armed naval guards seize Pier Seven at seven thirty. Why Pier Seven? Who holds the main customs warehouse lease on Pier Seven?"

A choking silence fell over the room. Every official stared down at the table.

Wu Yongqiang's face tightened, sweat shining along his temples. He turned slowly toward Qian Zhigang. "Answer Secretary Lin Haibo, Qian Zhigang. Which shipping group rents Pier Seven?"

Qian Zhigang swallowed hard, throat clicking. "Haiyuan Coastal Logistics, Mayor Wu Yongqiang. Along with three partner freight companies registered in Yunxi County."

"Haiyuan Coastal Logistics is a shell front for Chairman Deng Yongchang!" Municipal Finance Director Zhou Liqiang burst out, eyes wide with fear. "Everyone in the trade bureau knows Deng Yongchang runs Pier Seven's midnight container swaps! Last year when my auditors found 40 million yuan in missing customs taxes from Pier Seven, Qi Tongwei told us to drop it because the break was approved from upstairs!"

Wu Yongqiang slapped his palm onto the table. "Watch your mouth, Zhou Liqiang! Who told you I touched tax breaks for Deng Yongchang? Customs answers straight to Beijing, not to my mayor's office!"

Qian Zhigang turned chalk-white, lower lip shaking over his wet folder. "Mayor Wu Yongqiang, I am not blaming anyone. It is just that central customs inspectors asked my deputy who signed the city fast-track passes for Pier Seven's night barges over the past two years."

"And what did your deputy tell them?" Wu Yongqiang demanded, voice rising as his knuckles turned white.

"He told them the truth, Mayor Wu Yongqiang," Qian Zhigang whispered miserably. "Public Security Chief Qi Tongwei cleared the customs papers, and the city fast-track passes carried the red stamp of the Mayor's Deputy General Office."

Silence hit like a dropped safe.

Lin Haibo turned slowly to stare at Wu Yongqiang through the smoke, eyes cold and narrow. "Wu Yongqiang, you told the Standing Committee in March that Pier Seven's night lane carried fresh seafood exports. Did you read the cargo lists before your deputy office stamped them?"

"I sign two hundred approvals a week, Lin Haibo!" Wu Yongqiang shot back, throwing his fountain pen onto his desk pad. "You think I pry open shipping crates at two in the morning? Qi Tongwei vouched for Haiyuan Coastal Logistics! Deputy Secretary Gao Yuliang's office in Jingzhou City called my secretary twice telling us not to slow down coastal shipping! If anyone started this fire, it was Jingzhou City and Customs, not me!"

"Do not hide behind Deputy Secretary Gao Yuliang," Hou Zhenbang muttered darkly, glancing at the doors. "I called Jingzhou City's Provincial General Office ten minutes ago to ask about the naval guards at Pier Seven. The phone rang twenty times and nobody picked up."

Before Lin Haibo could snap back, the double doors swung open without a knock.

A lean man in a dark jacket walked in with two uniformed officers wearing provincial discipline badges. Every voice died mid-sentence.

The officer walked to the head of the table, set a white notice in front of Lin Haibo, and spoke flatly.

"By order of Handong Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin and the Central Joint Inspection Group," the officer said. "Starting right now, all Shanghai city officials at or above deputy bureau level are barred from leaving Shanghai limits until central audit interviews are done. Passports, travel passes, and official cars are suspended. Stay in this building until your names are logged."

The officer walked out, shutting the doors with a heavy click.

Nobody moved. Wu Yongqiang stared at the white slip as if it were a death sentence, breathing shallowly.

Across the table, Qian Zhigang's forgotten cigarette burned down to the filter between his shaking fingers. A sharp burn stung his skin. He flinched, yelped, and dropped the glowing butt into his lap, slapping wildly at his trousers while eleven pale city officials watched through the choking smoke.
