# Chapter 69: The Provincial Freight Alliance

At ten o'clock sharp, the crystal chandeliers above Grand Ballroom A dimmed slightly, and bright stage lights illuminated the wide blue backdrop bearing the emblem of the Lanshui Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum.

After two brief ceremonial welcomes from provincial commerce representatives, the host announced the keynote speaker with theatrical enthusiasm.

"Ladies and gentlemen, please welcome Chairman Song Baisheng of Wanfeng Freight Group, presenting the landmark initiative of this year's forum: the Provincial Smart-Logistics Consolidation Plan."

Thunderous applause rippled across the front VIP tables as a broad-shouldered man in his late fifties strode onto the stage. Song Baisheng wore a custom-tailored dark gray suit with a crimson silk tie, his face tanned and confident, his silver-flecked hair brushed back neatly.

In the front row, beside two European executives, Li Weiming applauded lightly with an approving, proprietary smile. Nobody in the room apart from a tiny circle of insiders knew that the three billion yuan in offshore bridge credit funding Wanfeng Freight Group's rapid expansion had flowed directly from the Northern Ledger's clearing trusts.

Song Baisheng stepped behind the glass podium, rested both hands casually on its edges, and beamed across the packed hall.

"Friends, colleagues, and leaders of Lanshui Province," Song Baisheng began in a resonant, booming voice. "For twenty years, our province's freight sector has been fragmented. Hundreds of tiny county trucking companies run outdated dispatch sheets, compete on razor-thin margins, and leave our coastal ports congested with uncoordinated cargo."

Behind him, a giant high-definition screen lit up with a glowing blue map of Lanshui Province, connecting every highway artery, rail hub, and coastal deep-water port to a single pulsing central hub labeled *Wanfeng Smart-Terminal Cloud*.

"Today, with international strategic advisory support and provincial modernization backing, Wanfeng Freight Group is launching the Provincial Freight Alliance," Song Baisheng declared smoothly. "Within ninety days, every regional logistics enterprise operating inter-city highway permits or coastal port access will integrate into our unified Smart-Logistics Consolidation Plan."

Across the rear tables labeled Small County Logistics, a sudden, uneasy hush fell over the room.

Staffers in crisp hotel uniforms moved swiftly down the aisles, placing thick glossy blue-bound contract booklets in front of every company representative.

Song Baisheng clicked his remote, and the screen flashed three bold bullet points.

"First," Song Baisheng announced, "all independent county fleets with fewer than two hundred heavy vehicles will transition into Wanfeng Franchise Tier Three. Second, franchise fleets will surrender independent dispatch rights and pay an eighteen percent platform management fee on every manifest. Third, any carrier that declines to sign the Smart-Terminal integration agreement by Friday will lose priority clearance at Lanshui East Port, Nanwan Container Terminal, and all provincial toll-rebate corridors."

At Table Twenty-four, General Manager Sun Haoran's face drained of color.

"Eighteen percent?" Manager Hu Dewei gasped under his breath, his fingers trembling as he flipped open the glossy booklet. "Our net profit margin on county routes is barely nine percent! If we pay eighteen percent to Wanfeng Freight Group, Hengxin Logistics will bleed dry in four months!"

"And if we refuse to sign, they cut off our port clearance and highway rebates," Sun Haoran whispered, his voice hoarse and tight. "Without port clearance, we lose Haiyue Group's coastal supply line and half our municipal accounts overnight. This is not an alliance, Hu Dewei. It is a forced takeover dressed up as modernization."

At the neighboring county tables, similar panicked murmurs broke out. Several small fleet owners rose half out of their chairs in shock, only to hesitate as Song Baisheng continued speaking with effortless authority.

"Of course, for smaller firms that lack the capital to upgrade their hardware," Song Baisheng added magnanimously, his smile warm as a shark's, "Wanfeng Freight Group is prepared to offer a generous equity buyout at sixty percent of net asset book value. We want every entrepreneur in Lanshui Province to transition peacefully into the modern era."

Sixty percent of book value meant surrendering decades of hard work, trucks, and client networks for pennies on the dollar.

Sun Haoran sank slowly back into his chair, his shoulders slumping under the sudden weight. He stared blankly at the glossy blue cover in front of him, thinking of the forty-two drivers, dispatchers, and mechanics back in Qingyan City who relied on Hengxin Logistics to feed their families.

"Why did they even invite us to this forum?" Hu Dewei muttered, his eyes red-rimmed with frustration. "So they could hand us our own funeral notice in person?"

On stage, Song Baisheng gestured warmly toward the front row, where Deputy Provincial Governor Wu Zhenbang sat nodding with solemn approval beside Li Weiming. The silent message to every businessman in the ballroom was unmistakable: Wanfeng Freight Group had provincial backing at the highest level, and resisting was futile.

* * *

While Sun Haoran sat pale and silent and Hu Dewei flipped frantically through the financial penalty schedules at the front of the booklet, Chen Xuan remained completely unruffled in the seat beside them.

He set his teacup neatly aside, pulled the glossy blue contract booklet closer, and began turning the pages steadily from the back forward.

Where other delegates stopped after reading the terrifying eighteen percent fee in Section Three, Chen Xuan read past the franchise schedules, past the hardware leasing appendices, and all the way into the dense, pale-gray technical compliance text printed in eight-point font on page forty-seven.

His expression did not change. His breathing stayed slow and even.

Then, near the bottom of page forty-eight, his index finger came to a quiet stop beside a sub-clause buried inside Article 14: *Automated Telemetry, Port Manifest Synchronization, and Cross-Border Cloud Mirroring*.

Chen Xuan read the dense legal paragraph twice, word by word.

According to Article 14, Section 3, every truck, warehouse scanner, and coastal terminal integrated into the Wanfeng Smart-Terminal Cloud was required to transmit real-time bill-of-lading manifests, container weights, customs clearance timestamps, and strategic mineral transit schedules automatically to an encrypted third-party analytics server hosted in Zurich for "European algorithmic route optimization."

In plain language, Wanfeng Freight Group was not merely trying to swallow small county trucking firms like Hengxin Logistics for local profit.

If every freight company in Lanshui Province signed this contract, a foreign server in Zurich would receive live, unfiltered shipping manifests for every coastal port in the province, including rare-earth minerals, industrial alloys, and municipal reserve cargo moving through Lanshui East Port.

Chen Xuan quietly picked up his cheap blue ballpoint pen and made one tiny, neat dot in the margin beside Article 14.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran murmured heavily beside him, rubbing his temples. "What do we do? If Song Baisheng forces a vote tomorrow morning, Hengxin Logistics does not have the strength to fight Wanfeng Freight Group."

Chen Xuan closed the glossy blue booklet gently and placed his hand on top of it. His voice remained calm, warm, and unhurried, cutting cleanly through the suffocating gloom at Table Twenty-four.

"Do not panic yet, General Manager Sun Haoran," Chen Xuan said quietly. "Never sign a contract on the first day before reading every page. And this booklet has a much bigger problem than an eighteen percent fee."

Sun Haoran looked up in surprise, catching the quiet, unshakable steadiness in his young clerk's eyes. Before he could ask what Chen Xuan meant, the morning panel ended in polite applause, and the ballroom doors swung open for the midday break.
