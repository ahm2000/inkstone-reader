# Chapter 33: The Knock That Never Came

Inside the front duty room of Old Street Station, the police radio desk let out one sharp burst of static and went dead.

Deputy Zhou Quan leaned over the counter, jabbing the talk button with a shaking thumb. "Car Three, come in. Car Seven, report where you are on Old Street. Do you hear me?"

Only silence answered. Every green light along the radio panel had gone black.

Zhou Quan slammed the handset down, spun around, and pushed open the frosted glass door of the director's office without knocking. Thick cigarette smoke hit him in the face.

Station Director Cheng Biao sat hunched behind his desk, collar unbuttoned beside an overflowing ashtray. He held his cell phone close to his face, tapping redial over and over with a sweaty thumb.

"Cheng Biao, the whole police radio system is dead," Zhou Quan blurted out, his voice pitching high. "Neither patrol car on Old Street is answering. My phone has zero bars, and the direct line to Chief Liang Guodong will not ring."

Cheng Biao took a rough drag, blew smoke through his nose, and slapped his phone face-down. "Do you think I am deaf? I have been trying to reach Liang Guodong for ten minutes."

"Then why are you just sitting there?" Zhou Quan snapped, leaning across the desk as his eyes darted toward the hall. "Something huge is happening in Yunxi tonight! Is it because of that hardware store owner we locked in Holding Cell Two? I told you at nine o'clock that Yan Tiehu's papers were sloppy! We did not even have a real witness statement!"

Cheng Biao's head snapped up, his face dark and cornered. He pointed his lit cigarette at Zhou Quan's nose. "Do not act like some innocent clerk now, Zhou Quan! Who signed the forty-eight-hour lockup slip? Who took the thick red envelope Yan Tiehu dropped in the back yard? You did not complain about sloppy papers while counting those bills!"

Zhou Quan flinched back half a step, jaw tight. "Chairman Deng Yongchang promised us Chief Liang Guodong had cleared the whole thing with County Magistrate Sun Weiming! How was I supposed to know the county's whole radio system would drop dead at midnight?"

"Shut your mouth!" Cheng Biao hissed, jumping up so fast his chair banged the filing cabinet. "Say Deng Yongchang's name a little louder, why do you not! Look at the hall!"

Both men froze as young night officer Lin Feng stumbled into the doorway. His cap was gone, and his lips shook.

"Director Cheng Biao," Lin Feng gasped in a dry whisper. "The iron yard gate is shut from the outside. Four people are standing right behind the bars in full black masks. No badges, no police cars, and nobody saying a word. One of them looked up at the camera, and every screen in the guard booth went black."

Cheng Biao's cigarette slipped from his fingers onto a stack of arrest forms. Neither he nor Zhou Quan brushed it off. Thin gray smoke curled between them as Old Street Station went cold as a tomb.

Two kilometers away, on the top floor of Hongrui Plaza, Chairman Deng Yongchang's office hung thick with cigarette smoke.

Deng Yongchang paced behind his wood desk in an unbuttoned silk shirt, gripping a dead satellite phone in his right hand and a burning cigarette in his left. Across the desk, chief aide Zhao Ming hunched over four burner phones lined up in a row, sweat soaking his white collar.

"Try Deputy Secretary Wu Jianjun in the province commerce office again!" Deng Yongchang roared, his voice cracking with anger and fear. "Try Shengyuan Capital's regional contact! Try the province police office! How can every phone in this building go dead at once?"

"Chairman Deng Yongchang, I tried all twelve numbers three times each," Zhao Ming stammered. His shaking hand knocked the fourth phone across the desk. "The office phones are dead. The internet box is dead. Even your satellite phone has no signal. Every cell tower in Yunxi County is dark."

Deng Yongchang spun on him, face red with panic, and swept a heavy crystal ashtray off the desk. It hit the carpet with a dull thud, spilling ash over Zhao Ming's shoes.

"Impossible!" Deng Yongchang shouted, jabbing a shaking finger at the dark window. "Do you know how much money Shengyuan Capital moves through Yunxi? Do you know whose names are in that Pier Four account book? Nobody in this province dares cut my lines without calling me first! Send a runner to Pier Four right now and tell Yan Tiehu to untie the boat and leave!"

"Chairman Deng Yongchang, I sent two guards to the elevator five minutes ago," Zhao Ming whispered, thin and scared. "The doors opened in the ground lobby, and neither came back up. The floor light has not moved since."

Deng Yongchang stopped mid-step. His chest heaved once, then went stiff. "What did you just say?"

Before Zhao Ming could answer, the hallway lights outside the frosted double glass doors flickered and dimmed low.

No elevator bell rang. No footsteps crossed the marble hall. Nobody knocked.

Yet against the frosted glass, four tall dark shapes appeared in total silence.

Deng Yongchang's breath caught. Through the narrow clear gap between the doors, he saw plain black gear and smooth, blank full masks. No badges showed on their shoulders. No hand reached for the brass handle. They did not break the lock or yell for him to open up. They simply stood beyond the glass without moving a muscle, sealing him inside his own tower.

"Chairman Deng Yongchang," Zhao Ming whimpered, backing until his spine hit the bookshelf. "Who are they? What do they want?"

Deng Yongchang could not force a word out. The cigarette between his fingers burned to the filter and stung his skin. He dropped it onto the carpet without feeling the burn. His knees went weak as cold water as he stared at the silent figures outside his doors.

Back inside Old Street Station, far down the dim hall from Cheng Biao's office, Holding Cell Two rested in complete peace.

No shouting reached this far. On the narrow wooden bed, Chen Mingde lay on his side under the clean wool blanket. His face was relaxed, his breathing slow and steady. Untouched by all the panic outside, he slept soundly through the night, safe until morning.
