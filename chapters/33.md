# Chapter 33: The Knock That Never Came

Inside the duty room of Old Street Station, the dispatch console emitted one sharp crackle of static and went permanently silent.

Deputy Zhou Quan leaned over the counter, jabbing the transmit bar with a trembling thumb. "Car Three, respond. Car Seven, report your position along Old Street. Do you copy?"

Only dead air answered. Every green status light along the radio rack had shifted to flat black.

Zhou Quan slammed the handset down hard enough to rattle the casing. He spun on his heel, shoved open the frosted glass door of the director's office without knocking, and stepped into a thick blue cloud of cigarette smoke.

Station Director Cheng Biao sat hunched behind his desk, his collar unbuttoned, an ashtray overflowing beside his elbow. He held his mobile phone three inches from his nose, hitting redial over and over with a sweating thumb.

"Cheng Biao, the entire dispatch net is dead," Zhou Quan blurted, his voice pitching too high. "Neither patrol car on Old Street is answering. My phone has zero bars, and the direct line to Chief Liang Guodong will not even ring."

Cheng Biao took a ragged drag from his cigarette, letting smoke spill from his nostrils before slapping his mobile face-down on the blotter. "Do you think I am deaf? I have been trying to call Liang Guodong for ten minutes."

"Then why are you still sitting there?" Zhou Quan snapped, leaning both palms across Cheng Biao's desk. His eyes darted toward the corridor. "Something huge is going down in Yunxi tonight! Is it because of the hardware owner we locked in Holding Cell Two? I told you at nine o'clock that Yan Tiehu's paperwork was sloppy! There was no real witness statement!"

Cheng Biao's head snapped up, his face darkening into a cornered scowl. He pointed his burning cigarette straight at Zhou Quan's nose. "Do not play the innocent clerk with me now, Zhou Quan! Who signed the forty-eight-hour detention slip? Who took the thick red envelope Yan Tiehu dropped in the back courtyard? You did not complain about sloppy paperwork when you counted those bills!"

Zhou Quan flinched back half a step, his jaw tightening. "Chairman Deng Yongchang promised us Chief Liang Guodong had cleared the whole thing with County Magistrate Sun Weiming! How was I supposed to know the county's radio grid would drop dead at midnight?"

"Shut your mouth!" Cheng Biao hissed, springing out of his chair so fast it struck the cabinet behind him. "Say Deng Yongchang's name a little louder, why do you not! Look at the hallway!"

Both men froze as a young night patrolman, Lin Feng, stumbled up to the open doorway. Lin Feng's cap was missing, and his lips shook as he spoke.

"Director Cheng Biao," Lin Feng gasped in a dry whisper. "The iron courtyard gate is closed from outside. There are four people standing right beyond the bars in full black masks. No badges, no patrol cars, no voices. One of them looked straight at the camera, and every monitor in the guard booth went black."

Cheng Biao's cigarette slipped from his fingers onto a stack of arrest forms. Neither he nor Zhou Quan moved to brush it off. Smoke curled slowly between them as the quiet inside Old Street Station turned cold as a tomb.

Two kilometers away on the top floor of Hongrui Plaza, the air inside Chairman Deng Yongchang's office hung thick with cigarette smoke.

Deng Yongchang paced behind his mahogany desk in an unbuttoned silk shirt, his loafers thudding across the carpet. A fresh cigarette burned between his left fingers while his right hand gripped a satellite handset that refused to connect.

Across the desk stood his chief aide, Zhao Ming, hunched over four unregistered burner phones lined up like dominoes. Sweat ran down Zhao Ming's temples and soaked his collar.

"Try Deputy Secretary Wu Jianjun in the provincial commerce office again!" Deng Yongchang roared, his voice cracking with fury and creeping dread. "Try Shengyuan Capital's regional liaison! Try the provincial public security office! How can every single phone in this building go dead at once?"

"Chairman Deng Yongchang, I have tried all twelve numbers three times each," Zhao Ming stammered, his hands shaking so badly that the fourth burner phone clattered across the wood. "Landlines are dead. The fiber router is dead. Even your satellite line will not lock onto a beam. Every signal tower in Yunxi County is dark."

Deng Yongchang whirled on him, his face blotched red with panic. He swept a heavy crystal ashtray off the desk. It hit the carpet with a dull thud, scattering gray ash across Zhao Ming's shoes.

"Impossible!" Deng Yongchang shouted, jabbing a trembling finger at the dark window. "Do you know how much money Shengyuan Capital moves through Yunxi every quarter? Do you know whose names sit behind that Pier Four ledger? Nobody in this province dares cut my lines without a phone call first! Send a runner down to Pier Four right now and tell Yan Tiehu to cast off the barge!"

"Chairman Deng Yongchang, I already sent two guards to the elevator five minutes ago," Zhao Ming whispered, his voice thinning to a squeak. "The elevator doors opened on the lobby, and neither of them came back up. The floor indicator has not moved since."

Deng Yongchang stopped mid-stride. His chest heaved once, then went rigidly still. "What did you just say?"

Before Zhao Ming could answer, the hallway lights outside the frosted double glass doors flickered once and settled into a dim glow.

No elevator chime had sounded. No footsteps had crossed the marble corridor. Nobody raised a hand to knock.

Yet against the frosted glass at the end of the office, four tall, dark silhouettes appeared in complete silence.

Deng Yongchang's breath caught in his throat. Through the narrow clear strip between the double doors, he saw featureless matte-black gear and smooth, expressionless full masks. No insignia glinted on their shoulders. No hand reached for the brass handle. They did not break the lock or demand entry. They simply stood on the far side of the glass in motionless silence, sealing him inside his own tower.

"Chairman Deng Yongchang," Zhao Ming whimpered, backing away until his spine hit the bookshelf. "Who are they?"

Deng Yongchang could not speak. The cigarette between his left fingers burned down to the filter, scorching his skin. He dropped it onto the carpet without feeling the burn, his knees turning soft as cold water as he stared at the silent, unknocking figures beyond his doors.

Back inside Old Street Station, far down the dim corridor from Cheng Biao's smoke-choked office, the iron bars of Holding Cell Two rested in complete peace.

No shouting reached this far down the hall. On the narrow wooden bunk, Chen Mingde lay on his side beneath the clean wool blanket, his face relaxed and his breathing slow, deep, and completely even. Untouched by the panic outside, he slept soundly through the night, entirely safe until morning.
