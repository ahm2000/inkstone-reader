# Chapter 33: The Knock That Never Came

Inside the front duty room of Old Street Station, the police radio desk let out one sharp burst of static and went completely dead.

Deputy Zhou Quan leaned over the counter, jabbing the talk button with a shaking thumb. "Car Three, come in. Car Seven, report where you are on Old Street. Do you hear me?"

Only dead silence answered. Every green light along the radio panel had gone solid black.

Zhou Quan slammed the handset down hard enough to rattle the desk. He spun around, pushed open the frosted glass door of the director's office without knocking, and walked straight into a thick cloud of cigarette smoke.

Station Director Cheng Biao sat hunched behind his desk with his shirt collar unbuttoned and an ashtray overflowing beside his elbow. He held his cell phone three inches from his face, tapping redial over and over with a sweaty thumb.

"Cheng Biao, the whole police radio system is dead," Zhou Quan blurted out, his voice pitching way too high. "Neither patrol car on Old Street is answering. My phone has zero bars, and the direct line to Chief Liang Guodong will not even ring."

Cheng Biao took a rough drag from his cigarette. He blew smoke out through his nose and slapped his phone face-down on the desk. "Do you think I am deaf? I have been trying to reach Liang Guodong for ten minutes straight."

"Then why are you just sitting there?" Zhou Quan snapped, leaning both hands across Cheng Biao's desk. His eyes darted nervously toward the hallway. "Something huge is happening in Yunxi tonight! Is it because of that hardware store owner we locked in Holding Cell Two? I told you at nine o'clock that Yan Tiehu's papers were sloppy! We did not even have a real witness statement!"

Cheng Biao's head snapped up, his face dark and cornered. He pointed his lit cigarette straight at Zhou Quan's nose. "Do not act like some innocent clerk with me now, Zhou Quan! Who signed the forty-eight-hour lockup slip? Who took the thick red envelope Yan Tiehu dropped in the back yard? You did not complain about sloppy papers while you were counting those bills!"

Zhou Quan flinched back half a step, his jaw tight. "Chairman Deng Yongchang promised us Chief Liang Guodong had already cleared the whole thing with County Magistrate Sun Weiming! How was I supposed to know the whole county's radio system would drop dead at midnight?"

"Shut your mouth!" Cheng Biao hissed. He jumped out of his chair so fast it banged against the filing cabinet behind him. "Say Deng Yongchang's name a little louder, why do you not! Look at the hall!"

Both men froze as a young night officer, Lin Feng, stumbled up to the open doorway. Lin Feng's cap was gone, and his lips shook as he tried to speak.

"Director Cheng Biao," Lin Feng gasped in a dry whisper. "The iron yard gate is shut from the outside. There are four people standing right behind the bars in full black masks. No badges, no police cars, and nobody saying a word. One of them looked straight up at the camera, and every screen in the guard booth went black."

Cheng Biao's cigarette slipped right out of his fingers and landed on a stack of arrest forms. Neither he nor Zhou Quan moved to brush it off. Thin gray smoke curled slowly between them as the quiet inside Old Street Station turned cold as a tomb.

Two kilometers away, on the top floor of Hongrui Plaza, the air inside Chairman Deng Yongchang's office hung thick with cigarette smoke.

Deng Yongchang paced behind his big dark wood desk in an unbuttoned silk shirt, his dress shoes thudding across the carpet. A fresh cigarette burned between his left fingers while his right hand gripped a satellite phone that refused to connect.

Across the desk stood his chief aide, Zhao Ming, hunched over four secret burner phones lined up in a row. Sweat ran down Zhao Ming's temples and soaked into his white collar.

"Try Deputy Secretary Wu Jianjun in the province commerce office again!" Deng Yongchang roared, his voice cracking with anger and growing fear. "Try Shengyuan Capital's regional contact! Try the province police office! How can every single phone in this whole building go dead at the same time?"

"Chairman Deng Yongchang, I have tried all twelve numbers three times each," Zhao Ming stammered. His hands shook so hard that the fourth phone slipped and clattered across the desk. "The office phones are dead. The internet box is dead. Even your satellite phone will not pick up a signal. Every cell tower in Yunxi County is dark."

Deng Yongchang spun on him, his face blotched red with panic. He swept a heavy crystal ashtray right off the desk. It hit the carpet with a dull thud, spilling gray ash all over Zhao Ming's shoes.

"Impossible!" Deng Yongchang shouted, jabbing a shaking finger at the dark window. "Do you know how much money Shengyuan Capital moves through Yunxi every few months? Do you know whose names are written in that Pier Four account book? Nobody in this province dares cut my phone lines without calling me first! Send a runner down to Pier Four right now and tell Yan Tiehu to untie the boat and leave!"

"Chairman Deng Yongchang, I already sent two guards to the elevator five minutes ago," Zhao Ming whispered, his voice thin and scared. "The elevator doors opened in the ground lobby, and neither of them came back up. The floor light has not moved since."

Deng Yongchang stopped mid-step. His chest heaved once, then went stiff and still. "What did you just say?"

Before Zhao Ming could answer, the hallway lights outside the frosted double glass doors flickered once and dimmed low.

No elevator bell had rung. No footsteps had crossed the marble hall. Nobody raised a hand to knock.

Yet against the frosted glass at the far end of the office, four tall, dark shapes appeared in total silence.

Deng Yongchang's breath caught in his throat. Through the thin clear gap between the double doors, he saw plain black gear and smooth, blank full masks. No badges showed on their shoulders. No hand reached for the brass door handle. They did not break the lock or yell for him to open up. They simply stood on the far side of the glass without moving a muscle, sealing him inside his own tower.

"Chairman Deng Yongchang," Zhao Ming whimpered, backing away until his spine hit the bookshelf. "Who are they? What do they want?"

Deng Yongchang could not force a single word out. The cigarette between his left fingers burned all the way down to the filter and stung his skin. He dropped it onto the carpet without even feeling the burn. His knees went weak as cold water as he stared at the silent figures standing outside his doors.

Back inside Old Street Station, far down the dim hall from Cheng Biao's smoke-filled office, the iron bars of Holding Cell Two rested in complete peace.

No shouting reached this far down the corridor. On the narrow wooden bed, Chen Mingde lay on his side under the clean wool blanket. His face was relaxed, and his breathing was slow, deep, and steady. Untouched by all the panic outside, he slept soundly through the night, completely safe until morning.
