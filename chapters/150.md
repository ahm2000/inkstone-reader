# Chapter 150: Leave on Time

At 4:47 on a Friday afternoon, the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai had stopped pretending to work. Everyone was watching the clock.

Chen Xuan sat at desk fourteen in a clean gray jacket and dark trousers. Beside his mouse pad sat his scratched green thermos of jasmine tea and half of a warm red-bean bun wrapped neatly in paper. On paper, he was still a junior operations supervisor earning twelve thousand yuan a month before tax. Just like three years ago, he had arrived at 8:55, finished every port spreadsheet before lunch, and kept his desk spotless.

Two desks over, Pan Xiulan snapped her notebook shut with a satisfied smile. "Eight minutes," she said lightly. "If I catch the elevator before five, I can make the 5:30 bus on Binjiang Avenue and still stop by the market for fresh dumplings."

Across the aisle, Zheng Qian leaned back in his chair and stretched both arms. "Sister Pan, you do not even have to worry about jinxing our row anymore. Ever since last month, Friday afternoons on the twelfth floor feel like a holiday."

Pan Xiulan laughed softly and nodded toward desk fourteen. "That is because nobody dares dump fake overtime on us now. Look at Chen Xuan. His screen has been clean since eleven this morning."

Zheng Qian grinned across the aisle. "Brother Chen, you turned down fifty thousand a month and a corner office on Monday, and here you are packing half a red-bean bun again. Are you really taking that home?"

Chen Xuan looked up mildly, unscrewed his green thermos, and took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea. "It is still good," he said in an even voice. "Wasting food is a bad habit."

Just then, the corner office door opened.

Unlike a few months ago, nobody froze or scrambled to fake typing. Department Manager Hu Dewei stepped out calmly, holding a thin blue folder. His face was neither red nor angry. He glanced briefly toward desk fourteen with a careful, almost polite nod before speaking to the room.

"Everyone," Hu Dewei said in a steady, mild voice, "General Manager Sun Haoran just signed off on the southeast dock weight sheets. Everything checked out cleanly before lunch. There is no overtime tonight, so wrap up your desks and go home on time."

"Thank you, Manager Hu!" Zheng Qian called out cheerfully.

"Have a good weekend, Manager Hu," Pan Xiulan added, already slipping her notebook into her purse.

Hu Dewei nodded, lingered for half a second as if wondering whether to say something else to Chen Xuan, then thought better of it and walked quietly back into his office.

Across the floor, chairs rolled back and jackets came off hooks. The chatter was light, warm, and unhurried.

Chen Xuan finished the last warm mouthful of jasmine tea, screwed the lid tight onto his scratched green thermos, and tucked both the thermos and the wrapped red-bean bun into his canvas bag. He clicked his monitor off cleanly.

The wall clock on the far pillar clicked to 4:55.

Chen Xuan stood up and slung his canvas bag over one shoulder. "Have a good weekend, Sister Pan," he said politely. "Have a good weekend, Zheng Qian."

"You too, Chen Xuan!" Pan Xiulan smiled warmly. "Say hello to your parents if you go back to Yunxi County!"

"I will," Chen Xuan replied with a small nod.

He walked unhurriedly down the carpeted aisle and stepped into the elevator hall. On a Friday at five minutes to five, Hengxin Tower's three elevators were normally crowded with office workers rushing down from the upper floors. Yet Chen Xuan never even raised a hand toward the call button.

As he reached the brass doors, the middle elevator chimed softly.

Its doors slid smoothly open to reveal an empty, spotless cab waiting on the twelfth floor just for him.

Chen Xuan stepped inside, pressed the ground floor button, and closed his eyes for six quiet seconds as the elevator glided down.

When he walked out through the glass lobby doors onto Binjiang Avenue, the winter sun was sinking low behind the river, painting the clouds in soft gold and pale rose. Streetlamps flickered on along the wide sidewalk, and the crisp evening air carried the warm smell of roasted sweet potatoes from the corner cart.

Far beyond this ordinary city street, a whole country was breathing easier tonight.

In the capital and across every province, forty national departments stood cleaner, sharper, and more honest than they had in twenty years. Corrupt rings that had clung to power for decades were gone, their names erased from high offices and their files locked behind iron doors.

Inside the national treasury, more than 800 billion yuan in stolen wealth was already moving back into public accounts, piece by steady piece. That money would pave new rural roads, rebuild county hospitals, heat winter classrooms, and keep small town streets like Old Street in Yunxi County bright and safe for ordinary families who only wanted an honest living.

High above the southern skyline of Shanghai, on the quiet stone terrace of an old hillside teahouse overlooking the river, two men in plain dark jackets stood beside a wooden railing, watching the city lights come alive across Binjiang Avenue.

"All forty department ledgers are balanced," Number 7 said quietly, pouring two small cups of clear tea. "The last overseas accounts cleared into the state treasury this morning."

Number 1 picked up his cup and looked out toward the distant stream of evening buses moving along the avenue. His weathered face was calm, free of any pride or hurry. "Good," Number 1 replied evenly. "Keep our teams quiet and out of sight. He wants peace, so we leave him in peace."

Number 7 gave a brief, silent nod and set the teapot down.

Down on Binjiang Avenue, Chen Xuan stopped by the old corner cart, paid five yuan for a hot roasted sweet potato wrapped in brown paper, and walked slowly toward the number twelve bus stop.

Fifty meters behind him, moving effortlessly through the evening crowd along the shadowed edge of the sidewalk, two quiet figures in plain black coats walked at the exact same unhurried pace. They wore no badges, spoke no words, and drew no eyes from the passersby around them. By their own silent, unexplained choice, the Black Guard team stayed cleanly within fifty meters of him, close enough to stop any shadow before it ever formed, yet far enough never to disturb his step.

Chen Xuan did not look back. He did not need to.

He broke the warm sweet potato in half, blew gently on the rising steam, and waited calmly as the headlights of the number twelve bus rolled into view down the bright winter street.

The work was finished, the storm had passed, and it was finally time for a quiet weekend.
