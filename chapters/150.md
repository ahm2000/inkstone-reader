# Chapter 150: Leave on Time

By Friday afternoon, the busy clatter of keyboards at Hengxin Logistics had slowed to a relaxed murmur. Outside the tall windows, river barges drifted along the Huangpu while nearly every clerk began glancing toward the round wall clock above the glass partition.

Chen Xuan sat comfortably at desk fourteen in his gray jacket and dark trousers, his green thermos of jasmine tea resting beside a small paper bag of roasted pumpkin seeds. On the company payroll, he was still the same junior operations clerk he had been for the past three years. Just as he had done every weekday, he had arrived at 8:55 AM sharp, cleared every port spreadsheet before noon, and kept his desk spotless.

Two desks over, Pan Xiulan snapped her notebook shut with a satisfied smile. "Almost time to go," she said cheerfully. "If I catch the elevator before 5:00 PM, I can beat the Friday rush on Binjiang Avenue and pick up fresh pork dumplings for my family."

Across the aisle, Zheng Qian wheeled his chair over with a huge grin he could barely contain. He leaned in and lowered his voice happily.

"Chen Xuan, I owe you dinner next week," Zheng Qian said, patting a fresh folder against his chest. "General Manager Sun just called me into his office after lunch. He promoted me to Senior Operations Coordinator with a raise to 26,000 yuan a month. He told me you put in a good word for me last week."

"You earned it yourself," Chen Xuan said warmly, sliding his paper bag of pumpkin seeds across the desk. "You stayed late three nights fixing the customs manifest. Take some seeds for the subway ride."

Zheng Qian grabbed a handful with a grateful laugh. "Still, if you had taken that Deputy General Manager offer, none of us would have moved up this fast. Are you sure you never regret turning down that deputy manager spot?"

"Not for a single second," Chen Xuan replied easily. "Wait until your first Tuesday evening budget meeting runs past dinner. Then tell me who made the smarter trade."

Pan Xiulan laughed out loud as she slung her tote bag over her shoulder. "He has a point, Zheng Qian. Nobody in this whole office looks as relaxed as Chen Xuan."

The minute hand on the wall clock clicked cleanly onto 4:55 PM.

Chen Xuan did not waste a second. He saved his final log, shut down his monitor, aligned his keyboard straight with the edge of his desk, and screwed the lid tightly onto his thermos. Tucking it into his worn canvas bag, he stood up and zipped his gray jacket.

"Have a good weekend, everyone," Chen Xuan said with a friendly wave.

"See you Monday, Chen Xuan!" Pan Xiulan and Zheng Qian called back together.

As he strolled past the glass corner office, General Manager Sun Haoran looked up from a stack of quarterly forecasts, glanced at the wall clock, and shook his head with a helpless, good-natured smile. He raised his teacup in a quiet salute through the glass, and Chen Xuan nodded back politely before stepping into the elevator.

Down on Binjiang Avenue, a cool late autumn breeze swept golden plane tree leaves along the wide sidewalk. Office workers streamed out of glass towers toward the subway stations, their scarves wrapped tight against the evening chill as car headlights flickered on along the riverfront road.

Chen Xuan walked at an unhurried pace, enjoying the crisp air. Two blocks from the riverfront, he stepped into the warm corner bakery that Qin Yuhan liked. The glass counter glowed under soft yellow lights, filled with freshly baked chestnut tarts, butter rolls, and soft milk bread.

"Two warm chestnut pastries, Auntie," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile.

The rosy-cheeked bakery auntie behind the counter beamed at him as she slipped the pastries into a paper box, then casually tucked a third golden tart into the corner.

"Getting chilly this week, young man," she chatted warmly, tying the box with red string. "Put the extra one in for your wife. Tell her not to skip dinner when she works late."

"Thank you, Auntie, she will be happy," Chen Xuan laughed, tapping his phone to pay before stepping back onto the avenue.

Outside by the bicycle rack, a delivery rider adjusting his helmet caught Chen Xuan's eye, gave an easy, natural nod like an old neighbor, and rolled smoothly into the evening traffic. Around the bus stop, an elderly uncle walking his small white dog chuckled at a pair of college students sharing roasted sweet potatoes. Hundreds of quiet, unhurried lives flowed along the avenue in easy harmony, relaxed and full of everyday warmth.

Chen Xuan walked toward the riverfront park as the streetlights flickered on along the embankment. Inside his pocket, his phone buzzed once.

He pulled it out and smiled at the message from Qin Yuhan: "Left the office early today! Waiting for you by the stone bench under the ginkgo trees. Did you remember the chestnut pastries?"

Chen Xuan tucked his phone away, lifted the warm paper box slightly in his hand, and quickened his step.

Just ahead, beneath the glowing streetlights and drifting golden leaves of the riverfront park, Qin Yuhan stood in her camel coat, waving eagerly with both hands as soon as she spotted his gray jacket. The cool breeze lifted the edge of her cream scarf, and her bright smile warmed the whole evening.

Chen Xuan smiled and walked straight toward her. Everything in the world was calm, steady, and right where it belonged, and he had left work right on time.
