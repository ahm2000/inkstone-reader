# Chapter 48: Forty-Two Black Cases

At four twenty-five in the morning, the Central Leadership Compound's briefing hall already smelled of stale tobacco. Four men sat around the long walnut conference table. No secretaries waited along the walls. No recording lights glowed overhead.

CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi tapped his cigarette against a glass ashtray with a sharp, restless click. His coat was buttoned unevenly from his hurried drive, and the lines around his eyes looked carved in stone.

"Let me make sure I heard you right, Premier Gu Chengshan," Gao Zhengyi said, voice edged with suspicion. "Three hours ago, my entire Commission building was sealed from the outside. My deputy directors could not leave their offices. My servers went dark. Then you call me at four, tell me to leave my phone in my car, and now General Qin Zhengguo claims Zero Guard ran a sweep across fourteen provinces?"

"Every word is accurate, Gao Zhengyi," Gu Chengshan replied, pale and rigid. Smoke drifted from the corner of his mouth. "You are not the only one locked in the dark. None of us had a line out until four."

"That is not an answer!" Gao Zhengyi snapped, leaning forward on his forearms. His eyes shifted sharply to Qin Zhengguo. "My commission polices corruption across the state. If three hundred and eighteen senior officials were pulled from their homes tonight, where were the warrants? Where were my case teams? Who gave Zero Guard authority to bypass the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection?"

Qin Zhengguo did not raise his voice. He looked straight across the smoke at Gao Zhengyi, his gaze flat and merciless. "Sit back, Gao Zhengyi. You will have your answer in two minutes. When you see the cases with your own commission's stamp on them, you may want a second pack of cigarettes."

Gao Zhengyi's jaw locked. His hand paused halfway to his mouth, the glowing cigarette trembling slightly. "Are you implying my own commission is compromised?"

"I am not implying anything," Qin Zhengguo said coldly. "Forty ministries were hit tonight. Do you truly believe yours is the only building in the capital with clean locks?"

Beside Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua exhaled a shaky plume of smoke and rubbed his forehead. "Stop tearing at each other before the doors even open," Zhou Zhenhua muttered. "Look at the clock. Four thirty."

Before the second hand finished its sweep, the heavy double doors swung inward without a sound.

All four men went dead silent.

Twelve figures stepped inside in two synchronized columns. Every operative wore featureless matte-black tactical gear, dark full-face visors, and heavy gloves. No rank patch, unit emblem, or serial number showed anywhere. Their boots struck the carpet in total silence. Nobody spoke.

In each gloved hand hung a matte-black biometric hard case, sealed with recessed steel latches and crisp white agency stencils along the top edge.

Gao Zhengyi's chair scraped back an inch as he stiffened. Zhou Zhenhua forgot to exhale, smoke leaking thinly from his parted lips. Even Gu Chengshan felt the muscles along his spine draw tight as the silent columns moved down both sides of the long table.

One by one, the operatives set the cases down in ruler-straight rows.

Military. State Security. Public Security. CCDI. Supreme Court. Supreme Procuratorate. NPC. CPPCC. Organization. NDRC. Finance. Customs.

Twelve more operatives followed, then another twelve, and a final six, moving without hesitation. Every case landed with the same muffled, heavy thud against the walnut. When the last row clicked into alignment, forty-two matte-black biometric hard cases stretched from one end of the table to the other, leaving barely enough space for the four ashtrays.

The lead operative stopped beside Gu Chengshan, set a thin matte-black index slate on the wood, and gave a single rigid nod. Not a word passed behind the dark visor.

Seconds later, all forty-two cases sat alone under the dim lights. The masked operatives withdrew silently outside the doors, and the heavy brass latch clicked shut from the exterior.

For ten full seconds, nobody moved.

Gao Zhengyi stared at the four matte-black cases directly in front of him, each bearing the stencil of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection. Color drained from his cheeks. "Four cases," Gao Zhengyi whispered, voice hollow. "Four full biometric vaults just for my own commission."

Zhou Zhenhua's eyes tracked down the table, his throat working hard. "Five for Finance," Zhou Zhenhua said, gripping his armrest until his knuckles turned white. "Four for NDRC. Four for Customs. Six for Military." He looked up at Gu Chengshan, genuine dread breaking through his composure. "Premier Gu Chengshan, look at the Supreme Court and the Supreme Procuratorate. Look at Organization. Look at the NPC and the CPPCC. Not a single pillar of the central government is missing from this table."

Gu Chengshan stared at the black wall of cases blocking his view of Qin Zhengguo. His cigarette had burned halfway down, a long column of gray ash dropping unnoticed onto his dark sleeve.

"Fourteen provinces," Gu Chengshan murmured, voice raspy. "Three theater commands. Six ports. Forty-two biometric vaults. This is not a disciplinary handoff. This is an autopsy of the entire bureaucracy."

"Are we being handed a case file, or a death sentence?" Gao Zhengyi asked sharply, his eyes darting from the CCDI cases back to Qin Zhengguo. "If even one list from this table leaks before dawn, the markets will collapse by nine. Half the provincial secretaries will panic and start shredding archives. Who is standing outside those doors right now, Qin Zhengguo? Are we free to walk out of this room?"

Qin Zhengguo sparked a fresh cigarette and blew a thin stream of smoke toward the ceiling, his hand steady only by sheer military discipline. "They are standing outside to keep everyone else out, not to keep us in," Qin Zhengguo said grimly. "Zero Guard does not govern. They do not hold trials, they do not issue statements, and they do not sit in our chairs. They cut out the rot, dropped the proof on our table, and left the cleanup to us."

"Forty-two cases," Zhou Zhenhua repeated weakly, eyes fixed on Finance and Customs. "If we do not sequence this cleanly, half the ministries will paralyze themselves before lunch."

"Then stop talking and look inside," Gu Chengshan said, jaw set hard. He crushed his cigarette into the ashtray, stood up, and lifted the matte-black index slate. One white line glowed across the dark glass: Case One, Executive Summary and Master Ledger.

"All twelve agencies are indexed under Case One," Gu Chengshan said quietly, setting the slate beside the nearest vault. "Gao Zhengyi, pull your chair closer. Zhou Zhenhua, Qin Zhengguo, come around here."

Nobody argued. The three men rose in heavy silence and stepped around the table, cigarette smoke trailing behind them.

Gu Chengshan looked once at the pale, tight faces of his three colleagues and pressed his flat palm onto Case One's biometric scanner.
