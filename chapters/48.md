# Chapter 48: The Port Contract

The raised voices brought General Manager Sun Haoran and Manager Hu Dewei hurrying to the doorway.

Sun Haoran took one look at Zhou Mingcheng and went pale, bowing sharply before crossing the threshold.

"Young Master Zhou! I am terribly sorry," Sun Haoran said quickly, sweat beading on his forehead. "Chen Xuan is only an operations supervisor. Please forgive him on my account."

Hu Dewei darted beside Chen Xuan and tugged at his sleeve. "Chen Xuan, apologize to Young Master Zhou and wait outside."

Chen Xuan did not stand. He lifted his teacup gently so Hu Dewei would not spill it, and looked up at Zhou Mingcheng with the same mild expression.

Seeing Sun Haoran grovel restored Zhou Mingcheng's smugness at once. He set the Bordeaux bottle down loudly beside the braised tofu.

"General Manager Sun, you came at a good time," Zhou Mingcheng said smoothly, glancing at Qin Yuhan. "You know how tightly Qingyan Port Authority controls berth permits this quarter."

Sun Haoran forced an anxious smile. "Everyone knows Shenghua Maritime Group holds the strongest berth allocations at the port."

"Three of the five deep-water slots," Zhou Mingcheng corrected lazily. "Hengxin Logistics holds the South Bay coastal shipping route. It is a prime route, wasted on a tiny firm like yours. My father wants Shenghua Maritime to absorb it before next month."

Sun Haoran's smile froze. The South Bay route made up nearly half of Hengxin Logistics' profit.

"Young Master Zhou, that is our main contract," Sun Haoran stammered. "We signed a three-year schedule."

"Schedules change when your berth permits expire on the first," Zhou Mingcheng said flatly. "Sign the transfer by Friday, and your remaining trucks can use East Dock. Oh, and fire this clerk today. I dislike glued shoes near my business partners."

Hu Dewei's knees went soft. Across the table, Qin Yuhan's eyes turned ice cold as she started to rise.

Before she could speak, Chen Xuan set his teacup down with a quiet click.

"Young Master Zhou," Chen Xuan said politely, his voice completely level. "Since you mentioned port permits, may I ask one small question about Berth Seven?"

The room went dead still. Hu Dewei stared at Chen Xuan in terror.

Zhou Mingcheng frowned in annoyance. "What would a warehouse clerk know about Berth Seven?"

"Very little," Chen Xuan replied modestly, folding his hands on the table. "I only noticed the port weigh-in sheets while checking our South Bay schedule last Friday. On Thursday night, a Shenghua Maritime barge cleared customs at Berth Seven listing twelve hundred tons of low-tariff grain."

Zhou Mingcheng's jaw tightened slightly.

Chen Xuan continued in the same calm, conversational tone. "Yet the harbor tugboat's draft log showed the barge riding low enough for three thousand four hundred tons. And the customs broker stamp belonged to a trading shell flagged by the Port Audit Office two days ago."

Chen Xuan looked up mildly. "I wondered if Shenghua Maritime fixed that clerical typo before quarterly customs reconciliation. Forms like that can trouble whoever signed the berth release."

Silence dropped over Private Room Three like an iron lid.

Zhou Mingcheng's smirk froze solid, and color drained from his cheeks.

Berth Seven was his father Zhou Guodong's private channel for under-declared imports. Even senior directors never spoke of it outside locked offices. How could a twelve-thousand-yuan clerk in glued shoes know the exact tonnage, the draft log, and the audit flag?

"What nonsense are you fabricating?" Zhou Mingcheng snapped, his voice turning harsh. "Do you know what slander costs in Qingyan?"

"I am not accusing anyone," Chen Xuan said gently. "Paperwork errors happen. I only thought I should mention it."

Qin Yuhan caught the genuine panic in Zhou Mingcheng's eyes. A sharp spark lit her gaze as she stood up and buttoned her cream blazer.

"That is enough, Zhou Mingcheng," Qin Yuhan said coldly. "Supervisor Chen Xuan is my invited guest. Hengxin Logistics is a partner of Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group. If Shenghua Maritime uses port permits to extort our contractors, Haiyue will review every warehousing agreement we share with your father."

Zhou Mingcheng whipped his head toward her, flushing dark red. "Yuhan, you are siding with a clerk against me? Our grandfathers drank tea together!"

"My grandfather drinks tea with many people," Qin Yuhan replied evenly. "That does not let you barge into my lunch or insult my guest. Let me be plain. I have zero interest in you, Zhou Mingcheng. Not today, and not ever."

She nodded once. "Jin Ke."

Jin Ke stepped to the door, and two broad-shouldered Haiyue security guards entered immediately.

"Escort Mr. Zhou to the front gate," Qin Yuhan ordered calmly. "Inform the desk he is not admitted without an invitation."

Humiliation burned hot in Zhou Mingcheng's chest. He glared at Chen Xuan, who was calmly wiping his fingers, then snatched his Bordeaux bottle off the table.

"Fine," Zhou Mingcheng hissed, shrugging off the guard's hand. "Remember today, Sun Haoran. And remember your mouth, Chen Xuan."

* * *

Outside in the gravel courtyard, Zhou Mingcheng hurled the Bordeaux bottle into a stone trash bin. Glass shattered with a loud crash, soaking the bin in red wine.

He yanked open his black sedan, dropped inside, and gripped the wheel until his knuckles turned white.

"A twelve-thousand-yuan clerk," Zhou Mingcheng snarled to the empty cabin. "Lecturing me on Berth Seven in front of Qin Yuhan!"

He grabbed his phone and dialed his father's port liaison.

"Ma Zhenlin, listen!" Zhou Mingcheng barked. "Put a full hold on every Hengxin Logistics manifest tonight. Reject their crane slots, stall their customs clearance, and bury their South Bay paperwork. I want Hengxin Logistics crushed at Qingyan Port by Friday!"

He threw the phone onto the passenger seat, his eyes glittering with fury.

* * *

Inside Private Room Three, Sun Haoran bowed deeply to Qin Yuhan, stealing a bewildered glance at Chen Xuan.

"Miss Qin, forgive the disturbance," Sun Haoran said shakily.

"Do not worry about your South Bay route, General Manager Sun," Qin Yuhan said politely. "Send the contract to Jin Ke tomorrow."

Sun Haoran bowed twice and practically dragged Hu Dewei out.

Qin Yuhan sat back down and exhaled. Across from her, Chen Xuan had already picked up the other half of his steamed bun.

"The radish soup is still hot," Chen Xuan said mildly, lifting the ladle. "Another bowl, Miss Qin?"

Qin Yuhan broke into a bright laugh. "Yes, please. Nothing rattles you, does it?"

"Getting angry ruins digestion," Chen Xuan replied, filling her bowl. "Let me write down our warehouse dispatch number for Miss Jin Ke."

He unzipped his canvas bag for a cheap ballpoint pen. As he reached past his thermos pouch, a small object shifted into the sunlight at the mouth of the bag.

It was an old, hand-carved wooden fishing float, painted faded yellow with the paint worn thin at the edges. Cut cleanly into its flat wooden base was a tiny square seal.

Chen Xuan grabbed his pen, tucked the float back inside, and wrote the number on a napkin. Qin Yuhan took it with a warm smile, quietly holding onto the image of that worn yellow wood.
