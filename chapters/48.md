# Chapter 48: Forty-Two Black Cases

At four twenty-five in the morning, the Central Leadership Compound's briefing hall already smelled of stale smoke. Four men sat around the long wood table. No secretaries waited along the walls. No recording lights glowed overhead.

Gao Zhengyi, Deputy Secretary of the CCDI, the state's top anti-graft body, tapped his cigarette against a glass ashtray with a sharp, restless click. His coat was buttoned unevenly from his hurried drive, and the lines around his eyes looked hard as stone.

"Let me make sure I heard you right, Premier Gu Chengshan," Gao Zhengyi said, his voice full of suspicion. "Three hours ago, my whole Commission building was locked from the outside. My deputy heads could not leave their offices. My computers went dark. Then you call me at four, tell me to leave my phone in my car, and now General Qin Zhengguo claims Black Guard ran a sweep across fourteen provinces?"

"Every word is true, Gao Zhengyi," Gu Chengshan replied, pale and stiff. Smoke drifted from the corner of his mouth. "You are not the only one locked in the dark. None of us had a phone line out until four."

"That is not an answer!" Gao Zhengyi snapped, leaning forward on his arms. His eyes shifted sharply to Qin Zhengguo. "My commission fights corruption across the whole state. If three hundred and eighteen top officials were pulled from their homes tonight, where were the warrants? Where were my teams? Who gave Black Guard the right to bypass the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection?"

Qin Zhengguo did not raise his voice. He looked straight through the smoke at Gao Zhengyi, his stare flat and cold. "Sit back, Gao Zhengyi. You will have your answer in two minutes. When you see the cases with your own commission's stamp on them, you may want a second pack of cigarettes."

Gao Zhengyi's jaw locked. His hand stopped halfway to his mouth, the glowing cigarette shaking slightly. "Are you saying my own commission is dirty?"

"I am not hinting at anything," Qin Zhengguo said coldly. "Forty ministries were hit tonight. Do you really think yours is the only building in the capital with clean locks?"

Beside Gu Chengshan, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua blew out a shaky stream of smoke and rubbed his forehead. "Stop fighting each other before the doors even open," Zhou Zhenhua muttered. "Look at the clock. Four thirty."

Before the second hand finished its sweep, the heavy double doors swung inward without a sound.

All four men went dead silent.

Twelve figures stepped inside in two neat rows. Every operative wore plain black gear, dark full-face visors, and heavy gloves. No rank patch, unit mark, or number showed anywhere. Their boots hit the carpet in total silence. Nobody spoke.

In each gloved hand hung a black fingerprint-locked case, sealed with steel latches and crisp white agency labels along the top edge.

Gao Zhengyi's chair scraped back an inch as he stiffened. Zhou Zhenhua forgot to breathe out, smoke leaking thinly from his parted lips. Even Gu Chengshan felt the muscles along his back pull tight as the silent rows moved down both sides of the long table.

One by one, the operatives set the cases down in ruler-straight lines.

Military. State Security. Public Security. CCDI. Supreme Court. Supreme Procuratorate. NPC. CPPCC. Organization. NDRC. Finance. Customs.

Twelve more operatives followed, then another twelve, and a final six, moving without pause. Every case landed with the same heavy thud against the wood. When the last row lined up, forty-two black cases stretched from one end of the table to the other, leaving barely enough room for the four ashtrays.

The lead operative stopped beside Gu Chengshan, set a thin black index tablet on the table, and gave a single stiff nod. Not a word came from behind the dark visor.

Seconds later, all forty-two cases sat alone under the dim lights. The masked operatives walked silently out the doors, and the heavy brass lock clicked shut from outside.

For ten full seconds, nobody moved.

Gao Zhengyi stared at the four black cases right in front of him, each stamped with the name of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection. Color drained from his cheeks. "Four cases," Gao Zhengyi whispered, his voice hollow. "Four full locked cases just for my own commission."

Zhou Zhenhua's eyes tracked down the table, his throat working hard. "Five for Finance," Zhou Zhenhua said, gripping his armrest until his knuckles turned white. "Four for NDRC. Four for Customs. Six for Military." He looked up at Gu Chengshan, real dread breaking through his calm. "Premier Gu Chengshan, look at the Supreme Court and the Supreme Procuratorate. Look at Organization. Look at the NPC and the CPPCC. Not a single part of the central government is missing from this table."

Gu Chengshan stared at the black wall of cases blocking his view of Qin Zhengguo. His cigarette had burned halfway down, a long line of gray ash dropping unnoticed onto his dark sleeve.

"Fourteen provinces," Gu Chengshan murmured, his voice rough. "Three theater commands. Six ports. Forty-two locked cases. This is not a normal handoff. This is a full teardown of the whole government."

"Are we being handed a case file, or a death sentence?" Gao Zhengyi asked sharply, his eyes darting from the CCDI cases back to Qin Zhengguo. "If even one list from this table leaks before dawn, the markets will crash by nine. Half the provincial leaders will panic and start shredding files. Who is standing outside those doors right now, Qin Zhengguo? Are we free to walk out of this room?"

Qin Zhengguo lit a fresh cigarette and blew a thin stream of smoke toward the ceiling, his hand steady only by sheer army discipline. "They are standing outside to keep everyone else out, not to keep us in," Qin Zhengguo said grimly. "Black Guard does not rule. They do not hold trials, they do not make speeches, and they do not sit in our chairs. They cut out the rot, dropped the proof on our table, and left the cleanup to us."

"Forty-two cases," Zhou Zhenhua repeated weakly, eyes fixed on Finance and Customs. "If we do not handle this step by step, half the ministries will freeze up before lunch."

"Then stop talking and look inside," Gu Chengshan said, his jaw set hard. He crushed his cigarette into the ashtray, stood up, and lifted the black index tablet. One white line glowed across the dark glass: Case One, Main Summary and Master Account Book.

"All twelve agencies are listed under Case One," Gu Chengshan said quietly, setting the tablet beside the nearest case. "Gao Zhengyi, pull your chair closer. Zhou Zhenhua, Qin Zhengguo, come around here."

Nobody argued. The three men stood up in heavy silence and walked around the table, cigarette smoke trailing behind them.

Gu Chengshan looked once at the pale, tight faces of his three colleagues and pressed his flat palm onto Case One's fingerprint scanner.
