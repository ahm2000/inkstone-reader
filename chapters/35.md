# Chapter 35: Gift Envy

On Monday morning in Hengxin Logistics' Operations Department, a long gold-and-red box stood in the middle of Zhang Pengfei's desk, and the whole floor had already walked around it twice.

"Colleagues!" Zhang Pengfei stood up, straightened his tie, and spoke in a voice that reached the back row. "The vote for Employee of the Quarter is at the end of the month, so I will not say one word about it! This is only a small thank-you for working with me. The tea comes from my uncle at the Municipal Trade Bureau. He sent it himself!"

He lifted the lid, and twelve small tins lay inside, each wrapped in gold paper.

"A box like that costs eight hundred yuan!" a young clerk whispered.

"Eight hundred? That is two weeks of my lunch money!"

"An uncle at the Trade Bureau..."

Sister Pan pushed up her glasses and pulled out the small blue notebook she used for her lunch bets, which she called the Gift Table of the Third Floor.

"Ranking," she announced, "is open!"

Everyone understood what Zhang Pengfei was really doing. A gift before a vote is called "warmth," and a gift after a vote is called "thanks." Zhang was a generous colleague, but to put it bluntly, he was buying twelve tins' worth of votes!

Lin Dong passed around salted peanuts and took third place. A girl from accounting gave out hand cream and took fourth.

At the end of the line, Sister Pan raised one eyebrow.

"Supervisor Chen?"

Chen Xuan was already walking down the aisle with his canvas bag. From it he took a stack of small clear bags, each holding a handful of pickled radish that still shone with chili oil.

"My mother made these on Saturday," he said. "She says they are better on rice, or with congee, or alone at three in the afternoon."

He set one on every desk, including Auntie Lu's cleaning cart and Zhang Pengfei's.

"Tea first, peanuts third, radishes last, and the odds against are ten to one," Sister Pan read aloud as she wrote.

The bet itself was simple: every colleague paid for a lunch, and whoever guessed the best gift at the end of the month got all the lunches. The radishes were the long shot.

"Ten to one on radishes?" somebody laughed.

"Ten to one!" said Sister Pan, delighted.

Zhang Pengfei smiled, tall and proud, and moved the tea box to the front of the pile with the label facing out.

Just then, a mild voice spoke from beside the filing cabinet.

"That's a nice box."

Lu Qinghe, sixty-one, the cleaning auntie, was wiping the cabinet with a gray cloth, and she did not even look up.

"I saw the same one on Fuxing Road, in the card shop," she said. "You buy a gift card for two hundred sixty, and they swap it for the box. The slip is stuck to the bottom, see? Nice ribbon, though."

A gift card is a prepaid card, and shops will trade it for goods. Everyone looked at the bottom of the box.

A small white slip was stuck there, stamped *Redeemed with gift card. Tianhe Tea Mart.*

Silence.

It was so complete that Chen Xuan heard the air conditioner click!

Zhang Pengfei's face went red from his collar to his ears.

Who had told her? How did a cleaner know the price of a card? Should he shout, or say it was a mistake?

Everyone waited for exactly that, because the loud answer was the one he had always given.

Instead, Zhang Pengfei sat down.

"My uncle really did send it," he said, very quietly. "He sent a card, and I traded it, because I wanted it to look like more."

"A card?" a clerk breathed.

"Quiet, he will hear you!" somebody hissed.

Nobody laughed, and Sister Pan slowly closed her notebook, while the girl from accounting set her tin of tea back on his desk, murmuring something about an allergy she did not have.

Chen Xuan had only just noticed that a contest was going on, so he raised his thermos.

"If there is a tin left, may I try one? The leaves in my thermos have been working since Friday. They deserve a day off."

Zhang Pengfei stared at him for a moment, then pushed a tin across the desk with two fingers.

"It's good tea," he said stiffly. "That part is true."

"Mm. It smells nice," said Chen Xuan, and that was all he said.

Sister Pan crossed out the odds on the radishes, then wrote a new line so small that nobody could see what it said.

---

By evening, only the hum of the monitors was left on the Operations floor.

Zhang Pengfei was still at his desk, and he was still ashamed. At lunch he had wanted to throw the tea box into the bin.

He closed his own report and, out of habit, opened Chen Xuan's weekly freight sheet, the one due to General Manager Sun on Tuesday morning, because beating that sheet was his private dream.

Then he stopped.

Row forty-one!

"Well, well," he whispered. "The great Chen Xuan."

A customer code had been copied wrong, one digit off, from an old template. Chen Xuan, the man who never made mistakes, had made one!

Firstly, if Zhang told Manager Hu tomorrow in front of everyone, he would gain a point. Secondly, Chen Xuan would lose one. It would be easy, and legal, and very satisfying.

Zhang Pengfei felt his heart quicken, because the chance in front of him was almost too easy.

So why did his hand not move? Was it the tea, the slip, or the man who had asked for one tin and said nothing more?

He looked at the radish bag still sitting beside his keyboard.

"Ah, forget it," he muttered, and corrected the digit himself, saved the file, and left no note.

---

The next morning, Chen Xuan opened his sheet and saw the fixed row at once. He checked the edit history, because he was careful, and saw whose name was on it.

"Hmm," he said, pleased, and set two red-bean buns on the corner of Zhang Pengfei's desk without a word.

When Zhang Pengfei came in, he stared at the buns for a very long time.

"Are those for him?" a clerk whispered.

"Don't ask," Sister Pan whispered back. "Just write it down."

"Supervisor Chen!" Hu Dewei hurried over with his flowerpot mug in one hand, the other tugging at his tie. "Quick news! The visitor from Haiyue Hospitality is coming next week. Miss Qin."

"Miss Qin?"

"That is all I know!" Hu Dewei's hand shook so hard that his tea splashed. "General Manager Sun says the Employee of the Quarter has lunch with her! I mean, with the group! Not that it concerns, I mean, why am I telling you?!"

He fled, pale and sweating, though nobody had said anything frightening.

Chen Xuan blinked, then went to his desk and looked at his chair.

It had been adjusted, and the height was exactly right, the back leaned at exactly his angle, and on the seat lay a soft blue heating pad, plugged in under the desk. A sticky note was pressed on top, in the neat, round handwriting from the cleaning schedule.

*Eat.*

By the door stood a gray cleaning cart, and on its top shelf sat a radio that never played.

"Funny," Chen Xuan said to himself. "Someone else knows about heating pads."

Beside the cart, Lu Qinghe wiped the same spot on the wall for the fourth time and did not look his way.
