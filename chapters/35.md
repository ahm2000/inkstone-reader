# Chapter 35: The Tunnel Under Old Street

Twelve meters under the dark stone pavement of Old Street, inside an old 1970s air-raid tunnel storage room linked to an empty city drain two blocks away, the main power switch died with a heavy thud.

Above ground, not a single porch light flickered along the quiet lane. The closed wooden front of Mingde Hardware stayed completely calm and untouched. Down inside the concrete room, however, the hum of three big computer racks dropped into a dying whine as dim red backup bulbs clicked on along the curved ceiling.

Su Wenhao spun around from the steel sorting table, his eyes wide behind his thin glasses. "The main power just cut out! The backup generator did not even turn on!"

Across the narrow concrete aisle, David Mercer slammed his palm onto the main desk, watching the locked computer screen freeze halfway through copying. His jaw locked tight. "Check the cable line to Hongrui Plaza right now, Su Wenhao! Did Deng Yongchang's idiots blow a city fuse again?"

Su Wenhao jabbed fast at a spare test laptop running on battery power. Sweat broke out right away along his hairline. "It is not a fuse! The cable line to Hongrui Plaza is dead. The spare radio link up the drain pipe is dead too. Even our auto-wipe timer just froze at sixty seconds without counting down! Somebody killed our remote switch from the outside!"

David Mercer shoved his chair back so hard it slid across the concrete floor. For one brief second, pure panic flashed across his usually calm face. "We are burned," he snapped in sharp English before switching fast into smooth, accented Mandarin. "Forget the remote switch! Pull the burn pins on the hard drives and light up the twenty-year paper account books right now!"

"Those burn pins will choke all the air out of a closed tunnel!" Su Wenhao yelled, his voice cracking as he scrambled toward three open steel trunks stacked with bound Shengyuan Capital transfer books and stamped shipping lists. "I will pour lamp oil on the paper books first! You smash the locked hard drives!"

"Do not argue with me, Su Wenhao, burn them!" David Mercer snarled. He wrenched open a metal drawer under the desk and grabbed a heavy hammer and a small gray pistol. "If state security walks out of here with those signed paper books and overseas bank drives intact, neither our backers abroad nor Shengyuan Capital will ever pull us out!"

Su Wenhao yanked the red cap off a metal oil can with shaking hands. He splashed sharp-smelling fuel unevenly across the top pages of the open account books. His chest heaved as he fumbled a metal lighter out of his jacket pocket. His thumb slipped twice against the wheel before a yellow spark jumped.

At the far end of the concrete room, the heavy 1970s steel door did not rattle or blow open.

Instead, its rusted iron wheel turned smoothly from the outside without even a squeak. The thick steel door swung quietly inward into the red emergency light.

Both men froze in place.

"Who is out there?" David Mercer shouted in sharp Mandarin. He swung his small pistol toward the dark doorway while his left hand hovered near the main hard drive rack. "Stop right where you are! Stay back or I shoot!"

Nobody shouted back. No flashlight beam cut through the dim red room.

Four dark figures dressed head to boot in plain black gear stepped smoothly out of the silent drain tunnel. Smooth, blank full masks covered their faces completely. They wore no badges, no rank strips, and no unit marks, and they moved without a shred of hesitation.

"Burn it!" David Mercer screamed, shifting his aim toward the lead figure. "Su Wenhao, drop the lighter right now!"

Su Wenhao flicked his thumb down. A bright yellow flame jumped up half an inch above the oil-soaked pages.

Before his wrist could even tilt down, a black-clad figure crossed the six paces between them like a shadow. One crisp chop struck the inside of Su Wenhao's elbow, turning his whole forearm numb at once. A second gloved hand plucked the lit lighter cleanly out of midair and snapped the lid shut before a single corner of paper could burn.

In the exact same heartbeat, David Mercer pulled his trigger. A second masked guard stepped inside his reach, jammed the top of the gun back with an open palm, and drove an elbow cleanly into Mercer's collarbone. The pistol clattered across the concrete floor. Mercer staggered back against the computer rack, gasping for air, before two smooth holds twisted his arms behind his back and forced him onto his knees beside the desk.

Not a single gunshot had sounded. Not a single hard drive had been scratched. Not one neighbor sleeping twelve meters above on Old Street had even turned over in bed.

Within ten seconds, two masked guards were already sliding the untouched hard drives into padded black carrying cases. A third guard sealed the oil-dampened paper account books inside airtight plastic sleeves, lifting every book whole without smudging a single red stamp.

On his knees beside the table, Su Wenhao shook from head to toe. Tears of pure fear stung his eyes as he stared at the blank dark eye shields around him. "Please," Su Wenhao stammered, his voice breaking apart. "I am only a file clerk! I only copied the account books Chairman Deng Yongchang's men sent down here! I never hurt anyone! I will sign a full confession right now!"

Beside him, David Mercer forced himself to breathe slower. His eyes darted wildly from one blank mask to the next, hunting for a leader's badge, a patch, or any human sign he could use. Seeing none, he swallowed hard and spoke fast in English, then repeated himself urgently in Mandarin.

"Listen to me carefully!" David Mercer said, his voice tight and strained even as he tried to sound calm. "You do not know who owns these drives! I carry foreign trade diplomatic protection backed by three overseas banks! If you hold me in secret, you will start an international crisis before sunrise! Let me make one call to our regional lawyer, and I will give you every password right now in exchange for a consulate handover!"

Only silence answered him. The dim red emergency lights cast flat red gleams across the smooth black masks.

David Mercer's calm mask finally broke. Sweat ran down his jaw as he stared up into the cold stillness of the guard standing right in front of him. "Are you State Security? Military Intelligence? Tell me who you work for! Name your price, name your office! Every man in this room can walk away rich if we talk right now!"

The masked Zero Guard looked down without a hint of curiosity, anger, or hurry.

From behind the plain black mask came a flat, even voice, cold as dry ice sliding over bare steel.

"Evidence secured. Subject held."

David Mercer's mouth hung open. His next frantic sentence died unspoken in his throat as cold dread finally crushed every bit of his training.

In complete silence, the masked guards lifted the sealed computer cases and trunks of account books. They led both bound men smoothly out through the old city drain, leaving Old Street quiet, safe, and sound asleep above them.
