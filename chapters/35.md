# Chapter 35: The Tunnel Under Old Street

Twelve meters beneath the dark cobblestones of Old Street, inside a reinforced 1970s civil-defense cargo vault linked to an abandoned municipal drainage junction two blocks away, the main power relay died with a dull mechanical thud.

Above ground, not a single porch lamp flickered along the quiet lane, and the shuttered front of Mingde Hardware remained completely untouched and silent. Down inside the concrete chamber, however, the hum of three cold-storage server racks dropped abruptly into a falling whine as dim red emergency bulbs clicked on along the arched ceiling.

Su Wenhao spun around from the steel sorting table, his eyes widening behind his wire-rimmed glasses. "The primary feed just cut! The backup generator did not kick in!"

Across the narrow concrete aisle, David Mercer slammed his palm onto the cold-storage console, watching the encrypted terminal freeze mid-sync. His tanned jaw locked hard. "Check the fiber bridge to Hongrui Plaza right now, Su Wenhao! Did Deng Yongchang's idiots trip a municipal breaker again?"

Su Wenhao jabbed furiously at a secondary diagnostics laptop running on battery power. Sweat broke out instantly along his hairline. "It is not a breaker! The fiber line to Hongrui Plaza is dead, the backup wireless relay up the drainage vent is dead, and our dead-man wipe timer just froze at sixty seconds without counting down! Somebody killed our remote trigger from outside!"

David Mercer shoved his chair back so hard it skidded across the concrete floor. For a split second, pure, unguarded panic flashed across his composed, foreign-trained features. "We are burned," he snapped in clipped English before switching sharply into fluent, accented Mandarin. "Forget the remote trigger! Pull the thermite pins on the server blades and burn the twenty-year paper ledgers right now!"

"Thermite will choke the air inside a sealed tunnel!" Su Wenhao yelled, his voice cracking as he scrambled toward the three open steel trunks stacked with bound Shengyuan Capital transfer books and stamped customs manifests. "I am using kerosene on the paper ledgers first! You shatter the encrypted drives!"

"Do not argue with me, Su Wenhao, burn them!" David Mercer snarled, wrenching open a metal drawer under the console to grab a heavy claw hammer and a compact matte-gray pistol. "If state counter-espionage walks out of here with those hard-copy signatures and the offshore clearing drives intact, neither our overseas handlers nor Shengyuan Capital will ever pull us out!"

Su Wenhao yanked the red cap off a metal kerosene tin with trembling hands, sloshing sharp-smelling fuel unevenly across the top pages of the open ledger trunk. His chest heaved as he fumbled a metal windproof lighter out of his jacket pocket. His thumb slipped twice against the flint wheel before a yellow spark jumped.

At the far end of the concrete chamber, the heavy 1970s blast door did not rattle or explode.

Instead, its rusted iron locking wheel turned smoothly from the outside without a single metallic squeak. The thick steel slab swung quietly inward into the red emergency glow.

Both couriers froze.

"Who is out there?" David Mercer shouted in sharp Mandarin, swinging his compact pistol toward the dark doorway while his left hand hovered over the server rack's main drive bay. "Stop right where you are! Stay back or I shoot!"

Nobody shouted back. No flashlight beam stabbed through the red gloom.

Four silhouettes clad head to boot in featureless matte-black gear stepped effortlessly out of the silent drainage corridor. Seamless, expressionless full masks covered their faces completely, bearing no rank, no department emblem, and no trace of hesitation.

"Burn it!" David Mercer screamed, shifting his aim toward the lead figure. "Su Wenhao, drop the lighter now!"

Su Wenhao flicked his thumb down, igniting a bright tongue of flame half an inch above the kerosene-soaked ledger pages.

Before his wrist could even tilt downward, a matte-black figure crossed the six paces between them like a shadow. A crisp strike clipped the inside of Su Wenhao's elbow, numbing his forearm instantly, while a second gloved hand plucked the lit lighter cleanly out of midair and snapped the lid shut before a single paper corner could char.

In the exact same second, David Mercer pulled his trigger, only for a second masked operative to step inside his arc, slap the slide back out of battery with an open palm, and drive his elbow cleanly into Mercer's collarbone. The pistol clattered harmlessly across the concrete floor. Mercer staggered back against the server rack, gasping for air, before two effortless locks twisted his arms behind his back and forced him onto his knees beside the cold-storage console.

Not a single gunshot had sounded. Not a single drive had been scratched. Not a single neighbor sleeping twelve meters above on Old Street had stirred.

Within ten seconds, two masked operatives were already detaching the unharmed encrypted server blades into cushioned black transport cases while a third sealed the kerosene-dampened paper ledgers inside airtight preservation sleeves, lifting every volume intact without smudging a single stamped seal.

On his knees beside the table, Su Wenhao trembled uncontrollably, tears of sheer terror stinging his eyes as he stared at the blank dark visors surrounding him. "Please," Su Wenhao stammered, his voice breaking completely. "I am only a technical archivist! I only copied the ledgers Chairman Deng Yongchang's people sent down! I did not kill anyone! I will sign a full confession right now!"

Beside him, David Mercer forced his breathing down, his eyes darting desperately from one featureless mask to another, searching for a commander's insignia, a badge, or any human crack he could exploit. Seeing none, he swallowed hard and spoke rapidly in English, then repeated himself urgently in Mandarin.

"Listen to me carefully!" David Mercer said, his voice tight and strained despite his attempt to sound professional. "You do not know who owns these drives! I carry foreign commercial diplomatic credentials registered through three offshore clearing banks! If you hold me off the books, you will trigger an international incident before sunrise! Let me make one call to our regional counsel, and I will hand over every decryption key on the spot in exchange for consular transfer!"

Silence answered him. The dim red emergency bulbs cast flat crimson reflections across the seamless black masks.

David Mercer's composure finally cracked. Sweat ran down his jaw as he looked up into the dead stillness of the operative standing directly in front of him. "Are you Ministry of State Security? Military Intelligence? Tell me who you report to! Name your price, name your department! Every man in this room can walk away rich if we negotiate right now!"

The masked Zero Guard operative looked down without a flicker of curiosity, anger, or impatience.

From behind the featureless black mask came a completely flat, neutral, genderless voice, chilled as dry ice sliding across bare steel.

"Evidence preserved. Subject detained."

David Mercer's mouth stayed open, the next frantic sentence dying unspoken in his throat as cold dread finally crushed every ounce of his training.

In total silence, the masked operatives lifted the sealed server cases and ledger trunks and guided both bound couriers cleanly out through the old drainage junction, leaving Old Street quiet, unharmed, and completely asleep above them.
