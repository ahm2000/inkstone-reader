# Chapter 35: The Tunnel Under Old Street

Twelve meters under the dark stone pavement of Old Street, inside a 1970s air-raid tunnel storage room linked to an empty city drain two blocks away, the main power switch died with a heavy thud.

Above ground, Mingde Hardware stayed calm and untouched. Below, three computer racks dropped into a dying whine as dim red backup bulbs clicked on along the curved concrete ceiling.

Su Wenhao spun from the steel sorting table, eyes wide behind his glasses. "The main power just cut out! The backup generator did not even turn on!"

Across the narrow aisle, David Mercer slammed his palm onto the main desk as the locked screen froze halfway through copying. "Check the cable line to Hongrui Plaza right now, Su Wenhao! Did Deng Yongchang's idiots blow a city fuse again?"

Su Wenhao jabbed at a spare battery laptop, sweat breaking along his hairline. "It is not a fuse! The cable line to Hongrui Plaza is dead. The spare radio link up the drain pipe is dead too. Even our auto-wipe timer froze at sixty seconds! Somebody killed our remote switch from outside!"

David Mercer shoved his chair back, panic flashing across his usually calm face. "We are burned," he snapped in English before switching into smooth, accented Mandarin. "Forget the remote switch! Pull the burn pins on the hard drives and light the twenty-year paper account books right now!"

"Those burn pins will choke all the air out of a closed tunnel!" Su Wenhao yelled, scrambling toward three open steel trunks stacked with bound Shengyuan Capital transfer books and stamped shipping lists. "I will pour lamp oil on the paper books first! You smash the locked hard drives!"

"Do not argue, Su Wenhao, burn them!" David Mercer snarled, wrenching open a drawer to grab a heavy hammer and a small gray pistol. "If state security walks out with those signed paper books and overseas bank drives intact, neither our backers abroad nor Shengyuan Capital will ever pull us out!"

Su Wenhao yanked the cap off a metal oil can with shaking hands and splashed fuel across the top pages. His chest heaved as he fumbled a metal lighter from his pocket. His thumb slipped twice before a yellow spark jumped.

At the far end of the room, the heavy 1970s steel door did not rattle or blow open.

Its rusted iron wheel turned smoothly from outside without a squeak, and the thick door swung quietly inward into the red light.

Both men froze.

"Who is out there?" David Mercer shouted in sharp Mandarin, aiming his pistol at the dark doorway while his left hand hovered near the drive rack. "Stop right there! Stay back or I shoot!"

Nobody answered. No flashlight cut the dim red room.

Four figures dressed head to boot in plain black gear stepped smoothly out of the silent drain tunnel. Smooth, blank full masks hid their faces. They wore no badges, rank strips, or unit marks, and moved without hesitation.

"Burn it!" David Mercer screamed, shifting his aim toward the lead figure. "Su Wenhao, drop the lighter right now!"

Su Wenhao flicked his thumb down. A yellow flame jumped half an inch above the oil-soaked pages.

Before his wrist could tilt, a black-clad figure crossed six paces like a shadow. One crisp chop struck inside Su Wenhao's elbow, turning his forearm numb. A gloved hand plucked the lit lighter cleanly out of midair and snapped the lid shut before a single page could burn.

In the same heartbeat, David Mercer pulled his trigger. A second masked guard stepped inside his reach, jammed the gun back with an open palm, and drove an elbow into Mercer's collarbone. The pistol clattered across the concrete. Mercer staggered against the computer rack, gasping, before two smooth holds twisted his arms behind his back and forced him onto his knees.

Not a gunshot sounded. Not a hard drive was scratched. Not one neighbor sleeping twelve meters above on Old Street turned over in bed.

Within ten seconds, two guards slid the untouched drives into padded black cases. A third sealed the oil-dampened account books inside airtight plastic sleeves without smudging a single red stamp.

On his knees beside the table, Su Wenhao shook from head to toe, tears of fear stinging his eyes. "Please," he stammered. "I am only a file clerk! I only copied the account books Chairman Deng Yongchang's men sent down here! I never hurt anyone! I will sign a full confession right now!"

Beside him, David Mercer forced slower breaths. His eyes darted from one blank mask to the next, hunting for a badge or leader. Finding none, he swallowed hard and spoke fast in English, then urgently in Mandarin.

"Listen carefully!" David Mercer said, voice strained as he tried to sound calm. "You do not know who owns these drives! I carry foreign trade diplomatic protection backed by three overseas banks! Hold me in secret and you start an international crisis before sunrise! Let me call our regional lawyer, and I will give you every password in exchange for a consulate handover!"

Only silence answered him. Red emergency light gleamed flat across the smooth black masks.

David Mercer's calm finally broke. Sweat ran down his jaw as he stared up at the guard in front of him. "Are you State Security? Military Intelligence? Tell me who you work for! Name your price, name your office! Every man in this room can walk away rich if we talk right now!"

The masked Black Guard looked down without curiosity, anger, or hurry.

From behind the plain black mask came a flat, even voice.

"Evidence secured. Subject held."

David Mercer's mouth hung open. His next frantic sentence died in his throat as cold dread crushed his training.

In silence, the masked guards lifted the sealed cases and trunks of account books. They led both bound men out through the old city drain, leaving Old Street quiet and sound asleep above.
