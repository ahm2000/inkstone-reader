# Chapter 35: Gift Envy

Qingyan City, Hengxin Logistics Group, Operations Department. Monday, 9:10 a.m.

On Monday morning, a long, flat, gold-and-red box stood in the middle of Zhang Pengfei's desk, and the whole floor had already circled it twice.

"Colleagues!" Zhang Pengfei stood up, straightened his tie, and spoke in a voice that carried to the back row. "Voting for Employee of the Quarter is at the end of the month, so I won't say a word about that. This is only a small thank-you for working with me. The tea is from my uncle at the Municipal Trade Bureau. He sent it himself."

He opened the lid. Twelve small tins lay inside, each one wrapped in gold paper.

"A box like that costs eight hundred yuan!" a young clerk whispered.

"Eight hundred? That's two weeks of my lunch money!"

"An uncle at the Trade Bureau..."

Sister Pan pushed her glasses up her nose and pulled out the small blue notebook she used for her lunch bets. She called it the Gift Table of the Third Floor.

"Ranking," she announced, "is open!"

Everybody understood what Zhang Pengfei was really doing. Gifts before a vote are called "warmth." Gifts after a vote are called "thanks." To put it nicely, Zhang was a generous colleague. To put it bluntly, he had bought twelve tins' worth of votes!

Lin Dong gave out salted peanuts and got third place. A girl from accounting gave out hand cream and got fourth. When Sister Pan reached the end of the line, she lifted one eyebrow.

"Supervisor Chen?"

Chen Xuan was already walking down the aisle with a canvas bag. From it he took a stack of small clear bags, each holding a handful of pickled radish, still glistening with chili oil.

"My mother made these on Saturday," he said. "She said to tell you they're better on rice, or with congee, or on their own at three in the afternoon."

He put one on each desk, including Auntie Lu's cleaning cart, and Zhang Pengfei's.

Sister Pan wrote in her notebook and read it out loud. "Tea, first. Peanuts, third. Radishes... last. Odds against: ten to one." Her bet was simple: colleagues paid a lunch to guess which gift would rank best at the end of the month, and the radishes were the long shot.

"Ten to one on radishes?" somebody laughed.

"Ten to one!" said Sister Pan, delighted.

Zhang Pengfei smiled, tall and proud, and set his tea box to the front of the pile, label out.

Then a mild voice spoke from beside the filing cabinet.

"That's a nice box."

Lu Qinghe, sixty-one, the cleaning auntie, was wiping the cabinet with a gray cloth, and she did not even look up.

"I saw the same one on the corner of Fuxing Road, in the card shop," she said. "You buy a gift card for two hundred sixty, and they exchange it for the box. The card slip is stuck on the bottom, see? Nice ribbon, though."

Everybody looked at the bottom of the box.

A small white slip was stuck there, stamped *Redeemed with gift card. Tianhe Tea Mart.*

The silence was so complete that Chen Xuan heard the air conditioner click!

Zhang Pengfei's whole face went red, from his collar to his ears. Everyone expected him to shout, or to pretend it was a mistake, or to accuse the cleaner.

Instead, he sat down.

"My uncle really did send it," he said, very quietly. "He sent a card. I traded it. I wanted it to look like more."

Nobody laughed. Sister Pan slowly closed her notebook.

Chen Xuan, who had only just noticed that a contest was going on, raised his thermos.

"If there's a tin left, may I try one? The leaves in my thermos have been working since Friday. They deserve a day off."

Zhang Pengfei looked at him for a moment, then pushed a tin across the desk. "It's good tea," he said stiffly. "That part is true."

"Mm. It smells nice," said Chen Xuan, and it was all he said.

Qingyan City, Hengxin Logistics, Operations floor. Monday, 6:20 p.m.

By six o'clock, the floor was empty except for the hum of the monitors.

Zhang Pengfei was still working, and he was still ashamed. He kept seeing the little white slip. At lunch he had wanted to pick up the tea box and throw it into the bin.

He closed his own report. Out of habit, he opened Chen Xuan's weekly freight sheet, the one due to General Manager Sun on Tuesday morning, because beating that sheet was his private dream.

Then he stopped.

Row 41. A customer code had been copied wrong, one digit off, from an old template. Chen Xuan, the man who never made mistakes, had made one!

Zhang Pengfei felt his heartbeat quicken. If he told Manager Hu tomorrow, in front of everyone, he would gain a point and Chen Xuan would lose one. It would be easy, and legal, and very satisfying.

He looked at the radish bag still sitting beside his keyboard.

"Ah, forget it," he muttered, and he corrected the digit himself. He saved the file and left no note.

Qingyan City, Hengxin Logistics, Operations floor. Tuesday, 8:55 a.m.

Chen Xuan opened his sheet and saw the corrected row 41 at once. He checked the edit history, because he was careful, and saw whose name was on it.

"Hmm," he said, pleased, and put two red-bean buns on the corner of Zhang Pengfei's desk without a word.

When Zhang Pengfei arrived, he stared at the buns for a very long time.

"Supervisor Chen," Hu Dewei said, hurrying over with his flowerpot mug in one hand and the other tugging at his tie. "Quick news. The visitor from Haiyue Hospitality is coming next week. Miss Qin."

"Miss Qin?"

"That's all I know!" Hu Dewei's hand shook so much his tea splashed. "General Manager Sun says the Employee of the Quarter has lunch with her! I mean, with the group! Not that it concerns, I mean, why am I telling you!"

He fled, pale and sweating, though nobody had said anything frightening.

Chen Xuan blinked, then sat down at his desk to look at his chair.

It had been adjusted. The height was exactly right, the back leaned at exactly his angle, and on the seat lay a soft blue heating pad, plugged in under the desk. A sticky note was pressed on top, in the neat, round handwriting from the cleaning schedule.

*Eat.*

Chen Xuan looked across the floor. By the door, a gray cleaning cart stood with a radio on its top shelf, the same radio that never played.

"Funny," he said to himself. "Someone else knows about heating pads."
