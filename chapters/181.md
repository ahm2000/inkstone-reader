# Chapter 181: Morning at Fudan University

The next morning, late November sunlight slanted across Handan Road, turning the fallen ginkgo leaves along the sidewalks into a bright yellow carpet. A crisp autumn breeze stirred the branches as Chen Xuan strolled through the main gate of Fudan University, his dented green metal thermos tucked comfortably under one arm. Beside him, Qin Yuhan wore a soft camel wool coat and a cream scarf, her expression relaxed on a rare Sunday morning away from Haiyue Tower.

Ten steps ahead, Chen Hao and Chen Yuan moved with restless excitement. Chen Hao walked backward with his hands shoved into his jacket pockets, staring up at the wide campus avenue lined with tall plane trees. Chen Yuan held up her phone, snapping photos of the stone gateway and the students cycling past with backpacks slung loosely over their shoulders.

"Walk facing forward, Chen Hao," Chen Xuan called out lazily, unscrewing the lid of his thermos for a slow sip of hot jasmine tea. "If you trip over a curb here, Fudan will not give you bonus points on your college entrance exam."

"Brother Xuan, you have zero romance in your bones," Chen Hao shot back, grinning as he spun around. "This is Fudan! Look at those buildings. Even the air smells smarter here."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft laugh and tucked her arm through Chen Xuan's elbow. "Let him enjoy it," she said gently. "When I first visited a university campus in high school, I walked around for three hours without sitting down once. Come on, let us show them Guanghua Twin Towers and the lawn in front of Xianghui Hall."

They followed the wide brick path toward the heart of the campus. Students sat in small clusters on stone benches reading textbooks or chatting over paper cups of coffee. Near the edge of the lawn, a gray-haired groundskeeper in a faded blue work jacket was raking ginkgo leaves into neat golden mounds. As Chen Xuan walked past, the older man looked up with a crinkled smile.

"Good morning, young folks," the groundskeeper said warmly, leaning on his bamboo rake. "Bringing the kids to see the school? Pick a sunny spot near the hall for photos. The light is best right now."

"Thanks, Uncle," Chen Xuan replied easily, nodding with a genuine smile. "The breeze is cool today. Make sure you keep your collar buttoned up."

"Will do, young man. Have a good walk," the uncle chuckled, returning to his leaves with unhurried sweeps. Qin Yuhan smiled at the brief, friendly exchange; she had always liked how naturally her husband got along with neighborhood elders, street vendors, and campus uncles alike.

They paused in front of Xianghui Hall, its red brick walls and white trim standing clean against the pale November sky. Qin Yuhan was explaining the history of the hall to Chen Yuan when a clear, resonant voice sounded from the shaded walkway beside the lawn.

"Well now, if it is not President Qin of Haiyue Group. I did not expect to see you trading boardrooms for my old campus on a Sunday morning."

Qin Yuhan turned in pleasant surprise. Walking toward them was a silver-haired gentleman in a neat brown tweed jacket, holding a folded newspaper under one arm. His eyes were sharp behind thin wire-rimmed glasses, yet his smile was full of warmth. It was Professor Shen Mingde, a retired economics professor from Fudan who had mentored several senior analysts in Shanghai and advised Haiyue Group on its university logistics research grant two years earlier.

"Professor Shen!" Qin Yuhan greeted him respectfully, stepping forward with a warm smile. "It is wonderful to see you looking so healthy. I brought my husband's younger cousins to tour the campus before their afternoon train."

Professor Shen Mingde chuckled and looked over at Chen Hao and Chen Yuan, who immediately straightened their backs and greeted him politely. "Good, very good," the professor said, nodding with approval. "Looking at a campus early gives a young person a clear direction. Just remember one thing when you choose your major later: study what keeps you curious, not just what sounds fashionable in the newspapers."

Then Professor Shen's gaze shifted back to Qin Yuhan, his tone turning genuinely admiring. "I followed the financial news closely over the past two months, Yuhan. When half the shipping and logistics firms in the Yangtze River Delta were panicking over credit freezes, Haiyue Group did not wobble once. You kept your suppliers paid, your routes steady, and your head cool. That takes real backbone."

Qin Yuhan flushed slightly at the praise. "You are too kind, Professor. I was fortunate to have a steady team, and a very calm home to go back to every evening." She glanced at Chen Xuan and introduced him with quiet pride. "Professor Shen, this is my husband, Chen Xuan."

Professor Shen shook Chen Xuan's hand, noticing the dented green thermos and the young man's unhurried posture. "Ah, so you are the steady anchor behind Shanghai's sharpest young executive," the professor teased kindly. "People in business always talk about strategy and capital, but having someone at home who keeps the lights warm and the mood light is worth more than ten market reports."

Chen Xuan smiled lazily and patted his thermos. "Professor Shen, you are giving me far too much credit. I just clock out at 4:55 PM, pick up fresh fish and greens from the market, and make sure Yuhan does not work through dinner. That is the full extent of my economic contribution."

Professor Shen burst into hearty laughter, and Qin Yuhan lightly nudged Chen Xuan's arm, though her eyes curved with fondness. After chatting for another ten minutes about the university's autumn lectures, they bid the professor farewell. As they walked back toward the south gate for an early lunch, Chen Hao whispered loudly to Chen Yuan that he was definitely going to study harder starting Monday, earning a quiet, satisfied smile from Chen Xuan.
