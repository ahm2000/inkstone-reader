# Chapter 181: Morning at Fudan University

Sunday morning in Shanghai broke clear and crisp, with bright autumn sunlight spilling over the plane trees along Handan Road.

Chen Xuan stepped out of the subway station with Chen Yuan and Chen Hao right beside him, carrying three cups of warm soy milk and a paper bag of pan-fried pork buns.

"Eat while they are hot," Chen Xuan said, handing them over. "Walking a big campus on an empty stomach is no fun."

Chen Hao took a huge bite of a pork bun, waving a hand in front of his mouth as he grinned toward the wide white stone gate ahead. Four bold dark characters stood above the entrance: Fudan University.

"Ge, look at that gate!" Chen Hao exclaimed, his dark eyes shining. "It looks even bigger than the photos on my phone!"

Chen Yuan laughed, dabbing soy milk from her chin. "Keep your voice down, silly. People will think we just wandered out of the mountains for the first time."

"We did come from Yunxi County," Chen Hao shot back cheerfully. "Last night I swore under the Oriental Pearl Tower that I am getting in here next year. Today I am checking out my future home!"

"Checking out your future home?" Chen Yuan raised an eyebrow. "Last month Mom had to chase you around the courtyard just to make you finish your physics paper. Keep this same energy when we get back to Handong Province tonight."

"That was before yesterday!" Chen Hao protested proudly. "Next monthly exam, top three in the grade, guaranteed!"

Chen Xuan chuckled as they walked through the gate onto the broad tree-lined avenue inside Fudan University. Students in hoodies rode past on shared bicycles, chatting about study groups and cafeteria dumplings. Ahead rose the Guanghua Twin Towers, pale stone walls gleaming above the green central lawn. Chen Hao slowed his steps, looking around in quiet wonder.

"It feels different here, Ge," Chen Hao murmured. "Back home, exam scores feel like a heavy stone. Here, people actually enjoy learning."

"Once you climb past the exam wall, the road opens wide," Chen Xuan said warmly, patting his shoulder. "A good school puts you in the same room with people who push you to think bigger."

They strolled past the lawn toward the red-brick halls near Xianghui Hall. Near a wooden bench sat a white-haired man in a gray wool cardigan, a steel thermos beside him and an open notebook across his knees.

Chen Hao stepped politely toward the bench and bowed his head.

"Excuse me, Grandpa," Chen Hao said respectfully. "I am a senior high school student from Handong Province visiting Shanghai. Could I ask where the School of Economics building is? I want a photo outside it for motivation."

The older man looked up from his notebook, his eyes crinkling behind round wire glasses.

"No bother at all, young man," he replied warmly. "Walk past Guanghua Twin Towers and turn left at the second willow tree. Planning to apply to Fudan next year?"

"Yes, Sir!" Chen Hao answered proudly. "I want to study economics. My older brother works in Shanghai, and he promised to treat me to a big meal in Lujiazui the day my admission letter arrives."

The gentleman chuckled, closing his notebook. "A fine goal. My name is Shen Mingde. I taught economics here at Fudan for thirty-six years before retiring last spring."

Chen Yuan's eyes widened. "A retired Fudan professor! Good morning, Professor Shen Mingde! I am Chen Yuan, and this is our older brother, Chen Xuan."

Chen Xuan inclined his head politely. "Good morning, Professor Shen."

"Sit for five minutes," Professor Shen Mingde invited warmly, gesturing to the stone bench across from him. "You said you are from Handong Province? Which county?"

"Yunxi County," Chen Hao answered eagerly as they sat down.

"Yunxi County!" Professor Shen Mingde smiled brightly. "One of the sharpest students I ever taught came to Fudan with the exact same fire in her eyes as your little brother."

"Who was that, Professor?" Chen Yuan asked.

"Qin Yuhan," Professor Shen Mingde said with clear pride. "She graduated from my class ten years ago, staying in the library until closing time every single night. Today she runs Haiyue Group right here in Shanghai."

Chen Xuan kept his face calm and pleasant, listening with a quiet smile.

"Haiyue Group!" Chen Yuan exclaimed softly. "That huge shipping company from the news!"

"Huge, and principled," Professor Shen Mingde agreed, sipping tea from his thermos. "Three weeks ago, Qin Yuhan came back quietly with no reporters. She set up a 20 million yuan Haiyue Student Scholarship Fund at Fudan for hardworking students from inland provinces like Handong Province. She never wants a bright county kid to give up on Shanghai over tuition."

Chen Hao's eyes lit up. "20 million yuan! That is amazing!"

Professor Shen Mingde smiled fondly. "She even called me last night after returning from a tough trip in Shenzhen. She sounded happier than I had heard her in years. She said Haiyue Group nearly hit a terrible storm down south, and a calm, ordinary young clerk from a local partner firm called Hengxin Logistics helped her steady the ship and save their southern route."

Beside Chen Xuan, Chen Yuan froze with her cup halfway to her mouth and turned slowly to stare at him.

Chen Hao blinked twice, jaw dropping. "Wait a minute! Hengxin Logistics? Ge, is that not your company? Did you not just come back from Shenzhen on Friday night?"

Chen Xuan did not flinch. He smiled easily with a light shrug. "Hengxin Logistics has dozens of clerks handling Shenzhen freight every week. I just helped clear a dock delay at Pier Nine. President Qin Yuhan is a generous leader who always gives extra credit to the people at the bottom."

Professor Shen Mingde laughed heartily, eyeing Chen Xuan with warm approval. "A modest young man! Tell you what, Chen Hao. Study hard this winter. If you make it into Fudan next autumn, come find old Shen Mingde on this bench. I will write a recommendation note for Haiyue Group's freshman scholarship myself."

"Thank you, Professor Shen!" Chen Hao jumped up and bowed deeply, beaming. "I will not let you down!"

After saying goodbye to Professor Shen Mingde and taking two photos in front of the School of Economics building and Guanghua Twin Towers, Chen Xuan took his siblings across the city to Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

They walked through the historic red-brick Xuhui campus, pausing beneath the classic arched gate and admiring the ivy-covered Old Library and quiet green courtyards.

"Both schools are incredible, Ge," Chen Hao said softly as they stepped back onto Huashan Road around noon. "Fudan is still my number one dream. Next year, I am coming back with my own luggage."

"And I will be at the station waiting to carry it for you," Chen Xuan promised warmly.

Fifty meters back across the street, beneath a shaded bus shelter, two quiet men in plain matte-black jackets checked the traffic smoothly as Chen Xuan hailed a cab toward Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station.
