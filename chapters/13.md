# Chapter 13: The Midnight Filing

Qingyan City, the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission. Saturday, a little before midnight.

This office was the city's anti-corruption watchdog, and it could investigate anyone in government, no matter how high the title.

That was why every official feared it.

At the night desk, a young clerk sat hunched over his third cup of instant noodles that week, expecting nothing but a quiet shift until sunrise.

Just then, footsteps echoed across the empty marble lobby.

A silver-haired gentleman in a crisp charcoal suit walked up to the counter and set a thick, neatly indexed folder beside the sign-in book, with a plain white business card on top.

"Good evening," Attorney Bai Qingzhou said politely. "This is an urgent pre-inspection complaint about Yunxi County's Old Street rebuilding project and Deputy Police Director Cheng Biao. It holds nineteen pages of checked proof: timed police holding records, clinic logs, witness statements, street video and relocation-fund payments. May I have a stamped receipt?"

"Of course, sir. Let me log it."

The clerk glanced at the card, and his chopsticks froze in the air!

Bai Qingzhou?

Even a junior clerk knew that name! He was a senior partner at one of the province's sharpest law firms, a famous courtroom lawyer who never took a weak case and never filed a page without solid proof.

With unsteady fingers, the clerk flipped open the cover sheet.

Four lines were printed at the top: *Arrest logged at five past two in the afternoon. Injury note from a private clinic at ten to two, fifteen minutes earlier. Eleven shop sales after overnight lockups. One hundred ninety million yuan missing from a six-hundred-forty-million relocation fund.* Under them, in bold: *Provincial Inspection Team arrives Wednesday.*

The clerk's face lost all its color!

Eleven forced sales, a staged fall on video, and a deadline on Wednesday!

In four days, provincial inspectors would be in Yunxi County, and a famous lawyer had just walked in with proof of a framed arrest and a trail of missing money.

If the night desk sat on it until Monday and the province found out first, every leader in this building would lose his post!

"Sir, please wait one moment!" The clerk's voice cracked as he grabbed the red desk phone, misdialed once, and punched the number again.

"Deputy Secretary Lin? It is the night desk! Yes, sir, I know it is nearly midnight, please listen! Attorney Bai Qingzhou is standing in front of me with a nineteen-page urgent complaint on Yunxi County!"

On the other end, Deputy Secretary Lin had picked up half asleep, ready to snap at a nervous rookie.

"Repeat that!" he said, sitting up in bed. "Which county?"

The clerk said it all again, and the moment Lin heard the words "eleven forced arrests," "video of a fake fall," "one hundred ninety million missing" and "Provincial Inspection Team arrives Wednesday," he said nothing at all.

Silence.

It lasted three full seconds, and the clerk could hear his own heart beating in his ears.

"Stamp his receipt politely," Lin's voice came back, tight and wide awake. "Lock the lobby doors behind him, scan every page to my secure tablet, and do not say one word to anyone in Yunxi! I am calling the Commission Secretary right now. And log that I was the one who called it in. Tonight, not next week."

Credit, like blame, moved fastest when everyone could see whose hands had touched it first.

"Take your time with the stamp, young man," Attorney Bai said with a mild smile. "The paper is not going anywhere."

The clerk stamped it with a shaking hand, and the heavy lobby doors locked behind the lawyer.

"Was that really Bai Qingzhou?" the night guard whispered from his stool.

"Do not ask me!" the clerk whispered back. "And do not tell a soul, because eleven families and a Wednesday deadline are riding on this!"

Across Qingyan City, lights came on in bedroom after bedroom.

Minutes after midnight, the Secretary of the Commission read the scanned clinic slip and the video stills. He was one of the ten top leaders running the city, and his face turned as grim as stone.

He dialed the city's police chief, who also served as a Vice Mayor, and the police chief answered on the second ring.

"Chief, I am sending you a file. Read page one."

"Secretary, it is past midnight. Can it not wait until morning?"

"Page one."

The police chief opened it in the dark, and after one line he sat up so fast that he knocked his water glass onto the floorboards!

"Is this real?" he asked.

"The video stills are real. Call Yunxi, and do it now."

Forty minutes down the highway, County Police Director Mai Zhenhua was jerked awake past midnight by his private phone shaking on the nightstand.

"Who is calling at this hour?" his wife mumbled, but he was already reaching for it.

Seeing the city police chief's personal number in the dark, he sat bolt upright and answered in a rush.

"Sir! Is there an emergency?"

"Mai Zhenhua, listen very carefully," the city police chief said, and his voice crackled cold through the speaker. "Your Deputy Director Cheng Biao locked up an Old Street hardware owner named Chen Mingde on Friday, using a private clinic note dated fifteen minutes before the arrest. Eleven other shopkeepers signed away their stores after similar holds. The Commission already has video of the staged fall, and provincial inspectors land on Wednesday! If Chen Mingde is still in your cell at sunrise, do not explain it to me. Explain it to the provincial team! Suspend Cheng Biao on the spot, and do not touch a single other document."

Click!

The line was dead, and Mai Zhenhua sat frozen on the edge of his bed, with cold sweat soaking the back of his pajamas.

"Cheng Biao," he whispered. "You fool." Would the province say he had known all along, and had anyone in the Commission already written his name down?

He could not answer either question!

Moments later, in another quiet house across Yunxi County, the phone beside Party Secretary Fan Weiguo's bed began to ring.

On the screen glowed four terrible words: Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission.

Fan Weiguo had not slept well for a month.

Would he dare to pick it up?
