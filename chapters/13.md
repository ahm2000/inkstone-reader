# Chapter 13: Ashtrays and Cold Smoke

Before dawn broke over the national capital, the mute, masked figures standing inside every major organ of the state received a single silent pulse. They did not leave yet. Instead, matte-black sealed cases were laid simultaneously across conference tables in over forty separate internal war rooms across the capital.

Inside the security and police pillar alone, ten separate internal meetings erupted behind locked steel doors: the Ministry of State Security's Counterintelligence Bureau, Domestic Security Bureau, Technical Reconnaissance Bureau, and Cyber Security Bureau, alongside the Ministry of Public Security's Economic Crime Investigation Bureau, Criminal Investigation Bureau, Anti-Terrorism Bureau, Border Control Administration, Special Police Command, and Armed Police Headquarters.

At the exact same minute, ten anti-corruption, judicial, and inspection bodies opened their own sealed cases behind closed doors: the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, the National Supervisory Commission, the Central Inspection Leading Group, the Case Supervision Office, the Asset Recovery Bureau, the Supreme People's Court, the Supreme People's Procuratorate, the Anti-Graft and Bribery Directorate, the Military Procuratorate, and the Judicial Audit Office.

Beside them, the Central Organization Department, the National Development and Reform Commission, the Ministry of Finance, the People's Bank of China, and the standing secretariats of the National People's Congress and the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference all locked their doors and lit their first cigarettes of a sleepless dawn.

No seal bore a department stamp. Only a single embossed zero.

Inside the windowless basement conference room of the Ministry of State Security, thick blue cigarette smoke already hung in heavy layers beneath the harsh ceiling lights. The crystal ashtray in front of the Minister was overflowing with crushed filters.

He broke the matte-black seal, slid the first three files across the mahogany table to his three deputy directors, and reached automatically for another cigarette. His thumb slipped twice on the lighter wheel before the flame finally caught.

For two full minutes, nobody spoke. Only the scrape of turning pages and the sharp crackle of burning tobacco filled the dead air.

"This cannot be real," the First Deputy Director whispered, his cigarette burning forgotten between his fingers until a long gray ash dropped onto the classified paper. "Operation Iron Lantern in Southeast Asia, four years ago. Three of our deepest undercover officers died in a single night. We blamed a local informant."

"Turn to page six," the Minister said, exhaling a ragged stream of smoke. His voice was hoarse with raw fury and cold fear. "The clearing payment to the foreign handler moved through an offshore trust in Zurich, funded by a shell firm in Qingyan City, backed by an underground lender named Huang Boren in Lanshui Province. The knife didn't come from abroad. It came straight out of our own Third Bureau."

"Who in the Third Bureau?!" the Second Deputy slammed his palm onto the table, rattling the teacups and sending a cloud of ash out of the tray. "Pull every access log! Seal the third floor right now!"

"Read the back annex before you bark!" the Minister snapped, crushing his half-smoked cigarette into the glass tray so hard it broke in half. "The Zero Guard already locked the Third Bureau two hours ago. Six of our own senior analysts were pulled out of their beds before they could touch a keyboard. We sat in this building for six years with a hole in our own roof, and we didn't even know it until a ghost unit we have never had clearance to see dropped the bodies and the proof onto our table!"

Not a single deputy dared to meet the Minister's eyes. Sweat trickled down stiff collars as the air in the basement grew suffocating.

* * *

Two kilometers away, inside the sealed seventh-floor war room of the Central Financial Commission, the air tasted equally bitter with stale coffee and heavy cigarette smoke. Six senior banking regulators and state-audit directors sat frozen around a glowing projection wall.

"Check the decimal column again!" the Chief Auditor barked, ripping off his glasses and stubbing out a cigarette with a hand that visibly shook. "Tell me that is a spreadsheet error! Tell me we are not looking at what I think we are looking at!"

"It is verified across three independent clearing backbones, Director," the lead forensic analyst replied, her voice thin and tight with terror. "Twenty years of hollowed-out sovereign land grants, rigged state-enterprise sell-offs, fake municipal debt vehicles, and cross-border shadow trusts across fourteen provinces. Huaxin Bank's regional clearing desk in Lanshui was only one of nineteen compromised faucets. Total verified capital flight and siphoned public assets exceed one hundred trillion yuan."

A heavy, choking silence dropped over the room.

A veteran vice governor of the central bank slumped back into his leather chair, his face the color of wet ash. He reached into his coat pocket for his nitroglycerin bottle with trembling fingers while his forgotten cigarette smoldered on the lip of the ashtray.

"Over one hundred trillion," he breathed, staring blankly at the ceiling. "Even a sixty-square-meter hardware buyout in a tiny county like Yunxi was feeding into the exact same offshore pipeline. Do you understand what happens if a single rumor of this leaks to the bond markets before noon? A coordinated run on those shadow trusts could shatter domestic liquidity overnight!"

"Nobody leaves this floor," the Chief Auditor ordered hoarsely, lighting a fresh cigarette off the butt of the old one. "Every phone stays in the steel box. If one word slips out before the Vice Premier calls the joint session, every man at this table goes straight to a disciplinary cell."
