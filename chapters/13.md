# Chapter 13: The Holding Cell at Old Street Station

In the second-floor duty office of Old Street Station, Station Director Cheng Biao leaned back in his creaking chair with a tea mug. Beside him, Deputy Zhou Quan refilled his own cup.

"You should have seen Chen Jianguo this afternoon, Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan said with a lazy grin. "He stood at the front counter for twenty minutes twisting his cap, begging to see Chen Mingde. The moment the clerk read out the violent assault charge, the old man went as white as chalk."

Cheng Biao took a slow sip. "Old Street shopkeepers treat a two-room hardware store like a family temple. One night on a cold bench and their pens move very fast."

"The clerk heard Chen Jianguo's son came back from Shanghai tonight," Zhou Quan said, checking his watch. It was twenty past eleven. "Just an ordinary office clerk from the city. I figured he and Chen Jianguo would be knocking downstairs by ten with a cash gift or a signed buyout paper."

"Give them half an hour," Cheng Biao said, tapping cigarette ash into a tin dish. "City clerks always arrive reading rules off their phones. Then nobody in Yunxi County picks up after dark, and their knees go weak. By midnight, Chen Jianguo and Chen Xuan will be waiting downstairs ready to talk terms with Deng Yongchang's people."

Zhou Quan chuckled. "Should we keep them waiting twenty minutes first?"

"Forty," Cheng Biao said smoothly. "A scared family signs faster after their tea goes cold."

Downstairs, the front doors stayed shut. Eleven thirty passed, then eleven forty-five. No footsteps came up the stairs, and the desk phone stayed silent.

Cheng Biao's fingers stopped tapping the armrest. He set his mug down and frowned at the closed door.

"Something feels off," Cheng Biao muttered. "Call the front desk. Ask if anyone from the Chen family came to the side gate."

Zhou Quan's grin disappeared. He picked up the phone, spoke for a few seconds, and hung up with a frown.

"Nothing, Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan said. "Nobody has walked through the gate since eight o'clock. Not Chen Jianguo, not Wu Lanying, and not that clerk son of theirs. Even Yan Tiehu has not called in."

Cheng Biao let out a slow breath. For ten years in Yunxi County, this trick had never failed. Lock up the head of the family before supper, let the relatives panic all evening, and before midnight someone stood at the counter with hunched shoulders and shaking hands. Midnight silence did not feel like surrender. It felt like a hidden trap.

Before Cheng Biao could speak, the private cell phone on his desk buzzed hard against the wood.

The screen flashed: Deng Yongchang.

Cheng Biao sat up fast, smoothing his jacket before answering. "Chairman Deng. You are up late. Do not worry about Chen Mingde. He is quiet in Holding Cell Two, and his family will give in by morning."

The voice that burst from the speaker was so hoarse and frantic that Zhou Quan flinched across the desk.

"Forget Chen Mingde for five seconds, Cheng Biao!" Deng Yongchang snarled, breathing fast and hard. "Did anyone from the city police or the provincial bank office call your station tonight?"

Cheng Biao blinked, his mouth suddenly dry. "No, Chairman Deng. Nothing came in all evening. What happened?"

"Hongrui Real Estate's main company bank accounts just got frozen overnight!" Deng Yongchang shouted, pure panic cracking his voice. "Three different banks locked all our payment accounts within four minutes! My finance director cannot even get a bank vice president to answer his private line! They blamed an automatic dirty-money check from above the province, Cheng Biao. Above the province!"

Cold sweat broke out along Cheng Biao's back. His knuckles turned white around the phone. "A bank check? At eleven on a Friday night? Chairman Deng, could it just be a normal computer check?"

"Normal computer checks do not lock four company bank accounts at the exact same time and block every payment before midnight!" Deng Yongchang barked. "And Yan Tiehu just stumbled into my office holding his wrist! He swears some quiet office clerk on Old Street shut him down in front of half a dozen men who did not even blink! Find out who that clerk is, Cheng Biao! Find out if anyone in Yunxi County has been asking about our demolition files!"

The line clicked dead.

Cheng Biao set the phone down on the desk. Across from him, Zhou Quan stared with wide, uneasy eyes, his hand frozen beside his cup.

"Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan said tightly, "did Chairman Deng say above the province? What does a small hardware shop have to do with company bank accounts?"

"Shut up and think," Cheng Biao snapped, his voice thin. He stood up and paced behind his chair. "An ordinary Shanghai clerk does not shut down Yan Tiehu, and an ordinary clerk does not freeze four bank accounts at midnight."

"Should we let Chen Mingde go?" Zhou Quan asked quickly. "Call it a mix-up over the report?"

Cheng Biao stopped dead, his jaw tight. "And look guilty if it is just a bank coincidence? No. Keep Holding Cell Two locked until Chairman Deng calls back. Keep everyone away from the file cabinet, and tell the front desk to call me the second any strange car turns onto this street."

Zhou Quan swallowed hard and hurried out, all his smugness gone.

Two floors down, inside Holding Cell Two, Chen Mingde sat upright on the hard wooden bench, his rough hands resting on his knees. His face looked tired, yet his eyes stayed steady. He knew he had never laid a finger on Ma Er. What hurt him most was picturing Wu Lanying crying at home and Chen Jianguo walking through wet alleys trying to borrow money they did not have.

Soft footsteps came down the hallway.

The night guard paused outside the iron bars. His coat collar was turned up and his cap was pulled low. After a calm glance down the hall, he slid a steel mug of steaming hot water through the slot and passed a clean, folded gray wool blanket between the bars.

Chen Mingde blinked in surprise. He stood up a little to take the mug as the warmth soaked into his cold fingers. "Thank you, friend. You did not have to go to this trouble."

Number 56 stood straight outside the bars. He kept his voice so low that it barely cleared the iron and could never reach the ceiling mic further down the hall.

"Your wife Wu Lanying is resting at your older brother's home in Fuxing New Village," Number 56 said quietly and calmly. "Chen Jianguo is safe with her. Your nephew Chen Xuan came back to Yunxi County, locked up Mingde Hardware, and sent Yan Tiehu away. Nobody in your family came to this station tonight, and nobody is signing away your shop."

Chen Mingde froze. His fingers tightened around the warm steel mug until his knuckles went pale. For a second he could not breathe, and then deep relief washed over his tired face.

"Chen Xuan is back?" Chen Mingde whispered, his voice shaking. "Wu Lanying is okay? My brother did not go beg them?"

"She is asleep, and Chen Jianguo stayed home," Number 56 said calmly.

Chen Mingde squinted at the shadowed face under the cap, confused yet deeply grateful. "Friend, forgive me for asking, but how do you know my nephew?"

"Everything is already being handled," Number 56 replied evenly. "Drink the water, wrap the blanket around your shoulders, and sleep until morning. You will walk out of here with your name cleared."

Without another word, Number 56 stepped back into the hallway on silent boots and went back to his watch.

Chen Mingde stared for a moment at the empty hall. Then he sat down slowly on the bench, pulled the blanket around his shoulders, and took a warm sip, letting out the first easy breath he had drawn all night.
