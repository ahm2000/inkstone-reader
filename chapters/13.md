# Chapter 13: The Holding Cell at Old Street Station

In the second-floor duty office of Old Street Station, Station Director Cheng Biao leaned back in his creaking chair with a tea mug. Beside him, Deputy Zhou Quan refilled his cup.

"You should have seen Chen Jianguo this afternoon, Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan grinned lazily. "Twenty minutes at the iron gate twisting his cap, begging to see Chen Mingde. The moment the clerk read the violent assault charge, he went white as chalk."

Cheng Biao took a slow sip. "Old Street shopkeepers treat a hardware store like a temple. One night on a cold bench and their pens move fast."

"The clerk heard Chen Jianguo's son came back from Shanghai tonight," Zhou Quan said, checking his watch. Twenty past eleven. "Just an ordinary clerk. I figured they would be knocking downstairs by ten with a cash gift or signed buyout paper."

"Give them half an hour," Cheng Biao said, tapping ash into a tin dish. "City clerks always arrive reading rules off phones. Then nobody in Yunxi picks up after dark, and their knees go weak. By midnight, Chen Jianguo and Chen Xuan will be waiting downstairs ready to talk terms with Deng Yongchang's people."

Zhou Quan chuckled. "Keep them waiting twenty minutes first?"

"Forty," Cheng Biao said smoothly. "A scared family signs faster after their tea goes cold."

Downstairs, the front doors stayed shut. Eleven thirty passed, then eleven forty-five. No footsteps climbed the stairs, and the phone stayed silent.

Cheng Biao set his mug down and frowned at the door.

"Something feels off," he muttered. "Call the front desk. Ask if anyone from the Chen family came to the side gate."

Zhou Quan's grin vanished. He made a brief call and hung up frowning.

"Nothing, Director Cheng Biao," he said. "Nobody came through since eight. Not Chen Jianguo, not Wu Lanying, and not that clerk son. Even Yan Tiehu has not called."

Cheng Biao exhaled slowly. For ten years in Yunxi County, this trick had never failed. Lock up the head of the family before supper, let relatives panic, and before midnight someone stood downstairs with shaking hands. Midnight silence did not feel like surrender. It felt like a trap.

Before he could speak, his private phone buzzed hard.

The screen flashed: Deng Yongchang.

Cheng Biao sat up fast, smoothing his jacket. "Chairman Deng. You are up late. Do not worry about Chen Mingde. He is quiet in Holding Cell Two, and his family will give in by morning."

The voice bursting from the speaker was so frantic that Zhou Quan flinched.

"Forget Chen Mingde for five seconds, Cheng Biao!" Deng Yongchang snarled, breathing hard. "Did anyone from city police or the provincial bank office call tonight?"

Cheng Biao blinked, mouth dry. "No, Chairman Deng. Nothing all evening. What happened?"

"Hongrui Real Estate's main accounts just got frozen overnight!" Deng Yongchang shouted, panic cracking his voice. "Three banks locked all our payment accounts within four minutes! My finance director cannot get one bank vice president to answer his private line! They blamed an automatic dirty-money check from above the province, Cheng Biao. Above the province!"

Cold sweat broke out along Cheng Biao's back, knuckles white on the phone. "At eleven on Friday night? Chairman Deng, could it be a normal computer check?"

"Normal checks do not lock four accounts at once and block every payment before midnight!" Deng Yongchang barked. "And Yan Tiehu just stumbled into my office holding his wrist! He swears some quiet clerk on Old Street shut him down in front of half a dozen men who did not blink! Find out who that clerk is, Cheng Biao! Find out if anyone in Yunxi is asking about our demolition files!"

The line clicked dead.

Cheng Biao set the phone down. Across from him, Zhou Quan stared with wide, uneasy eyes.

"Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan said tightly, "did Chairman Deng say above the province? What does a small hardware shop have to do with company accounts?"

"Shut up and think," Cheng Biao snapped, pacing behind his chair. "An ordinary Shanghai clerk does not shut down Yan Tiehu, and does not freeze four bank accounts at midnight."

"Should we let Chen Mingde go?" Zhou Quan asked quickly. "Call it a mix-up?"

Cheng Biao stopped dead, jaw tight. "And look guilty if it is a coincidence? No. Keep Holding Cell Two locked until Chairman Deng calls back. Keep everyone off the file cabinet, and tell the front desk to call me the second any strange car turns onto this street."

Zhou Quan swallowed hard and hurried out, all smugness gone.

Two floors down, inside Holding Cell Two, Chen Mingde sat upright on the wooden bench, rough hands on his knees. Tired, yet his eyes stayed steady. He knew he had never touched Ma Er. What hurt most was picturing Wu Lanying crying and Chen Jianguo walking wet alleys trying to borrow money.

Soft footsteps came down the hall.

The night guard paused outside the bars, collar up and cap low. After a calm glance down the hall, he slid a steel mug of steaming water through the slot and passed a clean gray wool blanket between the bars.

Chen Mingde blinked in surprise, taking the mug as warmth soaked into his cold fingers. "Thank you, friend. You did not have to go to this trouble."

Number 56 stood straight outside, keeping his voice too low to reach the ceiling mic down the hall.

"Your wife Wu Lanying is resting at your older brother's home in Fuxing New Village," Number 56 said quietly. "Chen Jianguo is safe with her. Your nephew Chen Xuan came back to Yunxi County, locked up Mingde Hardware, and sent Yan Tiehu away. Nobody in your family came to this station tonight, and nobody is signing away your shop."

Chen Mingde froze, gripping the mug until his knuckles paled. For a second he could not breathe, then deep relief washed over his tired face.

"Chen Xuan is back?" Chen Mingde whispered, voice shaking. "Wu Lanying is okay? My brother did not beg them?"

"She is asleep, and Chen Jianguo stayed home," Number 56 said calmly.

Chen Mingde squinted at the shadowed face, confused yet grateful. "Friend, forgive me for asking, how do you know my nephew?"

"Everything is already being handled," Number 56 replied evenly. "Drink the water, wrap the blanket around your shoulders, and sleep until morning. You will walk out with your name cleared."

Without another word, Number 56 stepped back on silent boots and returned to his watch.

Chen Mingde stared at the empty hall, then sat on the bench, pulled the blanket around his shoulders, and took a warm sip, drawing his first easy breath all night.
