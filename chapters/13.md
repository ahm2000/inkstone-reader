# Chapter 13: The Holding Cell at Old Street Station

In the second-floor duty office of Old Street Station, Station Director Cheng Biao leaned back in his creaking chair with a ceramic mug while Deputy Zhou Quan refilled his cup.

"You should have seen Chen Jianguo this afternoon, Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan said with a lazy grin. "Twenty minutes at the front counter twisting his cap, begging to see Chen Mingde. The moment the clerk read out the intentional assault charge, the old man went white as lime."

Cheng Biao took a slow sip. "Old Street shopkeepers act like a two-room hardware store is an ancestral hall. One night on a cold bench and their pens move very fast."

"The clerk heard Chen Jianguo's son came back from Qingyan tonight," Zhou Quan said, checking his watch. Twenty past eleven. "Some ordinary city desk clerk. I figured he and Chen Jianguo would be knocking downstairs by ten with a red envelope or a signed buyout sheet."

"Give them half an hour," Cheng Biao said, tapping ash into a tin dish. "City clerks arrive quoting rules from their phones. Then nobody in Yunxi County answers after dark, and their knees go soft. By midnight, Chen Jianguo and Chen Xuan will be waiting downstairs to talk terms with Deng Yongchang's people."

Zhou Quan chuckled. "Keep them waiting twenty minutes first?"

"Forty," Cheng Biao said smoothly. "A frightened family signs faster after their tea goes cold."

Downstairs, the lobby doors stayed shut. Eleven thirty passed, then eleven forty-five. No footsteps echoed up the stairs, and the intercom stayed silent.

Cheng Biao's fingers stopped tapping the armrest. He set his mug down and frowned at the closed door.

"Something is off," Cheng Biao muttered. "Call the front desk. Ask if anyone from the Chen family came to the side gate."

Zhou Quan's grin vanished. He lifted the receiver, spoke briefly, and hung up with a crease between his brows.

"Nothing, Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan said. "Nobody has walked through the gate since eight o'clock. Not Chen Jianguo, not Wu Lanying, and not that clerk son of theirs. Even Yan Tiehu has not called in."

Cheng Biao exhaled slowly. For ten years in Yunxi County, the script had never failed. Lock up the breadwinner before supper, let the family panic through dusk, and before midnight someone stood at the counter with hunched shoulders and shaking hands. Midnight silence did not look like surrender. It felt like a trap with its teeth hidden.

Before Cheng Biao could speak, the private mobile phone on his desk vibrated violently against the wood.

The screen flashed: Deng Yongchang.

Cheng Biao sat up fast, smoothing his jacket before answering. "Chairman Deng. You are up late. Do not worry about Chen Mingde, he is quiet in Holding Cell Two and his family will fold by morning."

The voice that tore from the speaker was so hoarse and ragged that Zhou Quan flinched across the desk.

"Forget Chen Mingde for five seconds, Cheng Biao!" Deng Yongchang snarled, breathing fast and uneven. "Did anyone from the municipal bureau or the provincial banking office call your station tonight?"

Cheng Biao blinked, his throat drying out. "No, Chairman Deng. Nothing came through all evening. What happened?"

"Hongrui Real Estate's main corporate escrow accounts just got hit with an overnight compliance freeze!" Deng Yongchang shouted, raw panic cracking his voice. "Three separate banks locked our clearing channels within four minutes! My finance director cannot even get a regional vice president to answer a private line! They cited an automated anti-money-laundering audit flag from above the province, Cheng Biao. Above the province!"

Cold sweat prickled along Cheng Biao's spine. His knuckles whitened around the handset. "An audit flag? At eleven on a Friday night? Chairman Deng, could it be a routine system check?"

"Routine maintenance does not freeze four corporate clearing accounts simultaneously and block every outbound transfer before midnight!" Deng Yongchang barked. "And Yan Tiehu just stumbled into my office holding his wrist, swearing some quiet office clerk on Old Street shut him down in front of half a dozen men who did not even blink! Find out who that clerk is, Cheng Biao! Find out if anyone in Yunxi County has been asking about our demolition files!"

The line snapped dead.

Cheng Biao lowered the phone onto the blotter. Across the desk, Zhou Quan stared at him with wide, uneasy eyes, his hand frozen beside his cup.

"Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan said tightly, "did Chairman Deng say above the province? What does a county hardware shop have to do with corporate clearing accounts?"

"Shut up and think," Cheng Biao snapped, his voice thin. He stood and paced behind his chair. "An ordinary Qingyan clerk does not shut down Yan Tiehu, and an ordinary clerk does not freeze four bank accounts at midnight."

"Should we release Chen Mingde?" Zhou Quan asked quickly. "Call it a misunderstanding over the witness statement?"

Cheng Biao stopped dead, jaw clenched. "And look guilty if it is a banking coincidence? No. Keep Holding Cell Two locked until Chairman Deng calls back. Keep everyone away from the file cabinet, and tell the front desk to call me the instant an unfamiliar car turns onto this street."

Zhou Quan swallowed hard and hurried out, his swagger gone.

Two floors below, inside Holding Cell Two, Chen Mingde sat upright on the wooden bench, calloused hands resting on his knees. His face was tired, yet his eyes stayed steady. He knew he had never touched Ma Er. What weighed on him was Wu Lanying crying at home and Chen Jianguo walking wet alleys trying to borrow money they did not have.

Soft footsteps sounded along the corridor.

The night-shift auxiliary guard paused outside the bars, station coat collar turned up and cap pulled low. After a calm glance down the hall, he slid a steel mug of steaming hot water through the slot and passed a clean, folded gray wool blanket between the bars.

Chen Mingde blinked in surprise, rising slightly to take the mug as heat soaked into his stiff fingers. "Thank you, comrade. You did not have to trouble yourself."

Number Fifty-six stood straight outside the grille, pitching his voice so low it barely cleared the bars and could never reach the ceiling microphone down the hall.

"Your wife Wu Lanying is resting at your elder brother's home in Fuxing New Village," Number Fifty-six murmured evenly. "Chen Jianguo is safe with her. Your nephew Chen Xuan has returned to Yunxi County, locked up Mingde Hardware, and sent Yan Tiehu away. Nobody in your family came to this station tonight, and nobody is signing away your store."

Chen Mingde froze, fingers tightening around the warm steel until his knuckles turned pale. For a second he could not breathe, and then quiet relief washed across his weathered face.

"Chen Xuan is back?" Chen Mingde whispered, voice trembling. "Wu Lanying is alright? My brother did not try to beg them?"

"She is asleep, and Chen Jianguo stayed inside," Number Fifty-six said calmly.

Chen Mingde peered at the shadowed face beneath the cap, confusion flickering through his gratitude. "Comrade, forgive me, how do you know my nephew?"

"Everything is already being handled," Number Fifty-six replied evenly. "Drink the water, wrap the blanket around your shoulders, and sleep until morning. You will walk out of here cleanly."

Without another word, Number Fifty-six stepped back into the corridor on silent boots to resume his watch.

Chen Mingde stared for a moment at the empty hall, then sat slowly on the bench, pulled the blanket around his shoulders, and took a warm sip, letting out the first quiet breath he had drawn all night.
