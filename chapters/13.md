# Chapter 13: The Holding Cell at Old Street Station

The duty office on the second floor of Old Street Station smelled of stale jasmine tea, damp wool coats, and the heavy tobacco Station Director Cheng Biao smoked whenever he stayed past nine. Rain tapped steadily against the barred glass overlooking the empty courtyard below, where a single blue sign flickered above the entrance.

Cheng Biao leaned back in his creaking chair, cradling a chipped ceramic mug. Across the cluttered desk, Deputy Zhou Quan poured himself tea from a dented thermos and chuckled.

"You should have seen Chen Jianguo this afternoon, Director Cheng Biao," Zhou Quan said. "Twenty minutes at the front counter with his cap twisted in his hands, begging to see Chen Mingde. When the clerk told him the injury report was already filed under intentional assault, the old man went white as chalk."

Cheng Biao took a slow sip. "Old Street shopkeepers act like a two-room hardware store is an ancestral palace. Give them one night in a cold holding cell and suddenly their pen moves very fast."

"The clerk heard Chen Jianguo's son came back from Qingyan tonight," Zhou Quan said, glancing at his watch. Twenty past eleven. "Works some ordinary desk job in the city. I figured he and Chen Jianguo would be pounding on the front glass by ten with a red envelope or a signed buyout sheet."

"Give them half an hour," Cheng Biao said lazily, tapping ash into a dish. "City clerks always think they know the law until nobody in Yunxi County answers their phone after dark. By midnight, Chen Jianguo and Chen Xuan will be sitting downstairs waiting to talk terms with Deng Yongchang's people."

Downstairs, the lobby doors stayed shut. Eleven thirty passed, then eleven forty-five. No footsteps echoed up the stairwell, and the desk intercom remained silent.

Cheng Biao's fingers stopped tapping the arm of his chair. He set his mug down and frowned toward the window.

"Call the front desk," Cheng Biao told Zhou Quan. "Ask if anyone from the Chen family came by the side gate."

Zhou Quan picked up the handset, spoke briefly in a low voice, and hung up with a puzzled crease between his brows. "Nothing, Director Cheng Biao. Nobody has walked through the gate since eight o'clock. Not Chen Jianguo, not Wu Lanying, and not that clerk son of theirs. Even Yan Tiehu has not called in."

Cheng Biao exhaled slowly through his nose. For ten years in Yunxi County, the script had never failed once. You held the breadwinner before dinner, let the family panic through dusk, and before midnight someone always arrived at the counter with bowed shoulders and trembling hands. Silence at midnight did not feel like surrender. It felt wrong.

Before Cheng Biao could speak again, the private mobile phone on his blotter began to vibrate violently against the wood.

The caller name flashed across the screen: Deng Yongchang.

Cheng Biao straightened in his chair, smoothing his uniform jacket before answering. "Boss Deng Yongchang. You are up late tonight. Do not worry about Chen Mingde, he is sitting quiet in Holding Cell Two and the family will fold by morning."

The voice that tore out of the speaker was so hoarse and ragged that Zhou Quan heard it clearly across the desk.

"Forget Chen Mingde for five seconds, Cheng Biao!" Deng Yongchang snarled, his breathing rapid and uneven. "Did somebody from the municipal bureau or the provincial banking office call your station tonight?"

Cheng Biao blinked, his mug forgotten. "No, Boss Deng Yongchang. Nothing came through the station all evening. What happened?"

"Hongrui Real Estate's main corporate escrow accounts just got hit with an overnight compliance freeze!" Deng Yongchang barked, his voice pitching higher with raw panic. "Three separate banks locked our clearing channels within four minutes of each other! My finance director cannot even get a regional manager to pick up a private line! They cited an automated anti-money-laundering audit flag from above the province, Cheng Biao. Above the province!"

A chill crept down the center of Cheng Biao's spine. The warm tea in his stomach turned sour. "An audit flag? At eleven at night? Maybe it is a routine system maintenance check."

"Routine maintenance does not lock four corporate clearing accounts simultaneously and freeze every outbound transfer on a Friday night!" Deng Yongchang snapped. "And Yan Tiehu just came stumbling into my office claiming some quiet office clerk on Old Street shut him down in front of half a dozen men who did not even blink. Find out who that clerk is, Cheng Biao! Find out if anyone in Yunxi County has been asking about our demolition files!"

The line went dead with a sharp click.

Cheng Biao stared at the dark screen for a long, heavy moment. Across the desk, Zhou Quan's easy smile had vanished completely. Neither man reached for his teacup again.

Two floors down, at the end of a damp pale-green corridor, Holding Cell Two lay under a single caged bulb.

Chen Mingde sat upright on the narrow wooden bench, calloused hands resting on his knees in his oil-smudged navy work jacket. His face was tired, yet his eyes stayed steady. He knew he had never touched Ma Er, and what weighed on him was not the cold bench, but Wu Lanying crying at home and Chen Jianguo pacing the wet streets trying to borrow money they did not have.

Soft footsteps sounded along the hall.

The night-shift auxiliary guard stepped up to the bars, his dark station greatcoat collar turned up and his duty cap pulled low over his eyes. After a calm glance down the empty corridor, he slid a steel mug of steaming hot water through the lower slot and passed a clean, folded gray wool blanket through the bars onto the bench.

Chen Mingde blinked in surprise, rising slightly to take the mug. The heat soaked immediately into his chilled fingers. "Thank you, comrade. You did not have to trouble yourself."

Number Fifty-six stood just outside the iron grille, his posture straight, his voice pitched so low it barely carried beyond the bars and could never register on the ceiling microphone down the hall.

"Your wife Wu Lanying is resting at your elder brother's home in Fuxing New Village," Number Fifty-six murmured evenly. "Chen Jianguo is safe inside with her. Your nephew Chen Xuan has returned to Yunxi County, locked up your shop, and sent Yan Tiehu away. Nobody in your family came to this station tonight, and nobody is going to sign away your store."

Chen Mingde froze, his fingers tightening around the warm steel mug. A quiet wave of relief washed across his weathered face, loosening the tight knot that had sat behind his ribs since two o'clock.

"Chen Xuan is back?" Chen Mingde whispered.

"Everything is already being handled," Number Fifty-six said calmly. "Drink the water, wrap yourself in the blanket, and sleep until morning. You will walk out of here cleanly."

Without waiting for a reply, Number Fifty-six stepped back into the dim corridor, his boots making almost no sound on the wet concrete as he resumed his silent watch over Holding Cell Two. Chen Mingde sat back down on the wooden bench, pulled the clean wool blanket around his shoulders, and took a slow, warm sip of water, letting out the first quiet breath he had drawn all night.
