# Chapter 13: The Cell Door

At three in the morning, Qian Yu's phone call woke the night duty chief, who read nineteen pages in his slippers and was wide awake by page four.

The chief woke Mai Zhenhua, Police Director of Yunxi.

Mai said "Yes" eleven times, sat on the edge of his bed, and could not feel his feet.

At midnight, Gong Yi had already carried a hot bowl of beef noodles with an extra egg and a gray blanket to the family window, like any cousin from the countryside.

Chen Mingde had eaten every strand and cried quietly into the egg.

Two streets away, in a pharmacy, Nurse Qiao "bumped into" Su Min. By dawn, a bed in the spring ward and a free flat on Willow Lane, key under a pot, waited for Wei Kun's family.

At five, Chen Xuan walked to the station alone with his thermos, to wait on the front steps.

His mother would come at 6:35 with soup.

* * *

Before the sky turned gray, Lei Bo slept on the guidance bench with his cap over his face, and he snored in time with the vending machine.

Wei Kun had not slept at all.

He sat behind the glass with a cold cup of tea and one honest notice, waiting for the sky to fall on him.

A plain gray sedan stopped at the steps with no flag and no siren.

The man who climbed out was short and heavy, in a dark jacket, and his hair still stood up on one side from the pillow.

He pushed the glass door open himself.

On his stool in the corner, old Ding looked up, and his stapler stopped.

"Director Mai," he said softly.

It worked like a fire alarm.

Lei Bo fell off the bench and landed in a salute, and Wei Kun and Zhong Hai rose behind the glass as one man.

Mai was also a Deputy County Magistrate, so he signed for every police station in Yunxi.

"Where is Director Cheng?"

"Resting in his office, Director Mai!"

Cheng Biao came out of the back corridor in his shirtsleeves, buttoning up, a toothpick in his teeth and a pillow crease on one cheek.

"Director Mai! So early! If I'd known, I would have waited at the gate!"

"Is the Chen case yours?"

"A small fight, Director. A driver, an old shopkeeper, a few bruises."

Mai took a slip of paper from his pocket.

"The driver's injury note is from Ankang Private Clinic, It's stamped ten to two. Your own log says the arrest was at five past. Tell me, Director Cheng, how does a man get treated fifteen minutes before he falls?"

The hall went so quiet that the vending machine sounded rude.

"Maybe the clocks don't match, Director," Cheng said.

"The county Party Committee and the city Discipline Commission have both decided. You're suspended from every post, starting now. Give me your ID and your keys."

The toothpick dropped out of Cheng's mouth, bounced once, and rolled to the toe of Mai's shoe.

"Lei Bo," he said hoarsely. "Tell the Director who runs this station."

Lei Bo looked at the ceiling, then at his own cap, then at the man he had laughed one second ahead of for two years.

"Director Cheng?" he said, puzzled and sincere. "I hardly know him!"

In the corner, old Ding coughed into his sleeve, his nearest thing to a laugh in six years.

Cheng dropped his keys into Mai's hand like a man handing over his teeth.

Two city officers took him by the elbows and walked him out of the glass doors.

On the steps outside sat a young man in sneakers with a steel thermos on his knees.

"Director," said Chen Xuan, without getting up, and drank some tea. "Remember the hour."

His voice was flat and cold, and Cheng's feet stopped as if the steps had frozen.

The officers pushed him on. "I know your face, clerk," Cheng hissed, and his jaw shook with hatred.

Then the car door shut on Cheng, and the steps were quiet again.

"Wei Kun," said Mai.

Wei Kun stepped out from behind the glass and stood straight, the red mark still faint on his cheek.

"Yes, Director."

"Bring me the key to the holding room."

Wei Kun's knees nearly gave way. He had braced for a different sentence.

* * *

In the holding-room corridor, Chen Mingde sat on the concrete bench with his hands between his knees, folding and unfolding a paper cup.

When the steel door opened, he stood up out of habit, the way he stood for customers.

"Mr. Chen Mingde," said Mai, and he took the old man's right hand in both of his and bowed.

Six officers stood behind him, eyes on the floor.

"We held you wrongly and hurt your name. I'm sorry, to you, to your wife, and to your daughter in Wuhan."

"Director, I'm only a hardware man," Mingde said. "I'm not important."

"You are to me." Mai turned his head slowly, not raising his voice. "I will remember every face in this corridor. If I ever have to apologize to a citizen like this again, you won't like where I do it."

Nobody breathed.

At the corridor mouth, Zhong Hai stood in front of Wei Kun with his hands twitching at his sides.

"Kun," he said. "Before the Director goes. Saturday morning I sent Lei Bo your time. Seven-fifty. They offered sixty thousand for Tiantian's heart."

Wei Kun's round face never hid anything. It went through shock, then old, wet pain.

His right hand closed into a fist.

Zhong Hai shut his eyes and waited.

The fist shook, and then it opened.

"Does Tiantian go in on Monday?"

"Monday," Zhong Hai whispered.

"Then go and sit with him." Wei Kun's voice cracked. "And do not talk to me today."

Zhong Hai bowed so low that his forehead nearly hit his knees, and he walked away.

Mai had heard every word.

He said nothing. From a Director, that is a kind of sentence.

* * *

At 6:35, on the front steps, a shout rang out.

"Brother!"

Chen Jianguo ran down the steps faster than he had run in twenty years, and the two brothers crashed together like falling walls.

Wu Lanying wept silently, both hands over her mouth.

Liu Meifang pushed a steel soup lid into Mingde's hands.

"Drink! It's hot! And if anyone says one word to my brother-in-law, I've got a ladle."

"I thought I'd miss Xiaoqing's graduation," he said.

Lei Bo scurried out with a blanket over his arm.

"Uncle Chen! I always said you were innocent! I told Director Cheng so myself!"

"Yesterday you told us the visiting room had pipe repairs," said Liu Meifang, and she took the blanket and tapped Lei Bo's chest with the ladle, once, not hard. "Thank you. Now go and stand somewhere."

Lei Bo stood somewhere.

"Heaven has eyes," Chen Jianguo said fiercely.

"It was luck," said Mingde, shaking his head in wonder. "A good director, an early car. I did nothing at all."

Chen Xuan put one palm over his face and kept it there for a count of three.

When it came down, he was holding out a bun.

"Eat, Second Uncle," he said. Then, to the tired Director with the pillow-flattened hair, "Morning, Director Mai. You came early. Have one."

Mai looked at the bun, then at the thin young man in sneakers.

A logistics clerk, he thought.

Still, somebody had filed nineteen pages with a receipt at 11:44 last night. Who does that for a hardware shop?

He took the bun in a daze, said, "Thank you, young man," and pocketed it.

His phone buzzed beside the bun.

One look at the screen drained the color from his heavy face.

*General Office, Provincial Department of Public Security.*

"Yes, sir," he said, and turned away from the steps.

"Yes. He is released, sir. Yes, he is with his family right now. Soup, sir. His wife has brought him soup."

Behind him the family laughed and cried at once.

At the glass door Wei Kun stood with his cap in his hands, and Chen Xuan gave him a small nod.

"Thanks for the water, Officer Wei. My uncle told me."

Wei Kun went red to the ears, and found that he was smiling.

Then Chen Xuan sat down again and unscrewed his thermos.

In his sedan, Mai shut the door and typed the young man's name into the police system.

Chen Xuan, twenty-nine, born in Yunxi. Honest, quiet, clean record. Ordinary and boring.

A chill ran down his back. The bun. The timing. A call from the province before breakfast, over a hardware man.

His thumb stopped over the next screen. Then he locked the phone.

A wise man closes a door he cannot see behind.
