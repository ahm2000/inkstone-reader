# Chapter 13: The Cell Door

Qian Yu stamped the receipt at 11:44 and woke the night duty chief. The chief read all nineteen pages in his slippers and was wide awake by page four.

At three in the morning he woke Mai Zhenhua, Police Director of Yunxi, because the folder named one of Mai Zhenhua's own men. Mai Zhenhua said "Yes" eleven times, then sat on his bed and could not feel his feet.

At midnight, the noodle-shop owner Gong Yi had brought beef noodles with an extra egg and a gray blanket to the family window. Chen Mingde had eaten every strand and cried quietly into the egg.

Two streets away, head nurse Qiao Peiling "bumped into" Su Min, Wei Kun's wife. By dawn a free bed waited for her.

At five, Chen Xuan walked to the station alone with his thermos and sat on the front steps. He could not sleep, and the steps were closer to his uncle than his bed. His mother would come at 6:35 with soup.

* * *

Lei Bo slept on the guidance bench with his cap over his face.

Wei Kun had not slept at all. He sat behind the glass with a cold cup of tea and one honest notice, waiting for trouble.

A plain gray sedan stopped at the steps with no flag and no siren.

The man who climbed out was short and heavy, in a dark jacket.

He pushed the glass door open. On his stool in the corner, the old records clerk Ding Shouyi looked up, and his stapler stopped.

Ding Shouyi said softly, "Director Mai."

It worked like a fire alarm.

Lei Bo fell off the bench and landed in a salute.

"Where is Director Cheng?"

Cheng Biao came out of the back corridor in his shirtsleeves. He pulled on his jacket, a toothpick in his teeth.

"Director Mai! So early! If I'd known, I would have waited at the gate!"

"Is the Chen case yours?"

"A small fight, Director. A driver, an old shopkeeper, a few bruises."

Mai Zhenhua took a slip of paper from his pocket.

"The driver's injury note is from Ankang Private Clinic. It's stamped ten to two. The street camera shows he fell at 2:03. Tell me, Director Cheng, how does a man get treated thirteen minutes before he falls?"

The hall went so quiet that the vending machine sounded rude.

Cheng Biao said, "Maybe the clocks don't match, Director."

"The county Party Committee and the city Discipline Commission, the Party's watchdog for officials, have decided. You're suspended from every post, starting now. Give me your ID and your keys."

The toothpick fell out of Cheng Biao's mouth and rolled to the toe of Mai Zhenhua's shoe.

He said hoarsely, "Lei Bo. Tell the Director who runs this station."

Lei Bo looked at the ceiling, then at his own cap.

"Director Cheng?" Lei Bo looked puzzled and sincere. "I hardly know him!"

Cheng Biao dropped his keys into Mai Zhenhua's hand. Two city officers walked him out.

On the steps sat a young man in sneakers with a steel thermos on his knees.

Chen Xuan did not get up. He drank some tea. "Director Cheng. The notice says seven-fifty. The clinic slip says ten to two. Those two papers will be read to you again and again, in a room with no phone, until you can explain them."

He said it mildly, like a man reminding a neighbor to lock his door. Cheng Biao's feet stopped anyway. It was the hour he had ordered Wei Kun not to write.

The officers pushed him on. Cheng Biao hissed, "Your whole family is in Dong's book, clerk. I'm not the one you should worry about."

Mai Zhenhua said, "Wei Kun."

Wei Kun stepped out from behind the glass and stood straight.

"Yes, Director."

"Bring me the key to the holding room."

Wei Kun's knees nearly gave way. He had expected to be punished, not trusted.

* * *

In the holding-room corridor, Chen Mingde sat on the concrete bench, folding and unfolding a paper cup.

When the steel door opened, he stood up out of habit, the way he stood for customers.

"Mr. Chen Mingde." Mai Zhenhua took the old man's hand in both of his and bowed.

"We held you wrongly and hurt your name. I'm sorry to you, your wife and your daughter in Wuhan."

Chen Mingde shook his head. "Director, I'm only a hardware man. I'm not important."

"You are to me." Mai Zhenhua turned to the officers by the wall, not raising his voice. "Every man who worked this corridor since Friday writes a statement by noon, on who gave which order. Whoever lies in one is finished. The next citizen held like this, the officer who signed for him sits on that bench within the hour, and I lock the door myself."

At the corridor mouth, Zhong Hai stood in front of Wei Kun, his hands twitching.

He said, "Kun. Before the Director goes. Yesterday I sent Lei Bo the time you wrote. Seven-fifty. They offered sixty thousand for Tiantian's heart."

Wei Kun's round face hid nothing. It went through shock, and then deep pain. His right hand closed into a fist. Zhong Hai shut his eyes and waited.

The fist shook, and then it opened.

"Does Tiantian go in on Monday?"

"Monday," Zhong Hai whispered.

"Then go and sit with him." Wei Kun's voice cracked. "And do not talk to me today."

Zhong Hai bowed so low that his forehead nearly touched his knees, and he walked away.

Mai Zhenhua had heard every word and said nothing.

* * *

At 6:20 the glass doors opened, and Chen Mingde came out into the morning with a gray blanket on his shoulders.

Chen Xuan stood up so fast that the thermos rolled down the steps.

"Second Uncle?" His voice cracked.

"Brother!" Chen Jianguo shouted from the street. He ran faster than he had in twenty years, and the two brothers held each other hard.

Wu Lanying cried silently, with both hands over her mouth.

Chen Xuan ran too. He pushed his face into his uncle's shoulder and cried like a small boy.

"I'm sorry. I'm sorry, Second Uncle. We were so slow. I sat there and I couldn't do anything."

"Little Xuan." Chen Mingde's big, rough hand held the back of his head. "You came. That was all I needed."

Wu Lanying pulled them all into the hug, and for a while nobody spoke.

Chen Jianguo said fiercely, "Heaven has eyes."

Chen Mingde shook his head. "It was luck. A good director, an early car. I did nothing at all."

Wu Lanying sobbed and held Chen Xuan's wet face. "Little Xuan, you sat out here since five."

"I just wanted to be the first one he saw." He wiped his nose on his sleeve and laughed, shaky and wet."

At 6:35 Liu Meifang came up the street with a steel pot of soup and pushed a lid into Chen Mingde's hands.

"Drink! It's hot! And if anyone says one word to my brother-in-law, I've got a ladle."

She put a second lid into Chen Xuan's hands. "Drink, you boy! You're so thin. Look at those wrists."

He drank. Then, with his mouth full, he saw the tired man beside the steps.

"Morning, Director Mai. You came early. Want a bun?"

Mai Zhenhua looked at the thin young man with red eyes. A logistics clerk, he thought. Still, somebody had filed nineteen pages at 11:44 last night.

He took the bun without thinking and said, "Thank you, young man." He put it in his pocket. His phone buzzed. One look at the screen, and his face went pale.

*General Office, Provincial Department of Public Security.*

He turned away from the steps. "Yes, sir."

"Yes. He is released, sir. Yes, he is with his family right now. Soup, sir. His wife has brought him soup."

At the glass door, Wei Kun stood with his cap in his hands. Chen Xuan gave him a small nod.

"Thank you for the water, Officer Wei. My uncle told me." His voice shook. "It meant a lot."

Wei Kun went red to the ears and found that he was smiling.

In his sedan, Mai Zhenhua typed the young man's name into the police system.

Chen Xuan, twenty-nine, born in Yunxi. Honest, quiet, clean record. Ordinary and boring.

A chill ran down his back. The bun. The timing. A call from the province before breakfast, over a hardware man.

His thumb stopped over a gray button marked Restricted. Then he locked the phone.

A wise man does not open a door when he cannot see what is behind it.
