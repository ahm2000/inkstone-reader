# Chapter 13: Behind Closed Doors

Before dawn broke over the national capital, the mute, masked figures standing inside the ministries received a single silent pulse. They did not leave yet. Instead, inside the Ministry of State Security, the Central Financial Commission, and the Defense Science Bureau, a matte-black sealed case was laid simultaneously on each conference table.

No seal bore a department stamp. Only a single embossed zero.

Inside the windowless basement conference room of the Ministry of State Security, the Minister broke the seal with unsteady fingers and slid the first three files across the table to his three deputy directors.

For two full minutes, nobody spoke. Only the sharp rustle of turning pages broke the dead silence.

"This cannot be real," the First Deputy Director whispered, his face turning the color of chalk. "Operation Iron Lantern in Southeast Asia, four years ago. Three of our deepest undercover officers died in a single night. We blamed a local informant."

"Look at page six," the Minister said, his voice hoarse and shaking with fury. "The clearing payment to the foreign handler moved through an offshore trust in Zurich, funded by a shell firm in Qingyan City, backed by an underground lender named Huang Boren in Lanshui Province. The leak didn't come from abroad. It came out of our own Third Bureau!"

"Who in the Third Bureau?!" the Second Deputy slammed his palm onto the table, rattling the water glasses. "Pull the duty logs! Lock the whole floor down right now!"

"Look at the annex," the Minister snapped back, his eyes red and bloodshot. "The Zero Guard already locked the Third Bureau two hours ago. Six of our own senior analysts were pulled out of their beds before they could touch a keyboard. And we didn't even know we had a hole in our own roof until a silent unit we have never heard of dropped the proof on our table!"

* * *

Two kilometers away, inside the high-security conference room of the Central Financial Commission, six senior banking regulators and state-audit directors sat frozen around a glowing projection wall.

"Check the decimal again!" the Chief Auditor barked, ripping off his reading glasses. "Tell me that is a formatting error!"

"It is not an error, Director," the lead forensic analyst replied, her voice trembling. "Twenty years of hollowed-out sovereign land grants, rigged state-enterprise sell-offs, fake municipal debt vehicles, and cross-border shadow trusts across fourteen provinces. Huaxin Bank's regional clearing desk was only one of nineteen faucets. Total verified capital flight and siphoned public assets exceed one hundred trillion yuan."

A heavy, suffocating gasp echoed around the table. One veteran vice governor of the central bank slumped back into his chair, clutching his chest.

"Over one hundred trillion," he whispered. "Even the smallest county demolition project in Yunxi was feeding into the same offshore pipeline. If this had stayed hidden another five years, a coordinated run on those shadow trusts could have crashed the entire domestic banking system overnight."

At the exact same hour, inside the Defense Procurement and Science Command Hall, three senior generals stared in grim disbelief at high-resolution intercepts of stolen naval propulsion telemetry, missile-guidance parameters, and national power-grid kill-switch schematics that had been queued for three encrypted overseas data drops before sunrise.

"Three hours," one major general muttered through gritted teeth. "If the lockdown hadn't jammed the eastern relays tonight, our entire coastal power grid and our next-generation propulsion data would have been sitting on a foreign desk by breakfast."

* * *

At five in the morning, the Senior Vice Premier convened an emergency cross-department session inside the main dark-wood hall of the State Council compound.

Around the long table sat the Minister of State Security, the Minister of Defense, the Chief Auditor of the Central Financial Commission, the Minister of Public Security, and the Deputy Secretary of the Central Discipline Commission. Behind every door stood silent, matte-black Zero Guard sentries, reminding every man in the room that not a single word was leaving this hall.

"Gentlemen," the Senior Vice Premier began, his face gray with exhaustion and cold dread. "Every one of your departments has now read its own portion of the Zero Guard dossier. Put everything on the table. Hold nothing back."

The Chief Auditor spoke first, his voice shaking as he slid a thick ledger forward. "Vice Premier, on the financial side alone, this is the largest economic crime in modern history. Over one hundred trillion yuan siphoned over two decades through rigged land buyouts, corrupt bank desks like Huaxin Bank, and underground lenders like Huang Boren. Every province has dirty hands in it. If we pull the plug wrong, markets will panic."

"Forget the markets for five seconds!" the Minister of Defense cut in sharply, his jaw tight as iron. "Vice Premier, my bureau found encrypted copies of our naval propulsion tests and missile-guidance calibration logs inside the exact same clearing network. Those files were scheduled to leave the country at four this morning!"

"And that isn't even the worst of it," the Minister of State Security said quietly, his voice dropping so low every head turned toward him. "Vice Premier, stop staring at the hundred trillion yuan. Turn to page twelve of the combined annex."

Papers flipped around the table. One by one, the color drained from every minister's face.

"National power-grid override keys," the Minister of State Security read evenly. "Strategic port-logistics schedules across six coastal hubs. Core agricultural seed-bank genetics. And the real identities of nineteen active overseas intelligence officers, three of whom are already buried because of leaks paid through these exact same underground banks. A coordinated external sabotage trigger was already being staged to hit our ports, our grid, and our banking clearing houses at the exact same time."

Dead silence crushed the room.

The Senior Vice Premier closed his eyes for three long seconds. When he opened them, every trace of political calculation was gone, replaced by raw, chilling clarity.

"One hundred trillion yuan is a massive wound," the Senior Vice Premier said softly, "yet a country of our size could rebuild one hundred trillion yuan in twenty years. What sits on page twelve would have broken the spine of this nation before we even knew we were bleeding. Compared to that, even a hundred trillion yuan is the smallest thing in this room."

He stood up slowly, gathering the matte-black binder with both hands.

"Nobody leaves this building. Cancel every schedule for the week. I am taking this straight up to the Red-Walled Hall right now."
