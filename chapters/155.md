# Chapter 155: Trouble on the Southern Sea

Monday morning arrived at Hengxin Logistics with ringing phones, tapping keyboards, and the warm smell of fresh tea drifting through the third-floor office.

Chen Xuan arrived five minutes before nine, carrying his scratched green thermos and a plain brown folder. He set the thermos beside his monitor and nodded to Wang Lei at the next desk.

"Morning, Xuan," Wang Lei grinned, spinning around with a steamed bun in his hand. "How was Grandma's birthday? Did you bring back any village peanuts?"

"In the top drawer," Chen Xuan said, sliding a small paper bag across the divider. "Share them with the team."

"You are the best," Wang Lei laughed. "Hey, did you see General Manager Sun? He walked in grinning like he won the lottery."

Right on cue, the glass door of the corner office swung open. General Manager Sun Haoran stepped out holding a crisp blue contract binder, his round face beaming. He clapped his hands twice.

"Everyone, pause for two minutes!" Sun Haoran called out cheerfully. "I have big news to kick off the week."

Chairs rolled back as twenty dispatchers and clerks gathered around.

Sun Haoran held up the binder proudly. "Early this morning, Hengxin Logistics signed a major coastal shipping contract with Haiyue Group! We are now their primary mainland partner for the new southern coastal route straight to Shenzhen!"

The office erupted into cheers and applause. Wang Lei whistled loudly, and even the quiet accounting clerks clapped with wide smiles.

"That is huge, Manager Sun!" a senior dispatcher called out. "How big is the first quarter order?"

"We signed for 8 million yuan for the first three months alone," Sun Haoran announced happily. "If we run this route cleanly, we are looking at 30 million yuan over the full year. That means real year-end bonuses for everyone in this room!"

Another round of applause rolled across the floor. Sun Haoran turned toward Chen Xuan and nodded approvingly.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran said warmly. "Your logs on last month's coastal runs had zero errors. Starting today, work directly with Wang Lei and the dispatch team to track the southern route schedules and harbor papers for Haiyue's ships."

"Understood, Manager Sun," Chen Xuan replied calmly. "I will keep the logs updated."

"Good! Now let us show Haiyue Group what Hengxin can do!"

Everyone scattered back to their desks in high spirits. Sun Haoran walked back toward his office, still smiling, when his desk phone rang sharply. A second later, his cell phone began buzzing in his pocket.

Sun Haoran picked up the landline. "Sun Haoran speaking. Yes, Director Lin from Haiyue! Congratulations to you too, we are ready to track the first vessel right now."

Through the glass wall, Chen Xuan watched the bright smile vanish completely from Sun Haoran's face.

"What?" Sun Haoran's voice rose sharply, cutting through the office chatter. "Held at the outer dock? Wait, why would Shenzhen Port refuse to let the ship berth? Every customs form was stamped before departure!"

The cheerful buzz in the office died instantly. Clerks stopped typing and exchanged uneasy glances. Wang Lei lowered his teacup, staring through the glass.

Sun Haoran listened for another minute, his knuckles turning white around the receiver. Sweat broke out on his forehead. "Missing harbor papers and broken cranes? Director Lin, that freighter is carrying 15 million yuan worth of factory machinery and winter parts! If it sits out in the bay paying holding fees, we will both lose our shirts! Keep me updated every hour."

Sun Haoran hung up slowly and walked back out into the main room. Every drop of color had drained from his face. He pulled a cigarette from his pocket, remembered the office rule, and just rolled it nervously between his fingers.

"Bad news, everyone," Sun Haoran said heavily, leaning against an empty desk. "Haiyue Group's first loaded freighter reached Shenzhen Port at dawn, and the local dock office froze it at Berth Seven. They claim the harbor papers are incomplete and two cranes broke down overnight."

"Broken cranes?" Wang Lei frowned. "Shenzhen Port has twelve heavy cranes on that pier. Somebody is blocking the gate on purpose."

An older dispatcher named Old Zhou sighed and rubbed his neck. "I ran freight to Shenzhen ten years ago. Half the private docks at Shenzhen Port are ruled by the local Shen Family."

Sun Haoran's eyes tightened. "Shen Jingzhou's clan?"

"Exactly," Old Zhou said grimly. "On paper, they run normal dock companies. In reality, they treat half of Shenzhen Port like their private yard. Any outside company that opens a direct line without bowing to Shen Jingzhou and paying private fees gets hit with missing papers and stalled cranes until their clients walk away."

"This is crazy," Sun Haoran muttered, pacing to the window. "If that ship stays trapped past Thursday, Haiyue's factory buyers will cancel the route, and Hengxin will owe 2 million yuan in delay penalties."

"What is Haiyue Group doing?" Wang Lei asked anxiously.

"Their logistics head is already booking flights south," Sun Haoran said, rubbing his temples. "Even Qin Zhengguo's side is taking this seriously. I will call the Shanghai port bureau to see if anyone has a contact in Shenzhen, though a local phone call will not move the Shen Family."

Sun Haoran hurried back into his office and shut the glass door. Around the room, the earlier excitement had turned into tense whispers.

Chen Xuan sat quietly at his desk, unscrewing his green thermos and taking a slow sip of hot jasmine tea.

The Shen Family. Shenzhen Port.

Two days ago, Liu Guoliang had bragged across Grandma's birthday table about drinking tea with the Shen Family's port managers. Yesterday afternoon, Qin Yuhan had mentioned her uncle's worry about the southern docks.

Right on cue, Chen Xuan's phone vibrated softly on his desk. He read the short message from Qin Yuhan: *Haiyue's first freighter is being held at Shenzhen Port. My uncle is flying down tomorrow morning to deal with the Shen Family. I may have to go with him.*

Chen Xuan looked at the screen for two quiet seconds, then typed a simple reply: *Let me know if you need a hand.*

He set the phone face down beside his thermos and calmly opened the shipping manifest for Haiyue's stranded freighter.

Eight hundred miles south in Shenzhen, fine winter rain tapped against the tall windows of a private waterfront tea room overlooking Shenzhen Port.

Inside, the air smelled of aged dark tea and faint cigar smoke. Through the rain-streaked glass, the massive blue hull of Haiyue Group's freighter sat motionless out near Berth Seven under silent cranes.

A lean man in his late forties wearing a dark silk vest poured amber tea into a tiny clay cup. Shen Jingzhou had sharp, narrow eyes and a cold smile that never reached them.

Across from him stood a port manager in a damp rain jacket. "Sir, Haiyue Group's managers have called the harbor office four times this morning. They say their papers are completely legal."

Shen Jingzhou picked up his tiny clay cup and took a slow sip.

"Let them call ten more times," Shen Jingzhou said softly, gazing down at the trapped ship through the rain. "Keep the cranes turned off and tell them the harbor clerk is sick until Friday. Outsiders from the north think a clean contract lets them sail into my waters. By the end of the week, Haiyue Group will learn who really owns the gates of Shenzhen Port."
