# Chapter 155: Trouble on the Southern Sea

Monday morning arrived on the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics with ringing desk phones, steady keyboard clatter, and the comforting aroma of freshly steeped jasmine tea drifting between the cubicles.

Chen Xuan walked through the glass doors at 8:55 AM, carrying his dented fifteen yuan green metal thermos in one hand and a neat cloth bag in the other. Outside the wide office windows, a pale late autumn sun shone over the Pudong skyline.

"Morning, Xuan!" Wang Lei grinned, spinning around in his swivel chair with a half eaten steamed bun in his hand. "How was the autumn harvest out in Hongshi Village? Tell me you did not come back empty handed."

"Check your desk," Chen Xuan smiled, setting a paper sack of roasted village peanuts and six bright orange persimmons across the low divider. "Share them with the row before they get too soft."

"You are a lifesaver!" Wang Lei laughed, grabbing a plump persimmon immediately. "Hey, did you notice General Manager Sun when you walked in? He came through the doors ten minutes ago grinning like he just won the city lottery."

Right on cue, the glass door of the corner office swung wide open. General Manager Sun Haoran stepped out wearing his sharpest navy suit, clapping his hands twice to catch everyone's attention.

"Everyone, pause your keyboards for two minutes!" Sun Haoran called out, his voice booming with pride. "I have huge news to start our week."

Chairs swiveled across the twelfth floor as twenty logistics clerks leaned in curiously.

"Earlier this morning, we officially finalized an 8 million yuan coastal transport subcontract with Haiyue Group!" Sun Haoran announced, holding up a thick blue contract folder. "Haiyue has a high value shipment of precision industrial equipment arriving along the southern maritime route, and they picked Hengxin Logistics to handle the inland transfer and warehouse staging here in Shanghai."

A wave of genuine cheers and applause broke out across the office. An 8 million yuan contract with a giant like Haiyue Group was the biggest deal Hengxin had landed all year.

"If we execute this first run cleanly without a single hitch," Sun Haoran beamed, "Haiyue's operations team has already hinted at a full year framework agreement. That means fat year end bonuses for every single desk on this floor!"

Wang Lei whistled softly under his breath, nudging Chen Xuan's shoulder. "Did you hear that? Year end bonuses! Maybe I can finally put a down payment on a new car."

Chen Xuan took a quiet sip from his green metal thermos and smiled warmly. He knew Haiyue's operations team ran strict vendor reviews, so Hengxin had earned the subcontract on honest reliability. It felt good seeing his coworkers celebrate.

Yet the cheerful buzz lasted less than twenty minutes.

Inside the glass corner office, Sun Haoran's landline rang sharply. He picked it up with a wide grin, only for the smile to vanish from his face within ten seconds. Through the glass wall, the entire twelfth floor watched his complexion turn pale as he gripped the receiver tighter, his other hand trembling slightly over his desk blotter.

Moments later, two dispatch monitors near the center aisle flashed with red delay alerts.

Sun Haoran hurried out of his office without even grabbing his suit jacket, sweat beading across his forehead. "Wang Lei! Pull up the southern vessel tracking log right now! Something just went terribly wrong!"

The laughter across the office died instantly. Clerks gathered around the central dispatch screen as Wang Lei tapped rapidly at his keyboard.

"Manager Sun, what happened?" a senior dispatcher asked anxiously. "Did the vessel hit bad weather?"

"Worse than weather!" Sun Haoran snapped, his voice tight with panic. "Haiyue Group's maritime director just called me directly. Their chartered cargo ship, carrying over 120 million yuan in precision machinery, was stopped and locked down at Shenzhen Pier Nine an hour ago!"

"Locked down?" Wang Lei stared at the red alert blinking on the screen. "All the customs papers were cleared before departure! On what grounds?"

"No real grounds at all!" Sun Haoran paced three tight steps, rubbing his temple. "Pier Nine is run by the Shen Family down in Shenzhen. Their berth manager slapped an arbitrary port security inspection hold on the vessel, froze the cranes, and told Haiyue's captain the ship is not moving an inch unless Haiyue wires a 15 million yuan priority clearance fee by tonight!"

Gasps echoed around the twelfth floor. Everyone in logistics knew how brutal maritime deadlines were.

"15 million yuan for nothing?" a clerk whispered in shock. "That is daylight robbery!"

"If that cargo misses tomorrow's factory delivery window," Sun Haoran groaned, slumping weakly against a desk edge, "Haiyue Group faces massive breach of contract penalties from their overseas buyers. And if the shipment collapses, our 8 million yuan subcontract gets wiped out before a single truck even rolls!"

As Sun Haoran scrambled back toward his office frantically dialing Haiyue's dispatch hotline, Chen Xuan remained seated calmly at his desk.

Inside his trouser pocket, his single smartphone buzzed gently.

Chen Xuan pulled it out and read a brief message from Qin Yuhan: "Emergency issue at our southern port. Calling an urgent board meeting on the 38th floor of Haiyue Tower now. Do not wait up for dinner if I run late."

Chen Xuan set his dented green thermos down beside his keyboard. For a fleeting second, the easygoing warmth left his eyes, replaced by a quiet, bone deep chill.

First, he replied gently to his wife: "Do not stress too much, and make sure you eat lunch on time. Everything will work out."

Then he opened another thread on the exact same phone and typed a single calm line to Number 1.

"Send me the full file on the Shen Family and Shenzhen Pier Nine. Let us see how big their appetite really is."
