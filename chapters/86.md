# Chapter 86: The Unrecorded Numbers

Late afternoon light pressed dull grey against the frosted transom windows of the central office's basement briefing room in Beijing. Cool air hummed softly from the ceiling vents, carrying the faint smell of paper ink and cold steel. Along the long conference table sat sealed cardboard boxes, stacks of printed charts, and unopened bottles of mineral water.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua sat at the head of the table with his collar unbuttoned and his sleeves rolled to his forearms. Across from him sat Gao Zhengyi, Deputy Secretary of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, his sharp eyes fixed on a thin grey folder resting beside his elbow.

"Read the bottom line again," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, his voice hoarse from twelve hours of emergency briefings. "Skip the lab jargon."

At the far end of the table, Director Tang Weiguo of the Special Technical Analysis Division adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses and flipped open a blue ledger.

"We finished auditing every digital trace from the past seventy-two hours across Beijing and Handong Province," Tang Weiguo said evenly. "The telecom switching logs, the interbank clearinghouse relays, the satellite routing buffers, and the municipal traffic nodes all show the exact same pattern. An external group operated across four ministries and two provincial networks, completed its evidence handovers, and powered down cleanly without leaving a single active backdoor."

Gao Zhengyi tapped the grey folder with two fingers. "Powered down, or went into hiding?"

"Powered down cleanly," Tang Weiguo replied. He slid a printed chart down the polished table. "A hostile actor leaves persistence scripts, siphons data, or probes defense servers. We found none of that. Every civil clearinghouse kept running at full speed. Not one yuan was diverted from any public treasury. Instead, the unit locked down twelve offshore shell accounts tied to Shanshui Group, routed verified transaction histories straight to our investigators, and restored every local network to its baseline state before disconnecting."

Zhou Zhenhua leaned back in his chair, rubbing the bridge of his nose. "And the authorization keys?"

"Completely unrecorded," Tang Weiguo said. "We cross-checked them against the Central Military Commission registries, the Ministry of State Security archives, and the Public Security encrypted directories. Zero matches. Not even an expired legacy prefix from twenty years ago. Whoever built that protocol designed it to sit outside every standard registry we maintain."

Silence settled over the basement room for a few seconds, broken only by the steady hum of the air vents.

Gao Zhengyi opened his folder and laid three thick case summaries side by side. "Look at the legal side of the ledger, Vice Premier. Every detainee brought in over the past three days is backed by hard, admissible evidence. Ministry Division Chief Zhao Dehan's cash ledgers match the property deeds down to the serial numbers. Zhao Ruilong's offshore wire transfers through Shanshui Group match the missing bank loans from Jingzhou. Even former Deputy Provincial Secretary Gao Yuliang's private approvals for illegal land seizures are documented with original signatures and timestamped bank vouchers."

"No fabricated files?" Zhou Zhenhua asked sharply.

"None," Gao Zhengyi answered without hesitation. "Our forensic accountants verified every receipt against the Four National Banks. If we had spent five years running undercover audits in Handong, we would have hoped to collect half of what is sitting on this table right now."

Zhou Zhenhua looked from the clean technical charts to the heavy case binders. His expression shifted from fatigue to clear, practical resolve.

"Then stop treating this like a mystery novel," Zhou Zhenhua said firmly. "Look at the facts in front of us. A foreign intelligence service would never hand us airtight proof to clean up our own provincial corruption while protecting our banking clearinghouses. A rogue domestic faction would never hand power back to the courts and walk away with zero demands. The only logical conclusion is that we encountered an unrecorded emergency reserve, an old contingency built to step in when systemic corruption reaches a breaking point and then withdraw once lawful authorities take over."

Tang Weiguo nodded slowly. "That matches the technical profile completely, Vice Premier. Their shutdown sequence was orderly and final. If we keep running deep-packet sweeps across regional servers looking for them, we will burn through three months of engineering bandwidth and find nothing but empty relay logs."

"We do not have three months to chase ghosts," Zhou Zhenhua said, sitting forward and resting both palms on the table. "Right now, Handong Province has billions in stalled municipal projects, unpaid contractor invoices, and county governments whose credibility has been shredded by Qi Tongwei and Zhao Ruilong's network. Every hour your division wastes trying to reverse-engineer an unrecorded ally is an hour stolen from tracing the 1.2 billion yuan still missing from Handong's land-compensation funds."

Gao Zhengyi smiled faintly, closing his folder. "That is the clearest order I have heard all week. My inspectors need Tang Weiguo's data team to map the remaining offshore accounts before Shanshui Group's overseas lawyers try to move the balance."

"Then make the transfer tonight," Zhou Zhenhua instructed. He picked up a black fountain pen and drew a crisp line across the top of the technical inquiry memo. "Director Tang, seal your telemetry audit into the closed central archive. Suspend all active tracking of the unrecorded unit immediately. Starting at 8:00 AM tomorrow, reassign your entire analytical desk to support Deputy Secretary Gao's financial recovery group and the Handong Special Task Force."

"Understood, Vice Premier," Tang Weiguo said, gathering his charts into his leather folio with visible relief. "We will have the offshore banking maps ready for the Jingzhou prosecutors before noon."

"Good," Zhou Zhenhua said. He unscrewed a bottle of mineral water, took a slow drink, and pulled the thick Handong economic stabilization file toward the center of his desk. "Let the courts try the guilty, let the banks recover the public funds, and let the people of Handong get back to work. That is the only scoreboard that matters."

Outside the frosted basement windows, autumn twilight settled cool and quiet over Beijing as the lights in the briefing room burned steadily on.
