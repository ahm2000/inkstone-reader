# Chapter 86: The Unrecorded Numbers

Late afternoon light pressed dull grey against the frosted transom windows of the central office's basement briefing room in Beijing. Cool air hummed softly from the ceiling vents, carrying the faint smell of paper ink and cold steel. Along the long conference table sat sealed cardboard boxes, stacks of printed charts, and unopened bottles of mineral water.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua sat at the head of the table with his collar unbuttoned and his sleeves rolled to his forearms. Across from him sat Gao Zhengyi, Deputy Secretary of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, his sharp eyes fixed on a thin grey folder resting beside his elbow.

"Read the bottom line again," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, his voice hoarse from twelve hours of emergency briefings. "Skip the lab jargon."

At the far end of the table, Director Tang Weiguo of the Special Technical Intelligence Desk opened the grey folder. Fine beads of sweat shone along his hairline despite the cool room.

"We finished running every test on all forty-two black evidence cases," Tang Weiguo said. "Those cases hold the full bank ledgers, land contracts, and bribe logs for Handong Deputy Provincial Secretary Gao Yuliang, Shanshui Group head Zhao Ruilong, former Ministry Division Chief Zhao Dehan, and former Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen. We ran paper forensics, ink dating, metal scans, radio sweeps, street camera pulls, and orbital satellite passes. Every single check came back blank."

Gao Zhengyi tapped the tip of his fountain pen once against the walnut table. "Blank means nothing?"

"Worse than nothing, Deputy Secretary Gao," Tang Weiguo replied, sliding three glossy charts across the table. "The paper stock has no mill watermark. The steel latches on the forty-two cases carry zero factory batch codes. Not one fingerprint, fiber, or hair was left inside or outside any case."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua leaned forward, studying the charts. "What about the couriers who dropped forty-two heavy cases across Beijing and Jingzhou City in a single night? There are ten thousand traffic cameras between here and Handong Province."

Tang Weiguo swallowed hard. "That is the part my senior analysts could not explain. Across all forty-two delivery points, every municipal camera, building security lens, and tollgate scanner within a short distance recorded clean, unbroken footage of empty streets. There was no static, no lost timestamp, and no sign of digital tampering. It is as if the lenses simply never saw anyone walk past."

Silence settled heavily over the basement room. Even the hum of the air vents sounded louder.

Gao Zhengyi picked up the second chart and turned it toward the Vice Premier. "Compare this against our file on the White Guard."

Tang Weiguo opened a second, thicker blue binder. "We have tracked the White Guard's outline for years, even though they operate deep in the shadows. We know they use numbered callsigns like Number 1, Number 7, and Number 11. We know they maintain registered shell firms, leased safehouses in cities like Jingzhou City, and encrypted satellite handshakes. They are disciplined and dangerous, yet they still exist inside the real world. They leave footprints."

"And the Black Guard?" Zhou Zhenhua asked softly.

"Zero personnel files," Tang Weiguo said. "Zero serial numbers on their rifles or visors. Zero bank accounts, zero supply trucks, zero radio traffic, and zero camera traces. Their members do not even wear numbers. They appear at a quiet distance, enforce the Black Order, and vanish without disturbing the dust."

Gao Zhengyi set his pen down squarely on the grey folder. "Tell the Vice Premier what happened at the White Guard's Jingzhou City safehouse last night."

Tang Weiguo glanced down at his notes. "Our passive listening post outside Jingzhou City caught one brief drop in White Guard encrypted chatter right after the Black Order was confirmed. Two plain-black figures walked straight into a triple-locked White Guard command post. The numbered operatives inside did not draw a weapon. They did not send a distress ping. They froze in their chairs for sixteen hours until the stand-down order lifted."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua exhaled slowly and leaned back in his chair. For the first time all afternoon, the exhaustion on his face gave way to clear, sober understanding.

"For years, half the ministries in Beijing thought the numbered White Guard was the sharpest blade hidden under the table," Zhou Zhenhua murmured. "We were looking at the outer fence and mistaking it for the fortress."

"The White Guard is only the visible shield," Gao Zhengyi agreed grimly. "The unnumbered Black Guard answers directly to the holder of the Black Order. No wonder Elder Song did not even raise his voice when the crisis phone rang."

Tang Weiguo hesitated, holding his pen above his notepad. "Vice Premier, Deputy Secretary, should my technical desk keep the satellite archive open? If we cross-check infrared passes over Yunxi County from the past six months, we might find a supply pattern."

"Stop right there," Gao Zhengyi snapped, his voice sharp as a blade. "Close the archive. Purge every working draft from your servers before you leave this floor."

Tang Weiguo froze. "Deputy Secretary?"

"You heard him," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said firmly. "No agency in Beijing is permitted to trace the Black Guard, scan Yunxi County, or open a file on the untouchable household there. That order comes straight from the top council. If any analyst gets curious and runs a quiet search on his own, he will be sitting in a remote border post before sunrise."

"Understood, Vice Premier," Tang Weiguo said at once, snapping both binders shut with trembling fingers. "Every draft will be destroyed within ten minutes."

Gao Zhengyi stood up and tucked the thin grey folder under his arm. "Our job is not to peer behind a curtain the founding elders nailed shut. Our job is to use the forty-two evidence cases sitting on this table to rip the rot out of Handong Province and every ministry that took Shanshui Group money."

Zhou Zhenhua rose beside him, buttoning his collar straight. "Come on. Premier Gu Chengshan has called the full council to the Great Hall for tonight's vote. Let us finish our own work before the sky gets any darker."
