# Chapter 86: The Unrecorded Numbers

Late Monday afternoon hung grey behind the frosted glass of the central secretariat's basement briefing room. Thick cigarette smoke drifted beneath the ceiling lamps, and two ashtrays sat heaped with crushed filters on the conference table.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua sat at the head with his collar unbuttoned. Beside him, CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi tapped an unlit cigarette against his saucer, staring at the slim grey folder in front of Director Tang Weiguo. Across from them sat Han Zhiyuan of State Security, arms folded tight, his face pale and rigid.

"Read the bottom line again," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, his voice hoarse from twelve hours of briefings. "Skip the technical padding."

Director Tang Weiguo swallowed hard. He had headed the national cyber and signals forensics bureau for fourteen years, yet his fingers trembled visibly as he lifted the top page.

"We finished the full audit of all forty-two matte-black cases at four-fifteen, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua," Tang Weiguo said, cold sweat glistening on his forehead. "Physical forensics, metallurgy, optical spectroscopy, electromagnetic residue, and orbital telemetry. Every channel came back blank."

Gao Zhengyi stopped tapping his cigarette. "Blank means your team missed something, Tang Weiguo. Try again."

"No, Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi, I mean mathematically blank." Tang Weiguo's voice cracked before he cleared his throat sharply. "Zero latent fingerprints on any outer shell, latch, or foam insert. Not wiped prints. Zero skin oils, zero microscopic fibers, zero dust particulates matching any known city."

Han Zhiyuan leaned forward, his jaw locking. "Every polymer mold leaves an injection mark. Every hinge pin comes from a mill."

"We sliced a corner off Case Nineteen under laser microscopy," Tang Weiguo replied, flipping to page two with a stiff thumb. "The carbon-composite weave matches no military contractor, aerospace plant, or state factory on our registry. Zero serial numbers. Zero batch stamps. The solid-state drives run custom firmware with zero vendor strings. Even the printed paper shows no watermark and no pulp signature from any domestic or imported mill."

Silence dropped over the table. Gao Zhengyi struck a match, bracing his wrist against the wood to keep it steady, and lit his cigarette. He blew a thin stream of smoke upward.

"You are telling me," Gao Zhengyi said, eyes narrowing, "that someone built forty-two untraceable containers, packed them with twenty years of bank ledgers and treason agreements, and delivered them across six cities and the capital without leaving a single thread?"

"It gets worse," Tang Weiguo whispered.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua lit a fresh cigarette from the glowing tip of his last one. "Keep talking, Tang Weiguo."

Tang Weiguo laid a glossy satellite printout beside the ashtray. It showed an overhead optical and thermal frame of the military auxiliary airfield outside Lanshui, timestamped 00:15. The runway was empty.

"We pulled every RF log, cell tower handshake, encrypted burst relay, and aviation transponder between midnight and four," Tang Weiguo said, knuckles white on the table edge. "Zero pings. Zero spoofed towers. Zero unregistered vehicles on highway toll cameras near any drop site. So we checked orbital reconnaissance."

Han Zhiyuan stared at the printout, his pupils shrinking. "That is the Lanshui north strip."

"Yes," Tang Weiguo said. "We checked four more airfields, including two outside the capital and the coastal strip near Yunxi. At 00:15, national overhead satellites recorded empty runways across all of them. Clean tarmac. Zero jet heat plumes. Zero rotor wash on infrared. Zero radar returns."

"Impossible!" Han Zhiyuan slapped his palm flat on the folder. "Forty-two heavy cases do not walk two thousand kilometers! Somebody spliced the ground archive!"

"We already checked!" Tang Weiguo's composure cracked, his voice rising before he choked it down to a ragged whisper. "I brought three cryptographic auditors from orbital command. The hash chains from the satellites to the ground receivers are unbroken. Not a single frame was altered. Either nothing landed on those runways, or whatever crossed our airspace does not register on our optics, our thermal sensors, or our radar."

No one spoke. The ventilation fan hummed low overhead.

Gao Zhengyi watched ash build on his cigarette until it broke onto his trousers. He did not brush it off. "And the sentries outside our gates right now?"

Han Zhiyuan answered without looking up from the empty runway photo. "We ran gait analysis and thermal imaging on the matte-black figures at the secretariat and Western Hills. Every lens returns flat noise. Their suits scatter thermal contrast to match ambient stone. When my deputy stood two meters from one and demanded a unit code, the figure did not blink, shift weight, or emit a single radio carrier."

"Unrecorded numbers," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua murmured.

All three men looked at him.

"That is what General Qin Zhengguo called them this morning," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, his eyes dark. He exhaled smoke slowly. "Not a secret army. Not a rogue faction. A unit absent from every payroll, military roster, and state security registry. And your audit just proved they sit completely beyond state tracking."

"Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua," Gao Zhengyi said carefully, keeping his voice low, "do we know whether they can hear inside this room?"

Tang Weiguo flinched so hard his chair scraped the tile. His eyes darted involuntarily toward the ceiling vents.

"Stop looking at the ceiling, Tang Weiguo," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua snapped coldly. "If they wanted to listen, they already would be. If they wanted us dead, we would not be debating satellite hashes."

"Then what do they want?" Han Zhiyuan asked, professional humiliation and dread tightening his throat. "They dropped Deng Yongchang's twenty-year web onto our desks. They locked down provincial factions without a registered bullet. They proved our blind spots are wide enough for a division. Nobody does that for free."

"They did not do it for us," Gao Zhengyi said quietly.

Han Zhiyuan turned sharply. "What?"

"Look at the sequence, Han Zhiyuan," Gao Zhengyi said, crushing his cigarette into the ashtray. "Yunxi came first. Chen Mingde framed on Old Street by thugs tied to Deng Yongchang's syndicate. Twenty-four hours later, Deng Yongchang's national web collapses into forty-two matte-black cases."

Han Zhiyuan swallowed hard. "All of this, forty ministries scrambling, orbital blind spots, twenty years of treason gutted in four hours, over one hardware shop in Yunxi?"

"That speculation never enters a written report," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua cut in like cold iron. "Not ever."

He shut Tang Weiguo's folder and pulled it under his elbow.

"How many saw the raw orbital check, Tang Weiguo?"

"Myself and three cryptographic auditors, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua," Tang Weiguo answered fast. "All four signed lifetime silence oaths."

"Seal the vault under my mark and Premier Gu Chengshan's counter-seal. Zero copies. Zero backups. Tell your staff it was a calibration drill, and never speak of matte-black cases or empty runways again."

Tang Weiguo nodded rapidly, bowed stiffly, and hurried out. The door clicked shut.

Gao Zhengyi let out a slow breath. "Premier Gu Chengshan chairs the forty-department session at eight-thirty. When he sees this folder, he will ask one question."

"Whether the state still controls its own shadow," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, staring at the grey cover.

Han Zhiyuan's fist clenched on the wood. "And our answer?"

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua tapped the folder once. "The truth. The shadow never belonged to us. Our only job tonight is finishing this clean-up so thoroughly it never has a reason to step into the light again."
