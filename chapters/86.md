# Chapter 86: The Unrecorded Numbers

Late Monday afternoon hung grey behind the frosted glass of the central office's basement meeting room. Thick cigarette smoke drifted under the ceiling lamps, and two ashtrays sat piled high with crushed butts on the long table.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua sat at the head with his collar unbuttoned. Beside him, CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi tapped an unlit cigarette against his saucer, staring at the thin grey folder in front of Director Tang Weiguo. Across from them sat Jin Rongzhi of State Security, arms folded tight, his face pale and stiff.

"Read the bottom line again," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, his voice hoarse from twelve hours of meetings. "Skip the lab talk."

Director Tang Weiguo swallowed hard. He had run the national cyber and lab testing bureau for fourteen years, yet his fingers shook clearly as he lifted the top page.

"We finished the full check of all forty-two black cases at four-fifteen, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua," Tang Weiguo said, cold sweat shining on his forehead. "Lab tests, metal checks, light tests, electrical traces, and satellite records. Every single check came back blank."

Gao Zhengyi stopped tapping his cigarette. "Blank means your team missed something, Tang Weiguo. Try again."

"No, Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi, I mean completely blank." Tang Weiguo's voice cracked before he cleared his throat sharply. "Zero hidden fingerprints on any outer case, latch, or foam pad. Not wiped prints. Zero skin oils, zero tiny cloth threads, zero dust specks matching any known city."

Jin Rongzhi leaned forward, his jaw locking. "Every plastic mold leaves a factory mark. Every hinge pin comes from a metal shop."

"We sliced a corner off Case Nineteen and put it under strong lab scopes," Tang Weiguo replied, flipping to page two with a stiff thumb. "The carbon shell matches no army maker, plane plant, or state factory on our list. Zero serial numbers. Zero batch stamps. The memory drives run custom code with zero maker tags. Even the printed paper shows no watermark and no paper fiber mark from any local or foreign mill."

Silence dropped over the table. Gao Zhengyi struck a match, resting his wrist on the wood to keep it steady, and lit his cigarette. He blew a thin stream of smoke upward.

"You are telling me," Gao Zhengyi said, eyes narrowing, "that someone built forty-two untraceable boxes, packed them with twenty years of bank books and treason deals, and dropped them across six cities and the capital without leaving one single clue?"

"It gets worse," Tang Weiguo whispered.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua lit a fresh cigarette from the glowing tip of his last one. "Keep talking, Tang Weiguo."

Tang Weiguo laid a glossy satellite photo beside the ashtray. It showed a top-down photo and heat picture of the backup military airfield outside Lanshui, stamped 00:15. The runway was empty.

"We pulled every radio log, phone tower signal, secret radio link, and flight signal between midnight and four," Tang Weiguo said, his knuckles white on the table edge. "Zero hits. Zero fake towers. Zero unlisted cars on highway toll cameras near any drop site. So we checked space satellites."

Jin Rongzhi stared at the photo, his eyes shrinking tight. "That is the Lanshui north strip."

"Yes," Tang Weiguo said. "We checked four more airfields, including two outside the capital and the coastal strip near Yunxi. At 00:15, national space satellites recorded empty runways at all of them. Clean tarmac. Zero jet heat trails. Zero helicopter heat on night cameras. Zero radar hits."

"Impossible!" Jin Rongzhi slapped his palm flat on the folder. "Forty-two heavy cases do not walk two thousand kilometers! Somebody tampered with the ground files!"

"We already checked!" Tang Weiguo's calm broke, his voice rising before he forced it back down to a ragged whisper. "I brought in three data experts from space command. The security proof from the satellites to the ground receivers is unbroken. Not one single frame was changed. Either nothing landed on those runways, or whatever crossed our skies does not show up on our cameras, our heat sensors, or our radar."

No one spoke. The air fan hummed low overhead.

Gao Zhengyi watched ash grow on his cigarette until it broke onto his pants. He did not brush it off. "And the sentries outside our gates right now?"

Jin Rongzhi answered without looking up from the empty runway photo. "We ran walk checks and heat cameras on the black-clad figures at the central office and Western Hills. Every lens returns flat static. Their suits blend in with the cold stone around them. When my deputy stood two meters from one and demanded a unit code, the figure did not blink, shift weight, or put out a single radio signal."

"Unrecorded numbers," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua murmured.

All three men looked at him.

"That is what General Qin Zhengguo called them this morning," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, his eyes dark. He blew out smoke slowly. "Not a secret army. Not a rogue gang. A unit missing from every payroll, army list, and state security file. And your check just proved they sit completely beyond state tracking."

"Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua," Gao Zhengyi said carefully, keeping his voice low, "do we know if they can hear inside this room?"

Tang Weiguo flinched so hard his chair scraped the tile. His eyes darted up toward the ceiling vents without meaning to.

"Stop looking at the ceiling, Tang Weiguo," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua snapped coldly. "If they wanted to listen, they already would be. If they wanted us dead, we would not be arguing over satellite records."

"Then what do they want?" Jin Rongzhi asked, sharp shame and dread tightening his throat. "They dropped Deng Yongchang's twenty-year web onto our desks. They locked down provincial gangs without one registered bullet. They proved our blind spots are wide enough for an army division. Nobody does that for free."

"They did not do it for us," Gao Zhengyi said quietly.

Jin Rongzhi turned sharply. "What?"

"Look at the order of events, Jin Rongzhi," Gao Zhengyi said, crushing his cigarette into the ashtray. "Yunxi came first. Chen Mingde was framed on Old Street by thugs tied to Deng Yongchang's gang. Twenty-four hours later, Deng Yongchang's whole national web collapses into forty-two black cases."

Jin Rongzhi swallowed hard. "All of this, forty ministries scrambling, space blind spots, twenty years of treason ripped open in four hours, over one hardware shop in Yunxi?"

"That guess never enters a written report," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua cut in like cold iron. "Not ever."

He shut Tang Weiguo's folder and pulled it under his elbow.

"How many people saw the raw satellite check, Tang Weiguo?"

"Myself and three data experts, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua," Tang Weiguo answered fast. "All four signed lifetime silence oaths."

"Lock it in the vault under my mark and Premier Gu Chengshan's seal. Zero copies. Zero backups. Tell your staff it was a routine test drill, and never speak of black cases or empty runways again."

Tang Weiguo nodded fast, bowed stiffly, and hurried out. The door clicked shut.

Gao Zhengyi let out a slow breath. "Premier Gu Chengshan leads the forty-department meeting at eight-thirty. When he sees this folder, he will ask one question."

"Whether the state still controls its own shadow," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua said, staring at the grey cover.

Jin Rongzhi's fist clenched on the wood. "And our answer?"

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua tapped the folder once. "The truth. The shadow never belonged to us. Our only job tonight is finishing this clean-up so well that it never has a reason to step into the light again."
