# Chapter 214: A Quiet Call to Terminal Six

The evening breeze from the Huangpu River rustled the golden leaves of the ancient ginkgo trees in the villa garden below.

Chen Xuan stood on the second-floor balcony of Emerald Riverside Villa, leaning his forearms comfortably against the carved stone railing. The night air was cool and crisp, carrying the distant, low hum of Shanghai's evening traffic and the faint glittering lights of the Pudong docklands five kilometers to the east.

In his hand, he held a sleek, unadorned white smartphone that bore neither manufacturer logos nor serial numbers.

He pressed the speed dial, and the encrypted line connected instantly without a single dial tone.

"Sir," Number 7's voice sounded crisp, respectful, and razor-sharp on the receiver.

"Number 7," Chen Xuan said softly, his tone relaxed and conversational, as if he were discussing tomorrow's grocery list. "There is a small annoyance down at Pudong Port Terminal Six. My wife's container vessel, the *Haiyue Star*, is being held at the pier by a corrupt harbor safety inspector named Captain Lu. He fabricated an irregular ballast water salinity balance to extort money. Some southern merchant named Qian Weidong from Tianhai International is paying him under the table."

On the other end of the private line, Number 7's voice tightened with cold, absolute efficiency. "Qian Weidong is already under active federal surveillance, sir. His company was one of the secondary domestic money-laundering channels identified in the Changbai Mountain ledgers. Director Hou Liangping and the Handong Provincial Anti-Corruption Bureau are currently mobilizing an enforcement unit toward Shanghai."

"Tell Hou Liangping to pick up his pace," Chen Xuan smiled faintly, watching a dry leaf drift down toward the koi pond. "My wife is sitting in the dining room with tears in her eyes, and her sparerib soup is getting cold. I want that pier cleared, Captain Lu stripped of his uniform, and the *Haiyue Star* cleared for departure before nine o'clock."

"Consider it done, sir," Number 7 answered without a heartbeat of hesitation. "I will activate the State Council Maritime Safety Directorate and dispatch the Shanghai Municipal Harbor Command immediately."

"And Number 7?" Chen Xuan added gently.

"Sir?"

"Ensure that Haiyue Group is issued a permanent National VIP Maritime Transit Permit. In the future, no harbor inspector anywhere along the eastern coastline will delay my wife's vessels for a single second."

"Understood, sir. The permanent green channel directive will be signed by the Minister of Transport within ten minutes."

Chen Xuan ended the call with a light tap, slipped the white phone back into his trousers, and turned to walk back inside.

In the dining room, the warm amber light of the crystal chandelier shone down upon the rosewood table. Qin Yuhan had finished washing her face and sat quietly in her chair. Although she had smoothed her hair and wiped away her tears, her delicate face still carried an undercurrent of deep worry, her dark eyes repeatedly flicking toward her smartphone on the tablecloth.

Chen Xuan sat down beside her with an easy, affectionate smile. He picked up the white porcelain ladle, filled her bowl with steaming, fragrant sparerib and winter melon soup, and placed a ceramic spoon into her fingers.

"Drink it while it is warm, Yuhan," Chen Xuan said gently, his voice smooth and comforting. "Winter melon soup clears internal heat and calms the mind. A busy company president cannot run a shipping empire on an empty stomach."

Qin Yuhan looked at the clear, savory broth, lifted the spoon, and took a slow, soothing sip. The delicate flavor of braised pork ribs and sweet winter melon spread warmth through her chest, easing the knot of tension in her stomach.

"It tastes so good, Xiao Xuan," she said softly, turning her eyes toward him with genuine gratitude. "I am sorry for coming home in such a terrible mood. You spent the whole afternoon preparing dinner, and I walked in shouting about corrupt inspectors and lost contracts."

"You never have to apologize to me, Yuhan," Chen Xuan said warmly, reaching across to gently smooth the soft wool of her sweater. "That is what a husband is for. When the outside world is loud, complicated, and unfair, you come home and leave all the noise on the doormat. In this house, you are just my wife."

Qin Yuhan's eyes glistened with tender emotion. She leaned her head against his shoulder, wrapping her arms tightly around his arm, resting her cheek against his cotton shirt.

"Sometimes I look at you, Xiao Xuan, and I think I am the luckiest woman in Shanghai," she murmured softly. "My college classmates married wealthy heirs who spend every night at nightclubs and banquets, arguing over inheritance. You stay home, you cook delicious soup, and you listen to me complain without ever getting impatient."

Chen Xuan chuckled, picking up a tender garlic shrimp with his chopsticks and feeding it into her mouth. "That is because my wife is the prettiest president in the city. How could I ever be impatient with you?"

Qin Yuhan giggled through her mouthful of shrimp, swatting his shoulder playfully. "Flatterer! But seriously, if Captain Lu holds the ship past midnight, Haiyue will face an 8 million yuan fine and our export clients in Qingdao will cancel their orders."

Chen Xuan looked at his wristwatch with an unhurried, serene expression. It was eight-forty in the evening.

"Do not worry, Madam President," Chen Xuan said with a quiet, confident smile. "Corrupt dock inspectors have a habit of tripping over their own shoelaces when they least expect it."

Before Qin Yuhan could ask what he meant, her smartphone on the table burst into life, vibrating furiously against the polished rosewood.
