# Chapter 46: Zero Losses, Total Capture

At three forty-five in the morning on Monday, the underground control room beneath the capital hummed with cold static. Along the far steel bench sat three members of the White Guard: Number 1, Number 7, and Number 9. Their pistols, earpieces, and tablets lay inside a sealed glass bin ten steps away. Two silent figures in plain black armor stood by the heavy door, their visors dark and unreadable.

At the center desk, a third Black Guard operative tapped a single glass key. The main screen shifted from yellow to flat white.

"Final sweep count complete," the Black Guard operative said. The guard spoke evenly into the line. "Fourteen provinces. Forty ministries. Three theater commands. Six ports. Three hundred and eighteen top targets arrested. Forty-seven foreign spies caught. One hundred and fourteen terabytes of unlocked proof sealed and sorted. Zero civilian deaths. Zero public leaks."

On the steel bench, Number 7 let out a sharp, shaky breath. His knuckles turned white against his knees. "Three hundred and eighteen," he whispered, staring at the floor. "In under three hours. Across fourteen provinces."

Number 9 did not look up. His shoulders were stiff, and he swallowed hard. "Every theater command," he murmured. "Even the northern ports. We spent four years building files on half those names, and Zero cleared the board before sunrise."

"Keep your voice down," Number 1 said quietly. His jaw was locked tight, a thin muscle jumping near his temple. Even without his earpiece and held under guard, he sat straight with his eyes fixed on the masked figure at the desk. "When the Black Guard moves, White Guard does not comment. We sit, we wait, and we stay clean."

The Black Guard operative did not turn around. Another glass key clicked under a black glove.

"Local hold check," the guard logged into the open line. "Yunxi County stays locked down in silence. Five local targets are still in place: Cheng Biao, Yan Tiehu, Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong. No arrest by us. They stay inside the county until provincial and local anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terror police take them at dawn."

Number 7's head snapped up. His eyes widened behind his visor, and before Number 1 could stop him, the words burst out.

"Wait. Why are those five still sitting in Yunxi?" Number 7 asked, leaning forward. "Deng Yongchang was the link that opened the whole chain. Liang Guodong signed the dirty arrest papers himself. Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao ran the street thugs, and Sun Weiming signed off on the demolition cover. Why pull three hundred and eighteen top officials out of forty ministries and leave five county rats in their own beds?"

Number 1's hand clamped onto Number 7's wrist like an iron grip. "Number 7," Number 1 rasped, his voice low and sharp. "Shut your mouth. You do not question Zero."

Number 7 stiffened, his jaw tight, though his chest still heaved. "I am not questioning the order. I just do not understand why we left the spark sitting right where it started."

The Black Guard operative paused. Slowly, the black helmet turned halfway toward the bench. No eyes showed behind the dark visor, yet the air in the room felt colder. Number 9 pulled his boots back slightly under the bench, fingers curling against the steel.

"Note for the record," the Black Guard operative said evenly. "White Guard has no say over Zero holds. I will state this once for the log. Yunxi County suspects Cheng Biao, Yan Tiehu, Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong are being held in place on purpose for a lawful local sweep."

"Lawful local sweep," Number 9 repeated softly, his brow furrowing beneath his hood. Then his eyes widened slightly as the reason clicked. "Because of Chen Mingde."

"Correct," the flat voice replied without emotion. "If Zero pulls five small county figures out of bed in the middle of the night without local warrants, provincial teams will ask why a national force stepped into a county hardware shop fight. Every file would point straight back to Chen Mingde and his family. By holding Cheng Biao, Yan Tiehu, Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong in place for a clean provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terror sweep, Chen Mingde walks out free under normal legal review. No strange marks. Zero trace back to Chen Mingde's nephew or his family."

Silence settled over the bench, thick and heavy.

Number 7 stared at the masked operative, color draining from his cheeks. "He thought of that before the first transport even took off," Number 7 whispered, his voice hoarse. "Neither Chen Xuan nor his family will even have to step foot inside a police station."

"Not a single call," Number 9 said, letting out a slow, shaky breath. "Not a single witness report. Chen Mingde's record gets cleared by the county prosecutor himself, the demolition scam gets buried under provincial charges, and the family eats breakfast like nothing happened."

Number 1 slowly let go of Number 7's wrist. His eyes stayed on the white screen where the numbers sat in neat columns: 318 top targets, 47 foreign spies, 114 terabytes, zero losses. A cold shiver ran down Number 1's spine, though he kept his face calm. He had led White Guard for seven years and thought he knew how big the man above them was. Tonight proved he had only ever seen the edge of the shadow.

"What about the capital?" Number 1 asked evenly, keeping his tone businesslike. "General Qin Zhengguo already used the vault line. Elder Song already confirmed the old founding code phrase. The red phones cannot stay dead past four."

"Red-line unlock is set for four in the morning," the Black Guard operative answered, turning back to the desk. "Forty-two black fingerprint-locked cases have been packed from the one hundred and fourteen terabytes of unlocked proof. Every agency's share is sorted. Delivery to the Central Leadership Compound briefing hall is set for four thirty."

Number 7 swallowed hard. "Forty-two cases. Every major office in the capital is going to bleed before noon."

"That is no longer White Guard's business," Number 1 said, standing slowly only after one of the door guards gave a short, stiff nod. Number 1 looked at Number 7 and Number 9, his eyes cold. "We are off the board. When the release order comes through, we split up, burn our logs, and never speak a word about tonight. Not even to each other."

"Understood," Number 9 said quietly, his hands still slightly shaky.

"Understood," Number 7 muttered, his eyes lingering one last second on the Yunxi County line before the screen went blank.

At the desk, the Black Guard operative pressed a final red glass seal. Overhead, the clock rolled toward four in the morning.

"Lockdown phase complete," the guard stated. "Starting capital red-line release. Beginning handoff."

Miles above the underground room, every silent red phone in the highest offices of the country prepared to ring at once.
