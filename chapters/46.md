# Chapter 46: Zero Losses, Total Capture

At three forty-five in the morning on Monday, the sub-level relay room beneath the capital hummed with cold static. Along the far steel bench sat three members of the Black Pine Guard: Number 1, Number 7, and Number 9. Their sidearms, earpieces, and encrypted tablets lay inside a sealed glass bin ten paces away. Two silent figures in featureless matte-black armor stood by the blast door, visors dark and unreadable.

At the center console, a third Zero Guard operative tapped a single glass key. The main ledger screen shifted from amber to flat white.

"Final sweep audit complete," the Zero Guard operative said. The voice came through the modulator flat, neutral, chilled, and completely genderless. "Fourteen provinces. Forty ministries. Three theater commands. Six ports. Three hundred and eighteen high-value principals detained. Forty-seven foreign cutouts captured. One hundred and fourteen terabytes of decrypted evidence sealed and indexed. Zero civilian casualties. Zero public leaks."

On the steel bench, Number 7 let out a sharp, shaky breath. His knuckles turned white against his knees. "Three hundred and eighteen," he whispered, staring at the floor. "In under three hours. Across fourteen provinces."

Number 9 did not look up. His shoulders were rigid, his throat bobbing as he swallowed hard. "Every theater command," he murmured. "Even the northern ports. We spent four years building files on half those names, and Zero cleared the board before sunrise."

"Keep your voice down," Number 1 said quietly. His jaw was locked tight, a thin muscle jumping near his temple. Even stripped of his earpiece and held under guard, his posture remained straight, his eyes fixed on the masked figure at the console. "When Zero activates, Black Pine does not comment. We sit, we wait, and we stay clean."

The Zero Guard operative did not turn around. Another glass key clicked under a matte-black glove.

"Secondary containment verification," the flat, genderless voice logged into the open relay. "Yunxi County local tier remains in place under silent isolation. Five local targets confirmed static: Cheng Biao, Yan Tiehu, Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong. No extraction ordered. Local assets remain sealed inside county boundaries pending transfer to provincial and local lawful anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism agencies at dawn."

Number 7's head snapped up. His eyes widened behind his visor, and before Number 1 could stop him, the words burst out.

"Wait. Why are those five still sitting in Yunxi?" Number 7 asked, leaning forward. "Deng Yongchang was the junction point that opened the chain. Liang Guodong signed the dirty detention papers himself. Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao ran the street muscle, and Sun Weiming signed off on the demolition cover. Why pull three hundred and eighteen principals out of forty ministries and leave five county rats in their own beds?"

Number 1's hand clamped onto Number 7's wrist like an iron vise. "Number 7," Number 1 rasped, his voice low and sharp. "Shut your mouth. You do not question Zero."

Number 7 stiffened, his jaw clenching, though his chest still heaved. "I am not questioning the order. I just do not understand why we left the spark sitting right where it started."

The Zero Guard operative paused. Slowly, the matte-black helmet rotated half a turn toward the bench. No eyes showed behind the dark plate, yet the air in the room felt several degrees colder. Number 9 pulled his boots back slightly under the bench, fingers curling against the steel.

"Protocol note for the record," the Zero Guard operative said in that chilled, genderless cadence. "Black Pine Guard has no authority over Zero containment tiers. The directive is logged once for archival clarity. Yunxi County suspects Cheng Biao, Yan Tiehu, Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong are deliberately held in place for lawful local sweep transfer."

"Lawful local sweep," Number 9 repeated softly, his brow furrowing beneath his hood. Then his eyes widened slightly as the logic clicked. "Because of Chen Mingde."

"Correct," the flat voice replied without inflection. "If Zero removes five minor county figures in the middle of the night without local warrants, provincial investigators will ask why a national apparatus intervened in a county hardware shop dispute. Every file would point straight back to Chen Mingde and his relatives. By holding Cheng Biao, Yan Tiehu, Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, and Liang Guodong in place for a clean provincial anti-corruption, anti-gang, and counter-terrorism sweep, Chen Mingde walks out of custody under standard legal review. Zero anomalies. Zero trace back to Chen Xuan or his family."

Silence settled over the bench, thick and heavy.

Number 7 stared at the masked operative, the color draining from his cheeks beneath his collar. "He thought of that before the first transport even lifted off," Number 7 whispered, his voice hoarse. "Neither Chen Xuan nor his family will even have to step foot inside a police station."

"Not a single summons," Number 9 said, letting out a slow, trembling breath. "Not a single witness statement. Chen Mingde's record gets wiped clean by the county prosecutor himself, the demolition fraud gets buried under provincial indictments, and the family eats breakfast like nothing happened."

Number 1 slowly released Number 7's wrist. His eyes stayed on the white ledger screen where the numbers sat in merciless columns: 318 principals, 47 foreign cutouts, 114 terabytes, zero losses. A cold shiver ran down Number 1's spine, though he kept his face unreadable. He had served Black Pine for fifteen years and believed he understood the scale of the man who stood above them. Tonight proved he had only ever seen the edge of the shadow.

"What about the capital?" Number 1 asked evenly, keeping his tone strictly procedural. "General Qin Zhengguo already used the vault line. Elder Song already verified the founding override phrase. The red lines cannot stay dark past four."

"Red-line unlock is scheduled for four hundred hours," the Zero Guard operative answered, turning back to the console. "Forty-two matte-black biometric hard cases have been compiled from the one hundred and fourteen terabytes of decrypted proof. Agency indexing is complete. Delivery to the Central Leadership Compound briefing hall is scheduled for four thirty."

Number 7 swallowed hard. "Forty-two cases. Every major body in the capital is going to bleed before noon."

"That is no longer Black Pine's concern," Number 1 said, rising slowly only after one of the door guards gave a brief, rigid nod. Number 1 looked at Number 7 and Number 9, his eyes cold. "We are off the board. When the release order comes through, we disperse, burn our logs, and never speak a word about tonight. Not even to each other."

"Understood," Number 9 said quietly, his hands still slightly unsteady.

"Understood," Number 7 muttered, his gaze lingering one last second on the Yunxi County line before the screen wiped clean.

At the console, the Zero Guard operative pressed a final red glass seal. Overhead, the clock rolled toward four in the morning.

"Lockdown phase complete," the flat, genderless voice stated. "Initiating capital red-line release. Commencing handoff protocol."

Miles above the concrete vault, every silent red telephone in the highest offices of the republic prepared to ring at once.
