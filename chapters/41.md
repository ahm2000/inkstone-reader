# Chapter 41: One Page, One Seal

By eight on Monday morning, the same page lay on desks all over Lanshui Province, and nobody knew who had put it there.

In Yunxi County, Yin Hongwei, the Labor Bureau chief, carried it into the Party Secretary's office the way a man carries a hot pot.

"Secretary Fan. Magistrate Tao. This came through the province's system at 11:40 last night. The sender line is empty."

Fan Weiguo read it once. A death at work must be reported in twenty-four hours. Hiding one was a crime. Five years in prison, at least. No grace period.

Then he looked at the seal at the bottom. It was round and red, with no office name. Inside the ring was a small branch of pine.

He had never seen it before. He did not need to. A rule with no office and a seal with no name could come only from the same place as the black card.

He thought of an old man fishing at Qingshan Reservoir, and of a young voice on the phone that had said, "Do not approach." His right hand drifted toward the bottom drawer, where the black card lay. He pulled it back.

Beside him, Tao Shu read the page with a warm, easy smile. He thought of the folder he had burned on Saturday. He thought of how fast a county magistrate could become a man with a guard outside his door. Whoever could write this page overnight, he told himself, could write one about him just as easily.

"A good rule," Tao Shu said. "The county will carry it out at once."

"Yes," Fan Weiguo said. "At once."

A young clerk named Hou Tianyou had followed his chief into the room with a reply form. He raised one hand.

"Chief Yin, sorry. Which office issued this rule? The form needs an office name."

Nobody answered. Yin Hongwei looked at the ceiling.

"Write 'the rule,'" he said at last.

"Which rule?"

"This rule."

"Okay." Hou Tianyou wrote it down slowly. "Do I write that it is signed by the pine branch?"

"Hou Tianyou," Fan Weiguo said, in a voice nobody had heard from him before, "write 'received' and leave the room."

Across town, at the inspection team's table in the county canteen, Gao Zhengyi was eating porridge when his aide showed him the same page on a phone.

He read it. His left hand slid inside his jacket and stayed there.

"Who else has seen this?"

"Every labor bureau in the province, Vice Director. By the morning news, every company will know."

Gao Zhengyi put down his spoon. His porridge had gone cold, and he did not notice.

"Then it is not a secret," he said. "It is an order. Do not look for who sent it. Make sure nobody in this province can say they did not see it."

* * *

At nine, two Labor Inspection officers walked into the office of Tiancheng Demolition. They were Cai Lan and Gu Zhenyu, both in thin jackets with the bureau's badge on the pocket.

A lawyer came in behind them. He wore a plain gray suit and a Legal Aid Center card, and he was the kind of man you forget while he is talking to you. He was Number One Hundred and Four of the Black Pine Guard, but the inspectors saw only the card. He had filed a complaint at eight sharp for Tang Xiaoyu.

Shen Jinbao came out of his inner office, a heavy man with a gold watch and a face the color of old rice.

"What is this? I know the rule. A company has thirty days to report. It has been sixty."

"The rule changed at 11:40 last night," Cai Lan said. "There are no thirty days now."

"A new rule cannot reach back. Everyone knows that."

"It does not need to," said Number One Hundred and Four. His voice was mild. "The crime is not the death. The crime is hiding it. You are still hiding it this morning. Every day you do not report it, the crime starts again."

Shen Jinbao opened his mouth and closed it.

He grabbed his phone and tried Cheng Biao again. It was his twelfth try since breakfast. A recorded voice said, "The number you dialed is powered off."

His brother-in-law was suspended, and he had turned off his phone. Shen Jinbao understood that as clearly as a slap.

In the corridor, someone cleared her throat.

Rong Xiaomei, the company accountant, was fifty-four and had worked for Shen Jinbao for nineteen years. She held a plastic shopping bag in both hands. Inside was a thick brown ledger.

"The real one," she said to the lawyer, very low. "The 600,000 is on page forty. Peng Hu signed for it. He left town at six this morning with a sports bag."

Through the glass door, Shen Jinbao saw it all.

"Rong Xiaomei." His voice cracked. "I paid for your daughter's college."

"I know, Boss Shen." Her hands shook, and her eyes were wet. "That is why I am so afraid. Peng Hu will be blamed first, and then whoever kept the books. I did not know what 'settle the family quietly' meant. I thought it meant flowers."

"Come to our office and say all of it," said Number One Hundred and Four. "The law looks kindly on the person who comes first."

She nodded and walked out with him. She did not look back.

Peng Hu did not get far, and he did not know he was being followed. At seven, on the expressway, two highway police officers stopped his old sedan for speeding. One of them was an honest officer who had never heard of the Black Pine Guard. He looked into the open sports bag on the back seat and saw 540,000 yuan in cash. Peng Hu had spent 60,000 on a watch and a phone.

The honest officer called it in. He never knew his partner had been waiting at that spot since six.

* * *

Shen Jinbao drove himself to the Banyan Club. His driver had quit by text at nine thirty.

The Banyan Club was a quiet house with no sign, behind a high wall in Qingyan's old west side. Inside, Huang Boren sat beside a small stove, pouring tea as softly as a man pours water for a sleeping child.

"Chairman Huang." Shen Jinbao could not sit. "A labor death. The inspectors came with a lawyer. I need eight million, for bail, for the fines, for lawyers who know the province."

"Sit, Shen Jinbao."

He sat.

"I do not lend to a man the inspectors are following," Huang Boren said gently. "A loan is a thread, and I do not like to hold a thread that leads back to me. But I would not leave an old friend alone on a bad Monday."

Shen Jinbao felt something loosen in his chest. "Chairman. Thank you."

"I will send a man to sit with you too."

"Too?" Shen Jinbao blinked. "Who else has a man?"

Huang Boren smiled and set down the teapot.

"Someone else who is having a bad Monday," he said. "Go home. Do not speak to anyone until he arrives."
