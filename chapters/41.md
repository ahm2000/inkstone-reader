# Chapter 41: Tomato Stakes

The text from Zhou Guilan came at 6:40 on Sunday morning, before the sun was fully up.

*Fence quarrel. Wear boots. Bring the girl.*

"Boots?" Chen Xuan looked at his phone. "Mom, it's a fence."

"When your grandmother says boots, you wear boots." Liu Meifang pulled a pair of Chen Hao's old rubber boots out of the closet. "And take her. A child should see a village on Sunday. Fresh air is good for thin people."

Tang Xiaoyu stood by the door in her big jacket. The blanket on Chen Yuan's bed was folded into a perfect square, and a brown glove was tucked into her sleeve. She put on the boots without a word. They were two sizes too big, and she walked in them like a duck.

Forty minutes later, the bus left them at the gate of Hongshi Village.

The vegetable plots lay on the slope behind the old houses, in neat rows of green. At the end of one row, near the irrigation ditch, a crowd of about ten people stood around two old women and one old man.

Chen Fugen, Chen Xuan's grandfather, was holding a hoe he had not used. Zhou Guilan sat on a low stool beside him with a pot of pickled eggs on her lap.

"There you are." She looked at Tang Xiaoyu from head to foot. "Too thin. Sit by me, child."

Beside her, on a folding stool, Zhao Dehai was peeling an egg with great care. He had his old straw hat on. He looked up and gave Chen Xuan a look of fierce disapproval, which in him meant welcome.

"You are late."

"Morning, Old Zhao. It's a Sunday."

"No excuse."

* * *

The quarrel was easy to see, though not easy to follow.

Dong Yuzhen, sixty-six, loud and round, stood on one side of a string fence with her fists on her hips. Her tomato plants were tall and heavy with fruit, and each one leaned on a bamboo stake.

On the other side stood Pei Daqing, seventy-two. Everyone called him Old Pei. He was thin and still, with a face like a stone wall. He did not shout. When someone was lying to him, he simply looked at them until they stopped.

"Your stakes are on my side," Pei Daqing said.

"My stakes have always been exactly where they are! You are the one who moved the string!"

"I did not."

"Then the string walked."

Chen Xuan watched them for a while. The words flew over his head, and some of them were strange for a vegetable garden.

"The head of the ditch has always taken first," Dong Yuzhen said. "That is what the head is for. The tail takes what is left."

"If the head always takes first, the tail starves in a dry year," Pei Daqing said. "A water quota is a number per row, not per mouth."

"And whose budget pays for your row next year?"

"It will be paid out of the same pot as yours."

Chen Xuan thought they sounded like two bosses at a meeting. He put down his green thermos and cleared his throat.

"Auntie Dong. Old Pei. How wide was each plot when the village gave them out?"

Both of them looked at him.

"Ten meters for both," said Zhou Guilan. "Fugen measured it with a rope. Five meters each."

"Oh." Chen Xuan smiled. "That's easy. My thermos is twenty-five centimeters. Twenty of them make five meters."

He laid the thermos down at the corner stone by the ditch and moved it forward, end to end, counting under his breath. The twentieth ended on a patch of bare dirt. The twenty-first would have landed under Dong Yuzhen's tomato stakes, over the string.

"One thermos over, Auntie Dong," he said gently. "On Old Pei's side."

"It cannot be," Dong Yuzhen said.

"Look at the dirt," said a small voice.

Tang Xiaoyu had crouched at the edge of the plot. She was pointing at a round dent in the soil, a little deeper than the rest.

"There is a hole here. It's round, like a post stood in it. Somebody pulled it out." She pressed her thumb in. It fit. "This is the old line. It's twenty thermoses from the corner stone."

Everybody was quiet. Dong Yuzhen looked at the hole. Then she looked at her grandson, who had been standing at the back of the crowd with a very red face.

"Dong Weimin."

"Grandma, I only wanted to give you more room."

"Go get a hammer."

Pei Daqing said nothing. But the stone face moved a little, and Zhou Guilan saw it.

"Old Pei is smiling," she whispered to her husband. "I have not seen that in four years. This girl is a lucky girl."

"It's the boots," Chen Fugen said. "Good luck boots."

* * *

That was when a man in a fleece vest and white sneakers came walking up the path.

He carried a camera on a strap and had the shy look of a hiker who had taken a wrong turn. He did not see Dong Yuzhen. She saw him at once.

"Xin Jun."

The man stopped as if his feet had been nailed to the path.

Dong Yuzhen strode across the string, dropped her voice, and spoke so clearly that the whole slope could hear.

"Xin Jun. Head of Xinhe District. Last March you told the Provincial Water Committee that every ditch in your district was lined with concrete. I read your report. Sixty percent of them are dirt. Your Land Bureau and your Water Affairs Office both signed it. Do you think we do not read?"

"Auntie, I only came for a walk."

"In a vest with your district seal on the zipper? Take your camera and go home and line your ditches."

Xin Jun's face had gone gray. "Yes, Auntie. Yes. Sorry." He turned and ran down the path, camera swinging, and he did not look back once.

"Who was that?" asked Chen Xuan.

"A man who came to see where the new road might go," said Dong Yuzhen, and then she smiled brightly. "Child, would you like a tomato? Take three. Take five."

* * *

Two rows away, a man in a gray jacket was hoeing cabbages with great concentration.

Number Nine had not looked at the plots once. He had seen all of them.

At the edge of the field, a man in a plain brown farm coat carried a sack of fertilizer on his shoulder. His back was straight as a post. He was Ren Gang, the colonel who guarded Zhao Dehai. He set the sack down, wiped his forehead and, for a moment, looked directly at the gray jacket.

Number Nine touched his ear. Ren Gang touched the brim of his cap. Neither of them said a word, and neither of them stopped working.

At the gate of the village, a man in a gray cap sat on a motorbike that was not running. He was Zero Guard. He had not moved since seven.

* * *

When the crowd had thinned and the eggs were nearly gone, Pei Daqing walked across the dirt and stopped in front of Tang Xiaoyu.

She stood very still. His eyes were deep and steady.

"What is your name?"

"Tang Xiaoyu. My dad works at Tiancheng. He's on night shift. He is coming for me soon."

Pei Daqing looked at her for a long time. The look would have made a liar cry. She did not cry. She held her breath and stared back.

"That was somebody's lie before it was yours," he said at last, very softly. "Somebody has been lying to you, child. We will make sure it ends."

Her chin shook once. Then she pressed her lips tight.

Chen Xuan stood a few steps off, his thermos under his arm. He did not understand what the old man meant, and he felt a cold line run down his back anyway. Then Chen Fugen slapped his shoulder.

"Chen Xuan. You are standing around with a thermos while others work. A man should hold a hoe."

"I'm thin, Grandpa. You said so."

"Hmph. I said eat more, not do less."

Zhou Guilan pressed an egg into the girl's hand. Zhao Dehai watched Pei Daqing walk away, and he gave a small nod, one old man to another.

Nobody was looking at the village road. At the first bend, two lanes from the gate, a black sedan had been parked with its engine running since nine. The plates were covered in mud. Nobody had stepped out, and nobody was going to.

Behind the cabbages, Number Nine's thumb began to move on his phone. *Black sedan, two lanes east. Not ours. Not Colonel Ren's. Not Zero.*
