# Chapter 211: The Handong Standing Committee

The next morning, December frost clung to the bare ginkgo branches outside the Handong Provincial Party Committee compound in Jingzhou. Inside the main conference hall, the heating hummed softly while steam rose from white porcelain teacups lined down the long walnut table.

Sha Ruijin set his fountain pen beside a thick blue binder and looked across the table. Sitting directly opposite him was the Provincial Governor, his equal in rank and his sharpest sparring partner on the committee. Between them sat Tian Guofu, the Provincial Discipline Inspection Commission Secretary who had come down from Beijing alongside Sha Ruijin, and Li Dakang, the Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary, whose jaw was already tight with impatience.

"Today we settle two items before the winter shipping peak locks up our ports," Sha Ruijin said, his voice steady and unhurried. "First, the coastal port customs integration between Handong and Shanghai. Second, the remaining funding for the Luzhou Airport cargo expansion and the Crescent Lake water cleanup."

The Provincial Governor did not rush to reply. He lifted the lid of his teacup, blew gently across the green leaves, and offered a polite smile that did not reach his eyes.

"Secretary Sha's urgency is admirable," the Governor said smoothly. "Still, winter freight is already heavy. If we force coastal port reforms through this month, smaller local shipping firms may choke on the new compliance checks. We should not chase pretty headlines while our docks lose volume."

He set his cup down and glanced toward Luzhou Party Secretary Yi Xuexi at the far end of the table.

"Look at Luzhou," the Governor added, laying his trap with casual ease. "Crescent Lake and the airport cargo link have dragged on for years. Comrade Yi Xuexi only took over Luzhou recently after a long stretch on the sidelines. Handing him another massive budget before winter is risky. What if the paperwork looks tidy in December, and by February the runway apron is still mud?"

The room went quiet. Several department heads aligned with the Governor watched to see how Sha Ruijin would defend a cadre he had personally promoted.

Sha Ruijin leaned back slightly and looked straight at the Governor.

"Governor, you raised a good point," Sha Ruijin said. "Why did Crescent Lake and the Luzhou Airport link stall for three whole years? Was the mud in Luzhou too deep, or did approval folders sit untouched inside provincial bureaus waiting for preferred contractors to win the dredging bids?"

The Governor's smile thinned. "Administrative review takes time when environmental standards are involved."

"Then let us hear the actual numbers," Sha Ruijin said. "Comrade Yi Xuexi, please brief the committee."

Yi Xuexi stood briefly to show respect to both the Party Secretary and the Governor, then sat straight and opened his tabbed folder.

"Secretary Sha, Governor, comrades," Yi Xuexi began. "Over the past ninety days, Luzhou cleared four layers of middlemen from the Crescent Lake dredging project and reopened every contract to open bidding. The provincial environmental lab passed our water tests twelve days ago. At Luzhou Airport, the new cargo apron foundation cured before the first December freeze. Cutting informal broker fees has already saved local factories eighteen percent on winter freight."

He slid copies of the audited invoices and lab stamps across the table. Not a single figure was padded.

The Governor glanced at the top page, his jaw stiffening. Caught cleanly in his own trap, he pivoted toward Jingzhou.

"Luzhou made progress, which is good," the Governor said coolly. "Yet coastal port reform depends on Jingzhou's feeder yards too. Guangming District's freight clearance center is still three weeks behind schedule. Secretary Li, perhaps your own backyard needs sweeping first."

Li Dakang did not flinch. He slapped his palm lightly against his notebook and let out a sharp, dry laugh.

"Governor, I never hide dirty laundry," Li Dakang snapped. "Guangming District is indeed behind, and I know exactly why. District Chief Sun Liancheng clocks out at 5:00 PM sharp, goes up to his roof with a telescope to stare at the stars, and refuses to sign a single freight permit unless someone hands him a promotion first. If he stalls one more truck this week, I will move my desk into his office and make him watch me work until midnight."

A few committee members hid quiet coughs behind their hands. Li Dakang was not done. He leaned forward, his sharp eyes locking onto the Governor's side of the table.

"Still, let us be fair to Sun Liancheng," Li Dakang added, twisting the knife with a straight face. "Where did a district chief learn that stalling good projects carries zero punishment? He watched certain provincial offices sit on Luzhou's paperwork for three years without losing a single bonus."

The Governor's face darkened. He opened his mouth to fire back, but Tian Guofu cleared his throat softly.

When the Discipline Inspection Secretary spoke, the temperature in the hall seemed to drop another degree.

"Comrades, I have one brief reminder," Tian Guofu said, placing a thin gray file on the table. "Last night, our discipline team received verified cross-provincial banking alerts. The bottlenecks slowing down Handong's coastal freight are not simple laziness. Private shipping brokers have been paying off port and bureau insiders to trap rival cargo and squeeze manufacturers for extra fees."

Tian Guofu's gaze swept slowly across the Governor's allies, lingering just long enough to make two bureau directors shift uneasily in their leather chairs.

"Anyone still protecting freight middlemen or stalling port reform for private favors will not be arguing in this conference room next month," Tian Guofu finished calmly. "They will be explaining their bank statements to my investigators."

Silence settled heavily over the walnut table. The Governor knew when a pit had become too deep to climb out of. Fighting further now would look like shielding corrupt brokers.

He forced a stiff nod and picked up his pen. "Discipline comes first, of course. Sign off on Luzhou's remaining Crescent Lake allocation, and let Jingzhou push the coastal customs link forward immediately."

"Good," Sha Ruijin said, closing his blue binder. "Winter cargo waits for nobody. Adjourned."

Outside, the December wind rattled the frozen branches, and the first cracks in the coastal shipping cartel were already spreading toward Shanghai.
