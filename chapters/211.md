# Chapter 211: The Jingzhou Connection

On Wednesday morning, the provincial standing committee conference room of the Handong Provincial Party Committee in Jingzhou was filled with quiet, disciplined intensity.

Thick velvet curtains were drawn back halfway, allowing pale autumn sunlight to illuminate the oval mahogany table. At the center of the room, thirteen leather armchairs were arranged with strict protocol.

Sha Ruijin, Secretary of the Handong Provincial Party Committee, sat at the head of the table. His dark jacket was neat, his reading glasses laid beside an official porcelain teacup painted with blue pine branches. To his left sat Governor Liu Weimin, while to his right sat Deputy Secretary Gao Yuliang. Further down the row sat Li Dakang, Municipal Party Secretary of Jingzhou, along with Ji Changming, Chief Procurator of Handong Province, and the standing committee directors of discipline, organization, and public security.

Before each standing committee member lay a confidential red-stamped folder titled *Briefing on the National Clean-Up of Coastal Logistics Syndicates and Residual Regional Assets*.

"Comrades," Sha Ruijin began, his voice calm, resonant, and carrying the natural authority of a seasoned leader. "Over the weekend, a joint federal operation coordinated under the personal supervision of Elder Song in Beijing successfully recovered 300 tons of strategic gold reserves in Changbai Mountain. The corrupt Seven-Harbor Consortium that monopolized northern shipping lanes for forty years has been permanently liquidated. However, the purpose of our standing committee meeting today is to address the local reality here in Handong."

Li Dakang leaned forward immediately, his sharp eyes fixed on the economic appendices. His face bore his characteristic expression of restless impatience, eager to strip away bureaucratic obstacles that slowed industrial growth.

"Secretary Sha," Li Dakang spoke up briskly, tapping a finger against the document. "For over eight years, the Seven-Harbor Consortium operated through local front companies tied to the old Shanshui Group network once run by Zhao Ruilong and Qi Tongwei. They controlled transit warehouses along the Jingzhou canal port and monopolized freight berths at the river terminals. Now that the consortium is shattered, those berths are tied up in legal disputes. Shippers cannot unload their cargo, and Jingzhou's industrial output is losing tens of millions every single day! We must seize this opportunity to clear out the stagnant elements and bring in modern, high-efficiency logistics partners who operate strictly within the law!"

Gao Yuliang smiled faintly, adjusting his silver-rimmed glasses with calm, measured elegance.

"Secretary Dakang's enthusiasm for GDP and economic momentum is well known to everyone at this table," Gao Yuliang remarked smoothly, his tone polite yet subtly cautious. "However, we must respect established administrative protocols. If the Jingzhou municipal government forcibly revokes riverfront operating licenses without exhaustive judicial review, we risk embroiling the province in prolonged civil litigation, much like the complicated aftermath of the Guangming Peak land parcel. Stability and the rule of law must guide our hand."

"Stability should never be an excuse for administrative paralysis, Secretary Yuliang!" Li Dakang countered with fiery conviction, his voice rising with urgent energy. "When a cancer is cut out of a patient's body, the surgeon does not wait three years to stitch the wound! Look at Shanghai's Haiyue Group. Yesterday, President Qin Yuhan took over the northern maritime feeder lanes and mobilized sixty freight teams within twelve hours without a single delayed container! That is what true modern enterprise looks like! If Jingzhou welcomes legitimate partners like Haiyue, our regional transport bottleneck will be solved before the end of the month!"

Sha Ruijin listened quietly to the spirited debate between the two veteran leaders, nodding with a faint, discerning smile. He lifted his porcelain teacup, took a measured sip, and set it down with a soft click that immediately quieted the room.

"Both Comrade Dakang and Comrade Yuliang have raised critical perspectives," Sha Ruijin said in a balanced, authoritative tone. "Economic reform cannot proceed without strict legal compliance, but legal procedure must serve the people's livelihood rather than protect entrenched privileges. The central leadership in Beijing has given explicit guidance: all legitimate, high-integrity commercial enterprises like Haiyue Group must be fully protected, while predatory syndicates attempting to seize control of public berths will be dealt with ruthlessly."

Sha Ruijin shifted his gaze down the table to Ji Changming. "Comrade Changming, has the Provincial Anti-Corruption Bureau identified who is currently attempting to take over Seven-Harbor's remaining riverfront routes?"

Ji Changming opened a classified report and adjusted his glasses. "Secretary Sha, Director Hou Liangping has already opened an active investigation. A private shipping conglomerate named Tianhai International, led by a southern merchant named Qian Weidong, is aggressively muscling into Pudong and Handong ports. Evidence indicates Qian Weidong has paid extensive bribes to regional maritime safety inspectors to fabricate safety infractions and delay competitors' vessels."

Li Dakang's face flushed with anger, and he slapped his palm on his notebook. "Bribing dock inspectors to sabotage lawful shipping? Right under our noses? That is intolerable!"

Sha Ruijin looked around the oval table. "Let us take a show of hands. All standing committee members in favor of authorizing Comrade Hou Liangping and the procuratorial task force to initiate immediate joint enforcement against Tianhai International and its corrupt ties."

Thirteen hands rose in absolute unison around the mahogany table.

"Passed unanimously," Sha Ruijin concluded firmly, closing his pen. "Secretary Dakang, ensure Jingzhou's municipal departments cooperate fully. Comrade Changming, instruct Hou Liangping that no official, regardless of background, will be permitted to obstruct the nation's trade arteries."
