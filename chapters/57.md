# Chapter 57: Ministry of State Security: The Mole Hunt

At five past seven on Monday morning, the third-floor briefing room of the Ministry of State Security lay shrouded in blue cigarette smoke. Ten bureau directors sat around a brushed-steel table, their eyes repeatedly drifting to the empty leather chair at the head.

Twelve hours earlier, their Deputy Director had sat there signing off on the quarterly port review. Two hours before dawn, his badge was revoked, his residence sealed, and his name deleted from the active roster.

Internal-Audit Bureau Director Han Dongsheng tapped a thick dossier against the steel. His stained fingers shook as he lit his fourth cigarette in thirty minutes. "Stop staring at the empty seat. He is not walking back in. The State Council memo is blunt: we submit our internal breach assessment before the eight o'clock War Room, or this ministry goes under external trusteeship."

Counter-Espionage First Bureau Director Tang Bowen let out a dry laugh that turned into a cough. "External trusteeship? Han Dongsheng, look at the seal on the boxes in the corner. We already have external trusteeship. Someone dumped twenty years of Zurich Northern Ledger routing tables onto our vault terminal at four fifteen, and our intrusion monitors never blinked."

"Because they did not use our network," Cyber-Defense Bureau Director Song Yiming snapped, shoving slipping glasses up his sweat-slicked nose with a trembling hand. "I ran three packet audits since five. Zero external handshakes. Zero firewall alerts. Those files appeared directly on an air-gapped server three floors underground. Either someone walked past twelve biometric checkpoints with a drive, or we are facing a hardware override we lack the clearance to log."

"Do not hide behind server jargon, Song Yiming," Foreign-Station European Desk Director Mei Guohua said sharply, leaning across the steel so her smoke drifted into his face. "Zurich Northern Ledger has been a ghost file on my desk since 2006. Every time my Geneva station got close to a shell broker moving maritime insurance through Zurich, headquarters froze my budget. Who signed those freeze orders?"

Everyone glanced at the empty chair, then turned slowly toward Personnel Security Bureau Director Yuan Wenkai.

Yuan Wenkai stiffened, his jaw locking hard. "If you imply I covered for the Deputy Director, say it into the recorder, Mei Guohua. Every freeze carried his personal stamp and a counter-signature from Domestic Port-Watch."

"Leave my bureau out of your suicide pact!" Domestic Port-Watch Bureau Director Shi Kang barked, slamming his ashtray onto the steel with a metallic clang. Ash scattered across the shipping charts. "My inspectors flagged anomalous container-weight variances in Ningbo, Tianjin, and Xiamen fourteen times in six years! Fourteen reports! Every single one vanished into Counter-Espionage Second Bureau for 'integrated review'!"

Counter-Espionage Second Bureau Director Fu Zhen did not flinch, though a vein pulsed along his neck. He took a deliberate pull on his cigarette and stared coldly at Shi Kang. "Your reports were garbage, Shi Kang. Half lacked broker identities, and half pointed at legitimate state-backed freight forwarders. You wanted me to raid three port authorities on your hunch?"

"My hunch just turned out to be a twenty-year continental siphon!" Shi Kang shouted, half rising, face crimson. "Read line forty-two of the Zurich Northern Ledger extract! Those 'legitimate forwarders' cleared dual-use rare-earth concentrates and stolen naval blueprints inside agricultural containers! Right under our noses!"

"Sit down, Shi Kang," Signals Interception Bureau Director Qiao Lian said softly, her knuckles white around her mug. "Shouting will not erase the timestamps. Look at the metadata Technical Forensics pulled off the overnight drop."

All eyes shifted to Technical Forensics Bureau Director Luo Pei. The gaunt man sat hunched over a tablet, eyes red-rimmed with a dread that silenced the room.

"The Zurich Northern Ledger did not just bribe port clerks," Luo Pei whispered rasply. "Look at the encryption keys masking their coastal relays. Two of those ciphers match our own Ministry of State Security rotation blocks from 2019 and 2022. Not cracked from outside. Exported cleanly from an internal key-generation terminal."

A suffocating silence fell over the steel table. Only burning tobacco crackled.

"Say that again," Tang Bowen demanded, his cigarette frozen an inch from his lips. "The Deputy Director was not the only mole in this building?"

"The Deputy Director lacked physical access to the key-generation vault," Luo Pei replied, lifting bloodshot eyes across the table. "Only three bureau directors in this room hold dual-custody tokens for that vault."

Chairs shifted instantly. Men pulled elbows back as if the steel had turned white-hot.

Foreign-Station Maritime Desk Director Cheng Jiakai crushed his cigarette into the overflowing tray, eyes darting between Song Yiming, Qiao Lian, and Fu Zhen. "Hold on. Before we tear each other's throats out, ask the bigger question. How did whoever sent these files crack a twenty-year Zurich network, trace every domestic port relay, and decrypt our own 2022 ciphers in one weekend?"

"We know who cracked it," Han Dongsheng muttered, staring at the blank, unnumbered cover sheet. "Nobody here is allowed to log the name, and none of us holds the grade to query them. Black Pine Guard went silent on every liaison channel at midnight. When the regular watchdogs lock down and a foreign ledger drops on our desk by sunrise, you do not need a memo to know what moved."

"I do not care what ghost moved in the dark," Tang Bowen snapped, sweat darkening his collar. "I care that our ministry chased shadows in Europe for twenty years while a syndicate ran a private customs highway through our harbors. At eight o'clock, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua will ask why State Security needed an unrecorded ghost unit to tell us our Deputy Director was on a Zurich payroll."

"Then we do not walk in empty-handed," Qiao Lian said sharply, sliding a printed list across the steel. "Every key-generation vault log from 2018 to last night is right there. Three deputy section chiefs under Counter-Espionage Second Bureau and two cyber-maintenance contractors under Song Yiming's floor signed in during off-hours matching the leaks."

Song Yiming went pale. "My contractors were vetted by Personnel Security!"

"And Personnel Security answers to Yuan Wenkai," Fu Zhen shot back, pointing a stained finger through the smoke. "Every dirty clearance passed his desk!"

"Enough!" Han Dongsheng slammed both palms onto the table. "Internal Audit has locked all five exits. Every director surrenders his phone, vault token, and division roster right now. Mei Guohua, Cheng Jiakai, issue burn notices to Europe and our maritime desks before Zurich realizes the ledger is dead. Shi Kang, freeze every flagged broker from Dalian to Shenzhen."

"And the three directors with vault custody?" Tang Bowen asked softly through gray smoke.

"They stay under armed guard until Internal Audit cross-checks the biometrics," Han Dongsheng replied, lighting a fresh cigarette with steady hands. "If another mole sits at this table, he is not reaching the eight o'clock War Room."

Nobody moved for five seconds. Then ten pairs of eyes slid sideways through the smoke, measuring colleagues they had trusted for twenty years.
