# Chapter 57: The Fishing Bank Revisited

Saturday morning at Qingshan Reservoir smelled of wet reeds, cold granite, and faint woodsmoke from the hillside orchards. The sun had just cleared the eastern ridge, laying a pale gold ribbon across water as flat as brushed tin.

Chen Xuan carried two cheap folding canvas stools under one arm and a battered red thermos under the other. Beside him, Grandfather Chen Fugen walked with his long bamboo rod resting across his shoulder, a dented white plastic bucket swinging from his free hand.

Three steps behind them came Tang Xiaoyu in the thick red sweater Zhou Guilan had knitted for her and clean canvas shoes. Her hands stayed tucked in her pockets as her eyes moved over the wide water with quiet wonder.

"Good weather for silver carp," Chen Fugen said warmly. "Even better weather for sitting in the sun."

"If we catch nothing, we still have Grandma's pork buns," Chen Xuan said gently.

Tang Xiaoyu looked down at the gravel path. "I have never seen a lake this big," she said softly.

"It is a reservoir, not a lake," Chen Fugen smiled. "Built before your Big Brother Chen Xuan was born. Come on, the willow bend is just ahead."

When they rounded the stand of yellowing willows, however, the gravel bend was not empty.

Two folding chairs stood beside the water's edge. On the lower chair sat Retired Governor Zhao Dehai in a faded navy windbreaker, a worn gray cap on his knee and a slender carbon rod propped on a forked branch. His posture was unhurried as he watched the faint ripple around his float.

Two paces behind him stood a young man in his late twenties wearing a neatly pressed charcoal coat, creased trousers, and polished leather shoes rimmed with damp mud. His expression held the polite, measured distance of someone used to provincial office corridors.

That was Zhao Mingyuan, Zhao Dehai's grandson, recently promoted to deputy section chief in the provincial capital.

Zhao Dehai heard footsteps on the gravel and turned. Seeing Chen Xuan and Chen Fugen, the weathered lines around his eyes softened into genuine warmth.

"Old Chen," Zhao Dehai called out, lifting a hand. "I thought the cold might keep you home this week."

"A little frost never stopped a Yunxi fisherman," Chen Fugen laughed, setting down his bucket. "Old Governor Zhao, you beat us to the best bend."

"Plenty of bank for all of us," Zhao Dehai said. His gaze lingered on Chen Xuan with quiet appreciation. "Young Chen Xuan. You look rested."

"Good morning, Grandpa Zhao," Chen Xuan said politely, inclining his head. "The water looks peaceful today."

Zhao Dehai looked past him to the thin girl in the red sweater, and his voice gentled at once. "And who is this young guest?"

"This is Tang Xiaoyu," Chen Xuan said, stepping half a pace aside. "She is staying with our family now. She studied hard all week, so I brought her out for some sun."

Tang Xiaoyu stood very straight. "Good morning, Grandpa."

"Good morning, Tang Xiaoyu," Zhao Dehai smiled. "Do not listen if Old Chen boasts about the fish here. Half of them grow three inches after he gets home."

Chen Fugen snorted as he opened his folding stools, while Tang Xiaoyu let out a small breath that was almost a laugh.

Zhao Mingyuan stepped forward with practiced courtesy, nodding respectfully to Chen Fugen and glancing kindly at Tang Xiaoyu before weighing Chen Xuan's plain gray windbreaker, scuffed sneakers, and ten-yuan stool. An ordinary county clerk, clearly, brought along to carry his grandfather's bucket.

"Grandpa, let me fetch the spare camp chair from the trunk for Old Mister Chen," Zhao Mingyuan offered smoothly.

"No need," Chen Fugen waved him off, snapping his stool open. "My back prefers wood and canvas."

* * *

Ten minutes later, a well-tuned engine hummed on the dirt turnout above the embankment, and car doors closed with a soft thud.

Qin Zhengming walked down the willow path carrying a square cedar box tied with silk cord. Behind him came Qin Yuhan in a cream knit coat and flat boots, holding a brown paper sack smelling of freshly roasted chestnuts.

Zhao Mingyuan straightened immediately. Across the province, the head of the Qin family and Haiyue Hospitality was a name that carried quiet weight in every government reception room.

"Uncle Zhao," Qin Zhengming called out warmly. "We heard you were staying at the reservoir guesthouse. I brought a cake of old Pu'er from my father's cellar."

"Qin Zhengming, you did not have to drive all the way out here," Zhao Dehai said, clearly pleased.

Qin Zhengming set the cedar box beside Zhao Dehai's chair, then turned with an unhurried, sincere nod to Chen Fugen and Chen Xuan. "Old Mister Chen. Chen Xuan. Good to see you both out in the sun."

"Good morning, Uncle Qin," Chen Xuan replied calmly, rising slightly from his stool. "Good morning, Miss Qin."

"Good morning, Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan said, her eyes curving with restrained warmth. Walking past Zhao Mingyuan, she crouched lightly beside Tang Xiaoyu's stone and held out the warm paper sack. "I bought these on the road out of town. They are still hot. Would you like some?"

Tang Xiaoyu glanced at Chen Xuan, who gave a small nod.

"Thank you, Sister," Tang Xiaoyu said carefully, taking two glossy chestnuts.

Qin Yuhan smiled, stood up, and took a natural spot half a step behind Chen Xuan's folding stool rather than returning to her father's side.

Zhao Mingyuan stepped forward to shake Qin Zhengming's hand with polished deference. "Chairman Qin, Miss Qin. I am Zhao Mingyuan, Grandfather's grandson."

"Young Zhao," Qin Zhengming said politely. "Your grandfather mentioned you are doing solid work in the provincial office."

"Still learning," Zhao Mingyuan smiled modestly, though his gaze drifted once more to Chen Xuan, who was calmly untangling Tang Xiaoyu's fishing line without a wasted motion. He could not quite understand why Qin Zhengming spoke to the plain-jacketed clerk with such careful ease, or why Qin Yuhan stood behind his stool.

* * *

Thirty meters up the slope, behind drooping willows, Colonel Ren Gang stood with his hands clasped behind his back, scanning the opposite shore with a veteran's discipline. Twenty paces left, leaning against a willow trunk with an unlit cigarette, stood Number Nine in a faded worker's coat.

Their eyes met. Both veterans recognized trained steel on sight and exchanged a silent, professional nod.

Then, in the same heartbeat, both men stiffened slightly.

Fifty meters back along the shadowed pine ridge, there was no movement and no glint of glass, yet the air carried the unmistakable, chilling weight of an unseen watch. The permanent Zero Guard team was holding its fifty-meter perimeter around Chen Xuan.

Number Nine lowered his eyes and took half a step deeper behind the willow trunk, instinctively yielding the line. Even Black Pine Guard never encroached on the Zero Guard.

Colonel Ren Gang exhaled slowly into the cold air, looked down once at Chen Xuan steadying a thin girl's bamboo rod by the water, and turned his eyes strictly toward the opposite shore.
