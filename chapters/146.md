# Chapter 146: Hengxin's Promotion Offer

At 8:55 on Monday morning, the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics felt louder than a wet market.

Phones rang along every row of desks. Two young clerks from the shipping desk hurried past with thick folders of new port orders. Ever since the Lanshui Forum had ended with Hengxin Logistics standing tall beside Qingyan's biggest trade names, new clients had been calling the front desk since seven in the morning.

Chen Xuan hung his clean gray jacket on the back of his chair at desk fourteen. He set his scratched green thermos beside his mouse pad, unscrewed the lid, and let a faint trail of jasmine steam rise into the cool morning air.

Two desks over, Zheng Qian spun his chair around so fast his coffee mug nearly tipped over.

"Chen Xuan, did you see the company group chat this morning?" Zheng Qian asked, his eyes wide and bright. "Three cold-chain importers from the south called to ask for Hengxin by name! Everyone is saying the Lanshui Forum turned our little firm into the hottest logistics name in Qingyan."

"I saw the notice on the wall downstairs," Chen Xuan said calmly. He opened his morning spreadsheet and tapped two keys. "It is good news for the year-end bonus."

Pan Xiulan leaned over the low partition with a warm smile. She wore a neat navy cardigan and held a fresh stack of customs receipts. "Good news? Brother Chen, you are too modest. General Manager Sun Haoran has been grinning since he stepped out of his car at eight o'clock. Half the fifteenth floor is talking about how you handled the port figures at Lanshui without breaking a sweat."

"I only read the numbers off the sheet," Chen Xuan replied mildly. "The dock team did the heavy lifting."

Before Pan Xiulan could argue, the glass doors at the elevator hall swung open wide.

General Manager Sun Haoran strode onto the twelfth floor in a freshly pressed dark suit, his round face shining with pride. Right behind him walked Department Manager Hu Dewei, clutching a notebook against his chest with a stiff, uneasy look.

Every keyboard on the twelfth floor paused at once. Chairs scraped back as clerks straightened up in their seats.

Sun Haoran did not stop at the front row or even glance toward Hu Dewei's corner office. He walked straight down the center aisle and stopped right in front of desk fourteen.

"Good morning, everyone," Sun Haoran called out, his voice booming with good cheer. "Keep your seats. I came down here today for one special reason."

He placed a heavy, friendly hand on the edge of Chen Xuan's desk.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran said warmly, looking down at him as if he were gazing at a block of solid gold. "At the Lanshui Forum, you saved this company's face and brought us more respect in three days than our sales team has earned in three years. Big partners from Haiyue and the port authority actually asked me who runs our operations desk."

Hu Dewei's jaw tightened beside him. A dull, sour shade crept into his cheeks as he stared at the floor.

"I am not a man who forgets hard work," Sun Haoran went on, raising his voice so every desk on the floor could hear. "I spoke with the board over the weekend, and we all agreed. Starting today, I want to promote you to Deputy General Manager of Hengxin Logistics."

A sharp collective gasp rolled across the twelfth floor.

Zheng Qian's pen slipped straight out of his fingers and clattered onto his desk. Pan Xiulan pressed both hands over her mouth, her eyes shining with sudden joy for her neighbor.

Hu Dewei's head snapped up so fast his glasses slid down his nose. His face turned a sickly shade of green. Deputy General Manager meant jumping clean over his head, straight onto the fifteenth floor.

"The pay is 50k yuan a month before bonuses," Sun Haoran added proudly, lifting his chin. "You will have a private corner office on the fifteenth floor, a company car allowance, and full say over the operations and port teams. You will report straight to me. What do you say, Chen Xuan?"

The entire twelfth floor held its breath. Fifty thousand yuan a month was more than four times what a junior supervisor made. For most young men in Qingyan, that single offer meant buying a new flat, driving a clean sedan, and never worrying about bills again.

Hu Dewei gripped his notebook until his knuckles turned white. He forced a stiff, twitching smile onto his lips, already wondering how he was going to survive with Chen Xuan sitting one floor above him.

Chen Xuan picked up his green thermos, took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea, and set it gently back on his desk.

He looked up at Sun Haoran with an easy, polite smile.

"Thank you for thinking of me, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said in a calm, even voice. "I have to turn it down."

For three full seconds, not a single person on the twelfth floor moved. Even the hum of the air conditioner sounded loud in the sudden hush.

Sun Haoran blinked twice, his proud smile freezing halfway across his face. "Turn it down? Did you hear me right, Chen Xuan? I said Deputy General Manager. Fifty thousand yuan a month."

"I heard you clearly, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan replied gently. "Deputy managers have to work weekends, General Manager Sun. I like my 12,000-yuan desk."

Zheng Qian choked on his own breath and let out a loud cough. Pan Xiulan stared at Chen Xuan as if he had just spoken in a foreign language. Across the aisle, two shipping clerks exchanged wide, stunned looks.

Hu Dewei's mouth fell open. The green envy drained from his face, replaced by total, blank disbelief.

Sun Haoran let out a short, nervous laugh and leaned closer over the partition. "Chen Xuan, you do not need to be shy in front of everyone. If you think 50k yuan is a bit low after what you did at Lanshui, we can talk inside my office. I can ask the board for a year-end share too."

"It is not about the money," Chen Xuan said simply, shaking his head with genuine courtesy. "A deputy manager has to attend evening dinners, answer phone calls on Sunday mornings, and sit through three-hour meetings. My desk here gets nice sunlight in the afternoon. I finish my spreadsheets before lunch, drink my tea, and leave at 4:55. That suits me much better."

Sun Haoran stared at him, searching his face for any sign of a joke. There was none. Chen Xuan's dark eyes were completely calm, clear, and content.

"You really would rather stay a junior operations supervisor at 12,000 yuan a month than take a private office upstairs?" Sun Haoran asked softly, half amused and half baffled.

"Yes, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said with a slight nod. "I am an ordinary worker. I like quiet days."

A quiet murmur rippled through the rows of desks. Everyone on the twelfth floor was staring at Chen Xuan like he had completely lost his mind. Who in Qingyan turned down a private office and 50k yuan a month just to protect their weekends?

Hu Dewei slowly closed his mouth. A strange wave of relief washed over his chest, quickly followed by a prickly unease. He could not decide if Chen Xuan was the biggest fool in the building or a man playing a game too deep for him to understand.

Sun Haoran stood still for a long moment. Then he slapped his own knee and let out a helpless, genuine laugh.

"Well," Sun Haoran said, shaking his head. "I have run companies for twenty years, and you are the first man to turn down a corner office because you like the sunlight at desk fourteen."

He straightened his suit jacket and turned a sharp, meaningful look toward Hu Dewei.

"The offer stays open whenever you change your mind, Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran said warmly. "And Manager Hu."

Hu Dewei jumped slightly and stood straight. "Yes, General Manager Sun!"

"Since Chen Xuan wants to stay at desk fourteen, you make sure his desk stays quiet," Sun Haoran said, his tone dropping a notch into firm authority. "No pointless overtime, no dumping other people's sloppy mistakes on him at five o'clock. Is that clear?"

"Crystal clear, General Manager Sun!" Hu Dewei said quickly, bobbing his head. "I will make sure nobody bothers him."

Sun Haoran gave Chen Xuan one last admiring pat on the shoulder, shook his head with a quiet chuckle, and walked back toward the elevators. Hu Dewei hurried right behind him, casting one bewildered glance back at desk fourteen before the glass doors closed.

The moment the elevator doors shut, the twelfth floor burst into noisy whispers.

Zheng Qian rolled his chair straight over to Chen Xuan's desk, grabbing the edge of the partition with both hands. "Brother Chen! Are you feverish? Let me feel your forehead! That was 50k yuan a month!"

"Sit down, Zheng Qian," Pan Xiulan said, though she was staring at Chen Xuan with the exact same wide-eyed amazement. "Chen Xuan, are you really sure? Even if you did not want to work weekends, you could have tried it for half a year and saved a small fortune."

"If I sat upstairs, I would miss the five o'clock bus traffic clearing out and my tea would always get cold during meetings," Chen Xuan replied smoothly. He turned back to his monitor and clicked open the morning port schedule. "Besides, Zheng Qian, your Tuesday dock sheet has a weight mismatch on line nine. Fix it before noon so you do not stay late today."

Zheng Qian blinked, looked down at his own desk, and groaned. "How did you even see my screen from over here?"

Chen Xuan took another unhurried sip of jasmine tea and smiled faintly. Outside the tall glass window, the winter sun shone bright and pale over Qingyan. Desk fourteen was quiet, warm, and exactly where he wanted to be.
