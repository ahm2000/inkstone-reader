# Chapter 128: Midnight Noodles and Article 14

Back in Three Willows Suite after dinner, a thin mist hovered over the dark water beyond the glass patio doors.

Inside the warm living room, Qin Yuhan had washed off her evening makeup and changed into a soft pale-pink silk pajama set. With her dark hair clipped up loosely and a pair of thin reading glasses perched on her nose, she sat at the wide rosewood table flipping steadily through Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen's forty-page gold-stamped bidding draft.

Five steps away in the open kitchenette, Chen Xuan wore a plain cotton apron over his t-shirt and hummed a relaxed tune. A small clay pot simmered gently on the induction stove, filling the suite with the rich, savory aroma of fresh river shrimp, sliced ginger, and toasted scallions.

Within ten minutes, he walked over carrying a wooden tray with two steaming bowls of hand-pulled noodles. Plump pink shrimp, tender green bok choy, and a perfectly poached egg rested in the clear golden broth.

"Break time, President Qin," Chen Xuan announced, setting a bowl gently beside her elbow and sliding a pair of bamboo chopsticks into her hand. "Your stomach barely got half a meal at that banquet because everyone kept walking over to toast you."

Qin Yuhan looked up from the dense legal print, her tired eyes instantly softening. "You brought fresh river shrimp from the resort kitchen?"

"One of the kitchen aunties saved the last scoop for me after dinner," Chen Xuan grinned, pulling up a chair across from her with his own bowl. "Eat while the broth is hot."

She lifted a spoonful of the golden soup and let out a quiet sigh of pure comfort. "Honestly, Chen Xuan, your midnight noodles beat every five-star hotel chef in Shanghai. If you ever leave Hengxin Logistics, you could open a noodle shop and retire in three years."

"Too much standing," Chen Xuan replied easily, blowing on his noodles. "My 12th-floor desk has a much softer chair."

As Qin Yuhan ate happily, Chen Xuan wiped his fingers clean on a napkin and reached over to turn the gold-stamped bidding draft toward himself. He flipped casually through the early sections as if he were scanning a neighborhood supermarket flyer, pausing only when his thumb stopped on page twenty-eight.

His brow lifted a fraction of an inch.

"Huh," Chen Xuan said softly, tapping the paper with one finger. "Yuhan, take a look at Article 14. Did you get to this page yet?"

Qin Yuhan set her spoon down and leaned closer, adjusting her reading glasses. "Not yet. I was still checking the berth lease schedule in Section 9. What does Article 14 say?"

"It looks pretty boring at first," Chen Xuan said in an easygoing tone, sounding just like a 12th-floor freight clerk complaining about paperwork. "It says any winning company has to route all customs clearance and container dispatch through a designated local heritage port operator for a 15-year lock-in period. On top of that, you have to deposit a 200 million yuan non-refundable performance bond within ten days of signing."

Qin Yuhan's expression sharpened immediately. She pulled the binder straight in front of her, flipped to the tiny cross-referenced appendix at the very back, and traced the lines with her pen.

"Wait a second," she murmured, her voice dropping cold. "Look at Footnote 3 in Appendix B. The designated operator charges an automatic dispatch surcharge of 300 yuan per container, with an annual adjustment clause that cannot be vetoed by the lessee."

"Three hundred yuan per container sounds small until you move half a million containers a year," Chen Xuan remarked mildly, taking another bite of his noodles. "That is 150 million yuan drained straight out of Haiyue Group every single year for fifteen years, even before you count the 200 million yuan bond. And look at the wording for who qualifies as a local heritage operator."

Qin Yuhan read the three narrow criteria listed in fine print. Her breath caught. "Registered in Jingzhou's old river district, holding a specific provincial river-dredging license issued earlier this year, and partnered with a local warehouse syndicate. That eliminates every major national shipping group in China. Only Cai Chenggong's newly reorganized company fits those exact lines."

She stared down at the gold-stamped document, a chill running down her spine. If she had signed the letter of intent tomorrow morning out of courtesy to Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen, Haiyue Group would have been legally trapped inside a massive wealth-siphoning scheme, and if the port project collapsed under the bloated fees, Haiyue Group would have taken the public blame.

Slowly, Qin Yuhan raised her eyes and stared across the table at her husband.

"Chen Xuan," she said slowly, blinking behind her glasses. "My legal assistants skimmed this draft in the hallway for twenty minutes and did not catch the cross-reference to Appendix B. How did you spot that in ninety seconds while eating noodles?"

Chen Xuan shrugged innocently and took a sip of shrimp broth. "I check freight surcharges and hidden port fees on the 12th floor every week. Whenever someone buries a fee in an appendix at the back, my eyes go straight to it. Occupational habit."

Qin Yuhan studied his calm, smiling face for two seconds, then laughed softly and shook her head. "My husband really is a hidden treasure."

While she hurried to fetch her tablet and highlight every line of Article 14, Chen Xuan stepped back to the kitchen counter to pour himself a splash of water. His single everyday smartphone rested beside the kettle, its screen lighting up briefly with a silent, encrypted text.

It was a single line from Number 1: *Young Master, the offshore shell links between Cai Chenggong and Vice Mayor Ding's brother-in-law are verified and ready if you wish us to act tonight.*

Chen Xuan glanced back at his wife, whose eyes were shining with sharp, determined energy as she typed notes onto her tablet. He smiled, tapped a short reply on his regular phone telling Number 1 to stand down and let tomorrow's forum play out on stage, and slipped the phone back into his pocket.
