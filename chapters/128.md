# Chapter 128: Tea on the Terrace

By seven thirty, the Grand Ballroom had grown warm and loud. Wine glasses clinked around every linen table, and the hum of two hundred voices bounced off the painted ceiling.

Sun Haoran had calmed down nicely after two generous plates of braised beef. A pair of friendly transport managers from the southern counties had recognized Hengxin Logistics from the Haiyue Group routes and pulled him into a lively chat about highway toll rates and winter tire prices.

Seeing that Sun Haoran was in good company, Chen Xuan gave him a quiet nod, picked up his scratched green thermos, and slipped out through the tall glass doors along the western wall.

Outside lay a wide stone terrace overlooking the Sha City River.

Cool evening air drifted up from the dark water below, carrying the faint smell of wet stone and river mist. Warm lamps lined the stone railing, leaving the far corners of the terrace in soft shadow. Forty paces away, near a neat row of trimmed boxwood hedges, the darkness shifted once and went completely still. Nobody inside the bright ballroom noticed the quiet Black Guard members keeping their silent watch within fifty meters of the terrace bench.

Chen Xuan sat down on a low wooden bench facing the river, unscrewed the lid of his green thermos, and let the warm jasmine steam rise against his face. He took a slow sip and watched a long cargo barge glide past a string of red buoys down on the water.

Two minutes later, the glass door slid open behind him.

Light footsteps clicked softly across the stone tiles. Chen Xuan did not even need to turn his head to know who it was.

"I thought I might find you hiding out here," Qin Yuhan said, her voice warm and slightly amused.

She walked over wearing a tailored dark-blue evening blazer over a crisp silk blouse, carrying a slim black leather folder and a thick white booklet tucked under her arm. She paused beside the bench and let out a long, tired breath.

"If I hear one more speech about revolutionary shipping visions tonight, I might jump into the river myself," she added with a faint smile.

"The water looks cold," Chen Xuan said mildly, shifting slightly to leave space on the bench. "Tea is warmer."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly and sat down beside him. She set her leather folder on her lap and glanced at the scratched green thermos in his hand.

"Still carrying the same trusty bottle," she teased. "I heard Chairman Song made a silly remark about it inside. My assistant told me he was rude to General Manager Sun as well."

"Chairman Song likes the sound of his own voice," Chen Xuan replied easily. He reached over to a small side table near the glass door where the hotel staff kept a stack of clean paper cups, picked up one, and poured her a steaming cup of jasmine tea. "Here. It is better than the sour white wine they are serving inside."

Qin Yuhan took the cup with both hands, letting the warmth soak into her fingers, and took a careful sip. Her shoulders relaxed a little.

"Thank you, Chen Xuan," she said softly. "You always make noisy places feel quiet."

For a few seconds, neither of them spoke. Below the terrace, the river barge blew one low horn as it passed beneath the distant bridge lights.

Then the light amusement faded from Qin Yuhan's eyes. She looked down at the white booklet resting on her lap, her fingers tracing the stiff paper cover.

"Chen Xuan," she said, lowering her voice slightly. "Can I ask you something honest? Not as a clerk from Hengxin Logistics, just as someone who notices things other people miss."

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said, taking a slow sip from his thermos.

"Haiyue Group is uneasy about tomorrow morning," Qin Yuhan admitted quietly. "My father called me twice on the train ride up here. On the surface, this Provincial Freight Data Alliance sounds clean and modern. One shared cloud system for all ports, warehouses, and trucking lines. Faster customs clearance, lower paperwork costs. That is what Deputy Governor Wu and Song Baisheng keep repeating."

She turned her head to look at him, her dark eyes sharp and troubled.

"But nothing in provincial shipping moves this fast," she said firmly. "Normally, a system linking twenty harbors and 80 billion yuan in annual cargo takes two years of public bids, safety checks, and trial runs. Wanfeng Freight announced the plan ten days ago, skipped the open bidding completely, and now Deputy Governor Wu is telling every company in the hall to sign the binding charter tomorrow morning or lose their port passes."

"That is a very sharp rush for a friendly upgrade," Chen Xuan agreed calmly.

"It gets stranger," Qin Yuhan went on, knitting her brows. "Our technical team checked Wanfeng Freight's background. Song Baisheng runs a big trucking fleet, yes, but his software office has fewer than twelve engineers. They could not build a province-wide shipping system in five years, let alone three months. Yet tonight he told reporters the whole platform is already built and ready to switch on before sunset tomorrow."

Chen Xuan listened without interrupting, his expression peaceful and attentive.

"And there is one more thing," Qin Yuhan murmured, glancing briefly back toward the bright glass doors of the ballroom. "Earlier tonight, I saw a foreign advisor standing near Song Baisheng's private table. His badge said Marcus Vance, European Logistics Advisory. He stayed in the shadows the whole time, watching everyone, and Song Baisheng's aides treated him like a prince. Something about the whole deal smells wrong, Chen Xuan. My gut tells me we are walking into a trap, yet our three senior lawyers read the draft charter this afternoon and said the fee terms looked fairly normal."

Chen Xuan set his green thermos down neatly beside his knee.

"May I see the handbook?" he asked gently.

Qin Yuhan blinked once, then handed over the thick white booklet. Across the front cover, neat dark-blue letters read: *Sha Provincial Freight Data Alliance: Draft Charter and Service Agreement*.

Chen Xuan did not bother with the glossy welcome letter on page one. He flipped straight past the colorful port maps, skipped the proud list of committee members, and passed right over the membership fee tables on page twelve.

His fingers moved steadily, unhurried, turning through the dense pages of fine print at the back as if he had read similar contracts a hundred times before.

Near the end of the booklet, he stopped.

He flattened **page 28** gently against his knee, scanned three short paragraphs with calm, clear eyes, and then tapped one quiet block of text near the bottom of the page with his index finger.

"Here," Chen Xuan said simply.

Qin Yuhan leaned closer, frowning slightly as she followed the tip of his finger.

"Article 14?" she read aloud, looking puzzled. "Cross-Border Technical Support and Backup Storage? Our legal team said that was just standard dull wording about server maintenance."

"Most people stop reading once a paragraph starts talking about server maintenance," Chen Xuan said softly, handing the open booklet back into her hands. "Read the third sentence slowly, Miss Qin. Every single word."

Qin Yuhan took the booklet and tilted page 28 toward the warm glow of the terrace lamp.

The evening breeze tugged lightly at a loose strand of hair beside her cheek. As her eyes moved across the quiet, ordinary-looking lines of Article 14, the warmth drained slowly from her face, and her dark eyes went razor-sharp.
