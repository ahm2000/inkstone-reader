# Chapter 26: The Midnight Filing

In the marble lobby of the Municipal Discipline Commission, a lone night clerk named Qian Yu sat behind a long desk with a cup of instant noodles and a crossword puzzle. For two years of night shifts, he had dropped every late complaint into a drawer to wait for Monday morning.

A man in a tailored gray suit crossed the lobby with unhurried grace. Qian Yu shot to his feet so fast that broth splashed onto his tie.

Number One Hundred and Four spoke pleasantly. "Please sit and finish your dinner. I'd hate to ruin your tie."

"No, no, sir. How can I help you?"

"I'm here to file a complaint."

He set a thin black folder on the counter with both hands and slid it forward.

"Nineteen pages, all in order. I'll need a signed receipt, an official stamp, and the exact time."

Qian Yu stopped mid-chew. Three companies were circled in red, followed by two officials.

Cheng Biao, second in command of the county police, Deputy Director of the County Police Bureau and Director of Old Street Station.

Lu Wenzhe, Executive Deputy Magistrate of Yunxi County.

Qian Yu's voice dropped to a whisper. "Sir, is this real?"

"I'm afraid it's entirely real." Number One Hundred and Four smiled in a way that made the young clerk's stomach drop. "If I were you, I'd wake someone up."

Qian Yu glanced at the wall clock: 11:44 p.m.

He reached for the phone. Whose door was he about to knock on at midnight?

* * *

Qian Yu stamped the receipt at 11:44 and woke the night duty chief. The chief read the nineteen pages in his slippers and was wide awake by page four.

At one in the morning, he called Deputy Secretary Lin of the city commission, who sent the case straight down to Yunxi. Kang Guangming, Secretary of the Yunxi County Discipline Inspection Commission, answered on the first ring, as if he had been sitting beside the phone since Friday night. He listened to the whole summary and said only, "By the book. Every page. Nothing lost."

Since the night of the matte-black cards, every official in the city had repeated one rule in low voices: do not touch anything in Yunxi. A sealed complaint with a stamped receipt was the one thing nobody dared to drop.

At three, the chief woke Mai Zhenhua, Director of the County Police Bureau, because the folder named one of Mai Zhenhua's own men. Mai Zhenhua said "Yes" eleven times in a row. Then he sat on the edge of his bed with his feet gone numb and opened the drawer of his nightstand. The black card lay face down where he had put it on Friday. He closed the drawer.

At four, Fan Weiguo, the County Party Secretary, signed Cheng Biao's suspension with a hand that would not stay still. Tao Shu, the County Magistrate, signed after him, and the city opened a case against Dong Haisheng. Neither asked who had sent the folder. Neither wanted to know.

Lu Wenzhe's name was in the folder too. But a deputy magistrate answered to the Standing Committee, which would meet at 2:30 that afternoon.

At midnight, Number Twenty-three had delivered beef noodles with an extra egg and a gray blanket to the family window. Chen Mingde had eaten every last strand, his eyes brimming as he finished the egg.

Two streets away, Number Ninety-five, under cover as head nurse, happened to run into Su Min, Wei Kun's wife. By dawn, a free hospital bed was waiting for her.

At five in the morning, Chen Xuan walked to the police station alone with his thermos and sat on the front steps. He could not sleep, and the steps felt closer to his uncle than his bed. His mother would arrive at 6:35 with soup.

* * *

Inside, Lei Bo slept on the reception bench with his cap pulled over his face.

Wei Kun had not slept at all. He sat behind the glass with a cold cup of tea and one honest detention notice, waiting for the storm to break.

A plain gray sedan pulled up to the steps with no flag and no siren. The man who stepped out was short and stocky, in a dark jacket.

At the glass door, he stopped and checked the dark corner behind it, the way a man does after a bad night. Only then did he walk in.

From his stool in the corner, Number Thirty-four, the records clerk, looked up, and his stapler froze mid-press. "Director Mai," he said softly.

The words landed like a fire alarm. Lei Bo tumbled off the bench and scrambled upright into a salute.

"Where is Director Cheng?"

Cheng Biao hurried out of the back corridor in his shirtsleeves, pulling on his jacket with a toothpick still between his teeth.

"Director Mai! You're here early. If I'd known, I would have waited at the gate."

"Is the Chen Mingde case yours?"

"Just a minor scuffle, Director. A driver, an old shopkeeper, a few bruises."

Mai Zhenhua pulled a slip of paper from his pocket.

"The driver's injury note is from Ankang Private Clinic. It's stamped 1:50. The street camera shows he fell at 2:03. Tell me, Director Cheng, how does a man get treated thirteen minutes before he falls?"

The lobby went so quiet that the hum of the vending machine sounded rude.

Cheng Biao swallowed. "Maybe the clocks didn't match, Director."

"The county Party Committee and the city Discipline Commission have made their decision. You are suspended from every post, effective immediately. Hand over your ID and your keys."

The toothpick dropped from Cheng Biao's mouth and rolled to the toe of Mai Zhenhua's shoe.

Cheng Biao turned hoarsely. "Lei Bo. Tell the Director who runs this station."

Lei Bo stared at the ceiling, then at his own cap. "Director Cheng?" He looked honestly puzzled. "I hardly know him!"

Cheng Biao dropped his keys into Mai Zhenhua's palm. Two city officers stepped forward and walked him toward the door. In the back corridor, Instructor Qiu Guoqiang, whom Cheng Biao had pushed aside for a year, watched in silence.

Outside on the front steps sat a young man in worn sneakers, holding a green thermos on his knees.

Chen Xuan shifted his legs to let the officers pass. He did not stand, and he did not speak.

Cheng Biao's footsteps faltered. He stared at the ordinary clerk in the early light, and a cold suspicion flared in his chest.

The officers nudged him forward. Passing Chen Xuan, Cheng Biao leaned down and hissed, "Don't celebrate yet, clerk. Dong Haisheng has your father's stairs, your mother's six o'clock walk to the market, and the back road your cousin takes in Wuhan. I'm not the one you should worry about."

Chen Xuan looked up at him. His voice was low and even.

"I'm not celebrating. I'm waiting for my uncle." He did not blink. "The accident report on Zhongshan Road, the one you signed. It is being pulled from the archive today."

Cheng Biao opened his mouth, and nothing came out. The officers pulled him on.

Inside, Mai Zhenhua called out, "Wei Kun."

Wei Kun stepped out from behind the glass and stood at attention. "Yes, Director."

"Bring me the key to the detention cell."

Wei Kun's knees nearly gave out. He had braced himself for punishment, not trust.
