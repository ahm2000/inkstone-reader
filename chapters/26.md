# Chapter 26: Sunday Dinner Without Second Uncle

By six Sunday evening, the folding table in Building 2 of Fuxing New Village held three dishes and a clay pot of ginger and pork rib soup. Outside the sixth-floor window, dusk settled over Yunxi County.

Liu Meifang set the pot down, then placed an extra white bowl and chopsticks at the empty spot facing the window.

"Second Brother likes sitting near the breeze for hot soup," Liu Meifang said quietly. "Even if he is not back tonight, his seat stays ready."

Beside the table, Wu Lanying gripped a wooden stool until her knuckles turned white. Two sleepless nights had left dark shadows under her eyes. Feeling her children watching, she forced a steady smile.

"You did not need to cook so much, Sister-in-law," Wu Lanying murmured. "We have eaten here since Friday."

"Do not talk nonsense," Liu Meifang replied, nudging her toward the bench. "Sit before it cools. Chen Rui, Chen Lin, pick up your bowls."

Fourteen-year-old Chen Rui sat stiff as a board. He knew the silence around his father's hardware shop on Old Street was no ordinary delay. Glancing at his mother's tight jaw, he searched Chen Xuan's face for any crack in that calm.

"Brother Xuan," Chen Rui asked softly, "is Dad really just helping police check paperwork like Mom said?"

Wu Lanying's hand froze over the serving spoon. Chen Jianguo dropped his eyes to his bowl.

Chen Xuan did not flinch. Placing braised greens into Chen Rui's bowl, he met the boy's tense gaze evenly. "Some people on Old Street tried a dirty trick over the shop," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Your dad did nothing wrong. He is coming home tomorrow."

Chen Rui stared for two seconds before his shoulders eased. "All right," he muttered, picking up his chopsticks.

Beside him, younger Chen Lin swung her legs and stared at the empty bowl. "Mom, will Dad definitely come home tomorrow afternoon? My bicycle chain still clicks by the school gate. Dad promised he would oil it and tighten the nut Monday."

Wu Lanying placed the tenderest rib into Chen Lin's bowl, steadying her trembling wrist. "Of course he will," she said softly. "Your dad never breaks a promise about your bicycle. Eat your greens."

"Brother Xuan, can you fix bicycle chains?" Chen Lin asked.

"Your dad is much better at it," Chen Xuan smiled, sliding warm water beside Wu Lanying. "Let him handle it tomorrow."

Wu Lanying looked up, eyes bright with held-back tears, and took a quiet sip to hide her shaking fingers.

At the table's head, Chen Jianguo barely touched his rice. On Friday night, before Chen Xuan arrived, Chen Jianguo had hurried to Old Street Station only to be brushed off by officers who refused to let him see his brother or leave a jacket.

He had listened to Chen Xuan all weekend. Now Monday morning was hours away.

Chen Jianguo cleared his throat softly so Chen Lin would not hear his dread. "Chen Xuan," he said across the steam, "tomorrow is Monday. Offices open at eight. I have been thinking all afternoon."

Liu Meifang froze. "Jianguo, let the children finish eating."

"Let me finish, Meifang," Chen Jianguo murmured, his weathered face lined with fifty years of avoiding trouble with uniforms. He looked at his son. "I listened all weekend. Still, your Second Uncle has been locked inside over forty-eight hours with no word."

"Dad," Chen Xuan said quietly, waiting.

Chen Jianguo swallowed hard, palms pressed to the table. "First thing tomorrow, I should go down myself. I can buy two cartons of good cigarettes, wait outside the gate, and beg the station chief or Deng Yongchang's people. Even if they want the hardware shop lease, we can sign it over. A shop is bricks and iron. Your Second Uncle is flesh and blood."

Silence dropped like a stone.

Wu Lanying's breath caught, chin trembling. "Big Brother, you cannot do that," she whispered. "You are over fifty. If you beg Deng Yongchang's men outside that gate, Mingde will never forgive himself."

"Pride does not unlock a cell door, Lanying," Chen Jianguo said hoarsely. "In Yunxi County, ordinary people do not beat Hongrui Real Estate or the men eating dinner with Deng Yongchang. If signing a lease brings Mingde out whole, I will bow ten times and call it a bargain."

Wu Lanying blinked hard to hold back tears. Chen Rui gripped his chopsticks white-knuckled, eyes burning with helpless anger.

Chen Xuan did not cut in.

When the room fell quiet, he set his chopsticks across his bowl, lifted a full ladle of hot ginger and radish soup, and poured it into Chen Jianguo's bowl without spilling a drop.

Only then did Chen Xuan meet his father's worried eyes with calm certainty heavier than iron.

"Tomorrow is Monday, Dad," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Second Uncle will walk out the front door with his name clean. Nobody in our family kneels to anyone."

For a full breath, nobody moved.

Chen Jianguo searched his son's face for bragging. For years he had seen Chen Xuan as a quiet clerk working a desk job in Shanghai. Yet looking into those dark, steady eyes, Chen Jianguo felt the cold knot in his chest loosen all at once.

"Tell me the truth," Chen Jianguo whispered. "Are you only saying this to stop an old man from doing something foolish?"

"I never lie to you, Dad," Chen Xuan replied steadily. "Stay home tomorrow, drink your tea, and wait. Nobody is signing away Second Uncle's shop, and nobody is buying cigarettes for Deng Yongchang's people."

Liu Meifang rested her hand on her husband's wrist. "Listen to Chen Xuan," she murmured. "When has he spoken like this without being sure?"

Chen Jianguo exhaled deeply. Picking up his spoon, he saw his hand no longer shook. "All right," he said quietly. "I will stay home tomorrow morning. I will listen to you."

Across the table, Wu Lanying blinked fast, blaming the soup steam, and sipped slowly. For the first time since Friday, the hot broth tasted warm all the way down.

After dinner, Chen Xuan washed dishes beside Liu Meifang and helped settle Chen Rui and Chen Lin into the spare room. By eleven, the apartment sank into quiet sleep.
