# Chapter 26: Sunday Dinner Without Second Uncle

By six o'clock on Sunday evening, the folding table in Building 2 of Fuxing New Village held three home-cooked dishes and a clay pot of ginger and pork rib soup. Outside the sixth-floor window, dusk had settled over Yunxi County.

Liu Meifang set the hot pot on a cork pad, then brought an extra white bowl and bamboo chopsticks from the cupboard. She wiped the clean rim once and placed them at the empty spot facing the window.

"Second Brother likes sitting near the breeze when he drinks hot soup," Liu Meifang said quietly. "Even if he is not back tonight, his seat stays ready."

Beside the table, Wu Lanying gripped a wooden stool until her knuckles turned white. Two sleepless nights had left dark shadows under her eyes, and her lips stayed pressed into a tight line. When she felt her children watching her, she forced her shoulders down and shaped a steady smile.

"You did not need to cook so much, Sister-in-law," Wu Lanying murmured. "We have been eating at your table since Friday."

"Do not talk nonsense," Liu Meifang replied, nudging her gently toward the bench. "Sit down before the soup cools. Chen Rui, Chen Lin, pick up your bowls."

Fourteen-year-old Chen Rui sat down with his back stiff as a board. He knew the silence around his father's hardware shop on Old Street was no ordinary delay. He glanced at his mother's tight jaw, then looked across the table at Chen Xuan, searching his older cousin's face for any crack in that calm.

"Brother Xuan," Chen Rui asked suddenly, his voice low, "is Dad really just helping the police check paperwork like Mom said?"

Wu Lanying's hand froze over the serving spoon. Chen Jianguo dropped his eyes sharply to his rice bowl.

Chen Xuan did not flinch. He placed braised greens into Chen Rui's bowl and met the boy's tense gaze evenly. "Some people on Old Street tried a dirty trick over the shop," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Your dad did nothing wrong. He is coming home tomorrow."

Chen Rui stared at him for two seconds before the stiff line of his shoulders eased. "All right," he muttered, picking up his chopsticks.

Beside him, younger Chen Lin swung her legs under the bench and stared at the empty white bowl. "Mom, will Dad definitely come home tomorrow afternoon?" she asked earnestly. "My bicycle chain still clicks by the school gate. Dad promised he would oil it and tighten the back nut on Monday."

Wu Lanying placed the tenderest rib into Chen Lin's bowl. Her chopsticks shook by a hair before she steadied her wrist. "Of course he will," she said softly. "Your dad never breaks a promise about your bicycle. Eat your greens too."

"Brother Xuan, can you fix bicycle chains?" Chen Lin asked.

"Your dad is much better at it than I am," Chen Xuan answered with an easy smile, sliding half a cup of warm water beside Wu Lanying's elbow. "Let him handle it tomorrow."

Wu Lanying looked up, her eyes bright with tears she refused to let fall in front of the children, and took a quiet sip to hide the shake in her fingers.

At the head of the table, Chen Jianguo had barely touched his rice. His rough hands lay flat beside his bowl. On Friday night, before Chen Xuan arrived in Yunxi County, Chen Jianguo had hurried to Old Street Station only to be brushed off by officers who refused to let him see his younger brother or even leave a clean jacket.

He had listened to Chen Xuan all weekend, staying away from the station. Now Monday morning was only hours away.

Chen Jianguo cleared his throat softly so Chen Lin would not hear the dread in his voice. "Chen Xuan," he said across the rising steam, "tomorrow is Monday. All the offices open at eight. I have been thinking all afternoon."

Liu Meifang froze with her chopsticks in midair. "Jianguo, let the children finish eating first."

"Let me finish, Meifang," Chen Jianguo murmured, his weathered face lined with fifty years of avoiding trouble with anyone in a uniform. He looked at his son. "I listened to you all weekend. Still, your Second Uncle has been locked inside for more than forty-eight hours with no word."

"Dad," Chen Xuan said quietly, waiting.

Chen Jianguo swallowed hard, pressing his palms to the table edge. "First thing tomorrow, I should go down there myself. I can buy two cartons of good cigarettes, wait outside the gate, and beg the station chief or Deng Yongchang's people for a way out. Even if they want the hardware shop lease, we can sign it over. A shop is just bricks and iron. Your Second Uncle is flesh and blood."

Silence dropped over the table like a stone.

Wu Lanying's breath caught. She set her chopsticks down, her chin trembling. "Big Brother, you cannot do that," she whispered past a tight throat. "You are over fifty. If you beg Deng Yongchang's men outside that gate, Mingde will never forgive himself."

"Pride does not unlock a cell door, Lanying," Chen Jianguo said hoarsely. "In Yunxi County, ordinary people do not win against Hongrui Real Estate or the men who eat dinner with Deng Yongchang. If signing away a lease brings Mingde out whole, I will bow ten times and call it a bargain."

Wu Lanying lowered her eyes and blinked hard so no tear would fall. Chen Rui gripped his chopsticks until his knuckles turned white, his dark eyes burning with helpless anger.

Chen Xuan did not cut in.

When the room fell quiet, he set his chopsticks neatly across his bowl, picked up the wooden-handled ladle, and stirred the ginger and radish soup once. He lifted a full ladle of hot broth with two slices of radish and poured it slowly into Chen Jianguo's bowl without spilling a single drop.

Only then did Chen Xuan meet his father's worried eyes with a calm certainty heavier than iron.

"Tomorrow is Monday, Dad," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Second Uncle will walk out the front door with his name completely clean. Nobody in our family kneels to anyone."

For a full breath, nobody moved.

Chen Jianguo searched his son's face for youthful bragging. For years he had seen Chen Xuan as a quiet clerk working an ordinary desk job in Qingyan. Yet looking into those dark, steady eyes, Chen Jianguo felt the cold knot in his chest loosen all at once.

"Tell me the truth," Chen Jianguo asked barely above a whisper. "Are you only saying this to stop an old man from doing something foolish tomorrow?"

"I never lie to you, Dad," Chen Xuan replied steadily. "Stay home tomorrow, drink your tea, and wait. Nobody is signing away Second Uncle's shop, and nobody is buying cigarettes for Deng Yongchang's people."

Liu Meifang rested her hand over her husband's wrist. "Listen to Chen Xuan," she murmured. "When has he ever spoken like this without being sure?"

Chen Jianguo let out a long breath. Picking up his spoon, he saw his hand no longer shook. "All right," he said quietly. "I will stay home tomorrow morning. I will listen to you."

Across the table, Wu Lanying blinked fast, pretending the soup steam had drifted into her eyes, and took a slow sip. For the first time since Friday, the hot broth tasted warm all the way down.

After dinner, Chen Xuan washed the dishes beside Liu Meifang and helped Wu Lanying settle Chen Rui and Chen Lin into the spare room. By eleven o'clock, the apartment had sunk into tired, quiet sleep.
