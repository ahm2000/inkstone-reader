# Chapter 110: Gossip on the Twelfth Floor

Monday morning arrived with Lujiazui's wide avenues gleaming freshly washed after the overnight rain, wet autumn leaves clinging to the granite curbs.

Since Qin Yuhan was heading straight to Hongqiao Airport to see her parents off on their flight back to Beijing, she had refused to let Chen Xuan ride Metro Line 2. Instead, her sleek black Maybach glided smoothly to a halt at the street corner one block south of Hengxin Logistics.

Inside the quiet cabin, Chen Xuan gathered his faded canvas bag, his scratched green enamel thermos, and a warm paper bag holding two steamed red-bean buns he had bought from a corner stall near Emerald Riverside Villa.

"Why do you always make me stop a block away?" Qin Yuhan asked from behind the wheel, wearing a tailored charcoal blazer and the mother-of-pearl wristwatch he had given her at Plaza 66. "If I pull up right in front of your building lobby, will your green thermos lose its hard-earned reputation?"

"Exactly," Chen Xuan said seriously, reaching over to place one of the warm red-bean buns into the console tray beside her. "My coworkers already think I live a peaceful, modest life. If they see me stepping out of a Maybach, they will assume I won the lottery and make me buy milk tea for the whole department every afternoon. Eat that bun before you get on the elevated highway."

Qin Yuhan glanced at the steaming red-bean bun, her expression softening. "Alright, Mr. Low-Key. Have a quiet day at the office. I will text you once my parents clear security."

"Drive carefully," Chen Xuan said with a wave before stepping out onto the freshly rinsed sidewalk.

He watched the black Maybach merge smoothly into morning traffic, then tucked the remaining bun into his bag and strolled the last hundred meters to Hengxin Logistics. Riding the crowded elevator up to the twelfth floor, he swiped his employee badge at 8:55 AM, five minutes ahead of the morning bell.

The moment he stepped into the open logistics office, he noticed an unusual buzz of excitement hanging in the air. Instead of nursing their Monday coffee in silence, four or five coworkers had gathered near the water dispenser, whispering eagerly over their phones.

"Chen Xuan, you are just in time!" called out Xiao Lin, a lively young clerk from the next row of desks. Her eyes were wide with enthusiasm. "Did you see the local forum posts from this weekend? Shanghai is going crazy over two huge rumors!"

Chen Xuan walked over to his desk, set down his canvas bag, and unscrewed the lid of his green enamel thermos. Steam scented with jasmine tea curled gently into the cool office air. "Good morning, Xiao Lin. What rumors? Did the canteen finally improve the beef noodles?"

Old Zhao, a veteran dispatcher with graying temples, laughed loudly from behind his monitor. "Forget the canteen! Xiao Lin has been talking nonstop for twenty minutes. Tell him the Plaza 66 story first."

"Listen to this," Xiao Lin said, leaning on the divider beside Chen Xuan's desk. "On Saturday afternoon, someone at a luxury menswear salon in Plaza 66 saw a young man dressed in plain sneakers pull out a solid matte-white card with no bank logo. The moment the regional director saw it, he practically bowed to the floor and chased out some arrogant developer's son on the spot!"

Chen Xuan took a calm sip of his jasmine tea. "Sounds dramatic. Maybe the man was just a very polite customer."

"You never get excited about anything," Xiao Lin teased, waving her phone. "Wait until you hear the second rumor! My cousin works in an accounting firm in the building across from Haiyue Tower. She swears the frosty, untouchable President Qin Yuhan is actually married! Nobody has seen her husband's face, though people say he is just a simple guy who does not even drive a luxury car."

Old Zhao whistled softly, shaking his head in disbelief. "President Qin of Haiyue Group? Married to a regular office worker? Come on, Xiao Lin, that sounds like a television drama. Every rich heir in Lujiazui has tried to ask her out for dinner and got turned down cold."

"My cousin swears she saw a brand-new Swiss watch on President Qin's wrist, and half the building is whispering that she just got married!" Xiao Lin insisted. "Just imagine what kind of man could win over someone like that without showing off a single cent. He must have incredible charm."

"Or maybe he just cooks well and clocks out on time," Chen Xuan offered mildly, tapping his keyboard to open Monday's shipping manifest.

Old Zhao chuckled and patted Chen Xuan's shoulder. "Now that sounds like you, Xuan! If cooking good dumplings and punching out at 4:55 PM was all it took, half the guys in this department would be married to billionaire presidents by now."

Just then, the glass door of the manager's office swung open with a sharp click. Hu Dewei stepped out holding a thick stack of autumn freight schedules, his narrow face pinched into its usual sour frown.

"Alright, break it up!" Manager Hu snapped, tapping the folder loudly against his palm. "Do I pay you people to stand around the water dispenser trading celebrity gossip? The southern route port clearance lists just came through, and every manifest needs to be verified before noon. Get back to your terminals!"

Xiao Lin scurried back to her chair with a sheepish grin, and Old Zhao turned promptly back to his dual monitors. Hu Dewei walked slowly down the aisle, stopping beside Chen Xuan's desk with his eyes narrowed, clearly hoping to catch an unfiled bill of lading or a late log he could lecture him about.

Instead, Friday's shipping logs were already sorted, labeled, and stacked cleanly on the corner of the blotter.

Hu Dewei's jaw tightened slightly as he found nothing to nitpick. He gave a stiff, begrudging grunt. "Keep your eyes on the new southern manifests, Chen Xuan. Do not slack off just because Friday's paperwork was clean."

"Understood, Manager Hu," Chen Xuan replied politely without looking up from his screen.

Once Hu Dewei stalked back into his glass office, the department settled back into the familiar, rhythmic clatter of keyboards and ringing desk phones. Chen Xuan leaned back comfortably in his slightly creaky swivel chair, took another slow sip from his dented green enamel thermos, and smiled faintly at his glowing spreadsheet. Three feet away, his coworkers were still trading hushed guesses about the mysterious man with the white card and Haiyue Group's hidden son-in-law, never once suspecting that both men were quietly checking container numbers right beside them.
