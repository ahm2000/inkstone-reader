# Chapter 110: The General's Granddaughter

Evening rain misted softly over the gray roof tiles of the old Qin family courtyard in western Qingyan.

Inside the warm dining room stood a plain round rosewood table under a soft yellow lamp, set with four simple home-cooked dishes: steamed river perch with ginger, stir-fried greens, braised tofu, and a clay pot of winter melon soup.

Qin Zhengming sat in a plain dark blue cotton jacket, setting down his folded evening newspaper. At fifty-four, he was an upright provincial official known across Qingyan for keeping clean books and refusing every private banquet invitation. Around his daughter, his strict official posture softened into easy warmth.

"Eat while the fish is hot, Yuhan," Qin Zhengming said, placing a clean piece of perch into his daughter's bowl. "You look like you had a busy day down at the docks."

Qin Yuhan smiled, setting her soup spoon down. She had changed out of her gray wool coat into a soft cream sweater.

"Not busy at all, Dad," Qin Yuhan said lightly. "Gate Seven cleared two hours faster than expected. Our surgical scanners are already sitting safely in the Haiyue warehouse."

Qin Zhengming paused mid-bite, his eyebrows lifting slightly. "Gate Seven? That is interesting. Before I left the provincial office at six, I saw an urgent note from the Customs Anti-Smuggling Bureau. Shen Guohua swept Gate Seven this morning, froze a shipping broker's license on the spot, and the discipline team pulled Deputy Director Zhou Desheng out of his office."

"I saw the whole thing from ten meters away," Qin Yuhan said with a quiet smile.

"Did that broker give Haiyue Group trouble?" Qin Zhengming asked, his voice sharpening.

"He tried to flatter me into skipping the queue," Qin Yuhan answered calmly. "When I refused, he took his temper out on the man standing right in front of me. A clerk from a small firm called Hengxin Logistics."

Qin Zhengming leaned back slightly, his interest caught. "Tell me about it."

"He looked completely ordinary," Qin Yuhan said, lifting her cup of warm tea. "A faded dark windbreaker, worn sneakers, and an old scratched green thermos. The broker, Zhou Mingcheng, mocked his clothes, demanded a 200k fast-lane fee, and then parked a van sideways across the weighbridge to trap Hengxin's truck."

"Disgraceful," Qin Zhengming frowned. "Those port sharks have grown far too bold lately. What did the Hengxin clerk do? Call the police?"

"No," Qin Yuhan said, her eyes crinkling as she remembered the scene. "He did not shout, did not lose his temper, and did not even look annoyed. Inside the hall, he quoted Joint Notice Nineteen word for word so calmly that the window clerk's hands shook. Outside at the scale, he took one sip of tea from his green thermos and sent a short text. Exactly five minutes later, Chief Shen Guohua walked straight out of the main building, froze Zhou Mingcheng's company, and personally waved Hengxin's truck through."

Qin Zhengming set his chopsticks down slowly. "Five minutes? Shen Guohua was transferred in two days ago. He is a hard official who does not move on random rumors."

"I thought so too," Qin Yuhan admitted. "And before Chief Shen turned away, he looked at that Hengxin clerk for a fraction of a second. Dad, you know that look. I grew up seeing young colonels look at Grandpa the same way."

Quiet settled over the dining table as rain tapped against the courtyard tree. Qin Zhengming thought of the sudden clean-up sweeping through Qingyan all week. Corrupt builders had fallen overnight, and every clean official felt a quiet wind clearing away years of rot.

"What was his name?" Qin Zhengming asked softly.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan answered. "We shared beef noodles outside Gate Seven afterward. I tested him a little, just to see if he would brag. He did not blink once. He blamed it on reading the wall notice and sending a tip to the harbor line, then paid for my noodles and left to catch the bus."

Qin Zhengming let out a warm chuckle. "A young man who can move a customs chief in five minutes, yet rides the bus and drinks from a scratched green thermos. If your grandfather, General Qin Zhengguo, heard that story, he would tell you to invite him for tea."

"I gave him my personal number," Qin Yuhan said easily. "Though I think he genuinely enjoys his quiet twelve-thousand-yuan desk job."

"Do not pry into his background, Yuhan," Qin Zhengming murmured. "People like that choose a quiet life for a reason. Treat him like a normal friend."

"I plan to," Qin Yuhan smiled.

Across town, on the top floor of a quiet gray office building overlooking the rainy Qingyan River, a single warm lamp lit a sparse wooden study.

Number 1 sat in a plain armchair beside the tall rain-streaked window, wearing a dark jacket over a black shirt. Between his fingers burned a half-smoked cigarette, a thin ribbon of smoke drifting calmly toward the ceiling.

Standing straight beside the wooden desk with a thin black folder was Number 7.

"Gate Seven is settled," Number 7 reported in a crisp voice. "Number 14's Qingyan desk passed the file directly to Shen Guohua's office at ten twenty-three. By ten twenty-eight, Mingcheng Freight Services was frozen, Zhou Mingcheng was in custody, and Deputy Director Zhou Desheng was pulled in by the discipline team. Hengxin Logistics' truck cleared on time, and Hu Dewei never noticed a thing."

Number 1 nodded once, tapping ash neatly into a glass ashtray on the windowsill. "Clean work. Anything else?"

"One small matter outside the usual scope," Number 7 said, opening the folder slightly. "At Gate Seven, Chen Xuan met Qin Yuhan, supply director of Haiyue Group. Afterward, she sat with him at a roadside noodle stall outside the port for twenty minutes and gave him her personal phone number."

Number 1 paused, the glowing tip of his cigarette hovering in the quiet room. "Qin Yuhan?"

"Twenty-seven," Number 7 answered crisply. "Daughter of provincial official Qin Zhengming. Granddaughter of four-star General Qin Zhengguo. Her background is completely clean, her company records are spotless, and she has no ties to anyone on our watchlists. She simply noticed Shen Guohua's reaction at the gate."

Number 7 closed the folder and waited. "The close-protection team from Black Guard held fifty meters the entire time and flagged no threat. Should I assign someone to watch Haiyue Group, or quietly steer her away from Hengxin Logistics?"

Number 1 took a slow draw from his cigarette and exhaled softly toward the dark glass, watching the distant harbor lights blur in the rain.

"General Qin Zhengguo is a good old soldier," Number 1 said calmly. "His son Qin Zhengming keeps clean hands, and his granddaughter has sharp eyes. None of them are enemies."

He stubbed the cigarette out cleanly in the glass tray and looked up at Number 7 with a faint, rare trace of warmth.

"Chen Xuan spent too many years sitting alone above everything," Number 1 added quietly. "Now he just wants a steady workday, hot tea, and a normal life in Qingyan. Do not interfere. Let them talk like ordinary people."

Number 7 gave a single sharp nod. "Understood."

Number 7 stepped quietly out and pulled the door shut without a sound. Alone by the window, Number 1 looked out across the rain-washed city toward the quiet streets where Chen Xuan's small apartment glowed with one ordinary light, and let the night stay peaceful.
