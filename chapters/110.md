# Chapter 110: The General's Granddaughter

Evening rain misted over the gray tiles of the old Qin family courtyard in western Shanghai.

Inside the warm dining room, a round rosewood table sat set with four home-cooked dishes: steamed river perch, stir-fried greens, braised tofu, and winter melon soup.

Qin Zhengming set down his newspaper in a dark blue cotton jacket. At fifty-four, he was an upright provincial official known across Shanghai for clean books and refusing private banquets. Around his daughter, his strict posture softened into warmth.

"Eat while the fish is hot, Yuhan," Qin Zhengming said, placing perch into her bowl. "Busy day at the docks?"

Qin Yuhan smiled, now in a cream sweater. "Not at all, Dad. Gate Seven cleared two hours early. Our surgical scanners are already safe in the Haiyue warehouse."

Qin Zhengming paused mid-bite, eyebrows lifting. "Gate Seven? Before I left the provincial office at six, I saw an urgent note from the Customs Anti-Smuggling Bureau. Shen Guohua swept Gate Seven this morning, froze a shipping broker's license, and the discipline team pulled Deputy Director Zhou Desheng from his office."

"I saw the whole thing from ten meters away," Qin Yuhan said with a quiet smile.

"Did that broker give Haiyue Group trouble?" Qin Zhengming asked, his voice sharpening.

"He tried to flatter me into skipping the queue," Qin Yuhan answered calmly. "When I refused, he took his temper out on the man in front of me. A clerk from a small firm called Hengxin Logistics."

Qin Zhengming leaned back, his interest caught. "Tell me about it."

"He looked completely ordinary," Qin Yuhan said, lifting her teacup. "A faded dark windbreaker, worn sneakers, and a scratched green thermos. The broker, Zhou Mingcheng, mocked his clothes, demanded a 200k fast-lane fee, and parked a van sideways across the weighbridge to trap Hengxin's truck."

"Disgraceful," Qin Zhengming frowned. "Those port sharks have grown far too bold. What did the Hengxin clerk do? Call the police?"

"No," Qin Yuhan said, her eyes crinkling. "He did not shout, lose his temper, or even look annoyed. Inside the hall, he quoted Joint Notice Nineteen word for word so calmly that the window clerk's hands shook. Outside at the scale, he took one sip of tea from his green thermos and sent a short text. Five minutes later, Chief Shen Guohua walked straight out of the main building, froze Zhou Mingcheng's company, and personally waved Hengxin's truck through."

Qin Zhengming set his chopsticks down slowly. "Five minutes? Shen Guohua was transferred in two days ago. He is a hard official who does not move on random rumors."

"I thought so too," Qin Yuhan admitted. "And before Chief Shen turned away, he looked at that Hengxin clerk for a fraction of a second. Dad, you know that look. I grew up seeing young colonels look at Grandpa the same way."

Quiet settled over the table as rain tapped against the courtyard tree. Qin Zhengming thought of the sudden clean-up sweeping Shanghai all week, where corrupt builders had fallen overnight and clean officials felt a quiet wind clearing away years of rot.

"What was his name?" Qin Zhengming asked softly.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan answered. "We shared beef noodles outside Gate Seven afterward. I tested him a little to see if he would brag. He did not blink once. He blamed it on reading the wall notice and sending a tip to the harbor line, then paid for my noodles and left to catch the bus."

Qin Zhengming let out a warm chuckle. "A young man who moves a customs chief in five minutes, yet rides the bus and drinks from a scratched green thermos. If your grandfather, General Qin Zhengguo, heard that story, he would tell you to invite him for tea."

"I gave him my personal number," Qin Yuhan said easily. "Though I think he genuinely enjoys his quiet twelve-thousand-yuan desk job."

"Do not pry into his background, Yuhan," Qin Zhengming murmured. "People like that choose a quiet life for a reason. Treat him like a normal friend."

"I plan to," Qin Yuhan smiled.

Across town, on the top floor of a quiet gray office building overlooking the rainy Shanghai River, a single lamp lit a sparse wooden study.

Number 1 sat in a plain armchair beside the rain-streaked window, wearing a dark jacket over a black shirt. A half-smoked cigarette burned between his fingers, smoke drifting toward the ceiling.

Standing straight beside the desk with a thin black folder was Number 7.

"Gate Seven is settled," Number 7 reported crisply. "Number 14's Shanghai desk passed the file to Shen Guohua's office at ten twenty-three. By ten twenty-eight, Mingcheng Freight Services was frozen, Zhou Mingcheng was in custody, and Deputy Director Zhou Desheng was pulled in by the discipline team. Hengxin Logistics' truck cleared on time, and Hu Dewei never noticed a thing."

Number 1 nodded, tapping ash into a glass tray on the windowsill. "Clean work. Anything else?"

"One small matter outside the usual scope," Number 7 said, opening the folder. "At Gate Seven, Chen Xuan met Qin Yuhan, supply director of Haiyue Group. Afterward, she sat with him at a roadside noodle stall for twenty minutes and gave him her personal phone number."

Number 1 paused, the glowing cigarette hovering in the quiet room. "Qin Yuhan?"

"Twenty-seven," Number 7 answered crisply. "Daughter of provincial official Qin Zhengming. Granddaughter of four-star General Qin Zhengguo. Her background is completely clean, her company records are spotless, and she has no ties to anyone on our watchlists. She simply noticed Shen Guohua's reaction at the gate."

Number 7 closed the folder and waited. "The close-protection team from Black Guard held fifty meters the entire time and flagged no threat. Should I assign someone to watch Haiyue Group, or quietly steer her away from Hengxin Logistics?"

Number 1 took a slow draw and exhaled toward the dark glass, watching distant harbor lights blur in the rain.

"General Qin Zhengguo is a good old soldier," Number 1 said calmly. "His son Qin Zhengming keeps clean hands, and his granddaughter has sharp eyes. None of them are enemies."

He stubbed the cigarette out cleanly and looked up at Number 7 with a rare trace of warmth.

"Chen Xuan spent too many years sitting alone above everything," Number 1 added quietly. "Now he just wants a steady workday, hot tea, and a normal life in Shanghai. Do not interfere. Let them talk like ordinary people."

Number 7 gave a sharp nod. "Understood."

Number 7 stepped out and closed the door without a sound. Alone by the window, Number 1 looked across the rain-washed city toward the quiet streets where Chen Xuan's small apartment glowed with one ordinary light, and let the night stay peaceful.
