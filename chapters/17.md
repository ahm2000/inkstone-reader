# Chapter 17: County Magistrate Sun Weiming's Tea Room

Two hundred meters east of the Yunxi County Government Compound, tucked behind a high gray-brick wall with no signboard above its lacquered doors, stood a private courtyard tea house reserved for a handful of local officials and contractors. Inside the rear pavilion, pale Saturday sunlight slanted across a polished rosewood table, catching the thin steam rising from two celadon cups.

County Magistrate Sun Weiming tilted the small clay teapot with a practiced flick of his wrist, guiding a pale green stream of pre-Qingming Longjing tea into the cup opposite him. At fifty-two, Sun Weiming carried himself with the unhurried ease of a man who had governed Yunxi County for seven years and had long forgotten what real resistance felt like. His hair was combed neatly back, his dark wool cardigan buttoned over a crisp collar, and his fingers moved over the tea tray as if measuring out favors.

Across from him sat Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong in civilian clothes, a slate-gray jacket draped over the back of his chair and thick forearms resting beside the saucer. Unlike Sun Weiming's refined posture, Liang Guodong had the heavy jaw of a man who preferred blunt leverage over polite ceremony.

"Deng Yongchang called my private number four times before nine o'clock," Liang Guodong said, draining half his cup in one swallow. "His voice shook like a man watching his warehouse burn. He says Hongrui Real Estate's county escrow accounts were frozen overnight, and two clearing channels in Lanshui went dark without warning."

Sun Weiming did not look up from the tea leaves unfurling in the pot. "He called my secretary twice and messaged my driver," he replied smoothly. "Deng Yongchang made too much money too fast. The moment a bank clerk asks for a signature, he loses his nerve and begs us to pull strings."

"So you think it is nothing?" Liang Guodong asked, setting his cup down with a sharp clink. "Four point eight billion yuan sits in those accounts, Sun Weiming. If the freeze holds past Monday, contractors will stop pouring concrete on the north bank, and the people above us will ask why Yunxi cannot keep its own house quiet."

Sun Weiming raised his eyes with a thin, faintly amused smile. "Use your head, Liang Guodong. If the provincial discipline committee or central auditors were moving against Yunxi, would they lock a county escrow account on a Friday night without notifying the municipal party secretary? Never. When the top moves, you hear thunder three days before the rain falls."

Liang Guodong rubbed his stubbled chin. "Then who?"

"Only two possibilities," Sun Weiming said, inhaling the grassy aroma of his tea. "Either a hungry municipal developer in Lanshui wants a piece of the riverside land transfer and paid a friendly branch director to stall Hongrui Real Estate for forty-eight hours, or Deng Yongchang's accountants triggered an automated tax-risk hold with sloppy filings. On Monday morning, the county finance office will send a formal inquiry to the municipal banking liaison. By Tuesday afternoon, the accounts will reopen, and Deng Yongchang will owe us both a deeper bow."

Liang Guodong let out a low, dry chuckle, the tension leaving his heavy shoulders. "A municipal developer playing clever games in our county. No wonder Deng Yongchang panicked. He has always been terrified of shadows."

"Panic makes businessmen sloppy," Sun Weiming said, his tone sharpening slightly. "Which brings us to Old Street. The south boundary of the riverside block still has one stubborn nail sticking out. Mingde Hardware. Every other storefront signed the relocation sheet last month. Only Chen Mingde refused and kept clinging to his grandfather's deed."

"Yan Tiehu and his crew went down on Thursday to persuade him," Liang Guodong said casually. "The old fool swung a wrench when they pushed into his shop, so Cheng Biao brought him in on an intentional injury and public disturbance hold. Clean paperwork, three signed witness statements before sunset."

"And has his family come to the station?" Sun Weiming asked.

"His older brother, Chen Jianguo, came yesterday afternoon looking pale as chalk," Liang Guodong replied with a dismissive wave. "Cheng Biao turned him away at the desk and said the case was under formal review. Funny enough, since yesterday evening nobody from the Chen family has come back to beg. Not Chen Jianguo, not Chen Mingde's wife Wu Lanying, nobody."

Sun Weiming's fingers paused beside the teapot. "Odd. Usually they are crying at the gate by the second morning."

"They are probably scraping up fine money, or listening to useless advice from Chen Jianguo's son who works as an office clerk in Qingyan," Liang Guodong said scornfully. "Ordinary county people think they can wait out a storm by keeping their heads down. They do not realize time works for us, not for them."

"We do not have weeks to waste on a hardware store," Sun Weiming reminded him softly. "The riverside blueprint has to clear final sign-off before the thirtieth. The people in Lanshui backing this project do not pay for delays. Make sure Chen Mingde's family feels the weight before Monday."

Liang Guodong nodded once. Pulling an unbranded mobile phone from his jacket pocket, he tapped a single saved number. After two rings, a brisk, deferential voice answered.

"Chief Liang Guodong," Cheng Biao said immediately.

"About Chen Mingde," Liang Guodong said, leaning back against the carved wooden chair. "Do not rush the release review. Hold him in the detention block for two more days under supplementary verification. No visitors, no phone calls, and no soft voices at the front desk if any relative calls. Let him sit in the cold until Chen Jianguo and Wu Lanying break and sign the Old Street demolition paper for Hongrui Real Estate. Clear?"

"Understood, Chief Liang Guodong," Cheng Biao answered smoothly. "Everything is documented by the book. Two more days will be no trouble. Deng Yongchang's people can bring the relocation paper straight to their door tomorrow afternoon once they get desperate."

"Good. Keep it tidy," Liang Guodong said, and ended the call.

He dropped the phone onto the rosewood table and refilled both celadon cups. "Two more days in a concrete cell will cure Chen Mingde of his attachment to a crumbling storefront. Once he signs, Hongrui Real Estate can flatten that corner by Wednesday."

"See that it stays clean," Sun Weiming murmured, raising his cup. "Yunxi County has stayed peaceful for seven years because everyone knows where the wall stands. Ordinary people bump their heads against it once, then learn to walk around."

The two officials tapped their cups together and drank in quiet satisfaction, watching bamboo leaves sway beyond the carved wooden lattice.

Across the narrow tree-lined street, forty meters away, the second-floor shutters of a closed stationery shop stood nearly shut behind dusty glass. Inside the dim room sat a folding aluminum table holding a matte-black acoustic receiver.

A crisp green waveform danced across its compact screen, converting microscopic vibrations from the tea house window glass into studio-grade audio.

Number Twenty-seven sat motionless behind the console in a dark hooded windbreaker and thin matte gloves. Through the bone-conduction earpiece, every word spoken by Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, and Cheng Biao had arrived crystal-clear.

On the display, an automated transcript stamped its cryptographic seal and pulsed green as the encrypted audio packet finished streaming to the Lanshui relay.

Number Twenty-seven watched the tea house courtyard for ten more seconds as Liang Guodong poured a third round of Longjing tea. Then, with one unhurried keystroke, Number Twenty-seven closed the channel, folded the tripod into a battered canvas case, and slipped silently down the rear stairwell.
