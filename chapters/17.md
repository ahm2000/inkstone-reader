# Chapter 17: Dead Calls

On Old Street, Gong's Noodles was the last light burning in the narrow lane.

A round table stood by the window with four chairs. Three were used every night. The fourth stayed empty, with a clean bowl and a pair of chopsticks in front of it.

Number Twenty-three, thin and flour-dusted in his glasses and apron, wiped a teacup behind the counter. Nobody in the Black Pine Guard knew his real name or face. The eyes on Old Street ran out of the back of his kitchen.

He flicked a small switch beside the cash register. A faint hiss came from a receiver wired to a microphone under the awning. The microphone pointed across the square at the barbecue stall.

The grill man at the next stall never turned a skewer without looking at the street. He was Zero's, and nobody at the tables guessed it.

At a corner table outside the stall, Lei Bo tapped his empty beer glass. Niu Dali looked at his phone for the fourth time.

"Still nothing?" Lei Bo said. His smug grin was wearing thin. "He texted twenty minutes ago. He was turning onto the ring road with the envelopes. Where is he?"

Niu Dali pressed redial. A flat voice answered. *The number you have dialed is powered off.*

"Yan Tiehu never turns his phone off. Never."

"Maybe the battery died," Yao Xiaolong said. He had stopped chewing his skewer.

Then Niu Dali's own phone rang. He snatched it up. "Did you find him?"

The caller was shouting so loudly that Lei Bo and Yao Xiaolong heard every word.

"Yan Tiehu's van is sitting on the outer ring road. The engine is running, the headlights are on, and the driver's door is wide open. His phone is smashed flat on the asphalt, and there is blood by the wheel!"

Niu Dali's face went white. His skewer slipped from his fingers and fell in the dirt.

"What about the road cameras?" Lei Bo grabbed his wrist across the table. "Call the traffic desk."

"I asked my cousin on night dispatch. The whole stretch of ring road went dark for four minutes. Not one frame. Yan Tiehu just vanished."

The silence at the table was like a bucket of ice.

Lei Bo's hand shook so hard that he knocked over his beer. It foamed across the plastic tablecloth. A man like Yan Tiehu did not get mugged. Someone had switched off every county camera and taken Hongrui's chief enforcer in four minutes. If they could do that, nobody at this table was safe.

"I have to call Dong Haisheng," Niu Dali said. His thumb slipped twice on the screen.

Inside Gong's Noodles, Number Fifty-six set his delivery helmet on the counter and made no sudden move. Three masked Zero Guard operatives stood in the shadows of the back kitchen. They had watched every breath in the shop since the Black Pine consoles were locked, half an hour ago.

"Zero took him off the road in four minutes," Number Fifty-six said in a low voice. "We were told to stay back. We could not lift a finger."

Number Twenty-three turned the switch off. Across the room, one black mask turned its head toward him and then away again. Nobody in the shop had spoken to it, and it had not spoken to them.

Number Sixty-eight kept her hands flat on the counter, in plain sight. "Number One was right. We got careless. Until the Boss gives us our authority back, we sit still and watch."

* * *

In the underground garage of Hongrui Real Estate, Dong Haisheng sat in the back seat of his black sedan with the engine off.

He had called Yan Tiehu's number six times. Six times, the same cold recording told him the phone was powered off.

Two minutes ago, Niu Dali had called about the empty van. Half a minute after that, Cheng Biao had called from the police station.

"Dong Haisheng, who did you people offend?" Cheng Biao had hissed. His voice cracked. "Four minutes of wiped cameras on a county road. A while ago, Director Mai Zhenhua sent an emergency order. Every police unit in the county is frozen inside its station. That is no street gang. If Yan Tiehu talks to whoever took him, we are both buried before Wednesday."

Dong Haisheng wiped his forehead with his sleeve. Yan Tiehu knew everything. He knew who took the envelopes, and he knew where the demolition money went. Under pressure, he would give it all away.

His thoughts went in circles. Was it a rival developer from Qingyan? An inspection team from the province, arriving early in plain clothes? Or something to do with Old Street?

Then his phone lit up on his knee.

The number was not saved, but he knew every digit. It belonged to Huang Boren, the private lender everyone in the county called Chairman Huang, who charged two percent interest a month.

Dong Haisheng's stomach dropped. He answered with both hands. "Chairman Huang."

"Dong Haisheng." The voice was soft and almost warm, like an uncle chatting at a New Year dinner. "Have you eaten?"

"Yes, thank you, Chairman. I was just seeing to Old Street."

"I heard Fan Weiguo spoke tonight and said there will be no more arrests," Huang Boren said. "And I heard that your head enforcer's van was found empty on the ring road."

Dong Haisheng froze. How did Chairman Huang already know?

"My driver waited outside Fuyuan Garden tonight," Huang Boren said, as if he had heard the thought. "And one of my men passed the ring road ten minutes ago."

"My three hundred million costs two percent a month," Huang Boren went on gently. "Do you know what that is every day?"

"Two hundred thousand, Chairman."

"Two hundred thousand. Your driver works almost four years to earn that, and you lose it before breakfast." The voice never rose. "On the thirtieth, Huaxin Bank reviews your 1.1 billion yuan loan. If construction has not started, they take everything. Then I take what is left of you."

"The last shops will be cleared by Monday. I swear it."

"Are they cleared yet?"

"Not yet."

"Your enforcer is gone, a clerk slapped him in front of the whole block, and Old Street still stands." Huang Boren gave a soft laugh. "Your daughter is in Grade Four at the river school, isn't she? Your driver picks her up at half past four. His brother owes me eight hundred thousand. I only mention it in passing. Clear that street and deal with the clerk by Monday. If you don't, I will send someone to help you, and you will not like his methods."

The line went dead.

Dong Haisheng sat in the dark car. His hand shook so hard that the phone knocked against the window.

Huang Boren pressed on him from above. Someone in the dark had made Yan Tiehu vanish without a sound. For the first time in ten years, Dong Haisheng felt real fear in his chest.

He dialed Niu Dali with a hoarse voice. "Yan Tiehu is gone, so you run the crew from tomorrow. No more arrests, because Director Cheng will not cover them. Lean on the tailor and the cobbler, or pay them off, but leave no marks on their faces. And put every ear you have on the street. I want to know who took him."

"Yes, Boss Dong. Right away."

Dong Haisheng dropped the phone on the leather seat and stared at the empty concrete pillars. With all his money, he felt like a rat in a dark room with the walls closing in.
