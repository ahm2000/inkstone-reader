# Chapter 17: County Magistrate Sun Weiming's Tea Room

Two hundred meters east of the Yunxi County Government Compound stood a private courtyard tea house with no signboard. Inside the rear pavilion, County Magistrate Sun Weiming tilted a small clay teapot with practiced ease, pouring pale green Longjing tea into the cup opposite him. At fifty-two, he had governed Yunxi County for seven years and carried himself like a man who had forgotten what real resistance felt like.

Across the table sat Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong in civilian clothes, thick forearms planted beside the saucer and his heavy jaw tight with impatience.

"Deng Yongchang called my private number 4 times before nine o'clock," Liang Guodong said, draining half his cup in one swallow. "His voice shook like a man watching his warehouse burn. He says Hongrui Real Estate's county escrow accounts were frozen overnight, and two clearing channels in Lanshui went dark."

Sun Weiming did not look up from the tea leaves. "He called my secretary twice and messaged my driver," he replied smoothly. "Deng Yongchang made too much money too fast. The moment a bank clerk asks for a signature, he loses his nerve and begs us to pull strings."

Liang Guodong set his cup down with a sharp clink, leaning forward. "So you think it is nothing? 4.8 billion yuan sits in those accounts, Sun Weiming. If the freeze holds past Monday, contractors stop pouring concrete on the north bank, and the people above us ask why Yunxi cannot keep its own house quiet."

Sun Weiming lifted his eyes, a thin, amused smile curving his mouth. "Calm down, Liang Guodong. If the provincial discipline committee or central auditors were moving against Yunxi, would they lock a county escrow account on a Friday night without notifying the municipal party secretary? Never. When the top moves, you hear thunder three days before the rain falls."

Liang Guodong frowned, rubbing his stubbled chin. "Then who has the reach to freeze 4.8 billion overnight?"

"Only two possibilities," Sun Weiming said, taking a slow sip. "Either a hungry municipal developer in Lanshui wants a piece of the riverside land transfer and paid a branch director to stall Hongrui Real Estate for forty-eight hours, or Deng Yongchang's accountants triggered an automated tax-risk hold with sloppy filings."

"And what do I tell Deng Yongchang when he calls a fifth time?" Liang Guodong asked gruffly.

"Tell him to stop sweating and wait," Sun Weiming replied coolly. "Monday morning, the county finance office sends an inquiry to the municipal banking liaison. By Tuesday afternoon, the accounts reopen, and Deng Yongchang owes us both a deeper bow."

Liang Guodong stared at him for a beat, then let out a dry chuckle as the stiffness left his shoulders. "A municipal developer playing clever games in our county. No wonder Deng Yongchang panicked. He has always jumped at shadows."

"Panic makes businessmen sloppy," Sun Weiming said, his tone sharpening as he set his cup down. "Which brings us to Old Street. The south boundary of the riverside block still has one stubborn nail sticking out. Mingde Hardware. Every other storefront signed last month. Only Chen Mingde refused and kept clinging to his grandfather's deed."

"Yan Tiehu and his crew went down Thursday to persuade him," Liang Guodong said with a careless shrug. "The old fool swung a wrench when they pushed inside, so Cheng Biao brought him in on an intentional injury and public disturbance hold. Clean paperwork, three witness statements before sunset."

Sun Weiming tapped one finger against the table. "And has his family come to the station?"

"His older brother, Chen Jianguo, came yesterday afternoon looking pale as chalk," Liang Guodong said dismissively. "Cheng Biao turned him away at the desk and said the case was under formal review. Funny enough, since yesterday evening nobody from the Chen family has come back to beg. Not Chen Jianguo, not Chen Mingde's wife Wu Lanying, nobody."

Sun Weiming's finger stopped. His eyes sharpened. "Odd. Usually they are crying at the gate by the second morning."

"They are probably scraping up fine money," Liang Guodong snorted. "Or listening to useless advice from Chen Jianguo's son. The boy works as an office clerk in Qingyan and came home yesterday. Small-town people think if they keep their heads down, a storm blows past. They never learn time works for us, not them."

"We do not have weeks to waste on a hardware store," Sun Weiming said, his smile vanishing completely. "The riverside blueprint has to clear final sign-off before the thirtieth. The people in Lanshui backing this project do not pay for delays. Make sure Chen Mingde's family feels the weight before Monday."

Liang Guodong's expression hardened at the mention of Lanshui. He nodded once, pulled an unbranded phone from his jacket, and tapped a saved number. After two rings, a brisk, deferential voice answered.

"Chief Liang Guodong," Cheng Biao said immediately.

"About Chen Mingde," Liang Guodong said, leaning back. "Do not rush the release review. Hold him in the detention block for two more days under supplementary verification. No visitors, no phone calls, and no soft voices at the front desk if any relative calls. Let him sit in the cold until Chen Jianguo and Wu Lanying break and sign the Old Street demolition paper for Hongrui Real Estate. Clear?"

"Completely clear, Chief Liang Guodong," Cheng Biao answered smoothly. "Everything is documented by the book. Two more days will be no trouble. Should I notify Chairman Deng's office?"

"Let Deng Yongchang's people know they can bring the relocation paper straight to Chen Jianguo's door tomorrow afternoon once they get desperate," Liang Guodong said. "Keep your hands clean."

"Understood, Chief Liang Guodong. Spotless," Cheng Biao promised.

Liang Guodong ended the call, dropped the phone onto the table, and refilled both cups with a satisfied grunt. "Two more days in a concrete cell will cure Chen Mingde of his crumbling storefront. Once he signs, Hongrui Real Estate flattens that corner by Wednesday."

"See that it stays tidy," Sun Weiming murmured, raising his cup. "Yunxi County has stayed peaceful for seven years because everyone knows where the wall stands. Ordinary people bump their heads against it once, then learn to walk around."

The two officials tapped their cups together and drank.

Across the street, forty meters away, the second-floor shutters of a closed stationery shop stood nearly drawn. Inside sat a folding aluminum table holding a matte-black acoustic receiver. A crisp green waveform danced across the screen, turning microscopic vibrations from the tea house window glass into studio-grade audio.

Number 27 sat motionless behind the console in a dark windbreaker and matte gloves. Through the bone-conduction earpiece, every word spoken by Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, and Cheng Biao arrived crystal-clear.

On the display, an automated transcript stamped its cryptographic seal and pulsed green as the encrypted audio packet finished streaming to the Lanshui relay.

Number 27 watched the courtyard for ten more seconds as Liang Guodong poured a third round of tea. Then, with one unhurried keystroke, Number 27 closed the channel, folded the tripod into a canvas case, and slipped silently down the rear stairwell.
