# Chapter 17: County Magistrate Sun Weiming's Tea Room

Two hundred meters east of the Yunxi County Government Compound stood an unmarked courtyard tea house. Inside the back room, County Magistrate Sun Weiming poured pale green tea with easy grace. At fifty-two, he had run Yunxi County for seven years and carried himself like a man who had forgotten what pushback felt like.

Across from him sat Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong in plain clothes, his heavy jaw tight with impatience.

"Deng Yongchang called my private phone four times before nine," Liang Guodong said, drinking half his cup in one gulp. "His voice shook like his warehouse was burning. He says Hongrui Real Estate's county accounts were frozen overnight, and two payment accounts in Sha City went dead."

Sun Weiming did not look up from the tea leaves. "He called my assistant twice and messaged my driver," he replied smoothly. "Deng Yongchang made too much money too fast. The second a bank clerk asks for a signature, he loses his nerve and begs us to pull strings."

Liang Guodong set his cup down sharply and leaned forward. "You think it is nothing? 4.8 billion yuan sits in those accounts, Sun Weiming. If the freeze lasts past Monday, builders stop pouring concrete on the north bank, and the people above us ask why Yunxi cannot keep its house quiet."

Sun Weiming lifted his eyes with a thin, amused smile. "Calm down, Liang Guodong. If provincial inspectors or national auditors were moving against Yunxi, would they freeze a county bank account on a Friday night without telling the city party secretary? Never. When the top moves, you hear thunder three days before the rain falls."

Liang Guodong frowned, rubbing his rough chin. "Then who can freeze 4.8 billion overnight?"

"Only two guesses," Sun Weiming said, sipping slowly. "Either a greedy Sha City builder wants a piece of the riverside deal and paid a bank manager to stall Hongrui Real Estate for two days, or Deng Yongchang's bookkeepers tripped an automatic tax flag with sloppy paperwork."

"And what do I tell Deng Yongchang when he calls a fifth time?" Liang Guodong asked gruffly.

"Tell him to stop sweating and wait," Sun Weiming replied coolly. "Monday morning, the county finance office will send a note to the city bank office. By Tuesday afternoon, the accounts will open, and Deng Yongchang will owe us both an even deeper bow."

Liang Guodong stared at him, then let out a dry chuckle as tension left his shoulders. "A city builder playing cheap tricks in our county. No wonder Deng Yongchang panicked. He always jumped at shadows."

"Panic makes businessmen sloppy," Sun Weiming said, his voice sharpening as he set his cup down. "Which brings us to Old Street. The south edge of the riverside block still has one stubborn nail sticking out. Mingde Hardware. Every other shop signed last month. Only Chen Mingde refused and kept clinging to his grandfather's deed."

"Yan Tiehu and his crew went down Thursday to talk sense into him," Liang Guodong said with a shrug. "The old fool swung a wrench when they walked in, so Cheng Biao locked him up for assault and disturbance. Clean paperwork, three witness statements before sunset."

Sun Weiming tapped one finger on the table. "Has his family come to the station?"

"His older brother, Chen Jianguo, came yesterday afternoon looking white as chalk," Liang Guodong said dismissively. "Cheng Biao turned him away and said the case was under review. Funny thing is, since yesterday evening nobody from the Chen family has come back to beg. Not Chen Jianguo, not Chen Mingde's wife Wu Lanying, nobody."

Sun Weiming's finger stopped. His eyes sharpened. "Strange. Usually they are crying at the gate by the second morning."

"They are probably scraping up fine money," Liang Guodong snorted. "Or listening to useless advice from Chen Jianguo's son. The boy works as an office clerk in Shanghai and came home yesterday. Small-town people think if they keep their heads down, a storm will blow past. They never learn that time works for us, not them."

"We do not have weeks to waste on a hardware shop," Sun Weiming said, his smile vanishing. "The riverside building plan needs final approval before the thirtieth. The people in Sha City backing this project do not pay for delays. Make sure Chen Mingde's family feels the pressure before Monday."

Liang Guodong's face hardened at the mention of Sha City. He nodded, pulled an unmarked phone from his jacket, and tapped a saved number. After two rings, a brisk, respectful voice answered.

"Chief Liang Guodong," Cheng Biao said at once.

"About Chen Mingde," Liang Guodong said, leaning back. "Do not rush his release check. Hold him in lockup two more days for extra questioning. No visitors, no phone calls, and no friendly talk at the front desk if relatives call. Let him sit in the cold until Chen Jianguo and Wu Lanying break and sign the Old Street buyout paper for Hongrui Real Estate. Clear?"

"Completely clear, Chief Liang Guodong," Cheng Biao answered smoothly. "Everything is written by the book. Two more days will be no trouble. Should I let Chairman Deng's office know?"

"Tell Deng Yongchang's people they can bring the buyout paper straight to Chen Jianguo's door tomorrow afternoon once they get desperate," Liang Guodong said. "Keep your hands clean."

"Understood, Chief Liang Guodong. Spotless," Cheng Biao promised.

Liang Guodong ended the call and refilled both cups with a satisfied grunt. "Two more days in a concrete cell will cure Chen Mingde of his storefront. Once he signs, Hongrui Real Estate knocks down that corner by Wednesday."

"Keep it neat," Sun Weiming murmured, raising his cup. "Yunxi County stayed quiet for seven years because everyone knows where the wall stands. Ordinary people bump their heads once, then learn to walk around."

The two officials tapped cups and drank.

Forty meters across the street, behind the nearly shut second-floor shutters of a closed stationery shop, a black window listening box sat on a folding table. A green sound wave danced across the screen, turning tiny vibrations on the tea house glass into sharp audio.

Number 27 sat still behind the screen in dark gear and gloves. Every word spoken by Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, and Cheng Biao came through crystal-clear.

The automatic text log stamped its time seal and flashed green as the audio file finished sending to the Sha City base.

Number 27 watched Liang Guodong pour a third round of tea. Then, with one calm tap, Number 27 shut the line down, packed the stand into a cloth bag, and slipped silently down the back stairs.
