# Chapter 17: County Magistrate Sun Weiming's Tea Room

Two hundred meters east of the Yunxi County Government Compound stood a private courtyard tea house with no sign outside. Inside the back room, County Magistrate Sun Weiming tilted a small clay teapot with easy grace, pouring pale green tea into the cup across from him. At fifty-two, he had run Yunxi County for seven years and carried himself like a man who had forgotten what real pushback felt like.

Across the table sat Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong in plain clothes. His thick arms rested beside his saucer, and his heavy jaw was tight with impatience.

"Deng Yongchang called my private phone four times before nine o'clock," Liang Guodong said, drinking half his cup in one gulp. "His voice shook like a man watching his warehouse burn down. He says Hongrui Real Estate's county bank accounts were frozen overnight, and two payment accounts in Lanshui went dead."

Sun Weiming did not look up from the tea leaves. "He called my assistant twice and messaged my driver," he replied smoothly. "Deng Yongchang made too much money too fast. The second a bank clerk asks for a signature, he loses his nerve and begs us to pull strings."

Liang Guodong set his cup down with a sharp clink and leaned forward. "So you think it is nothing? 4.8 billion yuan sits in those accounts, Sun Weiming. If the freeze lasts past Monday, builders stop pouring concrete on the north bank, and the people above us ask why Yunxi cannot keep its own house quiet."

Sun Weiming lifted his eyes, a thin, amused smile on his mouth. "Calm down, Liang Guodong. If provincial inspectors or national auditors were moving against Yunxi, would they freeze a county bank account on a Friday night without telling the city party secretary? Never. When the top moves, you hear thunder three days before the rain falls."

Liang Guodong frowned and rubbed his rough chin. "Then who has the power to freeze 4.8 billion overnight?"

"Only two guesses," Sun Weiming said, taking a slow sip. "Either a greedy city builder in Lanshui wants a piece of the riverside land deal and paid a bank manager to stall Hongrui Real Estate for two days, or Deng Yongchang's bookkeepers tripped an automatic tax flag with sloppy paperwork."

"And what do I tell Deng Yongchang when he calls a fifth time?" Liang Guodong asked gruffly.

"Tell him to stop sweating and wait," Sun Weiming replied coolly. "Monday morning, the county finance office will send a note to the city bank office. By Tuesday afternoon, the accounts will open back up, and Deng Yongchang will owe us both an even deeper bow."

Liang Guodong stared at him for a moment, then let out a dry chuckle as the tension left his shoulders. "A city builder playing cheap tricks in our county. No wonder Deng Yongchang panicked. He has always jumped at shadows."

"Panic makes businessmen sloppy," Sun Weiming said, his voice turning sharper as he set his cup down. "Which brings us to Old Street. The south edge of the riverside block still has one stubborn nail sticking out. Mingde Hardware. Every other shop signed last month. Only Chen Mingde refused and kept clinging to his grandfather's old deed."

"Yan Tiehu and his crew went down Thursday to talk sense into him," Liang Guodong said with a careless shrug. "The old fool swung a wrench when they walked in, so Cheng Biao locked him up on an assault and disturbance charge. Clean paperwork, three witness papers before sunset."

Sun Weiming tapped one finger against the table. "And has his family come to the station?"

"His older brother, Chen Jianguo, came yesterday afternoon looking white as chalk," Liang Guodong said with a dismissive wave. "Cheng Biao turned him away at the counter and told him the case was under review. Funny thing is, since yesterday evening nobody from the Chen family has come back to beg. Not Chen Jianguo, not Chen Mingde's wife Wu Lanying, nobody."

Sun Weiming's finger stopped. His eyes sharpened. "Strange. Usually they are crying at the gate by the second morning."

"They are probably scraping up fine money," Liang Guodong snorted. "Or listening to useless advice from Chen Jianguo's son. The boy works as an office clerk in Qingyan and came home yesterday. Small-town people think if they keep their heads down, a storm will blow past. They never learn that time works for us, not them."

"We do not have weeks to waste on a hardware shop," Sun Weiming said, his smile vanishing completely. "The riverside building plan has to get final approval before the thirtieth. The people in Lanshui backing this project do not pay for delays. Make sure Chen Mingde's family feels the pressure before Monday."

Liang Guodong's face hardened at the mention of Lanshui. He nodded once, pulled an unmarked phone from his jacket, and tapped a saved number. After two rings, a brisk, respectful voice answered.

"Chief Liang Guodong," Cheng Biao said right away.

"About Chen Mingde," Liang Guodong said, leaning back. "Do not rush his release check. Hold him in the lockup for two more days for extra questioning. No visitors, no phone calls, and no friendly talk at the front desk if any relative calls. Let him sit in the cold until Chen Jianguo and Wu Lanying break and sign the Old Street buyout paper for Hongrui Real Estate. Clear?"

"Completely clear, Chief Liang Guodong," Cheng Biao answered smoothly. "Everything is written up by the book. Two more days will be no trouble at all. Should I let Chairman Deng's office know?"

"Tell Deng Yongchang's people they can bring the buyout paper straight to Chen Jianguo's door tomorrow afternoon once they get desperate," Liang Guodong said. "Keep your hands clean."

"Understood, Chief Liang Guodong. Spotless," Cheng Biao promised.

Liang Guodong ended the call, dropped the phone onto the table, and refilled both cups with a satisfied grunt. "Two more days in a concrete cell will cure Chen Mingde of his old storefront. Once he signs, Hongrui Real Estate knocks down that corner by Wednesday."

"Make sure it stays neat," Sun Weiming murmured, raising his cup. "Yunxi County has stayed quiet for seven years because everyone knows where the wall stands. Ordinary people bump their heads against it once, then learn to walk around."

The two officials tapped their cups together and drank.

Across the street, forty meters away, the second-floor shutters of a closed stationery shop stood nearly shut. Inside sat a folding metal table holding a black window listening box. A clean green sound wave danced across the screen, turning tiny shakes on the tea house glass into clear, sharp audio.

Number 27 sat completely still behind the screen in a dark jacket and dark gloves. Through his earpiece, every single word spoken by Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, and Cheng Biao came through crystal-clear.

On the screen, the automatic text log stamped its locked time seal and flashed green as the private sound file finished sending to the Lanshui base.

Number 27 watched the courtyard for ten more seconds as Liang Guodong poured a third round of tea. Then, with one calm tap on the keyboard, Number 27 shut the line down, folded the stand into a cloth bag, and slipped silently down the back stairs.
