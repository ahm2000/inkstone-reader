# Chapter 17: Seating Order

In Conference Room Three of the Yunxi County Party Committee Building, nobody had to ask who held the power.

You only have to look at the chairs.

Eleven leather seats lined the long polished table, one for each member of the County Standing Committee. That was the small inner council that actually ran Yunxi County.

The closer a seat sat to the head, the higher its owner ranked.

Seat One belonged to Fan Weiguo, the County Party Secretary and Yunxi's top boss.

Seat Two belonged to Tao Shu, the County Magistrate and Deputy Party Secretary, who headed the county government.

On paper the two men held the same rank.

Everyone knew Seat One held the final word. Seat Two had spent eight quiet years waiting for it.

Seat Three belonged to Kang Guangming, head of the County Discipline Inspection Commission. That was Yunxi's own anti-corruption watchdog.

He was lean and stone-faced, and he had not smiled once since he walked in.

To put it nicely, his rank sat half a step below the top two.

To put it bluntly, his office could legally investigate everyone at this table, even the Party Secretary.

That was why nobody told jokes near him.

Seat Four belonged to Shao Wenhua, head of the Organization Department. He commanded no police and spent no funds, but every official's career crossed his desk.

Seat Five belonged to Lu Wenzhe, the Executive Deputy Magistrate.

"Executive" meant first among the deputies. He held the county's budget and ran the Old Street rebuilding office.

Even the teacups told the story.

The first two seats had bone china, and the rest had plain glass.

Right now Lu Wenzhe's face was the color of wet ash. His cup had rattled against its saucer for ten straight minutes!

Along the far wall sat Mai Zhenhua, the County Police Director, on a plain wooden chair with no nameplate.

He also wore the hat of Deputy Magistrate, yet he held no Standing Committee seat.

He could sign an arrest warrant, but he could not cast a vote.

"Comrades," Fan began, smooth despite thirty sleepless hours. "The City Party Secretary briefed me at seven this morning."

"The Provincial Inspection Team now arrives at nine tomorrow. Deputy Police Director Cheng Biao is suspended, and city investigators are already working in Yunxi."

"I have called this emergency session to settle our response, starting with the Old Street project."

Silence.

Nobody rushed to speak.

In a crisis, the first man to open his mouth is usually the first to lose his footing.

Fan let his eyes travel around the eleven faces and counted his allies.

Who stood behind him? Who leaned toward Tao Shu? Who would switch sides the moment trouble began?

His chest felt as tight as a drum.

Everyone knew this meeting was not about an old shopping street, but about who would still hold a chair next month!

"Secretary Fan," Kang said at last, flat and businesslike. "Before we discuss policy, I must put one fact on the record."

"The Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission sent its team into Yunxi last night without telling our county office. I learned of it from my own staff at six this morning."

"Noted, Secretary Kang," Fan said, and his throat felt dry.

A chill fell over the table.

"Not even one phone call?" someone breathed.

Every veteran understood at once.

City investigators normally told the local office first. Going around Kang meant the city trusted nobody here to keep a secret!

"Comrades, let's not panic over one noisy complaint!" Lu Wenzhe blurted, too loud, steadying his cup with his other hand. "Old Street is our biggest project, and the buyout prices went through proper county review!"

"If we freeze everything for one stubborn hardware seller, the county loses face and investors run away!"

"Who proposed stopping the entire project, Deputy Magistrate Lu?" Kang asked calmly.

"I only meant, that is, the investors," Lu stammered, and then his mouth snapped shut. Sweat beaded along his hairline!

Fan watched him from Seat One, and a cold, ugly suspicion sharpened in his gut. Lu signed every payment from the 640-million-yuan relocation fund.

Why would he sweat over one small hardware shop unless the books could not survive daylight?

"Secretary Fan, I believe we should be completely open," Tao Shu said smoothly. He looked rested and calm, wholly free of Friday's convenient cold.

"I propose a self-inspection team, and I will lead it myself."

"Before the provincial team arrives, we will check every Old Street contract and payment. We will hand every file over openly, because a clean leadership has nothing to hide."

"A generous offer," a quiet voice murmured from the middle of the table.

How reasonable! How fair! How selfless!

Yet Fan's stomach sank like lead. A team led by Tao would hold every account book in the county. The provincial inspectors would see none of them first!

Tao had waited eight patient years in Seat Two.

Now he reached for the top chair with the warmest smile in the room.

"Director Shao, what is your view?" Fan asked quietly.

Shao Wenhua kept smoothing the corner of his notebook and would not meet anyone's eyes.

"The Organization Department will follow the committee's decision," he said.

It was the classic veteran move: stay quiet until one side was clearly winning.

On the wall chair, Mai sat as still as a statue.

He thanked his luck that he had never signed an Old Street detention paper! Since midnight, one icy order from the city police chief had rung in his ears: *Release the shopkeeper, suspend Cheng Biao, and touch no other document.*

"Very well," Fan said steadily. "If there are no other proposals, we will vote on Magistrate Tao's team."

"It is only a formality," an aide whispered behind the chairs.

"Magistrate Tao's team will pass," someone else whispered back.

"Wait one moment, Secretary Fan," Kang said quietly. "Before we vote, I have one more report from my field staff. I want it entered in today's minutes."

He flipped open the folder in front of him. He did not glance at Fan or at Tao.

His flat, unblinking eyes settled on Seat Five.

"At a quarter past twelve today, hours after city investigators froze the Old Street accounts, two of my officers saw an official county sedan parked behind the private office of Hongrui Real Estate for forty minutes."

"The plate," Kang read evenly, "belongs to Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu Wenzhe's personal government car."

Every head at the long table snapped toward Seat Five.

"Hongrui's office?" someone breathed.

"His own car?"

"Forty minutes?"

Lu's fingers went numb, and his teacup slipped out of his hand.

Crack!

The cup hit the saucer's edge and split down the middle. Scalding tea spread across the mirror-bright wood in a dark tide, straight toward Fan's open red folder.

Around the eleven-seat table, nobody moved to wipe it up.
