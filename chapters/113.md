# Chapter 113: Hand in Hand Down Old Street

Once the lunch dishes were washed with Liu Meifang and Wu Lanying, and Chen Yuan and Chen Hao had been sent back to their afternoon review classes with pockets full of Shanghai fruit candies, the sixth-floor apartment finally quieted down.

Chen Xuan took Qin Yuhan by the hand and led her down the worn stone steps toward the heart of Old Street, where the gray flagstones still held the warmth of the afternoon.

a short distance behind them near the carved stone archway, two members of the Black Order walked unhurriedly past a display of woven bamboo baskets, blending seamlessly into the relaxed Saturday afternoon shoppers.

Every storefront along the bluestone lane had been freshly restored with clean cedar wood signboards, crisp white walls, and bright red lanterns swaying gently in the cool autumn breeze. Golden ginkgo leaves drifted down from the old trees behind the courtyard walls and settled along the edges of the gray tiles. Because the county government and the four national banks, including China Construction Bank and Huaxia Bank, had recently poured interest-free heritage funds into Old Street, the whole neighborhood smelled of roasted chestnuts, warm malt sugar, and fresh timber.

"It is so peaceful here," Qin Yuhan said softly, looking around at the smiling shopkeepers and children chasing paper pinwheels down the lane. "In Lujiazui, everyone walks as if they are five minutes late for a meeting. Here, even the wind feels unhurried."

"This is where I grew up," Chen Xuan said with a quiet smile, his other hand holding his dented green metal thermos by the strap. "When I was ten, I used to run down this exact alley after school just to buy a two-yuan sweet potato from the corner stove."

They paused outside Mingde Hardware, Second Uncle Chen Mingde's old shop near the middle of the lane. The wooden counter inside was bright and tidy, stacked with neat boxes of brass hinges, paintbrushes, and tools, while two local carpenters chatted cheerfully with the apprentice at the front desk. A new red banner hung beside the door celebrating the street's autumn heritage fair.

A few doors farther down, the sweet, nutty scent of toasted sesame drifted across the flagstones. Behind a gleaming copper pot stood Auntie Tian, her gray hair tied neatly under a clean white cloth as she sliced sheets of golden sesame candy with a heavy brass knife.

The moment she looked up and spotted Chen Xuan, her wrinkled face broke into a wide, joyful grin.

"Little Xuan! You are back!" Auntie Tian called out, wiping her flour-dusted hands on her apron. Her eyes immediately shifted to the graceful young woman holding his hand. "And this must be your wife, Yuhan! Oh, look at this girl. Your mother was right, she looks like a painting!"

"Hello, Auntie Tian," Qin Yuhan greeted her warmly.

Before Chen Xuan could even reach into his jacket pocket for his wallet, Auntie Tian picked up two warm, freshly cut squares of sesame candy wrapped in wax paper and pressed them straight into Qin Yuhan's palm.

"Taste it while it is still warm!" Auntie Tian insisted. "Do not dare offer me money, Little Xuan. If your mother hears I charged your new wife for candy, she will scold me all the way down the street."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly and took a bite. The warm sesame cracked crisply between her teeth, followed by the clean, mellow sweetness of homemade malt sugar without a trace of artificial syrup. Her eyes lit up immediately.

"Auntie Tian, this is wonderful," she said sincerely. "May I buy four big bags to take back to Shanghai for my parents and my grandfather? They love traditional snacks."

Auntie Tian beamed with pride and quickly packed four generous paper bags, slipping an extra handful of peanut brittle into the top while refusing to charge more than the regular local price. Chen Xuan carried the bags easily while Qin Yuhan nibbled happily on the rest of her warm candy.

Near the old stone bridge at the far end of the lane, they crossed paths with a sturdy man in a crisp police uniform walking his afternoon beat. It was Wei Kun, the honest Acting Director of Old Street Police Station.

Wei Kun stopped with a friendly nod when he saw Chen Xuan. "Back home for the weekend, Little Xuan? And congratulations on the wedding!"

"Thank you, Director Wei," Chen Xuan replied casually, nodding back like any simple hometown resident. "The street looks busier and cleaner than ever."

"It really is," Wei Kun said with genuine satisfaction, glancing down the sunlit bluestone lane. "Ever since the province cleared out those corrupt officials and street bullies earlier this year, honest merchants can finally do business in peace. Folks say the county is starting a brand new chapter."

After exchanging a few more polite words, Wei Kun continued his patrol down the lane. Qin Yuhan looked after him for a moment, then smiled up at Chen Xuan.

They walked together down the stone steps beside the narrow canal where weeping willows trailed yellowing autumn leaves across the clear water. The afternoon light turned warm and golden across the surface of the stream.

Qin Yuhan leaned her head lightly against Chen Xuan's shoulder as they stood by the stone railing. "You know," she murmured quietly, "I used to wonder why you never got anxious about anything in Shanghai. Even when everyone around you chases status and money, you are always calm, content, and steady. Walking down this street with you today, I think I finally understand."

Chen Xuan looked down at his wife, the autumn sunlight catching the soft curve of her cheek. He gently brushed a stray strand of hair behind her ear.

"A quiet life with the right person is hard to beat," he said simply. "Come on. Let us buy two roasted sweet potatoes before we head back, or my mom will ask why I starved you all afternoon. Tomorrow we head out to Hongshi Village for Grandma's 81st birthday."

Qin Yuhan smiled brightly, tightening her fingers around his as they walked back up the sunlit lane hand in hand.
