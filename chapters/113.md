# Chapter 113: Fifty Meters

By three in the afternoon on Friday, the weekly shipping forms on Chen Xuan's desk were already stamped, sorted, and stacked into three neat piles.

He checked the last column of container numbers, signed his name at the bottom corner, and carried the folder over to the section desk.

"All done for the week?" Wang Lei asked, looking up from his computer screen with a tired grin.

"All done," Chen Xuan said easily. "The pier receipts match the truck logs. Nothing left hanging over the weekend."

Wang Lei leaned back and stretched his arms. "You work faster than the rest of us put together. Go grab some fresh air by the river if you want. Just be back before five to punch out so the office clock stays happy."

"Thanks, Wang Lei. I will be back by four-forty."

Chen Xuan returned to his desk, picked up his worn paperback history book and his old green thermos, and tucked both under his arm. He walked out of the Hengxin Logistics building at a slow, unhurried pace, crossing the quiet two-lane road toward the Qingyan riverside park five minutes away.

Across the street, inside the parked silver sedan, Carl Mercer sat up straighter behind the wheel.

"That is him," Carl Mercer said softly, comparing the young man's face to the employee roster photo he had pulled up on his phone an hour earlier. "Plain blue shirt, dark trousers, green thermos. Looks like he is heading for the park."

Anton Novak checked the quiet street, slipped a small earpiece into his left ear, and reached inside his jacket to make sure his compact pistol sat snug in its shoulder holster. "Empty afternoon. Easy walk. Let us see who he meets down by the water."

"Stay back until he sits down," Carl Mercer said, stepping out of the car and locking the doors. "If someone comes to meet him, I want clear photos of both faces."

Inside the riverside park, the Friday afternoon air was cool and mild. A few older residents played chess near the entrance gate, and a mother pushed a stroller along the upper path. Near the water's edge, where weeping willows leaned over the stone bank, the benches were mostly empty.

Chen Xuan chose a wooden bench halfway down the lower path, well away from the main walkway. He sat down, set his green thermos beside him, unscrewed the metal lid, and took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea. Then he opened his book to the folded paper bookmark and began to read.

Behind him, separated by a strip of grass and a thick row of cedar trees, ran a narrow maintenance path shaded by low branches.

Carl Mercer and Anton Novak entered the park separately, then met up on the shaded path behind the cedars. From eighty meters back, Carl Mercer raised a small camera with a zoom lens and studied Chen Xuan's profile.

"Alone," Carl Mercer murmured into his collar mic. "No phone in his hand. No car waiting on the upper road. Just a kid reading a book."

"Look closer," Anton Novak whispered, gesturing toward the thicker hedges closer to the bench. "If we move up behind those bushes, we can get a clean angle on his face and see if anyone signals him from the river path. Nobody is walking this back trail."

Carl Mercer hesitated for half a second. The park was quiet. No guards stood near the gate. No black sedans idled at the curb. Everything looked completely ordinary.

"Keep it slow," Carl Mercer said. "Fifty meters is close enough. We stay behind the trees."

They stepped forward along the soft pine needles. Sixty meters. Fifty-five meters.

Neither man noticed the two figures standing completely motionless in the deep shade of the cedar trunks twelve paces ahead of them.

The moment Carl Mercer's boot crossed the fifty-meter line from Chen Xuan's bench, the air behind the trees shifted.

Zero Guard did not announce itself. There was no shout, no warning, and no sound of running feet.

Number 104 stepped out from behind a wide cedar trunk directly into Carl Mercer's path, dressed head to toe in plain black gear with a smooth black mask covering every inch of the face.

Carl Mercer froze for a split second. Twenty years of instinct kicked in at once. His right hand darted inside his brown jacket toward his pistol.

He never even cleared the leather.

Number 104 moved a half-step closer, caught Carl Mercer's wrist with a grip like iron, and twisted the arm downward in one sharp snap. The compact pistol slid free into Number 104's left palm without making a single metallic click. Before Carl Mercer could draw breath to shout, the edge of Number 104's hand struck the side of his neck with exact, measured force.

Carl Mercer's eyes went blank. Number 104 caught him by the collar before his knees hit the pine needles and lowered him silently to the grass.

Three paces behind, Anton Novak saw his partner drop in two seconds. His face turned white. He did not waste time reaching for his gun. He spun to bolt back toward the road while snapping a short folding knife out of his coat sleeve.

He took one stride before Number 109 stepped straight out of the hedge beside him, wearing the same plain black gear and full black mask.

Anton Novak slashed blindly at the masked figure's chest. Number 109 did not even step back. A gloved forearm caught Anton Novak's wrist, twisted the knife loose, and drove a hard elbow straight into the side of his ribs. Air rushed out of Anton Novak's lungs in a silent gasp. Number 109 slipped behind him, locked one arm smoothly across his throat, and held for two quiet seconds until his legs gave out completely.

Six seconds from start to finish.

Not a single shout had escaped. Not a single dry branch had snapped loud enough to carry past the cedars.

Twenty meters away, a plain gray utility van rolled quietly through the park's locked service gate and stopped behind the maintenance shed. Two more Zero Guard members stepped out of the sliding door, checked both unconscious men, removed their earpieces, burner phones, camera, and weapons, and bound their wrists neatly with black ties.

Number 104 spoke once, in a flat, neutral, genderless voice completely empty of emotion.

"Fifty-meter line crossed. Two armed foreign nationals. Weapons and phones bagged. Delivering straight to the Qingyan State Security drop point."

"Confirmed," Number 109 replied in the same cold, flat tone. "Maintain fifty-meter cover on Chen Xuan."

Within thirty seconds, the two unconscious trackers were lifted cleanly into the back of the gray van. The sliding door clicked shut, and the van pulled smoothly back out through the service gate toward the north road, leaving not even a scuffed boot print on the pine needles.

Behind the cedars, Number 104 and Number 109 melted back into the quiet shade of the trees, staying silently within fifty meters of the wooden bench, just as they did every single day without anyone ever asking them to.

Out by the water's edge, Chen Xuan turned a page of his history book.

A cool breeze ruffled the willow branches overhead, sending two yellow leaves drifting down onto the stone path beside his shoes. He picked up his green thermos, took another warm sip of jasmine tea, and watched a small cargo barge chug slowly down the middle of the river.

It was a peaceful Friday afternoon.
