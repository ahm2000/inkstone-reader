# Chapter 113: Fifty Meters

By three on Friday afternoon, the weekly shipping forms on Chen Xuan's desk were stamped, sorted, and stacked into three neat piles. He checked the final container numbers, signed the corner, and carried the folder to the section desk.

"All done for the week?" Wang Lei asked, looking up from his screen with a tired grin.

"All done," Chen Xuan said easily. "The pier receipts match the truck logs. Nothing left over the weekend."

Wang Lei stretched his arms. "You work faster than the rest of us put together. Go grab some fresh air by the river if you want. Just be back before five to punch out so the clock stays happy."

"Thanks, Wang Lei. I will be back by four-forty."

Chen Xuan returned to his desk, tucked his worn paperback history book and old green thermos under his arm, and strolled out of Hengxin Logistics toward the Shanghai riverside park five minutes away.

Across the street in the silver sedan, Carl Mercer sat up straighter.

"That is him," Carl Mercer said softly, comparing him to the roster photo on his phone. "Plain blue shirt, dark trousers, green thermos. Heading for the park."

Anton Novak slipped an earpiece in and checked the compact pistol in his shoulder holster. "Empty afternoon. Easy walk. Let us see who he meets by the water."

"Stay back until he sits down," Carl Mercer said, locking the car. "If someone meets him, I want clear photos of both faces."

Inside the park, the Friday air was cool and mild. A few older residents played chess near the gate, and a mother pushed a stroller along the upper path. Down by the water under weeping willows, the benches were mostly empty.

Chen Xuan took a wooden bench on the lower path, set his thermos beside him, took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea, and opened his history book.

Behind him, past a strip of grass and a thick row of cedars, ran a shaded maintenance path.

Carl Mercer and Anton Novak entered separately and met behind the cedars. From eighty meters back, Carl Mercer raised a small zoom camera and studied Chen Xuan's profile.

"Alone," Carl Mercer murmured into his collar mic. "No phone in his hand. No car on the upper road. Just a kid reading a book."

"Look closer," Anton Novak whispered, gesturing toward the thicker hedges ahead. "If we move behind those bushes, we get a clean angle on his face and see if anyone signals him from the river path. Nobody walks this back trail."

Carl Mercer hesitated half a second. The park was quiet. No guards by the gate, no black sedans idling at the curb. Everything looked completely ordinary.

"Keep it slow," Carl Mercer said. "Fifty meters is close enough. Stay behind the trees."

They stepped forward along soft pine needles. Sixty meters. Fifty-five meters.

Neither man noticed the two figures standing motionless in the deep cedar shade twelve paces ahead.

The moment Carl Mercer's boot crossed the fifty-meter line from Chen Xuan's bench, the air behind the trees shifted.

Black Guard did not announce itself. No shout, no warning, no sound of running feet.

The first Black Guard member stepped from behind a wide cedar trunk directly into Carl Mercer's path, dressed head to toe in plain black gear with a smooth black mask.

Carl Mercer froze for a split second before twenty years of instinct kicked in. His right hand darted inside his jacket toward his pistol.

He never cleared the leather.

The first guard stepped closer, caught Carl Mercer's wrist in an iron grip, and twisted downward in one sharp snap. The compact pistol slid silently into the guard's left palm. Before Carl Mercer could draw breath to shout, the edge of the guard's hand struck the side of his neck with exact force.

Carl Mercer's eyes went blank. The guard caught him by the collar before his knees touched the needles and lowered him silently to the grass.

Three paces behind, Anton Novak saw his partner drop in two seconds. His face went white. He did not reach for his gun. He spun to bolt toward the road while snapping a folding knife from his sleeve.

One stride later, a second Black Guard member stepped straight from the hedge beside him in matching black gear and full black mask.

Anton Novak slashed blindly at the masked figure's chest. The second guard did not even step back. A gloved forearm caught Anton Novak's wrist, twisted the knife loose, and drove a hard elbow into his ribs. Air rushed from Anton Novak's lungs in a silent gasp. The guard slipped behind him, locked one arm across his throat, and held for two quiet seconds until his legs gave out.

Six seconds, start to finish. Not a single shout escaped. Not a dry branch snapped loud enough to carry past the cedars.

Twenty meters away, a plain gray utility van rolled quietly through the locked service gate and stopped behind the maintenance shed. Two more Black Guard members stepped out, stripped both unconscious men of earpieces, burner phones, camera, and weapons, and bound their wrists with black ties.

The first guard spoke once, in a calm, quiet voice.

"Fifty-meter line crossed. Two armed foreign nationals. Weapons and phones bagged. Delivering straight to the Shanghai State Security drop point."

"Confirmed," the second guard answered crisply. "Maintain fifty-meter cover on Chen Xuan."

Within thirty seconds, the two trackers were lifted into the van. The sliding door clicked shut, and the van pulled smoothly back out the service gate, leaving not even a scuffed boot print on the pine needles.

Behind the cedars, the first and second guards melted back into the shade, holding their quiet fifty-meter perimeter just as they did every day without anyone ever asking.

Out by the water, Chen Xuan turned a page of his history book. A cool breeze shook two yellow willow leaves onto the stone path beside his shoes. He took another warm sip of jasmine tea and watched a small cargo barge chug down the river.

It was a peaceful Friday afternoon.
