# Chapter 113: Hand in Hand Down Old Street

At two-thirty on Saturday afternoon, the spring sun warmed the gray flagstones of Old Street in Yunxi County.

After helping Liu Meifang and Wu Lanying wash the lunch dishes and sending Chen Yuan and Chen Hao back to their afternoon review classes with a bag of Shanghai fruit candies, Chen Xuan took Qin Yuhan by the hand and strolled down the stone steps toward the heart of Old Street.

Fifty meters behind them near the stone archway, two members of the Black Order walked unhurriedly past a display of bamboo baskets, blending into the Saturday afternoon shoppers.

Every storefront along the bluestone lane was newly painted with clean wooden signboards and bright red lanterns beneath the eaves. Because the county government and the four national banks, including China Construction Bank and Huaxia Bank, had recently poured interest-free heritage funds into Old Street, the neighborhood smelled of fresh cedar, roasted sesame seeds, and sweet osmanthus cakes.

"Look at the stone pavement here," Qin Yuhan said softly, her fingers intertwined with Chen Xuan's as she looked up at the carved wooden eaves along the lane. "When you told me in Shanghai that Old Street had been repaired after that crooked developer left, I thought they had just patched the asphalt. This looks cleaner and more charming than the tourist lanes near the Bund."

"That is because the shopkeepers here actually live upstairs and sweep their own doorsteps every morning," Chen Xuan smiled, pointing ahead to a double-wide storefront where copper kettles, steel wrenches, and garden tools gleamed in the sunlight. "Look right there. That is Second Uncle Chen Mingde's shop, Mingde Hardware."

Before they even reached the threshold of Mingde Hardware, a white-haired man in a neat blue cotton jacket stepped out from the tailor shop next door with a pair of brass scissors tucked into his pocket. It was old master tailor Yu Changshun.

"Little Xuan! So it really is you!" Yu Changshun called out, his wrinkled face lighting up as he looked from Chen Xuan to the stunning young woman holding his hand. "Your Second Uncle told the whole street yesterday that you were bringing your bride home from Shanghai today!"

"Grandpa Yu, you still have the sharpest eyes on Old Street," Chen Xuan laughed, stopping in front of the tailor shop. "Yuhan, this is Grandpa Yu Changshun. When I was seven years old and ripped my school pants climbing the walnut tree behind the courtyard, Grandpa Yu sewed a little cloth tiger over the hole so my mom would not spank me."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly and bowed politely to the old tailor. "Hello, Grandpa Yu! Thank you for saving Chen Xuan from getting spanked when he was seven."

"What a sweet voice, and such good manners!" Yu Changshun beamed, turning back to his wooden counter for a moment before returning with a flat red paper box. Inside lay a pale peach mulberry-silk scarf with delicate hand-embroidered plum blossoms along the hem. "Yesterday when Mingde told us Little Xuan's bride was coming, my old wife and I finished stitching the hem on this silk scarf. Take it as a wedding blessing from your neighbors on Old Street!"

Qin Yuhan looked at the fine, even stitches along the silk edge and knew right away that a handmade piece like this took days of patient work. She accepted the red box with both hands and a deeply grateful smile. "Thank you so much, Grandpa Yu! Let me put it on right now."

She draped the soft peach silk scarf around the collar of her cream sweater, and just across the lane, Auntie Tian stepped out of her sesame-candy stall carrying a warm paper cone of freshly toasted peanut brittle.

"Look how well that peach color suits Little Xuan's bride!" Auntie Tian cheered, tucking the warm paper cone directly into Chen Xuan's free hand without letting him reach for his wallet. "Take this peanut brittle to eat while you walk! Do not even think about paying Auntie Tian on your first visit home with your wife!"

"Thank you, Auntie Tian!" Qin Yuhan called out happily, waving as she and Chen Xuan walked further down the sunny bluestone lane.

At the corner under a wide locust tree, aromatic white steam drifted out from the open wooden doors of Gong's Noodles.

Behind the wide wooden counter stood a tall, broad-shouldered man in a white cotton apron, cleanly slicing green scallions with steady, practiced hands. It was Number 23 of the White Order, who ran Gong's Noodles as Old Street's quietest guardian.

As soon as Number 23 saw Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan approaching hand in hand, he lowered his gaze respectfully, wiped a sunny corner table near the window with a clean towel, and spoke in the warm, hearty voice of an ordinary neighborhood noodle shopkeeper.

"Little Xuan, you brought your bride back!" Number 23 smiled, pulling out two wooden chairs without a trace of his real identity showing. "You two just had lunch at home, right? Sit down for five minutes. I just boiled a fresh pot of sweet fermented-rice dumplings with osmanthus honey. Two small bowls are on the house for the newlyweds!"

"Thank you, Brother Gong," Chen Xuan smiled easily, sitting down beside Qin Yuhan as Number 23 brought over two porcelain bowls filled with white glutinous rice balls floating in golden osmanthus syrup.

Qin Yuhan picked up her small porcelain spoon and tasted a spoonful of the warm, fragrant sweet soup. Her dark eyes lit up immediately.

"This osmanthus soup is delicious!" Qin Yuhan whispered to Chen Xuan in surprise, taking another warm spoonful. "Even the head pastry chef at the five-star hotel next to IFC Mall does not make fermented-rice dumplings this tender and fragrant."

"Brother Gong spent years practicing his craft before opening up on Old Street," Chen Xuan chuckled, handing her a clean napkin while Number 23 stayed quietly behind his counter polishing bowls.

When they stepped back out under the dappled shade of the locust tree ten minutes later, Qin Yuhan held Chen Xuan's hand a little tighter and leaned her shoulder gently against his arm as they walked.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan said softly, touching the handmade peach silk scarf around her neck, "do you know why I love this place already?"

"Because Auntie Tian gave us free peanut brittle?" Chen Xuan teased.

"Because everyone here looks at each other with real warmth, not with price tags or stock numbers," Qin Yuhan smiled softly. "Walking down Old Street holding your hand feels warmer than any luxury charity gala I have ever attended in Shanghai."

"Then we will come back every spring and autumn," Chen Xuan promised, squeezing her slim fingers gently in the afternoon sunlight.
