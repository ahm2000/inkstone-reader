# Chapter 76: The Car at Building 2

Outside Building 2 of Fuxing New Village, Chen Jianguo paced back and forth beside the weathered brick archway. His knuckles were white around the nylon strap of his cloth bag. Every few steps, he lifted his wrist to check his old steel watch, then squinted down the narrow residential lane toward the main gate.

"You told your mother and Second Aunt that Mingde would be back by 9:30 AM," Chen Jianguo muttered, stopping in front of Chen Xuan. "Look at the sky, Xuan. It is nearly time already. If we do not walk over to Old Street Station right now, what if Cheng Biao transfers him out of the county before anyone notices?"

"He will not be transferred, Dad," Chen Xuan said calmly. He leaned one shoulder against the concrete pillar by the stairwell, his green thermos tucked casually under his arm. "Cheng Biao is already in handcuffs. Give it two more minutes."

Beside them, Wu Lanying clutched a folded blue cotton jacket to her chest. She had carried it all the way from Old Street, worried that the holding cell at the station had left her husband chilled to the bone. Liu Meifang stood close at her side, holding her elbow steady and patting her hand.

"Jianguo, please stop pacing in circles," Liu Meifang said softly, though her own eyes kept flicking toward the gate. "You are making Lanying dizzy. Xuan already told us the red paint was washed off the hardware shop right in front of them. If the county crews are cleaning Old Street, the station cannot possibly keep Mingde locked up."

"I know, I know," Chen Jianguo sighed, rubbing the back of his neck. "It is just hard to breathe easy until I see my brother standing on his own two feet."

A few retired neighbors sat on wooden stools near the bicycle shed, shelling green beans into tin basins. They kept glancing over with sympathetic eyes. Everyone in Fuxing New Village knew Chen Mingde was an honest man who fixed leaky pipes and worn locks for half price, and word of his arrest last night had cast a heavy shadow over the courtyard.

Chen Xuan looked calmly down the lane. Beneath the rustle of poplar leaves, the quiet hum of a well-tuned engine approached from the main road.

"Here he is," Chen Xuan said.

Chen Jianguo spun around. Wu Lanying gasped and took half a step forward.

A clean black sedan turned smoothly through the iron gate of Fuxing New Village. It carried no flashing lights and sounded no siren, rolling slowly past the bicycle shed so it would not startle the residents. The tires crunched softly over the patched asphalt before the car came to a gentle stop right in front of Building 2.

The front passenger door opened first. A young man in a neat dark jacket stepped out, carrying a crisp manila folder. He walked quickly around the back of the car and pulled open the rear door with both hands.

A moment later, Chen Mingde stepped out into the bright morning sun.

He wore the same faded blue work shirt he had worn the day before, and his hair was a little rumpled from sleeping on a hard bench, yet he stood tall and unharmed. In his left hand, he carried a small clear plastic bag holding his leather belt, his shoelaces, and his worn brown wallet.

"Mingde!" Wu Lanying cried out, her voice cracking as she hurried forward.

"Lanying!" Chen Mingde blinked against the sunlight, a wide, relieved smile breaking across his tired face. Before he could take two steps, Wu Lanying wrapped the folded cotton jacket around his shoulders and gripped his arm as if afraid he might vanish.

Chen Jianguo strode over and clapped both hands onto his younger brother's shoulders, letting out a loud, shaky breath. "You are back! Thank goodness you are back!"

Liu Meifang pressed a handkerchief to her eyes, smiling through sudden tears. "Look how thin your shirt is. You must be freezing and starving."

The young official from the sedan stepped forward politely and held out a crisp white sheet stamped with a bright red seal. He had been sent directly by the provincial task force under Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping.

"Mr. Chen Mingde, this is your official notice of full clearance," the young man said respectfully, using both hands to present the document. "Your detention last night was unlawful and has been completely struck from every record. The Yunxi County office will send a formal letter of apology this afternoon, along with compensation for the damage to your shop shutter."

Chen Mingde took the paper carefully, his fingers trembling slightly as he stared at the official red stamp. "Thank you, comrade. Truly, thank you for looking into this so quickly."

"It is our duty," the young man replied with a courteous bow. "The provincial leadership and Jingzhou City take the safety of law-abiding citizens very seriously."

Before returning to the car, the young clerk's gaze drifted briefly toward the concrete pillar by the stairwell. He saw the quiet young man standing there with a green thermos, watching the family reunion with a gentle, ordinary smile. The clerk did not know who he was, nor had any name been spoken in the briefing room, yet an odd instinct told him to show the utmost respect to everyone in this courtyard. He gave a polite nod to the entire family, slipped back into the passenger seat, and the black sedan glided quietly away.

The neighbors by the bicycle shed broke into relieved chatter, calling out warm greetings to Chen Mingde.

Chen Xuan stepped forward from the pillar, taking the small plastic bag from his uncle's hand so Chen Mingde could slide his arms into the cotton jacket. "Welcome home, Second Uncle."

Chen Mingde looked at his nephew and patted Chen Xuan's arm warmly. "Good boy, Xuan. Sorry for making you worry on your day off."

"No trouble at all," Chen Xuan said with a quiet smile. "Let us go upstairs. Second Aunt has been waiting to make you hot tea."

Surrounded by his brother, his wife, and his family, Chen Mingde walked slowly into the cool shade of Building 2's stairwell, leaving the dark shadow of the previous night firmly behind him.
