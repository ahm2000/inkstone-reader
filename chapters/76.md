# Chapter 76: The Car at Building 2

At nine o'clock on Monday morning, Chen Jianguo could not stand still outside Building 2 of Fuxing New Village. He walked to the stairwell door, turned sharply, and walked right back. His knuckles were white around the strap of his bag. Every ten seconds he looked at his watch and squinted toward the front gate.

"You told your mother and second aunt that Mingde would be home at nine," Chen Jianguo muttered, stopping beside Chen Xuan. "It is nine oh two, Xuan! If we do not go to Old Street Station right now, what if Cheng Biao moves him out of the county?"

"He will not be moved, Dad," Chen Xuan said calmly. He leaned against the doorframe with his green thermos tucked under his arm. "Wait three more minutes."

Beside them, Wu Lanying held a folded jacket tight against her chest. Her eyes were red from crying all night, and her lip shook every time a car horn sounded on the road. Liu Meifang held her arm steady, her own jaw tight with worry.

"Jianguo, stop pacing, you are making Lanying dizzy," Liu Meifang said softly. She glanced anxiously at Chen Xuan. "Xuan, tell me the truth. Over boiled eggs this morning you sounded so sure. Did someone at the city office call you, or are you just trying to keep your second aunt calm?"

"Nobody called me, Mom," Chen Xuan replied, watching the lane calmly. "Cheng Biao broke the law in broad daylight. The people above Yunxi County do not need my phone call to see a fire that big."

"The people above Yunxi County!" Chen Jianguo laughed bitterly. He pushed his slipping glasses up with a shaking thumb. "When has the province ever dropped out of the sky at nine in the morning for a small hardware shopkeeper? Deng Yongchang eats dinner with Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming every week! Liang Guodong runs the police bureau like his own backyard! You stand here drinking hot water like we are waiting for a bus!"

Wu Lanying gasped and grabbed Liu Meifang's wrist. "Look. At the gate."

A black sedan with provincial government plates rolled silently past the rusted iron gate. There were no sirens and no flashing lights, only the quiet hum of a clean engine rolling steadily toward Building 2. Two neighbors leaning on the second-floor railing froze mid-motion, staring down at the rare provincial license plates.

Chen Jianguo's breath caught. All color drained from his face. "Provincial plates," he stammered, backing half a step toward Chen Xuan. "Why is a provincial car stopping here? Did Cheng Biao push the case higher? Xuan, stand behind me, do not say a word!"

The black car braked smoothly ten feet from the front steps. The front passenger door opened, and a tall, lean man in a dark gray jacket stepped out. His face was weathered and sharp, carrying the clear weight of a senior provincial officer. It was Han Zhiyuan, lead inspector of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group.

Han Zhiyuan walked briskly around the car. He gripped the back door handle and opened it with both hands, bowing his head slightly in clear respect.

"Mr. Chen Mingde," Han Zhiyuan said clearly. "We are here. Watch your step."

Chen Mingde stepped out of the back seat. His dark blue work jacket was wrinkled from the wooden bench, and his hair was messy. He blinked in the morning sun as if he still could not believe he was standing outside his brother's building.

"Mingde!" Wu Lanying cried out. She stumbled forward two steps, then froze in sudden fear of the tall official beside the car.

Chen Mingde saw his wife and brother, and all the stiffness melted from his shoulders. "Lanying! Jianguo! I am fine, completely fine!"

Han Zhiyuan stepped neatly aside so Wu Lanying could reach her husband. Then he held out a red-stamped official folder with both hands toward Chen Mingde.

"Mr. Chen Mingde, on behalf of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group and the provincial court oversight team, I am handing you your Cleared Name Paper and Wrongful Arrest Dismissal," Han Zhiyuan said gravely. "Every fake charge filed by Cheng Biao at Old Street Station has been wiped from the provincial records. You have zero record, zero open orders, and zero need to ever set foot inside a police station."

Chen Jianguo stared at the bright red provincial stamp through his fogged glasses, his mouth hanging open. "Wait, comrade leader," he blurted out in pure shock. "You did not bring him here on bail? What about Cheng Biao? What about Yan Tiehu and Hongrui Real Estate?"

"Cheng Biao, Yan Tiehu, County Police Chief Liang Guodong, Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming, and Hongrui Chairman Deng Yongchang have all been arrested by provincial officers this morning," Han Zhiyuan answered cleanly. "An official team is already checking the damage to Mingde Hardware. Full payout papers will be brought straight to your door."

Wu Lanying pressed both hands over her mouth. Tears spilled down her cheeks as she clutched Chen Mingde's sleeve.

Chen Mingde looked down at the red-stamped paper, his fingers trembling against the page. "Inspector Han Zhiyuan," he said hoarsely, raising his eyes in honest wonder. "I am just a man who sells hinges and bolts on Old Street. All night I thought my family was finished. How did a provincial team move so fast?"

"When rot reaches the bone, the province does not wait for a complaint," Han Zhiyuan replied smoothly, hiding every trace of the capital's thunder behind his calm face. "You suffered a great wrong, Mr. Chen Mingde. Go upstairs, rest, and eat with your family."

Turning to nod to the family, Han Zhiyuan's eyes passed over Liu Meifang and Chen Jianguo and landed squarely on the young man standing beside the doorframe.

Han Zhiyuan paused.

Chen Xuan had not rushed forward. He had not gasped at the provincial plates or flinched at the names of Sun Weiming and Deng Yongchang. He simply stood in an ordinary dark jacket holding a green thermos. He watched with a quiet, deep steadiness that made the hair on the back of Han Zhiyuan's neck prickle.

"And this young man is?" Han Zhiyuan asked, stepping half a pace closer as his sharp eyes studied him.

"My son, Chen Xuan," Chen Jianguo answered quickly, pride breaking through his shock. "Home for a few days. He works an ordinary office job in the city."

"Good morning, Inspector Han Zhiyuan," Chen Xuan said politely, nodding his head just a tiny bit. His voice was mild and completely free of nerves or flattery. "Thank you for bringing my second uncle home cleanly and on time."

On time.

Two simple words, yet Han Zhiyuan felt a sudden, strange jolt in his chest as his eyes met Chen Xuan's. There was no pride or boasting in the young man's gaze. There was only the calm weight of someone who had watched storms far bigger than Yunxi County break without ever raising his voice. For one strange second, the seasoned provincial inspector felt like a junior officer reporting a finished order.

"It is our duty, Mr. Chen Xuan," Han Zhiyuan said slowly, choosing his words with sudden care. "If anyone in Yunxi County causes your family even a tiny bit of trouble, my direct phone number is on the back of that paper."

"I doubt anyone will," Chen Xuan replied with a faint smile. "Have a safe drive back, Inspector."

Han Zhiyuan studied him for one heartbeat longer, shook off the chill with a quiet breath, and walked back to the car. As the black sedan backed out of the lane, Han Zhiyuan glanced into the side mirror and muttered to his driver.

"An ordinary office worker. Strange. I have never seen an ordinary office worker with eyes that still."

By the stairwell, Chen Jianguo looked down at his watch and stared at the face in disbelief.

Nine oh five sharp.
