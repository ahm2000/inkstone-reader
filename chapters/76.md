# Chapter 76: The Car at Building 2

At nine o'clock on Monday morning, Chen Jianguo could not stand still outside Building 2 of Fuxing New Village. He paced toward the stairwell door, turned sharply, and paced back, knuckles white around his satchel strap. Every ten seconds he checked his watch and squinted toward the gate.

"You told your mother and second aunt that Mingde would be home at nine," Chen Jianguo muttered, stopping beside Chen Xuan. "It is nine oh two, Xuan! If we do not go to Old Street Station now, what if Cheng Biao transfers him out of the county?"

"He will not be transferred, Dad," Chen Xuan said evenly, leaning against the doorframe with his green thermos tucked under his arm. "Wait three more minutes."

Beside them, Wu Lanying clutched a folded jacket against her chest, eyes red from crying all night. Her lip trembled at every horn on the road. Liu Meifang held her elbow steadily, jaw clenched tight.

"Jianguo, stop pacing, you are making Lanying dizzy," Liu Meifang said softly, glancing anxiously at Chen Xuan. "Xuan, tell me honestly. Over boiled eggs this morning you sounded so sure. Did someone at the municipal office call you, or are you just keeping your second aunt calm?"

"Nobody called me, Mom," Chen Xuan replied, watching the lane calmly. "Cheng Biao broke the law in broad daylight. The people above Yunxi County do not need my call to see a fire that big."

"The people above Yunxi County!" Chen Jianguo laughed bitterly, shoving his slipping glasses up with a shaking thumb. "When has the province ever dropped out of the sky at nine in the morning for a small hardware shopkeeper? Deng Yongchang dines with Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming every week! Liang Guodong runs the bureau like his own yard! You stand here drinking hot water as if we are waiting for a bus!"

Wu Lanying gasped and grabbed Liu Meifang's wrist. "Look. At the gate."

A black sedan with provincial government plates glided silently past the rusted iron gate. No sirens, no flashing lights, only the low hum of a clean engine rolling steadily toward Building 2. Two neighbors on the second-floor railing froze mid-motion, staring down at the unfamiliar provincial license prefix.

Chen Jianguo's breath caught. His face drained of color. "Provincial plates," he stammered, backing half a step toward Chen Xuan. "Why is a provincial car stopping here? Did Cheng Biao escalate the case? Xuan, stand behind me, do not say a word!"

The sedan braked smoothly ten feet from the steps. The front passenger door opened, and a tall, lean man in a dark charcoal jacket stepped out. His face was weathered and sharp, carrying the unmistakable authority of a senior provincial investigator. It was Han Zhiyuan, lead inspector of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group.

Han Zhiyuan walked briskly around the sedan, gripped the rear door handle, and opened it with both hands, inclining his head in clear respect.

"Mr. Chen Mingde," Han Zhiyuan said clearly. "We are here. Watch your step."

Chen Mingde stepped out of the back seat. His dark blue work jacket was wrinkled from the holding bench, his hair mussed, and he blinked against the morning sun as if still unable to believe he was standing outside his brother's building.

"Mingde!" Wu Lanying cried out, stumbling forward two steps before freezing in sudden fear of the official beside the car.

Chen Mingde saw his wife and brother, and the stiffness broke from his shoulders. "Lanying! Jianguo! I am fine, completely fine!"

Han Zhiyuan stepped neatly aside so Wu Lanying could reach her husband, then presented a crimson-stamped official folder with both hands toward Chen Mingde.

"Mr. Chen Mingde, on behalf of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group and the provincial judicial oversight team, I am delivering your Certificate of Exoneration and Wrongful Detention Dismissal," Han Zhiyuan said gravely. "Every fabricated charge filed by Cheng Biao at Old Street Station has been voided from the provincial registry. You have zero record, zero pending summons, and zero need to ever set foot inside a police station."

Chen Jianguo stared at the heavy red provincial seal through fogged lenses, his jaw slack. "Wait, comrade leader," he blurted out in sheer shock. "You did not bring him here on bail? What about Cheng Biao? What about Yan Tiehu and Hongrui Real Estate?"

"Cheng Biao, Yan Tiehu, County Public Security Chief Liang Guodong, Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming, and Hongrui Chairman Deng Yongchang have all been detained by provincial authorities this morning," Han Zhiyuan answered cleanly. "A formal team is already auditing the damage to Mingde Hardware. Official compensation paperwork will be delivered directly to your door."

Wu Lanying pressed both hands over her mouth, tears spilling down her cheeks as she clutched Chen Mingde's sleeve.

Chen Mingde looked down at the red-stamped certificate, fingers trembling against the paper. "Inspector Han Zhiyuan," he said hoarsely, raising his eyes in honest bewilderment. "I am just a man who sells hinges and bolts on Old Street. All night I thought my family was finished. How did a provincial team move so fast?"

"When rot reaches the bone, the province does not wait for a petition," Han Zhiyuan replied smoothly, keeping every trace of the capital's thunder behind his calm mask. "You endured an injustice, Mr. Chen Mingde. Go upstairs, rest, and eat with your family."

Turning to nod to the family, Han Zhiyuan's gaze passed over Liu Meifang and Chen Jianguo and landed squarely on the young man standing beside the doorframe.

Han Zhiyuan paused.

Chen Xuan had not rushed forward. He had not gasped at the provincial plates or flinched at the names of Sun Weiming and Deng Yongchang. He simply stood in an ordinary dark jacket holding a green thermos, watching with a quiet, bottomless steadiness that made the hair on the back of Han Zhiyuan's neck prickle.

"And this young man is?" Han Zhiyuan asked, stepping half a pace closer, his seasoned eyes probing instinctively.

"My son, Chen Xuan," Chen Jianguo answered quickly, pride breaking through his bewilderment. "Home for a few days. Works an ordinary office job in the city."

"Good morning, Inspector Han Zhiyuan," Chen Xuan said politely, inclining his head a fraction of an inch. His voice was mild and completely free of flattery or nerves. "Thank you for bringing my second uncle home cleanly and on time."

On time.

Two ordinary words, yet Han Zhiyuan felt a sudden, inexplicable jolt in his chest as his eyes met Chen Xuan's. There was no arrogance in the young man's gaze, only the calm weight of someone who had watched storms far larger than Yunxi County break without ever raising his voice. For one strange second, the seasoned provincial inspector felt like a junior officer reporting a completed order.

"It is our duty, Mr. Chen Xuan," Han Zhiyuan said slowly, choosing his words with unexpected care. "If anyone in Yunxi County causes your family even a whisper of trouble, my direct number is on the back of that certificate."

"I doubt anyone will," Chen Xuan replied with a faint smile. "Safe drive back, Inspector."

Han Zhiyuan studied him for one heartbeat longer, shook off the chill with a quiet breath, and returned to the sedan. As the black car backed out of the lane, Han Zhiyuan glanced into the side mirror and murmured to his driver.

"An ordinary office worker. Strange. I have never seen an ordinary office worker with eyes that still."

By the stairwell, Chen Jianguo checked his watch and stared at the dial in disbelief.

Nine oh five sharp.
