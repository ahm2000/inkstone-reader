# Chapter 38: Dinner Is at Six

At ten o'clock, Tang Xiaoyu's father had still not come.

She sat very straight on the edge of her chair and said nothing. Chen Jianguo watched the clock over the noodle counter and then watched her. At last, he took out his phone and dialed the number printed on a poster by the door.

A recording answered. He held the phone out so that everybody could hear it.

*The Civil Affairs Office is closed on weekends and opens Monday at nine. The Children's Shelter is full tonight. In an emergency, please call the police.*

"The police," Chen Jianguo said slowly. "They would put her on a bench in a hall until morning."

"I'm fine," Tang Xiaoyu said. "I can go back to the yard."

"What yard? Where is your father?"

She looked at the table. "He's late."

Chen Xuan said nothing. He knew he was not going to leave her out in the street, and he also knew it was not his place to say so. His father was the one who spoke at home, and the one who made the call.

Chen Jianguo put the phone away and stood up.

"Child, you are coming home with us. You sleep in my daughter's room tonight. On Monday we call the office, and they will sort it out properly."

"I don't want to be a trouble."

"A trouble would be leaving you here." He turned to the cook and wrote a number on a napkin. "If a Mr. Tang comes asking, give him this. It's our address too. Please tell him his girl is warm and fed."

Number Twenty-three took the napkin with both hands and nodded. His face did not change at all.

He would not need it. But he folded it carefully and put it in his shirt pocket, because it had come from the Boss's father.

* * *

Liu Meifang opened the door of Room 601 with her ladle still in her hand. She looked at her husband, then at her two sons, then at the thin girl in the huge jacket standing behind them.

"Jianguo. What is this?"

"A girl whose father is late," Chen Jianguo said. "She needs a bed tonight. I said yes."

Liu Meifang looked at the girl for exactly three seconds. Then her whole face changed.

"One more bowl!" she called into the kitchen, though nobody was in there. "Hao, get the heater. Yuan, make up your bed with the clean sheets. Come in, child, come in. Shoes are by the door, don't worry about the mat."

"We already ate noodles, Mom," Chen Hao said.

"Noodles is not dinner. I made ribs." She took the girl's wrists in both hands and turned them over. "Look at this. Like two chopsticks. Who sends a child out in a thin jacket? Too thin. Much too thin."

"Grandma will say the same thing," Chen Yuan said from the sofa. She had put down her red pen and was watching with her chin on her fist. "Dad, last time you went out for noodles, you brought home a stray cat. This time a girl. What is next, an elephant?"

"Chen Yuan."

"I'm only asking."

Liu Meifang was already dialing. She put the phone on speaker and laid it on the table.

"Mother, it's me. We have a girl here, thirteen, no father around tonight. Yes, in the house. Yes, a real girl."

A crackle came through, then the sharp voice of Zhou Guilan in Hongshi Village.

"Thirteen? Is she thin?"

"Very thin, Mother."

"I knew it. I could hear it in your voice. Put her on."

Tang Xiaoyu stared at the phone like it was a snake. Chen Yuan nudged her with an elbow.

"Hello," Tang Xiaoyu said stiffly.

"Child, you are too thin," Zhou Guilan said. "Eat the ribs. Then eat again. We talk tomorrow."

"Yes, Grandma." She said it without thinking, and her ears went pink.

Chen Xuan took off his shoes and sat down at the table. Liu Meifang put a bowl of ribs and rice in front of the girl and a second, smaller bowl in front of him.

"You eat too. You have been out all day."

"Mom. I had noodles."

"One more bowl won't kill you, you boy."

The girl picked up her chopsticks and ate the ribs slowly, one at a time, as if she were afraid somebody would take them back. Nobody watched her. Liu Meifang busied herself at the stove, with her back turned on purpose.

After a while, Chen Hao came out of his room with a white charger in his hand.

"Your phone is dying. I could see it in the noodle shop. Here, use mine."

"You need it."

"I have two." It was not exactly true, but he said it like a man telling the weather.

Liu Meifang put a new toothbrush still in its plastic and a folded blue towel on top of a pile of Chen Yuan's old pajamas. Tang Xiaoyu took the pile with both arms and looked at the floor.

She did not say thank you. Liu Meifang saw it and did not mind.

"Don't thank me," she said. "Just sleep."

Later, Chen Xuan noticed through the half-open door of his sister's room that the extra blanket had been folded into a perfect square and set on the chair. The girl lay on top of the quilt with her sneakers beside the bed. She was wearing her clothes, and a brown glove stuck out from under the pillow.

* * *

At eleven, the apartment was quiet. Liu Meifang sent Chen Xuan with a glass of warm water.

He knocked softly and opened the door a crack. The room was dark. A pair of eyes shone in the light from the hall.

"Water, if you want it," he said, and set the glass on the desk.

She did not touch it. She waited until his hand was on the doorknob.

"Why are you being nice to me?" she whispered. It came out thin and hard, a girl trying not to shake. "What do you want?"

Chen Xuan stopped. He did not turn on the light, and he did not make a speech.

"Dinner is at six," he said. "That's all."

He closed the door quietly. Behind it, there was a long silence. Then he heard the springs creak, once.

* * *

On the balcony, the wind smelled like river and fried onions.

Chen Xuan stood with his elbows on the rail and looked down at the lamps of Fuxing New Village. In his pocket the gray phone buzzed once.

*Number One: Number Twenty-three has sent me a report. A girl, Tang Xiaoyu, thirteen, says her father works for Tiancheng Demolition, in the yard on Dongqiao Road. She lied twice in the noodle shop. Do you want me to find her father?*

Chen Xuan did not answer for a while.

He was thinking about a rough brown glove, three sizes too big, which a child carried everywhere and slept beside. A man's glove was something you took with you when you could not take the man.

He did not want to be right.

He typed with one thumb. *Yes. Quietly. Tonight.*

*Yes, Boss.*

Across the road, on a bench under the street lamp, a man in a gray cap sat with his hands in his pockets, looking at nothing. Chen Xuan did not notice him.

Inside, his mother was washing the pot. Behind the closed door of Chen Yuan's room, the bed gave one more small creak. Chen Yuan herself had taken the sofa with a pillow and a blanket and was already asleep, and then the apartment slept.
