# Chapter 38: The Quiet Rule

The file was only three pages long, and Number One had read it four times.

He kept it on paper, in a courtyard house in Qingyan, under a lamp. He did not want this one on any screen.

Tang Dawei, forty-one, a laborer at Tiancheng Demolition. Sixty-one days ago he fell from a scaffold at the company's yard on Dongqiao Road. He died before the ambulance came.

The owner of Tiancheng, Shen Jinbao, was Cheng Biao's brother-in-law. He did not report the death. He paid a crew boss named Peng Hu 600,000 yuan to "settle the family quietly." That was ten years of Tang Dawei's pay.

Peng Hu kept every yuan. A "voluntary leave" form with a fake signature turned the dead man into a worker who had quit and gone away.

Tang Dawei had a thirteen-year-old daughter. Her name was Tang Xiaoyu. Peng Hu told her that her father had gone north for a job and would send for her. Then the company cleared her out of the workers' dormitory.

"Why is it legal?" Number Seven asked. He was the van captain, and he did not like paper.

Number One Hundred and Four, the legal desk of the Black Pine Guard, tapped the page with one finger.

"It is not legal. It is just not punished. The county rule gives a company thirty days to report a death at work, and Shen Jinbao used those thirty days to turn a dead man into a man who quit."

"And now?"

"Now it is a late form. The fine is 20,000 yuan."

Nobody in the room spoke. Number Seven's jaw was tight, and his big hands had closed into fists on his knees. Twenty thousand yuan was four months of a laborer's pay. It was the price of a father.

Number One closed the folder and picked up the gray phone.

"He should hear it from me tonight," he said. "Not in a message."

* * *

On the sixth floor of Building 2, Chen Xuan stood on the balcony with the glass door shut.

Behind the glass, the television was loud. Chen Hao was losing a video game. Chen Jianguo was asleep in his chair with his mouth open. Liu Meifang was packing ribs into a box for the bus.

"On a Sunday?" Chen Yuan shouted through the door. "Your boss is a vampire, Brother."

"Mm," Chen Xuan said, and lifted the gray phone to his ear.

Number One told him everything, short and plain. The fall. The silence money. The fake form. The thirty days.

Chen Xuan held the railing. The metal was cold under his hands.

Inside, Tang Xiaoyu was drying a cup. She held it up to the light to check it, the way someone does who is afraid of doing it wrong. Last night she had stood outside Gong's Noodles and said she was waiting for her dad.

His eyes went hot, and for a moment he could not see the street.

"Does she know?" he asked.

"No, Boss. She was told he went north."

Number One said nothing else. He waited a full minute and did not make a sound.

Chen Xuan wiped his face on his sleeve. When he spoke again, his voice was flat and quiet, and every word was exact.

"The thirty days end tonight. Anyone who hides a death at work goes to prison."

"Yes."

"Open Tiancheng. Permits, accounts, every site. The lawful way, at the Labor Inspection office, Monday morning."

"Yes."

"Find what Peng Hu took. Every yuan goes to the girl."

"Yes, Boss."

He hung up. Below the balcony, a scooter went by with its light on. He watched it until it turned the corner.

Then he put the phone in his pocket, wiped his face once more, and went inside.

"Your nose is red," Liu Meifang said at once. "Are you getting a cold? Sit. Soup."

"It's the wind, Mom."

"Always an excuse. Too thin and too tired. Chen Jianguo, wake up. Your son is sick."

"I'm not sick," Chen Jianguo said, with his eyes still shut. "A slow ox does not get sick. It just stands there."

Chen Hao groaned. "That one is bad even for you, Dad."

Chen Xuan sat down and drank the soup. Tang Xiaoyu put the cup on the shelf and looked at him for a moment.

"You look sad," she said.

"Spreadsheet," Chen Xuan said. "A bad one."

"Spreadsheets are not sad."

"Mine are."

She almost smiled. He took the last rib from the box and put it in her bowl before his mother could stop him.

"That's for the bus," Liu Meifang said.

"The bus can have an apple."

* * *

At 11:40 that night, Jiang Lixin was eating instant noodles in the night duty room of the Provincial Labor Department.

It was a quiet job. Nothing happened after ten except the heater clicking.

In the corner stood an old fax machine that nobody had used in a year. It began to hum.

Jiang Lixin stared at it with a fork halfway to his mouth. One page slid out and lay in the tray.

The top line said: Amendment to Article 17, Rules on Reporting Deaths at Work.

He read it slowly. A death at work must be reported within twenty-four hours. A company that hides one commits the crime of concealment. The sentence is not less than five years in prison. No grace period applies. The rule takes effect on receipt.

At the bottom there was a round red seal. It had no office name in it. Inside the ring was only a small branch of pine.

He looked at the top of the page. The line for the sender's number was blank.

He turned to his computer and opened the rules database. Article 17 was already there, with the new words. Beside it, the time of the change read 23:40. The line for the editor's name was empty.

Jiang Lixin checked the log. Nothing had been entered. No one had changed it. The rule had simply changed.

He had worked in this building for eleven years. A rule took months to change. First came a draft, then a meeting, then a vote, then a stamp from three different offices. He had never seen one change between two bites of noodles.

His hands felt cold. He tried the door of the room, though nobody could have come in. It was locked, as it always was.

He read the page a second time. Then he put down his fork and called his director.

It rang six times.

"Jiang Lixin," said Director Xu Zhongping, and his voice was thick with sleep. "If this is not a fire, I will remember it."

"Director, it is a rule. A new rule, from tonight. It is on the system and on a fax page. Nobody sent it."

"Who stamped it?"

"I cannot say, sir. A circle. No office name. And a small pine branch."

The line was silent so long that Jiang Lixin thought it had cut off. Then he heard a bed creak and a lamp click on.

"Read me the first line again," Director Xu Zhongping said quietly. "Slowly."
