# Chapter 24: The Stolen Blueprints

By eleven on Sunday night, behind a heavy biometric steel door adjoining the Financial Intelligence room in Lanshui, the Signals and Cyber chamber sat sealed inside a windowless Faraday vault. No wireless signal could enter or leave.

Seated at the air-gapped decryption rig, Number 34 stared at a matte-silver solid-state courier drive resting in a write-blocker cradle, unmarked and no larger than a cigarette lighter.

Three hours earlier, Black Pine field operatives Number 14 and Number 27 had entered the twenty-eighth floor of the Lanshui Grand River Hotel. Their target was a quiet middleman on a Hong Kong logistics passport who had met twice with Shengyuan Capital's deputy treasurer over the past forty-eight hours. While the middleman dined on steamed river fish downstairs, Number 14 bypassed the suite safe's optical lock without a scratch, and Number 27 performed a raw sector-by-sector clone of the drive in four minutes and twelve seconds. The original drive went back to the exact millimeter before dessert arrived.

Now, the cloned image sat inside Number 34's isolated sandbox. Beside him stood junior analysts Number 42 and Number 56, silent and watchful.

"Importing the thirty-two-character delivery hashes from Number 73," Number 34 said quietly, feeding an optical cartridge into the air-gapped reader. "Triple-cascade cipher. Layer one is commercial elliptic-curve. Layer two is a custom polynomial scramble. Layer three requires the exact Zurich disbursement timestamp from four days ago."

"Did the optical hash verify clean?" Number 42 asked quietly.

"Exact match," Number 34 replied. He entered the final seed and pressed execute.

Cooling fans spun up to a low whine. Blocks of encrypted noise fractured into clean directories across three dark monitors: `ARCHIVE_09_MARITIME`, `ARCHIVE_14_GRID_SOUTH`, `ARCHIVE_22_FRONTIER_LOG`.

Number 34 clicked open the first folder.

A high-resolution vector rendering expanded across the center display, rotating slowly in crisp white lines. Dense tables of signal-processing equations, phased-array module tolerances, and dual-band frequency-hopping parameters scrolled down the right screen.

Across the top margin of every sheet sat a crimson classification header reserved exclusively for top-tier domestic naval defense engineering.

Number 42's hand froze halfway to his keyboard. Behind him, Number 56 stopped breathing for two full seconds, eyes wide on the crimson header.

"Verify the structural signature," Number 34 ordered, his voice dropping to a razor-thin hush. "Tell me that is a commercial marine transceiver."

Number 42 ran a rapid geometry check against Black Pine's offline index. His fingers moved stiffly, as though the room had dropped below freezing. "Negative. Not commercial hardware."

"Identify it," Number 34 said tightly.

Number 42 swallowed once. "Active phased-array radar architecture deployed on our newest domestic destroyer class. Cooling-manifold tolerances, clutter-rejection algorithms, and electronic counter-countermeasure waveform tables."

"And if a foreign navy holds these schematics?" Number 34 asked flatly.

"They can model the radar's blind frequencies and jamming thresholds before a ship leaves port," Number 42 answered, knuckles pale on the desk edge. "Years of naval research, neutralized on paper."

Nobody moved. The quiet hum of the servers suddenly felt suffocating.

"Open archive fourteen," Number 34 said evenly, muscles along his jaw locked rigid.

The second directory unpacked across the screens: cryptographic certificates and dynamic network topology maps.

Number 56 leaned forward so fast his chair creaked, eyes widening behind dark frames. "Wait. Stop the scroll."

"What is it?" Number 34 asked sharply.

"Not provincial utility billing tables," Number 56 said, his voice dry and tense. "That is the core supervisory control architecture for the entire southern coastal power grid. Load-balancing authentication keys, ultra-high-voltage substation failover protocols, and automated dispatch override sequences."

"Give me the real-world impact," Number 34 said coldly.

Number 56 looked up, color drained from his face. "With these keys and topology maps, an adversary would not need a single missile to cripple six coastal provinces. During a crisis, one coordinated injection could desynchronize main transformer relays across the southern grid, burning out replacement turbines that take eighteen months to build. Half the industrial coast goes dark in ninety seconds."

Number 34 stared at the glowing grid lines for three long seconds, expression stone-cold, then clicked open the third folder: `ARCHIVE_22_FRONTIER_LOG`.

Spreadsheets, satellite-referenced terrain overlays, and encrypted rail timetables filled the monitor. Column after column detailed border-garrison logistics rosters: battalion rotations, underground fuel-depot coordinates, heavy-armor railhead loading windows, and backup satellite-relay frequencies across the southwestern and northern frontiers.

Number 42 let out a jagged breath. "Border supply lines, southern power-grid overrides, and active destroyer radar."

At the root of all three directories sat one verification receipt. Number 34 placed it beside the SWIFT transfer printout from Number 11's room.

The thirty-two-character delivery hashes matched to the last digit. The timestamps matched to the exact second.

The offshore gold-backed bearer instruments flowing out of Shengyuan Capital's hidden Lanshui vault to foreign defense-intelligence cutouts in Zurich, Singapore, and the Cayman Islands were not merely stolen money hiding overseas. They were dual-use operational funds paying domestic traitors inside strategic ministries, defense contractors, and grid bureaus while laundering twenty years of sovereign theft.

And at the very bottom of the chain, in a quiet county called Yunxi, Hongrui Real Estate was tearing down Old Street and murdering honest shopkeepers like Lin Guoliang simply to keep an underground rare-earth smuggling tunnel sealed.

"This is not local corruption," Number 42 whispered, barely audible inside the vault. "Not even smuggling or embezzlement."

"It is high treason," Number 34 said quietly. "Active foreign espionage, hiding behind county demolitions and municipal development bonds."

For ten full seconds, nobody spoke. Even hardened Black Pine Guard specialists, veterans who had dismantled cartels, neutralized corporate assassins, and navigated shadow wars for Chen Xuan across four continents, stood rigid before the three screens.

Stealing money from state projects was greed. Selling naval radar arrays, power-grid kill switches, and border-garrison rosters to foreign intelligence was cutting the nation's throat from inside.

Number 34 exhaled slowly, copied the decrypted evidence and full chain-of-custody hashes onto a hardware-encrypted obsidian cartridge, signed it with his numeric cipher, sealed it in a lead-lined case, and wiped volatile memory to zero.

"Maintain absolute chamber silence," Number 34 instructed Number 42 and Number 56. "Nobody leaves this floor. Nobody breathes a word of what rendered here."

"Understood," both analysts answered in tight unison.

Carrying the cold metal case, Number 34 unlocked the biometric door and stepped into the corridor. Beyond the glass partition overlooking Financial Intelligence, Number 1 stood motionless, hands clasped behind his back. One look at Number 34's rigid shoulders and the lead-lined case told Number 1 everything before a word passed between them.

"Bring it to the central console," Number 1 said quietly, eyes dark and unreadable. "Number 11 is finishing the retroactive sweep."
