# Chapter 24: The Stolen Blueprints

By eleven on Sunday night, behind a heavy steel door next to the Financial Intelligence room in Sha City, the the tech team chamber sat sealed inside a windowless shielded vault. No wireless signal could enter or leave the room.

Seated at an offline computer desk, Number 34 stared at a small matte-silver drive resting in a safe reader. It was unmarked and no larger than a cigarette lighter.

Three hours earlier, White Guard field agents Number 14 and Number 27 had entered the twenty-eighth floor of the Sha City Grand River Hotel. Their target was a quiet middleman traveling on a Hong Kong shipping passport who had met twice with Shengyuan Capital's deputy treasurer over the past two days. While the middleman ate steamed river fish downstairs, Number 14 opened the suite safe's lock without leaving a single scratch. Number 27 made a complete copy of the drive in four minutes and twelve seconds. They placed the original drive back in the exact same spot before dessert arrived.

Now, the copied drive sat inside Number 34's offline computer. Beside him stood Number 42 and Number 56, silent and watchful.

"Loading the thirty-two-character delivery codes from Number 73," Number 34 said quietly, sliding a disc into the offline reader. "Three layers of locks. Layer one is a standard commercial lock. Layer two is a custom scramble. Layer three needs the exact Zurich payout timestamp from four days ago."

"Did the code match cleanly?" Number 42 asked quietly.

"Exact match," Number 34 replied. He typed in the final key and pressed enter.

The computer fans spun up with a low hum. Blocks of locked text broke apart into three clean folders across three dark monitors: `ARCHIVE_09_MARITIME`, `ARCHIVE_14_GRID_SOUTH`, and `ARCHIVE_22_FRONTIER_LOG`.

Number 34 clicked open the first folder.

A sharp 3D drawing opened on the center screen, turning slowly in crisp white lines. Dense pages of radar math, antenna specs, and signal-switching tables scrolled down the right monitor.

Across the top of every single page sat a dark red top-secret stamp used only for the country's highest naval defense work.

Number 42's hand froze halfway to his keyboard. Behind him, Number 56 stopped breathing for two full seconds, his eyes wide on the red stamp.

"Check the design," Number 34 ordered, his voice dropping to a razor-thin hush. "Tell me that is a commercial ship radio."

Number 42 ran a quick check against White Guard's offline files. His fingers moved stiffly, as if the room had suddenly dropped below freezing. "No. Not commercial gear."

"Name it," Number 34 said tightly.

Number 42 swallowed once. "It is the main radar system used on our newest warships. Cooling pipe specs, signal-cleaning code, and anti-jamming charts."

"And if a foreign navy holds these plans?" Number 34 asked flatly.

"They can map the radar's blind spots and jamming limits before a ship ever leaves port," Number 42 answered, his knuckles turning pale on the desk edge. "Years of naval research, ruined on paper."

Nobody moved. The quiet hum of the machines suddenly felt heavy and suffocating.

"Open archive fourteen," Number 34 said evenly, the muscles along his jaw locked tight.

The second folder opened across the screens, showing secret access keys and live network maps.

Number 56 leaned forward so fast his chair creaked, his eyes widening behind dark glasses. "Wait. Stop the scroll."

"What is it?" Number 34 asked sharply.

"Not local power billing tables," Number 56 said, his voice dry and tense. "That is the main control system for the entire southern coastal power grid. Power-balancing keys, main station backup rules, and automatic remote shutoff commands."

"Give me the real-world damage," Number 34 said coldly.

Number 56 looked up, all color gone from his face. "With these keys and maps, an enemy would not need a single missile to cripple six coastal provinces. During a crisis, one timed strike could knock the main power switches out of sync across the southern grid. It would burn out giant generators that take eighteen months to build. Half the industrial coast goes dark in ninety seconds."

Number 34 stared at the glowing grid lines for three long seconds, his face stone-cold. Then he clicked open the third folder: `ARCHIVE_22_FRONTIER_LOG`.

Spreadsheets, satellite maps, and secret rail timetables filled the monitor. Column after column listed border troop supply plans: unit rotations, underground fuel storage sites, heavy-armor train loading windows, and backup satellite radio channels across the southwest and north borders.

Number 42 let out a ragged breath. "Border supply lines, southern power-grid shutoffs, and active warship radar."

At the bottom of all three folders sat one receipt. Number 34 laid it beside the bank transfer printout from Number 11's room.

The thirty-two-character delivery codes matched to the last digit. The timestamps matched to the exact second.

The overseas gold-backed cash papers flowing out of Shengyuan Capital's hidden Sha City vault to foreign military intelligence fronts in Zurich, Singapore, and the Cayman Islands were not just stolen money hiding abroad. They were active operating funds paying domestic traitors inside top ministries, defense firms, and power bureaus while washing twenty years of stolen state money.

And at the very bottom of the chain, in a quiet county called Yunxi, Hongrui Real Estate was tearing down Old Street and murdering honest shopkeepers like Lin Guoliang simply to keep an underground rare-earth smuggling tunnel sealed.

"This is not local corruption," Number 42 whispered, barely audible inside the vault. "Not even smuggling or theft."

"It is high treason," Number 34 said quietly. "Active foreign spying, hiding behind county demolitions and local building loans."

For ten full seconds, nobody spoke. Even hardened White Guard specialists, veterans who had broken crime rings, stopped corporate killers, and fought shadow battles for Chen Xuan across four continents, stood stiff before the three screens.

Stealing money from state projects was greed. Selling warship radar plans, power-grid shutoff keys, and border troop rosters to foreign intelligence was cutting the nation's throat from the inside.

Number 34 let out a slow breath. He copied the unlocked files and full proof records onto a locked black drive, signed it with his number code, sealed it inside a lead-lined metal case, and wiped the computer's memory clean to zero.

"Keep total silence in this room," Number 34 told Number 42 and Number 56. "Nobody leaves this floor. Nobody breathes a word of what appeared here."

"Understood," both analysts answered in tight unison.

Carrying the cold metal case, Number 34 unlocked the heavy steel door and stepped into the hall. Beyond the glass wall overlooking Financial Intelligence, Number 1 stood motionless, his hands clasped behind his back. One look at Number 34's rigid shoulders and the lead-lined case told Number 1 everything before a single word passed between them.

"Bring it to the main desk," Number 1 said quietly, his eyes dark and unreadable. "Number 11 is finishing the twenty-year check."
