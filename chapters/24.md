# Chapter 24: Come Back Monday

The next morning, Chen Xuan walked into the Old Street Police Station lobby with four red-bean buns and one clear goal, which was to find a proper place for Xiaoyu to stay until Monday.

The night duty officer had changed, and Instructor Qiu had gone home to sleep, so only the girl was left on the steel bench. She wore the same gray jacket, with her backpack on her knees and the station's blanket folded beside her in a perfect square.

"Good morning, Xiaoyu, did you sleep well?" Chen Xuan asked.

"Yes."

Auxiliary Officer Ma was passing with a mop, and he leaned over to whisper, "She didn't."

First came the ordinary road, so Chen Xuan found the Civil Affairs Bureau number on his phone and pressed call. The Civil Affairs Bureau is the county office that looks after people who have nobody else to turn to.

"Thank you for calling the Yunxi County Civil Affairs Bureau. Our office hours are Monday to Friday. To register a marriage, press one. To register a divorce, press two. To ask about subsistence allowances, press three..."

Chen Xuan listened to the whole list with patience, but there was no button for a child with nowhere to sleep.

He pressed zero, as the recording told him to, and a cheerful voice said, "Thank you for calling, goodbye." Then the line went dead!

Behind the glass, young duty officer Lei winced and said, "It does that to everyone, I am sorry to say."

Second, Chen Xuan tried the weekend duty number printed in tiny letters at the bottom of the bureau's website, and this time a real person answered.

It was a tired, kind voice that heard the whole story without once cutting in.

"I am sorry, I truly am. A child with no guardian goes to the temporary shelter in the city, which is a children's home run by the city. It is full until Monday, so all I can do is write her name in the log."

"Could a social worker see her today?"

"We have one social worker for the whole county, and she is off on weekends."

"Is there somewhere else she could stay until Monday?"

"A relative's home, yes, but a stranger's home, no. A foster family needs weeks of approvals, and the office does those, and the office..."

"Opens Monday," Chen Xuan finished gently.

"Yes, and I am so sorry."

To put it simply, the shelter was full, the one social worker was off, and the foster office was shut, so Xiaoyu had two more nights ahead of her, Saturday and Sunday, on a station cot or a bench if nobody took her.

Third, he turned to Officer Lei and asked whether the police could take a missing-person report for her father, Tang Dawei, a laborer.

"Only a family member can file one," Lei said unhappily. "She is a minor, so she cannot, and you are not a relative. I would go and find him myself if the rule let me. The rest room can keep her only until the shift changes at noon, and after that we have no one to watch her."

Lei looked at the girl and then at the clock, and he wanted to help very much, but he did not know how to bend a rule he had no power to bend.

Three questions, three honest answers, and not one villain in the lot! Every person in the chain was kind, careful and loyal to the rules, and at the end of the chain sat a thirteen-year-old with nowhere to go.

Only once had a voice changed, when Chen Xuan told the story on the phone and said the word "laborer," and there was a small pause and a faint, practiced sympathy that meant the voice had already decided how this story would end.

"I'll go," Xiaoyu said suddenly, standing up with her backpack. "I have a place."

"Where is that?" asked Chen Xuan.

She stared at her shoes. "The bus station."

Silence.

Hmm.

Chen Xuan glanced at the wall clock, then looked at his phone and called his mother.

"Mom, can I bring a guest home for a few days?"

"A guest?!" Liu Meifang's voice shot out of the speaker so loudly that Officer Lei jumped. "A girl guest? Xuan, how old is she, and does she cook?"

"She's thirteen."

"Thirteen?! What do you..." There was a pause, and Chen Xuan could actually hear his mother's face change. "Tell me everything."

He told her everything in a few short sentences, and she listened without a single interruption, which had never happened before.

"One more bowl!" Liu Meifang announced, like a general giving orders. "And she sleeps in your sister's room! Yuan! Yuan, get up and make space!"

---

Six floors, no elevator, and Xiaoyu counted every step under her breath until somewhere near the fourth landing, when she stopped, and Chen Xuan pretended not to notice.

Liu Meifang threw the door open with a soup ladle in her fist and looked the girl up and down, from her crooked hair to her rolled-up sleeves.

"Too thin!" she declared. "Come in, come in! Shoes off. No, wait, keep them on. No, off. Hao, bring slippers!"

Chen Yuan leaned in the hallway with her arms folded, trying very hard not to laugh.

"So this is the guest. Mom told the whole building you were bringing home a girl, Brother, and Auntie Zhang downstairs already asked if she is a cousin, a cat sitter or a spy!"

"Hmm, a mystery girl?" said Chen Xuan.

"Three floors of gossip in one morning. By tonight she will have a backstory and a middle name." Chen Yuan crouched to Xiaoyu's height. "Hi, I'm Yuan. The bed by the window is yours, and I'll take the folding bed. I snore, but only a little."

Xiaoyu looked at her and said nothing.

Chen Hao shuffled up with one earbud in. "My phone charger is the long one," he muttered. "You can have it, but don't tell Mom I gave up the long one."

"I heard that!" Liu Meifang shouted from the kitchen.

Then her phone rang, and she put it on speaker to wipe her hands, and a small, stern, very old voice filled the apartment.

"Meifang, Xuan says there's a girl. Is she thin?"

"Mother, you have not even met her yet!"

"I can tell from how Xuan said her name," said Grandma Zhou Guilan. "She is too thin! Feed her pickled eggs! I will come next Sunday!"

Xiaoyu ate at the family table with her back very straight, and she ate everything she was given, but she did not say thank you.

Chen Xuan noticed and did not mind, because some people do not say it once they have learned that whatever you take, you must pay back, and they are not sure yet what this will cost.

That afternoon he passed the open door of the girls' room, where Xiaoyu stood beside the bed with her hands at her sides, as if she were waiting to be told when to leave.

She had folded the quilt into a sharp square and set the pillow on top, though no one had slept there yet.

He did not go in, and he went to the kitchen instead and asked his mother, very quietly, if there was any more of the sweet pork.

---

That night, the apartment had gone dark and quiet, except for Chen Jianguo's snoring through two walls.

Chen Xuan was carrying a glass of water to the balcony when the door of the girls' room opened a crack, and Xiaoyu stood there in the dark, in one of Chen Yuan's old shirts, with her face a thin pale line.

"Why are you being nice to me?" she whispered, and her voice shook, but her eyes did not. "What do you want?"

Chen Xuan looked at her seriously, because she deserved a serious answer.

"Dinner is at six," he said. "That's all I want."

Xiaoyu stared at him. Was that really all? She waited for the rest of the sentence, and it never came.
