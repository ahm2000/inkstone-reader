# Chapter 24: The Stolen Blueprints

By eleven o'clock on Sunday night, the rain over Lanshui had hardened into a cold autumn downpour that hammered relentlessly against the tinted glass of the nineteenth floor. Behind a heavy biometric steel door adjoining the Financial Intelligence room lay the Signals and Cyber chamber, a windowless Faraday-shielded vault lined with copper mesh and sound-absorbing matte panels. No wireless signal could enter or leave its walls.

Seated alone at the primary air-gapped decryption rig, Number Thirty-four stared at a matte-silver solid-state courier drive resting inside a physical write-blocker cradle. The drive was no larger than a steel cigarette lighter, completely unmarked, with a tamper-evident polymer seal along its seam.

Three hours earlier, while the rain began to fall over the riverfront, Black Pine field operatives Number Fourteen and Number Twenty-seven had entered the twenty-eighth floor of the Lanshui Grand River Hotel. Their target was a quiet middleman registered under a Hong Kong logistics passport who had met twice with Shengyuan Capital's deputy treasurer over the past forty-eight hours. While the middleman sat downstairs dining on steamed river fish in the private banquet hall, Number Fourteen bypassed the room safe's optical lock without leaving a microscopic scratch, and Number Twenty-seven performed a raw sector-by-sector physical clone of the courier drive in four minutes and twelve seconds. The original drive was returned to the exact millimeter where it had rested before the dessert course reached the table downstairs.

Now, the cloned image sat mounted inside Number Thirty-four's isolated sandbox.

Beside Number Thirty-four stood two junior Signals and Cyber analysts, Number Forty-two and Number Fifty-six, watching the secondary telemetry monitors in dead silence. Neither of them knew the other's face outside this dim blue light, and neither spoke unless directly addressed.

"Importing the thirty-two-character delivery hashes from Number Seventy-three," Number Thirty-four said quietly, feeding an optical transfer cartridge from the Financial Intelligence room into the air-gapped reader. "The drive uses a triple-cascade cipher. Layer one is commercial elliptic-curve encryption. Layer two is a custom polynomial scramble. Layer three requires the exact clearing timestamp from the Zurich disbursement four days ago."

Number Thirty-four entered the final seed string and pressed execute.

The cooling fans inside the tower cabinet spun up to a low, steady whine. Across the three dark monitors, blocks of encrypted noise began fracturing into clean directory trees. Folder after folder unlocked in rapid succession: `ARCHIVE_09_MARITIME`, `ARCHIVE_14_GRID_SOUTH`, `ARCHIVE_22_FRONTIER_LOG`.

Number Thirty-four clicked open the first folder.

A high-resolution vector rendering expanded across the center forty-inch display, rotating slowly in crisp white lines against a pitch-black background. Beside it, dense tables of signal-processing equations, phased-array module tolerances, and dual-band frequency-hopping parameters scrolled down the right-hand screen.

At the top margin of every single blueprint sheet sat a crimson classification header reserved exclusively for top-tier domestic naval defense engineering.

Number Forty-two's hand froze halfway to his keyboard. Behind him, Number Fifty-six stopped breathing for two full seconds.

"Verify the structural signature," Number Thirty-four ordered, his voice dropping to a razor-thin hush. "Is that a commercial marine transceiver?"

Number Forty-two ran a rapid geometry comparison against Black Pine's offline technical index. His fingers moved stiffly, as though the air in the shielded room had suddenly turned below freezing. "Negative. That is the active phased-array radar architecture deployed on our newest domestic destroyer class. It includes the full cooling-manifold tolerances, the low-observable clutter-rejection algorithms, and the electronic counter-countermeasure waveform tables. If a foreign navy holds these schematics, they can model the radar's blind frequencies before a single ship leaves port."

No one in the room moved. The quiet hum of the air-gapped servers suddenly sounded deafening against the silence.

"Open archive fourteen," Number Thirty-four said evenly, though the muscles along his jaw had locked rigid.

The second directory unpacked across the left and center screens. Instead of mechanical blueprints, rows of encrypted cryptographic certificates and dynamic network topology maps filled the dark glass.

Number Fifty-six leaned forward, eyes widening behind his dark frames. "Those are not provincial utility billing tables. That is the core supervisory control architecture for the entire southern coastal power grid. Load-balancing authentication keys, ultra-high-voltage substation failover protocols, and automated dispatch override sequences."

"Explain the impact," Number Thirty-four said flatly.

"With these keys and topology maps," Number Fifty-six answered, his voice tight and dry, "an external adversary would not need missiles to cripple six coastal provinces. During a geopolitical crisis, a single coordinated injection could desynchronize the main transformer relays across the southern grid, burning out replacement turbines that take eighteen months to manufacture. Half the industrial coast would go dark in ninety seconds."

Number Thirty-four stared at the glowing blue grid lines for three long seconds, then clicked open the third and final folder: `ARCHIVE_22_FRONTIER_LOG`.

Spreadsheets, satellite-referenced terrain overlays, and encrypted rail-dispatch timetables filled the monitor. Column after column detailed border-garrison logistics rosters: battalion rotation schedules, strategic underground fuel-depot coordinates, heavy-armor railhead loading windows, and backup satellite-relay frequencies across the southwestern and northern frontier sectors.

Attached to the root of all three directories sat a single verification receipt file. Number Thirty-four opened the receipt and placed it side by side with the SWIFT mirror printout from Number Eleven's Financial Intelligence room.

The thirty-two-character delivery hashes matched down to the last digit. The timestamps matched down to the exact second.

The offshore gold-backed bearer instruments flowing out of Shengyuan Capital's hidden Lanshui vault to the foreign defense-intelligence cutouts in Zurich, Singapore, and the Cayman Islands were not merely stolen money looking for a safe haven. They were dual-use operational funds, paying off domestic traitors inside strategic ministries, defense contractors, and grid bureaus while laundering the proceeds of twenty years of sovereign theft.

And down at the very bottom of the chain, in a quiet county called Yunxi, Hongrui Real Estate was tearing down Old Street and murdering honest shopkeepers like Lin Guoliang simply to keep an underground rare-earth smuggling tunnel sealed from prying eyes.

"This is not local corruption," Number Forty-two whispered, his voice barely audible inside the copper-lined vault. "This is not even smuggling or embezzlement."

"It is high treason," Number Thirty-four said quietly. "Active foreign espionage, hiding behind county real-estate demolitions and municipal development bonds."

For ten full seconds, nobody inside the Signals and Cyber chamber spoke. Even hardened Black Pine Guard specialists, veterans who had dismantled transnational cartels, neutralized corporate assassins, and navigated geopolitical shadow wars for the Boss across four continents, stood completely rigid before the three glowing screens.

Stealing money from a state project was greed. Selling domestic naval radar arrays, southern power-grid load-balancing keys, and border-garrison logistics rosters to foreign intelligence services was cutting the throat of the nation from the inside.

Number Thirty-four exhaled slowly, reached for a fresh hardware-encrypted obsidian storage cartridge, and mirrored the decrypted evidence package along with its full chain-of-custody verification hashes. He signed the package with his numeric cipher, sealed the cartridge inside a lead-lined case, and locked the air-gapped rig down to zero volatile memory.

"Maintain absolute chamber silence," Number Thirty-four instructed Number Forty-two and Number Fifty-six. "Nobody leaves this floor. Nobody speaks a word of what rendered on these screens."

Carrying the cold metal case in his right hand, Number Thirty-four unlocked the heavy biometric steel door and stepped out into the dimly lit corridor.

Just beyond the thick glass partition overlooking the Financial Intelligence floor, Number One was already standing motionless in the pale blue light, his hands clasped behind his back, waiting. One look at the rigid line of Number Thirty-four's shoulders told Number One everything before a single word was spoken.
