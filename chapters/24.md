# Chapter 24: Come Back Monday

Yunxi County, Old Street Police Station. Saturday, 8:30 a.m.

Chen Xuan arrived with four red-bean buns and one clear goal: find a proper place for Xiaoyu to stay until Monday. The night duty officer had changed, and Instructor Qiu had gone home to sleep. Xiaoyu sat on the steel bench in the lobby, wearing the same gray jacket, with her backpack on her knees and the station's blanket folded beside her in a perfect square.

"Good morning," said Chen Xuan. "Did you sleep?"

"Yes."

Auxiliary Officer Ma, passing with a mop, leaned over and whispered, "She didn't."

First, the ordinary way. Chen Xuan found the Civil Affairs Bureau number on his phone and pressed call.

"Thank you for calling the Yunxi County Civil Affairs Bureau. Our office hours are Monday to Friday. To register a marriage, press one. To register a divorce, press two. To ask about subsistence allowances, press three..."

He listened to the whole list patiently. There was no button for a child with nowhere to sleep. He pressed zero, as the recording suggested, and a cheerful voice said, "Thank you for calling. Goodbye." Then the line went dead!

The young duty officer behind the glass, Officer Lei, winced in sympathy. "It does that to everyone."

Chen Xuan tried the weekend duty number printed in tiny letters at the bottom of the bureau's website. This time a real person answered, a tired, kind voice that listened to the whole story without interrupting.

"I'm sorry, I truly am," the voice said. "A child with no guardian goes to the temporary shelter in the city. It's full until Monday. I can write her name in the log, that's all."

"Could a social worker see her today?"

"We have one social worker for the whole county, and she's off on weekends."

"Is there somewhere else she could stay until Monday?"

"A relative's home, yes. A stranger's home, no. A foster family needs weeks of approvals, and those are done by the office, and the office..."

"Opens Monday," Chen Xuan finished gently.

"Yes. I'm so sorry."

With the shelter full until Monday, Xiaoyu had two more nights ahead of her, Saturday and Sunday, on a station cot or a bench if nobody took her.

Third, he turned to Officer Lei. "Could the police take a missing-person report for her father? Tang Dawei, a laborer?"

"Only a family member can file one," Lei said unhappily. "She's a minor, so she can't, and you're not a relative. I'd go find him myself if the rule let me. And the rest room can keep her only until the shift changes at noon. After that, we have no one to watch her."

Three questions, three honest answers, and not one villain in the lot! Every person in the chain was kind, careful, and following the rules. And at the end of the chain sat a thirteen-year-old with nowhere to go.

Only once, on the phone, had anyone's voice changed when they heard the word "laborer": a small pause, then a faint, practiced sympathy that meant, more than anything else, that the voice had already decided how this story would end.

"I'll go," Xiaoyu said suddenly, standing with her backpack. "I have a place."

"Where?" asked Chen Xuan.

She stared at her shoes. "The bus station."

"Hmm," said Chen Xuan. He looked at the clock. 9:10. He looked at his phone. Then he called his mother.

"Mom, can I bring a guest home for a few days?"

"A guest?!" Liu Meifang's voice shot out of the speaker so loudly that Officer Lei jumped. "A girl guest? Xuan, how old is she? Is she serious? Does she cook?"

"She's thirteen."

"Thirteen?! What do you..." There was a pause, and then Chen Xuan could actually hear his mother's face change. "Tell me everything."

He told her everything, in a few short sentences.

"One more bowl," Liu Meifang announced, in the voice of a general taking a hill. "And she sleeps in your sister's room! Yuan! Yuan, get up and make space!"

Yunxi County, Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. Saturday, 11:20 a.m.

Six floors, no elevator.

Xiaoyu counted every step under her breath. Somewhere around the fourth landing she stopped counting, and Chen Xuan pretended not to notice.

Liu Meifang threw the door open with a soup ladle in her fist. She looked the girl up and down once, from the crooked hair to the rolled-up sleeves.

"Too thin!" she declared. "Come in! Shoes off. No, wait, keep them on. No, off. Hao, bring slippers!"

Chen Yuan leaned in the hallway, arms folded, trying very hard not to laugh. "So this is the guest. Mom told the whole building you were bringing home a girl, Brother. Auntie Zhang downstairs already asked if the mystery girl is a cousin, a cat sitter, or a spy."

"Hmm?" said Chen Xuan. "A mystery girl?"

"Three floors of gossip in one morning. By tonight she'll have a backstory and a middle name." Chen Yuan crouched to Xiaoyu's height. "Hi. I'm Yuan. The bed by the window is yours, and I'll take the folding bed. I snore, but only a little."

Xiaoyu looked at her, and said nothing.

Chen Hao shuffled up with one earbud in. "My phone charger is the long one," he muttered. "You can have it. Don't tell Mom I gave up the long one."

"I heard that!" Liu Meifang shouted from the kitchen.

Her phone rang. Liu Meifang put it on speaker to wipe her hands, and a small, stern, very old voice filled the apartment.

"Meifang, Xuan says there's a girl. Is she thin?"

"Mother, you haven't even met her!"

"I can tell from how Xuan said her name," said Grandma Zhou Guilan. "She's too thin! Feed her pickled eggs! I'll come next Sunday!"

Xiaoyu ate at the family table with her back very straight, and she ate everything she was given. She did not say thank you. Chen Xuan noticed that and didn't mind. Some people don't say it because they've learned that whatever you take, you must pay back, and they aren't sure yet what this will cost.

That afternoon, when the girls' room was quiet, he passed the open door. Xiaoyu was standing beside the bed. She had folded the quilt into a sharp square and set the pillow on top, even though no one had slept there yet. Her hands were at her sides, as if she were waiting to be told when it was time to leave.

He did not go in. He went to the kitchen and asked his mother, very quietly, if there was any more of the sweet pork.

Yunxi County, Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. Saturday, 9:30 p.m.

The apartment had gone dark and quiet, except for Chen Jianguo's snoring through two walls.

Chen Xuan was carrying a glass of water to the balcony when the door of the girls' room opened a crack. Xiaoyu stood there in the dark, in one of Chen Yuan's old shirts, her face a thin pale line.

"Why are you being nice to me?" she whispered. Her voice shook, but her eyes didn't. "What do you want?"

Chen Xuan looked at her seriously, because she deserved a serious answer.

"Dinner is at six," he said. "That's all I want."
