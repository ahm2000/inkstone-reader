# Chapter 5: Above the Clouds

Thirty-eight thousand feet above the eastern hills, the matte-slate jet rode a river of thin, glassy air. Outside the oval cabin window, a pale half-moon hung above an unbroken sea of silver-edged clouds, hiding the winding mountain highways and sleepy county towns far below. Inside the walnut-lined cabin, the only sounds were the faint, steady hum of the turbines and the crisp rustle of paper as Chen Xuan turned another page of the dossier.

He drank slowly from the stainless-steel cup lid of his green thermos, finishing the last of the warm jasmine tea from Qingyan while his eyes traced the timeline of Yunxi County's Old Street redevelopment.

On the surface, the documents looked like an ordinary small-county urban renewal dispute. Old Street was a half-mile corridor of narrow brick shophouses built in the late eighties, home to hardware dealers, tailors, noodle stalls, and retired mill workers. Four years earlier, Yunxi County had announced a heritage-and-commercial renovation zone. Two years later, after three smaller local bidders withdrew under unexplained tax audits, the entire parcel had been awarded at thirty percent below appraised value to Hongrui Real Estate, fronted by Deng Yongchang.

Yet as Chen Xuan turned to the back appendix compiled by the Lanshui-Yunxi data desk, his fingers paused on a single margin note.

For a sleepy mountain county whose annual commercial housing demand barely filled two residential towers, Hongrui Real Estate was spending absurd sums of cash to force out forty-two remaining shopkeepers along the eastern block of Old Street, right where Mingde Hardware stood. Even stranger, the municipal demolition boundary did not stop at the commercial frontage. It bent sharply behind the back alleys to swallow an abandoned state-owned geological survey warehouse and a stretch of drainage culverts leading straight toward the old Qingfeng mountain ridge.

Chen Xuan tapped the edge of that zoning map lightly with his forefinger. People did not send hired thugs like Yan Tiehu to smash glass counters and frame an honest fifty-four-year-old hardware merchant over ordinary apartment lots. Not when a quiet buyout at fair market value would have cost less than a single month of legal fees. Someone wanted Old Street cleared fast, cleared completely, and cleared without outside eyes asking what lay beneath the flagstones.

A soft, single chime sounded from the brushed-metal console recessed into the walnut armrest. A steady green light blinked beside the secure satellite handset.

Chen Xuan closed the manila folder neatly, picked up the handset, and pressed the side switch. "Speak."

The voice that came through the encrypted channel was calm, low, and stripped of regional accent, carrying the quiet weight of a man who spent every waking hour watching the entire map from behind closed curtains. It was Number One, the unseen, nameless head of the Black Pine Guard. Even after a decade of service, Chen Xuan had never asked for Number One's birth name, and Number One had never asked for a title beyond his numeral.

"The Boss," Number One said steadily. "We have established live telemetry over Old Street Police Station and Hongrui Real Estate's county office. Our Lanshui relay confirmed that the assault report against Chen Mingde was filed at one fifty this afternoon by a site foreman working directly under Yan Tiehu. The medical injury certificate attached to the file was stamped by a private clinic owned by Deng Yongchang's brother-in-law forty minutes before the alleged altercation even occurred."

Chen Xuan's expression remained utterly serene, though the temperature in his dark eyes seemed to drop another degree. "Forty minutes before."

"Yes, the Boss," Number One confirmed. "Sloppy work by local thugs who assumed a hardware merchant's family had no reach beyond Yunxi County. Our advance watcher also confirmed that the station's deputy director, Zhou Quan, signed the temporary detention slip personally after receiving a twelve-minute phone call from Hongrui Real Estate's downtown office."

Chen Xuan looked out at the moonlit cloud deck stretching endlessly toward the western mountains. He thought of his mother's shaking voice on the phone, of Chen Jianguo being shouted away from an iron gate while holding a bottle of blood pressure pills, and of Second Uncle Chen Mingde sitting patiently beside a reed bank in the rain with a bamboo fishing rod.

"Listen carefully, Number One," Chen Xuan said, his voice unhurried, clear, and final. "Nobody in my family steps foot inside a police station. Handle the people who touched my Second Uncle, verify his safety tonight, and pull every root under Old Street. Do not miss a single brick."

There was a brief, respectful half-second of silence on the line as the full scope of the order registered. Not merely a release from detention. Not merely a local slap on the wrist for Yan Tiehu or Deng Yongchang. Every root under Old Street, down to the last buried stone.

"Understood, the Boss," Number One replied, his tone sharpening to cold steel. "Chen Mingde's safety and immediate lawful release will be verified before you reach the family courtyard. I am activating three divisions across Yunxi and Lanshui immediately."

"Do not startle my parents," Chen Xuan added softly. "Keep the noise away from their door."

"Not a whisper will reach their courtyard, the Boss. I will report again once the first layer is peeled back."

Chen Xuan set the handset cleanly back into its cradle.

Three hundred miles away, inside a windowless, whisper-quiet operations room hidden beneath an ordinary logistics warehouse in the provincial capital, Number One released the transmit key and entered a nine-digit authorization sequence into his console. Across the wide dark wall monitors, three separate division headers shifted from dormant gray to crisp emerald green: Field Intelligence, Financial Tracing, and Local Containment.

Orders went out without a single spoken name.

In Yunxi County, Number Fourteen and Number Nineteen stepped out of a quiet tea house two blocks from Old Street Police Station, turning their collars up against the mountain mist as they moved toward separate corners of the block. In Lanshui City, Number Twenty-three and Number Twenty-seven unlocked three encrypted banking terminals and began pulling ten years of clearing-house ledgers tied to Hongrui Real Estate, Deng Yongchang, and every provincial shell account that had ever fed them a single yuan. Along the darkened highway exits leading out of Yunxi toward the mountains, four silent sedans belonging to Local Containment eased onto the gravel shoulders, their headlights extinguished.

Across the dark glass screens of Black Pine's command wall, hundreds of tiny green indicator dots flickered awake simultaneously, spreading like quiet water across the topography of Lanshui and Yunxi County. Every road, every ledger, every telephone switchboard, and every back-alley warehouse connected to Old Street was suddenly inside a net that no county official or real-estate boss could even imagine existed.

High above the clouds, the cabin lights dimmed slightly as the private jet pitched its nose gently downward, breaking through the silver mist toward the waiting runway lights of Lanshui-Yunxi Regional Airfield.

Chen Xuan capped his green thermos, tucked the uneaten red-bean bun back into his canvas bag, and gazed calmly down at the dark mountain valley rising to meet him.
