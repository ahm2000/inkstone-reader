# Chapter 5: Home

Back in Room 601, Liu Meifang opened the door and saw her husband's left cheek. She dropped the ladle.

"Who did that?"

"Nobody," Chen Jianguo muttered. "I walked into a door."

"Yan Tiehu, that thug from Hongrui, slapped him." Chen Xuan's voice shook. "In front of the whole street."

"I will kill him!"

She snatched the ladle off the floor and marched toward the door. It took Chen Yuan and Chen Hao together to hold her back.

"Mom, he's twice your size!"

"Then I need a bigger ladle!"

Chen Hao laughed, and Wu Lanying laughed through her tears, and the room felt a little lighter.

Liu Meifang pointed at the kitchen. "Xuan, cold water, a towel. Go."

Chen Xuan filled a basin, wrung out a towel, and held it against his father's swollen cheek. His eyes were wet.

Chen Jianguo's whisper was rough. "Sorry, son. You shouldn't have seen your old father like that."

"Does it hurt, Dad? Please tell me the truth."

"Not much."

His father turned his face to the wall.

Chen Xuan had seen that old hand grab the brush. His throat hurt.

"Well?" Chen Yuan said from the dining table. "Two hours. Dad got slapped, the station turned you away, and what did you bring back? A thermos."

"The station said tomorrow."

"Tomorrow! Brother, you have never won an argument in your life."

"Yuan, enough." Liu Meifang did not sound angry. She sounded scared.

"Second Uncle is in a cell, and the only man in the family with a city job says nothing."

"I'm sorry, Yuan. I'm scared too. I don't know what to do either. I just don't want to stay home and do nothing."

"Don't say sorry." Chen Yuan covered her mouth. "That's worse."

Liu Meifang filled two bowls and set them down with a clack.

"Two bowls. I am counting."

Chen Xuan ate the first one slowly, because his mother was watching.

"Best ribs in the county, Mom."

"Can I go to the station with Dad tomorrow?" he asked between spoonfuls.

"To do what?" Chen Yuan cried. "Shout?"

"Sit. Just sit there, so Second Uncle knows we came."

Chen Jianguo frowned. "Stay home, Xuan. That's grown-up business."

"I'll sit quietly, Dad. I promise."

His father sighed and did not say no.

Chen Xuan looked at the red mark on his father's cheek.

"Dad, what time did he hit you?"

"Ten past eight. Why?"

"I want to remember it right, in case somebody asks." He said it so mildly that Chen Yuan rolled her eyes.

Then three slow knocks sounded on the door.

Nobody moved. Liu Meifang's hand went to the ladle.

Chen Xuan was nearest the door, so he opened it the way a boy would when the bell rang. He looked out at the dark stairwell.

The bulb was out, and the stairs were empty.

On the doormat lay a wet red paintbrush. A strip of red paper was taped to the door with one character on it: *chai*.

Yan Tiehu had not waited for tomorrow.

"Don't touch that, you boy!" Liu Meifang cried.

"I won't, Mom." His voice was small. "I'll take a picture first. The police will want it."

He photographed the brush on the mat, the red paper on the door, and the dead bulb above them. The time showed on the screen: 9:52.

Then he put the brush in the trash bin, peeled the paper off, and locked the door twice. His hands were not steady.

Chen Yuan stared at him, and for once she had nothing to say.

In the bathroom, with the tap running, he sent the photos from the gray phone. *Chief An. Light the stairwell tonight. Keep Yan Tiehu's name and plate. Nobody touches him yet.*

* * *

In the holding room, the bench was concrete. The cold came up through it.

Chen Mingde sat on his folded jacket with his hands under his arms.

At eleven that night, Lei Bo had come by with Hongrui's sale form and a pen.

"Sign, Old Chen. Go home, take a hot shower. Why suffer?" He tapped the pen on the bars. "Your girl studies in Wuhan, right? Graduates next summer. The big firms run a police check on every applicant. A father with a detention record fails it. Sign, and the record is never written."

"Please take it away." Chen Mingde had said it in the same voice he used for customers who wanted a discount.

His heart had pounded so hard that he thought the whole cell must hear it. But he did not sign.

He thought of his daughter in her dorm. She would never know any of this unless he told her.

Now there were footsteps, and a shadow fell across the bars.

It was Wei Kun, the duty officer, the thin one with tired eyes.

He looked down the corridor, then poured hot water from a steel thermos into a paper cup. He pushed it through the bars with a shaking hand.

"Drink it slowly. It's only water."

"Thank you, Officer."

Chen Mingde took the cup in both hands, like a warm stone.

"Will anyone tell my family tomorrow why I am here?"

"I don't know." Wei Kun looked at his own shoes. "I am sorry. I really don't know."

"Don't be sorry. You're shaking. Are you cold or scared?"

Wei Kun laughed, almost a sob.

"Both, Uncle."

"I thought so. You keep touching your phone. Someone is waiting for your call."

"My wife. Seven months pregnant."

"Then don't get into trouble for me."

"Listen." Chen Mingde sipped the water, and the heat crept into his chest. "When my nephew was seven, I took him fishing. A fish pulled, he pulled back, and the line snapped. I told him, 'Wait. Let the fish get tired first.' I am only a hardware man, Officer. But waiting is the one thing I am good at."

He added softly, "If you can, tell my wife I ate. She won't believe it, but tell her."

Wei Kun nodded once to the old man. Then he walked to the duty desk and sat down in front of the empty time box on the form.

For twelve years he had written whatever Cheng Biao wanted. Tonight, for the first time, he knew what he would not write: a false hour.

* * *

In a walled courtyard in the east of Qingyan City, a great map glowed on the study wall of An Zhiyuan, Chief of the Detail. Two hundred and fourteen green dots shone across it.

One dot, in Yunxi County, had turned yellow and begun to blink. It meant a message was waiting.

The duty captain spoke on the speaker. "Old Street Station, Chief. Our records clerk, Ding Shouyi. His note says, 'Awaiting order.'"

"Read me what happened."

The captain read it. An Zhiyuan's tea went cold.

"His father was slapped."

"Yes, Chief. By Yan Tiehu, called Brother Tie. We have the face and the license plate."

"And the Boss?"

"Number Nine, Mo Lin, the guard from the bus, is in the flat across the hall. He reports the Boss held a wet towel to his father's cheek and ate two bowls of soup. He cried a little at the table. Someone knocked three times and left a wet paintbrush on his mother's doormat, with a red paper on the door. He photographed it at 9:52 and sent it to us. Then he asked for light on the stairs and went to bed. He was asleep in eight minutes. He snores a little."

An Zhiyuan laughed, a short sound with a crack in it. "Soft as bread with his mother."

"Shall we move, Chief?"

"The Boss's order: keep the names, keep the plate, touch nothing yet." An Zhiyuan's voice went flat. "Put a new bulb in that stairwell tonight. Add the photograph to Yan Tiehu's file with the time on it. Write down every insult, every threat, with the name and the hour. I will not forget a single line."

"And if the uncle's heart gives out tonight?"

"Then he goes to People's Hospital, where Dr. Shen Wei, a heart specialist and one of ours, just swapped into the night shift. Head Nurse Qiao Peiling has the uncle's pill record on her desk."

"And somewhere safe for the Boss to sleep?"

"Apartment 302, Building 5, Willow Lane. We keep it ready for him. Bed made, rice in the jar, key under the geranium pot. He has never heard of it."

"Of course not." An Zhiyuan shook his head. "A nurse and a made bed wait for him, and he thinks life is just kind."

"What does the Boss plan for tomorrow?"

"Eight-thirty, Chief. He will go to the station's front hall and sit on the bench."

An Zhiyuan put both hands over his face. When he took them away, he was not smiling.

"He has told us only to record, Captain. When he says more, who in this county will still be standing?"
