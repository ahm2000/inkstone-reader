# Chapter 5: Home

Back in Room 601, Liu Meifang opened the door and saw her husband's left cheek. She dropped the ladle.

"Who did that?"

"Nobody," Chen Jianguo muttered. "I walked into a door."

"Yan Tiehu, that thug from Hongrui, slapped him." Chen Xuan's voice was quiet, almost flat. "Right in front of the whole street."

"I will kill him!"

Liu Meifang snatched the ladle off the floor and marched toward the door. It took Chen Yuan and Chen Hao together to hold her back.

"Mom, he's twice your size!"

"Then I'll get a bigger ladle!"

Chen Hao laughed, and Wu Lanying let out a shaky laugh through her tears, easing the tension in the room just a little.

Liu Meifang pointed toward the kitchen. "Chen Xuan, cold water and a towel. Go."

Chen Xuan filled a basin, wrung out a towel, and pressed it gently against his father's swollen cheek. His jaw was tight, though his touch stayed light.

Chen Jianguo's voice came out rough. "Sorry, son. You shouldn't have had to see your old father like that."

"Does it hurt, Dad? Tell me the truth."

"Not much."

His father turned his face toward the wall.

Chen Xuan still saw that weathered hand reaching for the paintbrush. A dull ache settled in his chest.

"Well?" Chen Yuan asked from the dining table. "Two hours. Dad got slapped, the station turned you away, and what did you bring back? A thermos."

"The station said to come back tomorrow."

"Tomorrow! Big Brother, you have never won an argument in your life."

"Chen Yuan, enough." Liu Meifang didn't sound angry. She sounded afraid.

"Second Uncle is sitting in a cell, and the only man in this family with a city job says nothing."

"I'm sorry, Chen Yuan," Chen Xuan said evenly. "I just don't want to sit at home and do nothing."

"Don't apologize." Chen Yuan covered her mouth. "That's even worse."

Liu Meifang filled two bowls and set them on the table with a clack. "Two bowls. I am counting."

Chen Xuan ate the first one slowly, knowing his mother was watching. "Best ribs in the county, Mom."

"Can I go to the station with Dad tomorrow?" he asked between spoonfuls.

"To do what?" Chen Yuan snapped. "Yell at them?"

"Sit. Just sit in the lobby, so Second Uncle knows we came."

Chen Jianguo frowned. "Stay home, Chen Xuan. That's grown-up business."

"I'll sit quietly, Dad. I promise."

His father sighed and did not say no.

Chen Xuan glanced at the red welt on his father's cheek. "Dad, what time did he hit you?"

"Ten past eight. Why?"

"I want to remember it right, in case somebody asks." He said it so mildly that Chen Yuan rolled her eyes.

Then three slow knocks sounded on the front door.

Nobody moved. Liu Meifang's hand went straight for the ladle.

Chen Xuan was nearest the door, so he stepped over and opened it. He looked out into the dark stairwell.

The overhead bulb was out, and the stairs were empty.

On the doormat lay a wet red paintbrush. A strip of red paper had been taped to the door with a single character painted on it: *chai*.

Yan Tiehu had not waited for tomorrow.

"Don't touch that!" Liu Meifang cried, clutching her apron.

"It's just paint, Mom. Go sit down," Chen Xuan said evenly.

Without a word more, he picked up the wet brush, dropped it straight into the trash bin, tore the red paper off the door, and turned the deadbolt twice. His face showed nothing at all.

Chen Yuan stared at him, and for once she had nothing to say.

Chen Xuan walked into the bathroom, locked the door behind him, and turned on the tap. Alone in front of the mirror, the mild look left his face completely, and his eyes went ice-cold.

He pulled out the gray phone and sent one line to An Zhiyuan.

*Guard Building 2. Yan Tiehu disappears tonight.*

* * *

In the detention cell, the bench was bare concrete, and the chill seeped straight up through it.

Chen Mingde sat on his folded jacket with his hands tucked under his arms.

At eleven that night, Lei Bo had stopped by with Hongrui's sale form and a pen.

"Sign, Chen Mingde. Go home, take a hot shower. Why put yourself through this?" He tapped the pen against the bars. "Your daughter studies in Wuhan, right? Graduates next summer. The big firms run a background check on every applicant. A father with a detention record fails that check. Sign, and the record is never written."

"Please take it away." Chen Mingde spoke in the exact same tone he used for customers asking for an unreasonable discount.

His heart had pounded so hard he felt sure the whole cell could hear it. Still, he did not sign.

He thought of his daughter in her university dorm. She would never know any of this unless he told her.

Footsteps echoed down the detention corridor as the night shift made its routine check.

Through the small window in the steel door appeared Wei Kun, the thin duty officer with tired eyes and a check-in clipboard tucked under one arm. Under the corridor camera, he paused as if inspecting the cell, then slid a paper cup of hot water from the duty-room dispenser through the narrow pass-through slot.

Cheng Biao had told the night shift to give the Old Street holdouts nothing to warm them up, letting the cold bench do the talking. Wei Kun kept his face flat and his voice low, as if he were only asking a routine question.

"Drink it slowly while it's hot. Is your heart holding up?"

"Thank you, Officer."

Chen Mingde cupped the paper between both palms, holding it like a warm stone. "Will anyone tell my family tomorrow why I am here?"

"I don't know." Wei Kun stared at his own shoes. "I am sorry. I honestly don't know."

"Don't be sorry. Your hands are shaking. Are you cold, or are you scared?"

Wei Kun let out a quiet laugh that sounded almost like a sob. "Both, Uncle."

"I figured. You keep checking your phone. Someone is waiting up for your call."

"My wife. Seven months pregnant."

"Then don't get yourself into trouble for my sake."

Chen Mingde sipped the water, letting the warmth spread into his chest. "Listen. When my nephew was seven, I took him fishing. A fish pulled, he yanked back, and the line snapped. I told him, 'Wait. Let the fish tire itself out first.' I am only a hardware man, Officer. Waiting is the one thing I know how to do."

He added softly, "If you get the chance, tell my wife I ate. She won't believe it, but tell her anyway."

Wei Kun nodded once to the older man. Then he walked back to the duty desk and sat down in front of the blank time box on the detention form.

For twelve years, he had written whatever Cheng Biao told him to write. Tonight, for the first time, he knew the one thing he would not put down: a false hour.

* * *

In a walled courtyard in the east of Qingyan City, a large map glowed on the study wall of An Zhiyuan, Chief of the Detail. Two hundred and fourteen green dots shone across it.

One dot, in Yunxi County, had turned yellow and begun to blink, signaling an incoming message.

The duty captain's voice came over the speaker. "Old Street Station, Chief. Our records clerk, Ding Shouyi. His note reads, 'Awaiting order.'"

"Read me what happened."

The captain read the report. An Zhiyuan's tea went cold in his cup.

"His father was slapped."

"Yes, Chief. By Yan Tiehu. We have his face and his license plate."

"And the Boss?"

"Number Nine, Mo Lin, the guard from the bus, is in the apartment across the hall. He reports the Boss pressed a cold towel to his father's cheek and ate two bowls of soup with his mother. Then someone knocked three times and left a wet red paintbrush on the doormat, with red paper taped to the door. The Boss threw it in the trash and just sent an order."

An Zhiyuan read the message on his screen. Every trace of warmth vanished from his lined face.

"They came to his mother's front door," An Zhiyuan said softly. His voice dropped to ice. "Put a new bulb in Building 2 tonight and station two men on the stairwell. Tell Tang Liqun to take Yan Tiehu off the road before he reaches Old Street. Keep it clean and silent, and make sure not a single camera catches it. Leave his van idling where his own men will find it, and put him somewhere dark where he can learn what real fear feels like."

The duty captain's breath caught. "Yes, Chief. Moving immediately."

"And if the uncle's heart gives out tonight?"

"Then he goes straight to People's Hospital, where Dr. Shen Wei, a cardiologist and one of ours, just swapped onto the night shift. Head Nurse Qiao Peiling already has the uncle's prescription record on her desk."

"And somewhere safe for the Boss to sleep?"

"Apartment 302, Building 5, Willow Lane. We keep it ready for him. Bed made, rice in the jar, key under the geranium pot. He has never even heard of it."

"Of course not." An Zhiyuan shook his head. "A nurse and a made bed wait for him, and he thinks the world is just kind."

"What does the Boss plan for tomorrow?"

"Eight-thirty, Chief. He will go to the station's front hall and sit on the bench."

An Zhiyuan covered his face with both hands. When he lowered them, he was no longer smiling.

"They mistook his patience for weakness, Captain. Tonight Yan Tiehu vanishes without a word. Let us see how well Dong Haisheng and Cheng Biao sleep when their favorite hound stops answering his phone."
