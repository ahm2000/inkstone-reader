# Chapter 5: Home

By the fourth flight of stairs up to Room 601 in Fuxing New Village, Chen Xuan could already smell pork-rib soup.

Before he could knock, the front door flew open!

"Xuan!" His mother, Liu Meifang, rushed out with a soup ladle in her hand and her eyes red. "You're here! Sit down, sit down. I made pork-rib soup, and I did not touch a bite while I waited for you."

"Mom, I told you to eat first."

"How can I eat while your Second Uncle sits in a cell?!"

Her voice cracked, and she spun back into the kitchen.

Bang!

Every pot in there was about to suffer.

On the edge of the sofa sat Second Aunt Wu Lanying, clutching a glass of cold water. She was always neat, but tonight her hair hung loose and her cardigan was buttoned wrong.

"Xuan," she said, standing up with trembling lips. "Your uncle has never hit anyone in his whole life. He nearly cries when he has to kill a fish!"

"I know, Second Aunt." Chen Xuan gently folded her shaking fingers back around the glass. "Please sit. Let's have some soup first, and then you can tell me everything."

It was a small gesture, but her shoulders loosened, and she sat down.

Soon.

The whole family crowded around the folding table.

His sharp-tongued sister, Chen Yuan, a junior high school teacher, kept refilling Second Aunt's bowl.

His younger brother, Chen Hao, hid behind a high-school math book with one earbud in his ear.

"Brother, you look like you just woke up," Chen Hao muttered.

"I did. On the bus."

"Classic!" Chen Yuan snapped. "Second Uncle is in a cell, and our big brother is napping on the bus!"

"Yuan!" their mother scolded.

"I am just saying, Mom. He is the only person alive who could snore through a flood!"

"Eat, all of you," Liu Meifang ordered, and she piled more rice into Chen Xuan's bowl. "A hungry man cannot think, and tonight we must think."

Chen Xuan said nothing and ate, because it was the fastest way to make his mother happy.

Chen Xuan drank a long spoonful of hot, salty soup. Then he set the bowl down and listened closely while Second Aunt laid out the whole trap.

Yunxi County was knocking down Old Street for a new mall, and Hongrui Real Estate had won the job.

Hongrui offered thirty-eight hundred yuan per square meter, which came to four hundred fifty-six thousand yuan for Second Uncle's corner shop of one hundred twenty square meters. Real shops on Old Street sold for eight thousand a square meter, and that made his nearly one million!

They called it relocation pay.

To put it plainly, it was robbery at less than half price!

Only three owners still refused, and Second Uncle's corner lot sat right where the mall's main entrance had to go.

"My own sister called this afternoon," Second Aunt said, and her voice broke. "She said, 'Our Dagen's wife settled for her shop on Renmin Road last spring, no trouble at all. Maybe Mingde is just being stubborn for no reason.'"

"What did you say to her?" Chen Xuan asked gently.

"Nothing. I hung up and cried into the dish towel." Second Aunt wiped her eyes. "But Old Yu saw that driver throw himself at the paint shelf. And the police walked in as if they had been waiting outside."

Clack!

Chen Yuan slammed her chopsticks down, and her face burned with anger.

"That is daylight bullying! Tomorrow morning I am marching straight to that station myself!"

"You are not going anywhere," Chen Jianguo barked. "You are a schoolteacher. One shout in there, and they will ruin your record."

"Then what do we do, Dad?" Chen Yuan's eyes turned red. "Sit here and wait while they break Second Uncle?"

Silence.

Every eye around the table turned slowly toward Chen Xuan.

He quietly set down his spoon.

"Second Aunt, please don't sign anything tonight, no matter who calls. Tomorrow at eight-thirty, Dad and I will go down to the police station, take a queue number like everyone else, and ask them to put their reason in writing."

Chen Yuan stared at him in total disbelief.

"Take a number at a police station? Brother, did you really bump your head on the bus?"

"No," Chen Xuan replied mildly, and he picked up his chopsticks again. "Their front hall has service windows and a ticket machine that prints queue numbers, just like a bank. You pull a number, wait until it flashes on the screen, and talk at the window. Nobody can say we made trouble."

"Xuan, your father already went today!" Liu Meifang cried anxiously. "They brushed him right off!"

"Then tomorrow we ask for the written detention notice," Chen Xuan said calmly. "By law, they must give the family a reason in writing. That is plan one. If the answer is wrong, plan two is to ask again, politely."

"Is that all?" Chen Yuan muttered. "Ask nicely, and ask again?"

"That is all," Chen Xuan said. "Grandfather's rule is an old one, but it works."

"And Second Aunt, one promise, please. No matter who calls you, don't sign a single sheet of paper. Not for any price."

"But what if they keep him locked up?" Second Aunt's eyes filled again. "What if they ruin our daughter's future?"

"I will bring him home," Chen Xuan said.

He said it as simply as a neighbor predicting clear weather tomorrow.

Nobody at the table truly believed he could pull off a miracle. Chen Yuan rolled her eyes, and Liu Meifang wiped her cheeks and dropped two more pork ribs into his bowl. Chen Jianguo cleared his throat and mumbled a proverb about still water running deep.

Everyone thought he was only trying to keep Second Aunt from collapsing.

Only Chen Xuan knew he meant every single word.

Behind his math book, Chen Hao pulled out his earbud and whispered to his sister.

"Does he even know a lawyer?"
