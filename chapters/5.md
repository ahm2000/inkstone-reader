# Chapter 5: Home

Back in Room 601, Liu Meifang opened the door and saw her husband's left cheek. She dropped the ladle.

"Who did that?"

"Nobody," Chen Jianguo muttered. "I walked into a door."

"Yan Tiehu, that thug from Hongrui, slapped him." Chen Xuan's voice was quiet, almost flat. "Right in front of the whole street."

"I will kill him!"

Liu Meifang snatched the ladle off the floor and marched toward the door. It took Chen Yuan and Chen Hao together to hold her back.

"Mom, he's twice your size!"

"Then I'll get a bigger ladle!"

Chen Hao laughed, and Wu Lanying let out a shaky laugh through her tears, easing the tension in the room just a little.

Liu Meifang pointed toward the kitchen. "Chen Xuan, cold water and a towel. Go."

Chen Xuan filled a basin, wrung out a towel, and pressed it gently against his father's swollen cheek. His jaw was tight, though his touch stayed light.

Chen Jianguo's voice came out rough. "Sorry, son. You shouldn't have had to see your old father like that."

"Does it hurt, Dad? Tell me the truth."

"Not much."

His father turned his face toward the wall.

Chen Xuan still saw that weathered hand reaching for the paintbrush. A dull ache settled in his chest.

"Well?" Chen Yuan asked from the dining table. "Two hours. Dad got slapped, the station turned you away, and what did you bring back? A thermos."

"The station said to come back tomorrow."

"Tomorrow! Big Brother, you have never won an argument in your life."

"Chen Yuan, enough." Liu Meifang didn't sound angry. She sounded afraid.

"Second Uncle is sitting in a cell, and the only man in this family with a city job says nothing."

"I'm sorry, Chen Yuan," Chen Xuan said evenly. "I just don't want to sit at home and do nothing."

"Don't apologize." Chen Yuan covered her mouth. "That's even worse."

Liu Meifang filled two bowls and set them on the table with a clack. "Two bowls. I am counting."

Chen Xuan ate the first one slowly, knowing his mother was watching. "Best ribs in the county, Mom."

"Can I go to the station with Dad tomorrow?" he asked between spoonfuls.

"To do what?" Chen Yuan snapped. "Yell at them?"

"Sit. Just sit in the lobby, so Second Uncle knows we came."

Chen Jianguo frowned. "Stay home, Chen Xuan. That's grown-up business."

"I'll sit quietly, Dad. I promise."

His father sighed and did not say no.

Chen Xuan glanced at the red welt on his father's cheek. "Dad, what time did he hit you?"

"Ten past eight. Why?"

"I want to remember it right, in case somebody asks." He said it so mildly that Chen Yuan rolled her eyes.

Then three slow knocks sounded on the front door.

Nobody moved. Liu Meifang's hand went straight for the ladle.

Chen Xuan was nearest the door, so he stepped over and opened it. He looked out into the dark stairwell.

The overhead bulb was out, and the stairs were empty.

On the doormat lay a wet red paintbrush. A strip of red paper had been taped to the door with a single character painted on it: *chai*.

Yan Tiehu had not waited for tomorrow.

"Don't touch that!" Liu Meifang cried, clutching her apron.

"It's just paint, Mom. Go sit down," Chen Xuan said evenly.

Without a word more, he picked up the wet brush, dropped it straight into the trash bin, tore the red paper off the door, and turned the deadbolt twice. His face showed nothing at all.

Chen Yuan stared at him, and for once she had nothing to say.

Chen Xuan walked into the bathroom, locked the door behind him, and turned on the tap. Alone in front of the mirror, the mild look left his face completely, and his eyes went ice-cold.

He pulled out the gray phone and sent one line to Number One.

*Absolute Order. Activate the Zero Guard. Lock down Building 2. Yan Tiehu disappears tonight.*

* * *

In the detention cell, the bench was bare concrete, and the chill seeped straight up through it.

Chen Mingde sat on his folded jacket with his hands tucked under his arms.

At eleven that night, Lei Bo had stopped by with Hongrui's sale form and a pen.

"Sign, Chen Mingde. Go home, take a hot shower. Why put yourself through this?" He tapped the pen against the bars. "Your daughter studies in Wuhan, right? Graduates next summer. The big firms run a background check on every applicant. A father with a detention record fails that check. Sign, and the record is never written."

"Please take it away." Chen Mingde spoke in the exact same tone he used for customers asking for an unreasonable discount.

His heart had pounded so hard he felt sure the whole cell could hear it. Still, he did not sign.

He thought of his daughter in her university dorm. She would never know any of this unless he told her.

Footsteps echoed down the detention corridor as the night shift made its routine check.

Through the small window in the steel door appeared Wei Kun, the thin duty officer with tired eyes and a check-in clipboard tucked under one arm. Under the corridor camera, he paused as if inspecting the cell, then slid a paper cup of hot water from the duty-room dispenser through the narrow pass-through slot.

Cheng Biao had told the night shift to give the Old Street holdouts nothing to warm them up, letting the cold bench do the talking. Wei Kun kept his face flat and his voice low, as if he were only asking a routine question.

"Drink it slowly while it's hot. Is your heart holding up?"

"Thank you, Officer."

Chen Mingde cupped the paper cup between both palms, grateful for the warmth. "Officer, will someone notify my family tomorrow about why I am here?"

Wei Kun glanced briefly toward the ceiling camera and kept his tone flat and professional. "By procedure, the written notice goes out within twenty-four hours. Keep your jacket under you so the cold from the bench doesn't settle in."

Chen Mingde looked into the tired, decent eyes of the younger officer and nodded respectfully. "You're a good man, Officer. Don't get yourself into trouble on my account."

Wei Kun gave a single stiff nod, marked his clipboard, and continued down the corridor. Back at the duty desk, he sat down in front of the blank time box on the detention form.

For twelve years, he had written whatever Cheng Biao told him to write. Tonight, for the first time, he knew the one thing he would not put down: a false hour.

* * *

In a walled courtyard in the east of Qingyan City, a large map glowed on the study wall of Number One, head of the Black Pine Guard. Two hundred and fourteen green dots shone across it.

One dot, in Yunxi County, had turned yellow and begun to blink, signaling an incoming message.

The duty captain's voice came over the speaker, tight with barely controlled anger. "Old Street Station, Chief. Number Thirty-four just flagged a red alert."

"Read it."

As the captain read the report line by line, the quiet study felt as if the temperature had dropped twenty degrees. Number One's teacup froze halfway to his lips, and his knuckles turned dead white against the porcelain.

"Repeat that," Number One said, his voice dangerously low. "Someone in a county town actually raised a hand and struck his father across the face?"

"Yes, Number One," the captain answered, his own voice shaking with disbelief and fury. "A local thug working for Hongrui Real Estate named Yan Tiehu. Even Number Thirty-four snapped his pen watching it on the station log. Our men on the ground are asking for permission to move."

Number One set the teacup down so hard the saucer cracked cleanly down the middle. For forty years, men at the very peak of the country had spoken softly in front of the Black Pine Guard, yet a street thug in Yunxi County had dared to humiliate the Boss's own father in public.

"What is the Boss doing right now?" Number One asked.

"Number Nine is stationed across the hall from their apartment, and the close-protection posts on the lobby, stairwell, and opposite rooftop never lost visual," the duty captain said rapidly, his voice tight with tension. "Our outer perimeter checkpoints scanned Yan Tiehu's van the second he entered Fuxing New Village. Rooftop sniper overwatch had him in thermal crosshairs with live ammunition chambered the entire way up, and two close-protection operatives stood behind the fifth-floor landing door with suppressed sidearms drawn. He carried only a paintbrush and paper, no lethal weapon, so under the Boss's standing non-exposure rule they held fire while recording every step. The Boss threw the brush straight into the trash, walked into the bathroom, and just sent a single line to your phone."

Number One's eyes narrowed into freezing slits. "No lethal weapon is no excuse. That man got close enough to strike the Boss's father on Old Street, and tonight he walked within three feet of his mother's front door. Every perimeter commander on shift tonight will write a full review."

The gray handset on Number One's desk lit up with a dark crimson border that had not glowed in years.

Number One's hand froze above the desk. Even the air in the quiet eastern courtyard seemed to stop moving as he read the words on the screen.

*Absolute Order. Activate the Zero Guard. Lock down Building 2. Yan Tiehu disappears tonight.*

Not a word of anger. Not a word of explanation. Just absolute, chilling finality.

Even Number One, head of the entire Black Pine Guard, had no command, no authority, and no permission over the Zero Guard. They were a specialized ghost force built solely for Chen Xuan himself. Nobody on earth, not even Number One, knew how many of them existed, what their faces looked like, or where every one of their hidden bays lay buried. Number One held only the activation relay, and it could only be triggered by a direct Absolute Order from the Boss.

"Absolute Order confirmed," Number One said, his voice dropping to dead stillness. He unlocked a matte-black case beside the wall map, turned the activation key, and pressed the crimson relay. "Pull every regular Black Pine Guard unit outside the perimeter. The Zero Guard is taking full, direct command of the fifty-meter inner ring and the entire ten-kilometer zone around Yunxi County."

On the other end of the speaker, the duty captain's breathing stopped for a full second. When he spoke again, his voice shook with pure awe. "The Zero Guard? Yes, Number One! All regular units are clearing the zone and holding the outer line under your command!"

"Tell Number Seven to pull Yan Tiehu off the ring road before he reaches Old Street," Number One ordered, his eyes cold as winter iron. "Keep it silent, wipe every camera on that stretch, leave his van idling where his own crew will find it, and put him in the dark until he understands what real fear feels like. Keep Number Ninety-two and Number Ninety-five on standby at People's Hospital in case the uncle's heart gives out tonight."

"Yes, Number One! Moving immediately!"

Within minutes, silent shadows took total control of the fifty-meter ring around Building 2 and the entire five-to-ten-kilometer zone stretching across Yunxi County. Every hidden defense grid woke up without a single sound. Concealed anti-missile batteries and anti-aircraft launchers unlocked beneath quiet hillside covers. Automated micro-turrets slid into firing arcs behind darkened rooftop vents. Live-ammunition sniper teams locked onto every street, intersection, and courtyard. Full-spectrum electronic jammers and counter-surveillance domes hummed alive, turning ten kilometers of airspace and satellite coverage into an impenetrable blind spot.

The night air around Fuxing New Village grew chillingly, suffocatingly still.

* * *

In the Lanshui Provincial Security Command Center, alarms chimed in a sharp, steady rhythm as an entire ten-kilometer grid over Yunxi County vanished from radar, traffic networks, and satellite feeds all at once.

The provincial duty director stared at his main wall screen, his face draining of color. When he typed his highest provincial authorization code to pull the reason, the screen flashed cold gray.

*INSUFFICIENT PRIVILEGE. YOUR CLEARANCE LEVEL IS NOT HIGH ENOUGH TO ACCESS THIS EVENT.*

"Insufficient privilege?!" the provincial director shouted, his hands shaking over the console. "That is my own province! Call the Provincial Party Secretary! No, call the retired old commander at his compound and ask for emergency authorization right now!"

Two minutes later, in a quiet villa inside the provincial leadership compound, an eighty-year-old retired provincial leader picked up his red phone, listened for ten seconds, and dropped his teacup onto the carpet.

"Shut your mouth right now!" the old man barked, his voice cracking with terror. "Do not send a single police car, do not fly a single drone, and do not ask another question! Pull every provincial unit back from Yunxi and pray to heaven none of our people caused this!"

* * *

Hundreds of miles away, inside a heavily guarded red-walled compound at the heart of the national capital, and deep beneath the earth in the National Supreme Military Command Center, the highest classified terminals in the nation flashed dark crimson.

*ABSOLUTE ORDER ACTIVATED. UNIT: ZERO GUARD. ZONE: LANSHUI PROVINCE, YUNXI COUNTY. RADIUS: TEN KILOMETERS. AUTHORITY: SUPREME.*

Across the entire country, the top tier skipped a beat.

Across the ocean, in heavily shielded intelligence watchrooms in Washington, Moscow, and London, senior duty officers jolted upright as orbital arrays reported a sudden ten-kilometer dead zone in eastern China, accompanied by a legendary Category-Zero defense signature that had not appeared on any global monitor in twenty years. Urgent calls tore through foreign defense ministries in the dead of night, asking the exact same frantic question: *What in God's name just woke up in Lanshui?*

Inside the underground National Supreme Military Command Center, every theater command, air defense network, and strategic missile brigade across the country shifted silently to full combat readiness.

At the head of the glass command deck stood the highest currently serving military commander in the nation, a four-star veteran who had commanded millions without ever losing his nerve. Tonight, cold sweat beaded along his collar as he stared at the crimson ten-kilometer ring glowing over a small county town.

"Every theater is armed and waiting for orders, General," his chief of staff reported in a tight whisper. "Air defense is hot. Naval fleets are on standby. Foreign satellites are already pinging the blackout. We have to know if this is an attack."

With no other choice, the supreme commander walked to the isolated red console and initiated a manual clearance call into the Black Pine Guard relay network. Layer by layer, voiceprint by voiceprint, operative by operative, his authorization was verified through five blind security nodes until the encrypted line finally connected to a single quiet desk in the eastern courtyard of Qingyan City.

"Number One," the four-star commander said, his voice tight with urgent, careful respect. "This is Supreme Military Command. Every military district in the country is at full combat readiness, and international intelligence rooms are already reacting to the blackout. Tell me what happened in Yunxi, and tell me whether the nation is going to war."

On the other end of the line, Number One sat alone beside his cracked porcelain saucer.

"Hold every theater command in place, General, and keep every outside force clear of Yunxi," Number One replied evenly. "As for what is happening inside that ten-kilometer zone, I cannot give you a full picture."

The commander's grip tightened on the receiver. "Even you don't know?"

"Even I have no command, no authority, and no permission over the Zero Guard once the Boss triggers an Absolute Order," Number One said quietly. "I do not know their exact numbers, I do not know every weapon system they have armed, and I cannot order them to stand down. All I can tell you is that I hope this ends quietly, General. Because while the Zero Guard is active, nobody on this earth can stop them except him."

Silence hung heavy over the encrypted line.

In the red-walled compound in the capital, the highest elders of the nation sat motionless over untouched cups of tea, staring at a single crimson dot on the map of Lanshui Province. Across the military command center, generals stood frozen under the harsh fluorescent lights, realizing that the entire country's war machine was ready for anything, yet completely powerless to step inside a single ten-kilometer circle.

Meanwhile, in Room 601 of Building 2, Chen Xuan turned off the bathroom tap, dried his hands on a clean towel, and walked back to his bed to sleep.
