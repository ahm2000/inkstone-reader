# Chapter 5: Above the Clouds

Thirty-eight thousand feet above the eastern hills, the gray jet rode smoothly above the clouds. Inside the cabin, only the low hum of the engines and the crisp turn of paper broke the quiet. Chen Xuan finished his warm jasmine tea from the cup lid of his green thermos.

On the surface, the file looked like an ordinary small-town rebuild dispute. Old Street was a half-kilometer row of late-eighties brick shops filled with hardware sellers, tailors, noodle stalls, and retired mill workers. Four years ago, Yunxi County marked it as an old-town rebuild area. Two years later, after three smaller local bidders dropped out under sudden tax checks, the land went at thirty percent below fair market price to Hongrui Real Estate, fronted by Deng Yongchang.

When Chen Xuan reached the back notes from the Jingzhou City-Yunxi data team, his fingers stopped on a single line.

For a quiet mountain county where new housing demand barely filled two apartment towers a year, Hongrui Real Estate was burning crazy money to force out forty-two remaining shopkeepers on the east block, right where Mingde Hardware stood. Stranger still, the tear-down border bent behind the back alleys to swallow an old government survey warehouse and water drains leading toward Qingfeng mountain ridge.

Chen Xuan tapped the map with one finger, his eyes narrowing. Builders did not hire quarry thugs like Yan Tiehu to smash counters and frame a fifty-four-year-old hardware seller over plain apartments. A fair buyout would have cost less than a month of legal bills. Someone wanted Old Street cleared fast and wiped clean before outsiders asked what lay under the paving stones.

A soft chime sounded from the armrest panel. A green light blinked beside the locked satellite phone.

Chen Xuan closed the folder, picked up the phone, and pressed the side switch. "Speak."

The voice on the locked line was calm, low, and flat. It belonged to Number 1, the unseen head of the White Guard.

"We have live monitoring over Old Street Police Station and Hongrui Real Estate's county office," Number 1 reported steadily. "Our Jingzhou City line confirmed the assault claim against Chen Mingde was filed at 1:50 by a site foreman working for Yan Tiehu. The injury slip was stamped by Yunxi People's Hospital's emergency desk forty minutes before the supposed fight even happened."

Chen Xuan's face stayed calm, though his dark eyes turned colder. "Forty minutes before."

"Correct," Number 1 said. "Sloppy work by local men who assumed a hardware seller's family had no reach outside Yunxi County. Our watcher also confirmed deputy chief Zhou Quan signed the hold slip himself right after a twelve-minute phone call from Hongrui Real Estate's downtown office."

"How is Chen Mingde holding up inside?" Chen Xuan asked, his tone flat and sharp.

"He is sitting alone in Holding Cell Two," Number 1 answered right away. "Sound checks confirm nobody has hit him. However, Zhou Quan's clerk warned him twenty minutes ago that unless his family signs the Mingde Hardware move-out paper before noon tomorrow, the case will go to the county prosecutor for an arrest review."

Chen Xuan's thumb rested still against the phone. He thought of his mother's shaking voice, of Chen Jianguo shouted away from an iron gate while holding blood pressure pills, and of Second Uncle Chen Mingde sitting beside a creek in the rain with a bamboo rod.

"Did your team check the back border of the Old Street land?" Chen Xuan asked quietly. "The old government survey warehouse and the water drains heading toward Qingfeng ridge."

A brief, alert pause followed. "We spotted that border bend ten minutes ago," Number 1 said, his voice sharpening. "Land papers list that warehouse as closed and empty since 1998. Yet town power logs show heavy, irregular high-power use on a nearby line over the past fourteen months. That does not match normal building tools."

"I thought so," Chen Xuan murmured. "Deng Yongchang is only the front man. Someone behind Hongrui Real Estate is using a town tear-down as cover, and they were careless enough to put handcuffs on my Second Uncle."

He turned toward the dark window, his voice slow, clear, and final. "Listen carefully, Number 1. Nobody in my family steps foot inside a police station. Handle the people who touched my Second Uncle, make sure he is safe tonight, and pull up every root under Old Street. Do not miss a single brick."

A half-second of silence followed as the full order sank in. Not just a release from holding. Not just a lesson for Yan Tiehu or Deng Yongchang. Every root under Old Street, down to the last buried stone.

"Understood," Number 1 replied, his voice hardening to steel. "Chen Mingde's safety and clean, lawful release will be confirmed before you reach the family yard. I am activating three teams across Yunxi and Jingzhou City right now."

"One more thing," Chen Xuan added softly. "Do not scare my parents. Keep all the noise away from their door."

"Not a whisper will reach their yard, Sir," Number 1 promised. "I will report again once the first layer peels back."

Chen Xuan placed the phone back in its cradle.

Five hundred kilometers away, in a windowless room under a warehouse in the provincial capital, Number 1 typed a nine-digit code. Across the wall screens, three team headers turned from gray to bright green: Field Watch, Money Tracing, and Local Containment.

Orders went out without a single spoken name.

In Yunxi County, Number 38 set down his teacup inside a tea house two blocks from Old Street Police Station and glanced at his wrist screen. Across the table, Number 19 saw the alert and stood up at once.

"Priority order from Number 1," Number 38 murmured as they walked outside. "He wants Chen Mingde out clean before midnight and every street camera along Old Street copied."

"I have the station's back lane and Zhou Quan's phone switch," Number 19 replied, splitting toward the other corner. "You take the clinic and Yan Tiehu's foreman."

In Jingzhou City, Number 31 and Number 27 unlocked three secure bank screens side by side. "Ten years of bank records on Hongrui Real Estate and Deng Yongchang," Number 31 said briskly, fingers flying over the keys. "Trace every coastal shell account back to its real owner."

"Pulling the first trade-zone transfer now," Number 27 answered, eyes locked on the rolling numbers. "Whoever pays Chairman Deng is about to lose their cover."

Along the dark highway exits out of Yunxi, four quiet cars from Local Containment pulled onto the gravel shoulders and turned off their lights.

Across the command wall, hundreds of green dots lit up over Jingzhou City and Yunxi County. Every road, bank record, phone line, and back-alley warehouse tied to Old Street had slipped inside a net no county official or developer could ever imagine existed.

High above the clouds, the cabin lights dimmed as the jet tipped downward toward Jingzhou-Yunxi Regional Airfield.

Chen Xuan screwed the cap onto his green thermos, tucked the uneaten red-bean bun into his canvas bag, and looked calmly down at the dark mountain valley rising to meet him.
