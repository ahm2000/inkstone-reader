# Chapter 184: Beijing's Four-Bank Crisis Room

By eight o'clock on Sunday night in Beijing, a biting northern wind swept dry leaves across the quiet courtyards behind the guarded red walls of the central compound.

Inside a wood-paneled briefing room on the third floor of the West Wing, however, the air hung heavy beneath a thick haze of blue-gray cigarette smoke.

Glass ashtrays stood along the dark walnut table, already holding half a dozen stubbed-out filters.

At the head of the table sat Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua in a dark Zhongshan jacket, elbows resting on the wood and a lit cigarette burning between two fingers.

Across from them sat four men whose signatures moved billions of yuan every single day: Fang Zhengming, National President of China Construction Bank; Han Guoliang, National President of ICBC; Tang Wenhao, National President of Bank of China; and Liang Qingshan, National President of Huaxia Bank.

For a full minute, the only sound was the crisp rustle of paper as Ding Guoping spread the Shenzhen arrest photos and financial charts across the table.

"Look at this face," Ding Guoping said in a low, gravelly voice, tapping a clear police photo of a pale, shaken man without his gold-rimmed glasses. "Koo Tianming. Arrested Thursday night inside Shen Wanshan's villa at Shenzhen Bay No. 1 alongside Shen Wanshan and Shen Jingzhou. Within four hours, Shenhai Shipping's 140 billion yuan coastal network collapsed like wet cardboard."

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua took a slow drag of his cigarette and exhaled toward the ceiling lamp. "Fourteen hours ago, our customs and anti-smuggling bureaus thought they had caught a southern shipping ring. Now tell the four bank presidents what your people pulled off Koo Tianming's money logs this morning."

Ding Guoping flipped open a red file and pushed four copies down the walnut table.

"Koo Tianming was not working for Shen Wanshan," Ding Guoping stated bluntly. "He was the chief bookkeeper for the Seven-Harbor Consortium. For twelve years, they used Hong Kong, Macau, Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Xiamen, Ningbo, and Shanghai as a giant pump. They drained raw rare-earth ore and gold from illegal southern mines and moved billions of yuan north into Jilin Province."

None of the four national bank presidents looked surprised.

Fang Zhengming, National President of China Construction Bank, spoke first.

"Vice Premier Zhou, Minister Ding," Fang Zhengming said steadily. "China Construction Bank ran a full sweep starting at seven o'clock this morning. Between 2016 and last month, eleven shell firms in Shenzhen and Hong Kong routed 18 billion yuan through fake forestry and timber-processing loans straight into three county branches bordering Changbai Mountain. Every loan was repaid on time with clean cash so our alarms never tripped."

Han Guoliang, National President of ICBC, tapped his cigarette sharply against the glass ashtray, his thick brows knitted together.

"ICBC found the other half of the pipe," Han Guoliang said gruffly, laying down a folder of power and machinery receipts. "Over the last eight years, 14 billion yuan in heavy mining machinery payments and high-voltage power fees flowed through our northern branches to an abandoned state tungsten mine deep inside the Changbai Mountain forest. That mine was sealed twenty-two years ago. Nobody is supposed to draw industrial power in those snow valleys, yet the meter bills are bigger than a steel mill."

Across the table, Tang Wenhao, National President of Bank of China, let out a bitter breath and slid a thick bundle of overseas transfer sheets toward Zhou Zhenhua.

"Bank of China traced the foreign-exchange end," Tang Wenhao said tightly. "Over the past five years, more than 9 billion yuan in overseas gold settlements and foreign payouts left Hong Kong shell accounts linked to Koo Tianming. One third went directly to a private armed security firm in Eastern Europe. The rest paid foreign buyers for refined rare-earth metals that never appeared on any Chinese export manifest."

Heavy silence settled over the smoky room.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua looked slowly toward the fourth banker, who had stayed quiet until now.

"President Liang Qingshan," Zhou Zhenhua said softly, his tone calm yet sharp. "Huaxia Bank has been very quiet tonight. Did your audit team find something different?"

Liang Qingshan, National President of Huaxia Bank, hesitated for two seconds.

"Vice Premier Zhou, our audit confirmed the exact same Changbai Mountain money trail," Liang Qingshan replied carefully. "Another 7 billion yuan in shipping guarantees tied straight back to those timber fronts. However, there is something else in our logs from Thursday night in Shenzhen and yesterday afternoon in Shanghai that you and Minister Ding need to hear."

Ding Guoping's eyes narrowed at once. "Speak plainly, President Liang."

"Thursday night at Shenzhen Bay No. 1," Liang Qingshan said slowly, "our Shenzhen chief He Zhengguo moved together with the Shenzhen chiefs of China Construction Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China. They carried out a police freeze order on Shen Wanshan's group in thirty minutes flat. None of us four presidents in Beijing told them to move so fast."

Fang Zhengming, Han Guoliang, and Tang Wenhao exchanged uneasy glances. None of them denied it.

"And yesterday afternoon in Shanghai," Liang Qingshan continued quietly, "a matte-white vault card carrying the highest priority code of a Huaxia Bank private vault was swiped at Plaza 66 for three winter coats costing 144k yuan. Our Lujiazui branch director ran down the escalator himself. The card holder was a quiet young man in a plain windbreaker. Twelve hours later, Koo Tianming's complete banking summary appeared inside all four of our national audit desks before sunrise."

The cigarette between Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua's fingers stopped halfway to his lips.

Beside him, Minister Ding Guoping went completely still. Neither man looked at the four bankers. Instead, Zhou Zhenhua and Ding Guoping exchanged one long, silent look across the drifting haze of smoke. Both remembered a certain night not long ago, when forty-plus officials signed a central order at two minutes to nine, and silent watchers vanished from Beijing and Sha Province without leaving a footprint.

"That matter stays inside this room," Zhou Zhenhua said evenly, stubbing his cigarette out in the glass ashtray. "Do not dig into that card, and do not question your Shanghai or Shenzhen directors. Focus on Changbai Mountain."

Liang Qingshan exhaled softly and nodded. "Understood, Vice Premier."

"Good," Ding Guoping said, turning back to the Changbai Mountain map. "Thermal scans and power-meter records confirm heavy industrial activity inside that mountain, and at least 6 billion yuan in stolen rare metals and the Seven-Harbor Consortium's master ledger are believed to sit inside that snow valley right now. Once word of Koo Tianming's arrest reaches Changbai Mountain, they will run. We need to seal that valley before dawn."

"Send in the northern garrison and armed police tonight," Han Guoliang urged, leaning forward. "Surround the mountain with armored trucks and helicopters and arrest every last one of them before sunrise!"

Before Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua could answer, the heavy double wooden doors at the back of the briefing room swung open cleanly.

Steady leather boots stepped onto the thick carpet. Every head around the smoky table turned at once.
