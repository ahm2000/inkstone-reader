# Chapter 184: Beijing's Four-Bank Crisis Room

Hou Liangping's encrypted transmission reached the capital before midnight. Deep beneath Beijing's main administrative building, the secure briefing room glowed under bright white overhead lamps, its long conference table spread with satellite terrain maps of the Changbai Mountain range, forensic loan ledgers, and customs clearance logs.

At the head of the table sat Premier Gu Chengshan, his expression grave and attentive. Beside him sat Elder Song, ninety years old yet steady as carved stone, his hands folded calmly over his dark wool tunic. Facing them sat the four men responsible for the pillars of the nation's banking system: Fang Zhengming of China Construction Bank, Han Guoliang of Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank, and Tang Wenhao of Bank of China.

Fang Zhengming opened the briefing without wasting a word. He stood and tapped the illuminated display board showing a web of red financial lines stretching from the Yangtze River Delta and Handong Province straight up into the northeast.

"Premier, Elder Song," Fang Zhengming reported clearly. "Once the 8 trillion emergency reserve deployment plugged the 1.2 trillion dirty-loan hole and calmed the 3.8 trillion regional credit shock this autumn, our joint audit teams finally had the breathing room to trace every missing yuan down to the last ledger. What we uncovered was a secondary drainage channel buried under agricultural and forestry subsidies. Over the past fourteen months, forty-two shell companies borrowed billions against phantom timber reserves in Changbai Mountain."

Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank followed immediately, sliding a concise summary sheet across the table. "Our provincial branches just cross-checked the new files sent up from Handong's discipline inspection team two hours ago. Guarantee firms tied to Zhao Ruilong's former Shanshui network diverted over 800 million meant for the Luzhou Airport corridor and Crescent Lake remediation straight into those same northern forestry accounts. Two neighboring provinces show matching leaks, all signed off by corrupt local credit officers who have already been detained for questioning."

Premier Gu Chengshan's brow furrowed deeply as he scanned the bank transfer dates. "So they used public infrastructure budgets and state forestry credits as a funnel while our markets were under pressure. Where did the cash go after it entered those northern accounts?"

Tang Wenhao of Bank of China rose, his face tight with controlled anger. "It was converted into physical gold, Premier. Our cross-border clearing desk matched the northern timber loans against abnormal bullion settlements moving through Shenzhen and Hong Kong brokers. Every time a tranche cleared overseas, corresponding ingots were smuggled back across the coast and shipped north by private rail freight under fake logging machinery manifests. According to our reconciled weight logs and satellite heat scans from the northern slope, roughly 300 tons of gold are sitting inside a hidden refinery valley in Changbai Mountain right now."

A heavy, suffocating silence dropped over the briefing room. Three hundred tons of gold represented a staggering share of national wealth, quietly siphoned out of the banking system and stockpiled inside a remote mountain valley by foreign-backed syndicates.

Han Guoliang of ICBC was the first to break the silence. His face was flushed with urgency as he slapped his open palm onto his red folder. "Premier, we cannot afford to sit here studying spreadsheets until morning! Late November weather in Changbai turns brutal overnight. Once the first heavy winter storms hit those passes, visibility drops to zero. If those criminals realize their Hong Kong brokers are under investigation, they will load that gold onto snow trucks or small cargo planes and slip it across the border before sunrise."

Han Guoliang stood up, leaning forward across the table. "I strongly recommend mobilizing armed police and rapid-response helicopters from the nearest regional garrison at dawn. Drop a heavy cordon straight into the valley, storm the refinery before they wake up, and lock down all 300 tons immediately. Speed is the only thing that saves those reserves!"

Fang Zhengming frowned slightly, glancing at the steep contour lines on the mountain map, while Liang Qingshan exchanged an uneasy look with Tang Wenhao. None of the other three presidents had military experience, yet rushing helicopters into a narrow granite canyon sounded risky even on paper. Premier Gu Chengshan did not rush to endorse the proposal. His fingers tapped once against the edge of his folder as he weighed the risk of delay against the danger of a hasty assault.

He turned his head toward Elder Song. "Elder Song, what is your view?"

Elder Song did not even raise his voice. He glanced calmly at the wall clock, where the red digits read 9:30 PM, and let out a quiet, measured breath.

"We stabilized the financial markets this autumn because we acted with precision rather than panic," Elder Song said smoothly. "The four of you are the finest bankers in the country, and your audit work tonight is exemplary. However, before bankers start planning a dawn helicopter assault into a granite mountain valley, I think we should listen to someone who actually commanded troops in the northern snow."

Han Guoliang blinked, momentarily caught off guard. "Elder Song, who did you invite?"

Before Elder Song could answer, the heavy double doors at the far end of the briefing room swung open with a crisp, authoritative sound. Heavy, steady footsteps crossed the threshold, and every man at the table turned to look.
