# Chapter 141: Number 1's Question

At 4:55 PM on Friday afternoon, Chen Xuan closed the shipping ledger on his desk, wiped his fountain pen with a tissue, and tucked his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos under his arm.

Outside the wide glass windows of the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics, late November sunlight slanted across the Shanghai skyline. Golden and brown plane leaves drifted lazily past the glass in the cool autumn breeze.

Across the office, General Manager Sun Haoran shrugged into his suit jacket with a bright grin. "Have a good weekend, Chen Xuan! Do not let me catch you thinking about freight bills on your days off."

"You never have to worry about that, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile. "The second I step out of this elevator, my brain shuts off until Monday at 9:00 AM."

"That is why you sleep better than the rest of us!" Sun Haoran laughed, waving as he headed toward the corridor.

Chen Xuan locked his drawer, clocked out right on the minute, and rode the elevator down to the lobby. Out on the tree-lined Pudong sidewalk, the late autumn chill felt crisp and clean. He zipped his plain dark jacket up to his collarbone and walked at an unhurried pace toward the neighborhood market to pick up fresh pork ribs and sweet winter bamboo shoots for dinner at Emerald Riverside Villa.

Halfway down the block, a cheerful college student walking a fluffy golden retriever slowed down to wave at him. "Hey, Brother Chen! Getting off work right on time again?"

"Always," Chen Xuan replied, bending down to scratch the dog behind its ears. "How did your midterm exam go?"

"Passed with plenty of room to spare," the student grinned. "My mom said I can finally stop surviving on instant noodles this week."

A few paces further on, the auntie who ran the corner fruit stall tossed an extra tangerine into his paper bag as he paid for a bunch of green onions. "Take it for your wife, young Chen. She works too hard at that tall building of hers. Make sure she drinks hot soup tonight."

"Thank you, Auntie Zhou. I will tell her you said hello," Chen Xuan said warmly.

He continued along the quiet stone footpath beside the riverside park, where the evening wind rustled through rows of yellowing willows. Just as he passed a wooden bench under an old camphor tree, the single regular smartphone in his pocket vibrated with a soft, steady chime.

Chen Xuan pulled it out and glanced at the caller ID. It showed a simple, unmarked string of digits routed cleanly through his everyday phone.

He sat down on the wooden bench, set his grocery bag beside his sneakers, and answered. "Afternoon, Number 1. Is something keeping you busy on a Friday evening?"

"Good evening, Young Master," Number 1's calm, steady voice came through the speaker. "I apologize for disturbing your walk home. A private message reached me from Beijing an hour ago, and I felt I should pass it along to you directly."

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said, unscrewing his green thermos for a quick sip of lukewarm tea.

"It is from Elder Song," Number 1 said carefully. "He stepped out of retirement during the banking crisis to help steady the cabinet, and now that the provincial cleanup in Handong is running smoothly, he has sent a personal request. He would like to invite you for one quiet cup of tea tomorrow morning at a lakeside pavilion in the Western Hills."

Chen Xuan paused, raising an eyebrow slightly. "Elder Song? The ninety-year-old gentleman?"

"Yes, Young Master," Number 1 confirmed. "He gave his personal guarantee that there will be no guards, no cameras, no aides, and no official records of any kind. Even Premier Gu Chengshan has not been told about this invitation."

Silence settled over the line for a few seconds as a pair of joggers trotted past along the riverside path.

"Young Master, may I ask a question?" Number 1 spoke again, his tone polite and genuinely curious. "You have always kept a firm wall between your quiet life in Shanghai and the people in Beijing. Why would Elder Song take such remarkable care to arrange a private meeting with zero witnesses, and are you inclined to go?"

Chen Xuan leaned back against the wooden bench and looked out over the shimmering river water. "Elder Song is not trying to pull me into politics, Number 1. An old soldier who spent seventy years looking after this country simply wants to sit down with another human being over a pot of tea and say thank you face to face. He cleared the whole hill because he respects my privacy."

Number 1 exhaled softly, understanding dawning in his voice. "That makes complete sense, Young Master. He is a man of his word. Still, if you prefer to decline, I can send a polite refusal tonight and no one will press the matter."

Chen Xuan thought for a moment. Tomorrow was Saturday. Qin Yuhan had a morning video conference with a supplier from Suzhou, and Elder Qin Zhengguo was still up in Beijing visiting old comrades until next week.

"No need to refuse an elder's kindness," Chen Xuan said with a relaxed smile. "Tell Elder Song I will be happy to share a cup of tea with him tomorrow morning. Have Number 7 arrange an early flight so I can land in Beijing around 8:30 AM and make it back to Shanghai before 3:00 PM."

"Right away, Young Master," Number 1 answered crisply. "I will handle every detail."

"Good. Enjoy your weekend, Number 1."

Chen Xuan hung up, slipped his single phone back into his pocket, and picked up his grocery bag. Now he just needed a believable excuse for Qin Yuhan so she would not wonder where he went on a Saturday morning.

"A quick Saturday inventory check at the suburban warehouse," Chen Xuan murmured to himself, chuckling as he walked toward the villa gate. "Simple, boring, and impossible to argue with."
