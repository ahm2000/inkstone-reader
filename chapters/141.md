# Chapter 141: Number 1's Question

At 4:55 on Friday afternoon, Chen Xuan closed the shipping ledger on his desk, wiped his pen, and tucked his scratched green thermos under his arm.

Across the Hengxin Logistics office, Sun Haoran shrugged into his suit jacket with a smile. "Have a good weekend, Chen Xuan! Do not work too hard on your rest days."

"You too, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said easily. "See you Monday morning."

He walked downstairs onto the cool Shanghai sidewalk. Fifty paces down the curb, a plain sedan sat with its engine off. Standing beside the rear door in a crisp white coat was Number 1, hands clasped and face calm.

Chen Xuan walked over at an unhurried pace. "You came in person today," he said mildly.

"Good afternoon, sir," Number 1 replied, opening the door. "May we speak somewhere quiet for ten minutes?"

"Of course."

Ten minutes later, they sat in a small private room at the back of an old neighborhood teahouse with a pot of roasted barley tea and two ceramic cups. Once the waiter closed the sliding door, Number 1 sat upright in his white coat, pulled an unaddressed white envelope from his pocket, and laid it on the table.

"Word arrived from the capital two hours ago," Number 1 said quietly. "General Qin Zhengguo sent it through our private line."

Chen Xuan poured two cups of barley tea, pushing one across. "Did something happen with the clean up?"

"No, sir. The forty departments are quiet, and the courts are handling the rest," Number 1 said, his dark eyes steady. "The message is from Elder Song, the ninety year old elder in the Western Hills."

Chen Xuan set the teapot down with a soft clink. "He is ready for that cup of tea."

"Yes, sir. Tomorrow morning, if your weekend is free," Number 1 said. "General Qin Zhengguo arranged a private landing slot at the capital's executive airfield. No flight list will carry your name, and no camera along the Western Hills road will record. Only Elder Song will sit in the pavilion, while General Qin Zhengguo waits fifty paces back along the stone path."

The room went quiet as a bicycle bell rang out in the alley. Number 1 folded his hands, his calm voice carrying a faint edge of genuine worry. "May I smoke, sir?"

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said, sliding a glass ashtray across.

Number 1 lit a cigarette, took one slow drag, and looked up. "Sir, forgive me for asking this. Do you truly want to meet Elder Song face to face?"

Chen Xuan picked up his cup and blew gently across the tea. "Why do you ask?"

"Because until today, you have been a ghost to everyone in the capital," Number 1 answered plainly. "Gao Zhengyi, Zhou Zhenhua, Ding Guoping, and the ministers who signed the joint orders only know the results, not your face or real name. That distance keeps your family in Yunxi County safe and your ordinary life in Shanghai untouched."

Number 1 tapped ash into the tray, his expression serious.

"Elder Song is the last living founder of his generation," Number 1 continued. "Even at ninety, his mind is sharp as a blade. Once you sit across from him, he will remember your face for the rest of his life, and General Qin Zhengguo will be fifty paces away. If you prefer, I can reply tonight that you are away on urgent business, or arrange a private phone call instead. Elder Song would understand."

Chen Xuan sipped his warm barley tea and looked at Number 1 with an easy smile. "You always think three steps ahead. That is why I trust you with the whole house."

Number 1 lowered his eyes. "It is my duty to worry, sir."

"Still, you do not need to worry about tomorrow," Chen Xuan said calmly, resting a hand on his scratched green thermos. "Elder Song is ninety years old, and he held the roof up when the sky shook. One cup of tea is only polite."

Number 1 stared at him for two seconds before the tight line between his eyebrows softened. He let out a breath and stubbed out his cigarette.

"Yes, sir," Number 1 murmured, bowing his head. "You are right. I was thinking like a guard, not like a guest."

"Being a good guard is nothing to apologize for," Chen Xuan said with a chuckle. "Besides, if Elder Song wanted to dig up my name, he could have ordered twenty bureaus to try last month. He did not. He only boiled a kettle of water."

"General Qin Zhengguo is a man of strict honor too," Number 1 agreed quietly. "He did not ask a single question about who you are, only what time your plane would touch down."

"Then let us not keep an old man waiting," Chen Xuan said. "What time should I be at the airfield tomorrow?"

"Seven thirty in the morning," Number 1 replied. "Number 7 will fly up with you in white gear on your private jet and handle the car in the capital. You can land by ten, take tea by eleven, and be back in Shanghai for dinner."

"Good. I have no plans for Saturday anyway," Chen Xuan said.

Number 1 glanced toward the frosted window. "There is one small matter, sir."

"The people outside?" Chen Xuan asked mildly.

"Yes, sir," Number 1 said softly. "Several Black Guard members are sitting in a van fifty meters down the alley. They never speak to our White Guard men, yet they follow wherever you go. Tomorrow in the Western Hills, if masked figures in plain black gear appear fifty meters from Elder Song's pavilion, his guards could react."

"Have they ever stepped closer than fifty meters, or raised a hand against anyone who meant no harm?" Chen Xuan asked.

"Never, sir."

"Then leave them be," Chen Xuan said simply. "They know their rules, and Elder Song's guards will know the difference between a threat and a shadow."

Number 1 nodded. "I will brief Number 7 tonight. Everything will be ready at seven thirty."

Outside in the cool evening air, Chen Xuan declined a ride home and walked off with his thermos tucked under his arm, while fifty meters behind him across the street, a quiet figure in a dark jacket walked on in silence.
