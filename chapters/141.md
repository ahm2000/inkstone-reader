# Chapter 141: Number 1's Question

At 4:55 PM on Friday afternoon, Chen Xuan closed the shipping ledger on his desk, wiped his fountain pen with a tissue, and tucked his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos under his arm.

Outside the wide glass windows of the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics, late November sunlight slanted across the Shanghai skyline. Golden and brown plane leaves drifted lazily past the glass in the cool autumn breeze.

Across the office, General Manager Sun Haoran shrugged into his suit jacket with a bright grin. "Have a good weekend, Chen Xuan! Do not let me catch you thinking about freight bills on your days off."

"You never have to worry about that, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile. "The second I step out of this elevator, my brain shuts off until Monday at 9:00 AM."

"That is why you sleep better than the rest of us!" Sun Haoran laughed, waving from the hallway. "Give my regards to Yuhan! Tell her not to work too hard."

"I will. See you Monday."

Chen Xuan rode the elevator down to the lobby and stepped out onto the crisp sidewalk. The late autumn wind carried the dry scent of fallen leaves and the sweet, smoky aroma of roasted chestnuts from the corner stall. He zipped his plain dark windbreaker up to his collarbone and strolled down the block at an unhurried pace.

Beside the bus stop, Uncle Wu stood behind his steaming iron drum, wearing a faded padded vest and a wool cap pulled low over his ears. The cheerful neighborhood elder looked like he had been roasting sweet potatoes on this exact street corner for thirty years.

"Heading home right on time again, Xuan?" Uncle Wu called out warmly, lifting the lid of the drum to let a cloud of fragrant steam curl into the chilly air. "I saved two of the honey-hearted ones on the side for your wife. Nice and soft."

"You always know what she likes, Uncle Wu," Chen Xuan chuckled, fishing a crumpled twenty-yuan bill from his pocket. "Keep the change. It is getting cold out here, so pack up early tonight."

"Listen to this kid telling an old man when to go home," Uncle Wu laughed, slipping two piping hot sweet potatoes into a brown paper bag and handing them over. "Go on, hurry back before they cool down. A good husband never lets his wife eat cold snacks."

Chen Xuan tucked the warm paper bag into the crook of his arm and nodded with a quiet smile. Neither of them gave a single stiff glance or formal gesture. Anyone passing by saw only a relaxed office clerk chatting with his favorite street vendor.

A few paces past the corner, the single everyday smartphone in Chen Xuan's jacket pocket vibrated softly against his ribs.

He paused beneath a tall plane tree, shifted his green thermos under his arm, and pulled out the phone. The screen showed a simple saved contact: Number 1.

Chen Xuan stepped aside from the foot traffic and answered. "Everything alright?"

"Good afternoon, Young Master," Number 1's steady, respectful voice came through the line. There was no background noise on his end, only the calm focus of a man who had spent decades handling impossible affairs without blinking. "I apologize for interrupting your evening commute, Young Master. An unusual request reached our secure channel an hour ago, and I felt I had to report it to you directly."

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said calmly, watching a red bus rumble past the intersection.

"It comes from Beijing," Number 1 said, lowering his voice slightly. "Specifically, from Elder Song."

Chen Xuan's eyebrows lifted a fraction. Ninety-year-old Elder Song was the last active founding elder still guiding the highest table in the capital. Even Premier Gu Chengshan listened when the old man spoke. After the 300 trillion yuan sovereign reserve had been transferred into the national treasury, Elder Song had personally stepped in to seal the audit files and keep greedy hands away from the money.

"What does the old gentleman want?" Chen Xuan asked.

"He is asking for one hour of your time tomorrow morning, Young Master," Number 1 replied carefully. "He wants to meet you face to face at a small lakeside pavilion in the Western Hills, completely alone. Just the two of you over a pot of tea."

Chen Xuan was silent for a moment, letting the cool November breeze brush his face. "Completely alone?"

"Those were his exact words, Young Master," Number 1 confirmed. "He gave his personal guarantee that the entire north ridge will be cleared before dawn. Every camera pole powered down, every guard post withdrawn to the south valley, no aides, no secretaries, and not even Premier Gu Chengshan informed. He asked only to thank the holder of the Black Order in person before his health gives out."

Number 1 paused, his tone growing cautious. "If you would like, Young Master, I can decline politely on your behalf. The top offices in Beijing have been desperate to learn even a single clue about your identity. Even with Elder Song's lifelong reputation for honor, stepping into the capital in person carries a risk you have no reason to take."

Chen Xuan looked down at the warm paper bag in his hand. He thought about the ninety-year-old man who had spent his whole life guarding the country's grain stores and keeping the lights on for hundreds of millions of families. If Elder Song had wanted to play tricks, he would never have sent a quiet request like this.

"No need to decline," Chen Xuan said softly. "An elder of his age and character deserves that much courtesy. Tell him I will be there tomorrow morning."

"Understood, Young Master," Number 1 answered at once, without a hint of hesitation. "I will arrange a private flight from the suburban airstrip early tomorrow morning and have Number 7 handle both the flight and the car in Beijing personally. Would you like to return to Shanghai in the afternoon?"

"Yes," Chen Xuan smiled lightly. "Make sure I am back in Pudong before 3:00 PM. If I miss dinner at home, my wife is going to lecture my general manager on Monday."

A faint, respectful warmth touched Number 1's voice. "Everything will be arranged smoothly, Young Master. Have a pleasant evening."

Chen Xuan ended the call and slipped his single smartphone back into his pocket. The roasted sweet potatoes still radiated gentle heat through the brown paper bag. Smiling to himself, he turned toward the subway entrance to head home to Emerald Riverside Villa and start dinner for Qin Yuhan.
