# Chapter 141: Number 1's Question

At 4:55 on Friday afternoon, Chen Xuan closed the shipping ledger on his desk, wiped his pen clean, and tucked his scratched green thermos under his arm.

Across the office of Hengxin Logistics, Sun Haoran was already shrugging into his suit jacket with a bright smile. "Have a good weekend, Chen Xuan! Try not to work too hard on your rest days."

"You too, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said easily. "See you Monday morning."

He walked down the stairs and stepped out onto the cool Qingyan sidewalk. The late-afternoon sun threw long, soft shadows across the street. Office workers hurried toward bus stops and noodle shops, glad the week was over.

Fifty paces down the curb, a plain black sedan sat with its engine off. Standing beside the rear door in a neat charcoal coat was Number 1. He did not wave or call out. He simply waited with his hands clasped in front of him, his face calm and unreadable.

Chen Xuan walked over at his usual unhurried pace. "You came in person today," he said mildly.

"Good afternoon, sir," Number 1 replied, opening the rear door. "May we speak somewhere quiet for ten minutes?"

"Of course."

They did not drive far. Ten minutes later, they were sitting across from each other in a small private room at the back of an old neighborhood teahouse. The wooden table between them held a pot of plain roasted barley tea and two thick ceramic cups. The waiter closed the sliding door and left them alone.

Number 1 did not touch his cup right away. He sat upright, his shoulders slightly tighter than usual. After a brief pause, he reached into his coat pocket, pulled out a plain white envelope with no address on it, and laid it flat on the table.

"Word arrived from the capital two hours ago," Number 1 said quietly. "General Qin Zhengguo sent it himself through our private line."

Chen Xuan poured two cups of barley tea, pushing one across the wood. "Did something happen with the clean-up?"

"No, sir. The forty departments are quiet, and the courts are handling the rest," Number 1 said. He hesitated for half a second, his dark eyes steady on Chen Xuan's face. "The message is from Elder Song. The ninety-year-old elder in the Western Hills."

Chen Xuan's hand paused gently on the handle of the teapot. Then he set it down with a soft clink. "He is ready for that cup of tea."

"Yes, sir. Tomorrow morning, if your weekend is free," Number 1 said. "General Qin Zhengguo arranged a private landing slot at the capital's executive airfield. No flight list will carry your name. No camera along the Western Hills road will be recording. Only Elder Song will sit in the pavilion, and General Qin Zhengguo will wait fifty paces back along the stone path."

The small room went quiet. Outside in the alley, a bicycle bell rang twice and faded away.

Number 1 folded his hands on the table. For the first time in months, his calm voice carried a faint edge of genuine worry. He reached slowly into his pocket, paused, and glanced at Chen Xuan.

"May I smoke, sir?"

"Go ahead," Chen Xuan said, sliding a small glass ashtray across the table.

Number 1 lit a cigarette, took one slow drag, and let the thin gray smoke drift toward the cracked window. He stared at the glowing tip for a moment before looking back up.

"Sir, forgive me for asking this," Number 1 said, his voice low and deeply respectful. "Do you truly want to meet Elder Song face to face?"

Chen Xuan picked up his cup of barley tea and blew gently across the surface. "Why do you ask?"

"Because until today, you have been a ghost to everyone in the capital," Number 1 answered plainly. "Gao Zhengyi, Zhou Zhenhua, Ding Guoping, even the ministers who signed the joint orders, none of them know your face or your real name. They only know the results. That distance keeps your family in Yunxi County safe. It keeps your ordinary life here in Qingyan untouched."

Number 1 tapped a tiny bit of ash into the glass tray. His expression was serious, almost heavy.

"Elder Song is the last living founder of his generation," Number 1 continued. "Even at ninety, his mind is sharp as a blade. Once you sit across from him in the Western Hills, he will remember your face for the rest of his life. And General Qin Zhengguo will be fifty paces away. If you prefer, I can send a polite reply tonight. I can tell General Qin Zhengguo that you are away on urgent business, or arrange a private phone call instead. Elder Song would understand."

Chen Xuan listened without interrupting. He took a small sip of the warm barley tea, set the cup down, and looked at Number 1 with an easy, quiet smile.

"You always think three steps ahead," Chen Xuan said warmly. "That is why I trust you with the whole house."

Number 1 lowered his eyes slightly. "It is my duty to worry, sir."

"Still, you do not need to worry about tomorrow," Chen Xuan said calmly. He rested one hand lightly on his scratched green thermos beside the teapot. "Think about who Elder Song is. He is ninety years old, and he held the roof up when the sky shook. One cup of tea is only polite."

Number 1 stared at him for two full seconds. The tight line between his eyebrows slowly softened. A quiet breath left his chest, and he stubbed out his half-finished cigarette in the glass tray.

"Yes, sir," Number 1 murmured, bowing his head slightly. "You are right. I was thinking like a guard, not like a guest."

"Being a good guard is nothing to apologize for," Chen Xuan said with a faint chuckle. "Besides, Elder Song is not the kind of man who sends people poking into a clerk's family tree. If he wanted to dig up my name, he could have ordered twenty bureaus to try last month. He did not. He only boiled a kettle of water."

"General Qin Zhengguo is a man of strict honor too," Number 1 agreed quietly. "When he spoke to me, he did not ask a single question about who you are. He only asked what time your plane would touch down."

"Then let us not keep an old man waiting," Chen Xuan said. "What time should I be at the airfield tomorrow?"

"Seven thirty in the morning," Number 1 replied, all business again. "Number 7 will fly up with you on your private jet and handle the car in the capital. The plane is already fueled at the south executive hangar outside Qingyan. You can land by ten, take tea by eleven, and be back in Qingyan in time for a late dinner."

"Good. I have no plans for Saturday anyway," Chen Xuan said.

Number 1 paused, his eyes flicking briefly toward the frosted window overlooking the quiet alley. "There is one small matter, sir."

"The people outside?" Chen Xuan asked mildly.

"Yes, sir," Number 1 said, keeping his voice low. "At least three Black Guard members are sitting in a plain van fifty meters down the alley right now. They have not spoken a word to my men in weeks, yet they follow wherever you go. Tomorrow we are flying into the capital, straight into the Western Hills. The guards around Elder Song are some of the finest soldiers in the country. If masked figures in plain black gear appear fifty meters from the pavilion, there could be tension."

Chen Xuan smiled faintly, picking up his green thermos. "Have they ever stepped closer than fifty meters?"

"Never, sir. Not by a single inch."

"Have they ever raised a hand against anyone who meant no harm?"

"No, sir."

"Then leave them be," Chen Xuan said simply. "They know their own rules better than we do. And Elder Song's guards are professionals. They will know the difference between a threat and a shadow."

Number 1 nodded slowly, accepting the answer without pushing further. "I will brief Number 7 tonight. Everything will be ready at seven thirty."

They walked back out into the cool evening air. The sky over Qingyan was turning a soft purple, and the smell of fried dumplings drifted from a corner stall down the block.

Number 1 opened the car door to offer Chen Xuan a ride home, but Chen Xuan shook his head politely.

"It is only a twenty-minute walk," Chen Xuan said, tucking his green thermos comfortably under his arm. "A bit of fresh air is good before an early flight."

Number 1 stood straight beside the black sedan and gave a quiet nod. "Good evening, sir. Have a restful night."

Chen Xuan waved lightly and turned toward the busy main road, blending right back into the Friday evening crowd. Fifty meters behind him, across the street beside a closed hardware shop, a quiet figure in a plain dark jacket glanced once at the fading sunset and walked on in silence.
