# Chapter 123: Lunch with Qin Yuhan

At twelve-fifteen, two blocks south of Haiyue Tower, steam rolled softly from the open kitchen of Old Zhou's Noodle and Dumpling House.

It was a modest corner shop with six wooden tables and a wide window facing the street.

Qin Yuhan sat by the window in a cream blouse and dark slacks, her jacket draped over her chair.

"I gave my driver the afternoon off," Qin Yuhan said as Chen Xuan sat down. "If I booked a private room at the Grand Harbor Hotel to thank you, you would probably skip lunch and eat instant noodles at your desk."

"Instant noodles are fast," Chen Xuan replied easily, setting his green thermos on the table. "Still, steamed pork dumplings beat instant noodles every day."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, the sharp edge she wore in boardrooms softening into ease.

"Eat while they are hot," Qin Yuhan said, pushing the bamboo basket closer. "And thank you again, Chen Xuan. My father, Qin Zhengming, looked over the port customs audit yesterday morning. If you had not caught that overcharge on the cold-chain containers before the sweep hit, Haiyue Group would have faced three months of frozen docks and ugly headlines."

"I just checked the shipping receipts," Chen Xuan said calmly, dipping a dumpling lightly into black vinegar. "Your finance clerks would have spotted the extra weight fee sooner or later."

"Later would have been too late," Qin Yuhan said, her clear dark eyes studying him over the rising steam. "That is the funny thing about you, Chen Xuan. You talk like an ordinary twelve-thousand-yuan clerk who just got lucky with a calculator. Yet every time Shanghai gets caught in a storm, you are somehow standing in the one dry spot on the street."

Chen Xuan chewed slowly, swallowed, and gave her a mild smile. "Maybe I just carry a good umbrella."

Qin Yuhan shook her head with an amused sigh, though she did not press the joke.

"Speaking of storms," Qin Yuhan said, lowering her voice slightly, "I heard Hengxin Logistics got invited to next month's Sha Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum."

Chen Xuan looked up. "Word travels fast inside Haiyue Tower."

"My secretary sits on the coastal shipping committee," Qin Yuhan said with a slight smile. "She saw the guest list this morning. General Manager Sun Haoran is listed with one assistant. Let me guess: Hu Dewei suddenly developed a bad back, and you are the one packing a suitcase."

Chen Xuan let out a quiet laugh. "Word travels very fast. Yes, I am going with General Manager Sun. Sha City is close to my home county of Yunxi anyway."

"Then I will see you there," Qin Yuhan said, her eyes brightening. "I am going to Sha City for Haiyue Group. My father wants me to represent our coastal shipping division on the main panel."

She reached for the small glass vinegar bottle to add a few drops to her saucer.

Her hand paused for half a second.

Across the narrow street, fifty meters away beside a corner coffee stall, two quiet men in plain dark coats stood side by side near a stone planter.

Qin Yuhan had arrived fifteen minutes before Chen Xuan.

She watched them for three quiet seconds. Neither man lifted his cup to his mouth. Neither checked his phone or spoke to the vendor. Their eyes moved in calm, steady sweeps across the crosswalk, the parked cars, and the front door of the noodle shop.

Qin Yuhan set the vinegar bottle down gently and looked back at Chen Xuan.

"Chen Xuan," she asked softly, her tone light yet razor-sharp, "do you know those two men across the street?"

Chen Xuan did not stiffen or turn too fast. He finished a spoonful of beef broth first, then glanced casually through the window toward the coffee stall fifty meters away.

"The two in dark coats?" Chen Xuan asked calmly.

"They bought two cups of coffee twenty minutes ago," Qin Yuhan murmured, resting her chin lightly on her hand. "I noticed them when I walked in. Not once have they taken a sip, so their coffee must be stone cold by now. Every time a delivery van slows down outside this shop, both of them shift their shoulders at once."

Chen Xuan looked back at her with a relaxed smile. "Haiyue Tower is two blocks away, Miss Qin. Half the office buildings on this street hire plainclothes guards to watch the side alleys at lunch. Maybe they are guarding one of your vice presidents, or maybe they just hate how bitter corner-stall coffee tastes."

Qin Yuhan stared at him for two full beats, her lips curving into a knowing smile.

"Right," she said softly. "Plainclothes building guards who happen to watch the exact noodle shop where a Hengxin clerk eats dumplings."

"People in Shanghai take their dumplings very seriously," Chen Xuan replied without missing a beat.

Qin Yuhan let out a quiet laugh and picked up her chopsticks again. She was smart enough to know when a man had no intention of answering, and polite enough not to push past the line he drew.

"Fine, keep your secrets," she said warmly. "Still, since we are both heading to Sha City next month, keep your eyes open inside the convention hall. Something about this forum feels rushed."

"How so?" Chen Xuan asked.

"Wanfeng Freight," Qin Yuhan said, turning serious. "Their chairman, Song Baisheng, has been calling coastal companies all week, pushing a new provincial freight data alliance. He wants twenty companies to sign a joint routing contract live on stage in front of the deputy governor, yet he refuses to send out the full technical contract ahead of time."

Chen Xuan listened quietly, his fingers resting beside his green thermos. "That does sound hurried for a provincial deal."

"My father taught me one rule about shipping contracts," Qin Yuhan said coolly. "When someone rushes you to sign in front of cameras, there is a line on page thirty they do not want you to read. Do not let Sun Haoran sign anything in Sha City before we read every page."

"Good advice," Chen Xuan said with a gentle nod. "I will make sure General Manager Sun keeps his pen in his pocket until we read the fine print."

Qin Yuhan smiled, looking noticeably lighter. "Which train are you taking north? If our times match, we can grab seats in the same carriage and split a pot of tea."

"Sun Haoran is booking the evening express next week," Chen Xuan replied. "I will send you the train number once he hands me the ticket."

"Deal," Qin Yuhan said, lifting her water glass in a small toast. "And next time, you are buying the dumplings."

Chen Xuan lifted his green thermos and tapped it lightly against her glass. Outside the window, fifty meters across the street, the two quiet men in dark coats still stood motionless beside the coffee stall, their untouched paper cups cooling in the breeze.
