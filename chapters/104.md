# Chapter 104: Bringing the Money Home

At two-thirty in the morning, the long war room connecting the Ministry of Finance and the central bank looked more like a tired factory floor than the heart of the country's banking system.

Thick gray cigarette smoke hung low under the white ceiling lamps. Empty white porcelain tea mugs and cold paper lunch boxes crowded the edges of the long conference table, pushed aside to make room for stacks of wide green-lined paper spreadsheets. Along the far wall, three large glowing display boards flashed rows of green settlement numbers as bank transfers cleared from coast to coast.

At the head of the table sat Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua. His white shirt sleeves were rolled up to his forearms, his tie was gone, and dark rings sat under his eyes from three straight nights with less than four hours of sleep. A lit cigarette rested between the fingers of his left hand while his right hand held a plain red pencil over a stack of settlement summaries.

Around the table sat ten of the country's most senior banking and finance leaders, including Central Bank Deputy Governor Pan Guojun, Ministry of Finance Director He Wenzhong, State Treasury Chief Song Weiping, and China Construction Bank Chairman Li Zhendong.

Nobody spoke for ten full seconds as the final line on the far wall clicked from yellow to solid green.

State Treasury Chief Song Weiping took off his reading glasses, rubbed his tired eyes, and let out a long, shaky breath. "Vice Premier, the final court-ordered batch from Hong Kong and Singapore just cleared. The ledger is balanced."

Zhou Zhenhua looked up from his paper sheet and took a slow drag of his cigarette. "Give me the exact figure, Song Weiping."

"12.04 trillion yuan," Song Weiping said, his voice quiet with awe. "The first 12 trillion yuan in stolen state land leases, rigged coal and rare-earth mining rights, port shares, and hidden overseas bank accounts is officially back inside the national treasury."

A low, collective exhale rippled around the smoky table. Several of the bank chairmen leaned back in their chairs for the first time since sunset.

Zhou Zhenhua did not smile yet. He tapped a clean edge of ash into a glass ashtray and turned his sharp gaze directly toward Central Bank Deputy Governor Pan Guojun.

"Moving 12 trillion yuan in seventy-two hours is a massive weight," Zhou Zhenhua said evenly. "If you drop a twelve-ton stone into a quiet pond, the splash knocks over every small boat near the shore. Tell me straight, Pan Guojun. Did we rattle the street?"

Pan Guojun leaned forward, flipping open a thin blue binder. "Not a single crack, Vice Premier. Every transfer moved strictly through state clearing channels after midnight, backed one-for-one by central bank reserve buffers. Yesterday morning, every bank branch across all thirty-one provinces opened at nine sharp with normal cash drawers and normal lines."

"What about the smaller cities?" Zhou Zhenhua asked sharply, tapping his red pencil against the table. "I am not only asking about Beijing and Shanghai. What about county branches in Henan, Sichuan, and Lanshui? Did any retired factory worker wake up worried about their 50,000 yuan savings book at the corner bank?"

China Construction Bank Chairman Li Zhendong answered right away, his voice steady and firm. "I checked our county withdrawal reports at midnight, Vice Premier. Daily cash withdrawals across our rural and county branches were actually two percent lower than a normal Tuesday. Ordinary depositors did not feel a single tremor. Their savings accounts, payroll deposits, and pension cards were never touched and never delayed by even one minute."

Zhou Zhenhua nodded once, his tight shoulders easing just a fraction. "Good. What about the local builders and state factories whose corrupt directors were arrested this week? If a crooked chairman goes to prison on Monday, I do not want thirty thousand ordinary workers missing their Friday paychecks."

Ministry of Finance Director He Wenzhong pushed a fresh sheet of paper down the table. "We set up temporary state-run payroll accounts for forty-two affected mines, steel mills, and port firms before the arrests even began, Vice Premier. Yesterday afternoon, 4.6 billion yuan in workers' wages went out on schedule. The crane operators, coal miners, and truck drivers got paid on time while their former directors sat in detention cells."

"That is how you clean a house without burning the roof down," Zhou Zhenhua murmured.

Across the table, a younger deputy from the Agricultural Bank spoke up carefully. "Vice Premier, two foreign financial newspapers in London are already guessing that Beijing froze massive offshore accounts. Some foreign currency traders tried to short the yuan in Hong Kong this afternoon, hoping to spark a panic."

Pan Guojun let out a cold, short laugh and reached for his teacup. "They tried for forty minutes. We met them head-on using a fraction of the overseas gold and foreign currency we just pulled back from Zurich and Singapore. They lost 900 million yuan before lunch and ran back into their holes."

A few tired chuckles broke out around the smoky table, easing the heavy late-night tension.

Zhou Zhenhua stubbed his cigarette out carefully in the glass ashtray and stood up, resting both hands on the edge of the table as he looked down the row of ten weary faces.

"Do not celebrate too early," Zhou Zhenhua told them in a calm, serious voice. "We just brought home the first 12 trillion yuan. Every person in this room has seen the full audit sheet from the forty-department clean-up. We know how much more is still out there, hidden behind fake leases, overseas trusts, and shell companies."

He pointed his red pencil toward the glowing green numbers on the wall board.

"Every single fen of that 12 trillion yuan, and every trillion that follows it, did not fall from the sky," Zhou Zhenhua said softly, his eyes steady and bright despite his exhaustion. "It came from farmers selling grain, factory hands working night shifts, small shopkeepers paying their taxes honestly, and soldiers standing watch on cold coastlines. A handful of greedy men stole it for twenty years and thought nobody would ever dare take it back."

Song Weiping nodded slowly, his throat tight. "The next wave of 8 trillion yuan in seized land and mineral rights is already lined up for court transfer next week, Vice Premier. We will move it just as quietly."

"Quietly, cleanly, and to the last fen," Zhou Zhenhua said firmly. "Put the first batch straight into the national pension pool, rural hospital funds, and coastal defense upgrades where ordinary families can feel it. Not one yuan goes back into a leaky local slush fund."

"Understood, Vice Premier!" all ten banking leaders answered together, their voices rough from smoke and long hours, yet full of fresh energy.

Zhou Zhenhua picked up his cold porcelain mug, took a sip of bitter tea, and looked at the wall clock. Almost three in the morning. In three hours, the breakfast stalls along the capital's side streets would light their coal stoves, and millions of ordinary people would head to work without ever knowing how close the storm had passed above their heads.

"Take two hours of sleep on the cots next door," Zhou Zhenhua told them with a faint, tired smile. "At six, we start on the next 12 trillion."
