# Chapter 104: Bringing the Money Home

At two-thirty in the morning, the war room between the Ministry of Finance and the central bank looked like a tired factory floor.

Gray cigarette smoke hung low under the ceiling lamps. Empty tea mugs and cold lunch boxes crowded the table beside green-lined spreadsheets. Along the far wall, three boards flashed green numbers as bank transfers cleared from coast to coast.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua sat at the head with his sleeves rolled up and his tie gone, dark rings under his eyes from three straight nights with little sleep. A cigarette rested between his left fingers while his right hand held a red pencil over a stack of bank summaries.

Around him sat the country's top finance leaders: Central Bank Deputy Governor Pan Guojun, Ministry of Finance Director He Wenzhong, State Treasury Chief Song Weiping, and the four national bank presidents who had not left the building in thirty hours: Fang Zhengming of China Construction Bank, Han Guoliang of ICBC, Tang Wenhao of Bank of China, and Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank.

Nobody spoke for ten seconds as the final line on the wall turned solid green.

Song Weiping took off his glasses, rubbed his red eyes, and let out a shaky breath. "Vice Premier, the final court-ordered batch from Hong Kong and Singapore just cleared. The ledger is balanced."

Zhou Zhenhua took a slow drag of his cigarette. "Give me the exact figure, Song Weiping."

"120.4 billion yuan," Song Weiping said, his voice filled with awe. "The first 120 billion yuan in stolen land leases, rigged mining rights, port shares, and hidden overseas accounts is officially back inside the national treasury."

A collective exhale rippled around the smoky table.

Zhou Zhenhua did not smile yet. He tapped ash into a glass tray and fixed his gaze on Central Bank Deputy Governor Pan Guojun.

"Moving 120 billion yuan in seventy-two hours is a heavy weight," Zhou Zhenhua said evenly. "Drop a twelve-ton stone into a quiet pond and the splash knocks over every small boat. Tell me straight, Pan Guojun. Did we rattle the street?"

Pan Guojun flipped open a thin blue binder with a tired grin. "Not a single crack, Vice Premier. Every transfer cleared after midnight on top of the 300 billion yuan free legal stabilization fund parked across China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank. With a floor that thick underneath us, every branch in all thirty-one provinces opened at nine sharp with normal cash drawers and normal lines."

"What about smaller cities?" Zhou Zhenhua asked sharply, tapping his pencil. "I do not only mean Beijing and Shanghai. What about county branches in Henan, Sichuan, and Jingzhou City? Did any retired worker wake up worried about their 50,000 yuan savings book?"

China Construction Bank President Fang Zhengming answered immediately. "I checked our county sheets at midnight, Vice Premier. Daily cash withdrawals across rural and county branches were two percent lower than a normal Tuesday. Ordinary families felt zero tremor. Savings books, payrolls, and pension cards were never delayed by one minute."

ICBC President Han Guoliang stubbed out his cigarette and nodded. "Normally, freezing three hundred dirty trust accounts in three days would dry up bank credit before dawn. Instead, our share of that 300 billion yuan clean reserve filled every gap before a single branch manager even noticed."

"Good," Zhou Zhenhua murmured, his shoulders easing a fraction. "What about factories and mines whose crooked directors went into custody this week? If a corrupt chairman goes to prison on Monday, I do not want thirty thousand honest workers missing Friday pay."

Ministry of Finance Director He Wenzhong slid a fresh sheet down the table. "Huaxia Bank and Finance opened clean payroll accounts for forty-two seized mines, steel mills, and port firms before the arrests started. Yesterday afternoon, 460 million yuan in wages went out on time. Crane operators, miners, and drivers got paid while their former directors sat in holding cells."

Huaxia Bank President Liang Qingshan smiled faintly over his cold mug. "Not one shipping dock missed a tide. Every trade credit line tied to Deng Yongchang's shell firms moved cleanly onto our books overnight."

"That is how you clean a house without burning the roof down," Zhou Zhenhua said softly.

Bank of China President Tang Wenhao let out a short, cold chuckle and lifted a printed wire sheet. "Vice Premier, two financial papers in London guessed Beijing froze massive offshore accounts. Foreign currency traders tried to short the yuan in Hong Kong this afternoon, hoping to spark a panic."

Zhou Zhenhua raised an eyebrow. "And?"

"They lasted forty minutes," Tang Wenhao said dryly. "Pan Guojun and I met them head-on using a corner of our overseas desk backed by the 300 billion yuan legal reserve. They thought they were pushing a shaky wall. They hit solid iron, lost 900 million yuan before lunch, and ran back into their holes."

Tired laughter broke out around the table, easing the late-night tension.

Zhou Zhenhua crushed his cigarette out and stood, resting both hands on the wood as he looked down the row of weary faces.

"Do not celebrate too early," Zhou Zhenhua told them calmly. "That 300 billion yuan clean shield behind China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank gave us a miracle nobody in banking history has seen. That does not mean we get lazy. We just brought home the first 120 billion yuan. Every man here knows how much stolen wealth is still out there."

He pointed his red pencil toward the glowing green wall.

"Every fen of that 120 billion yuan, and every billion after it, came from farmers selling grain, factory hands working night shifts, small shopkeepers paying taxes honestly, and soldiers watching cold coastlines," Zhou Zhenhua said quietly. "A handful of greedy men stole it for twenty years and thought nobody dared take it back."

Song Weiping nodded, his throat tight. "The next wave of 80 billion yuan in seized land and mineral rights clears next week, Vice Premier. We will bring it home just as quietly."

"Quietly, cleanly, and to the last fen," Zhou Zhenhua ordered. "Put this first 120 billion yuan straight into the national pension pool, rural hospital funds, and coastal defense upgrades where ordinary families can feel it. Not one yuan goes back into a leaky slush fund."

"Understood, Vice Premier!" all seven finance leaders answered together, voices rough from smoke yet full of energy.

Zhou Zhenhua sipped his cold bitter tea and glanced at the clock. Almost three in the morning. In three hours, Beijing's breakfast stalls would light their coal stoves, and millions of ordinary people would head to work never knowing how close the storm had passed overhead.

"Take two hours on the cots next door," Zhou Zhenhua said with a faint smile. "At six, we start on the next 120 billion."
