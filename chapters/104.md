# Chapter 104: The Mother-in-Law's Video Call from Beijing

On Sunday evening at Emerald Riverside Villa, the living room lamps cast a warm amber glow over the plush cream sofa.

Chen Xuan sat leaning back comfortably against the cushions in a plain white cotton T-shirt and soft drawstring house pants. In his lap sat a porcelain bowl of fresh Jiangxi navel oranges. With practiced, unhurried fingers, he peeled the bright rind off a plump orange in one unbroken spiral, pulled off every thin white string of pith, and separated a sweet wedge.

Sitting right beside him with her legs tucked lazily under a light cashmere throw blanket, Qin Yuhan was reading a shipping report on her tablet. Without even looking up from her screen, she opened her mouth slightly.

Chen Xuan popped the sweet orange slice straight in.

"Sweet?" Chen Xuan asked, peeling off another string of pith.

"Very sweet," Qin Yuhan murmured contentedly, chewing the juicy slice as she turned a page on her tablet. "Where did you buy these?"

"The old fruit vendor outside the subway exit," Chen Xuan said, popping the next slice into his own mouth. "He gets a truck straight from Ganzhou every Saturday morning. Much better than the waxed ones at IFC Mall."

Just as Qin Yuhan opened her mouth for a second slice, her phone on the glass coffee table began chiming with an incoming video call.

Qin Yuhan glanced at the screen. The relaxed curve of her shoulders stiffened slightly. The caller ID read: Mother (Beijing).

"It is my mother," Qin Yuhan said, sitting up straight and hastily smoothing her casual knit cardigan. "Cousin Haoran must have run his mouth to his father after Friday afternoon in the lobby, and now the news has reached Beijing."

"Answer it," Chen Xuan smiled, setting the orange peel neatly on a tissue. "A son-in-law has to say hello sooner or later."

Qin Yuhan took a breath, tapped the green accept button, and propped the phone on the coffee table stand.

The screen lit up with the stern, impeccably made-up face of Lin Shuyin, sitting in a grand mahogany drawing room in Beijing with a jade brooch pinned to her silk collar. Beside her edge of the frame, Qin Yuhan's father, Qin Zhengming, could be seen holding a teacup with a worried expression.

"Yuhan!" Lin Shuyin's sharp voice rang through the speaker immediately. "Your Second Aunt just called me from Shanghai! She said Haoran saw you walking out of Haiyue Tower on Friday arm in arm with some man carrying a green thermos! And when your father checked the civil registry yesterday, it turned out you secretly registered a marriage two weeks ago! Who is he? Put him on the camera right now!"

Qin Yuhan pursed her lips. "Mom, lower your voice. I told Dad last month that I was getting married so you would stop arranging blind dates with those Beijing real estate heirs. He is sitting right here beside me."

She angled the phone slightly toward Chen Xuan.

Lin Shuyin leaned close to her screen in Beijing, her sharp eyes scanning Chen Xuan's plain white cotton T-shirt, his relaxed posture on the sofa, and the half-peeled orange in his hands.

"Hello, Mom," Chen Xuan said warmly, giving a polite nod. "And hello, Dad. I am Chen Xuan."

In Beijing, Qin Zhengming cleared his throat awkwardly and nodded back. "Hello, Little Chen."

Lin Shuyin, however, looked as if she had just swallowed a lemon whole. Her chest heaved beneath her jade brooch.

"Who told you to call me Mom so casually?!" Lin Shuyin snapped. "Young man, let me ask you straight. Haoran said you work as a low-level clerk at some tiny freight company in Shanghai. What is your family background, what position do you hold, and how much do you make a month?"

"Mom!" Qin Yuhan protested, frowning.

"It is fine, Yuhan," Chen Xuan chuckled easily, pulling off the last tiny white thread from an orange wedge. He looked calmly into the camera. "My parents passed away when I was young, and my second uncle runs a small hardware store in Yunxi County in Handong Province. Right now I work as a document clerk at Hengxin Logistics here in Shanghai. My base salary is 12,000 yuan a month, plus a two-hundred-yuan punctual attendance bonus."

Silence hung over the video link for two seconds before Lin Shuyin nearly exploded.

"Yuhan! Have you lost your mind?!" Lin Shuyin cried out, slapping her palm on her mahogany side table in Beijing. "Your grandfather is Elder Qin Zhengguo, a retired four-star general! You graduated at the top of your class from Fudan University and run a shipping group worth 50 billion yuan! And you married a 12,000-yuan logistics clerk who counts a two-hundred-yuan attendance bonus?!"

"Chen Xuan treats me very well, Mom," Qin Yuhan said firmly, slipping her hand over Chen Xuan's wrist on the sofa. "I am not divorcing him, and I do not care about family business alliances."

"You stubborn girl!" Lin Shuyin pressed a hand to her forehead, breathing hard. "Wait until next month's family banquet in Beijing! If your grandfather sees this, my blood pressure is going to hit two hundred!"

Chen Xuan cheerfully held a plump, freshly peeled orange slice up toward the phone camera.

"Mom, do not let your blood pressure get high over a monthly paycheck," Chen Xuan said in a mild, friendly voice. "12,000 yuan buys plenty of fresh vegetables and fruit in Shanghai, and our company pays full social insurance too. You should eat some sweet oranges in the evening instead of drinking strong tea. It helps you sleep better."

Lin Shuyin stared at the orange slice on her screen, so stunned by his completely unbothered sincerity that she forgot her next scolding line. With a sharp huff, she ended the call.

The phone screen went dark.

For a brief second, the living room was quiet. Then Qin Yuhan looked at the orange slice still held between Chen Xuan's fingers, burst out laughing, and dropped her forehead onto his shoulder.

"You offered my mother an orange slice on video," Qin Yuhan laughed, her shoulders shaking against his cotton shirt. "And you bragged about your full social insurance!"

"Full social insurance is a very solid workplace benefit," Chen Xuan grinned, popping the orange slice into her smiling mouth as she leaned against him.
