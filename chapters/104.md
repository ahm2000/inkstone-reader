# Chapter 104: The Mother-in-Law's Video Call from Beijing

After dinner, the living room lamps at Emerald Riverside Villa cast a warm amber glow across the plush cream sofa. Beyond the tall glass doors, the dark river lay still, making the quiet warmth inside feel even cozier.

Chen Xuan leaned back comfortably against the cushions in a plain white cotton T-shirt and soft drawstring house pants, a porcelain bowl of fresh Jiangxi navel oranges resting in his lap. With unhurried, practiced fingers, he peeled the bright rind off a plump orange in one unbroken spiral, plucked away every thin white thread of pith, and separated a juicy wedge.

Curled right beside him with her legs tucked under a light cashmere throw, Qin Yuhan was scrolling through a quarterly shipping report on her tablet. Without even taking her eyes off the screen, she opened her mouth slightly.

Chen Xuan popped the sweet orange wedge straight in.

"Sweet?" he asked, peeling another thin thread of pith off the next slice.

"Very sweet," Qin Yuhan murmured contentedly, chewing the juicy fruit. "I swear oranges taste twice as good when someone else takes all the white strings off."

Before Chen Xuan could feed her another slice, a lively chime rang out from her tablet. A video call banner flashed across the top of the screen: Mom.

Qin Yuhan froze for half a second, then let out a soft, amused sigh as she sat up straighter and smoothed her loose dark hair behind her ears. "Zhou Mingcheng must have run straight to my mother after the coffee shop," she whispered with a quick grin before tapping the green button.

The screen brightened to reveal the warm study of the Qin family courtyard home in Beijing. Lin Shuyin sat in a dark silk cardigan beside a porcelain vase of autumn chrysanthemums, her posture rigid and her brows knitted tight.

"Yuhan!" Lin Shuyin spoke the instant the video connected, her voice sharp with disbelief. "Zhou Mingcheng called me in a complete fit saying you registered your marriage with a stranger you met over coffee! Tell me you were only trying to scare him off!"

"I was not trying to scare him off, Mom," Qin Yuhan replied calmly, tilting the tablet slightly so Chen Xuan came into view beside her. "We registered our marriage at the Pudong Civil Affairs Bureau this week. Meet my husband, Chen Xuan."

Chen Xuan leaned politely into the camera frame with an easy, unhurried smile. "Good evening, Auntie. Forgive the sudden news. The weather in Beijing must be turning chilly, so I hope you and Uncle are keeping warm."

Lin Shuyin stared at the screen in stunned silence for three full seconds, taking in Chen Xuan's plain white T-shirt, his calm smile, and the bowl of peeled oranges resting in his lap.

"You... you really married him?" Lin Shuyin pressed a manicured hand to her forehead, her eyes darting rapidly from Chen Xuan's relaxed face to her daughter's radiant complexion. "Yuhan, you run an entire shipping group! You did not even introduce him to your father and me first! Where does he work? What family is he from?"

"I work in operations at Hengxin Logistics here in Lujiazui, Auntie," Chen Xuan answered honestly, neither shrinking back nor putting on airs. "My parents live in Yunxi County in Handong Province. Nothing grand, just a straightforward life with good hours, no overtime, and plenty of time to cook hot meals for Yuhan."

Lin Shuyin's lips parted, ready to launch into a lecture about social standing and corporate ambition, yet she paused halfway as she noticed something unusual on the screen.

For the past three years, every time she video-called her daughter on a Friday evening, Qin Yuhan had been sitting alone behind a mountain of contracts in a cold office, pale from skipping meals and drinking black coffee. Tonight, her daughter's cheeks had a healthy rosy glow, her shoulders were completely relaxed, and she was leaning naturally against this young man's arm as if she had known him for years.

Before Lin Shuyin could find her next sharp question, a broad shoulder appeared behind her chair. Qin Zhengming stepped into the camera frame in a dark knit sweater, adjusting his reading glasses with steady, measured curiosity.

"So this is the young man who finally got our stubborn daughter out of her office before dark," Qin Zhengming said, his voice deep and surprisingly even. "Nice to meet you, Chen Xuan."

"Nice to meet you, Uncle," Chen Xuan replied with genuine respect.

"Zhengming, stop encouraging them!" Lin Shuyin protested, glancing up at her husband. "She got married without even holding a family dinner! What will your father say when he hears?"

Qin Zhengming chuckled quietly. "My father already heard an hour ago, Shuyin. When I told him Yuhan married a calm fellow who cooks dinner every night and refused to let Zhou Mingcheng push him around, the old general laughed so hard he nearly spilled his tea. He told me to stop meddling and said he wants to meet the boy in person and test him over a chessboard whenever Yuhan brings him up to Beijing."

Lin Shuyin let out a helpless huff, though the sharp edge in her eyes softened a fraction. She turned back to the tablet and lifted her chin firmly.

"We are not settling a marriage over a tablet screen," Lin Shuyin declared. "Your father and I are flying down to Shanghai next weekend. Book a quiet private room by the Bund for Sunday dinner. I want to sit across the table from Chen Xuan and judge him with my own two eyes."

"We will be waiting at Pine Courtyard, Auntie," Chen Xuan smiled warmly. "Have a safe flight down."

"Yes, Mom, we will book it tomorrow," Qin Yuhan agreed softly, her eyes twinkling with relief.

After a few more brisk reminders from Lin Shuyin to dress warmly, the call ended. The living room fell peacefully quiet again. Qin Yuhan set her tablet on the coffee table, curled back under the cashmere blanket, and rested her head comfortably against Chen Xuan's shoulder.

"Sorry about my mom," she murmured softly. "She still worries too much."

"She loves you," Chen Xuan said, lifting another peeled orange slice to her lips. "Now open up. One more wedge before the kettle boils for tea."

Qin Yuhan smiled, took the sweet slice, and closed her eyes in complete peace.
