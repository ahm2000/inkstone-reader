# Chapter 11: The Flower-Delivery Van on Riverside Road

At half past ten on Friday night, a faded white cargo van sat parked along Riverside Road. The words Qinghe Fresh Florals were painted on its side door. Inside the back of the van, soft blue light from computer screens shone across three men in dark jackets and gray half-masks.

Number 7 sat at the middle desk. His eyes stayed on a small green dot over Fuxing New Village. Across from him, Number 14 tapped his earpiece off and let out a quiet breath.

"The street watch just checked in," Number 14 said. "Chen Xuan locked up Mingde Hardware, sent Yan Tiehu away at the mouth of the lane, and walked back to his parents' apartment twelve minutes ago. Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang are inside with him. Nobody from the Chen family went near Old Street Station."

Number 7's shoulders relaxed a little. "Did Yan Tiehu try to follow him?"

"No," Number 14 said, a dry note in his voice. "Yan Tiehu saw our men inside Gong's Noodles and left holding his right wrist. Number 9 said he did not even look back twice."

"Good," Number 7 said. "Keep Number 9's team two blocks back from Fuxing New Village. No lights, and nobody standing near the stairs. Chen Xuan wants his family to sleep tonight without feeling watched."

Number 14 typed the order and glanced down the bench. "How close are we on what happened this afternoon?"

At the far desk, Number 34 pulled up three matching screens on the widest monitor. He had not left his seat since the private plane landed at the small airfield outside Yunxi County.

"I have the full timeline of the two o'clock incident on Old Street," Number 34 said. He leaned back and rubbed the bridge of his nose above his mask. "It is sloppy work. This is what local bullies put together when nobody has asked them a hard question in ten years."

Number 7 leaned forward and studied the white timestamps on the screen. "Walk me through it."

"At one fifty-four," Number 34 said, enlarging a street camera photo from the west end of Old Street, "a forty-one-year-old drifter named Ma Er walked past Mingde Hardware. He has no steady job and two old tickets for faking small insurance claims in Lanshui six years ago. Four minutes before he walked past the shop, a cheap prepaid phone in his pocket made two short calls to one of Yan Tiehu's drivers."

Number 14 narrowed his eyes at the screen. "A paid actor who fakes falls for money."

"A clumsy one," Number 34 replied. He moved the video forward by seven minutes. "At two zero one, Chen Mingde walked out of Mingde Hardware carrying a wooden crate of heavy metal pipe joints. Ma Er planted his heel on the wet curb, threw himself backward onto the stone pavement, and screamed that Chen Mingde shoved him over a shop buyout argument. Chen Mingde never even raised a hand."

"Did anyone call an ambulance?" Number 14 asked, frowning.

"Nobody," Number 34 said flatly. "There were zero emergency calls from Old Street between two and two thirty. Instead, at two zero seven, a white compact car owned by Yan Tiehu's nephew pulled up. They put Ma Er in the back seat and drove him straight to Yunxi People's Hospital."

Number 7's jaw tightened. "And the hospital played along."

"Even worse," Number 34 said. He brought up a hospital patient sheet next to a computer record. "At two thirty-eight, Deputy Director Fang Zhihua skipped the normal check-in line and signed a medical report for Ma Er. He wrote down a serious head injury and a bad neck sprain. That turned a fake fall into a violent attack, which let the police hold Chen Mingde without letting him out."

Number 14 leaned closer. "He wrote all of that in half an hour?"

"Faster than that," Number 34 said, zooming in on the hidden file details at the bottom of the page. "The form with Ma Er's name, ID number, and exact injury words was typed on Deputy Director Fang Zhihua's office computer at eleven fifteen this morning. That was almost three hours before Ma Er even stepped onto Old Street."

Number 14 let out a short, cold breath. "Typed up before lunch. They did not even wait to see which side of his head he pretended to hit."

"Now look at the third screen," Number 34 said quietly, pointing to an arrest paper highlighted in bright yellow. "Old Street Station Director Cheng Biao signed the lockup order for Chen Mingde at two twenty-six. Put the two clocks side by side. Cheng Biao stamped a paper blaming a hospital head injury twelve full minutes before Yunxi People's Hospital even saved Fang Zhihua's signature in its computer."

Silence fell inside the van. Number 7 stared at the yellow clock numbers without blinking.

"They were never trying to build a real police case," Number 7 said quietly. "They were trying to trap the family. Lock Chen Mingde up overnight, scare Chen Jianguo and Wu Lanying half to death, and wait for them to walk into Old Street Station tomorrow ready to sign away Mingde Hardware."

Number 14 rested his hand on the desk. "We already have the copied computer records and file times. Send them to the provincial inspectors tonight, and Cheng Biao and Fang Zhihua will be in handcuffs before noon."

"No," Number 7 cut in, shaking his head once.

Number 14 paused. "You think the provincial office will leak it?"

"I think files copied secretly from a computer are good for knowing the truth, not for standing up in court," Number 7 said evenly. "If we move too fast, Cheng Biao erases the local records, Fang Zhihua blames a computer clock error, and Ma Er disappears before sunrise. Worse, whoever stands behind Hongrui Real Estate smells trouble and hides before we see the whole game."

Number 34 looked up from his keyboard. "You think this goes higher than Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao."

"A street crew does not line up a police station director and a hospital deputy director before lunch unless someone higher is paying for it," Number 7 said. "Chen Xuan's order is simple. Prove Chen Mingde is innocent with real, solid original proof, and do not wake up the county until we pick the right hour."

Number 14 nodded slowly, settling back into his seat. "Then we take the real original records tonight while they still feel safe."

Number 7 touched two fingers to his earpiece to open the private radio channel.

"Number 19," Number 7 said. "You take Old Street. The bakery across the street from Mingde Hardware has a sharp outside camera under its second-floor roof. Pull the real storage hard drive cleanly, put an exact copy drive back in its place so the baker notices nothing, and save the proof record."

Two quiet clicks answered over the line.

"Number 27," Number 7 went on. "You take Yunxi People's Hospital. Pull the original hospital X-ray file and the server room time record for Ma Er's head scan. Prove the scan shows no injury at all. Leave zero mess and zero trace."

Another quiet double-click came back.

"Number 42," Number 7 finished. "Ma Er did not stay in a hospital bed. Phone tower signals put his phone near the old grain depot in the south district, probably in a back-alley mahjong parlor. Find him, check the payoff cash and the phone call record from Yan Tiehu, get proof of both, and follow him quietly until morning."

The third double-click snapped back cleanly.

Number 7 lowered his hand and turned to Number 34. "Keep watching the hospital and police station computers quietly. If Cheng Biao or Deng Yongchang makes even one unusual phone call, I want to know right away."

"Already listening," Number 34 said, his fingers moving back to the keys.

Outside the quiet white van, three dark figures slipped out into the wet streets of Yunxi County.
