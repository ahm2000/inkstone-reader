# Chapter 11: The Hostage Call

That evening, in Room 601 of Building 2, Fuxing New Village, Wu Lanying's phone rang on the folding table beside a basket of steaming bread, and the whole family stopped chewing.

The screen said Boss Dong.

"Don't answer that crook," Chen Jianguo said.

"Put it on speaker, Second Aunt," said Chen Xuan.

She tapped it with a shaking finger, and a smooth voice filled the room.

"Sister Wu! Good evening. Are you eating? Good, good. I'm only calling to help."

Ice clinked in a glass, and behind the voice came a thick laugh everybody in the room knew.

"Your husband is in a hard spot," said Dong Haisheng gently. "Ten days, maybe fifteen. Your daughter in Wuhan will carry that record to every job interview. But I have friends. Sign tonight, four hundred fifty-six thousand yuan, and Old Chen has breakfast at your table. Until then he's our guest. Guests get looked after, you understand."

Second Aunt's face went white.

This was a ransom call, and the man only called it a favor.

"Also, Sister Wu." The voice dropped, almost tender. "Your brother-in-law lives on the sixth floor, Building 2, I believe? Such steep stairs. And the stairwell light is so dim at night. Accidents happen to old people. Even Old Yu nearly met one this afternoon, I hear. I'd hate to hear a cane fall on those stairs, and nobody come."

Liu Meifang's ladle hit the floor.

Chen Hao stood up so fast that his chair fell.

Chen Xuan set his spoon down.

"Boss Dong."

A short silence.

"Who is this?"

"Chen Xuan. The nephew."

"Ah! The clerk. Young man, you are a sensible person. Tell your aunt to sign."

Chen Xuan looked at the basket of bread, and the steam rising from it.

"You called my mother's house," he said, flat and quiet. "You have until the bread is cold."

The laugh on the line stopped, and the ice stopped clinking.

Chen Xuan pressed the red button.

"Xuan," Chen Yuan whispered. She was not scolding. Her hand was shaking on the table. "He talked about Mom and Dad's stairs."

"I heard."

"What are you going to do?"

"Eat the rest of my soup," said Chen Xuan, and then he saw his sister's face and added, "and make a call."

Liu Meifang picked up her ladle.

"Let him come up the stairs," she said. "I've been waiting for a reason to hit somebody."

His phone buzzed on the table.

The name on it was the one he had messaged twice since last night.

Chen Xuan stepped out onto the balcony and shut the glass door.

"Boss!"

It was Chief An, and for the first time in his life he was nearly shouting.

"I heard everything. A threat on your mother's stairs, a hostage call. And Hu's crew just left its van with an iron pipe, walking toward the tailor's shop. Two hundred and fourteen of us are in position, Boss. Give me the word."

"Hmm," said Chen Xuan. "Chief An. Easy. And thanks."

"Yes, Boss."

"Hu's crew comes off that street tonight, all of them. The old men stay safe. My mother's stairs stay lit. Do it quietly."

"Quietly?"

"Mm."

When he came back in, Chen Hao was at the peephole.

"Brother, the stairwell just got so bright I need sunglasses. And there's a man in a property-office vest on a ladder with a flask of tea. He says nobody falls in his building."

"Oh, nice," said Chen Xuan, and picked up his spoon.

"That is the nicest repairman in the county," Liu Meifang announced. "He said Auntie."

Chen Yuan looked at her brother for a long time, and he drank his soup.

* * *

Two special-police vans stood at the edge of the car park behind People's Hospital, with their engines idling and eighteen men in black gear leaning on the doors.

They were the Qingyan City Special Police Brigade, home from a drill, tired and hungry.

Their leader, Team Leader Bao Zhen, was forty-one, with a jaw like a brick, and he took orders from nobody in a gray jacket.

The gray jacket was now walking toward him from the dark.

"Team Leader Bao?"

"Who are you?"

"A traveler. Number Nine."

He said it as if introducing himself at a bus stop.

Then the plain man reached into his pocket, took out something no bigger than a matchbox and placed it in Bao Zhen's palm.

It was a block of black metal, with a small carved pine branch along its edge and a plain 9 stamped on its face.

An ordinary person would see an ugly paperweight.

But Bao Zhen had heard of it twice in twenty years. Soldiers tell the story at two in the morning to scare recruits. Any elite team that sees the Black Pine Badge must obey its holder at once. No questions, even against its own commander.

Bao Zhen went completely still.

The color left his face, and his back straightened as if a rod had been pushed through it.

Then his hand went to his brow in a salute, without being told.

"Yes," he said.

Behind him, eighteen men stopped chewing.

His deputy, Fu Jian, stared at his leader's stiff arm, and he whispered, "Leader? Who is that?"

"Do whatever he says," Bao Zhen whispered back, still in the salute, "and ask nothing."

"Leader, he is wearing canvas shoes!"

"Ask nothing."

"Team Leader," said Number Nine, in a voice so mild it sounded like an apology. "A sweep. Anti-gang, regular and random. Hongrui's security team on Old Street, all eight men and their black van. Bring them in clean. No broken bones unless they start it. And no phone calls tonight. Thanks, Team Leader."

"Yes."

Nine turned and walked off into the dark, and Bao Zhen let his arm fall and found his palm wet.

On top of the hospital behind them, a rooftop floodlight flicked on, and a helicopter that had sat under a canvas cover all week turned its blades once, slowly.

"Gear up," he said. "We're a random sweep."

At the same hour, on Old Street, Big Tooth had his fist in Cobbler Sun's collar and his other hand drawn back.

Brother Hu was laughing.

Then the whole lane went white with headlights from both ends at once.

Black vans slid across the cobbles, and a dozen men in black helmets poured out.

"City Special Police! Hands on the wall!"

Thin Monkey dropped his pipe so quickly that it rang on the stones.

Big Tooth let go of Cobbler Sun, who sat down hard on his own doorstep with his mouth open.

At the arch, Team Leader Ruan Bo shut the lane behind them.

"What the," Brother Hu started.

A special policeman put him against the tailor's wall and kicked his feet apart.

"Do you know who I am?" Brother Hu shouted, with his cheek on the bricks. "I am Hongrui! My uncle is Director Cheng Biao of this station! Call him!"

"No phone calls," said Bao Zhen, and walked past him without looking.

Old Yu stood in his doorway with Ning's face pressed into his side, and he could not close his mouth.

"Is this a raid?" he whispered.

"A random check, Uncle," said a special policeman gently.

He glanced at the broken sign and added, "A very random one."

* * *

Back on the balcony, Chen Xuan's phone buzzed again.

"Boss." Chief An sounded cheerful and slightly afraid. "Six of Hu's men are in the Special Police vans, with two iron pipes bagged and nobody hurt. Two slipped away on foot: Hu's black-van driver, and a bookkeeper they call Abacus."

Chen Xuan listened.

Then he put his free hand over his face and stood very still.

"Hmm?" he said, through his fingers. "I meant a patrol car."

"You said tonight, Boss. Thin Monkey is eating a hot dumpling in a police van, and he has asked whether the cobbler's shoe will be arrested too."

"Mm. Good work, Chief."

"The Special Police said," Chief An added, and coughed, "that Brother Hu cried for his uncle at the station."

Chen Xuan lowered his hand.

His eyes were quiet, and his voice was just as soft as before.

"Chief An," he said. "Cheng Biao goes to the law."

"Yes, Boss."

"He slapped a man for a cup of water and sent a van after a tailor. Take all of him. Every file. And tonight Dong's phone rings with nobody on the line. Let him listen to the silence."

Far away in the quiet courtyard, the old man's breath stopped, and then he said, very slowly, "Yes."

In the living room, Chen Hao pressed his nose to the glass door.

"Brother is on the balcony with his hand on his face," he whispered. "Is he praying?"

"No," said Chen Yuan, watching their brother's back. "I think he is deciding something."
