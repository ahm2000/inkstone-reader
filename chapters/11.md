# Chapter 11: The Hostage Call

By two o'clock on Saturday afternoon, back at Room 601, the whole apartment was humming with nerves.

"Mom, if you stir that soup pot any harder, it's going to file a complaint," Chen Yuan sighed from the kitchen doorway.

"Don't talk to me about complaints!" Liu Meifang waved her big soup ladle like a general's stick. "Your uncle's been sitting in a cold cell for twenty-four hours, and I can't even keep my hands steady!"

The apartment buzzed with restless family nerves. Behind his bedroom door, Chen Hao groaned loudly every few minutes while losing a battle with a practice math exam. In the living room, Second Aunt sat on the edge of the sofa clutching her phone with both hands, never taking her eyes off the dark screen.

Chen Xuan sat at the dining table, quietly comparing the photo of his uncle's detention notice against Old Yu's signed statement, jotting down the timeline in neat rows.

Suddenly, Second Aunt's phone rang!

She jumped as if the handset had burned her fingers. Instantly, the entire apartment froze!

"It's him," she whispered, her face turning pale. "It's Boss Dong."

"Don't answer that crook!" Chen Jianguo shouted.

"Put it on speaker, Second Aunt," Chen Xuan said calmly, setting his pen down. "Let's hear what he wants."

With trembling fingers, she tapped the green button. A smooth, oily voice filled the quiet living room.

"Sister Wu? This is Boss Dong. Don't be frightened, I'm only calling to help. Your husband is in a tough spot, you know. Fifteen days of detention isn't a small thing. It leaves a permanent police record, and your daughter's university job hunt will suffer for it. Luckily, I have friends. If you sign the buyout contract today, I'll make one call and have Old Chen home at your dinner table tonight! Four hundred and fifty-six thousand yuan, cash in a week. Think about your family, Sister Wu!"

Second Aunt's fingers shook so violently she nearly dropped the phone!

He made it sound like a favor. It was her husband's freedom traded for half the value of their shop, and everybody in the room heard the extortion for exactly what it was.

"Four hundred and fifty-six thousand is still money," Second Aunt whimpered, tears spilling over her lashes. "Xuan, maybe I should sign the paper so your uncle can come home tonight."

"Second Aunt." Chen Xuan stood up, walked over smoothly, and gently pressed the red hang-up button.

The living room went dead silent.

"That shop is worth nearly one million yuan," Chen Xuan said steadily, looking straight into her eyes. "The second you sign, they'll claim Second Uncle agreed freely. Once your name is on that contract, nobody can undo it, not a lawyer, not a judge, nobody. Please trust me and wait one more day. Just one more day."

"Xuan, I want to trust you, I really do," Second Aunt sobbed softly. "But how can you promise he will be home in one day? You are only an office clerk."

"An ordinary logistics clerk, I know." Chen Xuan offered her a warm, gentle smile. "A supervisor, actually. Checking messy forms is literally my job. Their paper has an eighteen-hour legal gap, their injury note came from a shady private clinic, and we already have Old Yu's signed statement. They're the ones who should be nervous, not us."

Second Aunt stared up at him, her breathing slowly steadying.

For a long beat, nobody spoke. Then Chen Jianguo cleared his throat loudly and lifted one finger into the air. Out of lifelong family habit, everyone turned to hear his pearl of wisdom.

"As the old proverb says: the fisherman who holds the net isn't afraid of a thrashing carp!"

"Dad," Chen Yuan groaned, rubbing her temples, "that is not an old proverb."

"It's a brand-new one," Chen Jianguo declared proudly. "I just invented it!"

Second Aunt let out a wet, startled laugh that tumbled straight into a sob, yet it was a real laugh all the same. Liu Meifang hurried over and wrapped her arms tightly around her sister-in-law's shoulders.

A few minutes later, in a black Audi parked beside the river, Dong Haisheng stared down at his dead phone screen, his smooth face darkening like thunder.

The woman had hung up on him! Even worse, he'd clearly heard the nephew's calm voice right before the line clicked dead. Eleven signatures were already in his pocket, and now a nobody hardware seller and his stubborn office-clerk nephew were refusing to budge!

Before he could curse, his phone buzzed again. One name flashed across the screen: Chairman Huang.

Dong Haisheng's stomach sank! He swallowed hard and answered at once, his back straightening even inside his own car.

"Chairman Huang! Good afternoon!"

"Boss Dong." The voice on the other end sounded mild, almost drowsy, yet every word carried ice. "I dislike asking the same question twice. Is the corner lot cleared?"

"Monday, Chairman! Monday morning, I swear it!" Dong Haisheng's palm turned slick with sweat against the leather steering wheel. "The shopkeeper is locked up, and his wife is already breaking down. My friend at the police bureau promised he will sign by Monday!"

"I have zero interest in your small-town friends," Chairman Huang interrupted softly. "I know your bank is watching your loan, and I know what happens to a developer who defaults. Monday, Boss Dong. Not Tuesday."

Click. The line went dead.

Dong Haisheng sat frozen behind the wheel, staring across the gray river toward the dark rooftops of Old Street. For the first time in twenty years of wheeling and dealing, cold dread crept into his chest: he had borrowed far more than he could ever survive losing!

By eight that evening, in his own quiet study, Tao Shu did not have a cold.

Sitting comfortably in his quiet study in soft house slippers with a steaming mug of green tea beside him, the County Magistrate looked completely healthy. Behind his thin-rimmed glasses, his eyes were sharp and wide awake.

For eight long years as Yunxi's number-two official, Tao Shu had waited patiently for Party Secretary Fan Weiguo to slip up.

Tonight, he could smell the mistake in the air the way an old farmer smells coming rain.

Yet he hadn't written that secret complaint letter to the provincial capital.

He'd turned the puzzle over in his head for three straight days. Someone had sent an anonymous tip straight to the Governor's office, and people up in the province were already quietly asking questions. Would Tao Shu have loved to see Fan Weiguo stumble? Of course! Yet he knew better than to write anonymous letters himself. In officialdom, a poison arrow usually cuts the archer's own fingers first.

So who had sent it? One of the frightened shop owners? Unlikely. A local reporter? No county reporter had the guts. Somebody up in Qingyan City who knew far too much about Yunxi's relocation fund?

His wife had asked him once, over dinner, half-joking, why he never just took what was his. "Patience is for people who can't afford mistakes," he'd told her. "I can't afford them either. I just hide it better."

Tao Shu slid open his desk drawer and lifted out a thin cardboard folder. Written neatly on the cover in his own fountain-pen handwriting sat a single surname: Fan.

He didn't open it. He simply laid his palm flat across the cover, like a patient hunter keeping a hound quiet in the brush.

Not yet. A good hunter doesn't chase his prey. He stays still and waits for it to walk into the open.
