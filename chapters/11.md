# Chapter 11: The Flower-Delivery Van on Riverside Road

Rain misted down over Riverside Road in fine, drifting curtains, turning the wet asphalt into a dark mirror beneath the sodium streetlamps of Yunxi County. It was half past ten on Friday night. Across the low stone balustrade, the Yunxi River ran swollen and black, carrying broken willow twigs toward the old concrete bridge at the south end of town.

Parked half a block from a shuttered stone-carving shop sat a faded white cargo van. Sun-bleached red lettering along its door advertised Qinghe Fresh Florals beside a peeling peony decal, and a faint scent of damp chrysanthemum stems lingered around two plastic buckets strapped to the rear bumper for show.

Inside the windowless cargo bay, there were no flowers at all.

Matte-gray acoustic baffling lined the walls, swallowing the rain and the hum of a lithium battery pack beneath the floor. Along the left bulkhead, six anti-glare monitors glowed in a crescent, casting pale blue light across three seated figures in dark jackets and featureless charcoal half-masks covering everything below the bridge of the nose.

None knew the others' real names or faces. Inside the Black Pine Guard, a man held a number, a discipline, and an assignment.

Number Seven sat closest to the central console, hands folded loosely on his knees as he watched a single green status dot settle over Fuxing New Village on the county map.

"Perimeter detail checked in," Number Fourteen said quietly from the middle seat. "The Boss locked Mingde Hardware, turned Yan Tiehu away at the mouth of the lane, and returned to his parents' apartment twelve minutes ago. Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang are inside with him. Nobody from the Chen family went near Old Street Station."

"Good," Number Seven replied. "Keep Number Nine's outer watch two blocks back from Fuxing New Village. No lights, no visible silhouettes, no disturbance to the neighbors. The Boss wants his family to sleep tonight."

At the far end of the bench, Number Thirty-four leaned over a matte-black keyboard. His fingers moved with clipped, practiced rhythm, pulling three synchronized windows onto the widest central display. Signals and forensics were his domain, and he had been working since the private jet touched down at the regional airfield outside Yunxi.

"The reconstruction of the two o'clock incident on Old Street is complete," Number Thirty-four said, locking the timeline into place. "It is sloppy work. The kind of work local men do when nobody has challenged them in ten years."

Number Seven leaned closer, scanning the clean white timestamps arranged along a horizontal axis. "Walk me through it."

"At one fifty-four in the afternoon," Number Thirty-four began, highlighting a grainy municipal traffic frame from the west intersection of Old Street, "a forty-one-year-old drifter named Ma Er walked past Mingde Hardware from the canal. He has no fixed employment in Yunxi County and two prior citations for petty insurance fraud in Lanshui six years ago. Four minutes before he reached the hardware shop, a prepaid burner phone in his pocket exchanged two short calls with a number linked to Yan Tiehu's crew."

Number Thirty-four advanced the timeline by seven minutes.

"At two zero one, Chen Mingde stepped out of Mingde Hardware carrying a wooden crate of galvanized pipe elbows. Ma Er stepped onto the wet strip beside the curb, threw his own heel out from under him, and dropped onto the flagstones without Chen Mingde ever extending a hand. He clutched the back of his head and shouted that Chen Mingde had shoved him over a demolition dispute."

"Did anyone call an ambulance?" Number Fourteen asked.

"No," Number Thirty-four said. "County dispatch received zero calls from Old Street between two o'clock and two thirty. Instead, at two zero seven, a white hatchback registered to a nephew of Yan Tiehu pulled up to the curb. Two men lifted Ma Er into the back seat and drove him straight to Yunxi People's Hospital."

Number Thirty-four shifted the cursor to the second panel, where a scanned clinical intake sheet sat beside a raw server audit log pulled from the hospital's intranet.

"At two thirty-eight, Deputy Director Fang Zhihua bypassed emergency triage and signed an official diagnosis of moderate cerebral concussion and acute cervical sprain under Ma Er's name. That report classified the incident as intentional injury, giving the station grounds to hold Chen Mingde without bail."

Number Thirty-four zoomed in on a block of hexadecimal metadata at the bottom of the clinical form.

"The document template carrying Ma Er's name, national identity number, and exact symptom wording was created on Deputy Director Fang Zhihua's workstation at eleven fifteen this morning. Nearly three hours before Ma Er ever set foot on Old Street."

Number Fourteen let out a low, dry breath. "Pre-typed before lunch."

"It gets worse," Number Thirty-four said, highlighting the third panel in sharp amber. "Old Street Station Director Cheng Biao signed the administrative detention warrant for Chen Mingde at two twenty-six in the afternoon. Look at the two clocks side by side. Cheng Biao signed and stamped a detention order citing a hospital concussion diagnosis twelve full minutes before Yunxi People's Hospital even logged Deputy Director Fang Zhihua's signature into the electronic medical system."

Number Seven studied the amber timestamp in silence. Outside, a lone cargo truck rumbled past along Riverside Road, its tires hissing through standing water before fading back into the steady rain.

"They were not building a case," Number Seven said calmly. "They were building a lever. Hold Chen Mingde overnight, frighten Chen Jianguo and Wu Lanying, and wait for the family to walk into Old Street Station tomorrow morning ready to sign away the deed to Mingde Hardware."

"We already have the mirrored logs," Number Fourteen observed. "If we forward them to the provincial discipline office tonight, Cheng Biao and Deputy Director Fang Zhihua will be in cuffs before noon."

"No," Number Seven said evenly. "Mirrored packets from an unauthorized entry are intelligence, not courtroom evidence. Move early and Cheng Biao destroys the local backups, Fang Zhihua blames a sync error, and Ma Er vanishes. Worse, whoever sits behind Hongrui Real Estate retreats before we see the whole board."

Number Seven looked at the operational roster on the right display. "The Boss's instruction is clear. Secure Chen Mingde's innocence with hard, court-admissible originals without tipping off the county authorities until we choose the hour."

Number Seven tapped his earpiece once, opening the encrypted field channel.

"Number Nineteen," Number Seven said. "You are on Old Street. The bakery diagonally across from Mingde Hardware has an exterior high-definition camera under its second-floor awning. Secure the original storage drive cleanly, leave an identical clone in the housing so the baker notices nothing, and log the hash."

A single crisp double-click answered through the earpiece.

"Number Twenty-seven," Number Seven continued. "You are assigned to Yunxi People's Hospital. Pull the raw DICOM imaging archive and the physical server room timestamp log for Ma Er's cranial scan. Prove the scan itself is blank. Leave zero footprint."

Another quiet double-click returned over the line.

"Number Forty-two," Number Seven finished. "Ma Er did not stay in a hospital bed tonight. Cell-tower triangulation places his phone near the old grain depot in the south district, likely a back-alley mahjong parlor. Find him, verify the payoff cash and the call log from Yan Tiehu, secure proof of both, and keep a silent tail on him until morning."

The third confirmation clicked back cleanly.

Number Seven lowered his hand and looked back at the glowing county map. Rain streaked down the painted peony on the van's outer shell, while three silent shadows moved out into the wet streets of Yunxi County.
