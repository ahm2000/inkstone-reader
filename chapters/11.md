# Chapter 11: The Old Elders' Terror

Deep beneath the earth in the National Supreme Military Command Center, the massive subterranean hall hummed with raw tension.

Every theater command, air defense network, naval fleet, and strategic missile brigade across the country had silently shifted to full combat readiness. Green status boards had turned glaring amber and red.

At the head of the glass command deck stood General Qin Zhengguo, the highest currently serving four-star military commander in the nation. He had commanded millions of troops through border standoffs without ever raising his pulse. Tonight, cold sweat beaded along his collar, because ten fully masked, completely mute figures stood in a silent ring around his own command deck.

"Who cleared them onto this deck?!" General Qin Zhengguo barked at his chief of staff, his jaw clenched tight. "This is Supreme Military Command! Why is nobody answering me?!"

"General, they bypassed every biometric checkpoint on the elevator shaft!" his chief of staff whispered back, pale to the lips and barely daring to glance at the ten dark masks behind them. "Our own deck guards couldn't even draw their sidearms before they were already standing here. They haven't spoken a single word since they walked in. Meanwhile, every theater is armed and waiting for orders, fighter wings are holding the hundred-kilometer shell over Lanshui, and foreign satellites are pinging the blackout nonstop!"

General Qin Zhengguo forced himself to breathe and marched straight to the isolated red telephone bank. If nobody on his deck knew what was happening, he would get answers from the capital himself.

His first call went straight to the Minister of State Security.

"Minister!" General Qin Zhengguo barked the instant the line connected. "Ten masked operatives just walked onto my command deck, and an entire county in Lanshui has gone dark under a hundred-kilometer air shell! Is this your ministry's operation?! Why wasn't Supreme Military Command briefed?!"

"Don't shout at me, Qin Zhengguo!" the Minister snapped back, his own voice ragged with panic and fury. "Do you think I'm enjoying this?! Eight of those mute ghosts are standing in my living room right now! My own deputy is locked in his office with six more! We have zero clearance, zero files, and zero idea who they are! I was about to call YOU and ask if the military was staging a coup!"

"A coup?!" General Qin Zhengguo roared. "My troops are sitting on live warheads waiting to know if we're under attack!"

He slammed the receiver down and immediately punched the direct line to the Senior Cabinet Vice Premier.

"Vice Premier, I need authorization and clarity right now!" General Qin Zhengguo demanded. "The Minister of State Security is blind. The Central Guard Bureau is blind. Who ordered an Absolute Order in Yunxi County?!"

"Keep your voice down, General!" the Vice Premier barked sharply, though his breathing sounded fast and thin over the encrypted line. "Nobody in this cabinet signed anything! Five of them are watching me speak to you right now! Hold your theater commands in place, do not fire a single shot, and stop calling me until you find out what is going on!"

Click. The line went dead.

General Qin Zhengguo stared at the silent receiver, a cold chill finally cutting straight through his forty years of military iron. Every serving leader in the capital was blind, trapped under mute five-to-ten-man watches, barking at each other in mounting panic.

Only one kind of person in the entire country might know what could silence the capital like this.

With a heavy hand, General Qin Zhengguo dialed a private, non-networked red line that led to a quiet courtyard in the western hills of the capital. It belonged to a ninety-year-old retired Supreme Elder, a living legend who had helped build the nation's deepest security framework half a century ago.

The phone rang four times before an old, steady voice answered. "Zhengguo? Calling at this hour means trouble."

"Old Chief, forgive me for waking you," General Qin Zhengguo said rapidly, lowering his voice. "An Absolute Order just lit up across the country. Yunxi County in Lanshui is under a ten-kilometer blackout and a hundred-kilometer air lockdown. Fully masked, completely mute operatives are standing inside the capital's red-walled compound, every provincial command center, and right here on my own deck. Even the Minister of State Security and the Cabinet are blind. Old Chief, tell me who they are!"

On the other end of the line, the breathing stopped.

A heavy, rattling silence stretched for five long seconds. Then came the sharp clatter of a porcelain cup hitting a wooden table.

"Fully masked," the ninety-year-old elder whispered, his once-iron voice suddenly trembling with unmistakable, raw terror. "Completely mute. An Absolute Order. Oh heavens. No. No, it cannot be."

General Qin Zhengguo's heart skipped a beat. In forty years, he had never once heard fear in this old man's voice. "Old Chief! You know who they are! Tell me right now!"

"Shut your mouth, Qin Zhengguo!" the old elder barked back in sheer panic, his voice cracking. "Do not speak of them on any line! Do not ask their name! I knew they existed fifty years ago, and I prayed every single day of my life that they would never be activated! Lock your command center, keep every single soldier away from Yunxi County, and pray to heaven that none of us offended him!"

"Old Chief, please!" General Qin Zhengguo pleaded, sweat dripping down his jaw as he glanced back at the glowing crimson combat boards. "I cannot just sit blind! Every theater command in the nation is at full combat readiness! Foreign intelligence rooms in Washington and Moscow are already panicking over the orbital blackout! If one nervous pilot or one panicked officer makes a mistake tonight, we could trigger an international crisis! Give me something, anything, so I can understand whether this country is going to war!"

Silence hung heavy on the line for ten agonizing seconds. General Qin Zhengguo could hear the old elder's shaky, labored breathing.

"I cannot tell you who they are, and even I do not dare speak his name," the old elder finally rasped, terrified to the bone. "They are not under the military, not under the cabinet, and not under any ministry. If anyone on earth can help you understand even a fraction of what is happening, it might be the people tied to the Black Pine numbers."

"The Black Pine numbers?" General Qin Zhengguo repeated sharply.

"Write this down and burn the paper," the old elder whispered, reciting a ten-digit blind relay number in a shaking voice. "Call it. Pass their checks one by one. Maybe someone at the top of that chain can tell you whether to stand down. And Qin Zhengguo, if you ever tell a single living soul I gave you that number, I will deny knowing your name until the day I die!"

The line clicked dead.
