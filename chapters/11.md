# Chapter 11: The Flower-Delivery Van on Riverside Road

At half past ten on Friday night, a faded white cargo van sat parked along Riverside Road with Qinghe Fresh Florals painted on its door. Inside the windowless bay, matte monitors cast pale blue light across three operatives in dark jackets and charcoal half-masks.

Inside the Black Pine Guard, no man knew another's name or face. A man held a number, a discipline, and an assignment.

Number Seven sat at the central console, eyes fixed on a green status dot over Fuxing New Village. Across from him, Number Fourteen tapped his earpiece off and exhaled quietly.

"Perimeter detail checked in," Number Fourteen said. "Chen Xuan locked Mingde Hardware, turned Yan Tiehu away at the mouth of the lane, and returned to his parents' apartment twelve minutes ago. Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang are inside with him. Nobody from the Chen family went near Old Street Station."

Number Seven's shoulders eased slightly. "Did Yan Tiehu try to follow him?"

"No," Number Fourteen replied, a dry note in his voice. "Yan Tiehu saw our men at Gong's Noodles and left holding his right wrist. Number Nine says he did not even look back twice."

"Good," Number Seven said. "Keep Number Nine's watch two blocks back from Fuxing New Village. No lights, no silhouettes near the stairwell. Chen Xuan wants his family to sleep tonight without feeling watched."

Number Fourteen logged the relay and glanced down the bench. "How close are we on the afternoon incident?"

At the far console, Number Thirty-four locked three synchronized windows onto the widest display. He had not moved since the private jet landed at the regional airfield outside Yunxi.

"The reconstruction of the two o'clock incident on Old Street is complete," Number Thirty-four said, leaning back and rubbing the bridge of his nose above his mask. "It is sloppy work. The kind local men produce when nobody has asked them a hard question in ten years."

Number Seven leaned forward, scanning the white timestamps. "Walk me through it."

"At one fifty-four," Number Thirty-four said, enlarging a traffic frame from Old Street's west intersection, "a forty-one-year-old drifter named Ma Er walked past Mingde Hardware. No fixed job, two prior citations for petty insurance fraud in Lanshui six years ago. Four minutes earlier, a burner phone in his pocket exchanged two short calls with one of Yan Tiehu's drivers."

Number Fourteen narrowed his eyes at the screen. "A paid fall artist."

"A clumsy one," Number Thirty-four replied, advancing the timeline seven minutes. "At two zero one, Chen Mingde stepped out of Mingde Hardware carrying a wooden crate of galvanized pipe elbows. Ma Er planted his heel on the wet curb, threw himself backward onto the flagstones without Chen Mingde ever extending a hand, and screamed that Chen Mingde shoved him over a demolition dispute."

"Did anyone call an ambulance?" Number Fourteen asked, his brow furrowing.

"Nobody," Number Thirty-four said flatly. "Zero dispatch calls from Old Street between two and two thirty. Instead, at two zero seven, a white hatchback registered to Yan Tiehu's nephew pulled up, loaded Ma Er into the back seat, and drove him straight to Yunxi People's Hospital."

Number Seven's jaw tightened. "And the hospital played along."

"Worse," Number Thirty-four said, shifting to a clinical intake sheet beside a raw intranet audit log. "At two thirty-eight, Deputy Director Fang Zhihua bypassed emergency triage and signed a diagnosis of moderate cerebral concussion and acute cervical sprain under Ma Er's name. That classified the incident as intentional injury, letting the station hold Chen Mingde without bail."

Number Fourteen leaned in. "He wrote that in half an hour?"

"Faster," Number Thirty-four said, zooming onto hexadecimal metadata at the bottom of the form. "The template carrying Ma Er's name, national identity number, and exact symptom wording was created on Deputy Director Fang Zhihua's workstation at eleven fifteen this morning. Nearly three hours before Ma Er stepped onto Old Street."

Number Fourteen let out a short, cold breath. "Pre-typed before lunch. They did not even wait to see which side of his head he pretended to hit."

"Now look at panel three," Number Thirty-four murmured, highlighting a warrant in sharp amber. "Old Street Station Director Cheng Biao signed the administrative detention warrant for Chen Mingde at two twenty-six. Put the clocks side by side. Cheng Biao stamped a detention order citing a hospital concussion diagnosis twelve full minutes before Yunxi People's Hospital even logged Fang Zhihua's signature into its system."

Silence held inside the van. Number Seven stared at the amber timestamp without blinking.

"They were never building a criminal case," Number Seven said quietly. "They were building a lever. Hold Chen Mingde overnight, terrify Chen Jianguo and Wu Lanying, and wait for the family to walk into Old Street Station tomorrow ready to sign away Mingde Hardware."

Number Fourteen rested his palm on the console. "We have the mirrored logs and metadata. Forward them to the provincial discipline office tonight, and Cheng Biao and Fang Zhihua will be in cuffs before noon."

"No," Number Seven cut in, shaking his head once.

Number Fourteen paused. "You think the provincial office leaks?"

"I think mirrored packets from an unauthorized entry are intelligence, not courtroom evidence," Number Seven said evenly. "Move early and Cheng Biao wipes local backups, Fang Zhihua blames a sync error, and Ma Er vanishes before sunrise. Worse, whoever sits behind Hongrui Real Estate smells smoke and pulls back before we see the whole board."

Number Thirty-four looked up from his keyboard. "You think this goes higher than Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao."

"A demolition crew does not coordinate a station director and a hospital deputy director before lunch unless someone higher is paying for the clockwork," Number Seven said. "Chen Xuan's instruction is clear. Prove Chen Mingde's innocence with hard, court-admissible originals, and do not tip off the county until we choose the hour."

Number Fourteen nodded slowly, settling back into discipline. "Then we take the physical originals tonight while they are comfortable."

Number Seven touched two fingers to his earpiece, opening the encrypted field channel.

"Number Nineteen," Number Seven said. "You have Old Street. The bakery diagonally across from Mingde Hardware has an exterior high-definition camera under its second-floor awning. Secure the original storage drive cleanly, leave an identical pre-cloned drive in its place so the baker notices nothing, and log the hash."

A crisp double-click answered over the line.

"Number Twenty-seven," Number Seven continued. "You have Yunxi People's Hospital. Pull the raw DICOM imaging archive and the physical server room timestamp log for Ma Er's cranial scan. Prove the scan is blank. Zero disturbance, zero footprint."

Another quiet double-click returned.

"Number Forty-two," Number Seven finished. "Ma Er did not stay in a hospital bed. Cell-tower triangulation puts his phone near the old grain depot in the south district, likely a back-alley mahjong parlor. Find him, verify the payoff cash and the call log from Yan Tiehu, secure proof of both, and keep a silent tail on him until morning."

The third double-click snapped back cleanly.

Number Seven lowered his hand and turned to Number Thirty-four. "Keep the hospital and station networks under passive watch. If Cheng Biao or Deng Yongchang makes a single call out of routine, I want it."

"Already listening," Number Thirty-four said, his fingers returning to the keys.

Outside the quiet white van, three silent shadows moved out into the wet streets of Yunxi County.
