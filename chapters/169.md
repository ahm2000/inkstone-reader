# Chapter 169: Walk Along Shenzhen Bay

By 5:30 PM, the late November sun was sinking toward the western rim of Shenzhen Bay, painting the wide water in streaks of warm copper and soft violet. A cool autumn sea breeze swept across the coastal promenade of Shenzhen Bay Park, carrying the faint cry of white egrets gliding over the mudflats.

Qin Yuhan had traded her sharp black boardroom suit for a soft cream knit sweater, a light camel trench coat, and flat leather shoes. Her long dark hair fell loose over her shoulders, blowing gently in the wind as she linked her arm snugly through Chen Xuan's.

"Finally," she sighed softly, resting her cheek briefly against his shoulder as they walked past a row of swaying palms. "Two days of staring at shipping charts and listening to southern port directors dance around their words. My head was starting to spin."

"That is why you should take my career advice," Chen Xuan said with a completely straight face, holding her warm hand inside his coat pocket. "Quit Haiyue Group, come join me at Hengxin Logistics, and spend your afternoons checking three boxes of circuit boards at Shekou Dock. Zero stress, steady pay, and free roast pork buns from friendly aunties."

Qin Yuhan burst out laughing, her cool executive poise melting away in an instant. "You actually spent two full hours inspecting three containers?"

"Hey, every steel seal deserves respect," Chen Xuan protested mildly. "Besides, I had to make sure the paperwork was spotless so Manager Hu does not dock my attendance bonus. Without my bonus, how am I supposed to buy you dinner?"

"Mr. Chen, I am fairly sure we can still afford dinner," Qin Yuhan teased, her dark eyes curving like bright crescents. She squeezed his fingers, feeling the quiet warmth of his palm. Every time she stood in a tense conference room facing greedy rivals like the Shen Family, her shoulders stayed tight as bowstrings. The moment Chen Xuan walked beside her, cracking dry jokes and holding her hand, all that tension dissolved like mist.

A few dozen yards ahead, near a wooden viewing deck, a small cluster of evening snack carts glowed under warm string lights. The rich aroma of grilled squid, spiced curry fish balls, and sweet ginger milk drifted right into their path.

Qin Yuhan's steps slowed immediately. "Chen Xuan, look over there."

"Say no more," Chen Xuan grinned. "Wait right here by the railing. Do not let the sea wind chill your neck."

He jogged over to the stall, chatted cheerfully with the vendor for two minutes, and returned holding two paper cups of steaming ginger milk, a small bowl of golden curry fish balls, and two skewers of sizzling grilled squid brushed with cumin.

"Careful, it is hot," Chen Xuan said, lifting one fish ball on a bamboo pick and blowing on it gently before holding it up to her lips.

Qin Yuhan blushed faintly, glanced around at the passing evening strollers, and took a neat bite. The savory curry burst warmly on her tongue. "Delicious," she murmured happily, taking a sip of the sweet ginger milk. "Way better than that stiff banquet food at the hotel."

"You have a drop of sauce right there," Chen Xuan smiled. He pulled a clean tissue from his pocket and gently dabbed the corner of her mouth. His thumb lingered lightly against her warm cheek for a second before she playfully swatted his hand away, her ears turning pink.

Along the paved footpath nearby, a silver-haired couple strolled slowly behind a chubby corgi that waddled happily toward the grass. As they passed, the older lady smiled warmly at Qin Yuhan. "What a sweet young couple. Make sure you button your coat, girl, the bay wind gets chilly after dark."

"Thank you, Auntie," Qin Yuhan replied politely, smiling back. Neither she nor anyone else on the path paid any extra attention to the friendly retired couple or the young man in a gray tracksuit tying his shoelace on a wooden bench twenty paces back. They blended seamlessly into the peaceful autumn evening.

As the sky deepened into indigo and the skyline of Hong Kong began to sparkle like scattered diamonds across the dark strait, Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan leaned side by side against the wooden railing.

"Grandpa called me right before I came downstairs," Qin Yuhan said softly, cradling her warm paper cup in both hands. "He asked if you have been practicing your chess openings on this trip. He is still salty about losing three straight games to you at Emerald Riverside Villa last Sunday."

Chen Xuan chuckled softly. "Elder Qin plays chess like he used to command an armored division. All cannons forward, zero defense on the back rank. If I do not let him win one game next time, he might stop letting me eat his pickled radish."

"You had better not let him win on purpose," Qin Yuhan warned with a smile. "Grandpa has sharp eyes. If he catches you going easy on him, he will lecture you for an hour. Honestly, though, I have never seen him laugh as much as he does when you are around. Even my parents keep asking when we are coming back to Pudong so you can make your slow-braised lamb."

"Tell Mom and Dad I learned a new trick at Shekou Dock today," Chen Xuan said easily. "Dried tangerine peel from Xinhui. Next Sunday, I will cook a big pot."

Qin Yuhan turned to look at his calm profile against the golden lights of the promenade. She had spent years watching ambitious heirs and ruthless financiers chase power, money, and status until they forgot how to breathe. Yet the man standing beside her wanted nothing more than a quiet desk job, good food, and an unhurried walk by the sea.

She reached up, adjusted the collar of his faded cotton jacket against the cool breeze, and leaned her head softly against his arm. "Chen Xuan, whatever happens with the Shen Family this week, I am really glad you came to Shenzhen with me."

"Where my wife goes, I go," Chen Xuan said simply, wrapping an arm around her shoulders. "Now finish your squid before it gets cold. The wind is picking up."

Hand in hand, they walked slowly back along the glowing bay, leaving the noise of the business world far behind them.
