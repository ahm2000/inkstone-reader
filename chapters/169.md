# Chapter 169: Walk Along Shenzhen Bay

The evening breeze along Shenzhen Bay Park carried the cool scent of salt water and blooming flowers from the grassy slopes.

Along the wide stone walkway beside the sea, families pushed strollers, young couples leaned against the silver railing taking photos, and joggers moved past in steady rhythm.

For the first time since flying south from Shanghai, Qin Yuhan was not wearing a tailored business suit or high heels. Without the stiff armor of her office clothes, she looked younger, softer, and quietly tired.

Walking half a step beside her was Chen Xuan in his usual plain white cotton shirt and dark trousers.

He handed one cup to her. "Half sugar, less ice. Like the tea shop across from Lujiazui."

Qin Yuhan blinked in surprise as she took the warm cup. A faint, genuine smile touched her lips. "You remembered how I drink milk tea?"

"You ordered it twice during our Shanghai port meetings," Chen Xuan replied easily, taking a sip from his own cup. "You only drink coffee when you are angry at a board member."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft laugh, the tight line between her eyebrows melting away. She leaned her elbows lightly against the steel railing and looked out across the dark water toward Hong Kong.

For a full minute, neither of them spoke. The gentle slap of small waves against the concrete seawall filled the space between them.

"Every cargo container from the Haiyue Star finished clearing customs at four o'clock," Qin Yuhan said quietly, resting her chin near the rim of her warm cup. "All three southern buyers signed their delivery slips. We did not lose a single yuan in late penalties."

"Good," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Then you can sleep tonight."

"I should," Qin Yuhan murmured. She paused, watching the blinking lights of a ferry crossing toward the Hong Kong side. "Chen Xuan, can I ask you something honest? Yesterday at Pier Nine, when Shen Jingzhou threatened to bleed Haiyue Group for 8 million yuan a day, did you think I was foolish for refusing to call my grandfather?"

Chen Xuan turned his head slightly to look at her profile in the warm glow of the park lamps. "No."

Qin Yuhan gave a wry, self-deprecating smile. "Half of Haiyue's senior managers thought I was crazy. Vice President Fang called me twice on the flight down from Shanghai, begging me to ask General Qin Zhengguo to make one phone call to Shenzhen. He said one sentence from my grandfather would open every gate in Guangdong."

She tightened her slender fingers around the paper cup, her voice dropping lower and more honest than Chen Xuan had ever heard it.

"They do not understand," Qin Yuhan said softly. "Ever since I took over Haiyue Group at twenty-four, every old fox in Shanghai and Beijing has whispered behind my back. When we won a tough shipping bid, they said it was because of General Qin's name. When a bank approved our fleet loan, they smiled to my face and told their friends I was just a soldier's granddaughter playing company president."

Her eyes shone faintly in the lamplight, though she did not let a single tear fall.

"My grandfather spent fifty years wearing a uniform so people could sleep in peace," Qin Yuhan continued, her voice quiet and firm. "He never once asked anyone for a favor for our family. If I pick up the phone and hide behind his name the first time a dock bully blocks my ship, then every late night I spent checking shipping charts, every honest contract my team fought for, and every ounce of pride he gave me becomes worthless. I would rather eat the loss myself than stain his name."

The sea wind brushed a loose strand of hair across her cheek. She tucked it behind her ear and let out a long, quiet breath, looking a little embarrassed.

"Listen to me talking like this," Qin Yuhan said with a small, shy laugh. "I never even say these things to my mother. Why am I unloading all this on an office clerk from Hengxin Logistics?"

"Because you have been carrying it alone for four years," Chen Xuan said gently.

Qin Yuhan paused, looking up into his eyes.

Chen Xuan leaned lightly against the railing beside her, looking out across the water with the same calm steadiness she had seen at Pier Nine.

"A tree that leans on a stone wall falls the moment the wall moves," Chen Xuan said simply. "A tree that grows its own roots stands through any storm. Yesterday at Pier Nine, Shen Jingzhou brought officials, bodyguards, and an 8 million yuan threat, and you did not take one step back. General Qin Zhengguo would be proud of you."

Qin Yuhan stared at him for a moment. Something tight and cold that had sat inside her chest for years seemed to loosen all at once.

She looked down at her warm cup of milk tea, a soft blush rising faintly on her cheeks. "You know, for an ordinary office worker who hates overtime, you say things that sound a lot wiser than the business professors at Fudan University."

"I just watch people all day," Chen Xuan replied with a mild smile. "Most people make loud noise when they are scared. You stayed quiet and stood your ground."

Qin Yuhan chuckled softly and pushed off the railing, walking slowly beside him again down the palm-lined path.

"Still," Qin Yuhan said, glancing sideways at him with bright, curious eyes, "you have not explained how Chief Inspector Lu got arrested at five o'clock sharp yesterday, right after you took a sip from that green thermos and said you did not like working overtime."

Chen Xuan looked completely serious. "Shenzhen port authorities clearly care a lot about punctuality."

"Punctuality!" Qin Yuhan burst out laughing, a clear, joyful sound that made two passing joggers smile. She shook her head in mock defeat. "Fine. Keep your secrets, Clerk Chen. Just promise me one thing."

"What is that?"

"Whenever I have to walk into a lion's den," Qin Yuhan said, her voice turning warm and quiet again, "stay nearby with that green thermos. I do not know why, Chen Xuan, but whenever you are standing two steps behind me, everything feels a little lighter."

Chen Xuan looked at her gentle, unguarded smile under the golden streetlights of Shenzhen Bay Park. A rare, genuine warmth softened the depths of his eyes.

"Alright," Chen Xuan said simply. "As long as the lion's den closes before nine."

Qin Yuhan laughed again, her shoulder brushing lightly against his arm as they walked on toward the park exit.

Fifty meters behind them along the shaded grass edge, a quiet figure in a plain dark windbreaker matched their slow pace, watching every passing bicycle and shadow with silent care, never once intruding on the peaceful evening between them.
