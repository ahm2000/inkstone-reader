# Chapter 131: The Corner Table Speaks

By nine thirty in the morning, the main auditorium of the Sha City International Convention Center was packed to the back doors. Three hundred freight chairmen, port directors, and local reporters filled the velvet tables beneath bright spotlights and the red and gold banner of the Sha Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum.

In the front row, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang sat straight in his leather chair, nodding warmly whenever a camera swung his way, flanked by two senior transport officials.

"Everything is ready, Deputy Governor," the taller official murmured, checking his watch. "Song Baisheng promised the twenty signatures will be done before eleven."

"Good," Wu Zhenbang replied, pleased. "Sha City needs a bold headline this quarter. Once the Provincial Freight Alliance is signed on camera, the other coastal provinces will have to follow our lead."

Two rows back, Qin Yuhan sat neatly at the Haiyue Group table with a slim blue folder beside her teacup, her eyes cool and steady on the podium.

Far in the back corner by the side aisle sat the small table for Hengxin Logistics. Sun Haoran pulled at his tight necktie and leaned close to Chen Xuan.

"Chen Xuan, look at the front row," Sun Haoran muttered nervously. "Every big port chairman in the province is here, and Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang is sitting right below the podium. Just keep your head down and take notes, all right? We are only here to listen and show our faces."

"Do not worry, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said calmly, unscrewing his scratched green thermos for a quiet sip of warm jasmine tea. "I am only here to listen too."

"Good, good," Sun Haoran breathed out, wiping his damp palms. "A small Shanghai firm like ours does not need to draw attention. We just clap when everyone else claps."

Waiters walked the aisles, placing thick leather folders titled Provincial Freight Alliance Agreement onto every table.

Sun Haoran frowned at the dense pages. "They hand this out ten minutes before the speech? Who can read fifty pages in ten minutes?"

"Nobody is meant to read the back pages," Chen Xuan said mildly, flipping straight to the final section and pausing on a small gray heading: Article 14: Unified Data and Clearing Services.

"Article 14?" Sun Haoran squinted. "Boring computer rules. Half the chairmen here never read contracts past page three."

"That is what the writers counted on," Chen Xuan murmured.

At ten o'clock sharp, the stage lights brightened and Song Baisheng strode to the podium with a confident smile, while Li Weiming watched from the guest table behind his silver rimmed glasses.

"Honored leaders, fellow chairmen, and friends," Song Baisheng boomed. "For ten years, our provincial ports and trucking fleets have fought over routes, wasted fuel on empty return trips, and lost cargo to neighboring provinces. Today, that ends."

He gestured toward the screen showing green shipping lanes and rising profit charts.

"With the transport office's support, Wanfeng Freight has built the Provincial Freight Alliance," Song Baisheng declared. "Every member shares one unified scheduling platform. Customs clearance drops from two days to four hours, empty runs fall by half, and the provincial bank promises low interest credit lines to every company that signs today."

In the front row, Wu Zhenbang clapped slowly, and dozens of chairmen joined in, whispering eagerly about the loans.

Song Baisheng waited for the applause to fade. "Twenty major firms have agreed to lead the way, and television crews are ready to film the signing before the break. To keep things open and fair, does anyone have a quick question before we sign?"

It was a routine line meant to draw polite silence. Chairmen reached for their pens, and Wu Zhenbang checked his watch.

Then, far in the back corner, a hand rose quietly.

Sun Haoran panicked, grabbing Chen Xuan's sleeve with both hands. "Chen Xuan! Put your hand down! Are you out of your mind? Sit still!"

Chen Xuan gave him a reassuring smile, eased his sleeve free, and stood up as an usher handed him a wireless microphone. Three hundred heads turned toward the back corner, where a plain young man in an ironed white shirt stood beside a scratched green thermos.

On stage, Song Baisheng frowned into the lights. "Yes, the gentleman in the back. State your company and keep it brief."

"Good morning, everyone," Chen Xuan said clearly. "My name is Chen Xuan, an ordinary clerk from Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai. I have three simple questions about Article 14 at the back of this agreement."

Wu Zhenbang's smile stiffened, and Li Weiming's fingers froze over his notebook.

"First," Chen Xuan said politely. "Article 14 states that every provincial port and trucking firm must send its daily cargo lists, ship arrival times, and warehouse stock numbers through an overseas clearing server in Zurich. Why does a provincial freight alliance inside China need to send all our coastal shipping schedules to Zurich?"

A murmur rippled through the hall as port directors flipped rapidly to the back of their folders. Chen Xuan raised a second finger.

"Second," he continued evenly. "Page forty eight lists that Zurich firm as Alpine Marine Data Services. Public business records show it opened nine months ago in a rented mailbox office, with zero branches in China and no Chinese board members. Why are we handing the live schedules of twenty ports to a nine month old mailbox company?"

The murmur grew louder. Sun Haoran stared up as if watching a clerk walk into a tiger cage with a teacup, while Song Baisheng gripped the podium with white knuckles.

Chen Xuan raised his third finger, looking straight toward the stage.

"And third," Chen Xuan asked gently. "Who actually owns that Zurich company, and why does the fine print of Article 14 state that Wanfeng Freight receives a 15,000,000 yuan setup fee the very hour twenty companies sign this paper?"

When Chen Xuan lowered the microphone and sat calmly down beside his green thermos, not a single person in the three hundred seat auditorium made a sound.
