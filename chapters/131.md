# Chapter 131: Ding Yizhen's Backstage Threat

Loud applause inside the main hall of Shanshui Villa kept ringing even after the host announced a short afternoon tea break.

Half the harbor directors across Handong Province crowded around the center table, flipping back to page 28 of the bidding handbook. Thanks to Qin Yuhan reading Article 14 aloud on stage, not one shipping chairman had picked up a pen to sign.

Qin Yuhan stepped out through the side doors into the quiet east hallway to catch her breath.

Chen Xuan strolled behind her, carrying her cream wool coat over one arm and his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos in the other hand.

"Here," Chen Xuan said, pouring warm honey-lemon water into the metal lid. "Talking into a microphone dries your throat out fast. Drink this while it is warm."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft sigh, her shoulders relaxing as she took the lid. A bright smile touched her lips.

"Did you see Cai Chenggong's face when I read that line about the Zurich holding account?" she whispered, taking a sip. "He looked like he had swallowed a raw lemon."

"I saw it," Chen Xuan said mildly, leaning against the wood-paneled wall. "His hand shook so hard he nearly knocked over his water bottle. Good thing you read the fine print."

"Good thing you spotted the weird footnote on page 28 last night," Qin Yuhan corrected softly, her eyes warm. "Honestly, Chen Xuan, for someone who says he just stamps shipping forms on the 12th floor, your eyes are sharper than my whole legal team."

"Habit from checking warehouse manifests," Chen Xuan replied with a lazy shrug. "When a column of numbers looks crooked, it usually is."

Before she could answer, heavy leather footsteps echoed down the carpeted corridor.

Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen walked over with two stone-faced assistants trailing behind him. A polite smile sat on his mouth, yet his eyes were cold as stagnant water. His two assistants stopped ten paces back, blocking the side hallway from the main lobby.

"President Qin," Ding Yizhen said smoothly, halting three steps away. "What a lively speech in there. You certainly gave our shipping forum something to talk about."

Qin Yuhan straightened her posture and handed the thermos lid back to Chen Xuan. Her warm smile turned to crisp poise.

"Vice Mayor Ding," she replied evenly. "I only pointed out a drafting error in Article 14. Routing 300 million in public harbor deposits through an unregistered Zurich shell company would create serious compliance risks."

Ding Yizhen chuckled softly, though there was zero warmth in the sound.

"Compliance matters, of course," he said, clasping his hands behind his back. "Still, President Qin, Haiyue Group is a guest from Shanghai. Handong has its own rhythm. When outsiders overturn the table without asking why the dishes were set that way, they make life very hard for themselves."

"Are you saying the error was intentional, Vice Mayor?" Qin Yuhan asked coolly.

"I am saying that coastal warehouse permits and harbor leases in Jingzhou depend on local harmony," Ding Yizhen murmured, stepping half a pace closer. "Your four coastal permits are still on the municipal review desk. Paperwork can move in three days, or sit in a drawer for three years. It depends on whether a company shows respect."

Outside the arched window, a late-autumn gust shook golden ginkgo leaves down across the courtyard.

Ding Yizhen's gaze slid sideways to Chen Xuan, taking in his plain dark jacket and scuffed green metal thermos. Open disdain flashed across the Vice Mayor's face.

"And you must be President Qin's husband," Ding Yizhen said with a thin smirk. "I heard you work a quiet desk job in Shanghai. You should advise your wife to step back tonight and issue a polite clarification. Jingzhou nights are long, and outsiders who stir up trouble rarely sleep well."

Qin Yuhan's fingers tightened, ready to fire back, but Chen Xuan shifted half a step forward first.

He looked completely unbothered. He twisted the lid back onto his green thermos and smiled easily at Ding Yizhen.

"Thank you for the advice, Vice Mayor," Chen Xuan said pleasantly. "Though late-autumn wind off the lake makes people tense. Getting too worked up is hard on the blood pressure."

Ding Yizhen's eyes narrowed. "Are you lecturing me, young man?"

"Not at all," Chen Xuan replied mildly. "I just handle logistics paperwork for a living. Honest permits always find a way through, and crooked ledgers always catch up with their owners sooner or later."

"You have a sharp tongue for a clerk," Ding Yizhen snapped, his oily poise cracking. "Let us see how relaxed you look tomorrow morning when those permits are rejected."

Qin Yuhan stepped neatly beside Chen Xuan, linking her arm calmly through his.

"Haiyue Group relies on the law, Vice Mayor Ding, not backroom favors," she said clearly.

Ding Yizhen stared at them for three heavy seconds. His smile vanished completely. Without another word, he spun on his heel and marched back toward the main hall, his assistants hurrying after him.

Only after they left did Qin Yuhan exhale softly.

"He was not joking about those permits," she murmured, frowning slightly. "If he stalls the municipal office, our winter shipping schedule will take a real hit."

"Do not worry," Chen Xuan said warmly, draping her cream wool coat over her shoulders. "People who bark that loudly in a hallway are usually panicking inside. Let us focus on dinner first. I heard the kitchen makes decent braised lake fish."

Qin Yuhan looked at his calm face and laughed softly. "You really never panic, do you?"

"Panicking ruins digestion," Chen Xuan grinned, taking her hand.

As they walked toward the garden terrace, his single everyday smartphone vibrated once in his pocket. He did not even pull it out. He already knew Number 1's update: the Zurich transfer records had reached Hou Liangping's desk ten minutes ago.

Ding Yizhen was right about one thing. Tonight in Jingzhou was going to be a very long night, just not for Chen Xuan.
