# Chapter 131: Ding Yizhen's Backstage Threat

Loud applause inside the main hall of Shanshui Villa kept ringing even as Hou Liangping's investigators stepped calmly up to the committee table to seal the bidding folders.

Caught off guard by the sudden turn, the flustered host hurried to the microphone and announced a twenty-minute afternoon tea break.

Nobody moved toward the refreshment tables right away. Half the harbor directors across Handong Province crowded around the center aisle, flipping back to page 28 of the bidding handbook with dark, uneasy faces. Thanks to Qin Yuhan reading Article 14 aloud on stage, not a single shipping chairman had picked up a pen to sign.

Qin Yuhan slipped out through the tall side doors into the quiet east hallway to catch her breath.

Chen Xuan strolled unhurriedly behind her, carrying her cream wool coat over one arm and his dented green metal thermos in the other hand.

"Here," Chen Xuan said, twisting off the metal lid and pouring a cup of warm honey-lemon water. "Talking into a microphone dries your throat out fast. Drink this while it is still warm."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft sigh, her tight shoulders relaxing as she took the lid with both hands. A bright, genuine smile touched her lips.

"Did you see Cai Chenggong's face when I read that line about the Zurich holding account?" she whispered, taking a grateful sip. "He looked like he had swallowed a raw lemon whole."

"I saw it," Chen Xuan said mildly, leaning one shoulder against the wood-paneled wall. "His hand shook so hard he nearly knocked over his water bottle. Good thing you read the fine print before signing anything."

"Good thing you spotted that crooked footnote on page 28 last night," Qin Yuhan corrected softly, her eyes warm as she looked up at him. "Honestly, Chen Xuan, for someone who says he just stamps shipping forms at the office, your eyes are sharper than my whole legal team."

"Force of habit from checking warehouse manifests," Chen Xuan replied with a lazy shrug. "When a column of numbers looks uneven, there is usually someone hiding a leak at the bottom."

Before she could tease him further, heavy leather footsteps echoed down the carpeted corridor.

Vice Mayor Ding Yizhen walked briskly toward them with two stone-faced assistants trailing closely behind him. A smooth, practiced smile sat on his mouth, yet a thin sheen of sweat lingered at his temples, and his eyes were as cold as stagnant water. His two assistants halted ten paces back, quietly cutting off the side hallway from the main lobby.

"President Qin," Ding Yizhen said in a low, oily voice, stopping three steps away. "What a lively speech in there. You certainly gave our shipping forum and the investigators plenty to talk about."

Qin Yuhan straightened her posture and handed the thermos lid back to Chen Xuan. Her warm smile faded into crisp, executive poise.

"Vice Mayor Ding," she replied evenly. "I only pointed out a drafting problem in Article 14. Routing 300 million in public harbor deposits through an unregistered Zurich shell company would put every shipping group in that room at serious compliance risk."

Ding Yizhen chuckled softly, though there was zero warmth in the sound.

"Clerical mistakes happen during rushed drafts, of course," he said, clasping his hands behind his back to hide the tension in his fingers. "Still, President Qin, Haiyue Group is a guest from Shanghai. Handong has its own rhythm. When outsiders overturn the table instead of asking why the dishes were set that way, they make life very hard for themselves."

"Are you saying the clause was intentional, Vice Mayor?" Qin Yuhan asked coolly.

"I am saying that a brief joint clarification from Haiyue Group calling it a reading misunderstanding would calm the room and save everyone unnecessary trouble," Ding Yizhen murmured, stepping half a pace closer. "Your four coastal warehouse permits are still sitting on the municipal review desk. Paperwork can clear in three days, or sit in a locked drawer for three years. It all depends on whether a company shows respect."

Outside the arched window, a crisp late-autumn gust shook golden ginkgo leaves down across the stone courtyard.

Ding Yizhen's gaze slid sideways to Chen Xuan, taking in his plain dark jacket and scuffed green metal thermos. Open disdain flickered across the Vice Mayor's face.

"And you must be President Qin's husband," Ding Yizhen said with a thin smirk. "I heard you work a quiet desk job in Shanghai. You should advise your wife to step back tonight and issue that clarification before the inquiry drags on. Jingzhou nights are long, and outsiders who stir up trouble rarely sleep well."

Qin Yuhan's fingers tightened at her sides, ready to fire back, but Chen Xuan shifted half a step forward first.

He looked completely unbothered. He twisted the lid calmly back onto his green thermos and offered Ding Yizhen an easy, unhurried smile.

"Thank you for the advice, Vice Mayor," Chen Xuan said pleasantly. "Though late-autumn wind off the lake makes people tense. Getting too worked up is hard on the blood pressure."

Ding Yizhen's eyes narrowed sharply. "Are you lecturing me, young man?"

"Not at all," Chen Xuan replied mildly. "I just handle logistics paperwork for a living. Honest permits always find a way through, and crooked ledgers always catch up with their owners sooner or later."

"You have a sharp tongue for a clerk," Ding Yizhen snapped, his oily composure cracking at last. "Let us see how relaxed you look tomorrow morning when those four permits are rejected out of hand."

Qin Yuhan stepped neatly beside Chen Xuan, linking her arm calmly and firmly through his.

"Haiyue Group relies on the law, Vice Mayor Ding, not backroom favors," she said clearly. "We will not be issuing any clarification."

Ding Yizhen stared at the two of them for three heavy seconds. Every trace of his forced smile vanished. Without another word, he spun on his heel and marched back toward the main hall, his two assistants hurrying after him.

Only after the hallway emptied did Qin Yuhan exhale a quiet breath.

"He was not bluffing about those permits," she murmured, a faint crease forming between her brows. "If he stalls the municipal office before the investigators finish, our winter shipping schedule will take a real hit."

"Do not worry," Chen Xuan said warmly, draping her cream wool coat gently over her shoulders. "People who bark that loudly in a side hallway are usually panicking inside. Let us focus on dinner with your father first. I heard the villa kitchen makes decent braised lake fish."

Qin Yuhan looked at his unhurried face and let out a soft laugh. "You really never panic, do you?"

"Panicking ruins digestion," Chen Xuan grinned, taking her hand.

As they walked toward the garden terrace, his phone vibrated once in his pocket. He did not even bother pulling it out. He already knew Number 1's update: the Zurich transfer records had landed on Hou Liangping's desk ten minutes ago.

Ding Yizhen was right about one thing. Tonight in Jingzhou was going to be a very long night, just not for Chen Xuan.
