# Chapter 131: The Corner Table Speaks

By nine thirty in the morning, the main auditorium of the Lanshui International Convention Center was packed to the back doors.

Three hundred freight chairmen, port directors, and local reporters filled the long rows of velvet-covered tables. Bright spotlights shone down on the wide stage, where the red-and-gold banner of the Lanshui Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum stretched from wall to wall.

In the front row, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang sat straight in his leather chair, nodding warmly whenever a provincial television camera swung his way. Beside him sat two senior transport officials who leaned in and laughed politely at every remark he made.

"Everything is ready, Deputy Governor," the taller official murmured, glancing at his watch. "Song Baisheng promised the twenty signatures will be done before eleven."

"Good," Wu Zhenbang replied in a pleased low voice, smoothing the front of his dark jacket. "Lanshui needs a bold headline this quarter. Once the Provincial Freight Alliance is signed on camera, the other coastal provinces will have to follow our lead."

Two rows back, Qin Yuhan sat neatly at the Haiyue Group table. A slim blue folder rested beside her teacup. Her assistant leaned over to whisper something about the stage lights, and Qin Yuhan simply gave a brief, calm nod while her eyes stayed cool and steady on the podium.

Far at the back corner near the side aisle, a small white card marked the table for Hengxin Logistics.

Sun Haoran shifted uncomfortably in his chair, pulling at his tight necktie. He leaned close to Chen Xuan and whispered out of the corner of his mouth.

"Chen Xuan, look at the front row," Sun Haoran muttered nervously. "Every big port chairman in the province is here, and Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang himself is sitting right below the podium. Just keep your head down and take notes, all right? We are only here to listen and show our faces."

"Do not worry, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said calmly. He set his scratched green thermos beside his notebook, unscrewed the steel cap, and took a quiet sip of warm jasmine tea. "I am only here to listen too."

"Good, good," Sun Haoran breathed out, wiping his damp palms on his trousers. "A small Qingyan firm like ours does not need to draw attention in a room like this. If the big men want to sign an alliance, we just clap when everyone else claps."

Waiters walked quickly along the aisles, dropping thick leather folders onto every table. On the front cover, gold letters read *Provincial Freight Alliance Agreement*.

Sun Haoran opened his copy and frowned at the dense pages. "Wait a second. They only hand this out ten minutes before the speech? How is anyone supposed to read fifty pages in ten minutes?"

"Nobody is meant to read the back pages," Chen Xuan said mildly, flipping straight past the glossy charts and welcome letters to the very last section. His finger paused lightly on a heading printed in small gray type: *Article 14: Unified Data and Clearing Services*.

"Article 14?" Sun Haoran leaned over and squinted at the tiny print. "That looks like boring computer rules. Half the chairmen here do not even read their own contracts past page three."

"That is exactly what the writers counted on," Chen Xuan murmured, resting his hand quietly on the open page.

At ten o'clock sharp, the hall lights dimmed slightly and the stage lights brightened.

Song Baisheng strode up to the podium wearing a freshly pressed dark suit and a broad, confident smile. Down at the side table reserved for invited guests, Li Weiming adjusted his silver-rimmed glasses and watched the crowd with quiet satisfaction, tapping his pen lightly against his notebook.

"Honored leaders, fellow chairmen, and friends from across the province," Song Baisheng began, his voice booming smoothly through the speakers. "For ten years, our provincial ports and trucking fleets have fought each other over routes, wasted fuel on empty return trips, and lost cargo to neighboring provinces. Today, that ends."

He gestured proudly toward the wide screen behind him, where colorful charts showed rising profit lines and glowing green shipping lanes.

"With the strong support of the provincial transport office, Wanfeng Freight has built the Provincial Freight Alliance," Song Baisheng declared. "Every member shares one unified scheduling platform. Customs clearance drops from two days to four hours. Empty truck runs fall by half. And best of all, the provincial bank has promised low-interest credit lines to every company that signs today."

In the front row, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang clapped slowly and nodded in clear approval. Seeing the deputy governor's smile, dozens of company chairmen immediately joined the applause, some already exchanging eager whispers about the bank loans.

Song Baisheng waited for the clapping to fade, his face glowing with triumph.

"Twenty major firms have already agreed to lead the way," he announced smoothly. "The television crews are ready to film the signing right before the morning break. Before we bring the pens onto the stage, let us keep things open and fair. Does anyone have a quick question before we sign?"

It was a routine line meant to draw polite silence, or at most a friendly word of praise from one of Song Baisheng's own partners.

A few chairmen glanced around, already reaching for their fountain pens. Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang checked his wristwatch, eager to wrap up the vote before coffee was served.

Then, far in the back corner by the side aisle, a hand rose quietly into the air.

Sun Haoran's heart nearly stopped. He grabbed Chen Xuan's sleeve with both hands, his eyes wide with pure panic.

"Chen Xuan! Put your hand down!" Sun Haoran hissed under his breath. "Are you out of your mind? This is not Qingyan! Sit still!"

Chen Xuan only gave Sun Haoran a reassuring smile, gently eased his sleeve free, and stood up.

A young usher standing near the aisle hurried over automatically and handed him a wireless microphone. Across the huge auditorium, three hundred heads turned toward the back corner.

They saw a plain young man in a clean, ironed white shirt, holding a microphone in one hand while a scratched green thermos sat neatly beside his seat.

On stage, Song Baisheng squinted into the bright lights, mildly annoyed at the interruption. "Yes, the gentleman in the back. Please state your company and keep it brief."

"Good morning, everyone," Chen Xuan said. His voice was calm, clear, and completely unhurried, carrying easily through the speakers. "My name is Chen Xuan. I am an ordinary clerk from Hengxin Logistics in Qingyan. I have three very simple questions about Article 14 at the back of this agreement."

At the front row, Wu Zhenbang's smile stiffened slightly. At the side table, Li Weiming's fingers froze over his notebook.

"First," Chen Xuan said politely, glancing down at the open page in his hand. "Article 14 states that every provincial port and trucking firm must send its daily cargo lists, ship arrival times, and warehouse stock numbers through one overseas clearing company. The address listed for that computer server is in Zurich. Why does a provincial freight alliance inside China need to send all of our coastal shipping schedules to Zurich?"

A quiet murmur rippled across the middle rows. Several port directors flipped rapidly to the back of their folders, peering at the small gray print they had not noticed a minute ago.

Chen Xuan did not pause for long. He raised a second finger calmly.

"Second," he continued in the same even tone. "That Zurich clearing company is listed on page forty-eight as Alpine Marine Data Services. According to public business records, that firm was opened only nine months ago in a rented mailbox office, with zero shipping branches in China and not a single Chinese board member. Why are we handing the live schedules of twenty ports to a nine-month-old mailbox company?"

Now the murmur grew much louder. Chairmen leaned over to whisper sharply to their neighbors. Sun Haoran stared up at Chen Xuan as if he had just watched a quiet clerk walk straight into a tiger cage carrying a teacup.

On stage, Song Baisheng's broad smile vanished completely. He gripped both sides of the wooden podium, his knuckles turning white.

Chen Xuan raised his third finger, his gaze steady and polite as he looked straight toward the stage.

"And third," Chen Xuan asked gently. "Who actually owns that Zurich company, and why does the fine print at the bottom of Article 14 state that Wanfeng Freight receives a 15 million yuan setup fee the very hour twenty companies sign this paper?"

When Chen Xuan lowered the microphone and sat calmly back down beside his green thermos, not a single person in the three-hundred-seat auditorium made a sound.
