# Chapter 131: Ding Yizhen's Backstage Threat

Loud applause inside the main hall of Shanshui Villa kept ringing after the host called a tea break.

Half the harbor directors across Handong Province stood around the center table, pointing at page 28 of the handbook. Thanks to Qin Yuhan reading Article 14 aloud on stage, not one shipping chairman had signed.

Qin Yuhan stepped out through the side doors into the east hallway to catch her breath.

Chen Xuan strolled behind her, carrying her cream coat over one arm and his green thermos in his other hand.

"Here," Chen Xuan said, pouring warm honey-lemon water into the lid. "Talking into a microphone dries the throat out. Drink this while it is warm."

Qin Yuhan let out a soft sigh, her shoulders relaxing as she took the lid. A bright smile touched her lips.

"Did you see Cai Chenggong's face when I read the line about Zurich?" she whispered, leaning closer. "His forehead turned the color of a boiled crab."

"My wife looked impressive up there," Chen Xuan said lazily, tucking a strand of hair behind her ear. "Sharp suit, steady voice, and not one wobble in those high heels."

Qin Yuhan rolled her eyes playfully and sipped the water. "You sat at the back table eating walnut cookies the whole time. I saw you."

"Someone had to test the kitchen," Chen Xuan replied with a grin. "Otherwise how would the President of Haiyue Group know which pastries are worth packing?"

Before Qin Yuhan could tease him back, footsteps echoed down the red carpet.

Deputy Mayor Ding Yizhen marched around the pillar with his jaw clenched, followed by Cai Chenggong.

The smile vanished from Qin Yuhan's face. She handed the lid to Chen Xuan and stood straight.

"President Qin," Ding Yizhen said, his voice low and angry as he stopped three paces away. "You enjoy putting on a loud show in someone else's city."

"Deputy Mayor Ding," Qin Yuhan replied calmly. "I only read a clause printed on page 28 of Mr. Cai's booklet. If Article 14 is sound policy, why were the port chairmen surprised?"

Cai Chenggong stepped forward, his face dark red as he pointed at her folder. "You are from Shanghai, President Qin! What gives Haiyue Group the right to stir up trouble in Jingzhou City? That Zurich clause is international cooperation!"

"Routing 80 billion yuan of Handong shipping manifests to a Swiss company before our customs officers see them is not cooperation, Mr. Cai," Qin Yuhan answered evenly. "It is a security leak."

Ding Yizhen raised a hand to silence Cai Chenggong, then stepped closer, narrowing his eyes.

"Young people in Lujiazui think a loud voice can change local rules," Ding Yizhen said coldly. "Haiyue Group has four warehouse permits waiting on my desk. If you do not walk back onto that stage after the break and apologize, not one truck with a Haiyue logo will pass a Jingzhou City tollgate for three years."

"Is that an official decision, Deputy Mayor Ding, or a personal threat?" Qin Yuhan asked, lifting her chin.

"Call it whatever you like," Cai Chenggong sneered. "Without Deputy Mayor Ding's stamp, your company cannot move one container off the docks!"

Ding Yizhen stepped closer, glaring down at Qin Yuhan.

Right then, a pair of canvas sneakers stepped lazily across the carpet.

Chen Xuan moved squarely in front of his wife, blocking Ding Yizhen and Cai Chenggong. He screwed the cap onto his green thermos, tucked it under his arm beside Qin Yuhan's coat, and looked at Ding Yizhen with an unbothered smile.

"Excuse me, big leadership," Chen Xuan said mildly. "You are standing a bit close. My wife does not like people shouting after she drank her warm water."

Cai Chenggong stared at Chen Xuan's jacket and laughed. "And who are you? The coat boy? Get out of the way before security throws you out!"

"I am her husband," Chen Xuan replied pleasantly, not moving an inch. "And screaming in a hallway is bad for blood pressure at your age, Mr. Cai. One more shout and that collar button might pop."

"You little clerk!" Cai Chenggong barked, raising his hand.

"Enough," Ding Yizhen snapped, glaring at Chen Xuan. "Step aside, young man. In Jingzhou City, my word decides which companies stay open."

"Does it really, Comrade Ding Yizhen?" a deep voice asked from the end of the corridor.

Ding Yizhen froze as if ice water had been poured down his collar.

Around the corner walked Handong Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin, flanked by Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang and Qin Yuhan's father, Qin Zhengming.

"Father!" Qin Yuhan said in surprise, stepping around Chen Xuan's shoulder.

Qin Zhengming gave his daughter a warm nod, then glanced at Chen Xuan standing firmly in front of her and smiled with approval.

Ding Yizhen's knees buckled. He bowed awkwardly. "Secretary Sha! Secretary Li! I did not know you were inspecting the venue today!"

Sha Ruijin stopped in front of Ding Yizhen, his gaze cold.

"I came to listen from the upper gallery, Ding Yizhen," Sha Ruijin said evenly. "And I heard every word you just said. Since when does a deputy mayor treat city permits like private clubs to threaten honest enterprises?"

Cold sweat broke out on Ding Yizhen's forehead. "Secretary Sha, it was a misunderstanding."

"Save it for the review board," Li Dakang cut in sharply. "Suspending the signing was my order ten minutes ago. Are you going to block my trucks at the tollgate too, Ding Yizhen?"

Ding Yizhen went pale and lowered his head without breathing a word. Cai Chenggong shrank back against the wall, trembling.

Sha Ruijin turned his back on both of them and looked warmly at Qin Yuhan.

"Miss Qin, your father raised a sharp daughter," Sha Ruijin said with a genuine smile. "Spotting Article 14 saved Handong Province from a serious mistake today. The provincial committee owes you our thanks."

"Thank you, Secretary Sha," Qin Yuhan said politely. "Actually, my husband Chen Xuan noticed the back pages while we had tea last night."

Sha Ruijin, Li Dakang, and Qin Zhengming all looked at the relaxed young man holding a coat and an old green thermos. Not one of them had any clue that this clerk was the unseen benefactor behind the midnight folder on Sha Ruijin's desk and the 300 trillion yuan shield protecting the nation.

"Good eye, young man," Sha Ruijin said kindly. "Reading the fine print is a fine habit for a clerk."

"Thank you, Secretary Sha," Chen Xuan said cheerfully, patting his thermos. "My wife works hard, so I just do the proof-reading during tea time."

Qin Zhengming chuckled and clapped Chen Xuan on the shoulder. "Come on, you two. Come have tea with me."
