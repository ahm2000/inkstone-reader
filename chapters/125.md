# Chapter 125: Calm Before Sha City

Three hundred miles north of Shanghai, the conference room inside the capital's central inspection building smelled of strong green tea and cigarette smoke.

Stacks of blue case folders rose along the wooden table.

"Every coastal customs office has signed off on the new audit rules," Ding Guoping reported, tapping a thick folder stamped in red ink. "Forty-six crooked officials and port brokers from Shanghai and neighboring harbors are already transferred to the high court for final sentencing. Over 800 million yuan in illegal kickbacks and hidden port fees have been frozen and returned to the public treasury."

Zhou Zhenhua blew a thin cloud of smoke toward the tall window and tapped his ash into the glass tray. "Not one department dragged its feet once the 40-department joint order went out. Even the provincial banking offices handed over their offshore transfer logs without a second phone call."

Gao Zhengyi struck a match, lit his cigarette, and took a slow draw. "The big nets in the capital and Shanghai are closed," he said evenly. "What about the one loose thread?"

Ding Guoping flipped to the last page. "Li Weiming. He slipped out of Shanghai right after his port brokers were arrested. Provincial partners picked up a tip that he drove north to Sha City, though nobody has spotted his car at any hotel."

"He will surface soon," Gao Zhengyi said calmly, watching the smoke curl upward. "Men backed by overseas money do not sit quietly in dark rooms. They always look for a legal door to sneak their hardware back into the port schedules. Keep the provincial court teams on standby."

Across the western edge of the capital, far from the smoke-filled room, the evening courtyard in the Western Hills was completely peaceful.

Elder Song sat on a stone bench beside the lotus pond, pouring hot tea from a small clay pot into two white cups. His personal secretary stood two paces behind him, repeating the exact message that had just arrived from Number 1's office.

"He said he will bring his own thermos when he has a free afternoon," the secretary finished carefully.

For a full second, Elder Song froze with the clay teapot in midair.

"Bring his own thermos," Elder Song repeated, shaking his head in pure delight as he set the pot down. "Half the ministers in this city would climb the hill in the rain just to sit at this stone table, and that boy tells me he prefers his own dented cup."

"Should I send another note asking for a set date, Elder Song?" the secretary asked.

"No need," Elder Song said warmly, lifting his teacup. "Let him finish his business trip in Sha City first. When the dust settles and he has a quiet afternoon, I will have the kettle boiling."

At that same hour in Sha City, bright floodlights shone across the polished floor of the Sha City International Convention Center.

Workers were finishing the last row of velvet tables along the stage.

At the center of the empty auditorium, Song Baisheng patted a leather signing folder tucked under his arm.

"Every detail is locked in," Song Baisheng boasted, lighting a cigarette despite the no-smoking sign near the back doors. "The committee moved my speech to ten o'clock tomorrow morning, right after the deputy governor's opening remarks. Twenty provincial freight companies have already confirmed their front-row seats."

"And the technical annex?" Li Weiming asked softly, his eyes sweeping the row of cameras along the balcony rail.

"Bound at the back of the alliance folder," Song Baisheng chuckled, blowing smoke upward. "Nobody sees a copy until five minutes before we call them onstage for the group photo. Once those twenty chairmen sign on the dotted line in front of provincial television, Wanfeng Freight becomes the legal data hub for the whole province. Tomorrow night, we flip the power switch on your three backup servers in the bonded warehouse, and Zurich gets its coastal schedules back."

Li Weiming's tight shoulders finally relaxed.

"Shanghai cost us two brokers and three tracking crews," Li Weiming murmured coldly. "Tomorrow morning, we take back the entire province without firing a shot."

Song Baisheng laughed loudly, clapping Li Weiming on the shoulder. "Get some sleep, Old Li. Tomorrow we make 15 million yuan look like pocket change."

Meanwhile, back in Shanghai, the evening sky outside Chen Xuan's fourth-floor window had turned a deep, quiet blue.

There were no floodlights, no banners, and no stacks of court folders. Inside his small rented room, Chen Xuan stood beside his narrow bed, packing calmly for his trip.

He folded two clean, freshly ironed white shirts into his worn dark blue canvas bag, tucked in two pairs of cotton socks, and slid the Hengxin Logistics shipping summary folder along the inner lining so the paper would not bend.

Next, he walked to the small counter. He rinsed his green thermos with boiling water, dropped in a pinch of jasmine leaves, and filled it before screwing the dented steel cap shut with a soft click.

On the table sat his second-class express train ticket to Sha City, a meal-allowance envelope from Sun Haoran, and one warm red-bean bun wrapped in brown paper from the bakery downstairs. Chen Xuan tucked the warm bun into the side pocket of his canvas bag, slid the ticket into his shirt pocket, and checked the window latch.

He switched off the ceiling bulb, stepped into the hallway, and locked his wooden door.

Forty minutes later, Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station hummed with the evening rush of travelers carrying suitcases and snacks. Near the Platform Four gate, Sun Haoran waved eagerly in his navy suit, holding two cups of station soy milk.

"Chen Xuan! Over here!" Sun Haoran called out cheerfully. "The gate opens in five minutes. Did you bring your notebook?"

"Right in my bag, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile, walking over calmly with his canvas bag over one shoulder and his green thermos in his hand.

When the whistle sounded, they joined the line walking onto the cool platform and boarded Carriage Six of the express train north.

Fifty meters down the platform, just before the doors slid shut, two quiet figures in plain dark coats stepped smoothly into Carriage Five without looking back.

Inside Carriage Six, Chen Xuan stowed his canvas bag on the rack, took his window seat beside the excited Sun Haoran, and set his green thermos on the small tray.

With a low hum, the express train pulled gently away from Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station, picking up speed as it curved north into the dark countryside toward Sha City.

Chen Xuan leaned back against the cloth headrest, unscrewed the cap of his green thermos, and took a slow sip of warm jasmine tea. Outside the wide window, the bright lights of Shanghai faded peacefully into the night, while the northern rails stretched straight ahead toward the storm waiting in Sha City.
