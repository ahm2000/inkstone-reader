# Chapter 105: Brass Nails on Old Street

On Monday afternoon in Yunxi County, Handong Province, clear autumn sunlight warmed the old stone pavement of Old Street. A light breeze swept golden maple leaves past the wooden storefronts, carrying the familiar scent of roasted sweet potatoes from the corner stall.

Inside Mingde Hardware, Chen Mingde was tying a neat bundle of copper plumbing pipes for a local carpenter when a shiny silver delivery van pulled up cleanly beside the curb. The driver's door swung open, and Wang Lei hopped down wearing a crisp dark blue work jacket with a wooden clipboard tucked under his arm.

"Afternoon, Second Uncle!" Wang Lei called out cheerfully, carrying two sturdy cartons of stainless steel door hinges into the shop. "Here is the afternoon restock from the Jingzhou wholesale depot. I picked out the thick-gauge hinges and four boxes of solid brass nails just like you asked."

Chen Mingde wiped his hands on his canvas apron and stepped over with a broad smile. "Right on schedule, Wang Lei. Put the brass nails by the front counter. Half the courtyard gates on West Lane need new latches before the winter wind arrives."

Wu Lanying stepped out from behind the wooden counter carrying a paper cup of hot chrysanthemum tea and handed it to the young driver. "Have a drink before you head back to the highway, Wang Lei. Your depot has been busy lately."

"Busier than ever, Second Aunt," Wang Lei said happily, taking a grateful sip. "Ever since the county market board stopped charging those random inspection fees and cleared the roadblock near the freight yard, small shops all over Yunxi are ordering fresh stock again. My delivery route doubled in two weeks."

Chen Mingde checked the printed invoice line by line, counted out crisp bills from the cash drawer, and signed Wang Lei's clipboard neatly. Just as the silver van pulled away from the curb, Old Zhou, a retired electrician from two alleys over, stepped through the doorway with a cloth tool bag slung over his shoulder.

"Mingde, weigh me out half a jin of your one-inch brass nails," Old Zhou said cheerfully. "My grandson's wooden bookshelves wobbled loose, and steel nails rust too fast in the autumn damp."

"Coming right up," Chen Mingde replied, scooping bright brass nails onto the hanging metal scale until the needle settled dead center. He wrapped them neatly in brown kraft paper, tied the bundle with cotton twine, and handed it across the counter.

After Old Zhou left, Wu Lanying leaned against the counter with a gentle smile. "By the way, Xuan called from Shanghai during his lunch break while you were in the back yard. He asked how your shoulder is feeling and reminded us to put the thicker quilts out at night."

Chen Mingde chuckled warmly as he stacked the new hinge boxes along the wall. "That boy always worries about us. Tell him my shoulder feels ten years younger, and he should focus on taking good care of his wife and enjoying his steady work in Shanghai. Our shop is running better than it has in a decade."

Two hours later, seventy kilometers away in Jingzhou City, late afternoon sunlight slanted warmly across the conference table of the Handong Provincial Party Committee office. Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin sat opposite Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping, two thick blue audit binders spread open between them.

"We finished tracing the final layer of Shanshui Group's local subsidiaries," Hou Liangping reported, turning a tabbed spreadsheet toward Sha Ruijin. "Our forensic accountants uncovered fourteen remaining shell companies registered under former managers and county middlemen. As of 3:30 PM today, the provincial court has frozen 380 million yuan in liquid accounts and six unencumbered commercial plots."

Sha Ruijin scanned the columns of verified figures and nodded steadily. "Good work, Liangping. How is the merchant restitution track progressing in Yunxi and Jingzhou?"

"Faster than we projected," Hou Liangping replied, opening the second binder. "The provincial High Court and county civil benches set up a dedicated verification panel last week. Every small merchant, contractor, and shopkeeper who suffered unlawful fines, forced lease cancellations, or confiscated inventory during Shanshui Group's land grabs can submit receipts directly to the county courthouse."

"How many claims have been cleared so far?" Sha Ruijin asked.

"Forty-seven cases verified in full, with another sixty under active review," Hou Liangping said cleanly. "The county courts are paying every approved claim directly out of the recovered Shanshui assets, plus statutory interest. Every payout is posted on the public court noticeboard so there is zero room for backroom favoritism or quiet deductions."

Sha Ruijin closed the first binder and rested his hand lightly on the cover. "That is the part that matters most, Liangping. Putting corrupt officials behind bars makes loud headlines for a week, and then people move on. What actually restores faith in the law is a county court returning stolen money to a small storefront owner without asking for connections or favors."

"The Yunxi County court clerk told me this morning that three shopkeepers on Old Street actually stayed after their hearing just to applaud the judge," Hou Liangping said with a quiet smile. "They could hardly believe the paperwork took less than five days, and two of them went straight back to expand their autumn stock orders."

"Good," Sha Ruijin said, rising to look out at the golden autumn trees lining the Jingzhou boulevard. "Let us keep that standard permanent across every county in the province. When a hardware merchant can order a box of brass nails, pay his fair taxes, and trust the court to protect his contract, Handong's economy will stand on solid rock."
