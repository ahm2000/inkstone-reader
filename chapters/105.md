# Chapter 105: Brass Nails on Old Street

On Monday afternoon in Yunxi County, Handong Province, the sun shone warmly over the stone pavement of Old Street.

Inside Mingde Hardware, Chen Mingde was tying up a bundle of copper plumbing pipes for a local carpenter when a brand-new silver delivery van pulled up cleanly outside the curb. The driver's door swung open, and Wang Lei hopped down wearing a neat dark blue work jacket with a clipboard under his arm.

"Afternoon, Second Uncle!" Wang Lei called out cheerfully, carrying two cartons of stainless steel door hinges into the shop. "Here is the afternoon restock from the Jingzhou wholesale depot. I picked out the thick-gauge hinges just like you asked."

Chen Mingde wiped his hands on a clean cloth and beamed at the young man. "Little Lei! Look at that new van out front. Business at your freight route is going that well?"

"Better than I ever dreamed, Second Uncle," Wang Lei grinned, setting the cartons beside the counter while Wu Lanying poured him a cup of hot tea. "Since the province cleaned out those crooked road checkpoints and highway extortion gangs in Jingzhou City last week, our delivery trucks save three hours on every trip. Two furniture factories in the east district signed monthly transport contracts with me yesterday."

"That is because you work hard and keep an honest ledger," Chen Mingde said warmly, patting Wang Lei's shoulder. "Remember what Chen Xuan told you before he took the train back to Shanghai. Do not rush to borrow big bank loans, pay your drivers on time, and drive safely."

"I keep Brother Xuan's words pinned right above my dashboard," Wang Lei laughed, taking a grateful sip of tea. "Wait until next month when the summer melon harvest starts. I am driving a full box of fresh Yunxi sweet melons straight to Shanghai for Brother Xuan and his wife!"

After Wang Lei waved goodbye and drove off down the lane, three men in plain dark trousers and simple white short-sleeved shirts came walking slowly along Old Street. They had left their car outside the west archway so they would not block the narrow road.

Walking in the center was Handong Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin. To his left walked Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang, and to his right walked Anti-Corruption Bureau Director Hou Liangping.

Down at Gong's Noodles three doors away, Number 23 was wiping an outdoor table with a clean towel. He glanced once at the three officials, gave a brief, silent nod of acknowledgment toward Sha Ruijin, and went right back to setting out chopstick holders.

Sha Ruijin paused in front of the freshly painted wooden signboard of Mingde Hardware, looking at the green lucky bamboo on the counter and the neatly arranged shelves inside. Together with Li Dakang and Hou Liangping, he stepped through the open doorway.

"Good afternoon, master," Sha Ruijin said pleasantly to Chen Mingde. "Do you have small half-inch brass nails for fixing a wooden study bookshelf?"

Chen Mingde looked up at the three visitors with a friendly shopkeeper's smile, having no idea that the polite older man in front of him was the top official of Handong Province. "We sure do! Half-inch solid brass, rust-proof and clean-headed. Wait right here."

Chen Mingde reached up to the third shelf, took down a small square cardboard box of brass nails, and opened the lid so they could inspect the golden nails inside. "Four yuan a box. If you only need a dozen for one bookshelf, I can weigh out a small paper packet for one yuan."

"We will take the whole box," Sha Ruijin smiled, handing over a crisp five-yuan bill. "How is business on Old Street these days? Does anyone from the county offices or the street patrol ever come by charging extra fees?"

"Not a single penny!" Chen Mingde said proudly, handing back a one-yuan coin in change. "Acting Director Wei Kun and the new county magistrate came by last week and cancelled all those crooked demolition notices. Now the street sweepers come at six every morning, the tax clerk is polite as can be, and folks from three neighborhoods over come here to buy tools and eat noodles. It feels like the good old days again."

Beside Sha Ruijin, Li Dakang let out a quiet breath of relief, while Hou Liangping smiled warmly.

"That is very good to hear," Sha Ruijin said, tucking the four-yuan box of brass nails into his pocket. "Keep up the good business, master."

As the three men walked back toward the west archway, Sha Ruijin spoke in a low, steady voice to Li Dakang and Hou Liangping. "A four-yuan box of brass nails and a peaceful street. That is the real test of our clean-up in Handong, Dakang. Not how many slogans we print in the Jingzhou papers, but whether an honest shopkeeper like Chen Mingde can open his shutter every morning without fear."

"I will make sure every district in Jingzhou remembers that, Secretary Sha," Li Dakang replied firmly.

That night at eight-thirty, back in Shanghai, Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan were sitting side by side on the balcony of Emerald Riverside Villa enjoying the cool evening breeze off the Huangpu River when Chen Xuan's phone rang.

Chen Xuan put the call on speaker. Immediately, Chen Mingde's cheerful, booming voice filled the quiet balcony.

"Little Xuan! Are you and Yuhan done with dinner? I just wanted to tell you that our shop had its best Monday all year! Little Lei came by in his new delivery van, and three very polite older customers even bought a box of brass nails and asked if the county was treating us well!"

Sitting close beside Chen Xuan on the wicker loveseat, Qin Yuhan leaned her head gently against his shoulder, listening to the simple, happy warmth in his uncle's voice as the lights of Lujiazui sparkled across the river.

"I told you everything would be fine on Old Street, Second Uncle," Chen Xuan smiled, wrapping an arm around his wife's shoulders. "Make sure you lock up before nine and let Second Aunt soak her feet in warm water."

When the call ended, Qin Yuhan looked up at Chen Xuan with soft, bright eyes. "Your second uncle sounds so happy."

"He is a simple man," Chen Xuan chuckled, picking up his green thermos from the balcony table. "Give him an open shop and a quiet street, and he thinks he owns the world."
