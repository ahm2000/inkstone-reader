# Chapter 105: Brass Nails on Old Street

On Monday afternoon in Yunxi County, Handong Province, clear autumn sunlight warmed the old stone pavement of Old Street. A light breeze swept golden maple leaves past the wooden storefronts, carrying the familiar scent of roasted sweet potatoes from the corner stall.

Inside Mingde Hardware, Chen Mingde was tying a neat bundle of copper plumbing pipes for a local carpenter when a shiny silver delivery van pulled up cleanly beside the curb. The driver's door swung open, and Wang Lei hopped down wearing a crisp dark blue work jacket with a wooden clipboard tucked under his arm.

"Afternoon, Second Uncle!" Wang Lei called out cheerfully, carrying two sturdy cartons of stainless steel door hinges into the shop. "Here is the afternoon restock from the Jingzhou wholesale depot. I picked out the thick-gauge hinges just like you asked."

Chen Mingde wiped his hands on a clean cotton cloth and beamed at the young man. "Little Lei! Look at that brand-new van out front. Business on your county freight route is going that well?"

"Better than I ever dreamed, Second Uncle," Wang Lei grinned, setting the cartons neatly behind the counter. "Ever since those corrupt checkpoint bullies and local loan sharks got cleared out of the county, small factories and hardware shops are ordering twice as much stock. Nobody dares skim a single yuan off our shipping invoices anymore."

Right outside the open doorway, Acting Director Wei Kun of the Old Street Police Station walked past on his afternoon foot patrol, his uniform neat and his stride unhurried. He paused briefly to smile and nod at Chen Mingde and Wang Lei before continuing down the lane to chat with an elderly vegetable vendor.

"Look at Officer Wei," Chen Mingde murmured with quiet pride, pulling open a polished wooden drawer filled with gleaming brass nails and sorting them by size. "Honest officials, clean streets, and fair trade. Old Street has not felt this peaceful in twenty years."

Wang Lei leaned against the counter, smiling curiously. "By the way, Second Uncle, have you heard from Cousin Xuan in Shanghai lately? How is his new married life?"

Chen Mingde's weathered face softened immediately. "He called me on Sunday morning. He and Yuhan are getting along wonderfully. He says he still works his quiet office job at the logistics company, clocks out right on time, and cooks dinner for his wife every evening. A steady job and a warm home. That is all I ever hoped for him."

"Cousin Xuan always had the calmest temper among all of us," Wang Lei laughed, signing off on his delivery slip. "Tell him I said hello next time he calls. I have three more stops before sundown!"

Three hours away by highway, in the bright glass conference room of the Handong Provincial Party Committee in Jingzhou City, autumn sunlight slanted across a long mahogany table covered with neatly bound economic reports.

Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin set down his fountain pen and looked across the table at Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang and Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping.

"The third-quarter figures are confirmed," Sha Ruijin said, his voice steady and deeply satisfied. "Since the arrests of Zhao Dehan, Zhao Ruilong, Gao Yuliang, and the rest of the Shanshui network, Handong's private business registrations have risen by eighteen percent. Counties like Yunxi have seen small commerce bounce back faster than anyone predicted."

Li Dakang tapped his knuckles lightly against the clean blue folder in front of him, his sharp eyes gleaming with energy. "When you clear the rotten timber out of the river, the water flows on its own. Our municipal banks have already released 300 million in clean credit lines to local factories and freight operators. Not a single yuan is leaking into shadow accounts."

Hou Liangping nodded solemnly, closing his case summary. "Every remaining branch of the old Handa Faction has been audited and transferred to the courts. Public complaint letters in Jingzhou have dropped to a five-year low."

For a brief moment, the conference room fell quiet. All three men knew how close Handong had come to a financial and political storm months ago before the decisive evidence arrived and Beijing stepped in with iron clarity.

"Secretary Sha," Li Dakang said quietly, lowering his voice out of instinct. "To this day, nobody in the province knows who first triggered that cleanup. Every lead stopped at that unrecorded black seal."

Hou Liangping glanced toward the window. "Beijing's instruction was clear from the very first day. Honor the law, protect ordinary citizens, and never pry into the holder of the Black Order."

Sha Ruijin stood up and walked over to the tall window overlooking the golden autumn trees of Jingzhou City. His expression was calm and deeply respectful.

"Whoever that anonymous benefactor is," Sha Ruijin said softly, "they never asked for credit, rank, or a single word of praise. They only wanted clean streets, honest courts, and ordinary families able to earn a living in peace. The best way we can repay that trust is to make sure Handong stays clean."

Li Dakang and Hou Liangping nodded in silent agreement. Neither they nor anyone else in the provincial halls had the faintest idea that the figure they spoke of with such awe was sitting hundreds of kilometers away at an ordinary office desk.

Back in Shanghai, on the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics, the afternoon sun cast a warm rectangle of light across Chen Xuan's plain desk. His plain secondary phone vibrated once inside his pocket.

Chen Xuan pulled it out and glanced at a single encrypted line from Number 1: "Young Master, Handong's autumn audit is complete. Old Street is peaceful, and Mingde Hardware's afternoon deliveries arrived on schedule."

Chen Xuan read the line with a faint, satisfied smile and tapped delete. He unscrewed the cap of his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos, took a slow sip of warm jasmine tea, and glanced up at the digital wall clock ticking smoothly toward 4:55 PM.

Another quiet, ordinary autumn day was almost done.
