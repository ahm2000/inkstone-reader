# Chapter 105: Sha Ruijin Visits Old Street

At nine o'clock on a crisp Wednesday morning, a plain black sedan with ordinary provincial plates pulled up outside Old Street Police Station in Yunxi County. There were no escort lights, no banner, and no advance call to the county government.

Handong Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin stepped out in a dark blue windbreaker over a white shirt, looking more like a retired school principal than the province's top leader. His secretary, Lin Bo, followed with a black notebook.

Inside the tidy hall, two young officers were writing morning patrol logs behind the counter. Acting Director Wei Kun stepped from the back office carrying resident forms and froze mid-step. He recognized the face from provincial news in a heartbeat.

"Secretary Sha!" Wei Kun blurted, snapping a crisp salute so fast his folder nearly slipped. "I am Wei Kun, Acting Director of Old Street Station. We had no notice of your inspection!"

"If I gave notice, the street would be swept three times and everyone would stand in stiff rows," Sha Ruijin said with an easy smile. He shook Wei Kun's hand firmly. "Keep working. Let me see your duty logbook first."

Ten minutes later, while Sha Ruijin chatted with the young officers about night patrols, two black county cars braked hard outside. Yunxi County Party Secretary Xu Guoxiang and County Mayor Deng Weimin hurried in out of breath, pale and sweating. Someone from provincial traffic had spotted Sha Ruijin's plate too late.

"Secretary Sha! We are so sorry we were late!" Xu Guoxiang stammered, wiping his forehead. "We prepared the county conference room and our quarterly report at the county building."

Sha Ruijin set the neat logbook on the counter and turned calmly toward them.

"I did not drive two hours from the provincial capital to sit in a soft chair and hear a paper report, Xu Guoxiang," Sha Ruijin said evenly. "I came to see the one police station in Yunxi County that kept its spine straight when things went dark this past weekend."

He placed a firm hand on Wei Kun's shoulder in front of both county leaders and the staff.

"Wei Kun," Sha Ruijin said with genuine respect. "I read the provincial review on Yunxi County. When corrupt superiors tried to frame an honest Old Street shopkeeper and pressure your station into signing fake arrest papers, you refused to bend. You protected your beat when it was easier to look away."

Wei Kun's ears flushed, his posture straight as a pine. "Secretary Sha, that was only my basic duty. I could not let an innocent man be dragged away on my street."

"Basic duty is easy to preach in meetings and hard to keep when a crooked superior shouts in your face," Sha Ruijin said warmly. His smile faded into crisp authority as he looked at Xu Guoxiang and Deng Weimin. "Yunxi County needs more officers like Wei Kun who remember whom their badge serves. And Xu Guoxiang, why does his nameplate still say Acting Director?"

Xu Guoxiang's throat went dry. "We are finishing the formal papers right now, Secretary Sha! The county committee will make Director Wei Kun's full rank official this very week!"

"Do it properly and by the book," Sha Ruijin said, waving toward the door. "Now both of you go back to your offices and tend to your work. Do not trail behind me. I am going to walk down Old Street alone for twenty minutes."

"Secretary Sha, let me at least walk with you to introduce the street," Deng Weimin offered nervously.

"If you walk beside me in a dark suit with three assistants, nobody on Old Street will tell me the truth," Sha Ruijin replied plainly. "Go back to work. Lin Bo, wait by the car."

Leaving the stunned officials and his secretary at the gate, Sha Ruijin tucked his hands into his pockets and strolled into the morning sunlight of Old Street.

The brick lane smelled of steamed buns and sesame oil, shop shutters open under the sycamore trees. Fifty meters down, a dark blue awning hung over Mingde Hardware. Outside sat neighbor Yu Changshun with a chipped enamel mug of tea. Inside, Chen Mingde sorted brass screws behind the counter while Wu Lanying swept the doorstep.

Sha Ruijin stepped across the threshold like any older customer.

"Good morning, shopkeeper," Sha Ruijin said with a friendly smile. "Do you have a small box of two-inch brass nails?"

Chen Mingde looked up, his weathered face crinkling warmly, not recognizing the man at all. "Of course! Solid two-inch brass nails that never bend in hardwood. Hold on."

Chen Mingde pulled a red-and-white box from the rack, popped the lid to show the shiny brass heads, and set it on the counter. "Twelve yuan. Fixing a wooden cabinet?"

"An old bookshelf," Sha Ruijin said easily, handing over a twenty-yuan note. "One shelf came loose last week."

"Take these four brass washers too, no charge," Chen Mingde said kindly, dropping four metal rings into the box. "Put them under the corner brackets and that shelf will hold another twenty years."

Wu Lanying stepped inside and leaned her broom against the wall. "Rest a minute if you walked far, sir. We just boiled fresh water for tea."

"Thank you kindly, ma'am, just out for a short walk," Sha Ruijin said warmly. He took his eight yuan in change and glanced around the peaceful shop. "Lively street. Does anyone ever give you folks trouble down here anymore?"

Chen Mingde laughed, wiping the counter. "Not a bit! A while back we had a rough patch, to be honest, but the government cleaned those bad apples right out. Now the officers at Old Street Station are honest men, the neighbors look out for each other, and we sleep soundly every night."

"Good to hear," Sha Ruijin said softly, relief settling in his chest. "And your family? All well?"

"Couldn't be better," Wu Lanying beamed. "The shop is humming, my brother-in-law and sister-in-law live up the lane, and our nephew Chen Xuan went safely back to his ordinary office job in Shanghai. Honest work and warm meals on the table. What more could old folks ask for?"

Sha Ruijin looked at their peaceful smiles, closed his fingers around the box of brass nails, and slipped it into his pocket.

"Nothing more at all," Sha Ruijin said with a heartfelt smile. "Take good care of your health, both of you."

"You too, sir! Come back anytime!" Chen Mingde called cheerfully.

Sha Ruijin stepped back onto the cobblestones and walked slowly toward the lane's mouth. He had not given his name, and he never would. He had seen what mattered most: the small hardware shop was open, Old Street was peaceful, and the shopkeeper was truly safe and happy.
