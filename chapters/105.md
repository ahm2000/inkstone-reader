# Chapter 105: Zhao Dehai Visits Old Street

At nine o'clock on a crisp Wednesday morning, a plain black sedan with ordinary provincial plates pulled to the curb outside Old Street Police Station in Yunxi County.

There were no flashing escort lights, no welcome banner, and no advance call to the county government building.

Lanshui Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai stepped out onto the sidewalk in a simple dark blue windbreaker over a white shirt, looking more like a retired school principal than the top leader of the province. His secretary, Lin Bo, followed two steps behind with a black notebook.

Inside the tidy hall of Old Street Station, two young officers were filling out morning patrol logs behind the counter. Acting Director Wei Kun stepped out of the back office carrying resident registration forms and froze mid-step.

He recognized the face from provincial news in a single heartbeat.

"Secretary Zhao!" Wei Kun blurted out, snapping a crisp salute so fast his folder nearly slipped. "I am Wei Kun, Acting Director of Old Street Station. We had no notice of your inspection!"

"If I gave notice, the street would be swept three times and everyone would be standing in stiff rows," Zhao Dehai said with an easy smile. He stepped forward and shook Wei Kun's hand firmly. "Keep working. Let me look at your duty logbook first."

Ten minutes later, while Zhao Dehai was chatting with the two young officers about night patrols on Old Street, two black county cars braked hard outside the gate.

Yunxi County Party Secretary Xu Guoxiang and County Mayor Deng Weimin hurried through the glass doors out of breath, their faces pale and beaded with cold sweat. Someone from the provincial traffic office had spotted Zhao Dehai's car plate too late.

"Secretary Zhao! We are so sorry we were late!" Xu Guoxiang stammered, wiping his forehead. "We prepared the county conference room and our quarterly report right at the county building."

Zhao Dehai set the neat station logbook back on the counter and turned calmly toward the flustered county leaders.

"I did not drive two hours from the provincial capital to sit in a soft chair and listen to a paper report, Xu Guoxiang," Zhao Dehai said evenly. "I came to see the one police station in Yunxi County that kept its spine straight when things went dark last month."

He turned and placed a firm, approving hand on Wei Kun's shoulder in front of both county leaders and the station staff.

"Wei Kun," Zhao Dehai said clearly, his voice full of genuine respect. "I read the full provincial review on Yunxi County. When corrupt superiors tried to frame an honest Old Street shopkeeper and pressure your station into signing fake arrest papers, you refused to bend. You protected your beat when it would have been much easier to look away."

Wei Kun's ears flushed slightly, his posture straight as a pine. "Secretary Zhao, that was only my basic duty. I could not let an innocent man be dragged away on my street."

"Basic duty is easy to preach in meetings and hard to keep when a crooked superior shouts in your face," Zhao Dehai said warmly. He looked straight at Xu Guoxiang and Deng Weimin, his smile fading into crisp authority. "Yunxi County needs more officers like Wei Kun who remember whom their badge serves. And Xu Guoxiang, why does his nameplate still say Acting Director?"

Xu Guoxiang's throat went dry. "We are finishing the formal papers right now, Secretary Zhao! The county committee will make Director Wei Kun's full rank official this very week, without a day of delay!"

"Do it properly and by the book," Zhao Dehai said calmly, waving toward the door. "Now both of you go back to your offices and tend to your work. Do not trail behind me. I am going to walk down Old Street alone for twenty minutes."

"Secretary Zhao, let me at least walk with you to introduce the street," Deng Weimin offered nervously.

"If you walk beside me in a dark suit with three assistants, nobody on Old Street will tell me the truth," Zhao Dehai replied plainly. "Go back to work. Lin Bo, wait by the car."

Leaving both stunned county officials and his own secretary at the gate, Zhao Dehai tucked his hands into his windbreaker pockets and strolled alone into the warm sunlight of Old Street.

The brick lane smelled of steamed buns and roasted sesame oil. Shop shutters stood wide open under the sycamore trees, and neighbors chatted warmly across the lane.

Fifty meters down, a fresh dark blue canvas awning hung neatly over Mingde Hardware. Outside on a wooden stool sat neighbor Yu Changshun with a chipped enamel mug of tea. Inside the bright shop, Chen Mingde stood behind the worn counter sorting brass screws while Wu Lanying swept the doorstep.

Zhao Dehai stepped across the wooden threshold like any ordinary older customer.

"Good morning, shopkeeper," Zhao Dehai said with a friendly smile. "Do you have a small box of two-inch brass nails?"

Chen Mingde looked up at once, his weathered face crinkling with warmth. He did not recognize the man in the plain windbreaker at all. "Of course! Good solid two-inch brass nails that never bend in hardwood. Hold on."

Chen Mingde reached to the wooden rack behind him, took down a neat red-and-white box, popped the lid so Zhao Dehai could see the shiny brass heads, and set it on the counter. "Twelve yuan for the box. Fixing a wooden cabinet?"

"An old bookshelf," Zhao Dehai said easily, handing over a twenty-yuan note. "One shelf came loose last week."

"Take these four brass washers too, no charge," Chen Mingde said kindly, dropping four tiny metal rings into the box. "Put them under the corner brackets and that shelf will hold another twenty years."

Wu Lanying stepped inside with a warm smile and leaned her broom against the wall. "Rest a minute if you have been walking far, sir. We just boiled fresh water for tea."

"Thank you kindly, ma'am, I am just out for a short walk," Zhao Dehai said warmly. He took his eight yuan in change and glanced around the peaceful shop. "This is a lively street. Does anyone ever give you folks trouble down here anymore?"

Chen Mingde laughed easily, wiping the counter with a cotton cloth. "Not a bit! Not anymore. A while back we had a rough patch, to be honest, but the government cleaned those bad apples right out. Now the officers at Old Street Station are honest men, the neighbors look out for each other, and we sleep soundly every night."

"Good to hear," Zhao Dehai said softly, deep relief settling in his chest. "And your family? All well?"

"Couldn't be better," Wu Lanying beamed. "The shop is humming, my brother-in-law and sister-in-law live just up the lane, and our nephew Chen Xuan went safely back to his ordinary office job in Qingyan. Honest work and warm meals on the table. What more could old folks ask for?"

Zhao Dehai looked at the peaceful smiles on both their faces. He closed his fingers gently around the little cardboard box of brass nails and slipped it into his pocket.

"Nothing more at all," Zhao Dehai said with a quiet, heartfelt smile. "Take good care of your health, both of you."

"You too, sir! Come back anytime!" Chen Mingde called cheerfully.

Zhao Dehai stepped back onto the sunlit cobblestones and walked slowly toward the mouth of the lane. He had not said his name, and he never would. He had seen with his own eyes what mattered most: the small hardware shop was open, Old Street was peaceful, and the shopkeeper was truly safe and happy.
