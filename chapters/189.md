# Chapter 189: White Guard Moves North

By seven o'clock on Monday evening, December chill had settled gently over Puxi.

Inside his small rented fifth-floor apartment, Chen Xuan boiled a simple bowl of tomato-and-egg noodles, ate unhurriedly at the folding wooden table, washed his dish, and brewed a mug of hot green tea.

With the warm mug in his left hand, he slid open the glass door and stepped onto his narrow concrete balcony.

A crisp evening breeze brushed his face. Across the quiet compound, warm yellow lights glowed behind curtained windows, while the distant neon of Nanjing Road tinted the dark clouds soft amber. Farther east, the bright tip of the Oriental Pearl Tower rose above the skyline.

Five floors below, near the parked bicycles by the courtyard gate, a Black Guard member in a plain matte-black jacket stood casually with hands in his pockets, staying quietly within fifty meters of the building.

Chen Xuan rested his forearm on the cool metal railing, took a sip of tea, and pulled a slim matte-white phone from his windbreaker pocket. He pressed a single speed-dial key.

The line connected on the first ring.

"Sir," Number 1's steady, respectful voice answered clearly.

"Number 1," Chen Xuan said calmly as he watched distant Shanghai traffic glide past. "Are you still at Tomson Riviera?"

"Yes, Sir. Number 11 and I are finishing the final ledger review from Shenzhen. Pier Nine is clean, and the Four National Banks have locked every southern account tied to the Seven-Harbor Consortium."

"Good," Chen Xuan replied, unfolding the tiny sticky note against the railing. "Now open page fourteen of Number 11's northern file. Look up Beishan Timber and Cold-Chain Trading Co., Ltd."

A crisp rustle of paper sounded on the other end, followed by two seconds of silence.

"I have it right here, Sir," Number 1 said, his voice turning sharper immediately. "Beishan Timber and Cold-Chain Trading Co., Ltd. Registered ten years ago in Jilin Province as a dormant northern logging and cold-storage shell under Seven-Harbor's Hong Kong umbrella. Why do you ask?"

"Because their winter shipping order just landed on my desk at Hengxin Logistics three hours ago," Chen Xuan said evenly.

Number 1 paused in genuine surprise before letting out a quiet exhale. "They routed their freight through Hengxin Logistics? Right across your own desk?"

"Six heavy flatbed trucks leaving Shanghai Port's Yangshan yard at five tomorrow morning," Chen Xuan continued smoothly. "Listed as ordinary forestry road gear. Inside the crates are six German deep-bore mining drills, four minus-forty diesel generators, twenty tons of steel casing, and high-pressure chemical refining valves. Paid at triple rush rates from a Hong Kong account, heading straight north toward Changbai Mountain."

Rapid keyboard clicks sounded in the background as Number 1 checked White Order's northern shipping alerts.

"Sir, that connects everything," Number 1 said grimly ten seconds later. "Ever since Shenzhen collapsed and China Construction Bank, ICBC, and Huaxia Bank froze their southern lines, our northern watchers noticed three Hong Kong shell firms buying up winter diesel fuel and heavy haulage near Jilin. Until now, we did not know where they were sending it."

"Now we do," Chen Xuan said softly, watching white steam rise from his mug into the cold air.

"Should our Shanghai team seize all six flatbeds before they clear the Yangshan gate at dawn?" Number 1 asked crisply.

"No," Chen Xuan answered without hesitation. "I already stamped their Hengxin transit papers clean. If we grab six hired drivers at Shanghai Port, the people hiding on Changbai Mountain will smell smoke before noon. They will torch their files, dump their stockpile, and slip across the northern border before we even find the site."

"Understood, Sir," Number 1 replied immediately. "Let the trucks lead us straight to their front door."

"Send White Guard's northern branch tonight," Chen Xuan instructed, his voice calm and firm. "Do not wait for the trucks to reach Jilin. Put our people inside the foothills of Changbai Mountain ahead of the convoy. Find their drop point, map the whole valley, count every guard, and make sure not a single crate crosses the border without my word."

"Right away, Sir," Number 1 answered cleanly. "Number 12 and Number 44 are stationed at our northern staging base three hours west of Changbai Mountain with full winter equipment. I will send them out tonight. They will report the moment they locate the valley."

"Tell them to stay unseen," Chen Xuan added quietly. "If Seven-Harbor is rushing heavy drills and refining valves up a frozen mountain in December, they will not be using ordinary local watchmen."

"Yes, Sir. I will warn Number 12 personally."

Chen Xuan ended the call, tucked the matte-white phone away, and finished his warm tea. He listened for a moment to a neighbor's child practicing piano two floors below, then stepped back inside and locked the balcony door.

More than two thousand kilometers to the northeast, winter was another world entirely.

By midnight on Tuesday, thick snow was falling across the dark pine ridges bordering Changbai Mountain (Jhengbai). Howling gusts swept the empty mountain highway, pushing the temperature down to minus twenty-two degrees.

Inside a heated concrete garage tucked behind an old forestry depot, two matte-white heavy snow SUVs idled smoothly in the freezing air. Thick steel snow chains wrapped tightly around all four oversized tires of each vehicle.

Number 12, tall and broad-shouldered with calm, steady eyes, zipped up his thick matte-white winter parka and checked the straps holding three white-sealed gear crates in the rear bay. Beside him, Number 44, leaner and sharper-featured, tapped a fresh route map onto a matte-white rugged tablet and clipped a cold-weather radio to his shoulder.

"Number 1 just forwarded the exact Hengxin Logistics plate numbers from Shanghai Port," Number 44 said, glancing up from the glowing screen. "All six flatbeds cleared the Yangshan gate on schedule at five this morning. Even with rotating drivers, icy roads in Liaoning will keep them out until late Wednesday night."

"That gives us twenty-four hours ahead of them," Number 12 replied evenly, tossing thermal gloves onto the passenger seat. "How does the old Beishan Timber road look on the satellite map?"

"Half-buried under snowdrifts, except for one stretch," Number 44 pointed a gloved finger at a narrow white line winding deep into the northern slope of Changbai Mountain. "Twelve kilometers off the public highway, someone cleared an abandoned logging road with heavy snowplows three days ago. No tourist camp, no forestry station, and it runs straight into a deep bowl-shaped valley ten kilometers from the border ridge."

Number 12's eyes sharpened instantly. "A hidden valley right next to the border, plowed clean in December."

"And Number 1 told us to expect armed outsiders," Number 44 added, checking the white-sealed thermal binoculars and climbing ropes beside his seat. "No noise, no contact. Map the whole valley, find out what they are hiding, and send everything back to Shanghai."

Number 12 nodded once and climbed into the driver's seat. "Let us go before the storm gets heavier."

He pressed the remote switch on the dashboard. The heavy steel garage door rolled upward, letting stinging white snow blow across the floor.

Bright headlights cut through the swirling dark. One after another, the two matte-white snow SUVs pulled smoothly out onto the frozen highway and vanished into the towering pines of Changbai Mountain.
