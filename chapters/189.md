# Chapter 189: White Guard Moves North

By 6:40 PM, the dining room of Emerald Riverside Villa was filled with the rich, comforting aroma of simmering lamb bone broth, ginger slices, and red dates. Outside the tall patio doors, the December night wind brushed across the bare garden hedges, making the warm glow inside feel twice as inviting.

On his way home from Hengxin Logistics, Chen Xuan had stopped by the small neighborhood market two blocks from the compound. The butcher uncle behind the meat counter had greeted him like a favorite nephew, slicing paper-thin rolls of fresh lamb shoulder and tossing a crisp bundle of green onions into his bag for free while joking about how skinny young office workers looked in winter. Around the compound gate, an elderly security guard had chatted briefly about the dropping temperature before waving him through with a grin. Everything around him was warm, lively, and completely natural.

Inside the villa, Qin Yuhan sat across the dining table in a soft cream cashmere sweater, her hair tied up loosely with a wooden clip. She dipped a slice of lamb into the bubbling copper pot, swirled it in sesame sauce, and sighed happily.

"You really spoiled me with this," she said, smiling across the rising steam. "After three hours of budget charts this afternoon, I thought my brain was frozen solid."

"Eat more cabbage and tofu too," Chen Xuan said, placing a scoop of tender mushrooms into her bowl. "Otherwise you will blame me tomorrow when you wake up thirsty."

"Yes, Chef Chen," she teased, clinking her glass of warm plum juice against his.

After dinner, they cleared the table together in easy, unhurried silence. Qin Yuhan headed upstairs to take a hot bath and glance over a brief board memo before bed. Chen Xuan brewed a mug of warm water, stepped out onto the quiet back terrace around 8:30 PM, and leaned lightly against the stone railing to breathe in the crisp winter air.

A moment later, his phone vibrated once in his coat pocket. He pulled it out and answered calmly.

"Young Master," Number 1's steady, respectful voice came through the line. "We traced the northern freight manifest you sent over this afternoon."

"Quick work," Chen Xuan said quietly, watching his breath curl faintly into the cold air. "What did you find?"

"The Hong Kong clearing brokerage on the settlement sheet is a shell registered under a nominee director in Kowloon," Number 1 reported cleanly. "Three layers up, its capital flows connect directly to Kou Zhenshan's northern mining network. The fourteen crates cleared the Shenyang transfer yard at 5:10 PM Beijing (+8:00 GMT) and were loaded onto two heavy-duty snow flatbeds."

Chen Xuan took a slow sip from his mug. "Where are they headed?"

"East past Baishan, straight onto a decommissioned forestry road running into the deep ridges of Changbai Mountain," Number 1 replied. "There is no active timber operation registered in that sector. Even more interesting, our financial monitor picked up an encrypted settlement confirmation routed through a Zurich (+1:00 GMT) clearing relay earlier this afternoon, matching the exact tonnage of those chemical reagents."

Chen Xuan's eyes narrowed slightly. A deep-mountain gold refinery in Jilin was already serious enough, but a Zurich (+1:00 GMT) settlement relay meant foreign handlers were directly tied into the extraction chain, pulling unrecorded gold straight out of the northeast and laundering the value offshore.

"Who is closest in the northeast?" Chen Xuan asked evenly.

"Number 12 and Number 44 were already stationed on routine rotation near Changchun, Young Master," Number 1 answered immediately. "They boarded an evening express south to Baishan an hour ago and arranged a local four-wheel-drive truck through regular civilian channels. They are dressed in plain alpine winter gear and carrying wildlife photography equipment so they blend right in at local forestry checkpoints."

"Good," Chen Xuan said. "Tell Number 12 and Number 44 not to engage anyone and not to alert local county departments yet. If Kou Zhenshan has foreign personnel on site, there may be hired security around the perimeter. I want clean eyes on the valley, clear photographs of whoever is running the refinery, and exact coordinates."

"Understood, Young Master," Number 1 said firmly. "The snowstorm up on the Changbai ridges is expected to deepen over the next two days, with night temperatures dropping below minus 25 degrees. Number 12 and Number 44 will reach the outer ridgeline by tomorrow night and transmit full visual confirmation the moment they have a clean line of sight."

"Tell them to stay warm and keep their distance," Chen Xuan added calmly. "No unnecessary risks."

"I will pass your word directly, Young Master," Number 1 replied.

The call ended cleanly. Chen Xuan slipped his phone back into his pocket and stood for another quiet minute, looking out toward the distant lights of the Huangpu River shimmering through the winter haze.

Inside the house, the upstairs hallway light brightened. Qin Yuhan leaned over the wooden banister in her fluffy bathrobe, her damp hair wrapped in a towel.

"Xuan, why are you standing out in the cold?" she called down softly. "Come back in. I found that old Hong Kong comedy you wanted to watch."

"Coming right up," Chen Xuan called back with a warm smile.

He slid the glass terrace door shut, locked it neatly, and walked upstairs toward the glow of the living room, leaving the frozen northern ridges to wait until tomorrow night.
