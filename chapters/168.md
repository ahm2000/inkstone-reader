# Chapter 168: Afternoon at Shekou Dock

Far south along the Pearl River estuary, Shekou Dock smelled of salt water, warm diesel, and freshly steamed pork buns. Overhead cranes swung slowly across the container yard while a steady sea breeze carried the clatter of loading ramps across the berths.

Chen Xuan strolled past rows of stacked blue and red shipping containers at a leisurely pace that suggested he had nowhere to be for the rest of the week. Wearing a faded olive cotton jacket, dark casual pants, and comfortable sneakers, he tucked a cheap plastic clipboard holding Hengxin Logistics' routine freight slip under his left arm while swinging his dented green metal thermos.

"Container HX-4092, seal intact," Chen Xuan murmured to himself, squinting at the yellow steel lock on berth four. He scribbled a neat check mark on the paper, unscrewed his thermos lid, and took a slow sip of warm jasmine tea. "Three containers of Shanghai circuit boards. Everything matches. That counts as a full afternoon of hard work."

"Hey, young brother! You are moving slower than the tide today!"

A hearty laugh rolled across the concrete apron. Uncle Zhao, a sun-tanned forklift operator in his late fifties wearing a faded blue work vest and a cloth cap, hopped down from his yellow machine and ambled over with an easy grin. Behind him came Xiao Wu, a slender tallyman in his twenties with a pen tucked behind his ear.

"Uncle Zhao, good afternoon," Chen Xuan smiled warmly, lifting his thermos in greeting. "You cannot rush quality inspection. If I finish in ten minutes, Manager Hu back at Hengxin in Shanghai will think I skipped the dock and went straight to drink milk tea."

"Smart lad," Uncle Zhao chuckled, patting Chen Xuan on the shoulder like a favorite nephew. "Never let the office folks know how fast you can work, or they will pile twice as many slips on your desk. Wait a second, let me grab Auntie Lin."

A few yards away by the yard gate, a plump, cheerful woman in a clean apron pushed a small stainless-steel food cart. "Xiao Chen!" Auntie Lin called out, waving a pair of tongs. "I saw you wandering past berth four half an hour ago. Did you even eat lunch? Here, take two roast pork buns before they get cold!"

"Auntie Lin, you are spoiling me," Chen Xuan laughed, happily accepting the warm paper bag. "Let me pay you."

"Pay me what? Two buns cost four yuan!" Auntie Lin waved her hand dismissively, her round face crinkling with genuine warmth. "You look thin. Your wife works so hard in those tall office towers, so you have to keep your strength up if you are cooking dinner for her. By the way, if you make braised lamb this weekend, toss in two pieces of dried tangerine peel from Xinhui. Cuts the richness right out."

"Good tip," Chen Xuan said, biting into a fluffy bun and nodding with real appreciation. "I will try that when we get back to Shanghai."

Xiao Wu leaned casually against a wooden pallet, chewing on a toothpick. "Brother Chen, just a heads-up since you are checking Shanghai freight. Around noon, two slick guys in Hong Kong suits came down to berth four asking the yard clerk about Haiyue Group's southern containers. They flashed some offshore shipping agency card, looked at the manifest, and drove off toward Harbor Villa."

Uncle Zhao grunted with a grin. "Harbor Villa? Fancy waterfront place. Their private chef used to work at a three-star Cantonese restaurant in Kowloon. Best steamed wild grouper and crab roe tofu in Shenzhen, if you ever get a free meal there."

Chen Xuan chewed slowly, his expression staying completely relaxed and unhurried. "Hong Kong suits at a dusty dock? They must have ruined their shiny leather shoes. Thanks for telling me, Xiao Wu."

"No trouble at all," Xiao Wu grinned. "We dock folks look out for each other."

Neither Uncle Zhao, Auntie Lin, nor Xiao Wu showed an ounce of stiffness or military posture. They joked and leaned against crates like real southern port workers. No outsider could have guessed all three were Zero Guard members woven seamlessly into Shekou Dock.

Across the lane, under a tin tea stall, two men in plain polos and jeans sat over iced lemon tea. Number 11 and Number 12 of the White Guard watched the berth without masks or gear, looking like tired freight agents on break.

"Number 1, this is Number 11," he murmured softly into a regular Bluetooth earpiece. "Young Master is at berth four checking his Hengxin freight slip. Everything is calm. However, three dock locals just walked right up to Young Master and started chatting like old family friends."

Next to him, Number 12 tapped swiftly on a tablet resting beside his teacup, pulling up civil registries, tax records, and port employment archives. A minute later, his brows knitted together in quiet confusion.

"Clean," Number 12 whispered, tilting the screen. "Too clean. Zhao Ming has worked eighteen years at Shekou Port with full tax records, a flat in Bao'an, and two grandkids. Lin Guihua has held a food license here for eleven years. The young tallyman graduated from a local Shenzhen college three years ago. Every bank record and neighbor detail matches."

Number 11 frowned, watching Chen Xuan laugh as Auntie Lin brushed crumbs off his sleeve. "Fourth time this month," he muttered. "In Shanghai, outside Emerald Riverside Villa, and now at Shekou Dock. Everywhere Young Master goes, friendly locals drift beside him like a quiet tide, yet every single file is 100 percent airtight."

"Maybe people just like talking to him," Number 12 said doubtfully.

Number 1's calm voice came through the earpiece. "If their files are spotless and they show zero hostility toward Young Master, do not interfere. Keep watching the Hong Kong cars near the gate."

"Understood," Number 11 replied, though a faint, lingering puzzle remained in his eyes.

Back at berth four, Chen Xuan finished his second pork bun, wiped his fingers with a tissue, and felt his phone vibrate in his jacket pocket. He pulled it out and saw a message from Qin Yuhan.

*Are you done inspecting your three very important containers? My afternoon meeting just wrapped up early. Meet me at the hotel lobby at 5:00 PM? I want to walk by the bay.*

A warm smile spread across Chen Xuan's face. He typed back quickly: *Done and signed. I will be waiting downstairs with warm water.*

He waved goodbye to Uncle Zhao, Auntie Lin, and Xiao Wu, tucked his clipboard under his arm, and strolled lazily toward the bus stop, looking forward to an unhurried evening with his wife.
