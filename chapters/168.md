# Chapter 168: Night Shift at Yantian Port

Wednesday afternoon at Shekou Dock smelled of warm sea salt, diesel fuel, and grilled corn from a street cart outside the main gate.

Under a faded blue awning beside Loading Bay Four, Chen Xuan sat on a low red plastic stool with a clipboard on his knees. He wore his plain white shirt and dark trousers, his scratched green thermos resting on an upside-down crate beside him.

Across from him sat Lin Dashan, a sunburned fifty-year-old yard clerk, squinting at six yellow carbon-copy delivery sheets.

"Crate four, kitchen steelware, twelve tons, seal intact," Lin Dashan read aloud. "Crate five, faucets and brass valves, nine tons, seal intact. Crate six, kitchen knives and cutting boards. Everything matches Shanghai's stamp."

Lin Dashan slapped his heavy red rubber stamp onto each sheet with six loud thuds and slid the stack over.

"All done, Young Chen," Lin Dashan grinned, pulling a crumpled pack of cheap cigarettes from his pocket. "You are the easiest Shanghai clerk I have dealt with all year. Most folks from up north yell over every scratch on a box. You just sit here drinking tea."

Chen Xuan smiled lightly, signed the receipt line, and tucked the pink copies into his cloth folder. "No point yelling in this heat, Uncle Lin. The goods are safe, and the trucks are lined up. That is all Hengxin Logistics pays me to check."

"True enough!" Lin Dashan laughed, fanning himself with his cap. "Say, I heard there was a huge mess at Pier Nine yesterday afternoon. People say Chief Inspector Lu got hauled off in handcuffs right in front of the Shen Family's eldest grandson, and Haiyue Group's ship unloaded without a hitch. Were you over there?"

"I went to pick up our clearance slip," Chen Xuan said calmly, taking a warm sip of jasmine tea from his green thermos. "The port officials were very helpful."

Lin Dashan chuckled. "Somebody stepped on a real nail. Half the truckers at Shekou are whispering that Shenhai Shipping's fuel cards stopped working this morning."

Fifty meters away, leaning casually against a shaded row of cold-drink machines near the gatehouse, a quiet man in a plain dark jacket glanced across the yard. He looked like an off-duty driver waiting for a friend, yet his eyes missed nothing moving within fifty meters of Chen Xuan. Nobody on the busy dock gave him a second look.

Chen Xuan screwed the lid back onto his thermos and stood up. "Thanks for the help, Uncle Lin. I am heading back before five o'clock traffic hits."

"Safe trip, Young Chen!" Lin Dashan called out.

Forty kilometers east across the city, however, the real work began after dark at Yantian Port.

By ten o'clock at night, the giant container yard at Yantian Port glowed under tall orange floodlights. Stacks of steel boxes rose like canyon walls beside the deep-water berths. Near the outer fence of Berth Seven, two plain matte-white vans sat parked in the gravel lot reserved for electrical repair crews.

Inside the lead matte-white van, two men in crisp white collared shirts sat before a folding table holding three white-cased tablets, binoculars, and shipping ledgers.

Number 19 lowered his binoculars from the cracked side window and spoke calmly.

"Three flatbed trucks just entered Gate Nine," Number 19 said. "No logos on the doors. Each truck carries one dark-blue forty-foot container. The gate guard waved them straight past the weight scale without checking papers."

Beside him, Number 88 tapped swiftly on his white tablet, pulling up the evening gate sheet and customs log.

"Got them," Number 88 said, scanning rows of numbers at top speed. "On paper, those three containers belong to a scrap yard in Dongguan, listed as recycled iron wire worth 120k yuan. Funny thing is, the crane just lifted the first box, and the hook gauge showed twenty-eight tons. Recycled iron wire does not pack that dense."

Number 19 picked up his white phone and sent two short messages to the second matte-white van near the northern rail siding. "Check the tug barge at the outer pier, and trace those seal numbers from the last twelve months."

Three minutes later, a soft chime sounded on Number 88's white tablet. Scanned freight receipts, Hong Kong dock slips, and northern railway cargo stubs filled the screen.

Number 88 leaned forward, his easy expression turning razor-sharp.

"Number 19, look at this," Number 88 said quietly. "Those dark-blue boxes are not staying on the ocean ship. A small Hong Kong feeder boat picks them up outside the breakwater at midnight, swaps the labels in Hong Kong waters, and moves the payment through four shell offices tied to the Seven-Harbor Consortium."

"What is inside?" Number 19 asked.

"High-grade rare metals," Number 88 answered, pointing at a gray scan pulled from a transfer yard up north. "Refined tungsten, cobalt, and rare-earth alloys mined illegally in the southern hills and packed inside hollow steel pipes. Every week for four years, the Shen Family has moved three to five boxes out of Yantian Port while hiding the money inside normal shipping bills."

Number 88 scrolled down the railway list, and his fingers paused on the final destination stamp.

"Wait," Number 88 murmured. "The money does not stop in Hong Kong. Half of the clean cash goes right back north through trading firms in Dalian and Changchun, straight into three mining and timber companies at the foot of Changbai Mountain."

Number 19's eyes narrowed. "Changbai Mountain. Jhengbai."

"Yes," Number 88 said, tapping the total column on his white tablet. "I added every shipment, every Hong Kong transfer, and every northern freight bill over the past four years. Between Shenzhen, Hong Kong, and Changbai Mountain, this single smuggling pipeline has moved 140 billion yuan."

Silence settled inside the matte-white van. Outside, a giant crane rumbled along its rails, lowering the third dark-blue container onto the waiting barge.

"140 billion yuan," Number 19 repeated softly. "No wonder Shen Wanshan panicked when we froze 12 billion yuan yesterday. And no wonder the Seven-Harbor Consortium sent Koo Tianming across the bridge at midnight. Shenhai Shipping is only the southern mouth of a pipeline feeding straight into Changbai Mountain."

"Should we stop the barge tonight?" Number 88 asked, his hand hovering near his white phone.

"No," Number 19 replied calmly. "Not without orders. Grab three boxes tonight, and the people in Hong Kong and Changbai Mountain will cut the line and vanish. We trace every truck, boat, and bank account first."

Number 19 typed a short summary on his white phone and sent it straight to Number 1.

Back in his plain hotel room near MixC Mall, Chen Xuan stood by the sink, rinsing jasmine leaves out of his scratched green thermos. His ordinary black phone buzzed once on the nightstand.

Chen Xuan dried his hands, walked over, and read the brief message.

*Yantian Port night shift confirmed. Shen Family and Seven-Harbor Consortium moving rare metals and hidden money. Four-year total: 140 billion yuan. Northern end leads directly to Changbai Mountain (Jhengbai).*

Chen Xuan set the phone beside his thermos and looked past the bright towers of Shenzhen toward the dark northern sky.

"Changbai Mountain," Chen Xuan murmured softly, his eyes deep and still. "So the trail finally points home."
