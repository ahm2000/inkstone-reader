# Chapter 34: Juzi

The six-ten express from Qingyan pulled into Yunxi County Bus Station, and Chen Xuan stepped off with his canvas bag and his green thermos, expecting a quiet walk home to pork-rib soup.

Instead, two people were waiting at the gate: Chen Yuan with her arms crossed, and beside her, in a jacket two sizes too big, Tang Xiaoyu.

"Mom sent us," Chen Yuan said, "and she says the station is dark after seven, and I say you are twenty-nine."

"I wanted to see where you get off," Xiaoyu said, looking at her shoes.

Chen Xuan smiled and said, "Oh, nice."

They had walked ten steps when Xiaoyu stopped and tilted her head. "Do you hear that?"

Hmm?

Under the long metal bench by the ticket hall, something was crying with a thin, cracked, furious little sound.

Mew!

Xiaoyu crouched down, and a kitten the color of a tangerine peel looked back at her with huge eyes. It was so thin that its ribs showed through its fur, and it was shivering, yet it still opened its mouth and shouted at her as if she owed it money!

"Hello," Xiaoyu whispered, in a voice he had never heard from her, soft and almost shy, "you are loud."

"Whose is it, and why is it out here alone?" Chen Yuan asked.

Chen Xuan did what he always did first. He asked.

The snack vendor wiped his hands and shrugged. "It has been there since morning, and it is not mine." Who would leave a kitten in a place like this? The woman at the ticket window said strays were "not the station's business," and told them please not to touch it, because it might have a disease. A taxi driver laughed, "Cats? Every corner has one!"

So they wrote a note on the back of a bus receipt: *Found: orange kitten, very thin, very loud. Please call.* Xiaoyu wrote the number of the Chen home in her careful school handwriting, and they walked it to Old Beef Noodles, where the owner, a quiet man with a towel on his shoulder, taped it beside the menu without a word.

They carried the kitten, wrapped in Chen Xuan's scarf, to the Happy Paws Pet Clinic two streets away.

"Take a number, please," said the girl at the desk. "We have six ahead of you."

Chen Xuan took number seven and sat on a plastic chair with the kitten asleep on his knee, while a fat bulldog sneezed on his shoes for a long while and he said "Bless you" twice.

When it was finally his turn, the clinic's assistant weighed the kitten, filled in a card, and took the payment, and then she gave him one tiny, quick bow.

---

Back at Fuxing New Village, the verdict came before Chen Xuan even got his shoes off.

"No cats!" Liu Meifang held her soup ladle like a sword. "Cats shed in the soup, and cats climb the curtains!"

"It's only a kitten, Mom," said Chen Yuan.

"Today it is a kitten! Next month it is a cat with opinions!"

"The Zhangs upstairs got a dog last year," Liu Meifang added, mostly to herself. "It chewed through two sofas and a diploma, and I am not having that conversation at my age!"

Xiaoyu said nothing and stood in the kitchen doorway, holding the kitten against her chest with both hands, and she did not beg. She looked at the floor, like a girl who has been told no many times and is waiting politely to hear it again.

Silence.

Chen Jianguo came out of the bedroom in his slippers and peered at the orange shape.

"Orange is a lucky color," he said slowly. "A persimmon in the house means everything goes well, and an orange cat in the house is a bank at the door!"

"Dad," said Chen Yuan, "last week you said cats bring bad luck."

"That was a black cat," said Chen Jianguo with dignity. "This one is orange, and that is a different department!"

Liu Meifang looked at her husband, then at the girl, then at the kitten, who chose that exact moment to sneeze.

Slowly, the ladle came down.

"It sleeps in the bathroom and eats from its own bowl," she said, "and if it touches my curtains..."

"It won't," Xiaoyu said at once, and her face lit up! "I promise!"

---

That night in the quiet eastern courtyard in Qingyan City, Mo Lin, Detail Number Nine, sat at a small desk with a photograph in front of him. A low number means a high rank in the Detail, the hidden protectors who work for Chen Xuan, and the photograph had been taken by the clinic's assistant, who was one of them.

It showed a vaccination card, folded once, and he read it the way other men read an important court ruling: *Orange short-hair, female, about eight weeks, weight 640 grams, first vaccine given, deworming given, no fever.*

He read it twice, and then he read the weight a third time, in case six hundred and forty grams meant something else!

The assistant's message sat underneath the photograph: *I treated him exactly like any customer and did not skip the queue, but I bowed when he paid, and I could not stop it. I am very sorry.*

Was one bow a broken rule? Was it rigging? The order said to stay ordinary, and a bow was not ordinary, but it did not change anything around the Boss. Mo Lin decided it was a small mistake, and he decided it very carefully!

He was not a man who smiled easily, so he typed back: *Noted. Do not bow next time.*

After a moment he added: *Is the kitten healthy?*

*Yes,* came the answer.

"Good," Mo Lin said aloud, to nobody.

---

Late that night in the Chen apartment, the house was dark and quiet.

In the bathroom, a small box lined with an old towel sat beside the door, as promised. But when Chen Xuan came out for water, he heard a very small purr behind Xiaoyu's door.

He pushed the door open an inch.

Xiaoyu was asleep on her side, one arm hanging off the bed. On the floor beside her slipper, the orange kitten had curled into a tight ball, its nose tucked under her heel.

"Juzi," she had told Chen Hao at dinner. "Tangerine, because of the color."

Chen Xuan stood there for a long while, because the girl who had folded her blanket every morning for weeks and never asked for anything had asked for exactly one thing tonight, and it weighed six hundred and forty grams.

He closed the door softly.

In the kitchen, Chen Yuan sat at the table in her pajamas, holding the clinic bill up to the light.

"Brother," she said slowly, "the vet's assistant bowed when you paid."

"Hmm? She was being polite."

"She bowed at a cat bill." Chen Yuan lowered the paper and kept looking at him. "Nobody bows at a hundred and sixty-eight yuan."
