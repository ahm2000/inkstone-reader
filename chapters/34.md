# Chapter 34: Juzi

Yunxi County Bus Station. Friday, 7:00 p.m.

The 6:10 express from Qingyan pulled in, and Chen Xuan stepped off with his canvas bag and his green thermos, expecting a quiet walk home to pork-rib soup.

Instead, two people were waiting at the gate. Chen Yuan stood with her arms crossed, and beside her, in a jacket two sizes too big, stood Tang Xiaoyu.

"Mom sent us," Chen Yuan said. "She says the station is dark after seven. I say you're twenty-nine."

"I wanted to see where you get off," Xiaoyu said, looking at her shoes.

Chen Xuan smiled. "Oh, nice."

They had walked ten steps when Xiaoyu stopped. She tilted her head. "Do you hear that?"

Under the long metal bench by the ticket hall, something was crying. It was a thin, cracked, furious little sound.

Xiaoyu crouched down. A kitten the color of a tangerine peel looked back at her with huge eyes. It was so thin that its ribs showed through the fur, and it was shivering, yet it still opened its mouth and shouted at her as if she owed it money.

"Hello," Xiaoyu whispered. Her voice was one he had never heard before, soft and almost shy. "You're loud."

"Whose is it?" Chen Yuan asked.

Chen Xuan did what he always did first. He asked.

The snack vendor wiped his hands and shrugged. "Been there since morning. Not mine." The woman at the ticket window said strays were "not the station's business," and told them please not to touch it, because it might have a disease. A taxi driver laughed, "Cats? Every corner has one!"

So they wrote a note on the back of a bus receipt: *Found: orange kitten, very thin, very loud. Please call.* Xiaoyu wrote the number of the Chen home in her careful school handwriting. They walked it to Old Beef Noodles, where the owner, a quiet man with a towel on his shoulder, taped it beside the menu without a word.

Then they carried the kitten, wrapped in Chen Xuan's scarf, to the Happy Paws Pet Clinic two streets away.

"Take a number, please," said the girl at the desk. "We have six ahead of you."

Chen Xuan took number seven and sat down on a plastic chair with the kitten asleep on his knee. For fifty minutes a fat bulldog sneezed on his shoes, and he said "Bless you" twice.

Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. Friday, 9:00 p.m.

"No cats!" Liu Meifang held her soup ladle like a sword. "Cats shed in the soup! Cats climb the curtains!"

"It's only a kitten, Mom," said Chen Yuan.

"Today it is a kitten! Next month it is a cat with opinions!"

Xiaoyu said nothing. She simply stood in the kitchen doorway, holding the kitten against her chest with both hands. She did not beg. She looked at the floor, like a girl who has been told no many times and is waiting politely to hear it again.

Chen Jianguo came out of the bedroom in his slippers and peered at the orange shape.

"Orange," he said slowly, "is a lucky color. As the old proverb says, a persimmon in the house means everything goes well, and an orange cat in the house is a bank at the door."

"That is not a proverb," said Chen Yuan.

"It is now."

Liu Meifang looked at her husband, then at the girl, then at the kitten, who chose that exact moment to sneeze. The ladle came slowly down.

"It sleeps in the bathroom," she said. "It eats from its own bowl. And if it touches my curtains..."

"It won't," Xiaoyu said at once. Her face lit up! "I promise!"

Qingyan City, a quiet courtyard in the east. Friday, 10:15 p.m.

Mo Lin, Detail Number Nine, sat at a small desk with a photograph in front of him, taken by the clinic's assistant. The Detail is the hidden team of protectors who work for Chen Xuan and act only when he gives the signal, and the assistant was one of them.

It was a vaccination card, folded once. He read it the way other men read a ruling from the highest court: *Orange short-hair, female, about eight weeks, weight 640 grams, first vaccine given, deworming given, no fever.*

He read it twice. Then he read the weight a third time, in case 640 grams meant something else.

The assistant's message sat underneath the photograph: *I treated him exactly like any customer. I did not skip the queue. But I bowed when he paid. I could not stop it. I am very sorry.*

Mo Lin was not a man who smiled easily. He typed back: *Noted. Do not bow next time.*

After a moment he added: *Is the kitten healthy?*

*Yes,* came the answer.

"Good," Mo Lin said aloud, to nobody.

Fuxing New Village, Room 601. Friday, 11:30 p.m.

The house was dark and quiet.

In the bathroom, a small box lined with an old towel sat beside the door, as promised. But when Chen Xuan came out for water, he heard a very small purr behind Xiaoyu's door.

He pushed the door open an inch.

Xiaoyu was asleep on her side, one arm hanging off the bed. On the floor beside her slipper, the orange kitten had curled into a tight ball, its nose tucked under her heel.

"Juzi," she had told Chen Hao at dinner. "Tangerine. Because of the color."

Chen Xuan stood there for a long while. The girl who had folded her blanket every morning for weeks and never asked for anything had asked for exactly one thing tonight, and it weighed 640 grams.

He closed the door softly.

In the kitchen, Chen Yuan sat at the table in her pajamas, holding the clinic bill up to the light.

"Brother," she said slowly. "The vet's assistant bowed when you paid."

"Hmm? She was being polite."

"She bowed at a cat bill. A hundred and sixty-eight yuan." Chen Yuan lowered the paper and kept looking at him. "Nobody bows at a hundred and sixty-eight yuan."
