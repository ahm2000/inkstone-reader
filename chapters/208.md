# Chapter 208: The Husband's Stamp

Chen Xuan finished his bite of braised beef and set his chopsticks neatly across his bowl, amused by the sudden seriousness in his wife's voice. Outside the tall windows, pale winter clouds drifted slowly over the Pudong skyline, making the warm, fragrant executive office feel all the more peaceful.

Qin Yuhan wiped the corners of her mouth with a linen napkin and reached to the edge of the coffee table, sliding a crisp blue contract folder between their teacups.

"I was not joking a moment ago," she said, her tone warm yet genuinely curious. "This morning, Haiyue formally secured three northern coastal container routes that opened up after the Seven-Harbor shipping syndicate collapsed. Volume is going to double before the end of December, so we need a dependable Shanghai feeder partner right away."

Chen Xuan topped off her teacup with hot water from the glass kettle and leaned back comfortably against the cushions. "Sounds like a solid win for Haiyue. Congratulations, President Qin."

"Do not tease me," Qin Yuhan said, nudging his knee lightly with hers. "Your company, Hengxin Logistics, actually made our final shortlist. Operations pulled the last four months of dispatch data, and Hengxin's on-time record beat three much larger carriers."

Chen Xuan paused, a faint smile touching his mouth. "Is that right? No wonder Manager Hu looked like he was marching into battle this morning."

"I am not asking you to play favorites," Qin Yuhan said seriously, turning toward him on the sofa. "An 80 million yuan feeder contract is a big commitment for Haiyue, especially during the December rush. You actually sit in that dispatch office every day and handle the manifests yourself. Tell me honestly, Xuan. Can Hengxin's dispatch team really handle that level of winter volume without dropping containers?"

Chen Xuan did not answer immediately. He unscrewed the lid of his green thermos, took a slow sip of tea, and thought over the familiar faces in his department.

"Honestly?" he said calmly. "Middle management can be a little loud, and they love making thick binders nobody reads. That part is nothing special. Still, the frontline dispatch clerks, route coordinators, and port truck drivers are genuinely solid people. Sister Pan keeps the daily filing steady, Old Zhao knows every customs lane at Yangshan by heart, and Little Lin can balance forty container schedules without losing a single tracking number. Give them clear route sheets and fair schedules, and they will deliver on time every single day, snow or rain."

Qin Yuhan watched him quietly as he spoke. She had always admired that about him. Even when he talked about his modest desk job, he never looked down on the people around him, and he never exaggerated. If Chen Xuan said a team was reliable, she trusted his judgment more than ten glossy consulting reports.

"That is all I needed to hear," Qin Yuhan said with a bright, decisive smile.

She uncapped her black fountain pen, flipped open the blue folder right there on the coffee table, and signed her neat, flowing signature at the bottom of the 80 million yuan annual feeder logistics agreement. Then she stood briefly, walked over to her mahogany desk to fetch Haiyue Group's red corporate authorization seal from the drawer, and returned to press it firmly beside her name.

"There," she said softly, blowing lightly across the fresh red ink before glancing at him with playful eyes. "Officially approved with the husband's stamp."

Chen Xuan laughed quietly, shaking his head. "Careful, President Qin. If your board of directors hears you are consulting a logistics clerk over radish soup, they might start asking questions."

"Let them ask," Qin Yuhan replied lightly, closing the folder with a crisp snap. "My husband has better instincts than half the analysts in Lujiazui. Besides, I am only giving Hengxin the contract because their numbers earned it. I just happen to want one small extra guarantee from their management."

"Oh? What kind of guarantee?"

"That they stop dumping a mountain of messy old files on one person's desk and treat their dispatch team properly," Qin Yuhan said smoothly, raising an eyebrow. "You joked at dinner last night about clearing 300 old route folders before 4:55 PM, and I still noticed you rubbing your wrist before bed."

Chen Xuan blinked, then let out a warm, helpless laugh. Nothing ever escaped his wife's eyes when she was paying attention.

He began stacking the empty stainless steel tiers back into the checkered cloth, preparing to stand up. "All right, my work here is done. Let me clear out before your afternoon visitors come upstairs. I should head back to the office anyway so nobody wonders why I took an extra ten minutes at lunch."

Qin Yuhan reached out and caught the cuff of his gray sweater, tugging him gently back onto the sofa.

"Stay for ten more minutes and finish your tea," she said, a teasing glint returning to her eyes. "The guests waiting in the visitor lounge downstairs are General Manager Sun Haoran and your Manager Hu Dewei. Since I am about to hand your company an 80 million yuan contract, you might as well say hello."

Chen Xuan paused, amused by the sudden mischief in his usually composed wife. He had no interest in throwing weight around, yet watching Hu Dewei realize where he actually ate lunch sounded mildly entertaining.

"Suit yourself, President Qin," Chen Xuan said, setting the wrapped lunchbox neatly beside his thermos and leaning back against the leather cushions. "I will just sit here and drink my tea."

Qin Yuhan slipped her black heels back on, smoothed the front of her charcoal suit, and walked over to her desk intercom.

"Lin Wei," she said crisply into the speaker, "please escort General Manager Sun and Manager Hu up from the visitor lounge now."

"Right away, President Qin."

Three floors below, the visitor lounge door opened. Sun Haoran stood smoothly and buttoned his wool jacket, while Hu Dewei practically sprang out of his armchair, clutching his thick leather portfolio with white knuckles.

"Finally!" Hu Dewei breathed, checking his reflection in the brass elevator doors as they rode up. "General Manager Sun, watch closely. Once President Qin sees my three-hundred-page route audit, that feeder contract is as good as ours."

Sun Haoran glanced sideways at his excited subordinate and offered no reply. The elevator chimed softly at the top floor, and the two men stepped out toward the walnut doors of the executive suite.
