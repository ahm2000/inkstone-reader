# Chapter 51: 5:00 AM Summons Across Forty Departments

At five o'clock on Monday morning, the red phone in Vice Minister of Public Security Shen Weiguo's bedroom began to scream.

It did not ring like a normal home phone. It let out a sharp, steady double beep used only for top national emergencies. Shen Weiguo sat straight up before the third beep ended and grabbed the phone.

"Shen Weiguo speaking," he said, his voice rough from sleep.

"Central Compound office," a clipped male voice replied. "Top Red Emergency Order. Joint order from Premier Gu Chengshan and General Qin Zhengguo. Report to the West Gate by five thirty. No sending a deputy in your place. No phones or electronics past the inner gate. Confirm you heard this."

"Wait," Shen Weiguo barked, his knuckles turning white around the plastic. "What is the reason? Is this a border problem or something inside the country? Put Director Dai Chunsheng on the line."

"Director Dai Chunsheng cannot be reached," the voice said flatly. "Confirm you heard this, Vice Minister."

Ice slid straight down Shen Weiguo's spine. "Confirmed," he muttered. The line went dead.

Twelve minutes later, Shen Weiguo sat in the backseat of a black car speeding through Beijing's dark streets. He stuck a cigarette between his lips and clicked his brass lighter three times before the flame caught. His hand shook just enough to make the red tip bounce.

"Who else got called in?" Shen Weiguo snapped at his secretary, Zhang Kaiming, who sat stiffly in the front passenger seat holding a leather folder.

"Everyone, Vice Minister," Zhang Kaiming answered without turning around, his voice tight. "I checked the night desk before we left. Ten offices under the Military. Ten bureaus under State Security. Ten departments under Public Security. Ten divisions across Discipline Inspection, the Supreme People's Court, and the Supreme People's Procuratorate. Plus the Organization Department, the National Development and Reform Commission, the National People's Congress Standing Committee, and the CPPCC."

Shen Weiguo took a hard drag, blowing thick smoke from his nose. "Over forty national departments at five in the morning? That has not happened in twenty years. Did you reach Xu Wenbin?"

"No answer on his home phone or his secure phone," Zhang Kaiming said, swallowing hard. "I also called Chairman Fang Qiming at Shengyuan Capital and Vice Governor Lin Zhengde's office. Both lines rang once and went completely dead."

"Completely dead," Shen Weiguo repeated softly. The gray ash on his cigarette broke off onto his pressed pants. He did not brush it away. "A dead line means they did not turn their phones off by hand. Someone cut their numbers off the network."

"Vice Minister," Zhang Kaiming whispered, his eyes wide in the rearview mirror. "Should I call your wife and tell her not to answer unknown calls?"

"Shut your mouth and watch the road," Shen Weiguo hissed, his jaw clenching until his teeth ground together. "Do not make one single call from this car. Not one."

He leaned back into the leather seat, his mind racing through every deal and favor he had signed off on over the past five years. No one ordered a five o'clock summons across forty departments for a small scandal. Someone high up had pulled a pin, and nobody outside the West Gate knew who would still have a job by sunrise.

Three kilometers east, Lieutenant General Tang Jiandong of the Joint Staff Department climbed into his car while buttoning his olive uniform jacket. Inside, the air already smelled of stale tobacco from his aide, Major Luo Feng.

"Give me the secure phone," Tang Jiandong ordered the moment the door shut.

"Sir, Joint Operations already checked the red order," Luo Feng said quickly, handing over the black phone. "Ten military offices were called in at the same time. Logistics, Equipment, Political Work, Military Discipline, and six regional offices."

Tang Jiandong punched in a four-digit quick dial. The phone hummed twice, then gave a flat, hollow tone. That meant the other phone had been cut off from the military network. He tried a second number. The exact same dead tone came back.

"Both of them," Tang Jiandong muttered, his eyebrows pulling together hard. "I played chess with He Guoliang's old logistics deputy three nights ago. Now his line does not even exist."

"General, could this be a surprise readiness drill?" Luo Feng asked, his fingers tapping nervously on his briefcase.

Tang Jiandong gave him a sharp, cold look. "Do they drag the Supreme People's Court and the National Development and Reform Commission out of bed at five in the morning for an army drill, Major? Use your head. Light me a cigarette."

Luo Feng fumbled with a pack, dropping one cigarette before lighting another and handing it back. Tang Jiandong took a deep drag. Outside the window, dozens of black official cars merged quietly onto Chang'an Avenue. None of them used sirens. None of them flashed lights.

"General Qin Zhengguo signed the order," Tang Jiandong said slowly, smoke drifting across his weathered face. "If Qin Zhengguo is already inside the compound before dawn, and half the Southern logistics channel is cut off, somebody stepped on a landmine they could not hide."

"Who could pull in forty departments all at once?" Luo Feng asked under his breath.

Tang Jiandong jammed his half-smoked cigarette into the door ashtray so hard the filter snapped. "Stop asking questions you do not want answered," he growled. "And pray neither of our names is sitting in a folder on that table."

At five twenty, the line of cars outside the West Gate of the Central Compound stretched two hundred meters back. Armed guards stood in the cold wind, checking every face against printed paper lists instead of electronic scanners.

Inside a black sedan near the front, Supreme People's Court Vice President Han Qingshan rolled his back window down two inches to let out the heavy cigarette smoke. Beside him in the next lane, another back window slid down at the same time, showing the pale face of National Development and Reform Commission Vice Chairman Lu Mingyuan.

"Old Lu," Han Qingshan called softly across the narrow space between the cars. "Did your office get any word on what this is about?"

Lu Mingyuan gripped the window frame, a cigarette burning forgotten between his fingers. "Nothing," he rasped. "Just the joint order and thirty minutes to get here. Have you spoken to Lin Zhengde or Fang Qiming?"

Han Qingshan's eyes narrowed. "Why would I talk to them before dawn?"

"Do not play courtroom games with me, Han Qingshan," Lu Mingyuan snapped, panic breaking through his calm act. "Three vice chairmen from the banking and building committees did not answer their red phones. My driver passed He Guoliang's house ten minutes ago. Every light in the yard was dark, and there were no guards at his gate."

Han Qingshan's hand froze halfway to his mouth. "No guards?"

"Gone," Lu Mingyuan whispered, his voice shaking. "Not switched out. Just gone."

Before Han Qingshan could answer, a guard stepped between the two cars and tapped sharply once on each roof.

"Windows up," the guard ordered flatly. "No talking between cars. Get ready to hand over all phones, smartwatches, and recording devices at the inner gate."

Both windows slid shut at once. Inside his car, Han Qingshan stared straight ahead as cold sweat ran down his back under his wool coat. His heart beat hard against his ribs, and his mind spun with names of people he had shared tea with only days ago.

One by one, the black cars rolled slowly through the heavy steel gate. Forty of the country's highest departments walked blindly into the gray dawn, none of them knowing what waited inside.
