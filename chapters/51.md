# Chapter 51: 5:00 AM Summons Across Forty Departments

At five o'clock on Monday morning, the red phone in Vice Minister of Public Security Shen Weiguo's bedroom let out the sharp, steady double beep reserved for top national emergencies. Shen Weiguo sat straight up before the third beep ended and grabbed it.

"Shen Weiguo speaking," he said, voice rough from sleep.

"Central Leadership Compound office," a clipped male voice replied. "Top Red Emergency Order from Premier Gu Chengshan and General Qin Zhengguo. Report to the West Gate by five thirty. No deputies. No phones or electronics past the inner gate. Confirm."

"Wait," Shen Weiguo barked, knuckles white on the plastic. "What is the reason? Border trouble or domestic? Put Director Dai Chunsheng on the line."

"Director Dai Chunsheng cannot be reached," the voice said flatly. "Confirm you heard this, Vice Minister."

Ice slid down Shen Weiguo's spine. "Confirmed," he muttered. The line went dead.

Twelve minutes later, Shen Weiguo sat in the backseat of a black car speeding through Beijing's dark streets. He clicked his brass lighter three times before his cigarette caught, his hand shaking just enough to make the red tip bounce.

"Who else got called in?" Shen Weiguo snapped at his secretary, Zhang Kaiming, sitting stiffly in the front passenger seat.

"Everyone, Vice Minister," Zhang Kaiming answered without turning, his voice tight. "Ten offices under the Military. Ten bureaus under State Security. Ten departments under Public Security. Ten divisions across Discipline Inspection, the Supreme People's Court, and the Supreme People's Procuratorate. Plus the Organization Department, the National Development and Reform Commission, the National People's Congress Standing Committee, and the CPPCC."

Shen Weiguo blew thick smoke from his nose. "Over forty national departments at five in the morning? That has not happened in twenty years. Did you reach Qi Tongwei?"

"No answer on his home or secure phone," Zhang Kaiming said, swallowing hard. "I also called Zhao Ruilong at Shanshui Group and Deputy Secretary Gao Yuliang's office. Both lines rang once and went completely dead."

"Completely dead," Shen Weiguo repeated softly. Gray ash dropped onto his pressed pants, and he did not brush it away. "They did not turn their phones off. Someone cut their numbers off the network."

"Vice Minister," Zhang Kaiming whispered, eyes wide in the rearview mirror. "Should I call your wife and tell her not to answer unknown calls?"

"Shut your mouth and watch the road," Shen Weiguo hissed, teeth grinding. "Do not make one single call from this car."

He leaned back, his mind racing through every deal and favor he had signed in the past five years. Nobody ordered a five o'clock summons across forty departments for a small scandal. Someone high up had pulled a pin, and nobody outside the West Gate knew who would still have a job by sunrise.

Three kilometers east, Lieutenant General Tang Jiandong of the Joint Staff Department climbed into his car buttoning his olive uniform jacket. Inside, the air already smelled of stale tobacco from his aide, Major Luo Feng.

"Give me the secure phone," Tang Jiandong ordered as the door shut.

"Sir, Joint Operations verified the red order," Luo Feng said quickly, handing over the black phone. "Ten military offices were called in together: Logistics, Equipment, Political Work, Military Discipline, and six regional offices."

Tang Jiandong punched in a four-digit quick dial. The phone hummed twice, then gave the flat, hollow tone of a line severed from the military network. A second number returned the exact same dead tone.

"Both of them," Tang Jiandong muttered, brows knitting hard. "I played chess with Zhao Dehan's old logistics deputy three nights ago. Now his line does not even exist."

"General, could this be a surprise readiness drill?" Luo Feng asked, fingers tapping nervously on his briefcase.

Tang Jiandong gave him a cold look. "Do they drag the Supreme People's Court and the National Development and Reform Commission out of bed at five in the morning for an army drill, Major? Use your head. Light me a cigarette."

Luo Feng fumbled with the pack before handing one back. Tang Jiandong took a deep drag as dozens of black official cars merged quietly onto Chang'an Avenue without sirens or flashing lights.

"General Qin Zhengguo signed the order," Tang Jiandong said slowly through drifting smoke. "If Qin Zhengguo is already inside the compound before dawn and half the Southern logistics channel is cut off, somebody stepped on a landmine they could not hide."

"Who could pull in forty departments all at once?" Luo Feng whispered.

Tang Jiandong jammed his half-smoked cigarette into the ashtray so hard the filter snapped. "Stop asking questions you do not want answered," he growled. "And pray neither of our names sits in a folder on that table."

At five twenty, the line of cars outside the West Gate stretched two hundred meters back. Armed guards checked every face against printed paper lists instead of electronic scanners.

Inside a black sedan near the front, Supreme People's Court Vice President Han Qingshan rolled his back window down two inches to let out cigarette smoke. In the next lane, National Development and Reform Commission Vice Chairman Lu Mingyuan's window slid down at the same time, showing his pale face.

"Old Lu," Han Qingshan called softly across the gap. "Any word on what this is about?"

Lu Mingyuan gripped the window frame, a cigarette burning forgotten between his fingers. "Nothing," he rasped. "Just the joint order and thirty minutes to get here. Have you spoken to Gao Yuliang or Zhao Ruilong?"

Han Qingshan's eyes narrowed. "Why would I talk to them before dawn?"

"Do not play courtroom games with me, Han Qingshan," Lu Mingyuan snapped, panic breaking through. "Three vice chairmen from the banking and building committees did not answer their red phones. My driver passed Zhao Dehan's house ten minutes ago. Every light in the yard was dark, and there were no guards at his gate."

Han Qingshan's hand froze halfway to his mouth. "No guards?"

"Gone," Lu Mingyuan whispered, voice shaking. "Not switched out. Just gone."

A guard stepped between the cars and tapped sharply on each roof.

"Windows up," the guard ordered flatly. "No talking between cars. Prepare to hand over all phones, smartwatches, and recording devices at the inner gate."

Both windows slid shut. Cold sweat ran down Han Qingshan's back as his heart hammered against his ribs, his mind spinning with names of people he had shared tea with only days ago.

One by one, the black cars rolled through the heavy steel gate. Forty of the country's highest departments walked blindly into the pre-dawn dark, none of them knowing what waited inside.
