# Chapter 51: Tang Xiaoyu's First Day

At six forty on Monday morning, Room 601 smelled of steamed egg custard, sesame buns, and warm soy milk.

Tang Xiaoyu stood beside the table while Liu Meifang rolled up the cuffs of Zhou Guilan's hand-knitted red sweater for the second time. The crimson wool looked bright against the gray winter light.

"Two folds is just right," Liu Meifang said warmly. "Take two boiled eggs in your pocket in case the cafeteria gives small portions."

Tang Xiaoyu tucked the warm eggs into her coat and lifted Chen Hao's seven-year-old blue school bag. The canvas corners were faded soft, the brass zippers slid cleanly, and Chen Hao's grade-six name was still inked faintly inside the flap.

Chen Jianguo adjusted his glasses solemnly. "Xiaoyu, as the old saying goes, a tall pine grows from a straight seed."

"Dad," Chen Yuan said from the doorway, wrapping her scarf. "House Rule One applies to morning proverbs too. Eat your porridge."

Chen Hao grinned and tapped the blue bag at the door. "If anyone asks why the bag is old, tell them it belonged to a county math champion."

"You placed fourth," Chen Yuan reminded him dryly.

"Fourth out of three hundred counts," Chen Hao protested, drawing a quiet smile from Tang Xiaoyu as she followed Chen Yuan downstairs.

* * *

Yunxi No. 2 Middle School stood three bus stops south behind a red brick wall lined with bare plane trees.

Because Chen Yuan taught eighth-grade Chinese down the same corridor, she walked Tang Xiaoyu to Grade Seven, Class Three, introduced her to the homeroom teacher, and squeezed her shoulder once before heading to her own class.

Tang Xiaoyu took the window desk, set Chen Hao's blue school bag neatly beside her leg, and opened her notebook.

The first math lesson passed quietly. Trouble arrived three minutes into morning break.

A stocky boy in a bright white brand-name jacket strolled down the aisle with two laughing friends. His name was Xu Kaiwen, whose father, Xu Guofu, owned the biggest gravel yard in Yunxi County. Last autumn, Xu Guofu donated two ceiling projectors to the school, and Xu Kaiwen walked the halls as if his family owned the building.

Xu Kaiwen stopped beside Tang Xiaoyu's desk and nudged her worn blue bag strap with his clean sneaker.

"Hey, new girl," Xu Kaiwen said loudly, grinning back. "Where did you dig up that rag bag? Did the town dump have a sale?"

Nearby students lowered their heads over their books. Nobody wanted to cross Xu Kaiwen.

Tang Xiaoyu did not flinch. She bent calmly, lifted the blue strap off the floor, wiped the smudge off with her sleeve, and set the bag across her lap.

"I heard from the office that her dad died and she had nowhere to go," Xu Kaiwen sneered, leaning on her desk. "A charity orphan living off leftovers. Go sit in the back corner by the mop bucket."

The classroom fell silent.

Tang Xiaoyu lifted her head. Her eyes were clear, dry, and steady as winter ice.

"My bag was given to me by my brother," Tang Xiaoyu said in a quiet, even voice. "My sweater was knitted by my grandma. Both are cleaner than your mouth. Take your hand off my desk."

Xu Kaiwen's smirk vanished, his ears burning red in front of twenty classmates.

"You little charity beggar!" Xu Kaiwen snapped, raising his hand to sweep her notebook off the desk. "My father Xu Guofu paid for this school's equipment! One call and Principal Liang Zhengguo throws you back on the street!"

His fingers were two inches from her desk when a calm, clear voice cut through the room from the doorway.

"Put your hand down, Xu Kaiwen."

Every head turned. Chen Yuan stood inside the doorway, her lesson book under her arm, her gentle face set with quiet steel.

She walked down the aisle and stepped beside Tang Xiaoyu's desk, resting one steady hand on the girl's shoulder.

"Miss Chen, she insulted me first," Xu Kaiwen muttered, dropping his hand.

"I stood outside the window for thirty seconds, Xu Kaiwen. I heard every word," Chen Yuan replied evenly, facing the class. "In Yunxi No. 2 Middle School, no donation buys the right to mock a classmate's bag or family. Every student here has the exact same right to respect."

She looked down at Xu Kaiwen. "Walk with me to Principal Liang Zhengguo's office now."

* * *

Ten minutes later, in the third-floor office, Xu Kaiwen stood scowling at the floor.

Principal Liang Zhengguo sat behind his desk, visibly uncomfortable. He respected Chen Yuan, yet Xu Guofu's company was bidding on the north courtyard paving project.

"Teacher Chen, boys speak without thinking," Principal Liang Zhengguo began cautiously. "Perhaps Xu Kaiwen can write an apology letter and return to class."

The black landline on his desk rang sharply.

He checked the caller display, straightened abruptly, and lifted the receiver with both hands. "Hello? Deputy Director Lin Weidong! Good morning, sir."

For twenty seconds, the office went utterly still. Principal Liang Zhengguo's face turned pale and rigid, sweat beading along his temples.

"Yes, Deputy Director Lin Weidong," Principal Liang Zhengguo said quickly, sitting ramrod straight. "Yunxi County Education Bureau has zero tolerance for bullying, and every newly transferred Grade Seven student is under direct review. I will send the disciplinary record before noon."

He set the receiver down softly. Every trace of hesitation had vanished.

Principal Liang Zhengguo fixed Xu Kaiwen with a cold stare.

"Xu Kaiwen," the principal said flatly. "Mocking a classmate and threatening her with your father's money is a serious violation. You receive a formal warning in your permanent file, and effective immediately, you are transferred out of Class Three to Class Nine under the Dean of Discipline's watch. I am calling Xu Guofu now."

Xu Kaiwen's mouth fell open. "Principal Liang, my dad donated the projectors!"

"And I will return their invoice value to your father today if he objects," Principal Liang Zhengguo snapped. "Wait outside."

* * *

At twelve fifteen, Tang Xiaoyu sat at her clean window desk in Class Three.

She had finished her cafeteria lunch, which really was terrible just as Chen Xuan promised, though she ate every grain of rice anyway.

Two quiet girls from the next row walked over, the taller one holding out a bright tangerine.

"Hi, Tang Xiaoyu," she said shyly. "That was really brave earlier. Do you want half a tangerine?"

Tang Xiaoyu looked into her friendly eyes, nodded slowly, and took the fruit.

"Thank you," Tang Xiaoyu said softly. "My brother warned me about the cafeteria, so this helps."

The girls laughed and pulled up a stool beside her desk. Tang Xiaoyu peeled the tangerine neatly, her shoulders relaxing beneath Zhou Guilan's warm red sweater.

* * *

Across the street from Yunxi No. 2 Middle School, on the flat concrete roof of a four-story grain supply building, winter wind stirred dry leaves along the parapet.

Motionless in the deep shade beside the water tank stood a lone figure in matte-black, featureless gear. A smooth black mask concealed every trace of identity, bearing no badge and no reflection.

From the rooftop, the unseen operative of the permanent Zero Guard team watched Tang Xiaoyu share her tangerine safely at her desk for ten calm seconds.

"Target family member settled," a flat, neutral, genderless chilled voice murmured into a concealed mic. "Perimeter clear."

Without a rustle of cloth or sound on concrete, the figure stepped back into the shade and faded from sight as the afternoon school bell rang across the street.
