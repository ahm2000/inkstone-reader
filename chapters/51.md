# Chapter 51: 5:00 AM Summons Across Forty Departments

At five o'clock on Monday morning, the red-line desk phone in Vice Minister of Public Security Shen Weiguo's bedroom began to scream.

It did not ring like an ordinary landline. It emitted an unbroken double pulse reserved for Grade-One national emergencies. Shen Weiguo sat upright before the third pulse finished, snatching the receiver.

"Shen Weiguo speaking," he said, his voice hoarse from sleep.

"Central Compound Secretariat," a clipped male voice replied. "Grade-One Red Mobilization Seal. Joint authorization from Premier Gu Chengshan and General Qin Zhengguo. Report to the West Gate by five thirty. No deputy substitutions. No electronic devices past the inner checkpoint. Confirm receipt."

"Wait," Shen Weiguo barked, his knuckles turning white on the plastic. "What is the briefing code? Is this a border incident or an internal alert? Put Director Ma Zhenyu on the line."

"Director Ma Zhenyu is unavailable," the voice said flatly. "Confirm receipt, Vice Minister."

Ice slid straight down Shen Weiguo's spine. "Confirmed," he muttered, and the line clicked dead.

Twelve minutes later, Shen Weiguo rode in the backseat of a black sedan cutting through Beijing's dark avenues. He jammed a cigarette between his lips and clicked his brass lighter three times before the flame caught, his hand shaking just enough to make the ember bob.

"Who else got pulled?" Shen Weiguo snapped at his secretary, Zhang Kaiming, who sat rigidly in the front passenger seat clutching a leather folder.

"Everyone, Vice Minister," Zhang Kaiming answered without turning around, his voice tight. "I checked the duty log before we left. Ten directorates under the Military. Ten bureaus under State Security. Ten departments under Public Security. Ten divisions across the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, the Supreme People's Court, and the Supreme People's Procuratorate. Plus the Organization Department, the National Development and Reform Commission, the National People's Congress Standing Committee, and the CPPCC."

Shen Weiguo inhaled hard, letting thick smoke spill from his nostrils. "Over forty national departments at five in the morning? That has not happened in twenty years. Did you reach Xu Wenbin?"

"No answer on his home line or his encrypted handset," Zhang Kaiming said, swallowing visibly. "I also tried Deputy Director Fang Qiming at the Southern Liaison Desk and Director Lin Zhengde's office. Both lines rang once and dropped into carrier silence."

"Carrier silence," Shen Weiguo repeated softly. The ash at his cigarette tip broke off onto his pressed trousers; he did not brush it away. "Carrier silence means their handsets were not switched off by hand. They were pulled off the grid."

"Vice Minister," Zhang Kaiming whispered, eyes wide in the rearview mirror. "Should I call your wife and tell her not to answer unknown numbers?"

"Shut your mouth and watch the road," Shen Weiguo hissed, his jaw clenching until his molars ground together. "Do not make a single call from this car. Not one."

Three kilometers east, Lieutenant General Tang Jiandong of the Joint Staff Department climbed into his staff car while buttoning his olive tunic. Inside, the air already reeked of stale tobacco from his adjutant, Major Luo Feng.

"Give me the secure handset," Tang Jiandong ordered the moment the door shut.

"Sir, Joint Operations already confirmed the red seal," Luo Feng said quickly, handing over the black phone. "Ten military bureaus were summoned simultaneously. General Logistics, Equipment Development, Political Work, Discipline Inspection, and six theater liaisons."

Tang Jiandong punched in a four-digit speed dial. The handset hummed twice, then returned a flat mechanical tone signaling that the receiving terminal had been decommissioned from the military mesh. He tried a second number. The same dead tone answered.

"Both of them," Tang Jiandong muttered, his eyebrows knotting harshly. "I played chess with He Guoliang's old logistics deputy three nights ago. Now his terminal does not exist."

"General, could it be a live readiness drill?" Luo Feng ventured, fingers tapping nervously on his briefcase.

Tang Jiandong shot him a razor-sharp glare. "Do they drag the Supreme People's Court and the National Development and Reform Commission out of bed at five in the morning for a military drill, Major? Use your head. Light me a cigarette."

Luo Feng fumbled with a pack, dropping one stick before lighting another and passing it back. Tang Jiandong took a deep drag, watching dozens of official black-plate sedans merge silently onto Chang'an Avenue with no sirens and no flashing lights.

"General Qin Zhengguo signed the order," Tang Jiandong said slowly, smoke curling across his weathered face. "If Qin Zhengguo is inside the compound before dawn and half the Southern logistics channel is off the grid, somebody stepped on a landmine they could not bury."

"Who could trigger forty departments at once?" Luo Feng asked under his breath.

Tang Jiandong crushed his half-smoked cigarette into the door ashtray so hard the filter snapped. "Stop asking questions you do not want answered," he growled. "And pray neither of our names is sitting in a folder on that table."

At five twenty, the bottleneck outside the West Gate of the Central Compound stretched two hundred meters. Armed sentries checked every occupant's face against printed paper manifests instead of electronic scanners.

Inside a black sedan near the front, Supreme People's Court Vice President Han Qingshan cracked his rear window two inches to vent the choking cigarette haze. Beside him in the next lane, another rear window lowered simultaneously, revealing the pale face of National Development and Reform Commission Vice Chairman Lu Mingyuan.

"Old Lu," Han Qingshan called softly across the narrow gap. "Did your office get a briefing code?"

Lu Mingyuan gripped the window frame, a cigarette burning forgotten between his fingers. "Nothing," he rasped. "Just the joint seal and a thirty-minute clock. Have you spoken to Lin Zhengde or Fang Qiming?"

Han Qingshan's eyes narrowed. "Why would I speak to them before dawn?"

"Do not play judicial games with me, Han Qingshan," Lu Mingyuan snapped, panic cracking his polish. "Three vice chairmen from the banking and infrastructure committees did not answer their red lines. My driver passed He Guoliang's residence ten minutes ago. Every courtyard light was off, and there were no guards at his gate."

Han Qingshan's hand froze halfway to his mouth. "No guards?"

"Gone," Lu Mingyuan whispered. "Not replaced. Gone."

Before Han Qingshan could reply, a sentry stepped between the sedans and rapped sharply once on each roof.

"Windows up," the sentry commanded flatly. "No cross-vehicle communication. Prepare to surrender all phones, smartwatches, and recording devices at the inner gate."

Both windows slid shut instantly. Inside his car, Han Qingshan stared straight ahead as cold sweat trickled down his spine beneath his wool coat. One by one, the black sedans rolled through the reinforced gate, and forty of the country's highest departments walked blindly into the dawn.
