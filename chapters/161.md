# Chapter 161: Four Thirty at Pier Nine

Chen Xuan kept his call at Gate Three brief, leaving a quiet note for his contact to watch and wait before pocketing his phone. The rattled dock manager immediately alerted his superiors, and by 4:30 PM the standoff had moved to the main entrance of Pier Nine. A crisp late November wind blew off the Pearl River estuary, sweeping across the silent yard beneath the towering gantry cranes.

Heavy iron chains now wrapped twice around the main steel gates, secured by two thick brass padlocks, while two black security SUVs sat parked sideways across the customs clearance lane with hazard lights blinking. Manager Lin Jian and the legal team had gone to file an emergency port protest, and Qin Yuhan had traded her blazer for a warmer coat against the sea chill.

Outside the locked gate, Qin Yuhan stood in a tailored camel trench coat over a cream blouse, her dark hair pinned neatly back against the sea breeze. Her face remained calm, though her knuckles showed faint white against the edge of her tablet. Beside her, Haiyue Group's southern operations manager, Lin Jian, wiped sweat from his forehead despite the cool air.

"President Qin, all 40 containers are sealed inside," Lin Jian said, his voice tight. "The chartered vessel Haiyue Star is moored at Berth Three. If the gates do not open and customs clearance does not finalize before 5:00 PM, we miss the tide window. The Southeast Asia shipping consortium will trigger the penalty clause automatically. That means 80 million in liquidated damages, plus losing the entire southern shipping route."

Qin Yuhan glanced at the locked chains, then at the silent cranes behind them. "Have you reached the port authority duty desk again?"

"Three times," Lin Jian answered grimly. "They insist a local compliance hold was filed two hours ago and cannot be lifted without the terminal operator's sign-off. We both know who controls this terminal."

Standing half a step behind Qin Yuhan, Chen Xuan looked completely unbothered by the looming 80 million crisis. He wore a simple dark zip-up jacket, dark trousers, and clean sneakers. Draped neatly over his left forearm was Qin Yuhan's beige leather handbag. In his right hand, he held his dented green metal thermos.

He unscrewed the lid, blew gently across the rising steam, and took a slow sip of warm jasmine tea.

"The wind is picking up," Chen Xuan said mildly, reaching over to tug the lapel of Qin Yuhan's trench coat higher around her collar. "Do not catch a chill before dinner. You still owe me that roast goose at MixC Mall tonight."

Qin Yuhan's tense shoulders softened slightly. She shot him an exasperated yet fond glance. "Chen Xuan, we are about to lose an entire shipping corridor, and you are thinking about roast goose."

"A man has to keep his priorities straight," Chen Xuan replied with an easy smile. "You handle the major contracts. I handle carrying the handbag and making sure my wife eats on time."

Lin Jian nearly choked, staring at the husband of Haiyue Group's president. Everyone in Shanghai knew Qin Yuhan had married a junior logistics supervisor who worked a corner desk job at Hengxin Logistics, yet seeing him sip cheap tea during a port standoff still felt surreal.

Before Qin Yuhan could reply, the low rumble of engines echoed down the access road. Three polished black Mercedes sedans rolled toward the gate and stopped in a neat line. The rear door of the middle car opened, and a tall man in his late twenties stepped out.

Shen Jingzhou, heir of Shenzhen's prominent Shen Family, wore a sharp charcoal suit, a burgundy silk pocket square, and a heavy gold watch catching the autumn sun. Two slick legal advisors hurried to his side, while four broad-shouldered security guards fanned out behind him.

Shen Jingzhou strolled toward Qin Yuhan with a mocking smile.

"President Qin," Shen Jingzhou greeted her, spreading his hands. "What a shame to see you standing out here in the wind. Pier Nine is a busy place, and paperwork errors happen so easily when outsiders push into southern shipping lanes without saying hello first."

"Cut the act, Shen Jingzhou," Qin Yuhan said coolly. "Haiyue Group's customs manifest was approved three days ago. Your family's terminal company has no legal right to chain a public customs gate."

"Legal right is a flexible phrase in Shenzhen," Shen Jingzhou chuckled. He snapped his fingers, and a lawyer stepped forward with a blue folder. "It is 4:30 PM right now. You have thirty minutes before your vessel misses its slot and that 80 million penalty drops on Haiyue's head. Luckily for you, I am a reasonable man."

He tapped the folder. "Sign over Haiyue's Southeast Asia shipping route and your five-year lease at Pier Nine to Shen Maritime Holdings for a 1 million transfer fee. The moment your ink dries, my men cut the chains, the hold disappears, and your cargo sails on time."

Lin Jian's face flushed with anger. "1 million for a route we spent two years and 300 million building? That is daylight robbery!"

"I was speaking to President Qin," Shen Jingzhou said coldly. His gaze drifted past her and landed squarely on Chen Xuan. He blinked, taking in the beige handbag hanging from Chen Xuan's wrist and the chipped green metal thermos.

A loud, condescending laugh burst from Shen Jingzhou's throat.

"Wait a second," Shen Jingzhou sneered, pointing at Chen Xuan. "Is this the famous husband from Shanghai? The modest office worker who married the heiress of Haiyue Group? President Qin, you came all the way to Shenzhen to fight the Shen Family, and you brought a bag-carrier drinking tea out of a tin can?"

Qin Yuhan stepped half a pace forward, her eyes flashing with cold fire. "Watch your mouth, Shen Jingzhou. My husband is ten times the man you will ever be. He earns his living honestly, treats his family well, and does not hide behind padlocks to extort women. Tear up your contract. Haiyue will never sign under blackmail."

Chen Xuan patted his wife's shoulder gently, his expression completely calm. He glanced at his wristwatch, showing 4:33 PM, and smiled faintly at Shen Jingzhou.

"It is not even 5:00 PM yet," Chen Xuan said pleasantly. "Why are you in such a rush?"

Shen Jingzhou laughed harder, leaning back against his Mercedes. "Fine. Stand out here and freeze until 5:00 PM. Let us see who opens these iron gates for you and your little bag-carrier."
