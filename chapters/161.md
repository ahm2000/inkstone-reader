# Chapter 161: Four Thirty at Pier Nine

For five full seconds, the only sound outside Shenzhen Pier Nine was the dull slap of seawater against the concrete pilings and the distant hum of cranes at Shekou.

Then Shen Jingzhou threw his head back and burst out laughing.

It was a loud, ugly laugh that echoed off the iron gate. He bent forward slightly, slapping his thigh, then straightened up and pointed two fingers straight at Chen Xuan.

"Did everyone hear that?" Shen Jingzhou crowed, looking around at his six bodyguards and the two port inspectors. "He does not like working overtime! Oh, my stomach hurts! Somebody grab me a tissue!"

The six broad-shouldered bodyguards burst into rough laughter. Even Chief Inspector Lu Guobin let out a thin, mocking snort behind his hand.

Shen Jingzhou walked two steps closer, his eyes sweeping slowly down Chen Xuan from head to toe. He stared at the cheap gray windbreaker worn open over Chen Xuan's plain white cotton shirt, the faded dark trousers, the cloth folder under his arm, and the scratched green thermos gripped loosely in his right hand.

"Look at this guy," Shen Jingzhou sneered, flicking ash from a fresh cigarette he had just lit with his gold lighter. "A fifty-yuan windbreaker from a street stall, a dented tea bottle that looks like it came from a train station, and a tiny shipping clerk badge from Shanghai. And he stands at my gate and gives me a deadline?"

Shen Jingzhou raised his left wrist and tapped the face of his gold watch.

"It is four thirty right now, tea-boy," Shen Jingzhou said with a cruel grin. "You gave me until five o'clock. That means you have thirty whole minutes left. Tell me, what terrifying move are you going to pull out of that little cloth folder? Are you going to write a complaint letter to the dock office?"

Behind Chen Xuan, Haiyue Group's local port manager Zhou Ming wiped a bead of sweat from his forehead. His face was pale with worry. He stepped half a pace closer to Qin Yuhan and spoke in a tight, urgent whisper.

"President Qin, please think carefully," Zhou Ming muttered, glancing nervously at the massive hull of the Haiyue Star behind the locked gate. "Every hour we sit here costs us hundreds of thousands of yuan. The cold-chain fruit and medical goods in the front holds cannot wait seven days. Young Master Shen is not bluffing. The Shen Family controls half the private cranes from Shekou to Yantian Port."

Qin Yuhan did not even blink. Her posture stayed straight and graceful in the afternoon heat, her dark eyes fixed calmly on Shen Jingzhou.

"Thank you, Manager Zhou," Qin Yuhan said evenly. "You may step back."

Zhou Ming opened his mouth to argue, saw the iron resolve in Qin Yuhan's gaze, and swallowed hard before retreating two paces.

Shen Jingzhou caught the exchange and chuckled smugly. He took a long drag of his cigarette and blew a cloud of white smoke toward the sky.

"Listen to your manager, President Qin," Shen Jingzhou said, his tone dripping with arrogance. "He actually lives in Shenzhen, so he understands how things work down here. You might be a big deal in Shanghai, sitting in some glass office in Lujiazui looking at the Oriental Pearl Tower, thinking your money opens every door. Well, this is not Shanghai. This is Shenzhen."

He spread his arms wide, gesturing toward the busy coastline and the towering skyline rising in the haze to the north.

"Do you know where my family lives, tea-boy?" Shen Jingzhou asked, turning his mocking gaze back to Chen Xuan. "Shenzhen Bay No. 1. Three whole penthouses overlooking the water, worth 400 million yuan each. When my father Shen Wanshan hosts dinner at our club in Futian CBD, bank presidents and port directors wait in the lobby just to shake his hand."

Chief Inspector Lu Guobin immediately straightened his collar and nodded along.

"President Qin, listen to reason," Lu Guobin said stiffly, trying to sound official while glancing sideways to make sure Shen Jingzhou was pleased. "The Shen Family has supported Shenzhen's harbor growth for twenty years. If you let an ordinary clerk insult Young Master Shen at Pier Nine, I am afraid our safety review committee will have to check every single one of those six hundred containers by hand. That could take two full weeks."

"Two weeks," Shen Jingzhou repeated with a cold laugh. "At 8 million yuan a day, that is more than 100 million yuan gone in smoke. And all because you are too proud to sign a simple piece of paper."

He nodded to his assistant, who stepped forward again holding the thick printed contract and the black fountain pen.

"Sign over 30% of Haiyue Group's southern routes right now, President Qin," Shen Jingzhou said smoothly. "Let Shenhai Shipping run your unloading here, and tell your little tea-boy to apologize on his knees for wasting my afternoon. Do that, and I will have Chief Inspector Lu unlock the padlock before four forty."

The salty breeze ruffled a stray strand of hair across Qin Yuhan's cheek. She tucked it neatly behind her ear, her expression calm and completely unmoved.

"Shen Jingzhou," Qin Yuhan said clearly, her voice carrying across the wide concrete road. "You can keep your penthouses at Shenzhen Bay No. 1, and you can keep your fake inspection papers. Haiyue Group will not give up 30% of its southern routes. We will not give up 1%. Not today, and not ever."

Shen Jingzhou's smirk stiffened. "You really want to burn 100 million yuan just to save face?"

"I am not saving face," Qin Yuhan replied coolly. She stepped forward until she stood right beside Chen Xuan's shoulder, her gaze clear and steady. "I am simply waiting."

Shen Jingzhou stared at her in disbelief. "Waiting for what? For this guy in a cheap windbreaker to kick the iron gate down?"

"Mr. Chen said five o'clock," Qin Yuhan answered without a trace of doubt in her voice. "It is only four thirty. I have thirty minutes to spare."

For a heartbeat, Shen Jingzhou was too stunned to speak. He looked from the poised, famous president of Haiyue Group to the completely plain man standing beside her with a scratched green thermos. The contrast was so absurd that his face flushed dark red with anger.

"Fine! Wonderful!" Shen Jingzhou barked, waving a hand sharply at his assistant. "Bring my folding chair out of the trunk! Since President Qin wants to stand in the sun and watch a clown act for thirty minutes, I will sit right here and watch with her!"

The assistant hurried to the black luxury sedan and pulled out a sleek leather folding chair, setting it in the shade of the car. Shen Jingzhou dropped into it, crossed one linen-trousered leg over the other, and grinned darkly at Chen Xuan.

"Twenty-eight minutes left, clerk," Shen Jingzhou mocked, raising his cigarette in a lazy salute. "Show me what Hengxin Logistics can do. Call your little office manager in Shanghai! Call the police! Call whoever you want! Let us see who dares touch the Shen Family at Pier Nine!"

Chen Xuan did not argue. He did not even look annoyed.

He glanced down at the simple black wristwatch on his left wrist, checked the minute hand, and looked back up at Shen Jingzhou with calm, quiet eyes.

"Twenty-eight minutes is plenty," Chen Xuan said softly.

He tucked his cloth folder under his left arm, shifted the green thermos to his left hand, and walked five unhurried paces toward the quiet edge of the concrete dock road.
