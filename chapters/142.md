# Chapter 142: Flight to the Capital

Saturday morning arrived crisp and bright over Shanghai. At seven o'clock, the city streets were still half asleep, with only breakfast stalls puffing white steam into the cool air.

Chen Xuan locked his apartment door wearing his familiar cheap dark windbreaker, plain gray trousers, and worn canvas shoes. He carried no suitcase at all. In his left hand hung his scratched green thermos, freshly filled with hot water and a pinch of jasmine tea leaves. In his windbreaker pocket sat his phone, his keys, and two warm scallion pancakes wrapped in thin paper from the corner cart downstairs.

A plain black car driven by Number 7 picked him up at the edge of the residential block and took the quiet outer highway straight to the south executive hangar outside Shanghai.

Inside the wide gray hangar, a sleek matte-gray private jet stood ready on the clean concrete floor. Its engines were already humming softly, and the short boarding stairs were lowered.

Two pilots in neat dark uniforms stepped forward beside the stairs and inclined their heads respectfully as Chen Xuan walked over.

"Good morning, sir," the lead pilot said calmly. "Clear skies all the way north. Flight time to the capital will be two hours and fifteen minutes."

"Thank you for coming in early on a Saturday," Chen Xuan said warmly.

Number 7 followed Chen Xuan up the metal steps and pulled the cabin door shut with a solid click. Inside, the cabin was quiet, spacious, and smelled faintly of clean leather and cedar. Wide cream-colored seats lined both sides of the aisle, separated by smooth walnut tables.

Chen Xuan took a window seat near the middle, unzipped his cheap windbreaker halfway, and set his scratched green thermos neatly in the center of the polished walnut table.

The contrast almost made Number 7 smile. The cabin table alone was worth more than most people's cars, yet the faded, dented fifteen-yuan green thermos looked completely at home sitting right in the middle of it.

"Would you like breakfast, sir?" Number 7 asked politely, taking the seat across the aisle after fastening his belt. "The kitchen prepared fresh shrimp dumplings, steamed buns, and hot Longjing tea."

"I already bought two scallion pancakes on the way out," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile, patting his pocket. "Save the dumplings for yourself and the pilots. And my jasmine tea is still hot."

Number 7 chuckled softly, his usually sharp eyes warming with genuine amusement. "Yes, sir. I should have known better than to compete with that thermos."

Minutes later, the private jet rolled smoothly out of the hangar, lined up on the quiet side runway, and lifted effortlessly into the pale morning sky. Shanghai shrank into neat rows of rooftops, then vanished beneath a soft blanket of white clouds.

Once the plane leveled off, Number 7 opened a slim folder on his lap and spoke in a calm, steady voice.

"General Qin Zhengguo sent a short update an hour ago," Number 7 reported. "The weather in the Western Hills is cool, around fourteen degrees, with light wind by the lake. Elder Song woke at six as usual, ate a bowl of millet porridge, and has already moved to the stone pavilion."

"Ninety years old and still up at six," Chen Xuan remarked softly, looking out at the bright clouds. "That generation was built out of iron."

"They were," Number 7 agreed. He closed the folder and glanced across the aisle. "Number 1 told me you insisted on flying up alone with me instead of bringing a full escort team. Still, I positioned two of our cars at the capital's executive runway. One will drive us to the Western Hills, and the second will wait near the south gate."

"That is more than enough," Chen Xuan said. He unscrewed the dented metal lid of his green thermos, letting a gentle wisp of jasmine steam curl into the quiet cabin, and took a slow sip. "How is the capital looking after the clean-up?"

Number 7 leaned back slightly, his expression turning thoughtful. "Quieter than it has been in twenty years, sir. Half the luxury clubs along the third ring road closed their doors last week. People who used to brag about knowing deputy ministers are now walking to work with their heads down. Even the hotel lobbies feel different. Less shouting, fewer fake smiles."

"Good," Chen Xuan said simply. "A quiet city lets regular people get their sleep."

"There is one rumor floating around the ministries, though," Number 7 added with a faint, dry grin. "Several senior officials are convinced that a retired old marshal from the southern border came out of hiding to direct Gao Zhengyi and Zhou Zhenhua. They cannot believe anyone else could have moved forty departments in one week."

Chen Xuan laughed lightly, shaking his head. "Let them keep guessing. An old marshal from the southern border sounds much more impressive than an office clerk from Shanghai."

Number 7 smiled too, though his eyes held deep, quiet respect. He knew better than anyone that the calm young man sitting across from him in a cheap windbreaker could empty a corrupt ministry before his tea even cooled.

The rest of the flight passed in peaceful silence. Chen Xuan ate his two scallion pancakes cleanly over the paper wrapper, drank half his thermos of jasmine tea, and rested his eyes for twenty minutes while the jet hummed steadily northward.

At ten past ten, the jet descended smoothly through thin northern clouds and touched down on the capital's quiet executive runway. This strip lay far from the busy passenger terminals, surrounded by tall pine trees and low gray hangars. Not a single commercial airliner was in sight.

The jet taxied to a stop near the far end of the tarmac. Two plain black sedans were already waiting thirty paces away, their engines idling softly.

When the cabin door opened, cool northern air rushed in, carrying the crisp scent of pine needles. Number 7 stepped down the stairs first, his sharp eyes sweeping the tarmac, the perimeter fence, and the exit road out of habit.

Then Number 7 stopped halfway down the last step, his shoulders tensing slightly.

Chen Xuan stepped down behind him, zipping his cheap windbreaker against the cool breeze, green thermos tucked comfortably in his hand. "Something wrong?"

Number 7 did not point. He only nodded slightly toward the far side of the low concrete fence along the exit road, fifty meters ahead of the waiting sedans.

An unmarked dark van was already parked in the shade of the tall pines outside the gate. As Chen Xuan's feet touched the tarmac, the sliding side door of the van opened without a sound.

Three figures in plain black gear and full black masks stepped out smoothly onto the gravel. They carried no visible emblems and no heavy packs. They did not wave at the waiting sedans, did not glance at Number 7, and did not say a single word to White Guard.

Like dark water flowing around stones, the first Black Guard member moved briskly ahead along the outer pine line toward the main road, while the second guard and the third glided into clean, silent formation fifty meters ahead of the exit lane. Within ten seconds, they had melted neatly into the shadows of the trees and the moving sedan waiting beyond the gate, clearing the route ahead without making a sound.

Number 7 let out a slow, quiet breath, watching the empty stretch of road where the masked figures had just vanished.

"Our flight took two hours and fifteen minutes," Number 7 murmured, his voice filled with reluctant amazement. "They were already parked outside the fence before our wheels even touched the runway. Not a single call to our dispatch, not a single word to my drivers."

"They do not like small talk," Chen Xuan said mildly, walking calmly toward the first waiting black sedan.

Number 7 shook his head once, recovered his composure, and hurried forward to open the rear door. "Sometimes I wonder how they even know which runway we are using before the tower clears us."

"Do not waste sleep trying to figure them out," Chen Xuan replied with a faint smile as he climbed into the back seat and set his scratched green thermos beside him. "As long as they keep their fifty-meter rule, let them walk wherever they like."

Number 7 closed the door firmly, walked around to the front passenger seat, and nodded to the driver. "Western Hills. Take the north garden road."

The black sedan pulled smoothly off the quiet tarmac and rolled out through the open iron gate. Ahead on the tree-lined road leading west toward the hills, the morning traffic was light and peaceful, and fifty meters in front of them, a plain dark van kept pace effortlessly without ever looking back.
