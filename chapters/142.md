# Chapter 142: Flight to the Capital

The next morning at 6:45 AM, the excuse had already worked without a single question. Quietly leaving the bedroom, Chen Xuan stood in the front hallway of Emerald Riverside Villa, zipping his cheap dark windbreaker over a gray sweater.

He had just bent down to slip his feet into his worn canvas shoes when the bedroom door creaked open behind him.

Qin Yuhan shuffled out of the bedroom in her soft pink cardigan and house slippers, her long hair still slightly tousled from sleep. In her hands, she held his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos, freshly filled to the brim with steaming jasmine tea.

"I still think Sun Haoran is taking advantage of your good temper," Qin Yuhan murmured, stepping close to straighten his jacket collar. "Making a junior logistics supervisor ride all the way out to the suburban warehouse on a Saturday morning just to count wooden crates. He should at least pay you double overtime."

"It is barely a morning of checking inventory," Chen Xuan chuckled, catching her hand and pressing a warm kiss to her forehead. "Go back to bed for another two hours. I left hot millet porridge and pan-fried scallion buns keeping warm in the kitchen. Eat them while they are fresh."

"You always spoil me," Qin Yuhan sighed softly, a fond smile curving her lips as she handed over the green thermos. "Do not skip lunch out there. If you finish early, come straight home. Grandpa called last night and complained again that you have not come over to lose a chess game to him all month."

"Lose?" Chen Xuan raised an eyebrow with mock indignation. "Last time I let him take back three moves and he still lost his chariot."

Qin Yuhan laughed, her sleepiness melting away. "Keep your voice down or he will hear you from Beijing. Go on now, drive safely, and call me when you head back."

"I will be home in time to braise pork ribs for dinner," Chen Xuan promised.

He stepped out into the brisk morning air and walked down the quiet tree-lined lane of the villa complex. Near the main iron gate, Auntie Zhang was trimming the last yellow chrysanthemums along the flowerbed in a thick floral vest.

"Up so early on a Saturday, Xuan?" Auntie Zhang called out cheerfully, brushing soil from her gloved hands. "The wind has a real bite to it today. Make sure you keep your collar buttoned up."

"Will do, Auntie Zhang," Chen Xuan replied with a warm grin. "Do not stay out in the draft too long yourself."

"Do not worry about my old bones," she laughed, waving him off as naturally as any longtime neighbor.

Two blocks past the main avenue, beside a row of shuttered breakfast shops, a plain dark sedan sat idling at the curb. The moment Chen Xuan approached, the rear door opened smoothly.

Number 7 stepped out wearing a simple navy wool jacket and dark trousers. He wore no mask, no earpiece, and no tactical gear, looking entirely like a polite driver waiting for a family member.

"Good morning, Young Master," Number 7 greeted him with quiet respect, holding the door steady. "The airfield is clear and the flight plan is logged under a standard charter. We can be in the air within twenty minutes."

"Morning, Number 7," Chen Xuan said easily, sliding into the back seat with his green thermos resting on his lap. "Sorry to drag you out so early on a weekend."

"Never an inconvenience, Young Master," Number 7 replied smoothly as he took the wheel and merged into the light Saturday traffic.

As the sedan rolled toward the outskirts of Shanghai, Number 7 glanced in the rearview mirror, a faint crease of curiosity between his brows.

"Young Master, if I may mention one thing," Number 7 said evenly. "When I swept the streets around Emerald Riverside Villa yesterday evening as a routine precaution, I noticed quite a few regulars along your daily route. The sweet potato vendor by Hengxin, the caretaker woman at your gate, two delivery riders on the avenue."

Chen Xuan leaned back against the leather seat, calm and amused. "And?"

"I ran quiet background checks on every single one of them last night," Number 7 admitted, his voice carrying genuine puzzlement. "Every file came back 100 percent clean, logical, and airtight. Birth records, tax histories, family ties, all solid. There is not a single flaw anywhere, yet my instincts still felt something unusual about how evenly they are spread around your neighborhood."

Chen Xuan watched golden ginkgo leaves swirl across the highway and let out a soft chuckle. "If every file is clean and they are kind to my wife, then they are just good neighbors, Number 7. Do not waste your energy digging into honest folks."

"Understood, Young Master," Number 7 answered promptly, letting the question drop without another word.

Half an hour later, the sedan rolled onto a quiet general aviation strip far from the busy commercial terminals. A sleek white midsize jet waited on the sunlit tarmac, its engines humming softly. At the foot of the boarding stairs stood the captain and a cabin steward in simple dark civilian jackets.

"Welcome aboard, Young Master," both men said calmly, inclining their heads as Chen Xuan stepped up the stairs.

Inside the warm cabin, Chen Xuan took a window seat, unscrewed the lid of his dented green thermos, and took a slow sip of Yuhan's hot jasmine tea. The fragrance filled his nose as the jet accelerated smoothly down the runway and lifted into the pale blue November sky.

Across the aisle, Number 7 opened a thin folder. "Our latest confirmation from Beijing came in ten minutes ago, Young Master. Elder Song cleared the entire north ridge of the Western Hills before sunrise. Every camera along the perimeter road has been powered down, and every security post was pulled back to the south valley. There is not a single aide or guard anywhere near the lake."

"Good," Chen Xuan murmured, gazing out at the sea of white clouds glowing beneath the autumn sun. "Then let us not keep a ninety-year-old gentleman waiting."

Two hours later, the jet descended gently through the clear northern sky and touched down at a quiet airstrip on the western edge of Beijing. A plain black sedan waited beside the runway. Number 7 stepped out first and opened the rear door.

"This way, Young Master," Number 7 said calmly. "The Western Hills are twenty minutes up the road."
