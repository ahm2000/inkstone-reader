# Chapter 142: Flight to the Capital

Saturday morning arrived crisp and bright over Shanghai. At six forty five, golden sunlight filtered through the curtains while the street stalls downstairs puffed white steam into the cool air.

In the hallway by the door, Chen Xuan zipped up his cheap dark windbreaker over gray trousers and slipped into his worn canvas shoes. Qin Yuhan stood in her pink slippers and cardigan, sleepily handing him his scratched green metal thermos filled with fresh hot jasmine tea.

"I still think Sun Haoran is taking advantage of your good temper," Qin Yuhan murmured, straightening his collar. "Making a 12,000 yuan clerk ride all the way out to the suburban warehouse on a Saturday morning just to count wooden crates."

"It is barely an hour of counting," Chen Xuan chuckled, kissing her forehead warmly. "Go back to sleep for another two hours. I left hot millet porridge and steamed buns in the pot. I will be back before three o'clock with fresh chestnuts from the south market."

"You had better not let that warehouse supervisor keep you past lunch," Qin Yuhan warned playfully, tapping his chest. "My mother called yesterday and asked if we are coming over for dinner tomorrow night. She wants you to make your sweet and sour fish again, and Dad said he wants a rematch at chess after you beat Grandfather last week."

"Tell Father in law my rooks are always ready," Chen Xuan grinned. "Go rest, honey. See you this afternoon."

He stepped out, locked the apartment door gently, and walked downstairs. At the corner cart, he bought two hot scallion pancakes wrapped in brown paper and tucked them into his coat pocket.

Fifty paces around the corner, a plain black sedan waited out of sight of the apartment windows. Number 7 stood beside the open rear door in crisp white gear, bowing his head respectfully.

"Good morning, sir," Number 7 said quietly. "The road to the south executive hangar is completely clear."

"Good morning, Number 7," Chen Xuan smiled, climbing into the back seat with his green thermos. "Let us go."

Twenty minutes later, the sedan rolled straight into a wide gray private hangar outside Shanghai. Sitting on the concrete floor with its boarding stairs lowered was Chen Xuan's sleek pearl white private jet.

Two uniformed pilots stood at attention beside the stairs and bowed respectfully as Chen Xuan approached.

"Good morning, sir," the lead pilot said calmly. "Skies are clear all the way north. Flight time to Beijing is two hours and ten minutes."

"Thank you both for coming in early on a Saturday," Chen Xuan said warmly.

Number 7 followed Chen Xuan up the steps into the cabin and shut the door. Inside, soft cream leather seats lined both sides of the aisle beside polished walnut tables.

Chen Xuan took a middle window seat, unzipped his windbreaker halfway, and set his scratched fifteen yuan green thermos right in the center of the gleaming walnut table.

The contrast made Number 7 smile faintly. That single walnut table cost over 200,000 yuan, yet the dented metal thermos looked completely at home on it.

"Would you like breakfast, sir?" Number 7 asked politely, taking the seat across the aisle. "The cabin kitchen prepared crystal shrimp dumplings, crab roe buns, and hot Longjing tea."

"I already bought two scallion pancakes at the corner stall, and Yuhan brewed me a full thermos of jasmine tea before I left," Chen Xuan smiled, patting his pocket. "Eat the dumplings yourself and share the rest with the pilots. Nothing beats a three yuan street pancake while it is still crispy."

Number 7 chuckled softly. "Yes, sir. I should have known better than to compete with Madam's tea and the corner pancake cart."

Minutes later, the pearl white jet glided out onto the runway and lifted smoothly into the blue morning sky above the clouds.

Once the plane leveled off, Number 7 opened a slim white folder on his lap.

"Elder Song's personal secretary sent a final confirmation twenty minutes ago," Number 7 reported quietly. "The Western Hills lake is fourteen degrees this morning. Elder Song woke at six, ate millet porridge, and sent every single guard, secretary, and caretaker two kilometers away from the north garden. He walked to the stone pavilion alone."

"Ninety years old and still walking the hills at six in the morning," Chen Xuan remarked softly. "That generation was built out of iron."

"They truly were," Number 7 agreed, closing the folder. "Even Elder Qin Zhengguo and Premier Gu Chengshan were told only that Elder Song wanted a quiet morning alone by the water. Nobody in Beijing has any idea a plane from Shanghai is landing on the sealed executive runway."

"That saves everyone a headache," Chen Xuan said, taking a slow sip of hot jasmine tea. "How is the capital looking after the audits?"

Number 7 leaned back with a nod. "Cleaner than it has been in thirty years, sir. Your 300,000,000,000,000 yuan free gift stabilized every major bank overnight, and the treasury has already recovered over 820,000,000,000 yuan in stolen public assets across the forty cleaned out departments."

"There is one funny rumor making the rounds in Beijing, though," Number 7 added with a grin. "Half the ministry clerks are whispering that the mysterious holder of the Black Order must be a hundred year old recluse who meditates under waterfalls."

Chen Xuan laughed aloud, taking a bite of his warm scallion pancake. "A hundred year old hermit under a waterfall? That sounds way too cold. I much prefer my living room sofa and a hot bowl of noodles."

Number 7 laughed with deep respect. Only he and the White Guard knew that the sovereign who had steadied the nation's treasury with 300,000,000,000,000 yuan was currently planning to get back to Shanghai by three o'clock so his wife would not scold his general manager.

At nine forty five, the pearl white jet touched down silently on Beijing's pine ringed executive runway, where two plain black sedans waited.

As Chen Xuan stepped down the stairs with his green thermos, Number 7 glanced fifty meters ahead toward the outer pine gate.

Parked under the trees sat an unmarked dark van. Several figures in plain black gear and full black masks stood silently beside it without numbers or emblems. Without a word, the Black Guard members slipped into formation fifty meters ahead along the mountain road, clearing the route in total silence.

"They never miss a schedule," Chen Xuan smiled, climbing into the back seat. "Let us head up to the Western Hills before Elder Song's tea water gets too hot."
