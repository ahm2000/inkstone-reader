# Chapter 142: Flight to the Capital

Saturday morning arrived crisp and bright over Shanghai. At seven o'clock, the streets were still half asleep, with only breakfast stalls puffing white steam.

Chen Xuan locked his apartment door wearing his cheap dark windbreaker, gray trousers, and worn canvas shoes. He carried no suitcase. In his left hand hung his scratched green thermos of hot jasmine tea. In his pocket sat his phone, keys, and two warm scallion pancakes wrapped in paper from the corner cart downstairs.

A plain black car driven by Number 7 in crisp white gear picked him up at the edge of the block and drove straight to the south executive hangar outside Shanghai.

Inside the wide gray hangar, a sleek private jet stood ready on the concrete floor with its boarding stairs lowered.

Two uniformed pilots stepped forward beside the stairs and inclined their heads respectfully as Chen Xuan walked over.

"Good morning, sir," the lead pilot said calmly. "Clear skies north. Flight time to the capital is two hours and fifteen minutes."

"Thank you for coming in early on a Saturday," Chen Xuan said warmly.

Number 7 followed Chen Xuan up the steps and shut the cabin door. Inside, wide cream seats lined both sides of the aisle beside polished walnut tables.

Chen Xuan took a middle window seat, unzipped his windbreaker halfway, and set his scratched green thermos in the center of the walnut table.

The contrast almost made Number 7 smile. The table cost more than most cars, yet the dented fifteen yuan thermos looked right at home on it.

"Would you like breakfast, sir?" Number 7 asked politely, taking the seat across the aisle. "The kitchen prepared shrimp dumplings, steamed buns, and hot Longjing tea."

"I already bought two scallion pancakes on the way out," Chen Xuan smiled, patting his pocket. "Save the dumplings for yourself and the pilots. My jasmine tea is still hot."

Number 7 chuckled softly. "Yes, sir. I should have known better than to compete with that thermos."

Minutes later, the jet rolled out of the hangar and lifted into the pale sky above the white clouds.

Once the plane leveled off, Number 7 opened a slim folder on his lap.

"General Qin Zhengguo sent an update an hour ago," Number 7 reported. "The Western Hills are cool, around fourteen degrees, with light wind by the lake. Elder Song woke at six, ate millet porridge, and moved to the stone pavilion."

"Ninety years old and still up at six," Chen Xuan remarked softly. "That generation was built out of iron."

"They were," Number 7 agreed, closing the folder. "Number 1 told me you insisted on flying up alone with me instead of bringing a full team. Still, I positioned two cars at the capital's executive runway. One will drive us to the Western Hills, and the second will wait near the south gate."

"That is plenty," Chen Xuan said. He unscrewed his thermos lid and took a slow sip of jasmine tea. "How is the capital looking after the clean up?"

Number 7 leaned back. "Quieter than in twenty years, sir. Half the luxury clubs along the third ring road closed last week. People who bragged about knowing deputy ministers now walk to work with their heads down. Even the hotel lobbies feel quieter."

"Good," Chen Xuan said simply. "A quiet city lets regular people sleep."

"One rumor is floating around the ministries, though," Number 7 added with a dry grin. "Several senior officials believe a retired marshal from the southern border came out of hiding to direct Gao Zhengyi and Zhou Zhenhua. They cannot believe anyone else moved forty departments in one week."

Chen Xuan laughed lightly. "Let them keep guessing. An old marshal sounds much more impressive than an office clerk from Shanghai."

Number 7 smiled with deep respect. He knew the calm young man in the cheap windbreaker could empty a corrupt ministry before his tea cooled.

Chen Xuan ate his scallion pancakes, drank half his tea, and rested his eyes while the jet hummed northward.

At ten past ten, the jet touched down on the capital's executive runway, surrounded by tall pine trees far from the passenger terminals.

The jet stopped near two waiting black sedans.

When the door opened, cool pine scented air rushed in. Number 7 stepped down the stairs first, scanning the tarmac and the exit road.

Halfway down the last step, Number 7 tensed and stopped.

Chen Xuan stepped down behind him, thermos in hand. "Something wrong?"

Number 7 nodded toward the concrete fence along the exit road, fifty meters ahead of the sedans.

An unmarked dark van sat parked under the pines outside the gate. As Chen Xuan stepped onto the tarmac, the sliding door opened silently.

Several figures in plain black gear and full black masks stepped out onto the gravel without emblems or packs. They did not wave at the sedans, glance at Number 7, or speak a word to Number 7's drivers.

One Black Guard member moved briskly along the outer pine line toward the main road, while the others glided into silent formation fifty meters ahead of the exit lane. Within seconds, they melted into the tree shadows and the moving sedan waiting beyond the gate, clearing the route ahead in silence.

Number 7 let out a slow breath, watching the empty road.

"Our flight took two hours and fifteen minutes," Number 7 murmured in amazement. "They were parked outside the fence before our wheels touched down. Not a call to dispatch, not a word to my drivers."

"They do not like small talk," Chen Xuan said mildly, walking toward the first black sedan.

Number 7 shook his head and hurried to open the rear door. "Sometimes I wonder how they know which runway we use before the tower clears us."

"Do not waste sleep figuring them out," Chen Xuan smiled, climbing into the back seat with his thermos. "As long as they keep their fifty meter rule, let them walk wherever they like."

Number 7 closed the door, took the front passenger seat, and told the driver, "Western Hills. North garden road."

The black sedan rolled through the iron gate toward the hills while fifty meters ahead, a plain dark van kept pace effortlessly without looking back.
