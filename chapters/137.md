# Chapter 137: Black Monday at Haiyue Tower

Inside the 38th-floor boardroom of Haiyue Tower, the wide wall screen glowed with an ugly, plunging line.

By 11:30 AM on Monday morning, Haiyue Group's shares had dropped 10% in less than two hours and slammed straight into the daily floor. A massive wall of sell orders sat stacked at the bottom price, refusing to budge.

The air inside the glass-walled room felt heavy and tight, despite the crisp late autumn sky outside.

At the far end of the long conference table, Chief Financial Officer Lin Shufen hung up her desk phone with a trembling hand and looked toward the head of the room.

"That was the fourth bank since 10:00 AM," Lin Shufen said tightly, her face pale. "Shanghai Commercial Bank, Coastal Trust, East Harbor Bank, and Jingzhou City Credit. Every single branch manager repeated the exact same warning. If our share price stays pinned at the floor and the smuggling rumor is not cleared by Wednesday afternoon, they will call in 6 billion yuan in credit lines immediately."

A sharp gasp ran around the table.

Director Xu Minghui slammed his palm flat on the mahogany surface, rattling his porcelain teacup.

"I told you this would happen, President Qin!" Xu Minghui shouted, his face flushed with panic. "We should never have crossed Northern Ledger in Jingzhou! Look at the screen! There are 300 million shares sitting on the sell side, and every news feed in Shanghai is talking about a customs raid on our third dock!"

"There was no customs raid," Qin Yuhan said evenly.

She sat at the head of the table in a tailored charcoal wool blazer, her posture straight and her dark eyes calm. While half the directors around her sweated through their collars, she looked like the only steady anchor in the room.

"President Qin, it does not matter whether the raid happened or not!" another director interrupted nervously. "The market believes it! Every supplier in Pudong is calling our dispatch office right now. If four banks pull 6 billion yuan on Wednesday, our ships stop moving and our payroll freezes!"

"We should halt trading right now and call Northern Ledger's Shanghai representative," Xu Minghui pressed eagerly. "Offer them a forty percent stake in the third dock. Once they see we are willing to share the Handong route, this whole storm disappears overnight!"

Qin Yuhan's gaze shifted to Xu Minghui, sharp and cold enough to silence the room.

"Read the order book, Director Xu," Qin Yuhan said clearly. "Do you think panicked retail shareholders dumped 8 billion yuan of Haiyue stock in ninety minutes? This is a coordinated short attack by an overseas fund. They forged a customs memo, dumped borrowed shares to hit the floor, and timed it to frighten our lenders into forcing a fire sale."

She stood up and walked slowly toward the glowing wall screen, her heels clicking firmly across the polished floor.

"If we beg Northern Ledger today and hand over our third dock at half price, Haiyue Group will never stand upright again," she declared, her voice ringing with quiet steel. "Every time a foreign fund wants a piece of our business, they will just print another lie and watch us kneel. I will not sell a single meter of dock space to raiders."

Xu Minghui opened his mouth to argue, then shrank back under the fierce steadiness of her stare.

Qin Yuhan turned back to Lin Shufen and her assistant Zhou Mei.

"First, Zhou Mei, send our legal director directly to the provincial customs bureau with the original manifests, container seals, and automated port logs from the past thirty days," Qin Yuhan ordered crisply. "Do not wait for standard email replies. Have them stay at the bureau until an official clearance notice is signed and published before tomorrow's market open."

"Yes, President Qin," Zhou Mei replied immediately, already typing on her tablet.

"Second, Director Lin, unlock 1.5 billion yuan from our internal operating reserve," Qin Yuhan continued. "Notify our top twenty shipping partners and dock crews that every invoice will be paid three days early this week. Our ships sail on schedule, and not one crane stops turning."

Lin Shufen let out a shaky breath and nodded, some color returning to her cheeks. "Understood. I will arrange the transfers right away."

"Everyone else," Qin Yuhan said, looking around the table, "go back to your departments and keep your people calm. The meeting is adjourned."

One by one, the directors filed out of the boardroom, whispering anxiously among themselves. Xu Minghui lingered for a second at the glass door, muttering darkly before stepping into the hallway.

When the room finally fell silent, Qin Yuhan walked over to the tall window overlooking Lujiazui. Cold November wind swept pale clouds across the river far below.

Only now, completely alone, did she let her shoulders drop a fraction of an inch.

She knew the numbers better than anyone in that room. Clearing the customs rumor would stop retail panic, and 1.5 billion yuan in cash would keep the docks running for a week. Neither one could stop a foreign hedge fund willing to throw 10 billion or 12 billion yuan of short orders at Haiyue's stock tomorrow morning. If the banks panicked anyway, Haiyue was standing on a razor's edge.

Her phone vibrated softly against her palm.

She glanced down and saw a simple message from Chen Xuan: *Cold wind picked up this afternoon. Should I make braised pork ribs or hot lotus root rib soup for dinner tonight?*

A warm, genuine smile softened the tension in her eyes. Even with billions crashing outside her window, her husband was only thinking about what would keep her warm at the dinner table.

She typed back quickly: *Lotus root soup sounds wonderful. I will be home around 7:00 PM.*

She slipped the phone into her blazer pocket, took a deep breath, and turned back to her desk. She would fight the storm here on the 38th floor and leave every ounce of worry outside their front door.
