# Chapter 137: Black Monday at Haiyue Tower

By 11:30 on Monday morning, the wall screen inside the 38th-floor boardroom of Haiyue Tower glowed with an ugly, plunging line.

Haiyue Group's shares had dropped 10% in two hours and hit the floor.

Every few seconds, the red numbers ticked lower.

Inside the glass boardroom, the air felt hot and tight.

At the far end, Chief Financial Officer Lin Shufen hung up her desk phone with a trembling hand and looked toward the head of the room.

"That was the fourth bank since ten o'clock," Lin Shufen said tightly. "Shanghai Commercial Bank, Coastal Trust, East Harbor Bank, and Jingzhou City Credit. Every manager said the same thing. If our share price stays down and the smuggling rumor is not cleared by Wednesday afternoon, they will call in 6 billion yuan in loans immediately."

A loud gasp ran around the table.

Director Xu Minghui slammed his palm onto the stone table, rattling two water glasses.

"6 billion yuan!" Xu Minghui shouted, glaring at Qin Yuhan. "Where do we find 6 billion yuan in forty-eight hours? Our accounts only have 800 million yuan left after paying for the new cranes! If the banks freeze us Wednesday, we cannot even pay our dock workers on Friday!"

Beside him, Director Tang Weiguo wiped sweat from his forehead with a handkerchief and pointed at the plunging chart.

"Everyone is sharing that overseas report!" Tang Weiguo said loudly. "They claim customs officers raided our third dock for smuggled electronics! Three shipping clients already asked to move their containers to other ports!"

"It is a lie," Qin Yuhan said.

Her voice was not loud, yet it cut through the shouting like cold steel.

She sat upright at the head of the table in a tailored navy suit. Her eyes were red-rimmed from a sleepless night checking customs logs, and pale shadows sat beneath her lashes. Still, her back was straight, and her fingers rested calmly on her closed blue folder.

"Not a single customs officer stepped onto our third dock this weekend," Qin Yuhan said. "Every manifest from the past six months is clean and stamped. Our legal team went to the customs bureau at nine thirty for an official written statement."

"An official statement takes three days!" Xu Minghui yelled, leaning forward. "We do not have three days, Chairwoman! We just lost a tenth of our market value before lunch! And everyone here knows why!"

Several younger directors looked down at their notebooks. Xu Minghui did not stop.

"It is because of Friday in Jingzhou City!" Xu Minghui barked, pointing a trembling finger at her. "You stood up on stage beside some nobody clerk from a tiny freight office and tore up Wanfeng Freight's alliance on television! Everyone knows who backed that Zurich firm! Now overseas wolves are tearing our throat out, and that little clerk is sitting somewhere eating noodles without a care!"

"Mind your tone, Director Xu," Lin Shufen snapped, frowning sharply. "Chairwoman Qin stopped twenty ports from handing our schedules to a foreign mailbox firm."

"What good is pride if Haiyue Group goes bankrupt on Wednesday?" Tang Weiguo chimed in, his voice rising in panic. "We have thirty years of shares tied up here! Two brokerage houses in Hong Kong already called me asking if Haiyue will sell Dock One and Dock Two for quick cash!"

The room went dead quiet.

Qin Yuhan's dark eyes turned slowly toward Tang Weiguo. Her gaze was so sharp that he shrank back half an inch into his chair.

"Say that again, Director Tang," Qin Yuhan said quietly.

Tang Weiguo swallowed hard, glancing at Xu Minghui for support. "It is survival, Chairwoman. Dock One and Dock Two are our best assets. If we sell them today, even at a discount, we raise 5 billion yuan by tomorrow afternoon. We pay down the banks and keep Dock Three alive. Cutting off an arm to save the body is our only chance."

"I agree with Tang Weiguo," Xu Minghui said quickly, nodding hard. "Three other directors agree too. Call a board vote right now! Sell the two docks before the stock drops another ten percent tomorrow!"

Anxious murmurs of agreement rose from half the table. Several older directors nodded grimly at the red numbers blinking on the screen.

Qin Yuhan did not raise her voice. She picked up her teacup, took one sip, and set it down with a crisp clink.

"First," Qin Yuhan said clearly, meeting every pair of eyes around the table, "the Hong Kong brokers who called you this morning are the same people who paid for those fake smuggling rumors. They crashed our stock so you would panic and hand over our deepest docks for pennies."

Xu Minghui opened his mouth, but Qin Yuhan raised one hand slightly and stopped him cold.

"Second," she continued, calm and unyielding, "my father spent thirty years building those three docks out of mud flats and rock. As long as I sit in this chair, Haiyue Group will not sell a single meter of wharf to foreign short-sellers. If anyone wants a vote to sell our docks at a fire-sale price, put your name on paper. I hold fifty-two percent of the voting shares, and my answer is no."

Silence fell like heavy iron across the 38th-floor boardroom.

Xu Minghui's face turned gray. Tang Weiguo slumped back, muttering bitterly, "Fine. Have it your way. When the banks lock our doors Wednesday, do not blame us."

"Lin Shufen," Qin Yuhan said, turning to her Chief Financial Officer. "Release our six-month customs records to every newspaper and bank in Shanghai before two o'clock. Use 500 million yuan from our spare cash to buy back our shares this afternoon so the market sees we are not running."

Lin Shufen hesitated, worry clear on her face. "Chairwoman, brokers say the main seller is an overseas fund called Aegis Meridian. Their morning sell orders topped 8 billion yuan. Our 500 million yuan will barely slow them down tomorrow."

"I know," Qin Yuhan answered softly. For one brief second, pure tiredness showed in her red-rimmed eyes. Then her chin lifted again. "We still stand our ground. Meeting adjourned."

One by one, the frightened directors gathered their folders and filed out, whispering anxiously in the hall.

Soon, only Qin Yuhan remained inside the glass room. She walked to the window and looked down at the mist-covered cranes of Shanghai Harbor.

Her phone vibrated once. It was a short text from her father, Qin Zhengming: *"Do not bend. If the docks go down, we go down standing."*

Qin Yuhan let out a breath, her fingers tightening around the warm phone.

She glanced back at the red chart on the wall screen. For a fleeting second, she remembered the plain clerk in the white shirt standing at the corner table in Jingzhou City, holding his green thermos without fear.

She shook her head faintly and turned back to her desk. This was Haiyue Group's storm, and she would face Tuesday morning on her own two feet.
