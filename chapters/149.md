# Chapter 149: The Quiet Backer

Fifteen minutes after dinner, the carved rosewood Chinese chess board sat open on the low tea table in the villa living room.

Elder Qin Zhengguo perched his reading glasses on his nose, rolled up his gray sweater sleeves like a seasoned commander inspecting a battle map, and slammed his red chariot down with a crisp wooden clack.

"Watch closely, Zhengming," Elder Qin declared loudly to his son, who stood behind the sofa holding a steaming cup of aged Pu'er tea. "Last time this lazy boy caught me napping after two bowls of soup. Tonight I have locked down both sides of the river. Not even a mosquito can slip past my central elephant."

Across the low table, Chen Xuan leaned back comfortably against a sofa cushion with his green thermos resting by his knee. He stifled a relaxed yawn behind one hand, blinked his sleepy eyes, and casually hopped a wooden black horse neatly toward the center.

"Grandpa, your central defense looks very solid tonight," Chen Xuan said mildly. "I might just play it safe and protect my corner."

"Hah! Safe will not save you from my twin cannons," Elder Qin laughed, advancing a red pawn across the river.

Nearby on the wide armchair, Qin Yuhan and Lin Shuyin peeled sweet autumn tangerines into a porcelain dish, chatting softly about a charity gala next month and which winter coats to donate to a mountain school. Now and then Qin Yuhan glanced over at the chessboard with an amused smile, watching her grandfather furrow his brow in total concentration while her husband looked as if he might drift off to sleep at any second.

For the first ten moves, Elder Qin pressed forward with bold military rhythm. His red chariot roared down the right flank, capturing one of Chen Xuan's pawns with a triumphant snap of wood against wood.

"See that, Zhengming?" Elder Qin crowed, sipping his Pu'er tea with pride. "Press the center, choke the flanks, and never give the opponent breathing room. Xuan, your horse has nowhere left to run."

"Looks tight," Chen Xuan murmured, reaching out lazily to move a black advisor one small step sideways.

Qin Zhengming leaned forward slightly, adjusting his glasses as he studied the carved pieces. "Dad, wait a second. Look at the left edge of the board."

"Never mind the left edge, his chariot is trapped behind his own pawn," Elder Qin replied confidently, sliding his red cannon forward to pin Chen Xuan's flank. "You have nowhere left to run now!"

Chen Xuan blinked slowly, took a calm sip of warm jasmine tea from his thermos, and slid his black cannon neatly behind Elder Qin's front line.

Clack.

"Checkmate, Grandpa," Chen Xuan said gently.

Elder Qin froze with his teacup halfway to his lips. He leaned down over the rosewood board, squinting through his reading glasses for five full seconds. His red chariot was blocked by his own elephant. His twin cannons had no retreat path. The quiet pawn sacrifice Chen Xuan had offered six moves earlier had gently guided Elder Qin's entire formation into a clean, inescapable pocket.

"Wait a minute!" Elder Qin cried out in mock outrage, slapping his knee. "You pretended to be half asleep while laying an ambush on the left flank! That is pure trickery!"

Qin Zhengming burst out laughing behind the sofa. "Dad, he beat you cleanly without even raising his voice. Your twin cannons walked right into his net."

"Again! Reset the pieces!" Elder Qin insisted, though his eyes sparkled with genuine delight as he swept the wooden pieces back to their starting squares. "Honestly, Xuan, I cannot figure you out. You read four moves ahead on a chessboard like an old strategist, yet you turn down a deputy manager post just to keep your quiet desk and clock out early."

"Chess is just for fun after dinner, Grandpa," Chen Xuan smiled warmly as he lined up his black pieces. "Real life is much nicer when nobody is shouting check."

Lin Shuyin handed a plate of peeled tangerine segments to the tea table and shook her head with a soft laugh. "Let him win the next one, Xuan, or your grandfather will not sleep tonight."

Elder Qin snorted cheerfully and patted Chen Xuan's shoulder with a heavy, affectionate hand. In the old general's heart, there was not a single shred of doubt. His granddaughter had married a clever, warm-hearted, wonderfully lazy logistics clerk who loved home cooking and quiet evenings, and Elder Qin would not trade him for any billionaire in the country.

They played two more games before the living room clock chimed the late evening hour. Elder Qin won the final match by a single pawn after Chen Xuan conveniently overlooked a corner fork, leaving the old general beaming all the way to the front door.

Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan walked her family out to the courtyard under the crisp late autumn stars, waving until the taillights of their black sedan curved out of Emerald Riverside Villa.

Qin Yuhan shivered lightly in the cool breeze. "I will go warm up some honey water inside. Come in before you catch a chill."

"Right behind you," Chen Xuan smiled.

He lingered for one quiet moment beneath the soft amber glow of the garden lamp. Inside his jacket pocket, his phone vibrated once.

He pulled it out and glanced at the screen. It was a single line from Number 1: "Young Master, all autumn logistics corridors and Beijing channels remain calm. No disturbances to report."

Chen Xuan did not even need to reply. He locked the phone, slipped it back into his pocket, and looked out over the peaceful, darkened lawns of Pudong.

The world beyond the garden gate moved in vast, quiet tides at a word from him, yet inside the lit doorway behind him, honey water was warming and his wife was waiting.

Chen Xuan smiled softly and stepped back inside, closing the door against the cool autumn night.
