# Chapter 149: The Quiet Sovereign

Chen Xuan set his rice bowl down gently and stood up with a polite, easy nod.

"Good evening, Elder Qin," Chen Xuan said calmly, his voice warm and unhurried. "It is good to see you looking well. Please do not stand on ceremony in your own home."

That single quiet sentence snapped General Qin Zhengguo back to the present.

Two weeks ago in the capital, Elder Song had warned every senior commander that the young man who had cleaned out forty departments wished to live an ordinary life in Shanghai, undisturbed and unnamed.

General Qin Zhengguo swallowed hard and forced his stiff shoulders to relax.

"Ah, yes, of course," General Qin Zhengguo said, his deep voice strangely careful. "You must be Yuhan's friend from Hengxin Logistics. Young Chen, please sit down. Do not stand for an old man like me."

Qin Zhengming blinked twice.

"Dad, sit at the head of the table," Qin Zhengming said, pulling out the main chair. "Aunt Wu just warmed the chicken soup."

General Qin Zhengguo glanced at the head seat, glanced at Chen Xuan, and waved both hands as if the chair were hot iron.

"No, no, my clothes still carry dust from the plane," General Qin Zhengguo said quickly, hurrying around to the lowest seat near the door. "I will sit right here by the side. Young Chen, keep your seat."

Qin Yuhan's chopsticks paused in midair.

Before she could speak, General Qin Zhengguo sat straight-backed on the edge of his chair, picked up the soup ladle, and filled a fresh porcelain bowl. Holding it with both hands, he leaned forward and set it respectfully beside Chen Xuan's green thermos.

"Young Chen, winter nights in Shanghai are damp," General Qin Zhengguo said earnestly. "Drink some hot soup first. If anything is not to your taste, tell Zhengming and the kitchen will make something fresh right away."

"Thank you, Elder Qin," Chen Xuan replied smoothly with a mild smile. "The soup is wonderful. Please eat as well."

"Yes, yes, right away," General Qin Zhengguo answered almost like a junior officer taking an order.

Across the table, Qin Zhengming stared in growing bewilderment.

"Do not mind my father, Chen Xuan," Qin Zhengming said lightly. "He is just glad to see young people at the table. As I was saying earlier, if you ever tire of Hengxin Logistics, my offer at Haiyue Group stands. Start as a senior clerk in our supply office, and in two years I can make you a supervisor."

Clang.

General Qin Zhengguo's porcelain spoon slipped straight from his fingers onto the rim of his bowl.

The old general coughed hard into his fist, his face turning an alarmed shade of red.

"Zhengming!" General Qin Zhengguo barked, then hurriedly lowered his voice into a polite murmur. "Do not talk nonsense at dinner. Young Chen has his own plans. Who are you to tell him where to work?"

Qin Zhengming sat stunned. "Dad, I only meant it as a friendly offer. He helped Yuhan at the Sha Provincial Forum."

"Helping is one thing, and bragging about a clerk desk is another," General Qin Zhengguo muttered, glancing nervously at Chen Xuan.

Chen Xuan only smiled calmly and sipped his soup. "Uncle Qin meant well, Elder Qin. Besides, I really do enjoy my desk at Hengxin Logistics. The work is simple, and the bus stop is right outside the gate."

General Qin Zhengguo let out a quiet breath, nodding solemnly. "Well said. A peaceful life is worth more than gold. Nobody should disturb a man's peace."

The rest of the meal passed in a mix of warmth and quiet comedy.

Qin Yuhan watched silently, her dark eyes moving between her grandfather's stiff back and Chen Xuan's peaceful smile.

After dinner, Chen Xuan picked up his canvas bag and green thermos. "It is getting late, and the last direct bus to Binjiang Avenue leaves at nine. I should head out."

"I will walk you to the lane," Qin Yuhan said at once, grabbing her wool coat.

General Qin Zhengguo stood up immediately. "Yuhan, walk Young Chen all the way to the main road. Zhengming, come to my study for a moment."

As soon as Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan stepped into the lantern-lit courtyard, General Qin Zhengguo led his son into the wood-paneled study and shut the heavy door firmly.

The old general took a single cigarette from a tin box, lit it, and blew a slow ribbon of smoke toward the window.

"Dad," Qin Zhengming asked in a hushed voice. "Who is that young man? Why did you freeze when you saw him? Is he the son of an old comrade in the capital?"

General Qin Zhengguo turned around slowly. His eyes were sharper than Qin Zhengming had seen in twenty years.

"He is not the son of any comrade," General Qin Zhengguo whispered gravely. "Listen carefully, Zhengming, and remember every word."

Qin Zhengming straightened, his heart beating faster.

General Qin Zhengguo tapped his cigarette ash into a brass tray and locked eyes with his son. "Treat that young man better than you treat me, and never ask why."

The study went completely silent.

Qin Zhengming stood frozen, his breath catching. Everything rushed back at once: the sudden collapse of the short sellers attacking Haiyue Group, the frozen funds, the two-minute phone call outside the forum, and the quiet fall of corrupt officials across the province.

A cold shiver ran down Qin Zhengming's spine, followed by deep gratitude.

"I understand, Dad," Qin Zhengming whispered hoarsely. "I will never ask, and I will never speak a word of this."

Outside beneath the glowing courtyard lanterns, Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan walked slowly side by side under the bare winter plum branches. Neither hurried.

"My grandfather is usually not that nervous around guests," Qin Yuhan said softly, tucking her hands into her pockets. "I have never seen him drop a spoon in my whole life."

"The flight from the capital can make anyone tired," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

Qin Yuhan looked over at him, warm amusement shining in her eyes. "Right. Just like your mother calling about wool socks."

Chen Xuan let out a quiet, genuine laugh. "She really did ask about the wool socks."

They stopped at the dark wooden gate at the end of the lane, where yellow streetlights glowed over the quiet road.

"Thank you for dinner, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan said warmly.

"Come by again when you are free," Qin Yuhan said softly, with none of her boardroom guard left. "Do not just eat half a cold bun at your desk every day."

"I will remember that," Chen Xuan smiled. "Go inside before you catch a chill."

Qin Yuhan stood by the lantern-lit gate and watched him walk calmly toward the main road, his canvas bag over one shoulder. She did not know all of his secrets, and for the first time, she knew she did not need to.
