# Chapter 49: The First Page of the Dossier

The biometric scanner on Case One chimed with a low green pulse. Two recessed steel latches popped open with a sharp hiss, releasing dry vault air into the smoke-filled hall.

Gu Chengshan lifted the lid back. Inside sat a dense grid of matte-black solid-state drives totaling one hundred and fourteen terabytes. Resting on top was a thick linen-bound paper dossier with no cover title, only a red wax seal stamped with a blank circle.

"Paper," Zhou Zhenhua murmured through the cigarette haze. "Zero knows how our senior meetings work. They printed the executive ledger so nobody could claim a screen glitch."

"Read it, Gao Zhengyi," Gu Chengshan said quietly, setting the dossier squarely in front of the CCDI Deputy Secretary. "You wanted proof. Start at page one."

Gao Zhengyi did not reach for his glasses. With a fresh cigarette tucked between the fingers of his left hand, he broke the blank wax seal and opened the cover.

Silence closed around the four men as Gao Zhengyi's eyes dropped to the first bold summary table. His brow furrowed. Then his face froze.

"Read it out loud," Gu Chengshan prompted, voice tight.

Gao Zhengyi cleared his throat, the sound dry and rough. "Section One. Twenty-year compounded sovereign asset diversion. State procurement kickbacks, port customs under-invoicing, energy-grid inflation, offshore fund routing, and state-owned land transfers across fourteen provinces and forty ministries. Verified principal value, plus compounded yield and collateralized offshore leverage."

Gao Zhengyi stopped. His right hand gripped the page edge so hard the paper crinkled.

"What is the figure, Gao Zhengyi?" Zhou Zhenhua asked impatiently, leaning over his shoulder.

"104.6 trillion yuan," Gao Zhengyi read, barely above a cracked whisper.

Zhou Zhenhua went dead still, then pulled back from the table, his face turning the color of chalk. "Impossible," Zhou Zhenhua said, shaking his head sharply. "104 trillion? That is several times the annual fiscal intake of the republic. Nobody can hide 100 trillion yuan without the Ministry of Finance noticing!"

"Look at the next column, Zhou Zhenhua," Qin Zhengguo said grimly, pointing a thick finger at the lower page. "Twenty years compounded. They did not empty one vault in one night. They bled every port tariff, every railway tender, every grid contract, and rolled the stolen principal into foreign trusts and shadow banks for two decades."

"My God," Gu Chengshan breathed, scanning the neat rows of offshore account numbers, routing codes, and biometric signatures beneath the total. "Every single line carries a live bank hash and a signed approval chain. Look at column four. Deputy ministers. Two provincial governors. Three port directors. Four directors inside your own commission, Gao Zhengyi."

Gao Zhengyi's lips pressed into a bloodless line. His jaw worked once, twice, as he stared at the four names from his agency printed in black ink. "Two of those men drank tea in my office last Thursday," Gao Zhengyi muttered, voice shaking with cold humiliation. "They told me the coastal port audits were clean."

"Keep turning," Qin Zhengguo said flatly. "Elder Song did not give me the founding override phrase over stolen money. Turn to page two."

Gao Zhengyi drew a shallow breath and flipped the sheet.

The instant his eyes hit page two, the breath locked in his throat.

Across the top sat a stark header in dark red print: Section Two, Active Defense and Infrastructure Espionage, Cross-Referenced to Forty-Seven Detained Foreign Cutouts.

Beneath it lay decryption logs, frequency tables, and timestamped transfer receipts.

"Naval radar arrays," Gao Zhengyi whispered, his voice dropping so low the others had to lean in. "Full phased-array calibration tables for the Eastern and Southern Theater fleets. Exported through a marine logistics shell at Ningbo Port three weeks ago."

Qin Zhengguo's fist closed against the walnut table until his tendons stood out like cables. His face turned to stone, a dark vein pulsing sharply at his temple.

"Keep reading," Qin Zhengguo ordered, voice like grinding gravel.

Gao Zhengyi turned to page three, fingers stiff as wood. "Southern power-grid master dispatch keys," he read mechanically, eyes wide and glassy. "Live supervisory override certificates for seven ultra-high-voltage trunk corridors. Scheduled for delivery to a foreign cutout in Macau at six this morning. Less than ninety minutes from now."

Zhou Zhenhua staggered back half a step and caught a chair back to steady himself. "Ninety minutes," Zhou Zhenhua choked out. "If those keys had crossed into Macau at six, a hostile command could have collapsed the entire southern industrial grid with one keystroke. Every factory, every rail line, every hospital from the Yangtze to the Pearl River!"

"Look at the bottom half of page three," Gu Chengshan said, his voice trembling for the first time since four in the morning. His hand hovered over the printed coordinates. "Border-garrison deployment rosters. Western and Northern Theater rotation schedules, munition depot coordinates, and encrypted satellite relay windows. Sold piece by piece over the last fourteen months."

Nobody spoke. The smoke in the hall hung motionless under the dim lights.

Gao Zhengyi stared down at page three as if his mind had stopped turning. Between the fingers of his left hand, his forgotten cigarette burned past the paper line straight down into the filter, scorching his skin with a faint hiss and the sharp smell of singed flesh.

Gao Zhengyi did not flinch.

Qin Zhengguo reached across, gripped Gao Zhengyi's wrist, and knocked the glowing butt into the ashtray. "Drop it," Qin Zhengguo said softly. "You are burning your own hand."

Gao Zhengyi blinked slowly, stared at the red blister forming between his fingers, and did not reach for a handkerchief. He looked up at Gu Chengshan, his eyes wide with raw horror.

"The 100 trillion yuan," Gao Zhengyi whispered, voice cracking wide open. "Five minutes ago we stood here thinking 100 trillion yuan was the end of the world. It is not even the worst thing in this folder. It is the smallest horror on the table."

"Money can be frozen," Gu Chengshan said, voice hoarse and hollow as he stared at the radar tables. "Assets can be seized. Offshore shells can be clawed back over ten years. You cannot claw back a blind fleet. You cannot claw back a dead southern power grid. You cannot claw back border garrisons whose coordinates sit on a foreign general's desk."

"They were not just stealing from the state," Zhou Zhenhua rasped, openly shaken. "They were dismantling the walls from inside and handing over the keys to the gate. Forty-seven foreign cutouts inside our own ports and ministries, waiting for sunrise."

Qin Zhengguo clicked his lighter shut without lighting another cigarette and stared darkly down the line of forty-two black cases.

"Do you still want to ask who triggered Zero Guard, Premier Gu Chengshan?" Qin Zhengguo asked quietly.

Gu Chengshan stood motionless for a long moment. When he finally spoke, every trace of political pride had vanished from his face.

"No," Gu Chengshan said softly. "Whoever woke Zero tonight just saved the republic from a knife aimed straight at its spine. And none of us in this room even knew the blade was raised."
