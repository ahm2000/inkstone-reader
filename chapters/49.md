# Chapter 49: The First Page of the Dossier

The fingerprint scanner on Case One chimed with a low green light. Two steel latches popped open with a sharp hiss, letting dry air out into the smoke-filled hall.

Gu Chengshan lifted the lid back. Inside sat a tight grid of black hard drives holding one hundred and fourteen terabytes in total. Resting on top was a thick paper folder with no title on the cover, only a red wax seal stamped with a blank circle.

"Paper," Zhou Zhenhua murmured through the cigarette haze. "Zero knows how our top meetings work. They printed the main summary so nobody could blame a computer glitch."

"Read it, Gao Zhengyi," Gu Chengshan said quietly, setting the folder right in front of the CCDI Deputy Secretary. "You wanted proof. Start at page one."

Gao Zhengyi did not reach for his glasses. With a fresh cigarette tucked between the fingers of his left hand, he broke the blank wax seal and opened the cover.

Silence closed around the four men as Gao Zhengyi's eyes dropped to the first bold chart. His brow furrowed. Then his face froze.

"Read it out loud," Gu Chengshan urged, his voice tight.

Gao Zhengyi cleared his throat, the sound dry and rough. "Section One. Twenty years of stolen state money. Bribes on state deals, fake port bills, padded power-grid costs, overseas transfers, and state land sales across fourteen provinces and forty ministries. Total stolen money, plus twenty years of growth and overseas loans built on it."

Gao Zhengyi stopped. His right hand gripped the page edge so hard the paper crinkled.

"What is the number, Gao Zhengyi?" Zhou Zhenhua asked sharply, leaning over his shoulder.

"104.6 trillion yuan," Gao Zhengyi read, barely above a cracked whisper.

Zhou Zhenhua went dead still, then stepped back from the table, his face turning white as chalk. "Impossible," Zhou Zhenhua said, shaking his head hard. "104 trillion? That is several times the yearly tax income of the whole country. Nobody can hide 100 trillion yuan without the Finance Ministry noticing!"

"Look at the next column, Zhou Zhenhua," Qin Zhengguo said grimly, pointing a thick finger at the lower page. "Twenty years added up. They did not empty one vault in one night. They bled every port fee, every railway deal, every power-grid contract, and rolled the stolen money into foreign banks and shadow loans for two decades."

"My God," Gu Chengshan breathed, scanning the neat rows of overseas account numbers, bank codes, and signed approvals beneath the total. "Every single line has real bank proof and a signed paper trail. Look at column four. Deputy ministers. Two provincial governors. Three port directors. Four directors inside your own commission, Gao Zhengyi."

Gao Zhengyi's lips pressed into a pale line. His jaw worked once, twice, as he stared at the four names from his own agency printed in black ink. "Two of those men drank tea in my office last Thursday," Gao Zhengyi muttered, his voice shaking with cold shame. "They told me the coastal port checks were clean."

"Keep turning," Qin Zhengguo said flatly. "Elder Song did not give me the founding code phrase over stolen money. Turn to page two."

Gao Zhengyi drew a shallow breath and flipped the sheet.

The instant his eyes hit page two, the air locked in his throat.

Across the top sat a stark header in dark red print: Section Two, Live Military and Power-Grid Spying, Tied to Forty-Seven Caught Foreign Spies.

Beneath it lay unlocked logs, radio charts, and dated transfer records.

"Navy radar," Gao Zhengyi whispered, his voice dropping so low the others had to lean in. "Full radar setup charts for the Eastern and Southern Theater fleets. Sent out through a shipping shell company at Ningbo Port three weeks ago."

Qin Zhengguo's fist closed against the wood table until his hand stood out hard as stone. A dark vein pulsed sharply at his temple.

"Keep reading," Qin Zhengguo ordered, his voice rough as gravel.

Gao Zhengyi turned to page three, his fingers stiff as wood. "Southern power-grid master control keys," he read in a flat daze, his eyes wide and glassy. "Live override codes for seven main high-power lines. Set for delivery to a foreign spy in Macau at six this morning. Less than ninety minutes from now."

Zhou Zhenhua staggered back half a step and grabbed a chair back to steady himself. "Ninety minutes," Zhou Zhenhua choked out. "If those keys had crossed into Macau at six, an enemy could have shut down the entire southern power grid with one click. Every factory, every train line, every hospital from the Yangtze to the Pearl River!"

"Look at the bottom half of page three," Gu Chengshan said, his voice shaking for the first time since four in the morning. His hand hovered over the printed maps. "Border troop lists. Western and Northern Theater guard schedules, ammo depot maps, and secret satellite times. Sold piece by piece over the last fourteen months."

Nobody spoke. The smoke in the hall hung still under the dim lights.

Gao Zhengyi stared down at page three as if his mind had stopped working. Between the fingers of his left hand, his forgotten cigarette burned past the paper straight into the filter, scorching his skin with a faint hiss and the sharp smell of burned flesh.

Gao Zhengyi did not flinch.

Qin Zhengguo reached across, gripped Gao Zhengyi's wrist, and knocked the burning butt into the ashtray. "Drop it," Qin Zhengguo said softly. "You are burning your own hand."

Gao Zhengyi blinked slowly, stared at the red blister forming between his fingers, and did not reach for a tissue. He looked up at Gu Chengshan, his eyes wide with raw horror.

"The 100 trillion yuan," Gao Zhengyi whispered, his voice breaking wide open. "Five minutes ago we stood here thinking 100 trillion yuan was the end of the world. It is not even the worst thing in this folder. It is the smallest horror on the table."

"Money can be frozen," Gu Chengshan said, his voice hoarse and hollow as he stared at the radar charts. "Property can be seized. Overseas accounts can be pulled back over ten years. You cannot pull back a blind fleet. You cannot pull back a dead southern power grid. You cannot pull back border troops whose maps sit on a foreign general's desk."

"They were not just stealing from the state," Zhou Zhenhua rasped, openly shaken. "They were tearing down the walls from inside and handing over the keys to the gate. Forty-seven foreign spies inside our own ports and ministries, waiting for sunrise."

Qin Zhengguo snapped his lighter shut without lighting another cigarette and stared darkly down the line of forty-two black cases.

"Do you still want to ask who woke Black Guard, Premier Gu Chengshan?" Qin Zhengguo asked quietly.

Gu Chengshan stood motionless for a long moment. When he finally spoke, every bit of political pride had vanished from his face.

"No," Gu Chengshan said softly. "Whoever woke Zero tonight just saved the country from a knife aimed straight at its back. And none of us in this room even knew the blade was raised."
