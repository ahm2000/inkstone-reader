# Chapter 49: Old Man Qin's Tea

By four o'clock on Monday afternoon, winter sunlight hung low over the gray tile roofs of the Qin family courtyard in Qingyan.

Inside the glass pavilion overlooking the koi pond, Qin Zhengming sat behind his rosewood tea table. At seventy-six, the retired patriarch of Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group had neatly combed silver hair and hands steady as jade. A clay kettle hummed softly beside him.

Qin Yuhan slid open the cedar door, slipped off her wool coat, and sat across from him.

"Grandpa, I am back," she said with a quiet smile.

Qin Zhengming rinsed two porcelain cups without looking up. "You are early. Did the clerk bore you?"

"Not for a second," Qin Yuhan replied. "He was the most interesting person I have eaten with all year."

Qin Zhengming lifted his gaze and studied her. She was polite to everyone and impressed by almost no one.

"Tell me," he said, pouring the first rinse of aged Pu'er tea.

Qin Yuhan gave him the whole story: Chen Xuan's stiffly ironed gray shirt, straight blue tie, and the thin patch of clear shoe glue near his left toe. She described how he had expected a fifty-year-old executive, and how he broke a plain steamed bun in half and dipped it into braised tofu sauce without a hint of awkwardness.

"He did not brag once," Qin Yuhan said warmly. "When I asked why he wore no watch, he said the strap catches on boxes when he unloads trucks."

Qin Zhengming chuckled softly. "A man comfortable with a steamed bun is either very simple or very dangerous. Keep going."

"Zhou Mingcheng barged in uninvited halfway through lunch," Qin Yuhan said, her tone cooling. "He waved a 2012 Margaux, mocked Chen Xuan's glued shoes and twelve-thousand-yuan salary, and tried to force General Manager Sun Haoran to hand over Hengxin Logistics' South Bay shipping route."

Qin Zhengming frowned lightly. "Zhou Guodong never taught his boy how to read a person. Did you throw him out?"

"Right after Chen Xuan spoke," Qin Yuhan said, lifting her cup. "Before I could rise, Chen Xuan asked Zhou Mingcheng one polite question about Berth Seven. He quoted Thursday's grain tonnage, the tugboat draft log showing over three thousand tons, and a flagged customs broker stamp."

Qin Zhengming's eyes narrowed sharply. "And Zhou Mingcheng's face?"

"Completely white," Qin Yuhan said. "I had security escort him out and told him I had zero interest in him. Grandpa, if I had not seen Chen Xuan's file myself, I would never believe he was just a warehouse supervisor."

Qin Zhengming lifted the clay teapot to pour a second round. "A warehouse clerk does not accidentally notice tugboat draft logs on Berth Seven, Yuhan. And he does not carry the matte-black zero-one pass from Yunhai Grand Hotel."

"That is what I wanted to ask," Qin Yuhan said quietly. "Only a handful of matte-black zero-one passes were ever issued by Haiyue Group, all to provincial elders or above. Dai Wenbin bowed to him in front of twenty people. How did Chen Xuan get one?"

"Dai Wenbin did not bow to a card," Qin Zhengming said calmly as he tilted the teapot. "He bowed to the registry behind it. I still do not know which door Chen Xuan walked through."

"There was one other small thing," Qin Yuhan added casually. "At the end of lunch, he unzipped his worn canvas bag to pull out a cheap plastic pen for Jin Ke. Inside, next to his thermos pouch, I glimpsed an old wooden fishing float."

Qin Zhengming's wrist steadied the pot. "A fishing float?"

"Yes. Hand-carved wood, painted faded yellow, with the paint worn thin along the edges," Qin Yuhan described clearly. "And carved cleanly into its flat wooden base was a tiny square seal. Two crisp seal-script characters inside a thin border."

The clay teapot froze dead still in midair.

A steaming stream of amber tea missed Qin Zhengming's cup and poured straight onto the bamboo tray, spreading across the dark wood with a soft hiss.

Qin Yuhan sat up in alarm. "Grandpa! You are spilling the tea!"

Qin Zhengming did not move for three full seconds, his weathered face suddenly stripped of humor. Slowly, with a faint tremor in his fingers Qin Yuhan had never seen before, he set the clay teapot down on its coaster.

"Say that again, Yuhan," Qin Zhengming said in a low rasp. "Faded yellow wood. Hand-carved. A tiny square seal cut into the flat base."

"Yes, Grandpa," Qin Yuhan said, her pulse quickening at his gravity. "I only saw it for two seconds before he tucked it back in. Whose fishing float is it?"

Qin Zhengming wiped the spilled tea slowly with a linen cloth, reaching back twenty years.

"Eighteen years ago, when Retired Governor Zhao Dehai ran the provincial coast, we skipped banquets on Sundays to fish at East Ridge Reservoir," Qin Zhengming said softly. "Zhao Dehai carved three yellow cedar floats with his own pocketknife during one rainy autumn. He kept the best one and stamped his personal square seal into its base."

Qin Yuhan's breath caught. "Retired Governor Zhao Dehai?"

"I once offered him a Ming porcelain bowl for it," Qin Zhengming continued, his eyes deeply solemn. "Zhao Dehai laughed and said no porcelain could buy it. That little piece of wood was only for someone whose word carried the weight of a mountain, someone he trusted with the peace of the province."

Silence settled over the glass pavilion, broken only by the hum of the brass kettle.

Qin Yuhan stared at her grandfather, remembering the quiet young man dipping a steamed bun into tofu sauce two hours ago.

"Grandpa," Qin Yuhan whispered. "Are you saying Zhao Dehai gave that float to Chen Xuan?"

"There is only one yellow float bearing Zhao Dehai's personal seal," Qin Zhengming said firmly. "Combine that with the matte-black zero-one pass and the way he dismantled Zhou Mingcheng without raising his voice."

Qin Zhengming looked straight into her eyes. "Yuhan, that ordinary clerk in glued brown shoes stands at a height even Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group cannot measure. We cannot even see the top of the mountain he stands on."

A chill ran down Qin Yuhan's spine, mixed with deep relief that she had treated him with genuine warmth.

"What should we do?" she asked quietly.

"Three things," Qin Zhengming said decisively. "First, shred every file Jin Ke pulled on Chen Xuan. Never investigate him again, and never mention that yellow float outside this room."

"Understood," Qin Yuhan nodded at once.

"Second, secure Hengxin Logistics' South Bay shipping route quietly under Haiyue's master port agreement tomorrow morning," Qin Zhengming instructed. "Zhou Mingcheng stepped on a sleeping dragon's tail today. Shenghua Maritime Group is about to walk into an iron wall on its own."

"And the third?" Qin Yuhan asked.

Qin Zhengming lifted his teacup, his hand steady again, looking out at the winter pines.

"If a man of that stature chooses a gray shirt, steamed buns, and a twelve-thousand-yuan clerk desk, he wants a quiet life," Qin Zhengming said gently. "Treat him like a real friend, not a patron and not a mystery."

Qin Yuhan looked down at her cup, remembering Chen Xuan offering her the last bowl of radish soup.

"I can do that, Grandpa," she said softly.
