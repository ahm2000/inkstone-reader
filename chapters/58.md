# Chapter 58: CCDI and National Supervision: The 100-Trillion Shock

At seven twenty on Monday morning, the dark wooden table inside the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection meeting room vanished under stacks of bound bank printouts and a low cloud of cigarette smoke. Ten bureau chiefs sat shoulder to shoulder, their faces gray under the bright ceiling lights.

Gao Zhengyi sat at the center, his jacket draped over his chair and his shirt sleeves rolled halfway up his arms. He said nothing, just watched the veteran Chief Financial Auditor sitting across from him.

Lu Jianmin was sixty-one years old. He had checked three banking crises and forty ministerial crackdowns without ever blinking. Now his hands shook so hard the pages rattled like dry leaves. His eyes raced across the twenty-year national loss sheet, checking the final number three times, then a fourth.

Snap.

The sharp crack echoed across the quiet room. Everyone flinched. Blue ink spilled from the broken fountain pen in Lu Jianmin's fist, dripping across his pale knuckles right onto the grand total.

"104 trillion yuan," Lu Jianmin whispered, his voice hollow. "Stacked-up losses, stolen state guarantees, fake land prices, and hidden overseas deals over twenty years. Gao Zhengyi, this cannot be real. A country does not lose 100 trillion yuan without every bank in Beijing noticing."

"They did not steal it from the main central vaults, Lu Jianmin," Gao Zhengyi said coldly, tapping ash into a brass dish. "They drained it through ten thousand tiny pipes, one county street at a time. Open Section Nine."

First Inspection Bureau Chief Peng Dehua blew out thick smoke and flipped angrily to the tab. "Section Nine is city rebuilding! County demolition contracts! Gao Zhengyi, what does clearing an old street in a small county have to do with a 100 trillion yuan overseas money drain?"

"Look at the example on page six," Asset-Recovery Bureau Chief Cai Rong snapped, jabbing a finger hard enough to dent the paper. "Yunxi County. Old Street rebuilding project. Official land value: 480 million yuan. Actual bank loans pulled using those same deeds through three provincial shell companies: 6.2 billion yuan."

Silence hit the room like a slap. Second Inspection Bureau Chief Xu Mingfang squinted through her smoke, her eyebrows pulling tight. "6.2 billion yuan borrowed on a 500 million yuan block? How did a county land office clear a fourteen-to-one loan gap?"

"Because they did not borrow against it just once," Banking-Compliance Liaison Chief Ren Xiaolan cut in, her voice tight with dread as she flipped through the loan chains. "Look at the Yunxi developer. Deng Yongchang. His company handed those Old Street land papers to a provincial building fund, bundled them with forty-two other county demolition jobs across seven provinces, then packaged and sold the loans through coastal shell companies."

"And where did the cash go once the shell companies cleared the loans?" Case-Review Bureau Chief Shao Weimin demanded, leaning forward as his cigarette burned close to his fingers.

"Straight into the Zurich Northern Ledger," Cross-Border Tracing Bureau Chief Ding Yanhua answered grimly, slapping a second binder open beside his overflowing ashtray. "Every county demolition gave a tiny, noisy local payout to the developer, the county officials, and the street thugs who forced families to sign. In Yunxi, Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming and County Public Security Chief Liang Guodong pocketed a few million each to bury complaints and lock up stubborn homeowners. Meanwhile, the real prize, the fake land papers, went up to provincial middlemen, then out through fake shipping bills to Zurich."

County-Governance Oversight Chief Pan Zhiming went pale and pushed his chair back an inch. "Wait a minute. Let me follow that. In Yunxi County, a demolition gang run by Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao bullies ordinary families off Old Street. Sun Weiming stamps a fake land price report. And that tiny county file gets used to pull out a 1 billion dollar loan drain that bleeds national funds?"

"Multiply Yunxi County by two thousand counties over twenty years," Lu Jianmin said hoarsely, wiping blue ink off his shaking hand with a tissue. "Add piled-up interest, state bond gaps, rigged state buying, and stolen mining limits. Every county thought it was running a small local scam. Nobody at the bottom ever saw the top of the pipe, and nobody at the top ever got dust on his shoes walking down a county street."

State-Enterprise Supervision Chief Ge Hongwei crushed his cigarette out with shaking hands and stared at Gao Zhengyi. "Our inspection teams visited Yunxi's province twice in five years! How did our own auditors miss fourteen-to-one fake loan deals sitting right out in the open?"

"Because the provincial audit office was on the same payroll," Evidence-Verification Bureau Chief Wei Guoping snapped, sliding a red folder down the wooden table. "Three deputy directors of the provincial finance office are already on the overnight list of three hundred and eighteen arrested suspects. They buried every complaint from Yunxi and forty other counties as ordinary local demolition fights."

Peng Dehua cursed under his breath and lit a new cigarette off his old one. "This is betraying the country's economy. If we announce a 100 trillion yuan drain tomorrow, the bond market will crash before lunch, and provincial banks will face crowds pulling out their savings by three o'clock!"

"Which is why we are not announcing a panic, Peng Dehua," Gao Zhengyi said, his eyes hard through the swirling haze. "Read the recovery pages in the back. Whoever cracked this ring already froze the overseas bank accounts in Zurich, Singapore, and Luxembourg before dawn using top-level state codes we did not even know existed."

Lu Jianmin froze, his eyes going wide. "The overseas money is locked? Not drained away?"

"Locked and marked to be brought back," Ding Yanhua confirmed, his voice shaking with awe and fear. "My team checked the bank freezes ten minutes ago. Every shell account tied to the three hundred and eighteen suspects froze solid at two forty-five. No court order on earth moves that fast across four continents. Gao Zhengyi, who did this?"

Gao Zhengyi took a slow drag on his cigarette, the red tip glowing bright. "You do not write their name in reports or notes. Your job is to check every single domestic yuan, trace every provincial middleman, and lock down the paper trail before eight o'clock."

"What about the local Yunxi players?" Shao Weimin asked sharply, tapping the names Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Cheng Biao, and Yan Tiehu on his pad. "Why were none of them grabbed overnight? Every provincial backer above them is already locked up, yet Yunxi's crew is still sitting at home eating breakfast."

"Because they were left for us," Gao Zhengyi replied evenly, standing up and buttoning his jacket. "The night sweep cut out the giant cancer in the dark. Yunxi's local rot gets handled in broad daylight under ordinary discipline, anti-gang, and criminal laws. No mystery. No panic. Every framed homeowner walks out completely clean."

He looked around the smoke-filled table at the ten shaken chiefs.

"Forty minutes until Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua open the War Room. Wipe the ink off your hands, Lu Jianmin. Bring me the verified recovery totals, the bank safety fund plan, and arrest warrants for every provincial trust director on that page. Move."

Lu Jianmin stared down at his broken pen, his jaw tight, then grabbed a fresh pencil and pulled the 100 trillion yuan ledger back under the lamp.
