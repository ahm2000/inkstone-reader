# Chapter 58: CCDI and National Supervision: The 100-Trillion Shock

At seven twenty on Monday morning, the mahogany table inside the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection conference chamber disappeared beneath bound financial printouts and a low ceiling of cigarette smoke. Ten bureau chiefs sat shoulder to shoulder, faces gray under the overhead lamps.

Gao Zhengyi sat at the center, jacket draped over his chair and cuffs rolled halfway up his forearms. He stayed silent, watching the veteran Chief Financial Auditor across from him.

Lu Jianmin was sixty-one and had audited three banking crises and forty ministerial takedowns without blinking. Now his hands shook so hard the pages rustled like dry leaves. His eyes darted across the twenty-year compounded sovereign balance sheet, checking the decimal place three times, then a fourth.

Snap.

The sharp crack cut across the quiet room. Everyone flinched. Blue ink welled from the broken fountain pen in Lu Jianmin's fist, dripping across his white knuckles onto the ledger total.

"104 trillion yuan," Lu Jianmin whispered, voice hollow. "Compounded principal, diverted sovereign guarantees, rigged land valuations, and offshore arbitrage across twenty years. Gao Zhengyi, this cannot be real. A nation does not bleed 100 trillion yuan without every bank in the capital noticing."

"They did not bleed it from the central vaults, Lu Jianmin," Gao Zhengyi said coldly, tapping ash into a brass dish. "They bled it through ten thousand capillary tubes, one county block at a time. Read Section Nine."

First Inspection Bureau Chief Peng Dehua exhaled thick smoke and flipped angrily to the tab. "Section Nine is municipal redevelopment! County-level demolition contracts! Gao Zhengyi, what does a third-tier county clearance have to do with 100-trillion-yuan offshore siphon?"

"Look at the case study on page six," Asset-Recovery Bureau Chief Cai Rong snapped, jabbing a finger hard enough to dent the paper. "Yunxi County. Old Street redevelopment. Official municipal valuation: 480 million yuan. Actual bank credit drawn against those bundled deeds through three provincial trust vehicles: 6.2 billion yuan."

Silence hit like a physical blow. Second Inspection Bureau Chief Xu Mingfang squinted through her smoke, brows pulling tight. "6.2 billion borrowed on 500-million-yuan block? How did a county land bureau clear a fourteen-to-one leverage ratio?"

"Because they did not borrow it once," Banking-Compliance Liaison Chief Ren Xiaolan cut in, voice tight with dread as she flipped through the collateral chains. "Look at the Yunxi developer. Deng Yongchang. His company pledged those Old Street deeds to a provincial urban-investment platform, bundled them with forty-two other county demolitions across seven provinces, then securitized the package through coastal shadow trusts."

"And where did the cash go after the trusts cleared the loans?" Case-Review Bureau Chief Shao Weimin demanded, leaning forward as his cigarette burned toward his fingers.

"Straight into the Zurich Northern Ledger," Cross-Border Tracing Bureau Chief Ding Yanhua answered grimly, slamming a second binder open beside his overflowing ashtray. "Every county demolition yielded a tiny, noisy local profit for the fixer, the county magistrate, and the street thugs who forced residents to sign. In Yunxi, Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming and County Public Security Chief Liang Guodong pocketed a few million each to bury complaints and jail stubborn homeowners. Meanwhile, the real asset, the forged land certificates, went up to provincial intermediaries, then out through maritime freight invoices to Zurich."

County-Governance Oversight Chief Pan Zhiming went ashen, pushing his chair back an inch. "Hold on. Let me trace that. In Yunxi County, a demolition gang led by Yan Tiehu and Cheng Biao bullies ordinary households off Old Street. Sun Weiming stamps a fake appraisal. And that tiny county file becomes collateral for 1 billion-dollar credit siphon draining national funds?"

"Multiply Yunxi County by two thousand counties across twenty years," Lu Jianmin said hoarsely, wiping blue ink from his shaking palm with a tissue. "Add compound interest, sovereign bond spreads, rigged state procurement, and stolen mineral quotas. Every county thought they ran a small local racket. Nobody at the bottom saw the top of the pipe, and nobody at the top ever dirtied his shoes on a county street."

State-Enterprise Supervision Chief Ge Hongwei crushed his cigarette out with shaking force and stared at Gao Zhengyi. "Our inspection teams visited Yunxi's province twice in five years! How did our own auditors miss fourteen-to-one collateral fraud sitting in plain sight?"

"Because the provincial audit bureau shared the same payroll," Evidence-Verification Bureau Chief Wei Guoping snapped, sliding a red folder down the mahogany. "Three deputy directors of the provincial finance department are already on the overnight three-hundred-and-eighteen-person detention list. They buried every petition from Yunxi and forty other counties as 'routine civil demolition disputes.'"

Peng Dehua swore under his breath, lighting another cigarette off his stub. "This is economic treason. If we announce 100-trillion-yuan siphon tomorrow, the bond market collapses before lunch and provincial commercial banks face a run by three!"

"Which is why we are not announcing a panic, Peng Dehua," Gao Zhengyi said, eyes hard through swirling haze. "Read the recovery schedules in the back. Whoever cracked this network already froze the offshore clearing accounts in Zurich, Singapore, and Luxembourg before dawn using sovereign counter-keys we did not know existed."

Lu Jianmin froze, eyes widening. "The offshore principal is locked? Not liquidated?"

"Locked and tagged for repatriation," Ding Yanhua confirmed, voice trembling with awe and terror. "My team verified the Swift holds ten minutes ago. Every shell account tied to the three hundred and eighteen principals went immobile at two forty-five. No court order moves that fast across four continents. Gao Zhengyi, who did this?"

Gao Zhengyi drew slowly on his cigarette, embers glowing bright orange. "You do not write their name in reports or audit footnotes. Your job is to verify every domestic yuan, trace every provincial intermediary, and seal the paper trail before eight o'clock."

"What about the local Yunxi actors?" Shao Weimin asked sharply, tapping Deng Yongchang, Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Cheng Biao, and Yan Tiehu on his pad. "Why were none of them picked up overnight? Every provincial backer above them is in custody, yet Yunxi's circle is still home eating breakfast."

"Because they were left for us," Gao Zhengyi replied evenly, standing and buttoning his jacket. "The sweep cut out the continental cancer in the dark. Yunxi's local rot gets handled in broad daylight under ordinary discipline, anti-gang, and criminal statutes. No mystery. No panic. Every framed resident walks out clean."

He looked around the smoke-choked table at the ten shaken chiefs.

"Forty minutes until Premier Gu Chengshan and Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua open the War Room. Wipe the ink off your hands, Lu Jianmin. Bring me verified recovery totals, the bank-stabilization buffer, and arrest warrants for every provincial trust director on that page. Move."

Lu Jianmin stared down at his snapped pen, jaw tight, then grabbed a fresh pencil and pulled the 100-trillion-yuan ledger back under the lamp.
