# Chapter 58: The Seal on the Float

The sun climbed higher above the pine ridge, warming the gravel along the willow bend. Chen Fugen cast his line into the deeper channel, while Tang Xiaoyu sat beside him on a flat stone, clutching a short bamboo rod with both hands and watching her bright red bobber.

Chen Xuan sat two paces to their right on his cheap folding canvas stool in his plain gray jacket, elbows resting lightly on his knees. When he prepared his own line, he did not touch Chen Fugen's bait box. He checked the bare brass hook, adjusted the lead weight, and cast softly into the calm shallows.

Zhao Mingyuan, standing near his grandfather's chair with a stainless steel mug in hand, saw the bare hook clearly before it hit the water.

He glanced down at Chen Xuan's scuffed sneakers, faded sleeve, and cheap folding stool creaking on the stones.

"Mister Chen Xuan," Zhao Mingyuan said, his voice carrying the smooth politeness of a provincial-capital desk officer. "You did not put any bait on your hook."

Chen Xuan looked up mildly. "I know."

"Fishing with a bare hook is an old literary joke," Zhao Mingyuan remarked with a measured smile. "A young man in his twenties should be chasing real work and climbing while his legs are strong. Sitting on a ten-yuan stool with an empty hook looks peaceful, though it also looks like giving up before the race has started."

Beside Zhao Dehai, Qin Zhengming's fingers paused on the cedar tea box, expression neutral. Behind Chen Xuan, Qin Yuhan's gaze cooled slightly, though she stayed silent.

Chen Fugen frowned, but Chen Xuan lifted one hand lightly on his rod grip.

"You have a fair point, Section Chief Zhao," Chen Xuan said calmly, neither offended nor sharp. "Climbing is tiring work. Some people enjoy the view from the steps. I just prefer sitting by the water when the sun is out."

Zhao Mingyuan smiled wider, mistaking Chen Xuan's mildness for harmless resignation. "Ambition is not about enjoying the steps, Mister Chen Xuan. It is about making sure your family does not spend their lives watching others walk past. Still, everyone has their own limits."

Just then, a small minnow nudged the bare brass hook in the shallows.

Chen Xuan lifted his rod tip smoothly to clear the line from a drifting willow leaf.

As the nylon drew taut, the wooden fishing float rose clear of the surface and hung two feet above the water in direct morning sunlight.

It was a hand-carved spindle of pale yellow boxwood, oiled smooth from years of handling. Right at the widest curve, inked in dark vermilion lacquer beneath clear pine resin, sat a crisp square seal.

Two archaic characters were carved deep into the wood: *De Hai*.

Zhao Mingyuan's smile vanished.

Color drained cleanly from his cheeks. He stared at the yellow wooden float as if winter thunder had struck the stones between his polished shoes.

Every member of the Zhao family knew that seal. Retired Governor Zhao Dehai had carved three boxwood floats by hand when he left provincial office. One sat in his study. One had gone to an old comrade in the capital who served beside him for thirty years. The third had remained locked in his rosewood drawer for a decade, never given to a department head, mayor, or even Zhao Mingyuan's own father.

Zhao Mingyuan's lips parted, yet no sound came out. His fingers froze around the steel mug.

Chen Xuan lowered his rod tip with unhurried grace, letting the yellow float settle back onto the water. He did not glance at Zhao Mingyuan's face. He simply watched the tiny rings fade until the surface was glass again.

Silence held the gravel bend for three full seconds.

Then Zhao Dehai spoke. His voice was neither loud nor angry, yet it carried the quiet weight of a man who had chaired provincial standing committees with a single lifted eyebrow.

"Zhao Mingyuan," Zhao Dehai said calmly. "Put down that mug. Sit down."

Zhao Mingyuan jolted as if woken from a cold dream. "Grandpa, I did not mean to."

"Sit," Zhao Dehai repeated, eyes resting squarely on his grandson. "Take the porcelain cup from my basket, pour hot tea from the kettle, and offer it to Chen Xuan with both hands."

Zhao Mingyuan's ears burned crimson. Stiffly, he set his mug on the gravel, crouched beside the wicker basket, and poured steaming Pu'er. His hands trembled just enough to make the lid clink against the rim.

He walked over to Chen Xuan's folding stool and offered the cup with both hands, his head bowed low.

"Mister Chen Xuan," Zhao Mingyuan said tightly. "I spoke without thinking. Please forgive my rudeness."

Chen Xuan accepted the warm cup with both hands and nodded politely. "The tea smells good. Thank you, Section Chief Zhao."

He did not lecture or gloat. He simply took a slow sip and set the cup beside his thermos.

"Do you know why I gave Chen Xuan that float, Zhao Mingyuan?" Zhao Dehai asked quietly, looking back over the reservoir. "You spent five years in the provincial capital learning how to read titles, coats, and leather shoes. You think a loud stride is ambition and a quiet seat is weakness."

Zhao Mingyuan stood rigidly beside his grandfather's chair, unable to meet anyone's eyes.

"Real composure does not need a pressed coat to announce itself," Zhao Dehai said. "When a storm hits Yunxi County, the men who shout loudest run for cover first. The man sitting on that cheap canvas stool does not even blink. Sit beside him and learn how to keep your hands steady."

"Yes, Grandpa," Zhao Mingyuan whispered. "I understand."

Behind Chen Xuan's stool, Qin Yuhan lifted her cream knit sleeve slightly to hide a bright, delighted smile. Beside Zhao Dehai, Qin Zhengming opened the cedar tea box calmly, faint amusement warming his eyes.

Chen Fugen scratched his chin and muttered, "Well, look at that. Good wood really does hold its paint."

Before anyone replied, Tang Xiaoyu gave a sudden, sharp gasp.

Her red bobber had vanished beneath the surface, and her thin bamboo rod bent in a lively arc.

"Big Brother Chen Xuan!" Tang Xiaoyu cried, her careful voice breaking into pure excitement. "It is pulling! It is really pulling!"

"Hold the butt against your ribs, Tang Xiaoyu!" Chen Fugen called out, scrambling up. "Walk it sideways into the shallows!"

Chen Xuan set his tea down and stepped beside her, placing his hand lightly over hers only to steady the angle while letting her do the work. "Easy now. Keep the line tight. Walk two steps back."

Tang Xiaoyu planted her canvas shoes in the gravel and pulled with all her strength. A plump golden carp broke the surface in a bright spray of silver droplets before sliding cleanly onto the wet stones.

Chen Fugen scooped it into the plastic bucket with a triumphant laugh.

Tang Xiaoyu stared down into the bucket, cheeks flushed pink beneath her red sweater. "I caught one," she whispered. "I actually caught a fish."

"And the only one all morning," Qin Yuhan laughed softly, stepping over to look.

Zhao Dehai chuckled warmly, glancing at his own motionless float and Chen Fugen's empty line. "Well, Old Chen. The youngest fisherman at Qingshan Reservoir has beaten all of us today."

Chen Xuan looked at Tang Xiaoyu's bright, unguarded smile, then back at the quiet morning water. For a Saturday in late autumn, there was nothing better than this.
