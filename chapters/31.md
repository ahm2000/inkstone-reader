# Chapter 31: Stand-Down at 00:15

At fifteen minutes past midnight on Monday morning, every monitor inside the mobile command van along Riverside Road went blind in the same heartbeat.

Seven displays mapping Yunxi County's cameras, relays, and Shengyuan Capital's twenty-year asset chains snapped to flat matte black. A single hairline of white light cut across each screen, and a silent transfer bar raced from left to right faster than Black Pine Guard's hardware was built to sustain.

Number Fourteen's fingers froze half an inch above his keyboard. "What did you just touch?" he snapped, his voice tight as wire.

Beside him, Number Thirty-four shoved his rolling stool back until the frame clattered against the steel wall. Both of his hands rose away from the desk, palms open. "I did not touch anything," Number Thirty-four said, his breathing shallow and rapid. "My root keys just dissolved. The archive is uploading itself out of our mesh."

"Out of our mesh?" Number Fourteen's jaw clenched as he reached for the manual kill switch beneath the counter, his knuckles turning white. "Nobody penetrates a Black Pine mobile node in under a second without triggering the burn protocol."

"Do not touch that switch," a quiet voice ordered from the rear of the van.

The side door had opened and shut without a click. Number Seven stood in the narrow aisle, still wearing his dark windbreaker from Building 2. His face sat shadowed beneath his hood, yet the rigid set of his shoulders and the stillness in his hands made both technicians look twice.

Number Fourteen hovered over the red breaker, eyes darting between the racing bars and Number Seven. "Number Seven, we are losing every Yunxi file and all fourteen provincial chains. If an outside bureau cracked our relay, I have to fry the drives now."

"Leave it alone," Number Seven said flatly, folding his hands behind his back. "No outside bureau cracked anything. Look at the header."

Number Thirty-four squinted at the text above the transfer bar. Color drained from his face. "There is no header. No routing signature, no clearance code, and no receiving ID. Just an empty white bar."

"Because we lack the clearance to read who is taking it," Number Seven murmured.

Number Fourteen's hand fell away from the switch, pupils widening. "You were on the rooftop with him. Did Chen Xuan turn the second key?"

"Yes," Number Seven replied. "Ten minutes ago."

Silence dropped over the van as the transfer hit one hundred percent and every screen locked behind a blank obsidian seal.

Three kilometers away in the Lanshui safehouse, the same white line sliced across the operations wall.

Number Eleven was lighting a cigarette when the display died. His lighter dropped onto the concrete. Before it stopped rolling, his right hand flashed under his jacket toward his pistol.

A firm hand clamped over his wrist.

"Stop," Number One said.

Number Eleven jerked his head up. Number One stood motionless before the dead glass, a faint pulse beating beneath his jaw. "Number One, our master vault just transferred out," Number Eleven hissed, chest heaving. "Every provincial annex, asset map, and safehouse coordinate. Even our internal tracker went dark."

"I know," Number One said quietly, releasing his wrist once the younger operative's fingers uncurled. "I authorized the transfer at 00:08."

Number Eleven stared in shock. "To whom? Nobody stands above Black Pine Guard except Chen Xuan himself."

Before Number One answered, a faint click carried from the outer hallway.

The Lanshui safehouse sat behind three steel doors, four deadbolts, and a biometric lock keyed solely to Number One. No alarm sounded. The deadbolts retracted smoothly, and the inner door swung open six inches without a creak.

Number Eleven's shoulders snapped taut. Sweat broke out along his temples as two silhouettes stepped silently across the threshold and halted on the edge of the room.

They wore featureless matte-black gear from throat to boot, devoid of rank tabs or serial markings. Seamless, expressionless full masks sealed their faces. Neither figure carried a visible rifle, yet the cold absence of human hesitation in their stance made the air feel thin. They did not look at the dead screens or at Number Eleven's hand. They simply stood in the doorway like cast-iron statues, locking the room in silent containment.

Number Eleven did not dare breathe loudly. His eyes flicked back to Number One.

Number One inclined his head half an inch toward the two masked figures, receiving no shift in return. Slowly, he reached down and tapped the single amber light still blinking on his encrypted bridge. A red countdown beside it showed twenty-eight seconds remaining before Black Pine Guard's internal voice mesh dissolved completely.

"Riverside van," Number One said softly, his voice carrying clipped gravity. "Check in."

Inside the van, Number Seven answered immediately. "Number Seven here. Fourteen and Thirty-four are present. Screens are sealed. Containment protocol acknowledged."

"How many outside your doors?" Number One asked.

Number Seven listened to the unmistakable stillness beyond the thin steel shell of the van. "At least two. Nobody knocked. The street went dead quiet thirty seconds before 00:15."

"Do not open the doors," Number One instructed. "Do not reach for sidearms, do not touch the radios, and do not step outside until the lock clears."

Beside him, Number Eleven leaned closer to the tabletop microphone, his voice dropping to a strained whisper. "Number One, tell me plainly. Are we being dismantled? Are they here to audit us, or to erase us?"

Number One glanced once at the two motionless masked figures in the doorway. Their dark visors reflected only the dying amber glow. "Neither," Number One said calmly, though his fingers rested rigid against the steel desk. "Zero Guard does not waste motion auditing loyal hands. When Chen Xuan wakes them, every variable with a human pulse and a paper trail is cleared off the board. We built the case. We mapped the network. From 00:15 onward, Black Pine Guard does not exist until he says we do."

On the speaker, Number Fourteen's voice broke through, taut with awe. "I have served six years in the Black Numbers. I thought Zero was a ghost story used to keep branch commanders honest. What in Yunxi could make Chen Xuan turn that key?"

"Shut your mouth, Number Fourteen," Number One cut in, his voice dropping to a razor edge that made Number Eleven flinch. "Never ask that question again. Not on a wire, not in a sealed room, and not inside your own head. If Chen Xuan locked the country down at 00:15, then the country needed to be locked down."

Number Fourteen swallowed audibly. "Understood, Number One. My mistake."

"Number Seven," Number One said as the counter dropped below five seconds. "You were closest to him tonight. Was he angry?"

Inside the van, Number Seven thought of the quiet rooftop, the scratched green thermos, and Chen Xuan's calm nod before walking downstairs to sleep.

"No," Number Seven answered softly. "He was completely calm. That is what frightened me."

The counter hit zero. The amber light blinked out, cutting the bridge into total silence.

Number One lifted his headset off, set it beside the dead terminal, and folded his hands behind his back. Beside him, Number Eleven left his unlit cigarette on the floor and stood straight under the silent gaze of the two masked sentries in the doorway.
