# Chapter 31: Stand-Down at 00:15

At fifteen minutes past midnight Monday, every screen inside the command van on Riverside Road went black simultaneously.

Seven monitors had tracked Yunxi County's cameras, phone lines, and Shengyuan Capital's twenty-year money trails. One heartbeat later, a thin white line sliced across each black screen. A silent progress bar raced across faster than White Guard's computers were built to run.

Number 14 froze above his keyboard. "What did you just touch?" he snapped.

Number 34 pushed his stool back against the steel wall with a clatter, throwing both hands up. "I touched nothing," Number 34 gasped. "My password stopped working. Every file is copying itself out of our system."

"Out of our system?" Number 14 reached under the desk for the manual shutoff switch. "Nobody breaks into a White Guard van in under a second without tripping the wipe switch. I am cutting power now."

"Do not touch that switch," a quiet voice ordered from behind.

The side door had opened and closed without a click. Number 7 stood in the narrow aisle in his dark windbreaker from Building 2. His face stayed shadowed beneath his hood, yet his stiff shoulders and total stillness froze both men.

Number 14 hovered near the red breaker. "Number 7, we are losing every Yunxi file and all fourteen province trails. If an outside agency broke in, I have to fry the drives."

"Leave it," Number 7 said flatly, folding his hands behind his back. "No outside agency broke in. Look at the top line."

Number 34 leaned forward and squinted above the progress bar. All color left his face. "No top line. No tracking tag, no access code, no receiver name. Just an empty white bar."

"Because we do not have the right to see who is taking it," Number 7 said softly.

Number 14 dropped his hand, eyes wide. "You were on the rooftop tonight. Did Chen Xuan turn the second key?"

"Yes," Number 7 said. "Ten minutes ago."

Heavy silence filled the van. The bar hit one hundred percent, and every screen locked behind a blank black wall. Neither man dared touch his keyboard again.

Three kilometers away, inside the Sha City safehouse, the exact same thin white line sliced across the main screen wall.

Number 11 was lighting a cigarette when the display died. His lighter hit the concrete floor. Before it stopped rolling, his hand flashed under his jacket toward his gun.

A firm hand clamped over his wrist.

"Stop," Number 1 said.

Number 11 jerked his head up. Number 1 stood motionless before the dead glass, a muscle twitching along his jaw. "Number 1, our file vault just got pulled out," Number 11 hissed, chest heaving. "Every province folder, every money chart, every safehouse address. Even our tracking screen went dark. We have been hit."

"I know," Number 1 said quietly, releasing his wrist once his fingers relaxed. "We were not hit. I approved the transfer at 00:08."

Number 11 stared in disbelief. "To who? Nobody stands above White Guard except Chen Xuan. Who else could open our vault?"

A faint click echoed from the outer hall.

The safehouse sat behind three steel doors, four heavy locks, and a fingerprint lock coded only to Number 1. No alarm sounded. The locks slid back on their own, and the inner door swung open six inches without a creak.

Cold sweat broke along Number 11's temples as two dark figures stepped silently across the doorway.

They wore plain black gear from neck to boot, with no badges, rank strips, or numbers. Smooth, blank full masks hid their faces. Neither carried a rifle in plain sight, yet their total lack of hesitation made the air feel thin. Ignoring the dead screens and Number 11's hand, they stood in the doorway like iron statues, holding the room in quiet lockdown.

Number 11 barely dared breathe, glancing at Number 1.

Number 1 dipped his head half an inch toward the masked figures. Neither moved. Slowly, Number 1 tapped the single yellow light blinking on his private radio. Twenty-eight seconds remained before White Guard's voice line shut down for good.

"Riverside van," Number 1 said softly. "Check in."

Inside the van, Number 7 answered immediately. "Number 7 here. Number 14 and Number 34 are with me. Screens sealed. Lockdown understood."

"How many outside your doors?" Number 1 asked.

Number 7 listened to the dead stillness outside. No cars passed; no dogs barked. "At least two. Nobody knocked. The whole street went silent thirty seconds before 00:15."

"Do not open the doors," Number 1 ordered. "Do not reach for guns, touch radios, or step outside until the lock clears. Wait in your seats."

Number 11 leaned toward the mic, whispering tightly. "Number 1, tell me straight. Are we shut down for good? Are they checking our work, or wiping us out?"

Number 1 glanced at the motionless figures in the doorway. "Neither," he said calmly, fingers pressing hard against the steel table. "Black Guard does not waste time checking loyal men. When Chen Xuan wakes them, everyone leaving a paper trail gets pulled off the board. We built the case and mapped the network. From 00:15 onward, White Guard does not exist until he says we do."

Over the speaker, Number 14 broke in, voice tight with awe. "Six years in White Guard, and I thought Black Guard was a ghost story to keep local leaders in line. What in Yunxi could make Chen Xuan turn that key?"

"Shut your mouth, Number 14," Number 1 snapped so coldly that Number 11 flinched. "Never ask that question again. Not on radio, not in a closed room, not even in your own head. If Chen Xuan locked the country down at 00:15, the country needed to be locked down."

Number 14 swallowed hard. "Understood, Number 1. My mistake."

"Number 7," Number 1 said as the countdown dropped below five seconds. "You were closest to him tonight. Was he angry?"

Inside the van, Number 7 recalled the quiet rooftop, the green thermos, and Chen Xuan's calm nod before walking downstairs to sleep.

"No," Number 7 answered softly. "He was completely calm. That is what scared me."

The countdown hit zero. The yellow light died, and the radio fell silent.

Number 1 set his headset beside the dead screen and folded his hands behind his back. Beside him, Number 11 left his unlit cigarette on the floor, straightened his spine, and stood still under the silent watch of the two masked guards.
