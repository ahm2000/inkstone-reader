# Chapter 31: Stand-Down at 00:15

At fifteen minutes past midnight on Monday morning, every screen inside the command van on Riverside Road went black at the exact same second.

Seven screens had been tracking Yunxi County's street cameras, phone lines, and Shengyuan Capital's twenty-year money trails. One heartbeat later, all seven turned solid black. A thin white line cut across each screen. A silent progress bar raced from left to right, moving faster than White Guard's computers were ever built to run.

Number 14 froze with his fingers half an inch above his keyboard. "What did you just touch?" he snapped, his voice sharp and tight.

Beside him, Number 34 pushed his rolling stool back until the metal legs hit the steel wall with a loud clatter. He threw both hands up in the air, palms open. "I did not touch anything," Number 34 said, breathing fast and shallow. "My password just stopped working. Every file we have is copying itself out of our system."

"Out of our system?" Number 14 clenched his jaw and reached under the desk for the manual shutoff switch. His knuckles turned white as he hovered over it. "Nobody breaks into a White Guard van in under a second without setting off the wipe switch. I am cutting the power right now."

"Do not touch that switch," a quiet voice ordered from the back of the van.

The side door had opened and closed without even a click. Number 7 stood in the narrow aisle, still wearing his dark windbreaker from Building 2. His face stayed hidden in the shadow of his hood, yet the stiff set of his shoulders and the total stillness in his hands made both men freeze.

Number 14 kept his hand near the red breaker. His eyes jumped back and forth between the racing white bars and Number 7. "Number 7, we are losing every Yunxi file and all fourteen province money trails. If an outside agency broke into our system, I have to fry the hard drives right now before they get everything."

"Leave it alone," Number 7 said flatly. He folded his hands behind his back. "No outside agency broke into anything. Look at the top line of the screen."

Number 34 leaned forward and squinted at the space above the progress bar. All the color drained from his face. "There is no top line. No tracking tag, no access code, and no receiver name. It is just an empty white bar."

"Because we do not have the right to see who is taking it," Number 7 said softly.

Number 14 dropped his hand from the switch. His eyes went wide. "You were on the rooftop with him tonight. Did Chen Xuan turn the second key?"

"Yes," Number 7 said. "Ten minutes ago."

Heavy silence fell over the cramped van. The progress bar hit one hundred percent. Every screen locked shut behind a blank black wall. Neither Number 14 nor Number 34 dared to touch their keyboards again.

Three kilometers away, inside the Sha City safehouse, the exact same thin white line sliced across the main screen wall.

Number 11 was lifting a lighter to a cigarette when the display went dead. The lighter slipped from his fingers and hit the concrete floor. Before it even stopped rolling, his right hand flashed under his jacket toward his gun.

A firm hand clamped down over his wrist and stopped him cold.

"Stop," Number 1 said.

Number 11 jerked his head up. Number 1 stood motionless in front of the dead glass, a faint muscle twitching along his jaw. "Number 1, our main file vault just got pulled out," Number 11 hissed, his chest heaving. "Every province folder, every money chart, and every safehouse address. Even our own tracking screen went dark. We have been hit."

"I know," Number 1 said quietly. He waited until Number 11's fingers slowly relaxed, then let go of his wrist. "We were not hit. I approved the transfer at 00:08."

Number 11 stared at him in disbelief. "To who? Nobody stands above White Guard except Chen Xuan himself. Who else could even open our vault?"

Before Number 1 could answer, a faint click came from the outer hall.

The Sha City safehouse sat behind three steel doors, four heavy locks, and a fingerprint lock that only opened for Number 1. Not a single alarm went off. The heavy locks slid back smoothly on their own, and the inner door swung open six inches without a creak.

Number 11's shoulders snapped tight. Cold sweat broke out along his temples as two dark figures stepped silently across the doorway and stopped at the edge of the room.

They wore plain black gear from neck to boot, with no badges, no rank strips, and no numbers. Smooth, blank full masks covered their faces. Neither figure carried a rifle in plain sight, yet the cold lack of human hesitation in the way they stood made the air in the room feel thin and hard to breathe. They did not look at the dead screens. They did not look at Number 11's hand. They simply stood in the doorway like iron statues, holding the room in quiet lockdown.

Number 11 did not dare breathe loudly. His eyes flicked back to Number 1 for an order.

Number 1 dipped his head half an inch toward the two masked figures. Neither figure moved in return. Slowly, Number 1 reached down and tapped the single yellow light still blinking on his private radio. A red countdown beside it showed twenty-eight seconds left before White Guard's voice line shut down for good.

"Riverside van," Number 1 said softly, his voice calm and heavy. "Check in."

Inside the van, Number 7 answered at once. "Number 7 here. Number 14 and Number 34 are with me. Screens are sealed. Lockdown understood."

"How many are outside your doors?" Number 1 asked.

Number 7 listened to the dead stillness beyond the thin steel walls of the van. Not a car passed on the road. Not a dog barked. "At least two. Nobody knocked. The whole street went silent thirty seconds before 00:15."

"Do not open the doors," Number 1 ordered. "Do not reach for your guns, do not touch the radios, and do not step outside until the lock clears. Wait in your seats."

Beside him, Number 11 leaned closer to the desk microphone. His voice dropped to a tight whisper. "Number 1, tell me straight. Are we being shut down for good? Are they here to check our work, or are they here to wipe us out?"

Number 1 glanced once at the two motionless masked figures in the doorway. Their dark eye shields reflected only the fading yellow light from the desk. "Neither," Number 1 said calmly, though his fingers pressed stiff and hard against the steel table. "Black Guard does not waste time checking loyal men. When Chen Xuan wakes them up, everyone who leaves a paper trail gets pulled off the board. We built the case. We mapped the whole network. From 00:15 onward, White Guard does not exist until he says we do."

Over the speaker, Number 14's voice broke in, tight with shock and awe. "I have served six years in White Guard. I thought Black Guard was just a ghost story used to keep local leaders in line. What in Yunxi could possibly make Chen Xuan turn that key?"

"Shut your mouth, Number 14," Number 1 cut in. His voice turned so sharp and cold that Number 11 flinched right beside him. "Never ask that question again. Not on a radio, not in a closed room, and not even inside your own head. If Chen Xuan locked the country down at 00:15, then the country needed to be locked down."

Number 14 swallowed hard, loud enough to hear through the speaker. "Understood, Number 1. My mistake."

"Number 7," Number 1 said as the red numbers dropped below five seconds. "You were closest to him tonight. Was he angry?"

Inside the van, Number 7 thought back to the quiet rooftop, the scratched green thermos, and Chen Xuan's calm nod before he walked downstairs to go to sleep.

"No," Number 7 answered softly. "He was completely calm. That is what scared me."

The countdown hit zero. The yellow light blinked out, and the radio fell into total silence.

Number 1 lifted his headset off, set it neatly beside the dead screen, and folded his hands behind his back. Beside him, Number 11 left his unlit cigarette lying on the concrete floor. He straightened his spine and stood still under the silent watch of the two masked guards in the doorway.
