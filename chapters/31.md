# Chapter 31: Someone at the Vegetable Plot

In the Qingyan Municipal Party Committee office, the call that moved half a city began before the first tomato was picked, at not quite seven on Sunday morning.

The Office Director, the man who runs the schedule of the city's top leader, sat at his desk in the Municipal Party Committee building with a single sheet of notes. He had slept badly, because a message had come down to him in the night from far above the city, and he had read it four times.

He did not know who the old lady was, and he did not want to know. He only knew what to say, and to whom.

First he phoned the city police chief, who was also a Vice Mayor, and then he phoned Yunxi County's police director, and the words were the same each time.

"A retired senior leader will be at the Hongshi Village vegetable plots today, for cabbages. Nobody approaches her, nobody asks her name, and nobody inspects the compost. If her Sunday is disturbed, do not bother coming back to work on Monday."

The city police chief did not ask a single question.

In her day, when this woman walked into a meeting, the Mayor used to stand up. The current Mayor had heard that story at his first meeting, and he had stood up for her too, once, at her retirement party, out of habit.

Put plainly, an order from the very top had just landed on a police chief's desk, and it was about a woman in a straw hat!

---

Outside Hongshi Village, County Police Director Mai Zhenhua had been told, and now he was obeying.

He sat in the back seat of a plain black sedan, in plain clothes, two blocks from the plots, because his orders were "no uniform, one car, no approaching." Only a short while ago he had stood in front of a provincial inspection team without his knees shaking. Now he sat very still with his hands on his knees and prayed that nothing at all would happen.

What if a dog barked at the wrong moment? What if a child cried? Worst of all, somebody might ask who the old lady was, and he did not know.

In the front seat, his driver held a newspaper, and the man was so nervous that he had been reading it for ten minutes without noticing it was upside down.

"Director, should we move any closer?" the driver whispered.

"No. Turn the paper over," Mai Zhenhua said.

"Yes, Director."

A minute later, it was upside down again.

Mai Zhenhua sighed and left it. Thirty years a policeman, and he was guarding a cabbage patch with an upside-down newspaper!

---

Across town at the Xinhe District Office, Fang Jun, head of the district, was not told anything.

A district head runs one large area of the city, at the same rank as a county magistrate, and he is supposed to know about everything inside it. So when his chief of staff rushed into his office, he put down his tea.

"District head, something strange is happening. The city police have put two patrol cars on Hongshi Road, and a sanitation crew swept the whole road on a Sunday. The city office just asked us for our sewer maps, and somebody says there will be a routine inspection tomorrow that nobody here scheduled!"

Fang Jun went cold!

He had been at the plots that morning, and he had been scolded by name in front of villagers. He had walked away before the argument was finished, holding a bag of persimmons, which he now realized he had set down on the sidewalk and forgotten!

Not in his plan!

Sunday was supposed to be quiet. A week of unreturned phone calls was bad enough, but a swept road, patrol cars and sewer maps all at once meant something much bigger. Was it a complaint? Or was somebody above him angry, and nobody had the courage to say so?

No, no, no. He had done nothing wrong, except forget to call back!

"Did somebody report me?" he whispered. "What did I do? What did I miss?"

Down the hall, a junior clerk who had never once been invited to a leader's meeting was already composing the story she would tell at dinner, about the district head who got caught holding someone else's persimmons.

His chief of staff stared at him. "District head, what are you talking about?"

"Never mind," Fang Jun whispered. "Find out who scheduled that inspection."

"Nobody did, district head. That is the problem!"

---

At the Municipal Police Command Center, the youngest duty officer in the room was Gu Ming, twenty-four and brand new to the job, and he had no idea what was about to happen to him.

Two senior officers passed behind his desk, talking low.

"Keep Hongshi quiet. Nobody near it. It is her cabbage day."

"I know. I have already pulled nine plain-clothes men off their Sunday."

Gu Ming turned around. "Which old lady? What cabbage?"

Both men stopped dead!

The senior one leaned down and looked into his face. "Little Gu. Do you want to keep this job?"

"Yes, sir."

"Then you did not hear anything."

"Understood, sir."

Gu Ming went pale, and the room went quiet around him!

"Shh..." someone whispered.

Someone else mouthed the same question, and nobody answered.

In thirty seconds, Gu Ming had learned that cabbage could be a secret, and that his job was less safe than he thought!

---

Back in the Chen family courtyard in Hongshi Village, nobody knew any of this. The Chen family was dozing after a big village lunch, and all they knew was that a black car was parked by the old well and its driver was reading the newspaper wrong.

Anyone at the vegetable garden that morning could have told the police a great deal about the old lady. She was loud, she was short, and she had scolded a district head over an unreturned call.

Nobody at the garden had told the police, because nobody at the garden knew!

---

Late that night in his apartment, Fang Jun stood in front of his bathroom mirror and practiced.

"Ma'am, about the ditch budget," he said to his reflection. "I am so sorry. I was going to call. I have the sheet right here."

He frowned and tried again.

"Auntie Dong, I..."

Wrong!

He had said "Auntie Dong" to her face that morning, because she had always insisted on it. Tonight, after the swept road and the patrol cars, the name sounded far too small. He still remembered the title she had held when he was a young clerk carrying her coffee, and he could not make himself say it, even to a mirror.

He took out his little notebook, where three words sat in the hurried writing of that morning: *odd days, even.* Whoever that young man at the plots was, he had solved in two minutes what the district offices had argued over for eleven months.

But that was not what kept him standing in the bathroom light.

Which was worse, that she had seen him run away, or that she had not?

The routine inspection would arrive at nine a.m., and he had only one night!
