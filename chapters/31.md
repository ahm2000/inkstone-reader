# Chapter 31: Someone at the Vegetable Plot

The call that moved half a city began before the first tomato was picked, at not quite seven on Sunday morning, in the Municipal Party Committee office.

The Office Director of the Municipal Party Committee, the man who runs the city's top leader's schedule, sat at his desk with a single sheet of notes. He had slept badly. A message had come down to him in the night, from far above the city, and he had read it four times.

He did not know who the old lady was, and he did not want to know. He only knew what to say, and to whom.

First he phoned the city police chief, who was also a Vice Mayor, and then he phoned Yunxi County's police director. The words were the same each time.

"A retired senior leader will be at the Hongshi Village vegetable plots today. For cabbages. Nobody approaches. Nobody asks her name. Nobody inspects the compost. If her Sunday is disturbed, don't bother coming back to work on Monday."

The city police chief did not ask a single question. In her day, when this woman walked into a meeting, the Mayor used to stand up. The current Mayor had heard the story at his first meeting, and he had stood up for her too, once, at her retirement party, out of habit.

Put plainly: an order from the very top had just landed on a police chief's desk, and it was about a woman in a straw hat.

An hour later, in the lane outside Hongshi Village, County Police Director Mai Zhenhua had been told, and he was obeying.

He sat in the back seat of a plain black sedan, in plain clothes, two blocks from the plots. He had been told "no uniform, one car, no approaching." Only a short while ago, he had stood in front of a provincial inspection team without his knees shaking. Now he sat very still, with his hands on his knees, praying that nothing at all would happen.

In the front seat, his driver held a newspaper. The man was so nervous that he had been reading it for ten minutes without noticing it was upside down.

"Turn it over," Mai Zhenhua said.

"Yes, Director."

A minute later, it was upside down again.

Mai Zhenhua sighed and left it. Thirty years a policeman, and he was guarding a cabbage patch with an upside-down newspaper!

Across town that afternoon, at the Xinhe District Office, Fang Jun, head of the district, was not told anything.

A district head runs one large area of the city, at the same rank as a county magistrate, and he is supposed to know about everything inside it. So when his chief of staff rushed into his office, he put down his tea.

"District head, something strange is happening. The city police put two patrol cars on Hongshi Road. A sanitation crew swept the whole road, on a Sunday. The city office just asked us for our sewer maps. And somebody said there will be a routine inspection tomorrow, and nobody here scheduled it!"

Fang Jun went cold.

He had been at the plots that morning. He had been scolded by name in front of villagers. He had walked away before the argument was finished, holding a bag of persimmons, which he now realized he had set down on the sidewalk and forgotten.

"Did somebody report me?!" he whispered. "What did I do? What did I miss?"

Somewhere down the hall, a junior clerk who'd never once been invited to a leader's meeting was already composing the story she'd tell at dinner about the district head who got caught holding someone else's persimmons.

His chief of staff stared. "District head, what are you talking about?"

By three-thirty, at the Municipal Police Command Center, the youngest duty officer in the room, Gu Ming, twenty-four and brand new to the job, had no idea what was about to happen.

Two senior officers passed behind his desk, talking low.

"Keep Hongshi quiet. Nobody near it. It's her cabbage day."

"I know. I've already pulled nine plain-clothes men off their Sunday."

Gu Ming turned. "Which old lady? What cabbage?"

Both men stopped dead!

The senior one leaned down and looked into his face. "Little Gu. Do you want to keep this job?"

"Yes, sir."

"Then you didn't hear anything."

Gu Ming went pale. In thirty seconds he had learned that cabbage could be a secret, and that his job was less safe than he thought.

Back in Hongshi Village, nobody knew any of this. The Chen family was dozing after a big village lunch. All they knew was that a black car was parked by the old well and the driver was reading his newspaper wrong.

Anyone at the vegetable garden that morning could have told the police a great deal about the old lady. She was loud, she was short, and she had scolded a district head about a ditch budget while holding a hoe.

Nobody at the garden had told the police, because nobody at the garden knew!

Late that night, in his apartment, Fang Jun stood in front of his bathroom mirror and practiced.

"Ma'am, about the ditch budget," he said to his reflection. "I am so sorry. I was going to call. I have the sheet right here."

He frowned. He tried again.

"Auntie Dong, I..."

Wrong. He had said "Auntie Dong" to her face that morning, because she had always insisted on it. Tonight, after the swept road and the patrol cars, the name sounded far too small. He still remembered the title she had held when he was a young clerk carrying her coffee, and he could not make himself say it, even to a mirror.

He took out his little notebook. Three words, written in a hurry that morning: *odd days, even.* Whoever that young man at the plots was, he had solved in two minutes what the district offices had argued over for eleven months.

But that was not what kept him standing in the bathroom light.

He could not decide which was worse: that she had seen him run away, or that she hadn't.

The routine inspection would arrive at nine a.m., and he had one night.
