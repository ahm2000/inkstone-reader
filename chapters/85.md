# Chapter 85: Elder Song's Afternoon Tea

Long shadows stretched across the quiet stone yard in the Western Hills. Along the outer wall stood ten motionless figures in plain black gear, visors dark, rifles hanging still as iron. Even the mountain wind barely stirred their coats.

Inside the wooden pavilion, ninety-year-old Founding Elder Song poured dark Pu'er tea from a clay pot into two small cups. His spotted hands moved slowly, never spilling a drop.

General Qin Zhengguo sat across from him on a wooden stool, his cap resting beside a pack of unfiltered cigarettes. He had spent twelve hours shouting down spy chiefs and signing warrants, yet in front of Elder Song his shoulders carried a quiet weight.

"Drink while it is hot, Qin Zhengguo," Elder Song said in a voice dry as pine bark. "You look like you have been chewing iron since sunrise."

Qin Zhengguo took a slow sip and set the cup down. "Worse than iron, Elder Song. Rust."

Elder Song picked a cigarette from the pack. Qin Zhengguo leaned forward at once to light it, then lit one for himself.

"Give me the real count," Elder Song said, his cloudy eyes fixing on Qin Zhengguo. "Not the neat version written for the committee."

Qin Zhengguo's jaw tightened. "Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang broke after noon. He gave Gao Zhengyi three retired bank presidents and a Geneva middleman named Lukas Weber. In Sha City, Shengyuan Capital Chairman Fang Qiming and Vice Governor Lin Zhengde tore at each other's throats in separate rooms. Six provinces, Elder Song. They buried rare-earth and army-metal smuggling bunkers under county wrecking sites."

Elder Song took a calm puff. "And the army?"

A dark flush crept up Qin Zhengguo's neck. "Three senior colonels and eleven supply officers in the Southern Theater. They sold metal shipping logs through forty-seven foreign agents in Shanghai. I just fought Ding Guoping for a forty-eight-hour hold so State Security can trace their midnight radio bursts back to Zurich."

Elder Song nodded slowly, his eyes drifting for a heartbeat toward the nearest Black Guard sentry by the gate.

"And the banks?" Elder Song asked.

"1.2 trillion yuan in twenty years of hidden trails," Qin Zhengguo said softly. "An hour ago, second-tier family men in the war room tried to stall the freeze by shouting that fourteen provincial loan groups were too big to fail. Elder Lin and Elder Ye shut them up in sixty seconds."

A faint smile touched Elder Song's mouth. "Because Elder Lin opened the blue folder."

Qin Zhengguo exhaled slowly, staring at his glowing cigarette. "Elder Song, I have led troops on the border and sat on the Military Commission for ten years. When Fang Zhengming, Han Guoliang, Tang Wenhao, and Liang Qingshan confirmed that 300 billion yuan in clean, legal money had already cleared into China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank, half the ministers forgot how to breathe."

For a long minute, neither man spoke. Only burning tobacco and dry leaves broke the stillness.

Elder Song stared into his teacup, his old face filled with quiet awe.

"Think about what happened today, Qin Zhengguo," Elder Song murmured. "The holder of the Black Order, the one man who opens the secret vault Black Guard protects, did not just drop a knife on our desk to expose 1.2 trillion yuan of rot. He knew tearing out a twenty-year tumor could bleed our banks dry and panic ordinary families. So he handed the state 300 billion yuan of clean, legal wealth for free, with up to 500 billion yuan ready if needed."

Qin Zhengguo gripped his porcelain cup tight. "No loan contract. No bank shares. Not one title, not one seat, not a single favor asked in return. Elder Lin checked every paper twice because he could not believe any person alive would give away 300 billion yuan and ask for nothing."

Elder Song let out a slow, wheezing breath. "Seventy years ago in the mountains, you could see the enemy across the river. You aimed your rifle and held the ridge. Peace is harder, Qin Zhengguo. Twenty years of fast money turned smart men into termites. They hollowed out every beam while painting the front door gold."

He tapped ash onto the stone saucer. "Even our old houses grew soft. Today, one unseen hand gave us the broom to sweep out the traitors and 300 billion yuan of clean steel to hold the roof up while we sweep. Have you ever seen a heart like that in Beijing?"

Qin Zhengguo crushed his cigarette out and shook his head. "Never. Without that file and that reserve, we were five years from waking up inside an empty shell. We owe the holder of the Black Order more than words can pay."

Elder Song paused with his teacup halfway to his lips, then set it back down untouched. His cloudy eyes cleared suddenly, turning so sharp that Qin Zhengguo sat straight up.

"We do," Elder Song murmured. "A drowning man is always grateful for the hand that pulls him out. Yet a wise soldier still looks at the bank and asks why the hand reached down today."

Qin Zhengguo frowned, a chill creeping down his spine. "Elder Song, you are one of three living men who know the charter of the Black Order vault. You told us yourself that Black Guard does not answer to the Military Commission, State Security, or any office in Beijing."

"I did," Elder Song said softly. "And what is the first rule of that charter?"

"Total silence," Qin Zhengguo recited quietly. "The ledger watches and waits without stepping in until the holder decides a line has been crossed that the state can no longer see."

"Twenty years," Elder Song repeated, his cigarette burning forgotten toward his wrinkled fingers. "Those bank presidents built their Zurich channel in 2007. He Guoliang took his first secret bond payout in 2011. Fang Qiming dug his first wrecking bunker six years ago. The Southern Theater leaks began three winters ago. Black Guard held every bond code, every tape, and every Shanghai agent address the whole time."

Qin Zhengguo's breath slowed as a cold knot tightened in his chest.

"Wait," Qin Zhengguo whispered. "If the holder of the Black Order had all of this for years, and had 300 billion yuan ready the whole time, why did nothing move in 2011? Why did nothing move three years ago when my own supply bases started leaking?"

Elder Song did not answer right away. He glanced once more at the ten motionless black sentries ringing the Western Hills yard, silent proof of a power that had waited twenty years and then shaken the capital in one morning.

Then Founding Elder Song turned his ancient eyes back to Qin Zhengguo and asked the question that turned the general's blood to ice.

"Qin Zhengguo, after twenty years of silence, why did the holder of the Black Order finally open the gate today?"
