# Chapter 85: Elder Song's Afternoon Tea

Late Monday afternoon cast long shadows across the quiet stone yard in the Western Hills. Along the outer wall, ten motionless figures stood evenly spaced in plain black gear. Their visors were dark, and their rifles hung low and still as carved iron. They did not shift their weight, they did not look toward the pavilion, and even the cold mountain wind barely moved the hems of their coats.

Inside the open wooden pavilion, ninety-year-old Founding Elder Song poured dark, bitter Pu'er tea from a clay pot into two small cups. His spotted hands moved slowly, yet stayed steady enough not to spill a single drop.

General Qin Zhengguo sat across from him on a plain wooden stool, his uniform cap resting beside a pack of unfiltered cigarettes. He had spent the past twelve hours shouting down spy chiefs and signing arrest papers, yet here in front of Elder Song, his shoulders carried a quiet, heavy weight.

"Drink while it is hot, Qin Zhengguo," Elder Song said, his voice dry and raspy like old pine bark. "You look as if you have been chewing iron since sunrise."

Qin Zhengguo lifted the cup, took a slow sip of the bitter tea, and set it down. "Worse than iron, Elder Song. Rust."

Elder Song picked a cigarette from the pack on the table and tapped it gently against his thumb. Qin Zhengguo leaned forward at once to light it for him, then lit one for himself. Both men blew thin clouds of smoke into the cool yard air.

"Tell me the real count," Elder Song said quietly, his cloudy eyes fixing on Qin Zhengguo's face. "Not the cleaned-up version they are writing for the committee."

Qin Zhengguo's jaw tightened. "Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang broke an hour after noon. He gave Gao Zhengyi three retired state bank presidents and a Geneva middleman named Lukas Weber. In Lanshui, Shengyuan Capital Chairman Fang Qiming and Vice Governor Lin Zhengde tore at each other's throats in separate rooms. Six provinces, Elder Song. They buried rare-earth and army-metal smuggling rooms under county wrecking sites."

Elder Song did not blink. He took a small, calm puff from his cigarette. "And the army?"

A dark flush crept up Qin Zhengguo's neck, shame and anger mixing in his eyes. "Three senior colonels and eleven supply officers in the Southern Theater. They sold metal shipping logs through a ring of forty-seven foreign agents in Shanghai. I just fought Ding Guoping for a forty-eight-hour hold so State Security can trace their midnight radio bursts back to Zurich."

Elder Song nodded slowly. His eyes drifted for a brief moment toward the nearest black-clad Black Guard sentry standing silent by the yard gate.

"And the money?" Elder Song asked.

"100 trillion yuan in twenty years of hidden money trails," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice dropping so low it barely crossed the stone table. "Gu Chengshan and Zhou Zhenhua just ordered a quiet state takeover behind the banking system. If they had moved in public, twenty-two provincial loan groups would have failed before Friday."

For a long minute, neither man spoke. The only sound in the Western Hills yard was the faint hiss of burning tobacco and the soft rustle of dry leaves beyond the wall.

Qin Zhengguo stared down into his dark cup of tea, his fingers gripping the porcelain rim tight. "Elder Song, I have led troops on the border and sat on the Military Commission for ten years. I thought I knew where the cracks in the wall were. Today I realized we were five years away from waking up inside an empty shell. The loan committees, the power grid, six provincial governments, even my own supply bases. If that Zurich file had not dropped onto our desks this morning, none of our own checks would have caught the full web in time."

Elder Song let out a slow, wheezing breath that sounded like a bitter sigh. "When we fought in the mountains seventy years ago, we could see the enemy across the river. You aimed your rifle, you held the ridge, and you knew who stood behind your back. Peace is harder, Qin Zhengguo. Twenty years of fast money turned smart men into termites. They did not knock the house down. They hollowed out every main beam while painting the front door gold."

Qin Zhengguo crushed his cigarette out in the stone saucer and looked up sharply. "Then we should be grateful the vault opened when it did. Whoever controls Black Guard handed the state a knife sharp enough to cut out the whole rot in two days."

Elder Song stopped with his teacup halfway to his lips. Slowly, he set the cup back on the table without drinking. His cloudy eyes cleared suddenly, turning so sharp and piercing that Qin Zhengguo sat up straight without thinking.

"Grateful?" Elder Song murmured. "Yes. A man saved from drowning is always grateful for the hand that pulls him out of the river."

Qin Zhengguo frowned, feeling an unspoken chill under the old man's tone. "Elder Song, you were one of the only three living men who knew the original rules of the Zero vault. You told us yourself this morning that Black Guard does not answer to the Military Commission, does not answer to State Security, and does not take orders from any office in the capital."

"I did," Elder Song said softly. He leaned back slightly, his thin frame somehow casting a larger shadow than the decorated general across from him. "And do you remember the first rule of that charter, Qin Zhengguo?"

"Total silence," Qin Zhengguo recited quietly. "The ledger watches, records, and waits without stepping in until the holder decides that a line has been crossed that the state itself can no longer see."

"Twenty years," Elder Song repeated, his cigarette burning down toward his wrinkled fingers, forgotten. "Those three bank presidents built their Zurich channel in 2007. He Guoliang took his first secret bond payout in 2011. Fang Qiming dug his first wrecking bunker six years ago. The Southern Theater leaks started three winters ago. Black Guard had every single bond code, every helicopter tape, and every Shanghai agent address locked inside its vault the whole time."

Qin Zhengguo's breath slowed. A cold knot formed in his stomach as the meaning hit him like a physical blow.

"Wait," Qin Zhengguo whispered, his eyes widening slightly. "If the holder of the Black Order had all of this for years, why did nothing move in 2011? Why did nothing move three years ago when the Southern Theater bases first started leaking?"

Elder Song did not answer right away. He looked once more at the ten motionless black sentries ringing the Western Hills yard, silent witnesses to a power that had sat still for twenty years and then shaken the whole capital in one morning.

Then Founding Elder Song turned his deep, ancient eyes back to Qin Zhengguo, and asked the question that turned the blood in the general's veins to ice.

"Qin Zhengguo, after twenty years of silence, why did the holder of the Black Order finally decide to open the gate today?"
