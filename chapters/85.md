# Chapter 85: Elder Song's Afternoon Tea

Long shadows stretched across the grey stone courtyard in the Western Hills outside Beijing. Crisp autumn wind moved through the old pines, carrying the dry scent of fallen needles, damp earth, and drifting golden ginkgo leaves. Along the outer courtyard wall stood ten motionless figures in plain dark wool coats, their posture relaxed yet watchful, keeping the perimeter completely silent without a single visible weapon or disturbance.

Inside the open wooden pavilion, ninety-year-old founding elder Elder Song poured dark Pu'er tea from a small clay pot into two white porcelain cups. His spotted hands moved slowly and steadily, never spilling a single drop onto the carved elm table.

Retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo sat across from him on a wooden stool, his wool jacket unbuttoned at the collar. He had spent twelve straight hours mediating between Southern Theater Counterintelligence and the Ministry of State Security, and the lines around his eyes showed the weight of a long autumn day.

"Drink while it is hot," Elder Song said gently, sliding one cup across the elm wood. "You look like you have been arguing with two stubborn division commanders since breakfast."

Elder Qin picked up the warm porcelain cup, breathed in the earthy steam, and took a slow sip. His shoulders loosened almost immediately. "Worse. Lu Zhen and Shen Kuo. Both are sharp men, and neither likes anything moving in the dark outside their ledgers. Lu Zhen wanted to put intercept teams on the street to track down the Black Guard."

"He is a good soldier," Elder Song replied with a faint smile, lifting his own cup. "If a counterintelligence general did not get angry about an unrecorded force moving through his theater, I would worry that he had grown soft. Did you calm him down?"

"They both stood down," Elder Qin said. "They have enough real work locking down the coastal ports and tracking the foreign couriers who tried to pull forged ledgers out of Jingzhou. Still, old friend, I could see the question sitting behind both of their eyes."

"You mean the market close," Elder Song said softly.

Elder Qin nodded and set his cup down. "Premier Gu sent over the closing report twenty minutes before I drove up the hill. The 8 trillion yuan liquidity wall held without a single crack."

A quiet sense of relief passed between the two elders. Across the courtyard, a gust of autumn wind shook a handful of yellow ginkgo leaves down onto the grey flagstones.

"Tell me the final figures," Elder Song asked, though his calm eyes showed he already knew the outcome.

"Before 8:00 AM, the 1.2 trillion yuan bank injection reached every regional branch linked to Shanshui Group," Elder Qin said. "When the doors opened, cash counters ran normally, payroll transfers cleared on time, and not one honest factory or depositor suffered a delay. By 10:00 AM, the 3.8 trillion yuan backstop steadied every municipal bond and provincial credit line across Handong and the coast."

He let out a short, satisfied chuckle. "And the best part happened at 9:30 AM. Three offshore funds tried to dump regional bonds and push short contracts the second the bell rang. Our desks met them head-on with the 3 trillion yuan market-suppression wall. Within ninety minutes, the short-sellers ran out of margin and had to buy back at steep losses. By afternoon close, the market was calmer than it was last Friday."

Elder Song refilled both cups with dark, steaming tea. "A clean financial defense. No panic in the streets, no broken supply chains, and foreign speculators learned an expensive lesson. Yet you and I both know that plugging a 5 trillion yuan hole only buys us time."

Elder Qin's expression turned serious again. "Money can stop a bank run, but it cannot cure a twenty-year rot. Zhao Dehan, Zhao Ruilong, and Gao Yuliang are sitting in interrogation rooms right now. That is a good start. If we stop there, though, and simply pat ourselves on the back because the markets stayed green, another Shanshui Group will grow back in five years."

"Exactly," Elder Song said, his voice quiet yet firm. "That is why institutional reform is the only proper answer. Regional bank lending needs real independent audits, not rubber stamps from local committees. County police and land approval offices need strict oversight so no local official can fabricate a case or seize honest property for a developer's profit."

"Premier Gu and Vice Premier Zhou see it the same way," Elder Qin observed. "They are already preparing the joint clean-up order for the full Council vote. Real arrests for the corrupt, public demotions for those who covered for them, and firm support for upright officials like Sha Ruijin, Li Dakang, and Hou Liangping."

Elder Song looked out toward the pine-covered ridge where the late autumn sun was sinking into pale gold clouds. "When a family's roof leaks in a storm, you are grateful if a quiet neighbor helps shore up the beam before the house collapses. After the rain stops, however, it is your own duty to replace the rotten timber and rebuild the frame properly."

Elder Qin smiled slowly, understanding the old elder's meaning without another word needed. The holder of the Black Order had stepped in cleanly to prevent a national financial disaster, and now left the hard work of governance back in the state's hands.

"When this week's meetings finish," Elder Qin said, picking up his teacup again, "I am taking two days off to visit Shanghai. My granddaughter has been working herself half to death at Haiyue Group, and I want to make sure she actually sits down for a hot dinner instead of living on black coffee and board reports."

Elder Song laughed softly, the sound warm and unhurried in the cool mountain air. "Go have dinner with her, old friend. We are fighting to keep the country steady so people can enjoy quiet evenings like that."

As twilight settled over the Western Hills, the two elders drank their tea in companionable silence, watching the golden leaves drift across the stone courtyard.
