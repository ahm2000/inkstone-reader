# Chapter 85: Zurich Wakes Up

Freezing snow drifted silently past the triple-glazed windows of the private bank penthouse overlooking Lake Zurich.

Below the glass spire, the dark Swiss water lay smooth as black ink under the winter streetlamps. Inside the private boardroom on the top floor, however, the temperature felt ten degrees colder than the alpine night outside.

Around an oval slab of polished black granite sat four senior directors of the Northern Ledger. Above the center of the table, three thin glass monitors glowed with cascading columns of red liquidation figures.

One hundred and twelve trillion yuan in layered offshore clearing trusts, frozen or dissolved. Thirty-two provincial and ministerial assets across the eastern mainland, detained or stripped of authority. The Lanshui clearing hub, dismantled in forty-eight hours. Wanfeng Freight, bankrupted and sealed.

And at the very bottom of the rightmost screen sat a single grayscale photograph of Li Weiming, known in European banking circles as Marcus Vance, stamped with a red verification tag.

*Status: Detained in sealed mainland custody. Diplomatic inquiry rejected.*

Elena Rostova slammed a thick leather dossier onto the black granite, the sharp crack echoing off the glass walls.

"Three days," Elena Rostova said, her voice trembling with cold fury. "Three days since the Lanshui forum, and our intelligence desks in Singapore, Hong Kong, and London still cannot give me a single name. Who hit us?"

Across the table, Laurent Moreau adjusted his silver-rimmed spectacles, his expression tight and pale.

"Our clearing relays in Lanshui did not fail from a market shift," Laurent Moreau replied quietly. "Every single provincial bank protector we cultivated for twenty years was arrested simultaneously. The order did not come from a local governor. Someone reached straight into the central regulatory apparatus and pulled the plug on our entire Asian spine."

At the head of the table, Chairman Henrik Lindqvist sat completely still, his steepled fingers resting beneath his chin. His pale gray eyes moved slowly over the red numbers reflecting off the granite.

"Read the Lanshui field summary again," Henrik Lindqvist ordered softly. "From the moment Li Weiming entered Qingyan City."

Standing beside the glowing glass screens, Asia-Pacific desk director Victor Sterling cleared his throat and tapped a tablet.

"Two weeks ago, Li Weiming traced an anomaly in Qingyan City after our local assets under Zhao Dehai and Zhao Mingyuan collapsed," Victor Sterling reported briskly. "He sent two reconnaissance specialists to observe an ordinary logistics clerk named Chen Xuan at Hengxin Logistics. Both specialists vanished within ten seconds on East Avenue without triggering a single distress beacon."

"Vanished," Elena Rostova scoffed bitterly. "Trained European security contractors do not vanish in broad daylight unless they walk into a state-level black unit."

"Li Weiming then arranged for Chen Xuan to be invited to the Lanshui Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum so our regional partners could inspect him," Victor Sterling continued, wiping a bead of sweat from his temple. "Instead, within forty-eight hours of that forum opening, Wanfeng Freight collapsed, our provincial backers were swept up by central investigators, and Li Weiming himself was taken off the board."

Henrik Lindqvist leaned back slightly in his leather chair, his mouth curving into a thin, humorless line.

"A freight clerk," Henrik Lindqvist said evenly. "A twenty-five-year-old man who rides a public bus, lives in a thirty-year-old residential block, and packs homemade soup in a thermos. Do you expect this board to believe an ordinary office worker erased one hundred trillion yuan in Northern Ledger assets?"

"No, Chairman," Victor Sterling answered immediately. "Our analysts believe Chen Xuan is either a low-level courier or a deliberate decoy placed in front of the real power in Qingyan City. The real anomaly is not Hengxin Logistics. It is the corporate group that stepped directly into every single space our network left behind."

Victor Sterling swiped his tablet. The red casualty screens vanished, replaced by a crisp architectural photograph of a forty-story glass tower in Qingyan City and a corporate balance sheet.

*Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group.*

Laurent Moreau leaned forward, his eyes narrowing as he scanned the ownership chart.

"Haiyue Group," Laurent Moreau murmured. "Controlled by Qin Zhengming and his daughter Qin Yuhan. Family ties to General Qin Zhengguo in the southern military region. When Yunxi Old Street reopened, Haiyue signed the clean logistics contracts with Wang Lei and Hengxin Logistics. When Wanfeng Freight went down in Lanshui, Qin Yuhan was sitting in the same hotel."

"More importantly," Victor Sterling added sharply, "Haiyue Group is publicly traded on the mainland exchange. Its debt structure is healthy, its cash flow is steady, and it relies heavily on three regional commercial banks for seasonal property credit lines. Whoever is hiding behind Qingyan City clearly cares about keeping Haiyue Group intact."

Silence settled over the penthouse for five long seconds. Outside, a gust of alpine wind brushed dry snow against the glass.

"If a wolf refuses to step out of the fog," Henrik Lindqvist said calmly, "you do not waste ammunition shooting at shadows. You set fire to the house it guards."

Elena Rostova's eyes gleamed coldly. "You want to strike Haiyue Group through the open market."

"Not a strike," Henrik Lindqvist corrected softly. "An execution. We will not send more fixers to disappear in dark alleys. We will use the one battlefield where military generals and hidden bureaucrats cannot fire a gun without breaking their own laws. Capital."

He turned his cold gaze toward Victor Sterling.

"Activate Aegis Meridian Capital," Henrik Lindqvist commanded. "Route the capital through our Zurich, Cayman, and Singapore prime brokerage desks. I want Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group bleeding on the exchange by Monday morning."

Victor Sterling straightened immediately, a cruel confidence returning to his face.

"We already prepared the framework, Chairman," Victor Sterling said swiftly. "Aegis Meridian holds forty-two offshore trading shells with pre-approved northbound market access. At Monday's opening bell, we deploy layered short-selling blocks alongside high-frequency naked short positions hidden behind dark-pool swaps. Within two hours, we can force Haiyue's share price down fifteen to twenty percent."

"And the banks?" Laurent Moreau asked quietly.

"At ten in the morning, our Hong Kong research affiliates will release a coordinated series of fabricated off-book debt memos linking Haiyue Group to the Lanshui freight collapse," Victor Sterling replied smoothly. "Before noon, at least three of Haiyue's partner banks will freeze their revolving credit lines to protect their own balance sheets. By Tuesday afternoon, Qin Zhengming will be facing a liquidity cliff."

"Send Shen Kaiyuan to Qingyan City on the morning flight," Henrik Lindqvist instructed, his voice devoid of warmth. "Have him walk into Haiyue Tower with a fifty-one percent hostile takeover contract. Give Qin Yuhan and Qin Zhengming forty-eight hours to surrender control or watch their life work be liquidated."

Elena Rostova smiled thinly, tapping her pen against the granite table.

"And if the hidden sovereign behind Lanshui really is protecting Haiyue Group?" Elena Rostova asked.

"Then he will have to move billions of yuan in broad daylight to save them," Henrik Lindqvist answered coldly. "Money always leaves a fingerprint. The moment he reaches into the market to catch Haiyue Group, our Zurich clearing analysts will trace every wire, identify his true name, and crush both him and the Qin family together."

Henrik Lindqvist stood slowly and walked toward the towering glass wall overlooking the snow-covered lake.

Across the room, Victor Sterling was already speaking rapidly into an encrypted satellite phone, waking up trading desks in Hong Kong, Singapore, and Shanghai.

"Tell Shen Kaiyuan not to show mercy," Henrik Lindqvist said softly to the winter night. "By Tuesday close, Qingyan City will remember who still owns the money in this world."
