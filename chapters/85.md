# Chapter 85: Elder Song's Afternoon Tea

Late Monday afternoon cast long shadows across the quiet stone courtyard in the Western Hills. Along the outer perimeter wall, ten motionless figures stood spaced at exact intervals in featureless matte-black gear, their visors opaque, rifles slung low and still as carved iron. They did not shift their weight, they did not glance toward the pavilion, and not even the cold mountain wind seemed to stir the hem of their coats.

Inside the open-sided timber pavilion, ninety-year-old Founding Elder Song poured dark, bitter Pu'er tea from a clay pot into two small porcelain cups. His spotted hands were slightly slow, yet steady enough not to spill a single drop.

General Qin Zhengguo sat opposite him on a plain wooden stool, his uniform cap resting beside a pack of unfiltered cigarettes. He had spent the past twelve hours shouting down intelligence ministers and signing arrest warrants, yet here in the presence of Elder Song, his shoulders carried a quiet, heavy gravity.

"Drink while it is hot, Qin Zhengguo," Elder Song said, his voice dry and raspy like old pine bark. "You look as if you have been chewing iron since sunrise."

Qin Zhengguo lifted the cup, took a slow swallow of the bitter brew, and set it down. "Worse than iron, Elder Song. Rust."

Elder Song picked up a cigarette from the pack on the table, tapped it gently against his thumbnail, and waited as Qin Zhengguo leaned forward immediately to light it for him, then lit one for himself. Both men exhaled thin plumes of smoke into the cool courtyard air.

"Tell me the real count," Elder Song said quietly, his cloudy eyes fixing on Qin Zhengguo's face. "Not the cleaned summary they are preparing for the committee."

Qin Zhengguo's jaw tightened. "Energy Vice Minister He Guoliang broke an hour after noon. He gave Gao Zhengyi three retired state-bank presidents and a Geneva clearing intermediary named Lukas Weber. In Lanshui, Shengyuan Capital Chairman Fang Qiming and Vice Governor Lin Zhengde tore each other's throats out in separate rooms. Six provinces, Elder Song. They buried rare-earth and military-alloy smuggling bunkers underneath county-level real-estate demolitions."

Elder Song did not blink. He took a small, unhurried drag from his cigarette. "And the army?"

A dark flush crept up Qin Zhengguo's neck, shame and fury mingling in his eyes. "Three senior colonels and eleven depot quartermasters in the Southern Theater. Selling alloy dispatch logs through a forty-seven-man foreign cutout ring in Shanghai. I just fought Ding Guoping for forty-eight hours of custody delay so State Security can trace their midnight burst transmissions back to Zurich."

Elder Song nodded slowly, his gaze drifting for a brief moment toward the nearest matte-black Zero Guard sentry standing silent by the courtyard gate.

"And the money?" Elder Song asked.

"100 trillion yuan in compounded twenty-year shadow-asset trails," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice dropping so low it barely carried across the stone table. "Gu Chengshan and Zhou Zhenhua just ordered a silent sovereign-trust absorption behind the clearinghouse layer. If they had moved publicly, twenty-two provincial financing vehicles would have collapsed before Friday."

For a long minute, neither man spoke. The only sound in the Western Hills courtyard was the faint hiss of burning tobacco and the distant rustle of dry leaves beyond the wall.

Qin Zhengguo stared down into his dark cup of Pu'er tea, his fingers gripping the porcelain rim tightly. "Elder Song, I have commanded corps on the frontier and sat on the Military Commission for a decade. I thought I knew where the cracks in the wall were. Today I realized we were five years away from waking up inside a hollow shell. The finance committees, the energy grid, six provincial governments, even my own logistics depots. If that Zurich archive had not dropped onto our desks this morning, none of our internal audits would have caught the full web in time."

Elder Song let out a slow, wheezing breath that sounded almost like a bitter sigh. "When we fought in the mountains seventy years ago, we could see the enemy across the river. You aimed your rifle, you held the ridge, and you knew who stood behind your back. Peace is harder, Qin Zhengguo. Twenty years of fast money turned smart men into termites. They did not knock the house down. They hollowed out every load-bearing beam while painting the front door gold."

Qin Zhengguo crushed his cigarette out in the stone saucer and looked up sharply. "Then we should be grateful the vault opened when it did. Whoever controls Zero Guard handed the state a scalpel sharp enough to cut out the entire tumor in forty-eight hours."

Elder Song stopped with his teacup halfway to his lips. Slowly, he lowered the porcelain back onto the table without drinking. His cloudy eyes cleared suddenly, turning so sharp and piercing that Qin Zhengguo involuntarily straightened his spine.

"Grateful?" Elder Song murmured. "Yes. A man saved from drowning is always grateful for the hand that pulls him out of the river."

Qin Zhengguo frowned, sensing an unspoken chill beneath the old man's tone. "Elder Song, you were one of the only three living men who knew the original charter of the Zero archive. You told us yourself this morning that Zero Guard does not answer to the Military Commission, does not answer to State Security, and does not take orders from any ministry in the capital."

"I did," Elder Song said softly. He leaned back slightly, his frail frame somehow casting a larger shadow than the decorated general across from him. "And do you remember the first clause of that charter, Qin Zhengguo?"

"Absolute non-interference," Qin Zhengguo recited quietly. "The ledger watches, records, and waits in silence until the holder judges that a line has been crossed that the state itself can no longer see."

"Twenty years," Elder Song repeated, his cigarette burning down toward his wrinkled fingers, forgotten. "Those three bank presidents built their Zurich channel in 2007. He Guoliang took his first bearer tranche in 2011. Fang Qiming sank his first demolition bunker six years ago. The Southern Theater leaks began three winters ago. Zero Guard had every single serial number, every heliport recording, and every Shanghai cutout address locked inside its vault the whole time."

Qin Zhengguo's breath slowed. A cold knot formed in the pit of his stomach as the implication hit him like a physical blow.

"Wait," Qin Zhengguo whispered, his eyes widening slightly. "If the holder of Zero had all of this for years, why did nothing move in 2011? Why did nothing move three years ago when the Southern Theater depots first started leaking?"

Elder Song did not answer right away. He glanced once more at the ten motionless black sentries ringing the Western Hills courtyard, silent witnesses to a power that had sat dormant for two decades and then shaken the entire capital in a single morning.

Then Founding Elder Song turned his deep, ancient eyes back to Qin Zhengguo, and asked the question that turned the blood in the general's veins to ice.

"Zhengguo, after twenty years of silence, why did the holder of Zero finally decide to open the gate today?"
