# Chapter 85: Elder Song's Afternoon Tea

Long shadows stretched across the grey stone courtyard in the Western Hills outside Beijing. Crisp autumn wind moved through the old pines, carrying the dry scent of fallen needles, damp earth, and drifting golden ginkgo leaves. Along the outer courtyard wall stood ten motionless figures in plain dark wool coats, their posture relaxed yet watchful, keeping the perimeter completely silent without a single visible weapon or disturbance.

Inside the open wooden pavilion, ninety-year-old founding elder Elder Song poured dark Pu'er tea from a small clay pot into two white porcelain cups. His spotted hands moved slowly and steadily, never spilling a single drop onto the carved elm table.

Retired Four-Star General Elder Qin Zhengguo sat across from him on a wooden stool, his wool jacket unbuttoned at the collar. He had spent twelve straight hours mediating between military commanders, security chiefs, and banking regulators, and the quiet mountain air felt like a rare gift.

"Drink while it is hot, Zhengguo," Elder Song said gently, pushing one cup across the table. "You have been shouting down agency heads since sunrise. Your throat needs warm tea more than another briefing."

Elder Qin took a slow sip, letting the earthy warmth settle in his chest, then looked out toward the quiet sentinels along the grey stone wall. "Lu Zhen and Shen Kuo stood down without further argument once they saw the full forensic timeline. Still, watching two of our sharpest commanders realize how close our own relays came to failing was sobering."

"It should be sobering," Elder Song replied, cradling his teacup between weathered palms. "Twelve hours ago, a provincial corruption network in Handong nearly served as a doorway for foreign financial syndicates to drain 1.2 trillion yuan out of our clearing system. Had the holder of the Black Order not intervened with both the Black Guard's signal cutoff and a 300 trillion yuan sovereign reserve, our markets would have bled for months."

Elder Qin set his cup down carefully. "300 trillion yuan. Even after seeing the Central Bank's verification stamps with my own eyes, the scale still defies easy belief. And yet the person behind it asked for no title, no seat on the Top Council, and no public recognition. We do not even know his name or which city he calls home."

"Nor should we try to dig for it," Elder Song said calmly. "Think about why he acted the way he did. He did not interfere in routine policy debates, trade negotiations, or local elections. He stepped in only when systemic rot and foreign predation converged to threaten the country's financial foundation. Once the firebreak held, he stepped right back into the shadows."

Elder Qin leaned forward slightly, his expression thoughtful. "And his Black Guard will withdraw on schedule once the sweep wraps up. That leaves me with a question that matters far more than who he is, Old Song. What happens after they step back?"

Elder Song smiled faintly, recognizing the sharp strategic instinct that had made Qin Zhengguo a four-star general. "That is the only question worth asking over tea today. A strong nation cannot lean on an unseen guardian to rescue its ministries every time local officials grow corrupt. If we treat this 300 trillion yuan shield as a safety net for laziness, the next crisis will be worse than the last."

"Handong proved how quickly rot spreads when supervision sleeps," Elder Qin agreed quietly. "Shanshui Group bought county police stations, land bureaus, and deputy secretaries one favor at a time. By the time the bill came due, they had built a private empire right under the provincial capital's nose."

"Which is why real reform is the only way we earn the quiet trust of whoever stands behind that reserve," Elder Song said, his voice steady and firm. "Premier Gu has already pushed through the nationwide provincial audit mandate. Every municipal financing platform, every major land tender, and every cross-border clearing channel is going under permanent electronic oversight. And just as important, we must protect and promote officials who actually keep their hands clean."

"Sha Ruijin, Li Dakang, Hou Liangping, Ji Changming," Elder Qin listed with a nod. "Even down to grassroots county officers like that young deputy in Yunxi who refused to sign a fabricated detention slip. People like that are the real backbone of the system. Give them clear backing from Beijing, and the local syndicates cannot grow back."

"Exactly," Elder Song said. He refilled both cups with a thin stream of amber tea. "Power without accountability always invites predators, whether they wear foreign suits or local titles. If our courts stay honest, our banks stay transparent, and our officials answer to the law, then the holder of the Black Order will never need to intervene again. He can live whatever quiet life he has chosen in peace."

Elder Qin's stern features softened for the first time all afternoon. His thoughts drifted briefly south to Shanghai, where his granddaughter Qin Yuhan worked long hours steering Haiyue Group through tough commercial waters. She never asked for political favors, and she believed deeply in building an enterprise on fair competition and hard work.

"You know, Old Song," Elder Qin said softly, looking out at the autumn pines, "my granddaughter in Shanghai works twelve-hour days trying to build honest businesses and keep thousands of employees fed. She has no idea how close the financial floor came to shaking beneath her feet last night, and I am glad she does not. All the younger generation asks for is a fair country where honest work is enough to live peacefully."

"Then it is our duty to leave them that country," Elder Song said quietly, lifting his porcelain cup toward the western ridge as the autumn sun began to dip in bands of gold and amber.

Elder Qin raised his cup to meet his old comrade's. Neither man spoke another word as the mountain wind swept golden leaves across the grey stone courtyard, carrying the cool promise of a cleaner season ahead.
