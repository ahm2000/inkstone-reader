# Chapter 124: The Unmarked Cream Envelope

By seven-thirty that evening, the narrow lane leading to Chen Xuan's rented apartment building was dim and quiet.

A light sea breeze rattled the plane trees along the curb.

Just before the courtyard gate, a shadow stepped cleanly out from beside the bicycle shed.

Number 7 stood under the pale streetlamp in a plain light-gray jacket and dark trousers, looking no different from any tired night-shift foreman heading home from the docks. In his gloved right hand, he held a stiff, unmarked cream envelope sealed with a single dab of plain red wax.

"This came down from the capital two hours ago," Number 7 said quietly, offering the envelope with both hands. "A personal courier from the Western Hills delivered it straight to Number 1's courtyard. Nobody opened it."

Chen Xuan paused, shifted the warm paper bag to his forearm, and took the cream envelope. The paper was thick, smooth, and faintly scented with sandalwood ink. There was no address on the front and no sender's name on the back.

"Elder Song?" Chen Xuan asked calmly.

"Yes," Number 7 replied. "Number 1 checked the courier's seal and put the envelope on the afternoon flight straight to Shanghai. Number 1 is waiting by the line whenever you finish reading it."

"Thank you for walking it over," Chen Xuan said lightly. He slipped the envelope into his jacket pocket and glanced once at Number 7. "Anything new from Sha City?"

"Li Weiming met with Song Baisheng at Wanfeng Freight yesterday afternoon," Number 7 reported evenly. "Song Baisheng agreed to plug Zurich's three remaining backup servers into a provincial freight alliance contract during next month's Sha Provincial Forum. In return, Li Weiming promised him 15 million yuan in overseas consulting fees, with 6 million paid on signing day."

Chen Xuan nodded slightly, not a trace of surprise on his face. "Good. Do not touch the servers yet, and do not rattle Song Baisheng before the forum opens. Let him print his contract and invite all twenty freight companies onto the stage."

"Understood," Number 7 answered cleanly. "Our people in Sha City will stay back and keep eyes on the bonded storage room until you arrive."

"Go eat dinner," Chen Xuan said with a slight nod. "It is getting cool out here."

Number 7 inclined his head once, stepped back into the shadow of the bicycle shed, and vanished into the evening without a sound.

Chen Xuan climbed the concrete stairs to the fourth floor, unlocked his door, and switched on the warm ceiling light. His rented room was clean and simple: a wooden table, two folding chairs, a small electric kettle, and a narrow bed neatly made with a gray cotton blanket.

He set the red-bean buns on the table, plugged in the kettle, and rinsed his green thermos in the sink. Only after the kettle clicked off and he had poured fresh hot water over a pinch of jasmine leaves did he sit down and draw the unmarked cream envelope from his pocket.

Using a small steel fruit knife from the dish rack, Chen Xuan sliced neatly beneath the red wax seal.

Inside lay a single folded sheet of heavy rice paper.

He unfolded it flat beneath the overhead bulb. There were no polite greetings, no government titles, and no long paragraphs about the 40-department clean-up or the coastal sweep. Written across the center of the page, in steady brush-pen ink, was a single line.

*When the northern dust settles, come up to the Western Hills for one cup of tea.*

There was no signature at the bottom, only a small red stamp in the corner.

Chen Xuan looked at that single line of ink for several calm seconds. He could almost picture Elder Song sitting beside the stone pond in that quiet Western Hills courtyard, watching the autumn leaves drift across the water while half the capital rushed around sorting through detention orders.

A faint, genuine smile touched Chen Xuan's mouth.

He folded the rice paper neatly along its creases, slid it back inside the unmarked cream envelope, and tucked it safely beneath his small porcelain saucer.

Then he pulled his plain black second phone from his coat pocket and dialed the single saved speed-dial number.

The line connected before the first ring finished.

"I am here," Number 1's deep, steady voice came through the speaker.

"I read the note," Chen Xuan said easily, leaning back in his chair while steam curled up from his open green thermos.

There was a brief, respectful pause on the other end. "Elder Song's personal secretary waited at our front gate for half an hour before leaving," Number 1 said quietly. "He did not ask for a formal written reply. He only asked whether you received it in good health, and whether we should pass any word back up the hill."

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea and looked out his small window toward the distant harbor lights of Shanghai.

"Tell Elder Song I will bring my own thermos when I have a free afternoon," Chen Xuan said calmly.

Across the phone line, even the stone-faced Number 1 paused for a full heartbeat, quiet amusement warming his low voice. "Word for word?"

"Word for word," Chen Xuan replied. "He will understand. The tea in the Western Hills is good, but I prefer my own cup."

"I will send the message up tonight," Number 1 said. Then his voice returned to crisp business. "Also, Number 7 passed along your instruction about Sha City. The tech team already mapped the bonded storage room outside Sha City where Li Weiming hid those three backup servers. We can lock down the power and seize the drives tonight if you want."

"No rush," Chen Xuan said lightly. "If we grab the hardware tonight, Song Baisheng will claim he knew nothing about it, and half the crooked clerks on that forum committee will scatter back into the woodwork. Let them put their names on the stage banner first. General Manager Sun Haoran already booked my train ticket for next week."

"You are riding the second-class express north with Hengxin Logistics?" Number 1 asked evenly.

"Free ticket, meal allowance, and two paid days off afterward," Chen Xuan said with a quiet chuckle. "Why waste a good business trip? Keep everyone in Sha City quiet and out of sight until the forum starts."

"Understood," Number 1 replied cleanly. "Everything will be ready when your train pulls in."

"Good night, Number 1."

"Good night."

Chen Xuan ended the call, set the plain white phone face-down beside his elbow, and reached into the warm paper bag. He pulled out a soft red-bean bun, broke it in half, and ate it slowly with his hot jasmine tea.

Outside in the dark lane fifty meters below his window, the night was completely still. The capital was waiting, Sha City was setting its stage, and in his quiet little room, Chen Xuan finished his bun, washed his saucer, and went to bed early.
