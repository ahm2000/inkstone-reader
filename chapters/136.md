# Chapter 136: Zurich Strikes Back

Several hours later in Zurich (+1:00 GMT), the Sunday night was anything but peaceful. Cold rain tapped steadily against the tall windows of a wood-paneled boardroom overlooking the dark lake.

Inside, the air hung thick with cigar smoke and quiet fury.

Henrik Lindholm, senior partner of Aegis Meridian, stood at the head of the long oak table with both palms pressed flat on the polished wood. A printed news report from Handong Province lay directly in front of him. The front page showed a grainy photograph of Li Weiming being led out of a hotel lobby in handcuffs by Chinese police.

"Two years of careful groundwork," Henrik Lindholm said, his voice tight and cold. "More than 50 million yuan in consulting fees, offshore favors, and local arrangements. Gone in forty minutes."

Across the table, Thomas Bauer tapped ash from his cigar into a heavy crystal tray. His eyes stayed fixed on the second photograph clipped to the report. It showed the Jingzhou City Convention Center, with Qin Yuhan standing calm and straight behind the front microphone as provincial officials applauded.

"We underestimated the woman," Thomas Bauer said quietly. "And we underestimated how fast Handong's leadership would move once those land records surfaced. With Li Weiming in custody and Ding Yizhen finished, our whole channel into the Jingzhou port corridor is dead."

"Not dead," Lindholm snapped, straightening his tailored wool jacket. "Blocked at the front door. That only means we take the building from underneath."

He turned toward the far end of the table, where a lean man in his late thirties sat swirling a glass of dark scotch. Julian Ash was the chief trading partner of Aegis Meridian, heading one of the most aggressive short-selling desks in Europe. Unlike the older bankers in the room, Julian looked entirely unbothered by the rain or the bad news from China.

"Haiyue Group just signed the primary logistics framework for the new Handong port," Lindholm said, sliding a thick financial binder down the table. "If Qin Yuhan finishes that expansion, Haiyue will control the cleanest shipping route between Jingzhou and Shanghai. Aegis Meridian will lose its grip on three major coastal freight contracts. I want Haiyue broken before the ink dries."

Julian Ash opened the binder, flipped through two pages of balance sheets, and smiled thinly.

"You do not need local officials to break a public company, Henrik," Julian said smoothly. "You only need fear and leverage. Look at Haiyue's numbers. Their shipping assets are solid, yes, and their cash flow is healthy. That makes people careless. To fund the Handong expansion, they carry 6 billion yuan in short-term credit lines across four regional banks in Shanghai and Jingzhou."

Thomas Bauer leaned forward, frowning slightly. "Those four banks are cautious. They will not pull credit lines unless Haiyue's stock price collapses or a regulatory scandal hits."

"Then we give them both on the same morning," Julian replied, setting his glass down on the oak table.

He tapped the binder with one long finger. "Over the past ten days, Aegis Meridian has quietly borrowed 8 billion yuan worth of Haiyue Group shares through nine offshore accounts in Hong Kong and Singapore. We also secured an extra 4 billion yuan in standby short capacity for Tuesday."

Lindholm's eyes narrowed with sharp interest. "Walk me through the sequence."

"Simple and surgical," Julian said. "Before the Shanghai market opens tomorrow morning, our media contractors will release forged customs inspection memos across offshore financial forums and local retail chat groups. The story will claim that customs officers raided Haiyue's third dock in Pudong over undeclared cargo and frozen manifests."

"Customs will deny it eventually," Bauer pointed out.

"Eventually does not save a stock on Monday morning," Julian answered with a cold chuckle. "Government agencies move slowly. They check files, hold meetings, and draft careful statements. While they take twenty-four hours to verify the paperwork, retail investors will panic, and our desks will slam 8 billion yuan in borrowed shares straight onto the market."

Lindholm nodded slowly, the anger in his face sharpening into cold calculation. "You will pin Haiyue's stock to the 10% daily drop limit before noon."

"Before lunch," Julian confirmed. "Once the share price hits the floor and stays locked there, those four cautious banks will start making frantic phone calls to Haiyue Tower. They will threaten to call in their 6 billion yuan in loans if the stock does not recover. Haiyue's own board members will turn on Qin Yuhan in sheer terror."

"And Tuesday morning?" Lindholm asked.

"Even if customs issues a rushed clearance notice early Tuesday, nobody in Shanghai catches a falling knife," Julian said confidently. "Right on the opening bell, we drop the remaining 4 billion yuan in short orders. The stock will gap down another 10%, the banks will freeze Haiyue's accounts, and Qin Yuhan will have no choice except to sell off her prime docks at half price just to stay alive. Aegis Meridian walks in on Wednesday and buys the harbor for pennies."

Silence settled over the warm, wood-paneled room for a few seconds. Outside, wind rattled the wet glass, and the distant ferry lights across Lake Zurich blurred into pale streaks.

Henrik Lindholm picked up his own glass and raised it slightly toward Julian Ash.

"Make sure the drop is brutal, Julian," Lindholm said coldly. "I want Qin Yuhan to understand that winning a speech in Jingzhou means nothing when Zurich decides to erase your balance sheet."

"Consider it done," Julian replied, rising from his chair and buttoning his dark jacket. "Shanghai opens in a few hours. My traders are already at their desks, and the rumor packages are queued to go live before dawn."

He walked out of the boardroom toward the glass stairwell leading down to Aegis Meridian's trading floor, where dozens of monitors already glowed pale blue in the rainy Swiss night.

Six thousand miles east in Shanghai (+8:00 GMT), pre-dawn mist drifted peacefully over the Huangpu River, and the lights of Emerald Riverside Villa remained dark before sunrise.
