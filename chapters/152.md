# Chapter 152: Grandma's Eighty-First Birthday

The morning bus from Qingyan rattled down the country road and stopped beside the stone bridge at the edge of Hongshi Village.

Chen Xuan stepped down first, holding his woven shopping bag carefully so the walnut pastries inside would not get crushed. Behind him came his father, Chen Jianguo, carrying two cloth bags of fruit, and his mother, Liu Meifang, with a thermal box of braised pork.

A minute later, a dusty silver minivan pulled up beside them. Uncle Chen Mingde hopped out grinning, followed by Aunt Wu Lanying, cousin Chen Yuan, and cousin Chen Hao.

"Beat you by three seconds, Uncle Jianguo," Chen Hao joked, taking one of the fruit bags. "That means I get the biggest bowl of noodles."

"You always take the biggest bowl anyway," Chen Yuan laughed, smiling at Chen Xuan. "Hey, Xuan. You look rested. Still busy at Hengxin?"

"Just normal desk work," Chen Xuan replied with an easy smile.

Together, the family walked up the dirt lane toward the brick courtyard at the top of the slope, where warm wood smoke curled from the kitchen chimney. Fifty meters back near the stone bridge, a Black Guard member in a plain dark jacket leaned quietly beside an old bicycle, completely unnoticed by passing villagers.

At the open wooden gate, Grandma Zhou Guilan stood waiting in her clean padded jacket, her silver hair pinned neatly back. The moment she saw Chen Xuan, her eyes crinkled into a bright smile.

"Xuan, you made it," Zhou Guilan said, patting his cheek gently. "You still look a little thin. Are you eating proper lunches at work?"

"I eat plenty, Grandma," Chen Xuan answered softly, taking her cool hands. "Happy eighty-first birthday. I brought walnut pastries from Old Street and a wool shawl for the mountain wind."

He draped the dark red wool shawl across her shoulders. Zhou Guilan touched the soft wool and beamed.

"Look at this color," she said proudly to Liu Meifang and Wu Lanying. "My grandson still remembers my shoulders get cold."

"Come sit in the sun, Mom," Liu Meifang laughed, taking the bags. "Lanying and I will get the broth going."

Soon the courtyard filled with warmth and chatter. Chen Jianguo and Chen Mingde set out two round wooden tables and low bamboo stools. Old neighbors stopped by with eggs, peanuts, and birthday wishes. Chen Xuan sat beside Grandma Zhou Guilan, calmly peeling sweet tangerines for her.

Then a sharp, impatient car horn blasted right outside the wooden gate.

Everyone looked up. A shiny black sedan had squeezed halfway up the narrow lane, blocking the path. The driver's door swung open, and a stout man in a tight wool coat stepped out, adjusting a thick gold watch on his wrist.

Liu Meifang blinked from the kitchen doorway and sighed softly. "Oh, dear. That is my distant cousin, Liu Guoliang."

Liu Guoliang strode through the gate with his chin high, holding a glossy red gift box in one hand and his car keys in the other.

"Aunt Zhou! Happy eighty-first birthday!" Liu Guoliang boomed loud enough to carry down half the lane. "I just flew back from the south last night. Crazy busy down there, dealing with port orders every hour, yet I still drove two hours straight here to pay my respects."

Zhou Guilan stood up politely. "Guoliang, you came all this way. Sit down and have hot tea."

"Just a quick cup, Aunt," Liu Guoliang said, setting his shiny red box right in the center of the main table so every neighbor could see the foreign lettering. "Imported sea cucumber and tonic wine. Nearly three thousand yuan. At your age, you need real nutrition, not cheap street snacks."

His eyes flicked down to the two plain red boxes of walnut pastries near Grandma's elbow, and a faint sneer touched his mouth.

Chen Jianguo frowned slightly, setting the teapot down with a dull clink. "Sit down, Guoliang. Anything a child brings with a good heart is precious to Mom."

"Oh, Brother Jianguo, I am just speaking honestly," Liu Guoliang laughed, dropping onto a wooden chair and crossing his legs to keep dust off his shiny shoes. "People stuck in small towns do not see how fast the world moves. Down in Nanhai City, where I do business, nobody blinks at three thousand yuan. Last month alone, I helped move a coastal trade order worth 2 million yuan."

Several neighbors murmured politely. Liu Guoliang puffed out his chest even further.

"In fact, I work closely with the Shen Family down at Nanhai Port," Liu Guoliang went on, tapping the table with two fingers. "You folks probably have not heard of them. They control half the southern docks. Just last week, I had dinner with one of their port managers. One phone call from them, and cargo worth 10 million yuan clears the dock before noon."

Chen Mingde poured himself tea, his eyebrows knitting together. "That sounds like big business, Guoliang."

"It is all about connections," Liu Guoliang said smoothly. Then his gaze slid to Chen Xuan, who was still calmly peeling a tangerine on his low stool. "Speaking of which, Jianguo, I heard your boy Xuan is still at that little shipping office in Qingyan. Hengxin something?"

"Hengxin Logistics," Chen Jianguo answered evenly. "He works hard and likes it there."

"A desk clerk," Liu Guoliang chuckled with fake sympathy. "What do they pay a clerk there? Ten thousand? Twelve thousand a month?"

"Twelve thousand," Chen Hao muttered darkly, his jaw tight. "And he earns every cent cleanly."

Liu Guoliang laughed out loud and slapped his knee. "Twelve thousand yuan! Xuan, you are a grown man. A tiny 12,000-yuan clerk job barely pays for one good dinner down south! You sit behind a wooden desk shuffling paper for years, and all you bring your grandmother for her eighty-first birthday is a woven bag of bakery cakes."

The smile vanished from Chen Jianguo's face. His knuckles turned white around his teacup. Beside him, Chen Mingde set his cup down hard.

"Guoliang," Chen Mingde said in a low voice. "Today is Aunt Zhou's birthday. Watch how you talk."

"I am saying this for Jianguo's own good!" Liu Guoliang retorted, spreading his arms toward the watching neighbors. "Everybody knows how hard Jianguo worked his whole life. Yet his only son settles for a dead-end clerk desk and brings two cheap paper boxes to a birthday table. If Xuan wants to stop embarrassing his parents, he can come sweep floors at my warehouse down south. Maybe after a year, I can introduce him to a real shipping clan."

"You!" Chen Hao sprang half off his stool, his face red.

"Hao, sit down," Chen Xuan said calmly.

His quiet voice cut cleanly across the tense yard. Chen Xuan handed the peeled tangerine to Grandma Zhou Guilan, wiped his fingers, and met Liu Guoliang's gaze with completely steady eyes.

"Thank you for the offer, Uncle Guoliang," Chen Xuan said politely. "I like my job at Hengxin. It is peaceful, my parents do not worry about me, and Grandma likes the walnut pastries from Old Street."

"See that?" Liu Guoliang scoffed, pointing at Chen Xuan while turning to the neighbors. "No fire, no drive, not a shred of pride! Jianguo, Mingde, I hate to say it in front of everyone, but with a son like this, your whole branch of the family will stay stuck at the bottom forever!"

Chen Jianguo stood up, his chest heaving with quiet anger, ready to order Liu Guoliang out.

Before a word left his mouth, light, unhurried footsteps paused outside the open wooden gate.
