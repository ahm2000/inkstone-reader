# Chapter 152: Autumn Harvest in Hongshi Village

The next morning, thin wisps of wood smoke curled above the gray tile roofs of Hongshi Village. Behind the courtyard walls, terraced slopes glowed with patches of dry red vines and tall persimmon trees heavy with bright orange fruit.

A clean silver sedan rolled smoothly down the narrow stone lane, tires crunching over scattered leaves before stopping beneath the old locust tree outside Grandma Zhou Guilan's brick courtyard.

Chen Xuan pulled the handbrake and glanced over at the passenger seat with an amused smile. "Are you completely sure about this, President Qin? Once we step into that hillside field, there is no turning back. Those sweet potatoes do not care how many boardrooms you run."

Qin Yuhan unbuckled her seatbelt and gave him a playful glare. Today she had left her tailored office suits behind in Shanghai. She wore soft blue jeans, a warm cream knit sweater, canvas sneakers, and a simple hairpin holding her dark hair up in a neat ponytail.

"Stop teasing me," she said, checking the canvas gloves tucked into her jacket pocket. "I grew up visiting my grandfather in the countryside too, you know. Besides, you are the one who groans whenever you have to carry a box of printer paper on the twelfth floor. Let us see who gets tired first."

"Fair enough," Chen Xuan laughed, stepping out into the brisk morning air. "Loser washes the dishes tonight."

Before he could even open the trunk, the wooden courtyard gate creaked wide open. Grandma Zhou Guilan hurried out wearing a thick padded vest, her silver hair neatly combed and her face lighting up the instant she saw Qin Yuhan.

"Yuhan! You really came all this way!" Grandma said warmly, brushing right past her own grandson to take Qin Yuhan's hands in hers. "Look how thin your jacket is. The mountain wind gets sharp in late November. Xuan, why did you let your wife wake up so early without an extra scarf?"

"Grandma, I am standing right here," Chen Xuan chuckled, lifting a bag of fresh pastries and warm woolen socks out of the back seat. "I packed her thick coat right behind the seat, and she insisted on wearing the light sweater."

"I am not cold at all, Grandma," Qin Yuhan said gently, squeezing the old woman's rough, warm hands. "The air here smells wonderful. We came to help with the harvest so you and Second Uncle do not wear yourselves out."

Just then, Second Uncle Chen Mingde walked out from the side shed carrying two iron hoes and a stack of woven bamboo baskets. Behind him came cousins Chen Hao and Chen Yuan, both wearing padded vests and bright grins.

"Brother Xuan! Sister in law!" Chen Yuan called out, bounding down the stone steps. "You made it just in time! The persimmons on the upper ridge turned completely sweet after the frost earlier this week."

"Morning, Uncle. Morning, kids," Chen Xuan greeted them easily, taking one of the hoes from Second Uncle's hand. "Lead the way. My wife just challenged me to a digging contest."

Within ten minutes, the whole family was out on the sunlit hillside behind the village. The dry earth smelled clean and rich beneath the pale blue sky. Chen Xuan rolled up his sleeves and swung the hoe with a relaxed, steady rhythm. With two light turns of the blade, a cluster of plump, red skinned sweet potatoes tumbled free from the loose soil.

Qin Yuhan crouched beside the furrow, tapping the dry dirt off each sweet potato and lining them neatly inside a woven basket. She worked with the same quiet focus she brought to Haiyue Group, though a faint smudge of dust soon appeared near her cheek.

Chen Xuan paused, leaned on the wooden handle, and reached over to brush the dirt gently off her face with his thumb. "You missed a spot, President Qin."

Qin Yuhan blinked, then caught his wrist with a laugh. "Keep your hands on the hoe, Mr. Chen. Chen Hao is already half a row ahead of you."

Sure enough, seventeen year old Chen Hao was digging furiously a few yards down the slope, trying to show off. He yanked too hard on a thick root, lost his footing on the dry vine, and sat straight down in the soft dirt with a startled yelp. Chen Yuan burst out laughing so hard she nearly dropped her basket, and even Second Uncle shook his head with a fond grin.

After an hour of digging, six large baskets sat brimming along the edge of the terrace. They moved up to the persimmon grove next, where gnarled branches stood almost bare of leaves, making the bright orange fruit look like dozens of tiny paper lanterns hanging against the sky.

Chen Xuan picked up a long bamboo pole with a split fork at the tip. He slid the fork around a high stem, gave it a deft twist, and lowered the plump persimmon smoothly down into the cloth lined basket Qin Yuhan held beneath.

As they worked, a cheerful middle aged neighbor drove a dusty three wheeled electric cart along the dirt track below the grove. He wore a faded blue work jacket and a knit cap, looking like any other farmer heading home from the ridge.

"Morning, Xuan!" the uncle called out with a friendly wave, slowing the cart to toss two clean woven sacks over the low stone wall. "Saw your family filling up fast. Use these if your baskets run short. Tell Grandma not to forget the dried radishes she promised my wife!"

"Thanks, Uncle Li!" Chen Xuan called back naturally, catching the sacks with one hand. "Come over for tea after lunch!"

"Will do! Take care of your nice wife!" the uncle laughed, rattling happily down the lane without a second glance.

Qin Yuhan watched the cart disappear around the bend and smiled. "Everyone in your village is so warm and neighborly."

"That is the best part of coming home," Chen Xuan said softly, twisting down another glowing orange persimmon and dropping it neatly into her basket. "No meetings, no deadlines, just good soil and honest company."
