# Chapter 9: Ask Again

Chen Jianguo snatched the stamped sheet from the slot before it finished sliding out. His rough hands shook with anger. "Look at this, Xuan! Look at the nonsense they wrote!"

Chen Xuan gently took the notice and read it from top to bottom, running his thumb slowly beneath each line.

The form stated that Chen Mingde was ordered to serve ten days in jail for assault and hurting another person. Down at the bottom, right beside the red station stamp, sat the handwritten line: Time of Decision, Saturday, 7:50 a.m.

Chen Xuan paused and read that line a second time.

"Dad," he said quietly. "They handcuffed Second Uncle at two o'clock yesterday afternoon."

"I know! So what?"

"So the formal decision to detain him wasn't signed until 7:50 this morning. That's nearly eighteen hours later. For eighteen hours, they locked him in a cell without a single legal detention order on file."

Chen Jianguo's mouth opened, then snapped shut in shock!

"Think about it, Dad," Chen Xuan murmured, lowering his voice just enough so only the two of them heard. "If everyone in that office wanted to hide a dirty arrest, they would've backdated the line to Friday afternoon. Nobody checks twice. Whoever filled out this notice wrote the real time on purpose. That person didn't want to sign a lie."

Chen Xuan's eyes dropped to the final line listing the supporting evidence: Medical Injury Note, Ankang Private Clinic.

"And look here, Dad. There's a second problem. In a real assault case, an official police doctor has to examine the injury and write a formal report. They didn't use one. They used a slip from a private street clinic."

"Why does that matter?" Chen Jianguo asked, blinking fast.

"Because a private clinic will write whatever someone pays it to write."

Behind the glass barrier, Wei Kun froze with the last piece of his sesame bun halfway to his mouth!

His heart slammed against his ribs! How on earth had an ordinary company clerk spotted both fatal flaws in less than sixty seconds?! Looking at that mild young man standing outside the glass, Wei Kun felt an uncanny chill, as if a stranger had reached straight through the partition, laid a quiet hand on his shoulder, and whispered, *I know what you did.*

"Xuan, how do you know all this?" Chen Jianguo stared at his son in stunned disbelief.

"It's just paperwork, Dad."

To be honest, it really was an everyday skill. In three years auditing shipping bills at Hengxin Logistics, Chen Xuan had inspected more forms than he could count. He knew what an honest paper trail looked like, and he knew what a cooked one looked like. Real records had tiny, messy human quirks; forged ones were always too tidy, and their timestamps always slanted in the same direction.

Chen Jianguo's anger flared all over again! He puffed out his chest and took a sharp breath.

"They forged an arrest on my brother! I'm going to yell right here in this hall until every person in Yunxi hears!"

"Dad." Chen Xuan laid a gentle, steady hand on his father's sleeve. "Shouting won't fix a forged form. It'll only give them an excuse to call security."

"Then what will?!"

"Let's step outside first. I need to think."

They walked out into the crisp, bright Saturday morning. Chen Jianguo had been ready to shout for an hour, yet somehow, stepping into the cool sunlight beside his calm son, the helpless rage drained right out of his chest.

Inside Old Street Police Station, holding cell. 10:20 a.m.

"Old Chen."

Chen Mingde blinked awake. With the bare bulb burning nonstop overhead, he'd lost track of whether it was morning or night.

Cheng Biao stood outside the iron bars with both hands tucked into his leather jacket pockets. The friendly uncle smile from yesterday was completely gone.

"Your brother and your nephew stopped by the front lobby this morning," Cheng Biao said softly. "A polite little office clerk asking for written notices. Very cute. Now let me spell reality out for you, Old Chen. They can sit on those lobby benches for a solid week, and they still won't open this iron door. Only you can open it. Say yes to the buyout, sign the line, and walk home."

"My nephew came?" Chen Mingde asked quietly.

"That's what I just told you."

A slow, steady warmth spread across Chen Mingde's chest. Despite the cold concrete, the empty stomach, and a sleepless night, the corners of his eyes crinkled into a genuine smile.

"Then I'll keep waiting, Director Cheng."

Cheng Biao stared through the bars, his brow tightening!

For the first time since the Old Street buyout began, a thin thread of doubt crept under his skin. Eleven men had cracked when he laid out the math. Why on earth was this nobody, a small-town hardware seller with zero powerful friends, sitting on cold concrete smiling at him?!

"Suit yourself," Cheng Biao muttered coldly, turning down the hallway.

Across the road from the station, beneath an old locust tree, Chen Xuan pulled out his phone.

He stared at the dark screen for a long second. He hated dialing this contact. In three years, he'd only used it four times, and each time he felt oddly guilty, like a polite neighbor knocking on someone's door too late at night.

Still, he'd asked once, politely, at the front window, and the answer was crooked. By his grandfather's rule, now it was time to ask again.

He snapped a clear photo of the stamped detention notice, attached it, and typed slowly with one thumb, phrasing it exactly the way he spoke: *Hello, Chief An. Sorry to bother you on a Saturday. My Second Uncle, Chen Mingde, is being held at Yunxi's Old Street Police Station. The written notice says they decided to detain him at 7:50 this morning, even though they arrested him yesterday at 2:05 p.m., and their injury proof is just a private clinic slip. Something looks very wrong. Could you please check what's really going on? Strictly by the rules, nothing extra. Thank you.*

He read it once and pressed send.

A reply buzzed back in less than thirty seconds: *Understood, Boss. We'll look into the facts cleanly and report tonight. Please take care of your family.*

"Okay," Chen Xuan murmured, sliding the phone back into his pocket.

"Who were you texting?" Chen Jianguo asked curiously.

"A friend from the city who knows a lot about legal forms."

"Does he know any decent lawyers?"

"A few, I think."

"Good, good!" Chen Jianguo rubbed his rough palms together, suddenly feeling ten pounds lighter. "Then let's head back first. Your mother's been messaging me every five minutes asking if they locked us up too!"

"Hold on, Dad," Chen Xuan said, glancing toward the corner where the narrow lane curved toward Old Street. "Let's grab some fried dough sticks and stop by Old Yu's tailor shop on the way. If we're going to prove Second Uncle didn't touch that driver, we ought to talk to our eyewitness first."
