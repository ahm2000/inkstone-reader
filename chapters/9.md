# Chapter 9: Ask Again

Chen Jianguo snatched the stamped sheet from the slot before it finished sliding out.

His rough hands shook with anger.

"Look at this, Xuan! Look at the nonsense they wrote!"

Behind the glass, Wei Kun did not look up.

"Everyone always thinks their relative is innocent," he said, stacking the next form. His hands were not quite steady.

Chen Xuan gently took the notice and read it from top to bottom, running his thumb slowly beneath each line.

The form said that Chen Mingde was ordered to serve ten days in jail for assault and for hurting another person.

At the bottom, beside the red station stamp, sat one handwritten line: Time of Decision, Saturday, 7:50 a.m.

Chen Xuan paused.

He read that line a second time.

"Dad," he said quietly. "They handcuffed Second Uncle at two o'clock yesterday afternoon."

"I know! So what?"

"So the decision to detain him was not signed until ten to eight this morning. That is nearly eighteen hours later."

"For eighteen hours, they kept him in a cell with no detention order on file."

Chen Jianguo's mouth opened, then snapped shut!

To put it simply, a detention order is the paper that makes holding a person lawful. Without it, a locked cell is just a locked door.

"Eighteen hours?" he whispered.

"They are not supposed to," Chen Xuan said. "But think about it, Dad."

He lowered his voice, just enough so only the two of them heard.

"If everyone in that office wanted to hide a dirty arrest, they would have backdated this line to Friday afternoon. Nobody checks twice. Whoever filled out this notice wrote the real time on purpose."

"That person did not want to sign a lie."

Chen Xuan's eyes dropped to the last line, which listed the proof: Medical Injury Note, Ankang Private Clinic.

"And look here. There is a second problem. In a real assault case, a police doctor must examine the injury and write a formal report. They did not use one. They used a slip from a private street clinic."

"Why does that matter?" Chen Jianguo asked, blinking fast.

"Because a private clinic will write whatever someone pays it to write."

Behind the glass, Wei Kun froze with the last piece of his sesame bun halfway to his mouth!

His heart slammed against his ribs! How had an ordinary company clerk found both flaws in less than a minute?

Wei Kun looked at the mild young man outside the glass, and a chill ran down his back.

It felt as if a stranger had reached through the partition, laid a quiet hand on his shoulder, and whispered. I know what you did, he thought he heard.

"Xuan, how do you know all this?" Chen Jianguo stared at his son in disbelief.

"It's just paperwork, Dad."

It really was an everyday skill.

In three years of auditing shipping bills at Hengxin Logistics, Chen Xuan had checked more forms than he could count. He knew what an honest paper trail looked like, and he knew what a cooked one looked like.

After all, real records have small, messy human quirks. Forged ones are always too tidy, and their timestamps always lean the same way.

Hearing this, Chen Jianguo's anger flared up all over again. He puffed out his chest.

"They forged an arrest on my brother! I will yell right here until every person in Yunxi hears me!"

"Dad." Chen Xuan laid a steady hand on his father's sleeve. "Shouting will not fix a forged form. It will only give them an excuse to call security."

"Then what will?!"

"Let's step outside first. I need to think."

They walked out into the bright Saturday morning.

Chen Jianguo had been ready to shout for an hour, yet beside his calm son in the cool sunlight, the helpless rage drained right out of his chest.

Later that morning, inside the holding cell, a voice came from beyond the bars.

"Old Chen."

Chen Mingde blinked awake.

With the bare bulb burning all night, he had lost track of whether it was morning or evening.

Cheng Biao stood outside the iron bars, both hands tucked into his leather jacket pockets.

The friendly uncle smile from yesterday was gone.

"Your brother and your nephew stopped by the lobby this morning," he said softly. "A polite little office clerk, asking for written notices. Very cute."

"Now let me spell out reality, Old Chen. They can sit on those lobby benches for a solid week, and they still will not open this iron door."

"Only you can open it. Say yes to the buyout, sign the line, and walk home."

"My nephew came?" Chen Mingde asked quietly.

"That is what I just told you."

A slow, steady warmth spread across Chen Mingde's chest. Despite the cold concrete, the empty stomach and the sleepless night, the corners of his eyes crinkled into a real smile.

"Then I will keep waiting, Director Cheng."

Cheng Biao stared through the bars, and his brow tightened!

Failed?

Eleven men had cracked as soon as he laid out the numbers. He had expected tears, or anger, or a signature. Not a smile!

Was it stubbornness? Or did this family have a friend somewhere? No, no. A hardware seller with no powerful friends would not sit on cold concrete smiling at him.

So why was he smiling?

For the first time since the Old Street buyout began, a thin thread of doubt crept under Cheng Biao's skin.

"Suit yourself," he muttered coldly, and turned down the hallway.

Across the road from the station, under an old locust tree, Chen Xuan took out his phone.

He stared at the dark screen for a long second.

He disliked dialing this contact. In three years he had used it only four times, and each time he felt oddly guilty, like a polite neighbor knocking on a door too late at night.

Still, he had asked once, politely, at the front window, and the answer had been crooked. By his grandfather's rule, it was time to ask again.

He snapped a clear photo of the stamped notice, attached it, and typed slowly with one thumb, exactly as he spoke.

*Hello, Chief An. Sorry to bother you on a Saturday. My Second Uncle, Chen Mingde, is being held at Yunxi's Old Street Police Station. The written notice says they decided to detain him at 7:50 this morning, though they arrested him yesterday at 2:05 p.m., and their injury proof is only a private clinic slip. Something looks very wrong. Could you please check what is really going on. Strictly by the rules, nothing extra. Thank you.*

He read it once and pressed send.

Sent.

A reply buzzed back in less than thirty seconds: *Understood, Boss. We will look into the facts cleanly and report tonight. Please take care of your family.*

"Okay," Chen Xuan murmured, sliding the phone into his pocket.

"Who were you texting?" Chen Jianguo asked curiously.

"A friend from the city who knows a lot about legal forms."

"Does he know any decent lawyers?"

"A few, I think."

"Good, good!" Chen Jianguo rubbed his rough palms together and felt ten pounds lighter. "Then let's go back. Your mother has been messaging me every five minutes, asking if they locked us up too!"

"Hold on, Dad," Chen Xuan said, glancing toward the corner where the lane curved toward Old Street. "Let's buy some fried dough sticks and stop by Old Yu's tailor shop. If we are going to prove Second Uncle did not touch that driver, we should talk to our eyewitness first."

"Old Yu." Chen Jianguo's eyebrows shot up. "He saw everything, but will an old tailor really dare to say it out loud?"
