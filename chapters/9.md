# Chapter 9: Old Street After Dark

Liu Meifang hurried out of the kitchen wiping her hands on her apron the moment she heard the chairs scrape back. Her eyes darted nervously between Chen Jianguo and Chen Xuan.

"You are only going to Mingde Hardware to lock the shutter, right?" Liu Meifang asked quickly, catching the edge of the doorframe. "Promise me neither of you is turning down the side road toward Old Street Station. Going there tonight will only bring fresh trouble."

"We are not going anywhere near the police station, Mom," Chen Xuan promised calmly, slipping back into his damp shoes and picking up his dark-green steel thermos. "Just the hardware shop, then straight back."

Behind them, Chen Hao sprang off his folding stool. "Let me go too! If Yan Tiehu's thugs are hanging around Old Street, three pairs of hands are better than two!"

Chen Xuan turned back and rested one steady hand on his younger brother's shoulder. "Stay here, Chen Hao," he said gently, leaving no room for argument. "Look after Mom, Second Aunt, and Chen Yuan. Boil another kettle of hot water and make sure Second Aunt eats half a bowl of soup. We will be back soon."

Chen Hao opened his mouth to protest, met his eldest brother's quiet gaze, and slowly lowered his sneakers back onto the mat.

Ten minutes later, Chen Xuan and Chen Jianguo walked onto the northern mouth of Old Street.

Yunxi County's Old Street was a narrow historic lane barely six meters wide, paved in rain-worn bluestone flagstones and lined with two-story brick-and-timber shophouses. For forty years, this lane had been the quiet heartbeat of the county: tailors, clock repairmen, key cutters, and noodle boilers who knew three generations of every family by face.

Tonight, Old Street felt like a wounded animal holding its breath in the dark.

Half the overhead streetlamps along the lane had gone dark, leaving long wet shadows across the flagstones. The sharp smell of fresh industrial lacquer hung in the damp autumn air. Walking deeper into the lane, Chen Xuan counted them silently: eleven storefronts along both sides bore huge, jagged red characters for demolition, encircled in thick, dripping crimson rings whose paint still glistened wet under the sparse amber light.

"Look at what Hongrui Real Estate has done to this street," Chen Jianguo muttered darkly, clenching his hands inside his corduroy jacket pockets. "Not a single official redevelopment notice has been posted yet, and they are already slapping red paint onto people's walls in the dark. Market value for an Old Street storefront is at least eight thousand yuan per square meter! Yet Hongrui Real Estate waves a contract in front of old shopkeepers and offers three thousand eight hundred yuan per square meter! Less than half! Anyone who refuses gets their power cut on Tuesday, their water jammed on Wednesday, and Yan Tiehu banging on their shutter on Thursday!"

"Three thousand eight hundred yuan," Chen Xuan repeated softly, his eyes sweeping across the wet red circles. "That is not a demolition offer. That is daylight robbery dressed up as a contract."

Fifty meters ahead on the east side of the lane stood Mingde Hardware.

Its faded blue signboard hung slightly crooked above the eaves. Unlike its eleven scarred neighbors, its whitewashed brick walls remained clean and untouched by red paint. The corrugated iron shutter stood pulled halfway down at waist height, gaping open into a dark interior.

Chen Jianguo ducked, gripped the steel handle, and pushed the shutter upward with a harsh metallic rattle. He reached inside and flipped the plastic wall switch.

A bare sixty-watt ceiling bulb flickered twice, flooding the narrow twenty-square-meter shop in harsh yellow light.

Chen Jianguo stopped dead in the doorway, his breath catching sharply.

The interior of Mingde Hardware looked as though a storm had torn through it. The long glass display counter that Chen Mingde had wiped spotless every morning for twenty years lay shattered in jagged shards across the gray concrete. Wooden storage cabinets along the left wall had been yanked open, their compartment drawers dumped upside down across the aisle. Thousands of brass screws, washers, hex nuts, and nails lay spilled in glittering metallic drifts.

Near the rear workbench, a wide pool of cheap white wall paint sat congealing slowly across the dark floorboards beneath the high transom window.

Chen Jianguo's jaw tightened until a muscle pulsed along his cheek. "That white paint was not from today," he said in a low, shaking voice. "Thursday night, somebody threw a bucket of wall paint through the back transom window to intimidate your Second Uncle. Chen Mingde spent two hours scrubbing half of it himself so it would not ruin the ledgers. When Wu Lanying smelled turpentine Friday morning, he told her a stray cat had climbed through the window and knocked over a paint tin so she would not lose sleep."

Chen Xuan stood quietly in the doorway for a long moment, taking in every shard of glass, every overturned drawer, and the pale crust of white paint. His face showed neither rage nor theatrical grief, only a stillness cold enough to drain the warmth from the ceiling bulb.

Without a word, Chen Xuan stepped carefully around the broken glass. He set his green thermos on an intact upper shelf, slipped his canvas bag off his shoulder, and knelt calmly on the dusty floor beside the overturned drawers.

Reaching out with long, steady fingers, Chen Xuan began picking up the spilled brass screws one small handful at a time. He felt the fine threads between his thumb and forefinger, sorting M4 brass screws into the left wooden compartment and M5 brass screws into the adjacent slot, just as he had sat here at age seven sorting spilled hardware for Chen Mingde in exchange for two candied hawthorns from the corner cart.

Chen Jianguo watched his son kneeling calmly among the glass and brass dust. Something tight inside Chen Jianguo's chest loosened. He rubbed the back of his hand quickly across his eyes, found an old straw broom behind the door, and squatted beside Chen Xuan to gather the larger bolts and wrenches.

Outside in the narrow lane, wooden hinges scraped softly.

Two doors down, the narrow door of the tailor shop creaked open three inches. Sixty-eight-year-old Yu Changshun peered out nervously into the dim light, silver hair framing his temples and thick reading glasses perched low on his nose. His veined hands trembled against the doorframe as he watched the lit hardware shop, glancing anxiously toward the dark north mouth of Old Street.

Directly across the six-meter flagstones, however, Gong's Noodles glowed with warm golden light behind steamed glass panes. Through the propped folding door, the savory scent of slow-simmered beef broth and star anise drifted across the wet stones. Inside, quiet diners sat bent over steaming white bowls. Behind the wooden counter, a lean, broad-shouldered man in a clean white apron wiped down the tabletop in slow, unhurried strokes with a folded cloth.

Number Twenty-three did not look hurried, yet his dark, steady eyes rested over the narrow lane in silent watch.

As Chen Xuan lifted another drawer of sorted M4 and M5 brass screws back onto the shelf, his gaze shifted across the flagstones for a fraction of a second. Across the warm light of Gong's Noodles, Number Twenty-three's chin dipped by less than a millimeter.

Chen Xuan lowered his gaze back to the floor, picked up the dustpan, and swept the shattered glass cleanly out of the aisle.
