# Chapter 9: Absolute Order

Yan Tiehu whistled as he steered his black van off the side street of Fuxing New Village and onto the outer ring road.

Cheng Biao had phoned him after the dinner: Fan Weiguo wanted no more arrests. A paintbrush and a strip of red paper were not arrests. The job was cheap and clean. By morning, the clerk's mother would jump at every sound on her own stairs.

Yan Tiehu reached for a cigarette and grinned. Lei Bo, Niu Dali and Yao Xiaolong were waiting at the barbecue stall near Old Street. They wanted their beer and the red envelopes in his jacket.

Half a kilometer down the empty road, the lamps on that stretch went out, cut by a crew he could not see.

"Cheap county wiring," Yan Tiehu said, and tapped the dashboard.

Two black sedans slid out of a side lane. One stopped in front of his bumper. The other closed in behind him. A white box truck with no windows pulled up tight against his door.

There were no sirens, no flashing lights, not even a horn.

Yan Tiehu stamped on the brake and groped under his seat for his iron pipe. His fingers touched cold metal. Then his door was torn open from outside.

Two figures in matte-black gear and full-face masks stood beside the van. No skin showed. No badge showed. A gloved hand caught his wrist and twisted it behind him until the shoulder popped. They dragged him out and dropped him on the asphalt, all ninety kilos of him. His lip split on the road. His phone flew out of his pocket, and a boot crushed it.

"Who the hell are you?" Yan Tiehu roared, kicking. "I work for Hongrui! Director Cheng Biao is my boss!"

"Target secured," one of the masks said. The voice was flat and neither male nor female. It was the coldest sound Yan Tiehu had ever heard.

A thick hood came down over his head. One hard blow landed behind his ear, and the road went black.

* * *

When Yan Tiehu woke up, he was freezing.

A bucket of ice water hit his face and chest. He jerked upright, choking. Steel chains held his wrists and ankles to an iron chair. He was in a concrete cellar that smelled of rust and wet earth. A thin gray line under a steel door, twenty steps away, was the only light.

"Who is there?" His voice bounced back at him, thin and shaking. "Say a name. If it's money, Dong Haisheng will pay. How much do you want?"

Nobody answered. Slow footsteps walked a circle behind his chair.

Yan Tiehu had spent twelve years breaking kneecaps and scaring shopkeepers on Old Street. He had never felt this. Street rivals shouted. Police read out charges. A man who said nothing did not plan to let you walk out.

A steel bar tapped once on the back of his right hand. It was the hand that had slapped Chen Jianguo.

Then the bar came down hard.

Crack.

Yan Tiehu screamed until his throat tore. He fell forward against the chains.

"Wait. Stop. What did I do?" Tears and snot ran down his face. "Was it Old Street? Was it the Dongqiao bid? Give me a name. Just one name, please."

Ice water hit his face again. Nobody spoke.

In the dark, with broken fingers and no idea whether the next hour brought more steel or a ditch, his twelve years of pride collapsed completely. He began to spill everything. Not just the Old Street buyout or a few million in county bribes, but every single name, account, and dirty deal he had carried messages for over five years.

Outside the cellar, silent Zero Guard intelligence units fed every name, encrypted relay number, and offshore clearing code straight into the deepest national financial, customs, and military-counterintelligence backbones. Within two hours, the trace hit a live national-security tripwire that explained why the Zero Guard never took half measures.

Old Street's six hundred forty million yuan relocation fund was barely a grain of sand at the bottom of the pit. Tracing Dong Haisheng and Huang Boren's underground clearing channels upward through corrupt loan desks at Huaxin Bank tore open a sprawling network of over one point two trillion yuan in siphoned public infrastructure funds, rigged sovereign land grants, state-bank kickbacks, and illicit capital flight spanning fourteen provinces and funneled through layered shell trusts in Hong Kong, Singapore, Zurich, and the Cayman Islands.

Worse still, the corruption was only the cover. The exact same underground clearing houses financing Huang Boren and Dong Haisheng were actively moving foreign intelligence payouts to compromised officials, defense-procurement insiders, port administrators, and energy-grid engineers. Through those hidden channels, classified defense blueprints, strategic port-logistics schedules, rare-earth supply data, and encrypted communications keys were being smuggled out of the country to overseas handlers.

Every piece of the night's silence suddenly had a lethal purpose. The five-to-ten-man mute watches standing inside the capital, the provincial command centers, and the ministries were no longer just a security ring. They were a trap keeping every hidden mole in the country frozen in his chair, unable to wipe a server, burn a ledger, warn a foreign handler, or board a private jet.

Before dawn, without a single public warrant or newspaper headline, the Zero Guard silently intercepted three live overseas data drops, froze every cross-border clearing node, locked every private tarmac and border gate against every passport in the web, and sealed the entire trillion-yuan treason and espionage tree into an ironclad shadow dossier.

Inside the dark cellar, Yan Tiehu wept against his chains, never once guessing that by slapping an ordinary clerk's father on Old Street, he had pulled the single loose thread that unraveled a trillion-yuan espionage empire.

* * *

At the Lanshui Provincial Security Command Center, alarms chimed in a steady beat. A ten-kilometer square of Yunxi County had just gone blank on the radar, the traffic maps and the satellite feeds.

The duty director typed his highest provincial code to ask why. The screen turned gray.

*INSUFFICIENT PRIVILEGE. YOUR CLEARANCE IS NOT HIGH ENOUGH TO VIEW THIS EVENT.*

"Insufficient privilege?" His hands shook over the keys. "That is my own province. Get the Provincial Party Secretary. No, get the old commander at the leaders' compound. We need emergency approval."

Two minutes later, in a quiet villa inside that compound, an eighty-year-old retired army commander picked up his red phone. He listened for ten seconds. His teacup fell onto the carpet.

"Stop talking," the old man said, and his voice cracked. "Send no police car. Fly no drone. Ask no more questions. Pull every provincial unit back from Yunxi, and pray none of our people caused this."
