# Chapter 9: Old Street After Dark

Liu Meifang hurried out of the kitchen wiping her hands on her apron the instant she heard the chairs scrape back. Her eyes darted anxiously between Chen Jianguo and Chen Xuan.

"You are only going to Mingde Hardware to lock the shutter, right?" Liu Meifang asked quickly, gripping the doorframe. "Promise me neither of you is turning toward Old Street Station. Going there tonight will only invite fresh trouble."

"We are not going anywhere near the police station, Mom," Chen Xuan promised calmly, slipping into his shoes and lifting his dark-green steel thermos. "Just the hardware shop, then straight home."

"I told you already, I am not going back to that gate," Chen Jianguo added gruffly, tugging his corduroy jacket straight.

Behind them, Chen Hao sprang off his folding stool. "Let me go too! If Yan Tiehu's thugs are hanging around Old Street, three pairs of hands are better than two!"

Chen Xuan turned and rested one steady hand on his younger brother's shoulder. "Stay here, Chen Hao," he said gently, leaving no room for debate. "Look after Mom, Second Aunt, and Chen Yuan. Boil another kettle of hot water and make sure Second Aunt eats half a bowl of soup."

Chen Hao opened his mouth to argue, met his eldest brother's quiet gaze, and slowly lowered his sneakers back onto the mat. "Fine," he muttered, his shoulders dropping. "Call if anything happens."

Ten minutes later, Chen Xuan and Chen Jianguo stepped onto the northern mouth of Old Street.

Half the overhead lamps in the narrow six-meter lane were dark, and the sharp reek of fresh industrial lacquer hung in the damp air. Walking deeper in, Chen Xuan counted silently: eleven shophouses bore huge, jagged red demolition characters encircled in thick crimson rings whose paint still glistened wet.

Chen Jianguo's hands clenched inside his pockets. "Look at what Hongrui Real Estate has done to this street," he muttered bitterly. "Not a single official redevelopment notice has been posted, and they are already slapping red paint on people's walls in the dark."

"How much are they offering per square meter?" Chen Xuan asked, his eyes sweeping across the wet red circles.

"Three thousand eight hundred yuan," Chen Jianguo spat, his jaw working. "Market value for an Old Street storefront is at least eight thousand yuan per square meter! Not even half! Anyone who refuses gets their electricity cut Tuesday, their water jammed Wednesday, and Yan Tiehu banging on their shutter at midnight Thursday."

"Has anyone folded?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"Three families near the south end signed last week," Chen Jianguo said heavily. "Old Zhao from the bicycle stall has a sick wife. He could not sit in the dark for a week. Once they gave in, the rest of the street started locking up before sunset. Only your Second Uncle refused to budge."

"He put twenty years into that shop," Chen Xuan said quietly. "Three thousand eight hundred yuan is not a demolition offer. That is daylight robbery dressed up as a contract."

"Try telling that to Station Director Cheng Biao," Chen Jianguo murmured grimly.

Fifty meters ahead stood Mingde Hardware. Unlike its eleven scarred neighbors, its whitewashed brick walls remained clean. The corrugated iron shutter stood halfway down at waist height, gaping open into darkness.

Chen Jianguo ducked, shoved the shutter upward with a harsh metallic rattle, and flipped the wall switch.

A bare sixty-watt ceiling bulb flickered on, flooding the narrow twenty-square-meter shop in harsh yellow light.

Chen Jianguo stopped dead on the threshold, his breath catching sharply.

The long glass display counter Chen Mingde had wiped spotless every morning for twenty years lay shattered across the concrete. Wooden storage drawers along the left wall had been dumped upside down, spilling thousands of brass screws, washers, hex nuts, and nails. Near the rear workbench, a wide pool of cheap white wall paint sat congealing beneath the high transom window.

A muscle pulsed violently along Chen Jianguo's cheek.

"That white paint was not from today," Chen Jianguo said, his voice shaking. "Thursday night, someone hurled a bucket of paint through the back transom window to scare your Second Uncle. Mingde spent two hours scrubbing half of it himself so it would not ruin his ledgers. When Wu Lanying smelled turpentine Friday morning, he smiled and told her a stray cat knocked over a paint tin so she would not lose sleep."

Chen Jianguo rubbed a rough hand across his face, his voice dropping hoarse. "That fool of a brother never tells anyone when he is bleeding. He just sweeps it up and keeps quiet."

Chen Xuan stood motionless in the doorway for a long moment, taking in every shard of glass, every overturned drawer, and the pale crust of white paint. His face showed neither rage nor theatrical grief, only a stillness cold enough to drain the warmth from the ceiling bulb.

Without a word, Chen Xuan stepped around the broken glass. He set his green thermos on an upper shelf, slipped off his canvas bag, and knelt calmly on the dusty floor beside the overturned drawers.

Reaching out with steady fingers, Chen Xuan began gathering the spilled brass screws one small handful at a time. He rolled the fine threads between his thumb and forefinger, sorting M4 brass screws into the left compartment and M5 brass screws into the adjacent slot, just as he had sat here at age seven sorting hardware for Chen Mingde in exchange for two candied hawthorns.

Chen Jianguo watched his son kneel quietly among the glass and brass dust. Something knotted tight inside his chest loosened. He blinked hard, found an old straw broom behind the door, and squatted beside Chen Xuan to gather the larger bolts and wrenches.

"You still remember M4 and M5 by touch?" Chen Jianguo asked quietly, his voice steadier.

"Second Uncle made sure I never mixed them up," Chen Xuan replied softly, sliding a filled drawer straight. "He said a man who mixes his screws cannot keep his house in order."

A faint breath left Chen Jianguo's nose, almost a smile. "That sounds like him."

Outside in the lane, wooden hinges scraped softly.

Two doors down, the narrow door of the tailor shop creaked open three inches. Sixty-eight-year-old Yu Changshun peered out nervously, silver hair framing his temples and thick glasses perched low on his nose. His veined hands trembled against the frame as he watched the lit hardware shop, glancing anxiously toward the dark north mouth of Old Street.

Directly across the flagstones, Gong's Noodles glowed behind steamed glass panes. Through the propped folding door, quiet diners sat bent over steaming bowls. Behind the wooden counter, a lean, broad-shouldered man in a clean white apron wiped the tabletop in slow, unhurried strokes.

Number Twenty-three did not look tense, yet his dark, steady eyes rested over the lane in silent watch.

As Chen Xuan lifted another drawer of sorted brass screws onto the shelf, his gaze shifted across the flagstones for a fraction of a second. Inside Gong's Noodles, Number Twenty-three's chin dipped by less than a millimeter.

Chen Xuan lowered his eyes back to the floor, picked up the dustpan, and began sweeping the shattered glass cleanly out of the aisle.
