# Chapter 9: Absolute Order

Yan Tiehu whistled as he steered his black van off Fuxing New Village's side street and onto the outer ring road.

He had left a wet paintbrush on a doorstep, unscrewed the hallway bulb, and taped a strip of red paper to the door. The job had been cheap and clean, and there was not a single arrest for Fan Weiguo to complain about. By morning, that clerk's mother would be jumping at her own shadow on the stairs.

He reached for a cigarette and grinned to himself. Lei Bo, Niu Dali, and Yao Xiaolong were already waiting at the barbecue stall near Old Street for their drinks and red envelopes.

Half a mile down the empty ring road, the overhead streetlamps blinked out all at once.

Yan Tiehu frowned and tapped his dashboard. "Cheap county wiring."

Before the words left his mouth, two plain black sedans slid out of a feeder lane, one gliding smoothly across the asphalt in front of his bumper and the other closing tight behind his rear doors. At the exact same second, a windowless white box truck pulled flush alongside his driver's window, cutting off the shoulder.

There were no sirens, no flashing lights, and not even the sound of a horn.

Yan Tiehu's grin vanished. Instinct screamed through his spine as he slammed the brakes and lunged under his seat for an iron pipe.

His fingers had barely grazed the cold metal when his driver's door was wrenched open from outside.

Two figures in matte-black gear and featureless full-face masks stood beside the van. No skin showed. No badges showed. One gloved hand caught Yan Tiehu's wrist, twisted it backward until his shoulder popped, and hauled all two hundred pounds of him straight out onto the asphalt. His phone flew from his pocket and died under a heavy black boot with a crisp crunch.

"Who the hell are you?!" Yan Tiehu roared, kicking wildly. "I'm with Hongrui! Director Cheng Biao is my boss!"

"Target secured," one of the masked Zero Guard operatives spoke in a flat, completely neutral, genderless voice so cold it froze Yan Tiehu's blood. Before he could shout another word, a thick black hood dropped over his head, a single surgical strike landed behind his ear, and the road went black.

* * *

When Yan Tiehu woke, he was freezing.

A bucket of ice water crashed over his head and chest, jolting him upright with a choking gasp. Heavy steel chains bit into his wrists and ankles, bolting him to an iron chair in the center of a pitch-black concrete cellar that smelled of rust and damp earth.

No lamp shone. Only a faint gray crack under a steel door twenty feet away showed him he was underground.

"Who is there?!" Yan Tiehu's voice echoed back at him, raw and shaking. "Say a name! If it's money, Dong Haisheng will pay! How much do you want?!"

Only silence answered him in the dark.

Then slow, unhurried footsteps circled behind his chair.

Yan Tiehu jerked against the chains, his breath coming in ragged gasps. In twelve years of breaking kneecaps and scaring shopkeepers on Old Street, he had never once felt this kind of cold crawling up his spine. Street rivals shouted. Police read charges. Men who said nothing at all did not plan on letting you walk out.

A cold length of steel tapped once against the back of his knuckles, right on the hand that had slapped Chen Jianguo's face.

Crack!

Yan Tiehu screamed until his throat tore, pitching forward against the chains while sweat poured down his shaved head.

"Wait! Stop! Who did I offend?!" he shrieked, tears and mucus running down his face. "Was it Old Street? Was it the construction bid in Dongqiao? Tell me a name! Please, just tell me a name!"

Another splash of freezing water hit his face.

Still nobody spoke. Still nobody gave him a name.

In the pitch dark, with his fingers broken and no idea whether the next hour would bring more cold steel or a shallow ditch, Yan Tiehu's twelve years of arrogance collapsed into pure, choking terror. He babbled every rival, every debt, every dirty job he had ever done for Dong Haisheng and Cheng Biao, begging an empty room for mercy.

He never even thought of the quiet office clerk whose father he had slapped two hours ago.

* * *

In the Lanshui Provincial Security Command Center, alarms chimed in a sharp, steady rhythm as an entire ten-kilometer grid over Yunxi County vanished from radar, traffic networks, and satellite feeds all at once.

The provincial duty director stared at his main wall screen, his face draining of color. When he typed his highest provincial authorization code to pull the reason, the screen flashed cold gray.

*INSUFFICIENT PRIVILEGE. YOUR CLEARANCE LEVEL IS NOT HIGH ENOUGH TO ACCESS THIS EVENT.*

"Insufficient privilege?!" the provincial director shouted, his hands shaking over the console. "That is my own province! Call the Provincial Party Secretary! No, call the retired old commander at his compound and ask for emergency authorization right now!"

Two minutes later, in a quiet villa inside the provincial leadership compound, an eighty-year-old retired provincial leader picked up his red phone, listened for ten seconds, and dropped his teacup onto the carpet.

"Shut your mouth right now!" the old man barked, his voice cracking with terror. "Do not send a single police car, do not fly a single drone, and do not ask another question! Pull every provincial unit back from Yunxi and pray to heaven none of our people caused this!"
