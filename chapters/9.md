# Chapter 9: Old Street After Dark

Liu Meifang hurried from the kitchen wiping her hands on her apron the moment chairs scraped back, her eyes jumping anxiously between Chen Jianguo and Chen Xuan.

"You are only going to Mingde Hardware to lock the shutter, right?" Liu Meifang asked quickly, gripping the doorframe. "Promise me neither of you is turning toward Old Street Station. Going there tonight only brings fresh trouble."

"We are not going near the police station, Mom," Chen Xuan promised calmly, slipping on his shoes and lifting his green thermos. "Just the shop, then straight home."

"I told you already, I am not going back to that gate," Chen Jianguo added gruffly, tugging his corduroy jacket straight.

Behind them, Chen Hao jumped off his stool. "Let me go too! If Yan Tiehu's thugs are around Old Street, three pairs of hands are better than two!"

Chen Xuan rested a steady hand on his brother's shoulder. "Stay here, Chen Hao," he said gently. "Look after Mom, Second Aunt, and Chen Yuan. Boil another kettle of hot water and make sure Second Aunt eats half a bowl of soup."

Chen Hao opened his mouth, met his eldest brother's quiet eyes, and lowered his sneakers onto the mat. "Fine," he muttered, shoulders dropping. "Call if anything happens."

Ten minutes later, Chen Xuan and Chen Jianguo stepped onto the north end of Old Street.

Half the streetlamps were dark, and the sharp smell of cheap paint hung in the damp air. Walking in, Chen Xuan counted silently. Eleven shops had jagged red tear-down marks painted inside thick circles. The paint was still wet.

Chen Jianguo's hands clenched inside his pockets. "Look what Hongrui Real Estate has done to this street," he muttered bitterly. "Not one town notice posted, and they are slapping red paint on walls in the dark."

"How much are they offering per square meter?" Chen Xuan asked, eyes sweeping the wet red circles.

"Three thousand eight hundred yuan," Chen Jianguo spat. "The real price for an Old Street shop is at least eight thousand yuan per square meter! Not even half! Anyone who says no gets power cut Tuesday, water blocked Wednesday, and Yan Tiehu banging on their shutter at midnight Thursday."

"Has anyone given in?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"Three families near the south end signed last week," Chen Jianguo said heavily. "Old Zhao from the bike stall has a sick wife and could not sit in the dark for a week. Once they gave in, the rest locked up before dark. Only your Second Uncle refused to budge."

"He put twenty years into that shop," Chen Xuan said quietly. "Three thousand eight hundred yuan is not a buyout offer. That is daylight robbery dressed as a contract."

"Try telling Station Director Cheng Biao," Chen Jianguo murmured grimly.

Fifty meters ahead stood Mingde Hardware. Unlike its eleven marked neighbors, its white brick walls were clean. The metal shutter stood halfway down, gaping into the dark.

Chen Jianguo ducked, shoved the shutter up with a rattle, and flipped the switch.

A bare bulb flickered on, filling the narrow shop with harsh yellow light.

Chen Jianguo stopped dead in the doorway, breath catching.

The long glass counter Chen Mingde had wiped spotless every morning for twenty years lay smashed across the floor. Wooden drawers along the left wall had been dumped upside down, spilling thousands of brass screws, washers, hex nuts, and nails. Near the back workbench, cheap white wall paint sat drying under the high back window.

A muscle twitched along Chen Jianguo's cheek.

"That white paint was not from today," Chen Jianguo said, voice shaking. "Thursday night, someone threw a bucket of paint through the back window to scare your Second Uncle. Mingde spent two hours scrubbing half of it himself so it would not ruin his account books. When Wu Lanying smelled thinner Friday morning, he smiled and blamed a stray cat so she would not lose sleep."

Chen Jianguo rubbed a rough hand across his face, voice hoarse. "That fool never tells anyone when he is hurting. He just sweeps it up and keeps quiet."

Chen Xuan stood still in the doorway, taking in the broken glass, tipped drawers, and dried paint. His face showed neither loud anger nor showy grief, only a cold stillness that seemed to drain warmth from the bulb.

Without a word, Chen Xuan stepped around the glass, set his thermos on a shelf, slipped off his bag, and knelt beside the dumped drawers.

With steady fingers, he picked up spilled brass screws a small handful at a time, rolling the threads between thumb and finger to sort M4 screws into the left slot and M5 into the next, just as he had at age seven for two candied hawthorns.

Chen Jianguo watched his son kneel among broken glass and brass dust. Something tight in his chest loosened. Blinking hard, he grabbed an old straw broom behind the door and squatted beside Chen Xuan to gather bolts and wrenches.

"You still know M4 and M5 by touch?" Chen Jianguo asked quietly, voice steadier.

"Second Uncle made sure I never mixed them up," Chen Xuan replied softly, sliding a full drawer straight back in. "He said a man who mixes his screws cannot keep his house in order."

A faint breath left Chen Jianguo's nose, almost a smile. "That sounds like him."

Outside in the lane, wooden hinges scraped softly.

Two doors down, the tailor shop door creaked open three inches. Sixty-eight-year-old Yu Changshun peered out nervously, thick glasses low on his nose. His veined hands shook on the doorframe as he watched the lit shop and glanced anxiously up the dark street.

Across the stone lane, Gong's Noodles glowed behind steamed glass. Inside, quiet diners bent over steaming bowls. Behind the counter, a lean, broad-shouldered man in a clean white apron wiped the tabletop slowly.

Number 23 did not look tense, yet his dark eyes watched the lane in silence.

Lifting another sorted drawer onto the shelf, Chen Xuan glanced across the stones for a split second. Inside Gong's Noodles, Number 23 dipped his chin by less than a millimeter.

Chen Xuan lowered his eyes, picked up the dustpan, and began sweeping broken glass cleanly from the aisle.
