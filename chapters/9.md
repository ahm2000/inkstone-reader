# Chapter 9: The Eighteen Hours

Outside the station, Chen Jianguo grabbed the sheet out of the slot before it finished sliding.

"Ten days! For a fight, the police can lock a man up ten days with no judge. And Mingde never hit anybody!"

"Dad, can I read it too?" Chen Xuan's voice was thick.

Chen Jianguo held it out. Chen Xuan read it from top to bottom, with one thumb under each line.

"What time did the police take Second Uncle?"

"Two o'clock yesterday. Five past, Old Yu says."

"This box says *Time of Decision: Saturday, 7:50 a.m.* Is that a long time after?"

"What?" Chen Jianguo grabbed the sheet back. "Almost eighteen hours after the handcuffs! That box is the hour they decide to hold a man. They held him all night with no decision at all!"

"Eighteen hours." Chen Xuan wiped his nose with the back of his hand. "He's fifty-two, Dad. Did anybody even give him water?"

"I don't know, boy. I don't know."

"And this stamp says Ankang Private Clinic, ten to two. In a fight case, doesn't a police doctor write the report?"

"Ten to two. Ten to two." Chen Jianguo said it like a password. "The handcuffs went on at five past! They wrote the injury before it happened. A private clinic writes whatever somebody pays it to write. Xuan, they're the ones who broke the law!"

"But Dad, if they wanted to hide the eighteen hours, why does the form show the real time?"

"Boy, don't ask me riddles!"

Behind the lobby glass, Wei Kun watched the Chens through the glass. Somebody was reading the time he wrote. He did not know if that was good or bad.

"Then what do we do?" said Chen Jianguo.

Chen Xuan held the paper with both hands, his eyes wet.

"Dad, I have to take a work call."

"Now?" Chen Jianguo cried. "Your uncle is in a cell!"

"I'm sorry, Dad. It's quick. I'll come right back."

He walked ten steps away and took out his phone.

Last night he had used this number for the first time. It reached An Zhiyuan, head of the Detail, his secret guard. His order had been: watch, and touch nothing.

He took a photo of the notice and typed.

*Chief An. Thank you for last night. Now stop watching. Finish it. Eighteen hours is not a mistake. Lay out the notice, the clinic and the money by tonight. Keep the old tailor safe.*

The answer came at once: *Yes, Boss.*

He bought two fried dough sticks from the cart. He gave one to his father and bit into the other, and his father saw that his hand was shaking.

* * *

Inside the station, Zhong Hai sat alone at a steel table in the canteen with a lunch box he was not eating.

He was thirty-eight, Wei Kun's desk neighbor, and a good man in every way but one. He was afraid.

His six-year-old son, Zhong Tiantian, needed a heart operation. The hospital wanted 80,000 yuan by Monday. That was more than a year of his pay, and he had 9,000.

His phone buzzed.

*Lei Bo: Director Cheng has one question. What time did Wei write on the Chen notice? A 60,000 loan waits for a straight answer. No interest.*

He typed *7:50 a.m.* and pressed send before the rest of him could stop it.

The answer came in four seconds, a thumbs-up and *Good brother.*

Zhong Hai pressed his sleeve over his mouth and sat very still, like a man who had swallowed something that would not go down.

Down in the holding corridor, Cheng Biao stopped. He turned and slapped Lei Bo across the face.

"Seven-fifty! I wanted Friday afternoon in that box, and you stamped it without reading one word!"

"My fault, Director!" Lei Bo cried. From old habit, he began to laugh. It died as a red hand rose on his cheek.

Cheng Biao went on alone to the bars.

"Old Chen. Your brother and nephew came to the lobby. The clerk asked for paper. Only you can open this door. Sign."

He leaned closer.

"Your daughter. Wuhan, Room 412. She leaves the library at ten and walks the back road by the building site. On Monday a letter about her father's police record goes to her university. The back road is not my business. Sign tonight, and she never hears about any of this."

He tapped the toothpick against the bars, slowly, like a clock.

The hands of Chen Mingde, the hardware shopkeeper, began to shake between his knees.

His daughter.

For one second the cold of the concrete went into his chest.

Then he thought of one thing. His brother and his nephew had sat on a bench for two hours, for him.

The shaking slowed, and his mouth moved into a small smile.

"I won't sign, Director Cheng. Send your letter. My daughter knows her father never hit anybody. And my nephew is still on that bench."

Cheng Biao stared. He had broken eleven men in this corridor, and none of them had smiled.

In the hall he called Yan Tiehu and spoke low.

"Tiehu. The old tailor saw too much on Friday. This afternoon, on the river road, a van. Make it look like traffic. Visit him tonight anyway."

Then he waved Lei Bo over.

"Wei Kun wrote the true hour, and he knows who told him to write the false one. Bring him a cup of tea with the powder from Ankang. All of it. And Lei Bo, if you spill it, you drink the next one."

"To make him sleep, Director?"

"To make him whatever it makes him. A dead witness can't sign anything."

Lei Bo carried a paper cup of jasmine tea toward the duty window in both hands, like a man carrying a baby bird.

At the records-room door, a bent old man in sleeve covers stepped out with a stack of files and bumped Lei Bo's wrist.

The cup flew. Tea splashed across the tiles, and the cup split.

"Aiya! My old eyes!" Ding Shouyi dropped to his knees with a rag.

The rag wiped the puddle, and a small glass tube slid into his sleeve cover.

On the wet floor, the tea had left a gray foam.

Lei Bo stared at it, and his knees went soft. Nothing harmless makes foam.

"Forgive an old man, Officer Lei." Ding Shouyi poured plain tea from his own flask into a fresh cup. "Tell Wei it comes from you, and you get the credit."

"From me?"

"Who else carried it?"

It was the worst question Lei Bo had ever heard. And it was true. The one who carries the cup gets the blame.

He took the plain tea to the window with a hand that would not keep still.

"Thank you, Lei Bo." Wei Kun looked puzzled, but he drank. "It's good tea."

Lei Bo leaned on a wall and muttered, "The old fool tripped on his own feet. What luck."

In the records room, Ding Shouyi sealed the tube and typed. *Poison in cup for Wei Kun. Ordered by Cheng Biao, carried by Lei Bo, powder from Ankang. Cup saved. Wei Kun unharmed.*

* * *

Behind the bread factory sat a windowless van painted like a flower-delivery truck. Inside was Tang Liqun, captain of the Detail's van team. His screen flashed.

On the wall map, two hundred and fourteen green dots turned amber at once. Amber meant: get ready.

"Captain!" Pei Ruihan, his deputy, dropped his tea. "A message from the Boss, through Chief An. It is an order."

The van went silent.

Since last night they had recorded everything and touched nothing. Now the next word had come.

"Read it exactly."

"Finish it. Lay out the notice, the clinic and the money by tonight. Keep the old tailor safe."

Tang Liqun nodded. "Then we move. All of us, by the book. No questions."

A bread truck rolled out first, then a laundry van, then three plain gray cars with city plates.

A line scrolled across the screen from the records clerk. *Cheng Biao ordered Yan Tiehu to put a van on the old tailor this afternoon, river road. Also tea for Wei Kun, stopped. Recorded.*

Pei Ruihan spoke quietly. "That's a murder order. Do we stop it?"

Tang Liqun tapped the river road on the map, in the voice of a man ordering lunch.

"Bread truck at the arch by four. The doctor from Everbright Family Clinic on the corner with her bag open. Our man on the traffic team takes the arch beat."

"And if the van comes early?"

"Then the bread gets there early."

The voice of An Zhiyuan came over the speaker. It was dry and a little amused.

"Wake everyone. Last night he said 'touch nothing.' Now he says 'finish it.'"

What would happen to a county when a man who rarely gave orders said that word?
