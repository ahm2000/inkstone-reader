# Chapter 9: Old Street After Dark

Liu Meifang hurried out of the kitchen wiping her hands on her apron the moment she heard the chairs scrape back. Her eyes jumped anxiously between Chen Jianguo and Chen Xuan.

"You are only going to Mingde Hardware to lock the shutter, right?" Liu Meifang asked quickly, gripping the doorframe. "Promise me neither of you is turning toward Old Street Station. Going there tonight will only bring fresh trouble."

"We are not going anywhere near the police station, Mom," Chen Xuan promised calmly, slipping on his shoes and lifting his dark-green steel thermos. "Just the hardware shop, then straight home."

"I told you already, I am not going back to that gate," Chen Jianguo added gruffly, tugging his corduroy jacket straight.

Behind them, Chen Hao jumped off his folding stool. "Let me go too! If Yan Tiehu's thugs are hanging around Old Street, three pairs of hands are better than two!"

Chen Xuan turned and rested one steady hand on his younger brother's shoulder. "Stay here, Chen Hao," he said gently, leaving no room to argue. "Look after Mom, Second Aunt, and Chen Yuan. Boil another kettle of hot water and make sure Second Aunt eats half a bowl of soup."

Chen Hao opened his mouth to protest, met his eldest brother's quiet eyes, and slowly lowered his sneakers back onto the mat. "Fine," he muttered, his shoulders dropping. "Call if anything happens."

Ten minutes later, Chen Xuan and Chen Jianguo stepped onto the north end of Old Street.

Half the streetlamps in the narrow lane were dark, and the sharp smell of fresh cheap paint hung in the damp air. Walking deeper in, Chen Xuan counted silently. Eleven shops had huge, jagged red tear-down marks painted inside thick red circles. The paint was still wet.

Chen Jianguo's hands clenched inside his pockets. "Look at what Hongrui Real Estate has done to this street," he muttered bitterly. "Not a single real town notice has been posted, and they are already slapping red paint on people's walls in the dark."

"How much are they offering per square meter?" Chen Xuan asked, his eyes sweeping across the wet red circles.

"Three thousand eight hundred yuan," Chen Jianguo spat, his jaw working. "The real price for an Old Street shop is at least eight thousand yuan per square meter! Not even half! Anyone who says no gets their power cut Tuesday, their water blocked Wednesday, and Yan Tiehu banging on their shutter at midnight Thursday."

"Has anyone given in?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"Three families near the south end signed last week," Chen Jianguo said heavily. "Old Zhao from the bike stall has a sick wife. He could not sit in the dark for a week. Once they gave in, the rest of the street started locking up before dark. Only your Second Uncle refused to budge."

"He put twenty years into that shop," Chen Xuan said quietly. "Three thousand eight hundred yuan is not a buyout offer. That is daylight robbery dressed up as a contract."

"Try telling that to Station Director Cheng Biao," Chen Jianguo murmured grimly.

Fifty meters ahead stood Mingde Hardware. Unlike its eleven marked neighbors, its white brick walls were still clean. The metal shutter stood halfway down at waist height, gaping open into the dark.

Chen Jianguo ducked, shoved the shutter upward with a loud metal rattle, and flipped the wall switch.

A bare ceiling bulb flickered on, filling the narrow shop with harsh yellow light.

Chen Jianguo stopped dead in the doorway, his breath catching sharply.

The long glass counter Chen Mingde had wiped spotless every morning for twenty years lay smashed across the concrete floor. Wooden storage drawers along the left wall had been dumped upside down, spilling thousands of brass screws, washers, hex nuts, and nails. Near the back workbench, a wide puddle of cheap white wall paint sat drying under the high back window.

A muscle twitched hard along Chen Jianguo's cheek.

"That white paint was not from today," Chen Jianguo said, his voice shaking. "Thursday night, someone threw a bucket of paint through the high back window to scare your Second Uncle. Mingde spent two hours scrubbing half of it himself so it would not ruin his account books. When Wu Lanying smelled paint thinner Friday morning, he smiled and told her a stray cat knocked over a paint tin so she would not lose sleep."

Chen Jianguo rubbed a rough hand across his face, his voice turning hoarse. "That fool of a brother never tells anyone when he is hurting. He just sweeps it up and keeps quiet."

Chen Xuan stood still in the doorway for a long moment, taking in every piece of broken glass, every tipped drawer, and the pale crust of white paint. His face showed neither loud anger nor showy grief, only a stillness cold enough to drain the warmth from the yellow bulb.

Without a word, Chen Xuan stepped around the broken glass. He set his green thermos on an upper shelf, slipped off his canvas bag, and knelt calmly on the dusty floor beside the dumped drawers.

Reaching out with steady fingers, Chen Xuan began picking up the spilled brass screws one small handful at a time. He rolled the fine threads between his thumb and finger, sorting M4 brass screws into the left slot and M5 brass screws into the next slot. It was just like when he sat here at age seven sorting hardware for Chen Mingde in exchange for two candied hawthorns.

Chen Jianguo watched his son kneel quietly among the broken glass and brass dust. Something knotted tight inside his chest loosened. He blinked hard, found an old straw broom behind the door, and squatted beside Chen Xuan to gather the larger bolts and wrenches.

"You still know M4 and M5 by touch?" Chen Jianguo asked quietly, his voice steadier.

"Second Uncle made sure I never mixed them up," Chen Xuan replied softly, sliding a full drawer back in straight. "He said a man who mixes his screws cannot keep his house in order."

A faint breath left Chen Jianguo's nose, almost a smile. "That sounds like him."

Outside in the lane, wooden hinges scraped softly.

Two doors down, the narrow door of the tailor shop creaked open three inches. Sixty-eight-year-old Yu Changshun peered out nervously, his silver hair framing his face and thick glasses resting low on his nose. His veined hands shook on the doorframe as he watched the lit hardware shop and glanced anxiously toward the dark north end of Old Street.

Right across the stone lane, Gong's Noodles glowed behind steamed glass panes. Through the propped door, quiet diners sat bent over steaming bowls. Behind the wooden counter, a lean, broad-shouldered man in a clean white apron wiped the tabletop in slow, unhurried strokes.

Number 23 did not look tense, yet his dark, steady eyes watched the lane in silence.

As Chen Xuan lifted another drawer of sorted brass screws onto the shelf, his gaze shifted across the stones for a split second. Inside Gong's Noodles, Number 23 dipped his chin by less than a millimeter.

Chen Xuan lowered his eyes back to the floor, picked up the dustpan, and began sweeping the broken glass cleanly out of the aisle.
