# Chapter 9: The Eighteen Hours

Outside the station, Chen Jianguo snatched the sheet from the slot before it had even finished sliding out.

"Ten days! For a fight, the police can lock a man up for ten days without a judge. And Chen Mingde never hit anybody!"

"Dad, let me see it too," Chen Xuan said quietly.

Chen Jianguo handed it over. Chen Xuan read it from top to bottom, sliding his thumb beneath each line.

"What time did the police take Second Uncle?"

"Two o'clock yesterday. Five past two, Yu Changshun says."

"This box says *Time of Decision: Saturday, 7:50 a.m.* Isn't that a long time later?"

"What?" Chen Jianguo snatched the sheet back. "Almost eighteen hours after they cuffed him! That box is supposed to show when they decided to hold him. They kept him locked up all night with no decision at all!"

"Eighteen hours," Chen Xuan murmured. "He's fifty-two, Dad. Did anyone even give him water?"

"I don't know, son. I don't know."

"And this stamp says Ankang Private Clinic, 1:50 p.m. In an assault case, isn't a police doctor supposed to write the injury report?"

"One-fifty. Ten to two." Chen Jianguo repeated it like a man cracking a code. "The handcuffs went on at five past two! They wrote the injury report before the fight even happened. A private clinic writes whatever someone pays it to write. Chen Xuan, they're the ones who broke the law!"

"Wait, Dad. If they wanted to hide the eighteen hours, why does the form show the real time?"

"Don't ask me riddles right now!"

Behind the lobby glass, Wei Kun watched the two of them through the window. Someone was actually reading the timestamp he had written. He had no idea whether that would save him or ruin him.

"So what do we do now?" Chen Jianguo asked.

Chen Xuan folded the notice neatly and slipped it into his pocket.

"Dad, I need to take a quick work call."

"Now?" Chen Jianguo snapped. "Your uncle is sitting in a cell!"

"Sorry, Dad. Just one minute. I'll be right back."

He walked ten paces away and pulled out his phone.

Last night he had dialed this number for the very first time. It went straight to An Zhiyuan, head of the Detail, his hidden guard. His order then had been simple: watch, and touch nothing.

He photographed the notice and typed a short message.

*Chief An. Thank you for last night. Stop watching now and finish it. Eighteen hours is not a clerical mistake. Lay out the notice, the clinic, and the money by tonight. Keep the old tailor safe.*

The reply arrived at once: *Yes, Boss.*

On his way back, Chen Xuan bought two fried dough sticks from a street cart. He handed one to his father and bit into the other, calm and unhurried.

* * *

Inside the station, Zhong Hai sat alone at a steel table in the canteen with an untouched lunch box in front of him.

He was thirty-eight, Wei Kun's desk partner, and a decent man in every way except one. He was afraid.

His six-year-old son, Zhong Tiantian, needed heart surgery. The hospital wanted 80,000 yuan by Monday. That was more than a year of Zhong Hai's salary, and he only had 9,000.

His phone buzzed.

*Lei Bo: Director Cheng has one question. What time did Wei Kun write on the Chen notice? A 60,000 loan is waiting for a straight answer. No interest.*

Zhong Hai typed *7:50 a.m.* and hit send before his conscience could stop his thumb.

The reply came four seconds later, a thumbs-up and *Good brother.*

Zhong Hai pressed his sleeve against his mouth and sat completely still, like a man who had swallowed something bitter that refused to go down.

Down in the detention corridor, Cheng Biao stopped dead in his tracks. He spun around and slapped Lei Bo across the face.

"Seven-fifty! I told you to put Friday afternoon in that box, and you stamped it without reading a single word!"

"My fault, Director Cheng!" Lei Bo stammered. Out of reflex, he started to give an apologetic laugh, which died instantly as a red welt rose on his cheek.

Cheng Biao walked on alone to the iron bars.

"Chen Mingde. Your brother and nephew came by the lobby. That little clerk asked for paperwork. Only you can open this door. Sign."

He stepped closer to the bars.

"Your daughter. Wuhan, Room 412. She leaves the library at ten and walks down the back road past the construction site. On Monday, a letter about her father's police record goes to her university. What happens on that back road is none of my business. Sign tonight, and she never hears a word about any of this."

He tapped his toothpick against the iron bars, slow and rhythmic, like a ticking clock.

Chen Mingde's hands began to tremble between his knees.

He thought of his daughter, all alone in another city.

For a split second, the cold of the concrete cell seemed to sink straight into his chest.

Then he thought of one simple fact. His brother and his nephew had sat on a hard lobby bench for two hours, waiting for him.

The trembling slowed, and the corner of his mouth lifted into a small smile.

"I won't sign, Director Cheng. Send your letter. My daughter knows her father never laid a hand on anyone. And my nephew is still out there waiting for me."

Cheng Biao stared at him. He had broken eleven men in this corridor, and not one of them had smiled back.

Out in the hall, his nerves already frayed from Yan Tiehu vanishing without a trace the night before, he called Niu Dali and kept his voice low.

"Niu Dali. The old tailor saw too much on Friday. This afternoon, on the river road, put a van on him. Make it look like a traffic accident. And pay him a visit tonight either way."

Then he waved Lei Bo over.

"Wei Kun wrote the real hour, and he knows who told him to falsify it. Bring him a cup of tea with the powder from Ankang. All of it. And Lei Bo, if you spill a drop, you drink the next cup."

"Just to make him sleep, Director Cheng?"

"To make him whatever it makes him. A dead witness can't sign a statement."

Lei Bo carried a paper cup of jasmine tea toward the duty window in both hands, stiff as a man holding a live grenade.

At the records-room door, a bent old man in protective sleeve covers stepped out carrying a stack of files and bumped squarely into Lei Bo's wrist.

The cup flew. Tea splashed across the floor tiles, and the paper cup split open.

"Aiya! My old eyes!" Ding Shouyi dropped to his knees with a rag.

As the rag swept over the puddle, a tiny glass sample tube slipped neatly into his sleeve cover.

On the wet tiles, the spilled tea had left a thin rim of gray foam.

Lei Bo stared at it, his knees turning weak. Nothing harmless foams like that.

"Forgive an old man, Officer Lei." Ding Shouyi poured plain tea from his own thermos into a fresh cup. "Tell Wei Kun it came from you, and you still get the credit."

"From me?"

"Who else carried it over?"

It was the worst question Lei Bo had ever heard, and it was completely true. The man who carries the cup takes the blame.

He carried the plain tea to the window with a hand that refused to stay steady.

"Thank you, Lei Bo." Wei Kun looked surprised, then took a sip. "Good tea."

Lei Bo leaned back against the wall and muttered under his breath, "The old fool tripped over his own feet. What a stroke of luck."

Back inside the records room, Ding Shouyi sealed the glass tube and typed a line into his phone. *Poisoned tea meant for Wei Kun. Ordered by Cheng Biao, carried by Lei Bo, powder from Ankang. Sample secured. Wei Kun unharmed.*

* * *

Behind the bread factory sat a windowless van painted to look like a flower-delivery truck. Inside, Tang Liqun, captain of the Detail's mobile team, watched his monitor flash.

On the wall map, two hundred and fourteen green dots shifted to amber all at once. Amber meant stand by to move.

"Captain!" Pei Ruihan, his deputy, set his teacup down hard. "A message from the Boss, relayed through Chief An. It's a live order."

The van went dead quiet.

Since last night they had recorded everything and touched nothing. Now the next word had finally come.

"Read it verbatim."

"Finish it. Lay out the notice, the clinic, and the money by tonight. Keep the old tailor safe."

Tang Liqun nodded once. "Then we move. Every unit, strictly by the book. No shortcuts."

A bread truck pulled out first, followed by a laundry van, and then three plain gray sedans with city plates.

A new line scrolled across the screen from the records clerk. *Cheng Biao ordered Niu Dali to hit the old tailor with a van this afternoon on the river road. Also poisoned tea for Wei Kun, intercepted. Logged.*

Pei Ruihan spoke quietly. "That's a murder order. Do we intervene?"

Tang Liqun tapped the river road on the map, his tone as calm as a man ordering lunch.

"Bread truck at the arch by four. Our doctor from Everbright Family Clinic on the corner with her kit open. Our man on the traffic squad takes the arch beat."

"And if the van shows up early?"

"Then the bread truck gets there early."

An Zhiyuan's voice crackled over the speaker, dry and faintly amused.

"Wake everyone up. Last night he said 'touch nothing.' Today he says 'finish it.'"

What happens to a county when a man who almost never gives orders finally speaks that word?
