# Chapter 108: Dinner with the In-Laws by the Bund

The next evening, golden lanterns lit the quiet stone garden of Pine Courtyard, a private restaurant tucked one block behind the Bund. Through the wide lattice window on the second floor, the tall towers of Lujiazui and the glowing spheres of the Oriental Pearl Tower shimmered across the dark waters of the Huangpu River.

Inside the private dining room, Qin Yuhan's parents sat waiting at the round rosewood table, having flown in from Beijing earlier that Sunday afternoon.

Her father, Qin Zhengming, wore a dark gray standing-collar jacket. He carried the steady, upright posture of a longtime state industry director and sat reading a printed port briefing through wire-rimmed glasses. Beside him sat Qin Yuhan's mother, Lin Shuyin, dressed in an embroidered silk shawl and pearl earrings, her back straight with quiet Beijing poise.

When the carved wooden sliding door slid open, Qin Yuhan walked in alongside Chen Xuan.

Tonight, Chen Xuan had left his green enamel thermos tucked safely in the Maybach's cup holder. Dressed in the dark navy wool suit from Plaza 66, he looked calm, composed, and effortlessly polished.

"Dad, Mom," Qin Yuhan greeted warmly. "I hope the flight was smooth."

"Uncle, Auntie," Chen Xuan added with a relaxed, respectful smile. He set the plain paper bag beside Qin Zhengming's chair and handed a small ribboned box to Lin Shuyin. "I heard Uncle likes Wuyi tea, so I brought a cedar box a friend from an old tea shop saved for me. And these are fresh osmanthus pastries from a bakery near our neighborhood. They are still warm."

Lin Shuyin accepted the pastry box politely, though her gaze swept over Chen Xuan from his neatly combed hair down to his polished leather shoes. Compared to the brief video call last week, where he had looked half-asleep in a plain home shirt, he looked far more presentable in person. Still, her tone remained measured as everyone took their seats.

"Sit down, Chen Xuan," Lin Shuyin said after the waiter poured warm water. "Yuhan tells us you work in logistics at Hengxin."

"Yes, Auntie," Chen Xuan answered easily, resting his hands comfortably beside his teacup. "I handle shipping schedules and daily dispatch logs. It is a steady desk job, the pace suits me, and I clock out at 4:55 PM on the dot."

Lin Shuyin paused, her manicured fingers resting lightly on the tablecloth. "A junior clerk's pay is fine for a single young man starting out. Still, Yuhan runs Haiyue Group, and everyone in our circle in Beijing keeps asking about her husband. Have you considered moving into management, or letting Yuhan arrange a director position for you?"

"Mom," Qin Yuhan began softly, her brow tightening slightly. "We already talked about this on the phone. Chen Xuan likes his pace, and I do not want him dragged into corporate politics."

Chen Xuan gently patted Qin Yuhan's hand under the table to reassure her, then smiled warmly at Lin Shuyin. "Auntie, I understand why you worry. Honestly, I am a laid-back person by nature. If I became a manager, I would spend every night stuck in meetings and banquets. Keeping regular hours means I can stop by the market on my way home, steam fresh sea bass, simmer a pot of lotus root soup, and make sure Yuhan never has to eat cold takeout after a twelve-hour day."

Lin Shuyin opened her mouth to counter, then hesitated. She glanced across at her daughter and noticed the quiet, genuine softness in Qin Yuhan's eyes, along with the sleek mother-of-pearl wristwatch resting on her wrist.

Before Lin Shuyin could speak again, the waiter returned carrying a clay teapot brewed from the cedar box Chen Xuan had brought. The moment the lid lifted, a deep, roasted floral aroma drifted across the rosewood table.

Qin Zhengming, who had been silently observing his son-in-law over his glasses, lifted his small porcelain cup and took a slow sip. His hand stopped halfway back to the saucer.

The tea was impossibly smooth, carrying the rich mineral depth and clean autumn warmth of genuine twenty-year aged Wuyi rock tea. Having spent decades drinking fine tea in Beijing ministries, Qin Zhengming knew a single box of this caliber was almost impossible to find even on the private market.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Zhengming said slowly, setting his cup down with newfound curiosity. "Where exactly did you say your friend got this tea?"

"A retired tea merchant in an old alley in Pudong was clearing out his back cellar," Chen Xuan replied without missing a beat, lifting the pot to refill his father-in-law's cup. "He owed me a small favor from helping move some heavy crates last month, so he let me take a box for a couple hundred yuan. If you like it, Uncle, please take the rest back to Beijing tomorrow."

Qin Zhengming chuckled softly, convinced Chen Xuan had simply stumbled into blind luck without knowing the true value of what he held. "You have remarkable good fortune, young man. I will gladly take it."

As the dishes arrived, the conversation turned naturally to work. Qin Zhengming mentioned a stubborn bottleneck at a coastal container port where freight trucks were backing up every evening despite adding extra cranes along the docks.

Chen Xuan listened politely while using his chopsticks to pick the fine bones out of a piece of steamed river perch and placing the clean meat onto Qin Yuhan's plate.

"Uncle, I only look at small dispatch sheets," Chen Xuan said casually, "but whenever our warehouse yard gets backed up, it is rarely the cranes. Usually the customs paper check and the night-shift gate handover happen in the exact same twenty-minute window. If you stagger the shift change by half an hour and pre-clear the empty return trucks on a separate lane, the queue clears itself."

Qin Zhengming blinked, his chopsticks pausing midair. He stared at Chen Xuan for three full seconds, running the simple math through his head. The idea was so clean and practical that it cut straight to the root of a problem his engineers had argued over for two weeks.

"Not bad at all," Qin Zhengming murmured with genuine approval. "Sharp, practical thinking. Sometimes people sitting high up overlook the simplest fix on the ground."

Across the table, Lin Shuyin watched Chen Xuan quietly refill Qin Yuhan's soup bowl before touching his own food. Her stiff shoulders relaxed slightly. He really was just an unambitious logistics clerk with no grand family backing, she thought, yet he had calm manners, clear common sense, and treated her daughter with steady, unhurried care. By the time the lanterns glowed bright against the dark Bund skyline, the quiet tension at the table had melted completely into warm conversation.
