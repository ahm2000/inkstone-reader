# Chapter 108: One Text at the Harbor

Outside the clearance hall, the rain had thinned into a damp gray mist drifting off the harbor.

Chen Xuan walked to Lane Two and handed the stamped release slip up to Zhao Kang in the cab of the blue Hengxin Logistics truck.

"You got it stamped already?" Zhao Kang asked, staring at the crisp red seal. "Without paying a single yuan?"

"Everything on our sheet was legal," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Pull onto the Gate Seven scale, Master Zhao. I will walk beside the barrier."

Zhao Kang grinned, started the engine, and eased the heavy truck onto the weighbridge. The green light blinked above the gantry, confirming the cargo weight matched the manifest.

Just as the yellow barrier arm began to lift, a battered white cargo van roared out of the brokerage parking strip and swerved across the concrete exit lane. Its tires screeched on the wet asphalt, stopping sideways three meters in front of Hengxin's bumper and blocking Gate Seven completely.

Zhao Kang hit his air brakes with a sharp hiss and leaned out. "Hey! Move that van off the exit lane!"

Two burly men climbed out of the van and leaned against its doors with lazy smirks. Behind them walked Zhou Mingcheng, rolling an unlit cigarette between his fingers.

"Sorry, driver," Zhou Mingcheng called mockingly. "Our van just broke down on the exit apron. The repair truck will not arrive until tomorrow."

Zhao Kang flushed with anger. "You drove it right in front of me! That is deliberate blocking!"

"Watch your mouth," Zhou Mingcheng said coolly, fixing his stare on Chen Xuan. "You love quoting port rules, little clerk? See if there is a rule that fixes a broken van."

From the covered walkway, Qin Yuhan stepped out holding Haiyue Group's release slip. She paused with a quiet frown, her hand slipping toward her coat pocket to call the port duty office.

Then she stopped, because Chen Xuan had not even blinked.

Chen Xuan did not shout or argue. He unscrewed his scratched green thermos, took a slow sip of warm jasmine tea, and screwed the cap shut.

"Manager Zhou," Chen Xuan said evenly, "are you sure you want to leave that van parked across a customs weighbridge?"

"Very sure," Zhou Mingcheng sneered. "My uncle Zhou Desheng has sat in the port building for twelve years. If you want your truck out before noon, bow your head, apologize for embarrassing me inside, and wire the 200k fast-lane fee. Otherwise, your valves sit in the rain until Friday."

Chen Xuan nodded once, as if confirming the afternoon weather.

He reached into his windbreaker pocket, pulled out his plain black phone, and opened a single silent chat thread linked to Number 14's Qingyan desk.

His thumb moved calmly across the screen. Four words.

Gate Seven. Zhou Mingcheng.

He pressed send and slid the phone back into his pocket.

Zhou Mingcheng saw the cheap black phone case and let out a loud laugh. "Look at him! Texting his little supervisor at Hengxin Logistics! Go ahead, call your manager, call the police, call whoever you want. Let us see who dares touch Mingcheng Freight Services at Gate Seven!"

The two men by the van laughed along. Beside the cab, Zhao Kang paced anxiously, checking his watch every few seconds.

Chen Xuan simply stood by the yellow railing with his hands resting on his green thermos, watching the gray harbor cranes.

One minute passed. Two minutes. Four minutes.

Zhou Mingcheng tapped his gold watch with a smirk. "Well? Where is your rescue team, little clerk? Still on hold?"

At the five-minute mark, the heavy glass doors of the main Qingyan Port Administration Building, two hundred meters up the dock road, swung open.

No sirens sounded. No horn blared.

A tall, stern-faced man in his late forties wearing a crisp dark blue customs uniform strode straight out of the main building at a sharp pace. Behind him marched six uniformed officers from the Customs Anti-Smuggling Bureau and two plain-clothed discipline inspectors carrying sealed folders.

Every port guard along the walkway snapped straight to attention.

Zhou Mingcheng's smirk froze. His smug color faded slightly when he recognized the man in front.

It was Shen Guohua, the newly appointed chief of the Qingyan Customs Anti-Smuggling Bureau, transferred into the city two days earlier during the provincial clean-up.

Zhou Mingcheng forced a bright, tight smile and hurried forward with both hands outstretched.

"Chief Shen! What brings you down to Gate Seven in this rain?" Zhou Mingcheng called smoothly. "If there is a yard issue, my uncle Zhou Desheng can send someone right over to help."

Shen Guohua did not slow by half a step. He walked straight past Zhou Mingcheng's outstretched hands as if he were a wooden post, stopping between the blocked Hengxin truck and the white van.

"Are you Zhou Mingcheng, head of Mingcheng Freight Services?" Shen Guohua asked, his voice hard as cold iron.

"Yes, Chief Shen, that is me," Zhou Mingcheng said, his smile cracking. "My van just stalled, and we were just about to push it aside."

"Save it," Shen Guohua cut him off flatly. He nodded to the officer beside him, who opened a stamped blue folder. "Effective immediately, Mingcheng Freight Services' port brokerage license is suspended and frozen on the spot. We have verified complaints of illegal 200k fast-lane extortion, interfering at Counter Three, and deliberately blocking a cleared customs weighbridge."

Zhou Mingcheng's knees went weak. "Wait, Chief Shen, there is a misunderstanding! Let me call my uncle Zhou Desheng right now!"

"You will not reach him," Shen Guohua said coldly. "Deputy Director Zhou Desheng was escorted out of his office on the fourth floor four minutes ago by the provincial discipline team. His phone is already sealed."

Every drop of color drained from Zhou Mingcheng's face, leaving it snow-white. The unlit cigarette slipped from his shaking fingers into a puddle by his polished shoes.

"Take Zhou Mingcheng and his two men in for questioning," Shen Guohua ordered sharply. "Seal their office behind Counter Five, freeze their accounts, and call the port tow truck to clear this van off the lane right now."

"Yes, Chief!"

Within thirty seconds, two officers gripped Zhou Mingcheng by the arms. The two burly men scrambled into the white van with pale faces and drove it off the exit lane before the tow truck even arrived.

As the concrete apron cleared, Shen Guohua stepped aside and raised a crisp gloved hand, personally waving Hengxin Logistics' blue truck straight through Gate Seven.

Zhao Kang leaned out his cab window, grinning in stunned relief, and rolled cleanly past the lifted barrier.

Before turning back toward the clearance hall, Shen Guohua paused for a single heartbeat. His gaze shifted briefly to the plain man in the faded windbreaker standing quietly by the railing. Shen Guohua did not salute or speak. He gave only a tiny, almost invisible nod of deep respect, then turned swiftly away to keep Chen Xuan out of sight.

In the hall doorway, Lin Xiaodong and dozens of tired clerks let out a quiet wave of relief.

On the covered walkway, Qin Yuhan stood completely still. She had watched Chen Xuan send a short text, counted five exact minutes on her watch, and caught that fleeting glance from the Anti-Smuggling chief.

Down by the gate, Chen Xuan tucked his scratched green thermos into his canvas satchel, watched Zhao Kang's truck merge onto the outer road, and walked calmly out Gate Seven into the damp street as if nothing had happened.
