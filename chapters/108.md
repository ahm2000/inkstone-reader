# Chapter 108: Dinner with the In-Laws by the Bund

On Sunday evening, golden lanterns illuminated the quiet stone garden of Pine Courtyard, a private restaurant tucked one block behind the Bund. Through the wide lattice window on the second floor, the cool autumn twilight settled over the Huangpu River while the tall towers of Lujiazui and the glowing spheres of the Oriental Pearl Tower shimmered across the water.

At the round rosewood table sat Qin Yuhan's parents, who had flown in from Beijing earlier that afternoon.

Her father, Qin Zhengming, wore a dark gray standing-collar jacket. He carried the steady, upright posture of a longtime state industry director and sat reading a printed port briefing through wire-rimmed glasses. Beside him sat Qin Yuhan's mother, Lin Shuyin, dressed in an embroidered silk shawl and pearl earrings, her back straight with quiet Beijing pride.

When the carved wooden sliding door opened, Qin Yuhan walked in alongside Chen Xuan.

Tonight, Chen Xuan had left his green enamel thermos tucked safely in the Maybach's cup holder. Dressed in the dark navy wool suit from Plaza 66, he looked calm, composed, and effortlessly polished.

"Dad, Mom," Qin Yuhan greeted warmly. "I hope the flight was smooth."

"Uncle, Auntie," Chen Xuan added with a relaxed, respectful smile. He set a plain paper bag beside Qin Zhengming's chair and handed a small ribboned box to Lin Shuyin. "I heard Uncle likes Wuyi tea, so I brought a cedar box a friend from an old tea shop saved for me. And these are fresh osmanthus pastries from a bakery near our neighborhood. They are still warm."

Lin Shuyin accepted the pastry box politely, though her eyes swept over Chen Xuan from his neatly combed hair down to his polished shoes. She had to admit he looked far more presentable than she had imagined, yet her tone remained measured as everyone took their seats.

"Sit down, Chen Xuan," Lin Shuyin said after the waiter poured warm water. "Yuhan tells us you work in logistics at Hengxin. On the 12th floor, was it?"

"Yes, Auntie," Chen Xuan answered easily, resting his hands comfortably by his teacup. "I handle shipping schedules and daily dispatch logs. It is a steady desk job, 12,000 yuan a month, and I clock out at 4:55 PM on the dot."

Lin Shuyin paused, her manicured fingers resting lightly on the tablecloth. "12,000 yuan is fine for a single young man starting out. Still, Yuhan runs Haiyue Group, and everyone in our circle in Beijing asks about her husband. Have you considered moving into management, or letting Yuhan arrange a director position for you?"

"Mom," Qin Yuhan began softly, her brow tightening slightly. "We already talked about this. Chen Xuan likes his pace, and I do not want him dragged into corporate politics."

Chen Xuan gently patted Qin Yuhan's hand under the table to reassure her, then smiled warmly at Lin Shuyin. "Auntie, I understand why you worry. Honestly, I am a lazy person by nature. If I became a manager, I would spend every night stuck in meetings and banquets. Working my regular hours means I can stop by the market on my way home, cook braised pork ribs and hot soup, and make sure Yuhan never has to eat cold takeout after a twelve-hour day."

Lin Shuyin opened her mouth to counter, then hesitated. She glanced at her daughter and noticed the quiet, genuine softness in Qin Yuhan's eyes, along with the sleek mother-of-pearl watch resting on her wrist.

Before Lin Shuyin could speak again, the waiter returned with a clay teapot brewed from the cedar box Chen Xuan had brought. The moment the lid lifted, a deep, roasted floral aroma drifted across the rosewood table.

Qin Zhengming, who had been silently observing his son-in-law over his glasses, lifted his small porcelain cup and took a slow sip. His hand stopped halfway back to the saucer.

The tea was impossibly smooth, carrying the rich mineral depth and clean autumn warmth of genuine twenty-year aged Wuyi rock tea. Having spent decades drinking fine tea in Beijing ministries, Qin Zhengming knew a single box of this quality was almost impossible to find even on the private market.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Zhengming said slowly, setting his cup down with newfound curiosity. "Where exactly did you say your friend got this tea?"

"A retired tea merchant near Old Street was clearing out his back cellar," Chen Xuan replied without missing a beat, pouring more tea for his father-in-law. "He owed me a small favor from helping move some crates, so he let me take a box for a couple hundred yuan. If you like it, Uncle, take the rest back to Beijing tomorrow."

Qin Zhengming chuckled softly, convinced Chen Xuan had simply stumbled into blind luck without knowing what he held. "You have good fortune, young man. I will gladly take it."

As the dishes arrived, the conversation turned to work. Qin Zhengming mentioned a stubborn bottleneck at a coastal container port where freight trucks were backing up every evening despite adding extra cranes.

Chen Xuan listened politely while using his chopsticks to pick the fine bones out of a piece of steamed river perch and placing the clean meat onto Qin Yuhan's plate.

"Uncle, I only look at small dispatch sheets," Chen Xuan said casually, "but whenever our warehouse gets backed up, it is rarely the cranes. Usually the customs paper check and the night-shift gate handover happen in the same twenty-minute window. If you stagger the shift change by half an hour and pre-clear the empty return trucks on a separate lane, the queue clears itself."

Qin Zhengming blinked, his chopsticks pausing midair. He stared at Chen Xuan for three full seconds, running the simple math through his head. The idea was so clean and practical that it cut straight to the root of a problem his engineers had argued over for two weeks.

"Not bad at all," Qin Zhengming murmured with genuine approval. "Practical thinking. Sometimes people high up overlook the simplest fix on the ground."

Across the table, Lin Shuyin watched Chen Xuan quietly refill Qin Yuhan's soup bowl before touching his own food. Her stiff shoulders relaxed slightly. He really was just an unambitious 12,000-yuan clerk with no grand family backing, she thought, yet he had calm manners, sharp common sense, and treated her daughter with steady, unhurried care. By the time the lanterns glowed bright against the dark Bund skyline, the quiet tension at the table had melted completely into warm conversation.
