# Chapter 210: Tea at Qibao Old Street

At 4:55 PM sharp, Chen Xuan saved his last container dispatch sheet on the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics, shut down his monitor, and tucked his dented green metal thermos under his arm.

Before he could even reach the frosted glass exit, Hu Dewei practically vaulted out of his corner office and hurried ahead to hold the heavy door open with both hands.

"Heading home right on time, Mr. Chen? Wonderful! The December wind picked up this afternoon, so please zip up your jacket," Hu Dewei said, smiling so stiffly that his cheeks looked ready to cramp. "And please do not worry about tomorrow's route binder. I already split it evenly among three clerks!"

A few young dispatch coworkers glanced up from their desks, blinking in amused confusion at the sudden transformation of their famously grumpy manager.

"Thanks, Manager Hu," Chen Xuan replied with an easy smile. "See you tomorrow morning."

Down on the street, crisp December dusk was already settling over Shanghai. Streetlamps flickered to life along the wide avenue, casting warm amber pools across the chilly sidewalk. Parked neatly along the curb was Qin Yuhan's dark sedan, its hazard lights blinking gently.

Chen Xuan opened the passenger door and climbed inside, greeted immediately by the soft hum of the seat heater and the faint scent of jasmine. Qin Yuhan sat behind the wheel in a camel cashmere coat and a thick cream knit scarf, her hair falling loosely over her shoulders.

"You left the 38th floor early," Chen Xuan teased, buckling his seatbelt.

"Finished my last port call at 4:40 PM," Qin Yuhan said with a bright smile as she pulled smoothly into the evening traffic. "And I do not feel like going straight back to Emerald Riverside Villa yet. How about we drive west to Qibao Old Street for a walk and a pot of hot tea?"

"Sounds perfect," Chen Xuan said, leaning back comfortably.

By 6:00 PM, winter twilight had fully draped itself over Qibao Old Street in Minhang District. Strings of red lanterns glowed along the curved tiled eaves and arched across the stone railings of Puhui Tang Bridge, their warm reflections rippling across the dark, cold canal water below.

The December air was sharp and refreshing, filled with the comforting scents of sweet roasted chestnuts, steamed glutinous rice cakes, and simmering broth from the narrow alleyway kitchens.

Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan strolled hand in hand across the smooth flagstones. Finding her fingers chilly, Chen Xuan tucked her right hand together with his left deep inside his warm down coat pocket. Qin Yuhan laughed softly and leaned her shoulder briefly against his arm as they walked.

Near the foot of the stone bridge, a middle-aged vendor in a thick navy padded vest was turning glossy brown chestnuts inside a wide iron wok filled with hot black sand. Steam rose cleanly into the cold night air.

"Cold evening, young man!" the chestnut vendor called out cheerfully, grinning at Chen Xuan with the easy familiarity of a longtime neighbor. "Fresh batch just came off the fire. Grab a bag to warm your wife's hands!"

"Give us two pounds, Uncle," Chen Xuan answered with a relaxed smile, scanning the payment code on his single smartphone.

The vendor scooped a generous paper bag full of piping-hot chestnuts and tossed an extra handful on top with a friendly wink. "Take your time walking by the canal. The wind near the bridge gets brisk after 6:00 PM."

A few paces away, a young couple in puffy winter jackets paused to laugh over a photo by the stone balustrade, while an elderly auntie sweeping outside a traditional pastry shop called out a warm reminder to watch the damp steps near the water's edge. None of them looked tense, sharp, or watchful in the slightest. They were simply warm, natural neighborhood faces enjoying a quiet Shanghai winter evening, blending seamlessly into the bustling old street while keeping a silent, invisible ring of peace around Chen Xuan and his wife.

Chen Xuan cracked open a hot chestnut, peeled away the dark shell, and held the golden kernel up to Qin Yuhan's lips. She bit into it happily, her eyes crinkling in the lantern light.

"Still warm and sweet," she murmured. "You know, I still laugh whenever I think about Hu Dewei's face in my office today. I honestly thought he was going to faint right onto my carpet."

"Go easy on him," Chen Xuan chuckled, peeling another chestnut for himself. "He practically sprinted to open the 12th-floor door for me at 4:55 PM. I think he learned his lesson."

They climbed the creaking wooden stairs of a quiet riverside teahouse overlooking the canal and chose a window table. A waitress soon brought a steaming ceramic pot of Longjing green tea, two thin porcelain cups, and a small dish of toasted sesame candy.

At the corner table nearby, two retired uncles in wool caps argued good-naturedly over a wooden chessboard. One of them glanced up, caught Chen Xuan's eye, and offered a warm, grandfatherly nod as if they met here every Tuesday night. Chen Xuan nodded back with a genuine smile.

Qin Yuhan cradled her hot teacup in both hands, watching the red lanterns sway gently over the dark water outside.

"Xuan," she asked softly, "do you truly never get tired of that small desk on the 12th floor? With your eye for logistics, you could run a whole division at Haiyue if you ever wanted."

Chen Xuan poured a fresh stream of fragrant green tea into her cup and shook his head slowly. "Too many meetings, too many stiff suits, and no time to simmer winter radish soup. A quiet desk, clocking out at 4:55 PM, and sitting here eating roasted chestnuts with you under the lanterns of Qibao Old Street? There is nothing in the world I would trade this for."

Qin Yuhan's smile softened into pure warmth as she reached across the wooden table to lace her fingers through his.

Beside his teacup, Chen Xuan's single smartphone gave one brief, silent buzz. He glanced down at the screen. It was a short text from Number 1:

"Young Master, the northern port handover cleared every customs checkpoint smoothly. Let us know if you need anything this week."

Chen Xuan tapped two quick words with his thumb, "All good.", and set the phone face down beside the sesame candy. Outside the wooden lattice window, the cold December breeze rippled across the lantern-lit canal, while inside the teahouse, the night remained warm, unhurried, and completely peaceful.
