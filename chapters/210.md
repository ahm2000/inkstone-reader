# Chapter 210: Tea at Old Street

By six-thirty on Tuesday evening, the autumn twilight settled gently over the historic quarters of Jingzhou in Handong Province.

Red paper lanterns hung from the carved wooden eaves of the old courtyard houses along the stone-paved canal street. The scent of roasted chestnuts, sizzling pan-fried dumplings, and fragrant osmanthus tea drifted through the evening air, mingling with the chatter of neighborhood residents and evening strollers.

Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan walked hand in hand down the flagstone lane, dressed in casual autumn sweaters and comfortable walking shoes.

Qin Yuhan had left her executive heels and dark designer suits back at the hotel suite. She wore a soft lavender knit sweater, loose beige trousers, and a cream wool scarf wrapped loosely around her slender neck. In her right hand she held a brown paper bag of freshly roasted sugar chestnuts, still warm from the iron roasting drum.

"Here, husband, open your mouth," Qin Yuhan smiled, skillfully peeling a glossy dark shell with her fingertips and popping the golden, steaming chestnut into Chen Xuan's mouth.

Chen Xuan chewed the sweet, nutty chestnut with pure satisfaction, carrying his scratched green thermos tucked securely under his left arm. "Delicious. The street vendors in Jingzhou use real mountain honey to roast these. Much better than the ones near the Shanghai subway."

"Everything tastes better when I do not have thirty corporate emails buzzing in my pocket," Qin Yuhan sighed happily, leaning her head against his shoulder as they strolled past an ancient stone bridge spanning the quiet canal.

Earlier that afternoon, following the signing of the coastal logistics agreements, Qin Yuhan had flown to Jingzhou to attend a provincial shipping forum hosted by the Handong Provincial Government. Instead of attending the lavish evening banquet with local developers and district chiefs, she had slipped out of the hotel lobby through the side entrance, meeting Chen Xuan at the canal bridge for a quiet street dinner.

"You should have seen Hu Dewei's face this afternoon," Qin Yuhan giggled, looking up at him with dancing eyes. "When I handed Sun Haoran that 80 million contract, Hu Dewei looked like he had seen a four-star general drop from the ceiling! I thought his knees were going to give out right onto my carpet."

Chen Xuan chuckled warmly, adjusting her wool scarf to protect her throat from the evening canal breeze. "Poor Hu Dewei. When I walked back onto the twelfth floor at Hengxin this afternoon to clock out, he ran over to my desk with two bags of imported Yunnan black tea and insisted on cleaning my computer keyboard himself."

"Did you take the tea?" she asked playfully.

"Of course I took it," Chen Xuan smiled. "Free tea is free tea. Though I still prefer your roasted jasmine."

Qin Yuhan beamed, threading her fingers tightly through his. "Good boy. If you drink another woman's tea, I will confiscate your green thermos for a week."

They reached a small, traditional open-air teahouse tucked beside an ancient weeping willow at the edge of the canal. Wooden square tables and bamboo stools were set out beneath red silk lanterns, where elderly local residents sat sipping green tea and listening to a traditional Pingtan ballad played on an acoustic pipa across the water.

Chen Xuan pulled out a bamboo stool for Qin Yuhan, wiping the surface with a paper napkin before letting her sit.

"Waiter, a pot of local Biluochun green tea, and a plate of pan-fried pork buns," Chen Xuan called out to the apron-clad teahouse owner.

"Coming right up, young man!" the owner called back cheerfully.

Across the narrow canal, the gentle plucked notes of the pipa rose into the evening sky, clear and peaceful against the soft splash of oared wooden sampans moving along the water.

Qin Yuhan rested her chin in her palms, watching the lantern reflections shimmer across the dark surface of the canal. "Jingzhou is so peaceful at night. Shanghai is always in such a rush. Everyone is running after money, promotions, and bigger apartments."

"People run because they think peace is waiting for them at the finish line," Chen Xuan said softly, pouring a small cup of hot water from his thermos to rinse their porcelain teacups. "They forget that peace is something you carry with you in your pocket every day."

Qin Yuhan turned her gaze toward him, her eyes filled with boundless warmth. She reached across the small wooden table and covered his hand with her own.

"Sometimes, Xiao Xuan, I look at you and wonder how an ordinary 12,000-yuan logistics clerk can be wiser than half the billionaires in China," she murmured with a tender smile.

Chen Xuan laughed softly, turning his hand over to interlock his fingers with hers. "That is because billionaires have to worry about stock prices and board meetings. An ordinary clerk only has to worry about whether his wife has enough roasted chestnuts to eat."

Qin Yuhan giggled, leaning across the table to bump her forehead gently against his. "Then you are doing a magnificent job, my ordinary clerk."

As the hot Biluochun tea was served in small celadon cups, neither of them paid any attention to the two discreet black sedans parked quietly at the end of the stone alley fifty meters away, where four plain-clothed guards kept silent watch over the canal bridge.
