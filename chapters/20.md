# Chapter 20: Standing Down

Behind the bread factory sat a windowless van painted to look like a flower-delivery truck. All night, Number Seven and Number Nineteen had sat motionless at their locked consoles while five fully masked, mute Zero Guard operatives stood silently inside the van, stripping them of every shred of authority.

Suddenly, every Zero Guard operative turned in perfect unison, opened the rear doors without a word, and vanished into the daylight like smoke.

One second later, the crimson suspension banner vanished from the main monitor. Two hundred and fourteen green dots snapped back to life and shifted immediately to glowing amber.

*ZERO GUARD LOCKDOWN LIFTED. BLACK PINE GUARD AUTHORITY RESTORED.*

Beneath it, a second crimson line remained permanently locked at the bottom of every console, refusing to clear.

*PERMANENT ASSIGNMENT: ONE ZERO GUARD TEAM REMAINS IN SUPREME PROXIMITY. DO NOT APPROACH. DO NOT INTERFERE.*

"Number Seven." Number Nineteen shot to his feet. "The lockout just cleared. Our authority is back, and Number One just relayed a live order from the Boss."

Nobody in the van looked relaxed anymore. A night without power under the Zero Guard's eyes had burned the lazy habits out of every operative.

"Read it verbatim," Number Seven snapped, his eyes sharp as steel.

"The Zero Guard is standing down. Your authority is restored. Finish it. Lay out the notice, the clinic, and the money by tonight. Keep Yu Changshun safe."

Number Seven stood straight and looked at every operator in the van. "Last night we failed him, and the Boss had to wake the Zero Guard himself to clean up our mess. Never again. Every perimeter gets double-checked. Every threat is cut off before it reaches him. Move out, every unit, by the book. By tomorrow, nobody who touched his family will be free."

A bread truck pulled out first, followed by a laundry van, and then three plain gray sedans with city plates.

A little before eleven, a new line scrolled across the screen from the records clerk. *Cheng Biao ordered Niu Dali to hit the old tailor with a van this afternoon on the river road. Also poisoned tea for Wei Kun, intercepted. Logged.*

Number Nineteen spoke quietly. "That's a murder order. Do we intervene?"

Number Seven tapped the river road on the map, his tone as calm as a man ordering lunch.

"Bread truck at the arch by four. Our doctor from Everbright Family Clinic on the corner with her kit open. Our man on the traffic squad takes the arch beat."

"And if the van shows up early?"

"Then the bread truck gets there early."

Number One's voice crackled over the speaker, heavy with quiet awe.

"No more complacency, Number Seven. Last night we thought he woke the Zero Guard over one street thug and our own negligence. Ten seconds ago, as they withdrew, they dropped the Yunxi portion of their root-out dossier onto my desk, while routing the upper files straight to the highest sealed court in the capital. In one night, tracing the money behind Old Street tore open over one hundred trillion yuan in twenty-year sovereign asset theft across fourteen provinces, and even that hundred trillion turned out to be the smallest thing in the file. The exact same underground clearing houses funding Dong Haisheng and Huang Boren were paying foreign spies inside core ministries to smuggle naval propulsion telemetry, missile-guidance logs, national power-grid override keys, seed-bank genetics, and the names of our overseas agents out of the country. Right now, the Vice Premier and the highest elders in the capital are already sealing the upper tribunals."

Inside the command van, Number Seven and Number Nineteen went completely still, cold awe gripping both of them.

"Every overseas drop was intercepted before dawn, every offshore channel is frozen, and every border gate is locked," Number One continued, his tone turning to winter iron. "The national traitors and foreign spies are already in the Zero Guard's hands, and none of them will ever see daylight again. Our job from the Boss is Yunxi County: bury Cheng Biao, Dong Haisheng, Lu Wenzhe, and every corrupt hand on Old Street cleanly by the book. And the Zero Guard itself has decided to leave one permanent team beside him without a single word of explanation. Look at that red line on your console and never forget it. Do not disappoint him twice."

What happens to a county when a man who almost never gives orders finally speaks that word?

* * *

An hour later, across the lane from the station, the Yu Tailor Shop smelled of steam and hot iron. The door was shut.

Chen Xuan knocked twice, paused, and knocked once more.

"Yu Changshun? It's Chen Xuan, Chen Mingde's nephew. I brought soy milk."

The latch clicked. Yu Changshun's face appeared in the narrow gap, nearly as gray as his hair.

"I'm closed."

"Your sign still says open, Yu Changshun."

"I saw nothing, Chen Xuan." The old tailor looked away. "Old eyes. Too much dust on this street. Go home."

"Please, Yu Changshun. My father's cheek is bruised, and my uncle is sitting in a cell," Chen Xuan said quietly. "I sent Dad home to rest. Let me just sit for a minute. The soy milk is getting cold."

Yu Changshun hesitated, then stepped aside.

At the end of the cutting table, his granddaughter Yu Ning was coloring a rabbit purple.

She looked up at the paper bag in Chen Xuan's hand.

"Is that a fried dough stick?"

"It is." He had bought a second pair on the way. Chen Xuan tore one in half and handed it to her. "Do rabbits come in purple?"

"Mine does."

"Fair enough. Then it's the right color."

Yu Ning giggled, and the tension in Yu Changshun's shoulders eased a fraction.

She leaned forward and told Chen Xuan brightly, "Grandpa has a secret. A big man patted my head earlier. Then Grandpa hid a fat envelope in the thread drawer. He cried when he thought I was busy drawing."

Yu Changshun's face crumpled.

"Yu Ning, go wash your hands."

He sank onto his wooden stool and pressed both palms over his eyes.

His voice came out muffled. "Twenty thousand yuan. From Niu Dali. 'You saw nothing,' he told me. He knew the exact hour Yu Ning walks the river lane, and he reminded me nobody there looks out their window. Chen Xuan, she's nine years old."

Chen Xuan pulled up a stool beside him and set a warm cup of soy milk into his hands. "Yu Changshun, you don't have to play the hero. You just have to tell the truth once, on paper."

"And then what?"

"That sheet goes straight to a lawyer I know, nowhere near Hongrui. Nobody on this street has to know who wrote it. Your name stays locked away."

"They'll find out."

"Then they'll have to find the lockbox first. Leave that envelope right where it is. Don't spend a single bill. It has Niu Dali's fingerprints all over it. A bribe sitting untouched in a drawer is a witness too."

Yu Changshun stared at him.

"Young man, who are you?"

"Just an office clerk. I check forms for a living."

Chen Xuan took out a plain notebook and a pen.

Yu Changshun's hand reached out as if it had made up its mind ahead of the rest of him. He wrote slowly in neat, square characters.

*On Friday, at about 2:03 p.m., I saw Mingde Hardware's counter from my doorway. Chen Mingde never stepped out from behind it. A young man in a driver's uniform looked at Yan Tiehu and threw himself into the paint shelf. At 2:05 the police arrived and handcuffed Chen Mingde.*

He signed his name and pressed his inked thumb beside it. A tear fell onto the page and puckered one corner.

"Thank you, Yu Changshun." Chen Xuan folded the page away. "And from today on, Yu Ning won't walk to her mother's workshop alone. I'll make sure of it."

He glanced out the window toward the noodle shop across the lane. A black security camera hung under its red awning, pointed straight down the street.

"There's a second witness. A camera doesn't get tired, and it doesn't get scared. Who runs that shop?"

"Gong's Noodles. A quiet fellow, been here three years. Makes a good broth."

"A quiet man with good broth. I'll have to stop in for a bowl sometime."

Behind the lowered shutter of the noodle shop, Number Twenty-three stared at his monitor and grinned. He was the shop owner, and Number Twenty-three of the Black Pine Guard.

"He talked straight to my camera!" he told Number Fifty-six, the delivery rider who had slipped in through the back door. "A camera doesn't get tired, he said. That's our cue to use it."

"I heard, Twenty-three. I sat right next to him in the lobby. He ate a red-bean bun."

"Look at this."

Number Twenty-three slapped nine weeks of public records onto the counter.

"Eleven shopkeepers signed away their stores, every single one the morning after an 'assault' arrest. And every injury slip came from Ankang Private Clinic, which shares an accountant with Hongrui."

"Same clinic, same price." Number Fifty-six's grin vanished. "That's not a clinic. That's a vending machine."

"And the detention notice says Saturday, 7:50 a.m., in black and white. Someone inside that station refused to write the lie." Number Twenty-three tapped the screen. "The driver ran out onto the pavement before he fell, and my camera caught that. A shopkeeper has every right to hand his own footage to a lawyer, right through the front door, with a signed receipt."

Across the lane, a man in a dark suit sat at a roadside tea stall with a leather briefcase beside his chair.

Number Twenty-three nodded toward him. "Number One Hundred and Four, from the Black Pine Guard's legal desk, just happened to feel like having tea. He'll carry the footage and Yu Changshun's statement to the city tonight. If Hongrui's street crew shows up on this street after dark, the mobile team will be waiting at the corner, and the Boss will say how far it goes."

Number Fifty-six nodded. "I'll bring the buns."
