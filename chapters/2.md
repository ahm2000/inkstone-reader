# Chapter 2: The Shaking Voice

Chen Xuan stepped out of Hengxin Tower onto Binjiang Avenue as dusk settled over Qingyan. Commuters hurried toward the subway stairs against the evening chill.

He adjusted his canvas bag beside a stone planter. His plan for the night was simple: buy half a pound of lean pork ribs, simmer lotus root soup, and read two chapters of a paperback history of maritime trade before ten. Three years of that quiet rhythm had taught him to value ordinary nights.

The smartphone in his left pocket vibrated twice.

Expecting a grocery reminder, Chen Xuan took it out and stopped. The screen showed three consecutive voice messages from his mother, Liu Meifang, stamped forty seconds apart. The last was forty-eight seconds long.

Liu Meifang almost never sent voice messages on workdays. Even on weekends she texted brief lines asking if he had eaten, reluctant to disturb his job. Chen Xuan paused half a second before raising the speaker to his ear.

The first message opened with a ragged breath. "Chen Xuan, are you off work yet?" Liu Meifang's voice shook so badly her words broke apart. "Something terrible happened to your Second Uncle today. At two o'clock, men from Old Street Police Station walked into Mingde Hardware and put handcuffs on Chen Mingde in front of the whole street!"

Chen Xuan's fingers tightened around the phone. His posture stayed still, yet all warmth faded from his eyes.

He tapped the second message.

"They claimed your Second Uncle beat a demolition foreman from Hongrui Real Estate with a steel pipe," Liu Meifang sobbed in disbelief and terror. "Chen Xuan, you know Chen Mingde! He has never even killed a chicken in his whole life! Two thugs walked into his shop at noon, smashed his glass counter, and yelled at him to sign the relocation slip. When he pulled your Second Aunt Wu Lanying away from the glass, one threw himself onto the floor screaming his ribs were broken!"

Chen Xuan's breath did not quicken. His gaze dropped to the stone tiles, going completely still.

The third message played immediately, carrying the clatter of a porcelain cup.

"Your Dad heard and ran straight to Old Street Station at three," Liu Meifang choked out. "Chen Jianguo stood outside the iron gate begging them to pass Chen Mingde his blood pressure medicine, and the duty officer shouted at him to get lost before they locked him up for obstructing official business too. Your Second Aunt Wu Lanying fainted outside the shop. Chen Xuan, we do not know anyone in Yunxi County with power. What are we going to do?"

The recording ended. Taxi horns blared and clerks laughed past with milk tea, oblivious to the motionless man beside the planter.

Chen Xuan stared down at the dark glass.

Second Uncle Chen Mingde did not belong near steel pipes or handcuffs. He was fifty-four, slight in the shoulders, with knuckles stained from sorting brass screws and bicycle chains at Mingde Hardware. He bumped his knee against a wooden stool in his own shop and muttered an apology to the stool out of habit.

When Chen Xuan was seven and coughed through half a summer fever, Chen Mingde had closed his shop for a full Saturday, borrowed a bamboo rod, and sat beside Yunxi Creek for six straight hours without catching a fish, just so the boy could watch the red bobber float. When rain rolled in at dusk, he draped his only dry denim jacket over Chen Xuan's head and walked two miles home soaked to the bone, smiling so the child would not feel guilty.

Today, someone in Yunxi County had put iron handcuffs on that man in front of his neighbors and shouted Chen Jianguo away from a police station gate.

Something shifted deep inside Chen Xuan's chest. There was no loud rage, no trembling hands, no heat in his cheeks. Instead, a vast, icy stillness settled over him, immovable as winter granite. For three years he had laid down his weight and lived as a quiet clerk. Now a stray hand had reached straight toward his own hearth.

Chen Xuan exhaled slowly and dialed his mother.

She picked up before the first ring finished. "Chen Xuan!" Liu Meifang gasped, her breath wet and ragged. "Did you hear my messages?"

"Mom, I heard every word," Chen Xuan said. His voice was warm, steady, and unhurried, immediately slowing her frantic breathing. "Sit down and drink warm water. Do not cry."

"How can I not cry?" Liu Meifang whispered, sniffling hard. "Chen Mingde has high blood pressure and has never spent a night away from home in thirty years. Your Dad is pacing in the courtyard saying he will go back to Old Street Station tonight and kneel at the gate if he has to."

In the background came the scrape of a chair and Chen Jianguo's hoarse voice muttering about finding the deputy director.

"Listen carefully, Mom," Chen Xuan said, gentle yet threaded with unmistakable steel. "Do not let Dad go back to the police station. Tell Chen Jianguo to lock the front door, boil hot ginger soup for Second Aunt Wu Lanying, and stay inside. Neither Dad nor anyone in our family is to step foot near Old Street Station tonight or tomorrow. Promise me."

Liu Meifang hesitated, her voice dropping to a frightened whisper. "Your Dad says if nobody from our family begs them, Hongrui Real Estate will frame your Second Uncle into prison before morning. Who else in Yunxi will speak for him?"

"I will handle it," Chen Xuan replied simply. "I have left the office and I am coming home right now. Tell Dad that going to that gate only gives them another chance to bully him. I will be in Yunxi before midnight. Tell him to wait at home and not worry."

"Before midnight?" Liu Meifang sounded startled through her tears. "Do not lie to comfort me! The last highway coach leaves at six ten, and the mountain road takes four hours in the dark! Do not rush and get hurt!"

"I am not taking the bus, Mom," Chen Xuan said calmly. "A friend from work has a fast ride heading toward Lanshui tonight, and I am catching it now. How is Second Aunt Wu Lanying?"

"She woke up twenty minutes ago," Liu Meifang murmured, steadying at his certainty. "She is lying on our bed shaking and blaming herself for arguing about the counter. Your Dad is pouring her hot water."

"Good. Stay beside her, keep Dad indoors, and do not open the gate for strangers," Chen Xuan said. "If anyone from Hongrui Real Estate knocks, do not argue and do not step outside. Wait for me."

"Alright," Liu Meifang breathed, the terror easing slightly. "I will hold your Dad back. Be careful."

He waited until her breathing smoothed out before ending the call.

Chen Xuan pocketed his ordinary phone, then drew a second device from his jacket lining. It was matte black, heavier than a commercial handset, with no logo and no icons.

His thumb pressed the side sensor. A bare green terminal line blinked awake.

Chen Xuan typed three plain words, hit send, and returned the black phone to his jacket.

*Cloud Gate. Yunxi.*

Turning from the subway crowd, he walked calmly toward the side lane behind Qingyan South Park.
