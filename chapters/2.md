# Chapter 2: The Shaking Voice

Chen Xuan stepped out of Hengxin Tower onto Binjiang Avenue as evening settled over Shanghai. Office workers hurried toward the subway.

He adjusted his canvas bag beside a stone planter. His plan for the night was simple: buy lean pork ribs, cook lotus root soup, and read two chapters of a paperback on sea trade before ten. Three years of this routine had taught him to enjoy ordinary nights.

The phone in his left pocket buzzed twice.

Expecting a store alert, Chen Xuan pulled it out and froze. Three voice messages from his mother, Liu Meifang, had arrived forty seconds apart. The last was forty-eight seconds long.

Liu Meifang almost never sent voice messages on workdays, and on weekends sent only short texts asking if he had eaten. Chen Xuan raised the phone to his ear.

The first message opened with a ragged breath. "Chen Xuan, are you off work yet?" Liu Meifang's voice shook so hard her words broke. "Something terrible happened to your Second Uncle today. At two o'clock, men from Old Street Police Station walked into Mingde Hardware and handcuffed Chen Mingde in front of the whole street!"

Chen Xuan's fingers tightened on the phone. He did not move, yet all warmth faded from his eyes.

He tapped the second message.

"They claimed your Second Uncle beat a site foreman from Hongrui Real Estate with a steel pipe," Liu Meifang sobbed in fear and disbelief. "Chen Xuan, you know Chen Mingde! He has never even killed a chicken! Two thugs walked into his shop at noon, smashed his glass counter, and yelled at him to sign the move-out paper. When he pulled your Second Aunt Wu Lanying away from the glass, one shoved him hard into the shelves!"

Chen Xuan's breathing stayed slow and steady. His eyes dropped to the pavement, completely still.

The third message played right away, carrying the clatter of a teacup.

"Your Dad heard and ran to Old Street Station at three," Liu Meifang choked out. "Chen Jianguo stood outside the iron gate begging them to pass Chen Mingde his blood pressure pills. The officer yelled at him to get lost before they locked him up too. Your Second Aunt Wu Lanying fainted outside the shop. Chen Xuan, we do not know anyone in Yunxi County with power. What are we going to do?"

The message ended. Taxi horns honked and clerks walked past laughing, paying no mind to the still man beside the planter.

Chen Xuan stared down at the dark screen.

Second Uncle Chen Mingde did not belong near steel pipes or handcuffs. He was fifty-four, thin in the shoulders, with fingers stained from sorting brass screws and bike chains at Mingde Hardware. When he bumped his knee against a stool, he muttered sorry to the stool out of habit.

When Chen Xuan was seven with a bad summer cough, Chen Mingde closed his shop for a whole Saturday. He borrowed a bamboo rod and sat beside Yunxi Creek for six hours without catching a fish, just so the boy could watch the red float bob. When rain poured at dusk, he put his only dry denim jacket over Chen Xuan's head and walked three kilometers home soaked, smiling so the boy would not feel bad.

Today, someone in Yunxi County had put handcuffs on that man in front of his neighbors and shouted Chen Jianguo away from a police gate.

Something shifted inside Chen Xuan's chest. There was no loud anger, no shaking hands. A deep, cold stillness settled over him, hard as stone. For three years, he had set aside his old weight to live as a quiet clerk. Now a careless hand had reached straight into his home.

Chen Xuan exhaled slowly and called his mother.

She answered on the first ring. "Chen Xuan!" Liu Meifang gasped, breathless. "Did you hear my messages?"

"Mom, I heard every word," Chen Xuan said, his voice warm and steady, slowing her breathing at once. "Sit down and drink warm water. Do not cry."

"How can I not cry?" Liu Meifang whispered, sniffling. "Chen Mingde has high blood pressure and has never spent a night away from home in thirty years. Your Dad is pacing in the yard saying he will go back to Old Street Station tonight and kneel at the gate if he has to."

In the background came a scraping chair and Chen Jianguo's hoarse voice talking about finding the deputy station chief.

"Listen carefully, Mom," Chen Xuan said, gentle yet carrying steel. "Do not let Dad go back to the police station. Tell Chen Jianguo to lock the front door, boil ginger soup for Second Aunt Wu Lanying, and stay inside. Nobody in our family goes near Old Street Station tonight or tomorrow. Promise me."

Liu Meifang hesitated in a frightened whisper. "Your Dad says if nobody begs them, Hongrui Real Estate will frame your Second Uncle and send him to prison before morning. Who else in Yunxi will speak for him?"

"I will handle it," Chen Xuan replied simply. "I left work and I am coming home right now. Going to that gate only gives them another chance to bully Dad. I will be in Yunxi before midnight. Tell him to wait at home."

"Before midnight?" Liu Meifang sounded startled through her tears. "Do not lie to comfort me! The last highway bus leaves at 6:10, and the mountain road takes four hours in the dark! Do not rush and get hurt!"

"I am not taking the bus, Mom," Chen Xuan said calmly. "A friend from work has a fast ride heading to Jingzhou City tonight. How is Second Aunt Wu Lanying?"

"She woke up twenty minutes ago," Liu Meifang murmured, calmer now. "She is on our bed shaking and blaming herself for arguing about the counter. Your Dad is pouring her hot water."

"Good. Stay beside her, keep Dad inside, and do not open the gate for strangers," Chen Xuan said. "If anyone from Hongrui Real Estate knocks, do not argue and do not step out. Wait for me."

"Alright," Liu Meifang breathed, panic easing. "I will hold your Dad back. Be careful."

He waited until her breathing steadied before hanging up.

Chen Xuan pocketed his ordinary phone and pulled a second one from inside his jacket lining. Flat black, heavier than normal, with no brand and no icons.

His thumb pressed the side sensor. A plain green text line blinked open.

Chen Xuan typed three words, hit send, and slipped the black phone away.

*Cloud Gate. Yunxi.*

Turning from the subway crowd, he walked calmly toward the side lane behind Shanghai Century Park.
