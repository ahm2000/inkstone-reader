# Chapter 2: Old Street

Mingde Hardware on Yunxi County's Old Street smelled of machine oil, hemp rope and old wood.

Chen Mingde could walk behind his counter with his eyes closed and still find any screw a customer asked for in three seconds.

Behind him stood a wall of small wooden drawers, each one labeled in his neat square handwriting: M4, M5, flat head, brass. Every Spring Festival, little Chen Xuan used to sit on the high stool by the counter and read those labels out loud until he fell asleep.

Today, a stranger sat on that stool.

For nine days in a row, four men had lounged in the shop every afternoon. They never shouted, and they never touched a shelf. They crossed their legs, blew smoke at the ceiling and stared at the old shopkeeper without blinking.

Regular customers took one look through the doorway and hurried across the street.

"Old Chen."

At last, the man with the thick gold chain stood up. Dong Haisheng, boss of Hongrui Real Estate, had a smooth face and a polite smile that never reached his eyes.

"It is one-thirty now," he said softly. "I have waited nine days, and even my patience has an end."

"I'm sorry, Boss Dong." Chen Mingde gripped the edge of the counter. "I can't sign."

"Four hundred fifty-six thousand yuan, wired within a week," Dong Haisheng said. "Plenty of men on this street were glad to get it. Why are you so different?"

"But this shop is a hundred twenty square meters, on the corner!" Chen Mingde's voice shook. "A shop like mine on Old Street is worth nearly a million!"

"A thing is only worth what somebody will pay for it, Old Chen." Dong Haisheng's smile did not move at all.

To put it simply, that price was the whole trick. Hongrui called it relocation pay, but it was less than half of what the shop would fetch on the open market. The company could name any number it liked, because Old Street was coming down for a new mall, and a shopkeeper who stayed behind would soon be the last man standing in the rubble.

Eleven owners had already signed, and only three were left.

Silence.

Chen Mingde's heart pounded against his ribs.

He was not a brave man, and he knew it. His wife teased him that he could not win a fight against a chicken. But this shop was twenty-two years of his life! It had paid for his daughter's university and kept the family fed without begging anyone for a single yuan.

And four hundred fifty-six thousand yuan could not even buy another storefront in Yunxi County.

Once he signed, the family's living was gone forever!

He could not do it, and in his heart he knew he never would.

"My wife and I talked it over last night," Chen Mingde said quietly. "We will not sign."

Dong Haisheng kept smiling, but inside he was counting.

Eleven owners had given in, most of them by the third day, but this one had held out for nine!

Why? Was he holding out for a higher price? Or had somebody told him to hold?

It did not matter. The mall's main entrance had to stand on this exact corner, and one stubborn old man was holding up the whole plan.

So today it would end.

"Then I'm afraid we have a problem," Dong Haisheng sighed, and gave the smallest nod toward the door.

A young man in a neat driver's uniform had been standing quietly beside the stacked paint cans. He looked at Chen Mingde, then at his boss, and swallowed hard.

The next moment, he threw himself sideways into the metal paint shelf!

Crash!

Cans clattered and rolled across the floor. The driver landed on his back, clutched his shoulder and howled loud enough to wake the whole lane.

"He hit me! Help! This crazy old man attacked me!"

Chen Mingde stood frozen behind the counter, still holding a cardboard box of steel washers in both hands.

He had not moved an inch!

Was this some kind of sick joke?

Before he could shout that he had touched no one, two uniformed police officers marched in through the front door.

It was five past two, sharp.

So fast, Chen Mingde thought, and a chill ran down his back. They had not even run, so they must have been waiting outside!

"Chen Mingde?" The taller officer snapped open a pair of silver handcuffs. "You are suspected of assault and injuring another person. Come with us!"

"I never laid a finger on him!" Chen Mingde cried. "Ask anyone out there! Ask my neighbors!"

"Save your story for the station."

"Please, let me call my wife first!"

"At the station."

Click.

The steel bit cold around his wrists.

Outside, a hushed crowd had already gathered along Old Street, and in the doorway stood Dong Haisheng, smiling.

"Such a shame, Old Chen," he said gently. "I did tell you we might have a problem."

"Old Chen never even stepped out from behind his counter!"

"Shh! Keep your voice down. That is Boss Dong from Hongrui..."

"What can a plain shopkeeper do against people like that?"

"If he had signed like everyone else, none of us would be standing here gawking!"

"Eleven others managed it. What makes him so special?"

"Poor Old Chen..."

"Look at Boss Dong. He is not even surprised."

By the tailor's doorway, Old Yu shook his gray head and could not meet his friend's eyes. Across the lane, Meng Xiaofang from the steamed-bun stall clapped a hand over her mouth.

"Somebody has to tell his wife," she whispered.

Not one of them dared to step forward.

Don't shout, Chen Mingde told himself, and he held his back straight inside the patrol car. His wife would hear this in ten minutes, and she already had enough to carry.

Through the rear window, he watched his hand-painted sign shrink away: MINGDE HARDWARE, in red letters he had brushed on himself.

Then he thought of seven-year-old Chen Xuan yanking too hard on a bamboo fishing pole by the river.

"Don't pull back when the fish pulls," he had told the boy. "Wait. Let it wear itself out."

He could wait. If an old shopkeeper knew one thing, it was how to wait.

Back on the street, Old Yu pulled out his phone and found the number of his friend's wife.

His thumb hung above it and would not press.

How do you tell a woman a thing like that?
