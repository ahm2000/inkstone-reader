# Chapter 2: The Shaking Voice

Stepping through the revolving glass doors of Hengxin Tower, Chen Xuan met the crisp October twilight of Qingyan head-on. A cool wind smelling of damp asphalt, roasted chestnuts, and river mist moved down Binjiang Avenue. Across the street, office workers poured out of twin granite towers toward the subway stairs, their dark coats blending into a single hurried tide beneath the rising amber streetlights.

Chen Xuan adjusted the canvas strap on his shoulder and paused beside the stone planter near the south steps. He had planned a simple evening: pick up half a pound of lean pork ribs from the neighborhood market, simmer lotus root soup on a low flame, and read two chapters of a paperback history of maritime trade before turning in at ten. Three years of living by that small, disciplined rhythm had made him genuinely fond of its stillness.

The ordinary smartphone in his left trouser pocket vibrated twice.

Chen Xuan pulled it out, expecting a property management notice or a grocery app reminder. Instead, the screen showed three consecutive voice messages from his mother, Liu Meifang, stamped forty seconds apart. The last one was forty-eight seconds long.

Liu Meifang almost never sent voice messages on workdays. Even on weekends, she texted short, careful lines asking whether he had eaten or whether Qingyan had turned cold, always afraid of interrupting his job.

Chen Xuan paused half a second before raising the speaker to his ear.

The first message opened with wind and a sharp, unsteady breath. "Chen Xuan, are you off work yet?" Liu Meifang's voice shook so badly her syllables broke apart. "Something terrible happened to your Second Uncle today. At two o'clock this afternoon, men from Old Street Police Station came into Mingde Hardware and put handcuffs on Chen Mingde right in front of the whole street!"

Chen Xuan's fingers tightened slightly around the phone. His posture did not shift, yet the loose warmth of the autumn evening seemed to recede from his shoulders.

He tapped the second message.

"They claimed your Second Uncle beat a demolition foreman from Hongrui Real Estate with a steel pipe," Liu Meifang sobbed in disbelief and terror. "Chen Xuan, you know Chen Mingde! Your Second Uncle has never even killed a chicken in his whole life! Two thugs walked into his shop at noon, smashed his glass counter, and when he pulled your Second Aunt Wu Lanying out of the way, one of them threw himself onto the floor screaming that his ribs were broken!"

The third message played immediately, carrying the faint clatter of a porcelain cup against a kitchen table.

"Your Dad heard and ran straight to Old Street Station at three o'clock," Liu Meifang choked out. "Chen Jianguo stood outside the iron gate begging them to let him pass Chen Mingde his blood pressure medicine, and the officer on duty shouted at him to get lost before they locked him up for obstructing official business too. Your Second Aunt Wu Lanying fainted on the pavement outside the shop. Chen Xuan, we do not know anyone in Yunxi County with power. What are we going to do?"

The recording ended. The street noise of Qingyan rushed back into Chen Xuan's ears: an impatient taxi horn, two young clerks laughing past with milk tea, a distant commuter train crossing the river bridge.

Chen Xuan stood motionless beside the stone planter, staring down at the dark glass of his phone.

In his memory, Second Uncle Chen Mingde was not a man who belonged anywhere near steel pipes or iron handcuffs. Chen Mingde was fifty-four, slight in the shoulders, with soft hands stained along the knuckles from sorting brass screws and bicycle chains at Mingde Hardware. He was the sort of man who accidentally bumped his knee against a wooden stool in his own shop and muttered an apology to the stool out of habit.

When Chen Xuan was seven and had spent a summer coughing from a stubborn fever, Chen Mingde had closed his hardware shop for a full Saturday, borrowed a bamboo rod, and sat beside Yunxi Creek for six straight hours without catching a single fish, simply because young Chen Xuan wanted to watch the red bobber float. When a rain squall rolled in at dusk, Chen Mingde had draped his only dry denim jacket over the boy's head and walked two miles home soaked to the bone, smiling the entire way so the child would not feel guilty.

Today, someone in Yunxi County had put iron handcuffs on that man in front of his neighbors and shouted Chen Jianguo away from a police station gate.

Something shifted deep inside Chen Xuan's chest. There was no loud rage, no trembling hands, no heat rushing to his cheeks. Instead, a vast, icy stillness settled over him, heavy and immovable as winter granite beneath a frozen sea. It was the quiet of a sovereign who had laid down his crown for three peaceful years, only to find a stray hand reaching toward his own hearth.

Chen Xuan exhaled slowly, letting cold clarity sharpen every edge of his mind, and dialed his mother.

The call connected before the first ring finished. "Chen Xuan!" Liu Meifang cried, her breath ragged. "Did you hear my messages?"

"Mom, I heard every word," Chen Xuan said. His voice was warm, steady, and unhurried, carrying a quiet strength that immediately slowed the frantic breathing on the other end. "Take a deep breath for me. Sit down and drink warm water. Do not cry."

"How can I not cry?" Liu Meifang whispered, sniffling hard. "Chen Mingde has high blood pressure, and he has never spent a night away from home in thirty years. Your Dad is pacing in the courtyard right now saying he wants to go back to Old Street Station tonight to beg the deputy director."

"Listen to me carefully, Mom," Chen Xuan said, gentle yet threaded with unmistakable steel. "Do not let Dad go back to the police station. Tell Chen Jianguo to lock the front door, make hot ginger soup for Second Aunt Wu Lanying, and stay inside. Neither Dad nor anyone in our family is to step foot near Old Street Station tonight or tomorrow. Promise me."

"But if your Dad does not beg them, who will speak for your Second Uncle?"

"I will handle it," Chen Xuan replied simply. "I have already left the office, and I am on my way home right now. I will be in Yunxi before midnight. Tell Dad to wait at home and not worry about a single thing."

"Before midnight?" Liu Meifang sounded startled through her tears. "Chen Xuan, the last highway coach to Yunxi leaves at six ten, and the mountain road takes four hours in the dark! Do not rush and get hurt!"

"I am not taking the bus, Mom. A friend from work has a fast ride heading toward Lanshui, and I am catching it now. Stay with Second Aunt Wu Lanying, keep Dad indoors, and leave the rest to me."

He waited until his mother's breathing steadied before ending the call.

Chen Xuan slid the ordinary smartphone into his left pocket, then drew a second device from the inner lining of his gray jacket. It was matte black, slightly heavier than a commercial handset, with no brand logo and no consumer icons.

His thumb pressed the side sensor. A bare green terminal line blinked to life.

Chen Xuan typed three plain words, hit send, and returned the black phone to his jacket.

*Cloud Gate. Yunxi.*

Then he turned away from the crowded subway stairs and walked calmly toward the quiet side lane behind Qingyan South Park.
