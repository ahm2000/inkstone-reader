# Chapter 2: The 6:10 Bus

The bus station smelled of fried dough and diesel.

Chen Xuan bought a thirty-two-yuan ticket and stood at Gate Six, out of the wind.

Then his phone buzzed with a voice message from his mother.

Liu Meifang, his mother, had the loudest voice in the family. This time it was shaking.

"Xuan, listen, and don't panic. The police took your Second Uncle away this afternoon. At two o'clock, out of his own shop, in handcuffs, in front of the whole street. Your father ran to the station and they would not let him in. They say he beat somebody up. Your uncle would not hurt a fly!"

Her voice broke.

"Come home as fast as you can."

Chen Xuan's heart dropped. The phone almost slid out of his cold fingers.

He played it again.

His Second Uncle, Chen Mingde, had run a small hardware shop on Yunxi's Old Street for over twenty years. He said sorry to chairs when he bumped them. When Chen Xuan was seven, this uncle had sat beside him for six hours with a fishing rod and never once said that nothing was biting.

Now people said that man had beaten someone in the middle of the day.

His throat tightened and his eyes went hot. Under the fear, something cold and steady settled in his chest. It felt heavier than anger.

He typed slowly with his thumb, because his hand was not steady. *Mom, I'm coming. I'm on the 6:10 bus. Please don't cry. Eat something. I'll be there soon.*

On the bus he took a window seat on the right. A plain middle-aged man in a faded gray jacket got on last, sat three rows back, and closed his eyes like any tired worker.

Chen Xuan ate his bun and watched the city lights slide past. He thought about his uncle.

Three rows behind him, the gray jacket opened one sharp eye. He had no name anyone knew, only a number: Number Nine of the Black Pine Guard. He tapped three words into a phone.

Somewhere ahead, inside a windowless van painted like a flower-delivery truck, Number Seven read them off his screen. *The Boss is on the bus.*

The Boss was Chen Xuan. The Black Pine Guard was a secret organization that watched over him day and night. Inside it, nobody knew another member's real name or face, and that included the head of the organization. There were only numbers. The van, the gray jacket, even the open elevator at work belonged to them.

Above the captain hung a wall map glowing with two hundred and fourteen green dots. Each dot was one of their people, in vans, on rooftops, in a bakery, on a quiet airfield. Chen Xuan knew a few of them and had no idea how many there were. They moved only when he gave an order. He had given none.

Every one of them was waiting for one man, and that man was eating a red-bean bun.

"Any order from the Boss?" his deputy asked.

"None."

"His uncle was arrested four hours ago, Number Seven. If he says one word, what happens?"

"Then every dot on this map moves, and nothing in this province sleeps tonight." Number Seven did not look away.

"So pray he stays calm."

* * *

Chen Jianguo, Chen Xuan's father, was waiting at the Yunxi bus station on his old electric scooter. He said nothing on the ten-minute ride home, and he gripped the handlebars too tight.

In Building 2 of Fuxing New Village, Liu Meifang's voice reached the stairs long before she did.

"Is that him? Jianguo, is that our son? Look how thin he is. Does that company not feed him?"

Chen Xuan ran up the last stairs with his green thermos, his father puffing behind him.

At the sixth floor his mother pulled him inside by the sleeve.

"Mom." His voice was not steady. "Is Second Uncle okay? Did anybody hurt him?"

"Don't ask me, you boy. I don't know anything. Sit. Eat first."

On the sofa his Second Aunt, Wu Lanying, was holding a wet towel to her face. Her eyes were red and swollen.

"Little Xuan," she said. "Your uncle never hit anyone in his whole life."

She grabbed his hand with both of hers and began to cry.

Chen Xuan's eyes went hot. He did not know what to say, so he held on. He cried a little too, like a boy.

Chen Hao, seventeen, sat at the dining table. "They took him in handcuffs, Big brother. I saw it from the bus."

Chen Yuan, his sister, sounded thin and fast. "I called six people. Nobody picks up. Brother, what do we do?"

"Stay out of it, Xuan," Liu Meifang said, wiping her eyes. "You are a clerk."

Chen Jianguo sat down heavily. Nobody spoke.

Then he cleared his throat. He had a habit. When life got hard, he made up an old saying and pretended it was famous.

"Don't worry," he said. "Everyone knows a slow ox still gets to the field. Mingde will be home soon."

"Dad." Chen Yuan was nearly crying. "That's not a real saying. You made it up."

"I know." His voice cracked. "But if I don't say something, I'll cry."

Wu Lanying laughed a little. Then she cried again.

"Nobody comes near us now," she whispered. "Only one person came this week. Wang Lei, from Xuan's class. He's poor now. He drives a delivery tricycle. He bought one hinge and paid in small crumpled bills, just to give us some business."

Chen Xuan went still.

In senior year, his meal card had run empty. Wang Lei had put eight yuan in his hand every day for a month. He had never mentioned it once.

Chen Xuan remembered every yuan. His chest hurt.

"Second Aunt." Chen Xuan's voice was small. "Is Second Uncle's shop locked?"

"The police took him so fast," she said. "Nobody closed anything. The shop has been open since two o'clock. Anyone can walk in."

Chen Jianguo stood up. "I'll go and lock it."

"I'll come with you, Dad."

"You stay home, Xuan. This is grown-up business."

"Please, Dad. I can't sit here." His eyes were wet again. "I have to do something."

Chen Jianguo looked at his son's face. Then he gave in.

"Stay behind me, and touch nothing."

Wu Lanying took a small brass key from her pocket. Her hand shook so hard that she dropped it.

Chen Xuan picked it up.

"The back door," she said. "The front shutter has been broken since Thursday."

Liu Meifang pushed a folded blanket and a small bottle of pills into his arms. "Mingde's heart medicine. The cells are cold. Give them to someone who will take them in."

She twisted her apron in her hands. "Come back soon, you boy. I made your favorite ribs. I'll keep them warm."
