# Chapter 2: Old Street

Yunxi County, Old Street, Mingde Hardware. Friday, 1:30 p.m.

The little shop smelled of machine oil, hemp rope, and old wood. Chen Mingde could walk behind his counter with his eyes closed and find any screw a customer asked for in three seconds flat.

Behind him stood a wall of small wooden drawers, every one labeled in his neat square handwriting: M4, M5, M6, flat head, brass. Every Spring Festival when Chen Xuan was small, the boy used to perch on the high stool by the counter and read those labels aloud until he nodded off.

Today, a stranger sat on that stool.

For nine straight days, four men had lounged in his shop every afternoon. They didn't shout or smash a single shelf. They just crossed their legs, blew thick cigarette smoke into the air, and stared at him without blinking. Regular customers took one look through the doorway and hurried across the street.

They looked like men paid to sit until an old shopkeeper broke.

"Old Chen."

At last, the man with the thick gold chain stood up. Dong Haisheng, boss of Hongrui Real Estate, had a smooth face and a polite smile that never reached his eyes. "It's one-thirty. I've waited nine days. Even my patience runs out eventually."

"I'm sorry, Boss Dong," Chen Mingde said, gripping the edge of the counter. "I can't sign."

"Four hundred and fifty-six thousand yuan. Cash, wired within a week."

"Boss Dong, my shop is a hundred and twenty square meters on the corner! On Old Street, a shop like mine is worth nearly a million!"

"I know what you think it's worth." Dong Haisheng's smile didn't budge an inch. "Old Chen, a thing is only worth what somebody will pay for it."

Chen Mingde fell silent, his heart hammering hard against his ribs.

He wasn't a brave man, and he knew it. His wife teased him that he couldn't win a fight against a chicken. Still, this shop was twenty-two years of his life! It had paid his daughter's university tuition and kept the family fed without begging anyone for a yuan. At normal market prices, his corner shop was worth nearly one million yuan. Dong Haisheng was offering less than half. In Yunxi County, 456,000 yuan couldn't even buy a replacement storefront, let alone keep his family going. Once he signed, their livelihood was gone forever!

He couldn't do it. He simply couldn't.

"Then I'm afraid we have a problem," Dong Haisheng sighed, giving a tiny nod toward the door.

A young man in a neat driver's uniform had been standing quietly beside the stacked paint cans. He looked at Chen Mingde, glanced back at Dong Haisheng, and swallowed hard.

The next instant, the driver threw himself sideways straight into the metal paint shelf!

Crash!

Paint cans clattered and rolled all across the floor. The driver hit the concrete on his back, clutched his shoulder, and howled loud enough to wake the entire lane!

"He hit me! Help! This crazy old man attacked me!"

Chen Mingde froze behind the counter, still holding a cardboard box of steel washers in both hands.

He hadn't moved an inch! Was this some kind of sick joke? Before he could shout that he hadn't touched anyone, two uniformed police officers walked briskly through the front door.

It was 2:05 p.m. sharp.

So fast, Chen Mingde thought, a chill rushing down his spine. They didn't even have to run. They'd been waiting outside!

"Chen Mingde?" the taller officer barked, snapping open silver handcuffs. "You're suspected of assault and injuring another person. Come with us!"

"I never laid a finger on him!" Chen Mingde cried, his voice shaking. "Ask anyone out there! Ask my neighbors!"

"Save your story for the station."

The steel cuffs bit cold around his wrists. Outside, a hushed crowd had already gathered along Old Street.

"Wait, Old Chen didn't even step out from behind his counter!" someone muttered under their breath.

"Shh! Keep your voice down! That's Boss Dong from Hongrui Real Estate."

"What can an ordinary shopkeeper do against people like that?"

"If he'd just signed like everyone else, none of us would be standing here gawking," someone else muttered, not quite under their breath. "Eleven people managed it. What makes him so special?"

By the tailor's doorway, Old Yu shook his gray head bitterly, unable to meet his friend's eyes. Across the lane, Meng Xiaofang from the steamed-bun stall clapped a hand over her mouth. Nobody dared step forward.

Don't shout, Chen Mingde told himself, holding his back straight inside the patrol car. His wife would hear about this in ten minutes, and she'd already have enough to carry.

Through the rear window, he watched his hand-painted sign shrink into the distance: MINGDE HARDWARE, red letters he'd brushed on himself. He remembered seven-year-old Chen Xuan yanking too hard on a bamboo fishing pole by the river. "Don't pull back when the fish pulls," he'd told the boy. "Wait. Let it wear itself out."

He could wait. If there was one thing an old shopkeeper knew how to do, it was wait.
