# Chapter 167: The Envoy from Hong Kong

By half past eleven that night, a black sedan with dual Hong Kong and Guangdong plates glided off the Shenzhen Bay Bridge and turned into Futian CBD.

Ten minutes later, the car stopped at the private entrance of the Yunhai Club, across the wide avenue from the towering glass spire of Ping An Finance Center.

Inside the top-floor walnut lounge, Shen Wanshan stood by the tall window with his hands behind his back. Outside, the red crown lights of Ping An Finance Center blinked slowly against the night clouds.

The oak doors opened and closed softly behind two newcomers.

Walking in was a lean man in his late fifties wearing a tailored charcoal suit, a crisp white shirt without a tie, and thin wire-rimmed glasses. His silver-streaked hair was combed back neatly, and his expression held the calm chill of a banker who never needed to raise his voice.

Koo Tianming, senior envoy of the Seven-Harbor Consortium. Behind him walked his quiet assistant, Liang Zhenhua, carrying a black leather briefcase.

Shen Wanshan forced a practiced smile and offered both hands. "Mr. Koo, thank you for crossing the bay at this hour. I prepared thirty-year Pu-erh tea to take the night chill off."

Koo Tianming shook his hand briefly. His palm was cool and dry. "Save the politeness, Old Shen. I left a dinner in Central Hong Kong and crossed the border in forty minutes because your steward said the Shenzhen docks might stop moving by Friday."

Koo Tianming sat at the rosewood table and crossed one leg neatly over the other. Ignoring the steaming teacup, he tapped a slim cigarette from a silver case and lit it with a clean click.

Liang Zhenhua opened the briefcase and laid three printed sheets across the wood.

"Before I left Hong Kong," Koo Tianming said softly, exhaling pale smoke, "our contacts in Futian CBD sent these. China Construction Bank locked 4 billion yuan at five sharp. ICBC froze your cargo accounts six minutes later. Huaxia Bank pulled 3 billion yuan at five twenty. And Bank of China shut the door in your finance director's face."

Shen Wanshan's smile faded. He sat down and lit his own cigarette. "A temporary misunderstanding, Mr. Koo. My grandson clashed at Pier Nine with Haiyue Group from Shanghai. Qin Yuhan's family has ties in Beijing, so I am calling old friends to smooth things over by tomorrow afternoon."

Koo Tianming looked at him through his glasses with quiet mockery.

"Old Shen, we have worked together for twelve years," Koo Tianming said evenly. "Do not insult my intelligence. If you believed Haiyue Group did this, you would not have called Hong Kong at midnight with your hands shaking."

Shen Wanshan's fingers tightened around his lighter. "I called Hong Kong because our Yantian Port shipments leave every Thursday night. If my accounts stay frozen for two days, the tugboat crews and customs brokers will start asking questions. Neither of us wants that."

"Correct," Koo Tianming said coldly. "Now stop hiding half the story. Tell me what actually happened at Pier Nine between four and five o'clock."

Shen Wanshan took a slow drag and spoke plainly.

"My grandson locked down Qin Yuhan's ship, the Haiyue Star, using Chief Inspector Lu Guobin. We wanted 30% of Haiyue's southern lines. Qin Yuhan refused. Then a young man stepped out from behind her."

"Who?" Koo Tianming asked.

"Nobody we know," Shen Wanshan frowned. "He wore a plain white shirt, carried a cloth folder and a scratched green thermos, and claimed to be an ordinary clerk from Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai. He told my grandson to open the gate by five o'clock because he did not like working overtime."

Shen Wanshan tapped his ash into the crystal tray. "At five sharp, Lu Guobin was arrested on the dock. The port director ran down in person to cut the chain. Within twenty minutes, three national banks locked 12 billion yuan of Shen Family money without a single court paper or phone leak."

The lounge went dead still.

Koo Tianming's right hand stopped halfway to the ashtray.

For three long seconds, he did not move. An inch of gray ash broke off his cigarette and fell onto his dark tailored trousers. He did not even glance down to brush it away.

Behind him, Liang Zhenhua frowned. "Mr. Koo? Are you alright?"

Koo Tianming's eyes narrowed behind his glasses as his mind raced back two weeks to the lakeside offices of Zurich.

"Twenty minutes," Koo Tianming whispered. "No warning calls. No middlemen. Three major banks moving like one hand at the exact minute of a deadline."

Shen Wanshan leaned forward, sensing the chill in the room. "Mr. Koo, do you know who this young man works for?"

Koo Tianming took one slow, hard pull on his cigarette and set it on the ashtray. His smooth Hong Kong calm had sharpened into cold steel.

"Two weeks ago in Zurich," Koo Tianming said quietly, "the Seven-Harbor Consortium lost the Northern Ledger. Every private shipping account we kept in Europe vanished overnight. 400 million dollars in clean reserves, locked and stripped in under twenty minutes. Not one Swiss banker gave us five seconds of warning. Not one lawyer knew whose signature approved it."

Shen Wanshan's breath caught. "The Northern Ledger? Your European backbone? I heard a banking audit hit Zurich, but nobody knew it was that fast!"

"It was not an audit," Koo Tianming said, his voice like ice scraping stone. "Our people in London and Singapore spent fourteen days digging through every channel we own. All we found was a rumor of a clean, silent hand using white-sealed papers and numbered men. Nobody knew where they went after Zurich."

He looked slowly out the tall window toward the dark water of Shenzhen Bay.

"Until tonight."

Cold sweat prickled along Shen Wanshan's neck. "You mean that clerk from Hengxin Logistics belongs to the exact same unseen power that broke Zurich?"

"Or he is the messenger they sent south," Koo Tianming replied grimly. "Either way, Old Shen, your grandson just poked a sleeping tiger right on our doorstep."

"Then what do we do?" Shen Wanshan demanded, stubbing out his cigarette hard. "Should we pause the Yantian Port shift tomorrow night? Pull the ships back to Hong Kong?"

"Never!" Koo Tianming snapped, his calm breaking for the first time. "The buyers in the north are already waiting for this month's batch! If we stop the Changbai Mountain line for even one week, the people above me in the Consortium will tear both of us apart! The containers at Yantian Port move on schedule tomorrow night, no matter what."

Shen Wanshan swallowed hard, his jaw clenched. "Then how do we handle Qin Yuhan and this Chen Xuan? If we cannot hit him openly, are we supposed to let 12 billion yuan rot in frozen accounts?"

Koo Tianming leaned back slowly, brushing the gray ash off his knee with two steady fingers. A cold, calculating light returned to his eyes.

"We do not attack a tiger in the dark," Koo Tianming said softly. "First, we turn on the light and see whether the clerk is the tiger itself, or just a small fox borrowing someone else's shadow."

Shen Wanshan's eyes lit with grim understanding. "A test."

"Invite Qin Yuhan and that clerk to dinner," Koo Tianming murmured, lifting the untouched teacup at last. "Be polite. Smile. Offer peace. And while they sit at your table, we will spring a clean trap that forces whoever stands behind him to show their hand."
