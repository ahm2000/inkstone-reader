# Chapter 167: The Envoy from Hong Kong

At 11:45 PM, a black Hong Kong-plated Maybach glided past the gate of Shenzhen Bay No. 1 and stopped under the marble canopy. Lin Zhaorong stepped out into the chilly late November breeze in a tailored charcoal coat and gold-rimmed glasses. As Victor Leung's senior fixer inside the Seven-Harbor Consortium, he had swallowed a dozen stubborn port companies without ever raising his voice.

Upstairs in the Shen penthouse, Lin Zhaorong ignored the tea Shen Wanshan poured. He flipped through a slim folder on Haiyue Group and tapped a photo of Chen Xuan walking beside Qin Yuhan.

"Qin Yuhan is sharp and careful," Lin Zhaorong said smoothly. "Her husband, Chen Xuan, is a Shanghai logistics clerk who clocks out at 4:55 PM and buys groceries on his way home. Background decoration. Ignore him."

Shen Jingzhou nodded eagerly. "Exactly what I told my father!"

Lin Zhaorong did not glance at him. "Your mistake was shouting threats in a lobby. In Hong Kong, we set a nice table and let the numbers break their spine. Tomorrow evening, send her a gilded invitation to Harbor Villa for a Thursday night apology banquet. Tell her the Shen Family wants peace and a joint southern charter."

"What if she sees through it?" Shen Wanshan frowned.

"She will," Lin Zhaorong replied calmly. "And she will still come, because refusing a peace table in front of southern shipping heads makes Haiyue Group look weak. Once she steps inside Harbor Villa, I will lay down three offshore freight liens and a port insurance freeze. She will either sell us her southern route for 120 million yuan, or watch her Shanghai containers rot on the dock."

Shen Wanshan exhaled slowly, eyes gleaming. "Clean. I will have the invitation printed before dawn."

Far north, as the late November sun broke cold over Handong Province, a fierce clash was already unfolding inside the Provincial Party Committee conference room in Jingzhou.

Steam rose from porcelain mugs along the oval table. Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin sat calmly at the head, facing the Provincial Governor, who held the exact same ministerial rank and led the rival camp.

"Secretary Sha, I fully support Luzhou's growth," the Governor said with a practiced smile, tapping his binder. "The Luzhou Airport expansion and the Crescent Lake corridor matter deeply. Still, promoting Yi Xuexi to a heavier provincial role right now feels rushed. Luzhou's budget is tight, and the Crescent Lake review needs careful sequencing. Why not let the provincial construction group take the airport bid first while Yi Xuexi stays focused on municipal stability?"

Several officials lowered their eyes. Everyone saw the pit the Governor had just dug. Handing the airport bid to the provincial construction group meant steering 400 million yuan in contracts to his own camp while sidelining Yi Xuexi.

Sha Ruijin took a slow sip of tea and looked straight across the table.

"Governor, your fiscal caution is admirable," Sha Ruijin said evenly. "Which makes me curious. Three years ago, when the provincial construction group drafted the first Crescent Lake plan under your former deputy, why did the budget swell by 200 million yuan without laying one mile of drainage pipe?"

The Governor's smile stiffened. "Shifting material costs and complex wetland geology."

"Strange," Sha Ruijin said lightly. "After Yi Xuexi took over as Luzhou Party Secretary, he cleared the zoning backlog in four months, cut drainage costs by 150 million yuan, and restored the wetland water to grade three. If we sideline the one cadre who solved Crescent Lake, what message are we sending Handong? That real work gets punished while stalling gets rewarded?"

Beside Sha Ruijin, Provincial Discipline Inspection Secretary Tian Guofu leaned forward, his tone dry and sharp. "I checked the old Crescent Lake files last week. Several subcontracting invoices still have unsigned audit gaps. If the provincial construction group wants the Luzhou Airport bid, my commission would be glad to finish auditing their old books first."

Silence dropped heavy over the room. The Governor's jaw clenched. He had dug a procedural pit for Yi Xuexi, only to fall straight into a deeper one himself. With Tian Guofu holding the audit ledger open, arguing further looked like shielding dirty accounts.

"Since Comrade Yi Xuexi has proven results," the Governor said stiffly, "we can support his advancement, provided the airport bidding stays transparent."

"Of course," Sha Ruijin smiled faintly. "Transparency is what we all want."

Minutes after the meeting ended, Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang cornered Guangming District Chief Sun Liancheng in the marble corridor.

"Sun Liancheng!" Li Dakang barked, waving a stack of port-rail transfer permits. "Three logistics firms have waited two weeks for Guangming District to stamp these freight clearances! Why are they still on your desk?"

Sun Liancheng adjusted his coat with the unbothered calm of a man who had stopped chasing promotions years ago. "Secretary Li, two clauses overlap with provincial transport rules. I am only a district chief. If I stamp them rashly and violate procedure, I cannot bear the responsibility."

"You have plenty of energy to sit on your balcony every night stargazing through a telescope," Li Dakang fumed, "yet you cannot spend twenty minutes signing a freight permit!"

"Stargazing costs the state zero yuan and breaks zero rules," Sun Liancheng replied politely, bowing just enough to stay respectful while refusing to budge. "Once the municipal transport office sends written clarification, I will sign at once."

Li Dakang pointed a trembling finger at him, speechless, when his phone buzzed. His wife Ouyang Jing was calling from Jingzhou City Bank.

"What is it?" Li Dakang snapped.

"Keep your voice down," Ouyang Jing said briskly. "A southern shipping client with Hong Kong backing wants our bank to issue a 300 million yuan bridge guarantee for a Shenzhen port takeover. The paperwork looks rushed, and two provincial acquaintances keep calling my desk to push it. Does the city have any project in Shenzhen?"

Li Dakang's sharp instincts clicked instantly. "Jingzhou City Bank answers to banking law, not favors! If the risk smells crooked, reject it! And stop calling my office about bank loans!"

He hung up and marched down the stairs, unaware that the same Hong Kong hands testing Jingzhou City Bank were already setting a trap far south in Shenzhen.
