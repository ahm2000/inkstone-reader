# Chapter 47: The Premier Wakes to Silence

At four in the morning, the red phone on Premier Gu Chengshan's desk gave a sharp click, its dead yellow light flipping green.

Gu Chengshan had not slept since one. Sitting in an unbuttoned jacket beside a half-full ashtray, he snatched up the phone at once. The secure line chimed twice, linking three people.

"Qin Zhengguo!" Gu Chengshan snapped, tight with three hours of bottled anger. "Where in hell have you been? Every red line in the compound has been dead since one fifteen. My own guards locked the hall doors and kept my secretary outside. Three theater commands went dark. Tell me right now if we are looking at a coup!"

On the second line, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua breathed raggedly before a lighter clicked. "It is not just the theater commands, Premier Gu Chengshan," Zhou Zhenhua broke in, dragging hard on a cigarette. "My chief of staff just pounded on my door. Half the deputy ministers we tried to reach are gone. Their house guards stand outside empty bedrooms. Nobody fired a shot, nobody sounded an alarm, and nobody will say who took them!"

For three seconds, the third line held only crackling tobacco and a long exhale.

"Put your pen down, Zhou Zhenhua," General Qin Zhengguo said at last, his voice rough and heavy as iron. "There is no coup. If there were a coup, none of us would hold a live phone."

Gu Chengshan lit a cigarette with his free hand, smoke curling past his narrowed eyes. "No riddles, Qin Zhengguo. You hold the vault line to White Guard. Did White Guard move without State Council approval?"

"White Guard did not move," Qin Zhengguo replied flatly. "White Guard was disarmed and locked in a basement holding room at one ten."

Zhou Zhenhua coughed hard on his smoke, then went completely still. "What did you say?" he whispered. "Who has the power to disarm White Guard?"

Gu Chengshan's fingers froze as a cold weight sank into his stomach. "Black Guard," he breathed. "Black Guard woke up."

"Yes," Qin Zhengguo said, inhaling deeply. "I called the White Guard vault line at one forty when the Eastern Theater line dropped off my board. I got a dead seal. No voice, no code prompt. I had to wake Elder Song for the founding-era code phrase. Even with Elder Song's phrase, they gave me no command line. Just a thirty-second report before cutting me off."

"Elder Song gave you the code?" Zhou Zhenhua's voice jumped in panic. "What did they report? Why did they pull deputy ministers from bed at two in the morning?"

"Sit down, both of you," Qin Zhengguo said quietly. "If you are standing, sit down right now."

Gu Chengshan stayed on his feet, gripping his desk until his knuckles turned white. "Give me the numbers, Qin Zhengguo."

"Fourteen provinces," Qin Zhengguo said, each phrase landing like a hammer. "Forty ministries. Three theater commands. Six major ports. Three hundred and eighteen top officials arrested. Forty-seven foreign spies caught alive before crossing a border or destroying files. One hundred and fourteen terabytes of unlocked account books, messages, and signed bank transfers."

Gu Chengshan's lighter slipped from his fingers and clattered onto the desk. He did not pick it up.

"Three hundred and eighteen," Zhou Zhenhua choked out as a chair scraped backward. "Qin Zhengguo, are you out of your mind? Three hundred and eighteen top officials across forty ministries? That is half the top leadership!"

"It gets worse," Qin Zhengguo said, voice dropping lower. "The summary puts the stolen state money over twenty years at more than 100 trillion yuan. Government buying, port customs, power grids, overseas shell companies, all tied into one ring. Along with the money, live military spying. Real defense secrets handed straight to those forty-seven foreign spies."

Dead silence fell over the three-way line.

Gu Chengshan stared at the gray smoke rising from his ashtray, his chest tight. Over 100 trillion yuan. Live military spying inside three theater commands. All of it rotting under their noses while they signed papers and ran weekly meetings.

"My God," Zhou Zhenhua breathed, shaking openly. "100 trillion. And military secrets. How did none of our inspectors catch a single thread?"

"Because the inspectors were part of the thread, Zhou Zhenhua," Qin Zhengguo snapped, raw anger breaking through. "Forty ministries. That includes oversight. That includes ports. That includes people who sat across from us at the last big meeting and smiled while we poured them tea."

Gu Chengshan took a hard pull on his cigarette until his mind cleared. His eyes narrowed sharply. "Qin Zhengguo," he said slowly. "Black Guard does not wake on its own. Not in thirty years. Somebody triggered it tonight. Who?"

Four seconds of tense silence stretched over the humming line.

"Tell me, Qin Zhengguo," Gu Chengshan pressed, leaning over his desk. "You spoke to Elder Song. You got the vault report. Who holds the key right now?"

"Stop," Qin Zhengguo said, cold enough to cut straight through rank. "Do not finish that sentence, Premier Gu Chengshan. Do not write a name on a notepad, and do not ask your aides to check files."

"I am the Premier of the State Council!" Gu Chengshan shot back, slapping his palm on the desk. "Three hundred and eighteen officials vanished under my roof in three hours, and you tell me not to ask who gave the order?"

"Yes, I am," Qin Zhengguo answered without flinching. "Elder Song said two sentences before hanging up. First, Black Guard only wakes when the sovereign seal itself orders it. Second, if they had not moved tonight, we would have woken up next month with our southern power grid dark and our navy radar blind. We do not ask who holds the key. We take the handoff and pray our own offices are clean."

Gu Chengshan's mouth opened, then slowly closed as blood drained from his face.

On the second line, Zhou Zhenhua let out a hollow laugh that died at once. "Pray our own offices are clean," he repeated under his breath. "When does the handoff happen?"

"Four thirty," Qin Zhengguo said, checking his watch. "Central Leadership Compound, main briefing hall. Black Guard is bringing the proof straight to us. Call Gao Zhengyi at the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection right now. Tell him to bring no secretary, no phone, and walk in alone."

"I will call Gao Zhengyi myself," Gu Chengshan said, steadying his voice even as his fingers shook putting out his cigarette. "Thirty minutes. Nobody else enters that hall."

"Thirty minutes," Zhou Zhenhua echoed grimly.

The three phones clicked down, leaving Premier Gu Chengshan alone in his smoke-filled study.
