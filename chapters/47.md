# Chapter 47: Monday at Noon

At eleven forty on Monday morning, Chen Xuan stood outside the oak doors of the Haiyue partner restaurant on West River Road and checked his collar in the glass.

Liu Meifang had ironed his gray shirt until the cuffs stood stiff. His blue tie sat straight, and his brown leather shoes were polished clean, though the thin line of clear glue near the left toe still caught the sun.

Beside him, General Manager Sun Haoran wiped his neck with a handkerchief. Manager Hu Dewei stood half a step behind, clutching a leather folder.

"Remember what we went over, Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran said tightly. "Keep your answers brief. Do not mention the cafeteria, and do not ask to pack leftovers."

"I understand, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said calmly.

"Call her Miss Qin," Hu Dewei added, glancing nervously down the hall. "Haiyue Hospitality and Property Group holds forty percent of the hotel supply contracts in this district."

Chen Xuan nodded. He still pictured a strict fifty-year-old executive with reading glasses on a chain. Adjusting his worn canvas bag, he followed the hostess down a quiet bamboo-screened corridor.

Sun Haoran and Hu Dewei hovered nervously outside Private Room Three. Sun Haoran knocked twice, opened the door just wide enough to bow, and announced Supervisor Chen Xuan before backing into the lounge.

Chen Xuan stepped inside, and the door clicked shut behind him.

* * *

The room was small and quiet, with a round wooden table for four and tall windows overlooking winter pines.

Seated by the window was a woman of twenty-eight in a cream blazer and black knit top, her hair pinned loosely with a wooden clip. Her eyes were clear, sharp, and faintly amused as she took in his ironed gray shirt, blue tie, and canvas bag.

Beside her sat a younger woman in a charcoal suit with a slim notebook open on her knee.

Chen Xuan paused for half a beat. He had expected fifty and severe, not someone younger than himself who looked thoroughly bored by luxury.

"Good afternoon, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan said politely. "I am Chen Xuan from Hengxin Logistics. Thank you for the invitation."

Qin Yuhan smiled, humor gleaming in her eyes. "You look surprised, Supervisor Chen. Did Manager Hu tell you I had gray hair?"

"No, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan replied honestly. "I guessed that myself. My mother told me to wear a tie for a senior manager."

Beside her, Jin Ke's pen paused over her notebook as she hid a smile.

Qin Yuhan laughed softly and gestured to the chair across from her. "Please sit. This is my assistant, Jin Ke. Put your bag on the empty chair."

Chen Xuan sat down and noticed the table. There were no wine glasses or banquet platters. Four steaming home-style dishes sat on the Lazy Susan: braised tofu with minced pork, stir-fried winter greens, radish and rib soup, and a bamboo steamer of plain white steamed buns.

"I skipped the banquet menu," Qin Yuhan said, resting her chin on her knuckles as she tested him. "The chef downstairs was offended. I hope you do not mind simple food."

"Not at all," Chen Xuan said genuinely. "Banquet dishes are too oily at noon. They make reading manifests in the afternoon hard."

Jin Ke lifted her eyes slightly. Most men across from Qin Yuhan spent ten minutes praising vintage wine or hesitating before touching a steamed bun.

Chen Xuan lifted a warm bun naturally, broke it cleanly in half, and dipped one edge into the braised tofu sauce. He took a bite and nodded in quiet approval.

"The tofu is fresh," he remarked. "And the sauce is not too sweet. My mother makes it almost the same way."

Qin Yuhan's smile deepened as she poured him hot barley tea. "Your file says twelve thousand yuan a month, no car, no watch, and the top error-free record in the district for two quarters. Do you not find checking forms tedious?"

"Not really," Chen Xuan replied plainly. "A clean form means the driver gets paid on time and the warehouse crew goes home before dark. When paperwork gets sloppy, the workers at the bottom wait three weeks for wages."

Qin Yuhan paused with her teacup halfway to her lips. There was no rehearsed humility in his tone and no pitch to impress her.

"Why no watch?" she asked.

"My phone tells time," Chen Xuan said, taking some greens. "And a watch strap catches on boxes when I unload the back dock."

Qin Yuhan let out a genuine laugh, her shoulders relaxing for the first time all week. Tired of pretentious suitors flashing Swiss steel before the soup arrived, sitting across from Chen Xuan felt like crisp autumn air.

"Tell me about Yunxi County," she said. "Is the food really better there?"

"The ribs are," Chen Xuan said warmly. "My sister put a rule on the fridge that everyone eats at six. Even my father cannot recite proverbs until the soup is served."

Jin Ke glanced at Qin Yuhan and quietly closed her notebook.

* * *

Midway through lunch, heavy footsteps and an urgent protest broke the quiet corridor outside.

"Sir, please, Private Room Three is reserved for Miss Qin! You cannot go in without an invitation."

The oak door swung open with a sharp thud.

A young man in his late twenties strode in past the flustered waiter. He wore a tailored charcoal suit, a thick gold chronograph, and heavy cedar cologne, carrying a bottle of vintage Bordeaux by the neck.

"Yuhan," he said with a smooth smile. "What a coincidence. The manager said you were dining here. I could not let you drink house tea when I had a 2012 Margaux in my trunk."

Qin Yuhan's warmth vanished instantly. She set her chopsticks down and met him with cool, flat eyes.

"Zhou Mingcheng," she said evenly. "This is a private lunch. Did the hostess not tell you I was not receiving guests?"

Zhou Mingcheng, heir to Shenghua Maritime Group, waved dismissively. "Our families have been partners for fifteen years, Yuhan. You should not hide in a back room eating cafeteria food."

He stepped closer to pull out the empty chair, spotted Chen Xuan's canvas bag, and looked across the table.

Zhou Mingcheng's gaze swept slowly over the ironed gray shirt, the cheap blue tie, the half-eaten steamed bun, and dropped under the table to Chen Xuan's glued brown shoes.

A mocking smirk curved his lips.

"Well," Zhou Mingcheng drawled with contempt. "Is that shoe glue on the left toe? Hengxin Logistics? The tiny freight firm that begs my father's port office for scrap berths?"

Chen Xuan set his teacup down softly, completely calm. "The glue holds well in the rain," he said mildly.

Zhou Mingcheng barked a laugh and turned to Qin Yuhan. "Yuhan, a twelve-thousand-yuan clerk eating steamed buns in a Haiyue private room? If you wanted charity work, you could have sent rice to his warehouse instead of wasting your Monday."

Jin Ke stood up immediately, her face cold. "Mr. Zhou, you were not invited. Please leave."

Zhou Mingcheng ignored her, resting his knuckles on the table and staring down at Chen Xuan.

"Listen, little clerk," Zhou Mingcheng sneered. "Finish your bun, grab your cloth bag, and wait in the hallway with your boss. Adults have business to discuss."
