# Chapter 47: The Premier Wakes to Silence

At four in the morning, the red phone on Premier Gu Chengshan's study desk gave a sharp click. The dead yellow light flipped to green.

Gu Chengshan had not slept since one. He sat in an unbuttoned jacket beside a half-full ashtray. The instant the light turned green, his hand snapped onto the phone. The secure line chimed twice, linking three people at once.

"Qin Zhengguo!" Gu Chengshan snapped into the phone, tight with three hours of bottled anger. "Where in hell have you been? Every red line in the compound has been dead since one fifteen. My own guards locked the hall doors and kept my secretary outside. Three theater commands went completely dark. Tell me right now if we are looking at a coup!"

On the second line, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua breathed fast and ragged, followed by the click of a lighter. "It is not just the theater commands, Premier Gu Chengshan," Zhou Zhenhua broke in, his voice cracking as he dragged hard on a cigarette. "My chief of staff just pounded on my door. Half the deputy ministers we tried to reach are gone. Their house guards are standing outside empty bedrooms. Nobody fired a shot, nobody sounded an alarm, and nobody will say who took them!"

For three seconds, the third line held only the crackle of burning tobacco and a long breath out.

"Put your pen down, Zhou Zhenhua," General Qin Zhengguo said at last. His voice was rough, stripped of polite tone, heavy as cold iron. "There is no coup. If there were a coup, none of us would be holding a live phone."

Gu Chengshan's jaw tightened. He tapped out a cigarette with his free hand and lit it. Smoke curled past his narrowed eyes. "Do not give me riddles, Qin Zhengguo. You hold the vault line to White Guard. Did White Guard move without State Council approval?"

"White Guard did not move," Qin Zhengguo replied flatly. "White Guard was disarmed and locked inside a basement holding room at one ten."

Across the line, Zhou Zhenhua coughed hard on his smoke, then went completely still. "What did you just say?" Zhou Zhenhua whispered. "Who has the power to disarm White Guard?"

Gu Chengshan's fingers froze around his cigarette. A cold weight settled into his stomach. "Black Guard," Gu Chengshan breathed. "Black Guard woke up."

"Yes," Qin Zhengguo said. Another long inhale carried through the phone. "I called the White Guard vault line at one forty when the Eastern Theater line dropped off my board. I got a dead seal. No voice, no code prompt. I had to wake Elder Song in the middle of the night just for the old founding-era code phrase. Even with Elder Song's phrase, they gave me no command line. Just a thirty-second report before cutting me off."

"Elder Song gave you the code?" Zhou Zhenhua's voice jumped up in panic. "What did they report? Why did they pull deputy ministers out of their beds at two in the morning?"

"Sit down, both of you," Qin Zhengguo said quietly. "If you are standing, sit down right now."

Gu Chengshan stayed on his feet, gripping the edge of his desk until his knuckles went white. "Give me the numbers, Qin Zhengguo."

"Fourteen provinces," Qin Zhengguo said, each phrase landing like a hammer blow. "Forty ministries. Three theater commands. Six major ports. Three hundred and eighteen top officials arrested. Forty-seven foreign spies caught alive before they could cross a border or destroy their files. One hundred and fourteen terabytes of unlocked account books, messages, and signed bank transfers."

Gu Chengshan's lighter slipped from his fingers and clattered loudly against the desk. He did not pick it up.

"Three hundred and eighteen," Zhou Zhenhua choked out. On his end, a chair scraped sharply backward. "Qin Zhengguo, are you out of your mind? Three hundred and eighteen top officials across forty ministries? That is half the top leadership!"

"It gets worse," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice dropping lower. "The summary given through Elder Song's code puts the stolen state money over twenty years at more than 100 trillion yuan. Government buying, port customs, power grids, overseas shell companies, all tied into one ring. Along with the money, live military spying. Real defense secrets handed straight to those forty-seven foreign spies."

Dead silence fell over the three-way line.

Gu Chengshan stared at the gray ribbon of smoke rising from his ashtray. His chest felt tight, as though the air had been pulled out of the room. Over 100 trillion yuan. Live military spying inside three theater commands. All of it rotting right under their noses while they signed papers and ran weekly meetings.

"My God," Zhou Zhenhua breathed, his voice shaking openly. "100 trillion. And military secrets. How did none of our own inspectors catch a single thread of this?"

"Because the inspectors were part of the thread, Zhou Zhenhua," Qin Zhengguo snapped, raw anger flashing through his calm. "Forty ministries. That includes oversight. That includes ports. That includes people who sat across from us at the last big meeting and smiled while we poured them tea."

Gu Chengshan took a hard pull from his cigarette until his mind cleared again. His eyes narrowed, cold and sharp. "Qin Zhengguo," Gu Chengshan said slowly. "Black Guard does not wake on its own. Not in thirty years. Somebody triggered it tonight. Who?"

Silence stretched for four full seconds. Only the faint hiss of the secure line filled the gap.

"Tell me, Qin Zhengguo," Gu Chengshan pressed, leaning over his desk with a hard edge. "You spoke to Elder Song. You got the vault report. Who holds the key right now?"

"Stop," Qin Zhengguo said, his voice cold enough to cut straight through Gu Chengshan's rank. "Do not finish that sentence, Premier Gu Chengshan. Do not write a name on a notepad, and do not ask your aides to check any files."

"I am the Premier of the State Council!" Gu Chengshan shot back, slapping his palm on the desk. "Three hundred and eighteen officials vanished under my roof in three hours, and you are telling me not to ask who gave the order?"

"Yes, I am," Qin Zhengguo answered without flinching. "Elder Song said two sentences before he hung up. First, Zero only wakes when the sovereign seal itself orders it. Second, if Zero had not moved tonight, we would have woken up next month with our southern power grid dark and our navy radar blind. We do not ask who holds the key. We take the handoff and pray our own offices are clean."

Gu Chengshan's mouth opened, then slowly closed. Blood drained from his face as the full weight of those words sank in.

On the second line, Zhou Zhenhua let out a shaky, hollow laugh that died right away. "Pray our own offices are clean," Zhou Zhenhua repeated under his breath. "When does the handoff happen?"

"Four thirty," Qin Zhengguo said, checking his watch. "Central Leadership Compound, main briefing hall. Black Guard is bringing the proof straight to us. Call Gao Zhengyi at the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection right now. Tell him not to bring a secretary, not to bring a phone, and to walk in alone."

"I will call Gao Zhengyi myself," Gu Chengshan said, his voice steady again, though a slight shake stayed in his fingers as he put out his cigarette. "Thirty minutes. Nobody else enters that hall."

"Thirty minutes," Zhou Zhenhua echoed grimly.

The three phones clicked down, leaving Premier Gu Chengshan alone in the thick smoke of his quiet study.
