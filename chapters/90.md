# Chapter 90: The Sentries Vanish

A cool autumn wind moved through the tall pines of the Western Hills, carrying the clean scent of cedar bark and damp stone across the courtyard. Inside the timber study, a ceramic pot of oolong tea steamed softly beside a stack of sealed folders.

Elder Qin Zhengguo, the retired military commander whose word still carried weight across every theater command, stood behind the tall glass doors with his hands clasped behind his back. His eyes remained fixed on the outer gate pillars a short distance across the flagstone yard.

Two figures in plain black gear stood there. Plain black masks covered their faces. Neither carried a visible badge, rank patch, or serial number. They had held that exact stance since sunrise, untouched by wind or cold.

Behind him, his aide Colonel Gan Weihan glanced at his wristwatch and shifted his weight. "Elder Qin Zhengguo, the central stand-down order has reached all seven military regions. Should I keep the compound guard platoon on alert through the night?"

"Tell them to stand down and get some sleep," Elder Qin Zhengguo replied calmly without turning. "Putting another row of rifles in this yard only makes us look nervous."

Colonel Gan Weihan hesitated. "You are certain they will leave on schedule?"

"The notice said 9:00 PM," Elder Qin Zhengguo said. "Whoever commands the Black Order does not waste words, and they do not miss a mark by a single second."

Down the hallway, the tall wooden clock began its soft chime for 9:00 PM.

On the very first note, both black-clad sentries moved as one. Each took a single smooth step backward into the deep shadow behind the stone gate pillars. There was no crunch of gravel, no scrape of boot leather, no whisper of fabric, and no engine rumble beyond the outer wall.

Before the third chime faded, the space beside the pillars was completely empty.

Colonel Gan Weihan stepped forward, pressing close to the glass and squinting into the dark pines. "They are gone. Just like that. Not even a taillight on the ridge road."

"You will not see a taillight," Elder Qin Zhengguo murmured, his expression quiet and deeply thoughtful. "If they wanted to be tracked, we would have found their base twenty years ago."

A gentle knock sounded at the side door, and the wooden panel swung open. Elder Song, the senior state elder who lived in the neighboring courtyard, walked in leaning lightly on his dark mahogany cane. His wool coat was buttoned to the collar, and his silver hair was ruffled by the mountain breeze.

"Your gate is clear too, I see," Elder Song said, stepping over to the glass doors and looking out at the empty flagstones.

"Cleared on the first chime of 9:00 PM," Elder Qin Zhengguo answered. He gestured toward the low table. "Sit down, old friend. The tea is still hot."

Colonel Gan Weihan quietly poured two cups of pale amber oolong, set them down, and stepped out into the corridor to leave the two elders alone.

Elder Song lowered himself into the wooden armchair and lifted his cup, warming his palms against the porcelain. "At my gate, there were two of them near the cypress wall. I watched them for ten minutes. Not a blink, not a shift of the heel. Then 9:00 PM arrived, and the wind blew through an empty corner."

On the desk, the red secure telephone chimed once. Elder Qin Zhengguo reached over and lifted the receiver. For ten seconds he listened in silence, then gave a brief nod and set the handset back down.

"That was Premier Gu Chengshan's duty office," Elder Qin Zhengguo said. "Every compound in the Western Hills and every ministry perimeter in central Beijing cleared at the exact same second. Not a single sentry remains in sight. Not one incident was logged."

Elder Song let out a long, quiet breath and gazed down at the curling steam above his cup. "When the Black Order appeared yesterday, half the younger ministers panicked. They thought a coup was coming. They thought someone wanted to seize the table."

"Fools," Elder Qin Zhengguo said softly, though without anger. "Someone with that level of reach and discipline does not need to fight for a seat at the table. If the holder of the Black Order wanted power, they would not have waited quietly in the shadows while corrupt networks rotted an entire province."

"No," Elder Song agreed, his voice low and grave. "They stepped in for one reason only. Someone crossed a line in Yunxi County and touched a household that should never have been touched."

Elder Qin Zhengguo sat across from him, his weathered hands resting on his knees. "Do you know what unsettled me most today? It was not the speed of the lockdowns. It was the restraint. They froze the capital without firing a round, waited for Premier Gu Chengshan to clean out the Handong rot through proper channels, and pulled back the moment the order was fulfilled."

"That is the mark of an absolute hidden backer force," Elder Song said quietly. "No boast. No demand for credit. No name left on any file."

"And no one in Beijing knows who holds the token," Elder Qin Zhengguo added. "Not Premier Gu Chengshan, not the intelligence bureaus, and certainly not us. All we have is a single instruction: keep the country clean, and never disturb that simple Yunxi household."

Outside the glass doors, the mountain wind rustled through the pine needles. The courtyard lights shone on bare stone and empty shadows, peaceful yet heavy with a lesson neither elder would ever forget.

"Tonight we sleep easier," Elder Song said as he finished his tea. "Yet I hope the younger generation remembers this evening for the next fifty years. There is a ceiling above all of us, and tonight it reminded us to walk straight."

Elder Qin Zhengguo looked out once more at the silent pillars. "Let them remember," he said quietly. "It will keep the country safe."
