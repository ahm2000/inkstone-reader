# Chapter 90: A Letter from the Western Hills

Late afternoon light slanted through the tall cypresses of the Western Hills, laying long shadows across the stone courtyard of the national capital's highest compound.

Inside the plain timber conference room, the air smelled faintly of cedar and cooled jasmine tea.

Three men sat around a dark walnut table.

The Senior Vice Premier rested one hand beside a crimson dossier stamped with the highest classification seal in the country. Across from him sat the Minister of State Security, his eyes sharp behind wire spectacles. To his right sat General Qin Zhengguo, wearing an unadorned olive uniform with four gold stars gleaming quietly on each shoulder.

"Aegis Meridian is finished," the Senior Vice Premier said, his voice low and steady. "Every offshore clearing shell, every shadow sovereign bond, and every covert shipping registry they built over twenty years dissolved inside forty-eight hours."

He tapped the crimson folder gently.

"More than one hundred trillion yuan in treasonous capital was frozen and repatriated into the central treasury before a single foreign desk noticed. Not one domestic bank suffered a run, and not one ordinary family lost a single fen."

The Minister of State Security opened a thinner folder and slid four black-and-white photographs across the wood.

"That was only the first half," the Minister of State Security said softly. "Two nights ago, four tier-one foreign contractors formerly employed by Aegis Meridian entered Lanshui City on a retaliation contract."

General Qin Zhengguo leaned forward, his weathered jaw tightening as he picked up the top sheet.

"I recognize two of these names," General Qin Zhengguo said, his baritone heavy with four decades in uniform. "Former special operations instructors armed with suppressed carbines and thermal optics. How did our coastal bureau intercept them before a shot was fired?"

"We did not," the Minister of State Security replied calmly.

Silence settled over the walnut table.

"At three o'clock yesterday morning," the Minister of State Security continued, "all four men were left sitting outside the rear gate of our Lanshui City field station, bound with ordinary nylon ties. Beside them sat four encrypted drives, a complete list of domestic collaborators, and four signed confessions."

General Qin Zhengguo stared down at the medical evaluation.

"No broken bones," General Qin Zhengguo murmured. "No interrogation scars. Yet all four veterans are suffering acute shock."

"Read the bottom paragraph," the Senior Vice Premier said quietly.

According to the surgeon's notes, the four mercenaries had crossed into a residential district in Lanshui City after midnight. Before they could reach the apartment stairwell, their thermal optics went completely dark.

Every single one of them repeated the same fragmented memory during debriefing.

Featureless matte-black masks. Gear that swallowed the streetlights without reflection. No footsteps on the wet asphalt. Only a flat, neutral, genderless voice, chilled like dry winter iron, speaking a single sentence in their own language before their knees gave out.

*You stepped inside fifty meters.*

General Qin Zhengguo set the paper down slowly. He had commanded entire theater groups and stared down foreign fleets without blinking, yet a quiet chill moved down his spine.

"A permanent perimeter," General Qin Zhengguo whispered. "Fifty meters around him at all times."

"Yes," the Minister of State Security said. "Our analysts estimate that the unit holding that ring is completely separate from the Black Pine Guard. We have no roster, no budget trail, and no satellite footprint for them. They exist for one purpose alone, to ensure nothing touches the man inside the circle."

"And the man inside the circle?" the Senior Vice Premier asked gently, though he already knew the answer.

"At seven thirty this morning," the Minister of State Security replied, a faint smile touching his mouth, "he bought two pork steamed buns from a street cart near his apartment, caught the 7:45 public bus, and clocked in at Hengxin Logistics three minutes early."

General Qin Zhengguo exhaled slowly, leaning back in his wooden chair.

"He dismantled a hundred-trillion-yuan foreign dagger aimed at the spine of this country," General Qin Zhengguo said softly. "He handed us the traitors without spilling innocent blood, without crashing a single market, and without asking for a medal or a seat at the table. And then he went back to checking freight bills."

"We should brief the Old Elder," the Senior Vice Premier said, rising to his feet. "He asked to be informed the moment the Lanshui City file was verified."

* * *

A stone corridor lined with potted plum trees led to a small sunlit study overlooking the western pines.

The ninety-year-old founding Supreme Elder sat in a worn wicker chair near the open lattice window. He wore a simple washed gray cotton tunic, with a knitted wool blanket resting across his knees despite the mild autumn sun.

His hair was pure silver now, his hands spotted with age, yet his eyes remained remarkably clear and steady.

The three senior leaders stood respectfully before him while the Senior Vice Premier delivered the summary in a calm, unhurried voice.

The founding Supreme Elder listened from start to finish without interrupting once.

When the report ended, the old man looked out at the swaying cypress branches for a long, quiet minute.

"Four foreign rifles," the elder murmured, his voice thin yet resonant. "And not one glass window in Lanshui City was shattered. Not one child woke up crying in the night."

"Not one, Elder," General Qin Zhengguo answered gently.

The old man glanced down at his scarred right hand, where a shard of shrapnel from a long-ago river crossing still rested beneath the skin.

"In my youth, we fought with rusted rifles so that ordinary people could one day eat warm steamed buns and walk to work in peace," the founding Supreme Elder said softly. "Now I am ninety years old, and a young man I have never met has guarded that exact peace for us from behind an office desk."

He turned slowly toward the plain wooden desk beside his chair.

"Bring me a sheet of plain cream paper," the old man said. "No letterhead. No red header. No seal."

The Senior Vice Premier stepped forward personally and laid a single sheet of unmarked cream stationery beside the inkstone.

The founding Supreme Elder picked up his old black fountain pen. His fingers trembled slightly at first, then steadied with the quiet discipline of a lifetime.

In neat, unhurried dark ink, he wrote four short lines.

*This old man is ninety years of age and cannot travel far without growing tired. You have sheltered the peace of this land without ever asking for a name or a reward. If it is not an imposition on your rest, might this old man invite the Boss to spare half an hour for one quiet cup of tea by the water? No ceremony, no escort, only two ordinary people sharing a pot of leaves.*

He signed no title at the bottom, only the simple brush of his family surname.

The founding Supreme Elder folded the cream paper, slipped it into a matching unmarked envelope, and pressed the flap shut with his thumb.

"Send this through the sealed relay to the man they call Number One," the elder said gently, looking up at General Qin Zhengguo. "Tell him it is not an order. It is only an old man's request. If the young man wishes to rest on his day off instead, I will understand completely."

General Qin Zhengguo accepted the unmarked cream envelope with both hands, bowing his head slightly.

"I will see it delivered tonight, Elder," General Qin Zhengguo promised softly.

Outside the window, the wind moved peacefully through the pines of the Western Hills, carrying the quiet weight of a gratitude too deep for titles or ceremonies.
