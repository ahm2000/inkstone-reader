# Chapter 90: The Sentries Vanish

At one minute to nine, General Qin Zhengguo stood behind the glass doors of his Western Hills study, a lit cigarette burning between his rough fingers. Across the stone yard, two figures in plain black gear stood completely still beside the outer guard pillars. They had not moved since dawn.

Behind him, Colonel Gan Weihan checked his watch uneasily. "General Qin Zhengguo, the central order just reached all seven military zones. Should I keep the guard unit on standby until morning?"

"Leave them in their quarters," General Qin Zhengguo said without turning, his eyes fixed on the two dark figures. "Another group of rifles in this yard only makes us look foolish."

The hallway clock struck the first chime of nine.

At that exact instant, both black-clad guards took one smooth step backward into the shadow behind the pillars. No crunch of gravel. No rustle of cloth. No shadow crossing the lit wall.

By the second chime, the pillars stood empty.

Colonel Gan Weihan's clipboard clattered onto the floor. He lunged for the wall intercom and barked, "Gate One! Gate Two! Eyes on the outer pillars right now!"

Static hissed before a breathless duty officer answered. "Colonel Gan Weihan, they are gone! Both figures stepped into Camera Four's blind spot at nine o'clock sharp. Floodlights are on and the hillside motion sensors are live. Nothing went off. Zero gates opened!"

Colonel Gan Weihan stared at the intercom, drained of color, then turned to General Qin Zhengguo. "General Qin Zhengguo, how do two men vanish from a locked military base without tripping one heat sensor?"

"They did not leave through the gates, Colonel Gan Weihan," General Qin Zhengguo said quietly, a muscle jumping in his locked jaw. "Our gates never held them in the first place."

Colonel Gan Weihan fumbled once as he picked up his clipboard. "Should I file a security breach report with the central garrison?"

"Throw that thought away right now," General Qin Zhengguo snapped, eyes cutting back to his aide. "Premier Gu Chengshan's third rule is already active. Nobody ever stood by those pillars. Write down an ordinary quiet watch."

Colonel Gan Weihan snapped to attention and swallowed hard. "Yes, General Qin Zhengguo."

General Qin Zhengguo stared out the glass until the cigarette burned down and stung his fingers, then dropped the butt into a crystal tray.

"Tell the zone commanders to carry out their arrests on time," General Qin Zhengguo said, his voice low and hollow. "The watchers have left the yard."

***

Outside the west wing of the Great Hall, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi stepped onto the stone stairs at nine o'clock, both carrying locked briefcases with the signed order.

Halfway down, Gao Zhengyi stopped dead and grabbed Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua's sleeve.

"Look at the west arch," Gao Zhengyi whispered.

Under the courtyard lamps, four black-clad guards moved together in perfect rhythm. They took one smooth step back into the pillar shadows. No turn of the head. No salute. No parting word.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua hurried to the railing and looked down. Empty stone stretched to the iron gates. Not a single boot print marked the damp ground near the fountain.

"Gone," Gao Zhengyi breathed, letting go of the sleeve. He reached for his cigarettes, then stopped because his hand shook too hard. "Nine o'clock sharp. The order gets signed at eight-fifty-eight, and two minutes later they disappear into thin air."

"Did you expect them to ask for a receipt?" Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua asked softly, jaw tight. "They waited until the ink dried on all forty-plus names. Not a minute early. Not a minute late."

"Wait," Gao Zhengyi said, a cold thought cutting through his fatigue. "What about Sha City? What about Yunxi?"

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua's secure phone buzzed. He checked the coded message from the provincial duty desk, then watched two more alerts pop up right below it.

"Sha City provincial compound cleared at nine o'clock sharp," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua read flatly. "All five main buildings in Yunxi, the city committee, police bureau, discipline office, court, and prosecutor's office, cleared at the exact same second. No sightings on any exit road. No footprints."

Gao Zhengyi let out a ragged breath. "No threat. No goodbye. No demand for a seat at the table. They just walked away and left us holding forty-seven arrest warrants."

"Which is far scarier than a threat," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua murmured, tightening his grip on his briefcase as he headed toward his black car. "Come on, Gao Zhengyi. We have a country to clean before sunrise."

***

In a quiet courtyard three streets north of the inner ring, Elder Song sat beside a dim lamp and an untouched cup of oolong tea. Across from him, his longtime aide Lin Haibo looked out toward the round stone gate.

At nine o'clock sharp, two dark figures beside the stone lanterns stepped back together and faded into the night.

Lin Haibo took a startled step toward the door, chest tight. "Elder Song, they are gone. Both of them. Not a sound on the stone path."

"Sit down, Lin Haibo," Elder Song said gently, his old hand steady on the table. "Do not chase shadows. You will only catch a cold."

Lin Haibo walked back with wide, fearful eyes. "Elder Song, I have worked beside you for twenty-two years. I thought I knew every hidden unit left from the founding days. Even Premier Gu Chengshan looked pale when those cases arrived. Why did they leave tonight without leaving a single way to reach them?"

Elder Song looked past the lamp toward the dark, empty gate as wind rustled the bamboo.

"Because the gate has closed," Elder Song said quietly.

Lin Haibo frowned. "Closed?"

"They did not come to rule us, Lin Haibo. They did not stay to watch over us either," Elder Song said, his voice heavy with fifty years of memory. "When a house lets rot spread for twenty years until it touches the wrong doorstep, an unseen hand sweeps the roof clean in one night and lays the account book on the master's table. Once the master signs his name and picks up the broom, the hand pulls back."

Lin Haibo swallowed hard, staring out at the empty yard. "And what if the state fails to finish what they started? What if someone shields Deng Yongchang's people, or digs into who sent those guards?"

Elder Song lifted his cold teacup, then set it down untouched, his gaze grave and distant.

"Then we will see what happens when the gate opens a second time," Elder Song murmured. "Pray that Gu Chengshan and Zhou Zhenhua never give them a reason."

Across the capital, Western Hills, Sha City, and the five lit government buildings of Yunxi, empty stone steps settled back into an ordinary night. Black Guard was gone without a trace, leaving the state alone with the reckoning it had been handed.
