# Chapter 90: The Sentries Vanish

At one minute to nine, General Qin Zhengguo stood behind the glass doors of his Western Hills study, a burning cigarette between his calloused fingers. Across the swept stone courtyard, two figures in featureless matte-black gear stood motionless beside the outer guard pillars, exactly where they had stood since dawn.

Behind him, Colonel Fang Qiming checked his watch uneasily. "General Qin Zhengguo, the central directive just cleared all seven theater commands. Should I keep the perimeter battalion on standby until morning?"

"Leave them in their barracks," General Qin Zhengguo said without turning. His eyes never left the two silhouettes under the courtyard lamps. "Another company of rifles in this yard only embarrasses us."

The brass clock in the hallway struck the first chime of nine.

At the exact instant the hammer hit metal, both matte-black sentries took one synchronized step backward into the shadow line behind the pillars.

No crunch of gravel. No rustle of fabric. No shadow crossing the lit stone wall.

By the second chime, the pillars stood empty.

Colonel Fang Qiming's clipboard slipped from his fingers and clattered onto the floor. He lunged for the wall intercom, jammed the gatehouse channel, and barked, "Gate One! Gate Two! Visual on the outer pillars now!"

Static hissed before a breathless duty officer answered. "Colonel Fang Qiming, they are gone! Both figures stepped into Camera Four's shadow at nine-zero-zero sharp. Floodlights are up and hillside motion sensors are live. Nothing tripped. Zero gates opened!"

Colonel Fang Qiming stared at the intercom, face bloodless, then turned slowly to General Qin Zhengguo. "General Qin Zhengguo, how do two men vanish out of a sealed military compound without tripping a single infrared beam?"

"They did not leave through the gates, Colonel Fang Qiming," General Qin Zhengguo said quietly, his jaw locked so hard a muscle jumped beneath his cheek. "They were never bound by our gates to begin with."

Colonel Fang Qiming bent to retrieve his fallen clipboard, his fingers fumbling once against the metal clip before he got a grip on it. "Should I file a perimeter breach alert with the central garrison?"

"Tear up that thought right now," General Qin Zhengguo snapped, eyes cutting back to his aide with sudden sharpness. "Premier Gu Chengshan's third rule is already in force. Nobody was ever standing by those pillars. Log an ordinary quiet watch."

Colonel Fang Qiming stood stiffly at attention, swallowing hard. "Yes, General Qin Zhengguo."

General Qin Zhengguo turned back to the glass until ash burned down and stung his fingertips. Only then did he drop the butt into a crystal tray.

"Tell the theater commanders to execute their arrests on schedule," General Qin Zhengguo said, voice low and hollow. "The spectators have left the courtyard."

***

Outside the west annex of the Great Hall, Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi stepped onto the granite stairs at the stroke of nine, carrying locked briefcases holding the signed directive.

Halfway down, Gao Zhengyi stopped dead and caught Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua by the sleeve.

"Look at the west archway," Gao Zhengyi whispered.

Beneath the stone lamps of the central secretariat's courtyard, four matte-black sentries shifted in flawless unison. One smooth step back into the colonnade shadow. No turn of the head. No salute. No parting word.

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua walked swiftly to the balustrade and looked down. Empty flagstone stretched to the iron gates. Not a boot print marked the damp stone near the fountain.

"Gone," Gao Zhengyi breathed, releasing Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua's sleeve. He reached for his cigarettes, then stopped when his hand shook too hard to pull one free. "Nine o'clock sharp. The directive gets signed at eight-fifty-eight, and two minutes later they evaporate."

"Did you expect them to ask for a receipt?" Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua asked softly, jaw tight. "They waited until the ink dried on all forty-plus signatures. Not a minute earlier. Not a minute later."

"Wait," Gao Zhengyi said, a cold thought cutting through his fatigue. "What about Lanshui? What about Yunxi?"

Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua's secure phone buzzed before he could answer. He checked the encrypted flash from the provincial duty desk, watching two more alerts stack immediately beneath it.

"Lanshui provincial compound cleared at nine-zero-zero," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua read flatly. "All five pillar sites in Yunxi, municipal committee, public security, discipline office, court, and procuratorate, cleared at the exact same second. Zero sightings on any exit road. Zero footprints."

Gao Zhengyi exhaled a slow, ragged breath. "No threat. No farewell. No demand for a seat at the table. They simply walked away and left us holding forty-seven arrest warrants."

"Which is far more terrifying than a threat," Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua murmured. He tightened his grip on his briefcase and started down the steps toward his black sedan. "Come on, Gao Zhengyi. We have a country to clean before dawn."

***

In a quiet courtyard three streets north of the inner ring, Elder Song sat beside a dim lamp and an untouched cup of oolong tea. Across from him stood his longtime aide, Lin Haibo, watching the carved moon gate.

At nine o'clock sharp, two dark silhouettes standing beside the stone lanterns beyond the moon gate stepped back together and dissolved into the night.

Lin Haibo took an involuntary step toward the door, chest tight. "Elder Song, they are gone. Both of them. Not a sound on the flagstones."

"Sit down, Lin Haibo," Elder Song said gently, his weathered hand steady on the wood. "Do not chase shadows. You will only catch a chill."

Lin Haibo hesitated, then returned to the table, eyes wide with dread. "Elder Song, I have served beside you twenty-two years. I thought I knew every hidden branch left from the founding generation. Even Premier Gu Chengshan looked pale when those cases arrived. Why did they withdraw tonight without leaving one contact channel behind?"

Elder Song looked past the lamp toward the dark, empty moon gate. Wind rustled faintly through the courtyard bamboo.

"Because the gate has closed," Elder Song said quietly.

Lin Haibo frowned. "Closed?"

"They did not come to rule us, Lin Haibo, and they did not stay to supervise us," Elder Song said, his voice heavy with fifty years of memory. "When a household lets rot spread for twenty years until it touches the wrong doorstep, an unrecorded hand sweeps the rafters clean in one night and sets the ledger on the master's table. Once the master signs his name and picks up the broom, the hand withdraws."

Lin Haibo swallowed hard, staring at the empty courtyard. "And if the state fails to finish what they started? If someone shields Deng Yongchang's remnants, or digs into who sent them?"

Elder Song lifted his cold teacup and set it down untouched. His gaze turned sober and distant.

"Then we will learn what it looks like when the gate opens a second time," Elder Song murmured. "Pray that Gu Chengshan and Zhou Zhenhua never give them a reason."

Across the capital, Western Hills, Lanshui's boulevards, and the five illuminated pillars of Yunxi, empty stone steps settled back into ordinary night. Zero Guard was gone without a trace, leaving the state alone with the reckoning it had been handed.
