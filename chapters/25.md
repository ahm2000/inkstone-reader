# Chapter 25: Ten Yuan for Worms

Near five o'clock, Zhao Dehai pressed a small object into Chen Xuan's hand. "Take this."

It was a slender fishing float carved from yellow wood, worn smooth by years of handling. A tiny square seal was engraved into its base.

"That crooked plastic thing of yours is useless. No wonder your line never moves. Try this one."

Chen Xuan turned it over in his fingers. "That's nice. Looks old."

"Just an old float. Keep it, or you'll blame my reservoir for having no fish."

"Thanks, Mr. Zhao Dehai. If I still catch nothing, I'll blame the float."

He tucked it into his canvas bag beside his green thermos, then pulled out a glass jar topped with a red cloth lid and set it in the old man's hands.

"My grandma's pickled eggs. Bring your grandkids next Saturday, and I'll buy the bait."

Zhao Dehai stared down at the jar for a long moment.

"Hm. Eggs." His voice turned gruff. He cradled the jar in both hands as though it were fragile porcelain, refusing to let his young aide, Liang Yuhang, carry it.

Chen Fugen snatched up the heavy tackle box before his grandson could reach for it.

"You're too thin to carry this, Chen Xuan."

"Alright, Grandpa." Chen Xuan sighed happily and let his grandfather tug him up the path by the sleeve. "He's a nice retired clerk. Borrowed ten yuan and gave me a float."

"A clerk who eats three buns," Chen Fugen grumbled, though he looked pleased.

Behind the willows, Ren Gang caught Liang Yuhang before Zhao Dehai reached the car.

"Liang Yuhang, you swept this bank at dawn and somehow missed a bakery van parked in a cabbage field."

"I only counted the boats, Colonel."

"Did you see that float? The seal on its base is the old gentleman's personal mark. In eleven years, he has never given it to anyone. Today he traded it for ten yuan's worth of worms."

Liang Yuhang blinked in disbelief. "To a young man with no worm on his hook?"

"To a young man whose guards carry the Black Pine. One of them is Number Nine. There are only thirty like him in the entire country. I don't know who that boy is, but anyone who commands those men stands far above a province."

The color drained from Liang Yuhang's face.

"A powerful family?"

"Higher than that."

Over the next ridge, Number Nine spoke into a phone no larger than a matchbox.

"Number One. The old man sitting beside the Boss is Zhao Dehai, Retired Governor of Lanshui. His detail is led by Colonel Ren Gang, forty men strong. This afternoon, Zhao Dehai gave the Boss a fishing float bearing his personal seal. Everyone in Lanshui recognizes that seal. Whoever holds it is counted as his friend."

A long silence hung over the quiet courtyard in Qingyan.

Then Number One, head of the Black Pine Guard, let out a short, dry laugh.

"Does the Boss have any idea who he's been feeding buns to?"

"He called him, sir, 'a nice retired clerk.'"

In the back seat of a black sedan, Zhao Dehai watched the road roll past with his hands resting on his knees. The jar of pickled eggs sat securely in his lap. Nobody had given him a simple, honest gift in years.

A lazy, decent boy, he thought. He never once asked who I was. I like how steady he is.

"Liang Yuhang. Hand me your phone."

He dialed a number from memory.

"Gao Zhengyi. It's Zhao Dehai."

In a living room in the provincial capital, Gao Zhengyi, a Provincial Vice Director, dropped his television remote and bolted upright, his hand flying straight to his collar.

"Governor! Yes, Governor!"

"Your inspection team is scheduled for Yunxi on Wednesday. Move it up to tomorrow, Monday, at nine."

"Tomorrow, Governor? We aren't finished preparing."

"Good. Anyone given time to prepare also has time to hide things. The surface looks calm to me, and I don't trust calm water. Also, there's a duty officer at Old Street Station named Wei Kun. If he's the man I heard about, shake his hand."

"Yes, Governor."

The line clicked dead.

"Who was that?" his wife called from the hallway.

"My old mentor." Gao Zhengyi was still fumbling with his tie.

Why on earth would a retired governor ask him to shake a county policeman's hand? The old man had never asked him for a personal favor before.

* * *

Eleven chairs encircled the long oak table. Every seat silently measured how much its occupant mattered.

The Standing Committee was the inner circle that truly ran Yunxi County. The closer a man sat to the Party Secretary, the greater his authority.

Seat One faced the door. Party Secretary Fan Weiguo sat there in a crisp white shirt, slowly turning his teacup.

Seat Two belonged to County Magistrate Tao Shu, who ran the county government. He had waited eight years for Fan Weiguo's chair.

Tao Shu smiled at him like an attentive nephew. Inside Tao Shu's briefcase lay a gray folder marked with a single name: *Fan Weiguo.* He had spent years filling it with every misstep the Party Secretary had ever made.

Seat Three was Kang Guangming of the Discipline Commission. His mandate was to investigate officials, even Seat One. Fan Weiguo could never read what sat behind his quiet eyes.

Seat Four was Shao Wenhua, head of the Organization Department, the office that appointed and promoted every official in the county. He had not spoken a word in nine straight meetings.

Seat Five was Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu Wenzhe. He controlled the county's finances and was Tao Shu's closest ally at the table.

"Secretary," Tao Shu said smoothly. "The provincial inspection team arrives Wednesday at nine. I propose forming a county self-review group, led by myself. We will put every Old Street file in order so the inspectors see clean paperwork."

He rested one finger lightly on his briefcase. "I have kept a folder on you for six years, Secretary. It includes the Riverside land lease your brother-in-law won. If my group handles the review, the inspectors will never lay eyes on it."

If Fan Weiguo refused, Tao Shu would whisper to the city that the Party Secretary did not trust his own county. If he agreed, Tao Shu would control every ledger in Yunxi by Wednesday morning.

"Secretary Fan." Kang Guangming had not spoken for an hour, and every head turned toward him. "Before any vote, there is something I need to read into the record."

He drew a single sheet from his folder.

"This morning, the official car of Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu Wenzhe was parked for forty minutes behind the private office of Hongrui Real Estate. At the rear entrance. My officers observed it directly."

Lu Wenzhe let out a laugh that was a shade too loud. "I went over to tell Dong Haisheng to cooperate with the authorities!"

"After the city opened an investigation into Dong Haisheng at four this morning?" Kang Guangming asked mildly. "Through the back door?"

Lu Wenzhe gripped his porcelain teacup so hard it cracked in his palm.

Beneath the table, Tao Shu's hand clenched and released. His proposed self-review group had just been tied to an official caught sneaking into a developer's back door.

Fan Weiguo felt the knot in his chest loosen. He seized the opening.

"Comrade Lu Wenzhe. Effective immediately, you are removed from the Old Street project. Secretary Kang will seal your office records. Magistrate Tao, your offer is considerate, but the inspectors will decide for themselves when they arrive."

"Of course, Secretary." Tao Shu kept his polite smile intact. Leaning slightly closer so only Fan Weiguo could hear, he murmured, "You just sealed a deputy magistrate's office without a committee vote. I kept the minutes myself. If the inspectors ask, your name is the only one on that order."

In Seat Four, Shao Wenhua capped his fountain pen and gave Fan Weiguo one small, deliberate nod. He still said nothing, yet in that room it was the loudest statement anyone had made.
