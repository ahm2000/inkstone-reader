# Chapter 25: The Number That Doesn't Fit

Past midnight, inside the nineteenth-floor Financial Intelligence command room in Sha City, silence pressed heavily on everyone's ears. Nobody sat at the side desks anymore. Every analyst stood still along the back wall, eyes locked on the twelve-meter main screen.

Number 1 stood two steps behind Number 11's desk, his dark coat buttoned to the collar, standing straight and still. Beside his elbow sat the lead-lined metal case Number 34 had brought from the tech team forty minutes earlier.

"Shengyuan Capital's Sha City vault is not a one-province system," Number 11 said, his voice flat and calm as his fingers moved across the keypad. "Its login method, shell company layers, and overseas transfer paths all follow one shared pattern. Using that pattern, we ran a twenty-year check across our entire domestic and overseas financial archive."

"How many provinces share that pattern?" Number 1 asked quietly.

"Fourteen," Number 11 answered.

Number 1 did not blink, yet the quiet tension in his shoulders sharpened. "Show me the check."

Number 11 hit enter. A dark map of the country unfolded across the main glass wall. Fourteen provinces lit up in yellow, linked by glowing lines of money draining out of inland mines, coastal shipping ports, and state factory belts toward three overseas hubs: Singapore, Zurich, and the Cayman Islands.

In the top right corner, a stark white counter began rolling upward as White Guard's tracking software matched twenty years of transfers against customs records, bond gaps, and land books.

"Year one through year five," Number 11 said calmly. "Rigged sales of state assets, cheap coal and rare-earth deals, and hidden local bonds."

White numbers blurred upward. 3 trillion yuan. 7 trillion. 10 trillion flashed past without slowing. Along the back wall, two junior analysts exchanged a stiff glance.

"Year six through year twelve," Number 73 added from the next desk, his voice low and dry. "Skimming from deep-water port leases, rigged state energy import deals, and layers of hidden debt rolled through provincial banks."

The counter crossed 25 trillion yuan, then 40 trillion. Number 73's jaw tightened as the numbers climbed past 60 trillion.

"Year thirteen through year twenty," Number 11 said, his eyes fixed on the screen. "Compounded profit skimming, illegal state currency swaps, off-the-books rare-earth smuggling routes like Old Street in Yunxi County, and conversion into overseas gold-backed cash papers."

78 trillion. 91 trillion. 98 trillion.

With a soft chime, the white numbers locked in the center of the black glass wall.

The verified twenty-year loss of state money across fourteen provinces exceeded 100 trillion yuan.

Nobody spoke. Number 73 let out a slow, shaky breath and lifted his hands off the keyboard. Even for specialists used to tracking national wealth funds and central bank accounts, a number over 100 trillion yuan refused to fit inside an ordinary mind.

"Check for duplicates," Number 11 murmured to Number 73, his own voice barely steady. "Make sure nothing was counted twice between local shell companies and overseas accounts."

"Checked twice," Number 73 replied quietly, staring at the white numbers. "Every single yuan is net stolen state money. That is the real floor, not the ceiling."

That was not just stolen cash. That was twenty years of schools never built, bridges left weak, defense research starved of funds, and ordinary families like Chen Mingde's and Lin Guoliang's crushed by county thugs just to protect the smallest valves of a hidden machine. It was a deep wound carved straight out of the heart of the country.

Yet as Number 1 stared at the glowing white numbers, that huge figure somehow shrank in his eyes.

His gaze dropped to the small red-flagged folder sitting beside the lead-lined case. On the side monitor, paused in crisp white and red lines, sat Number 34's three unlocked files: active warship radar blueprints, southern coastal power-grid control keys, and border troop supply rosters.

Beside those stolen secrets, even 100 trillion yuan looked like the smallest piece on the board. Money could be traced, taken back, and rebuilt over decades. Exposed warship radar plans, power-grid shutoff keys, and betrayed border supply lines could cost hundreds of thousands of lives in a single afternoon.

"Number 11," Number 1 said, his calm voice carrying a weight that made every operative straighten at once. "Is the proof across all fourteen provinces completely airtight?"

"Every entry is double-checked against independent bank records and overseas bank proof," Number 11 answered at once. "Zero guesses. No missing pieces."

"Number 34," Number 1 continued, turning toward the glass wall. "Has the locked hard drive data moved past the middleman's drop point in Sha City?"

"The last batch cleared four days ago," Number 34 answered evenly, stepping forward half a pace. "The next scheduled handoff is in seventy-two hours, using the Sha City vault's backup transfer window."

"Do we have eyes on the middleman?" Number 1 asked.

"Number 14 and Number 27 have him watched inside the Sha City Grand River Hotel," Number 34 confirmed. "He has no idea his safe was ever opened."

Number 1 stood silent for ten full seconds, staring at the dark glass wall.

For the first time in fifteen years of leading White Guard, Number 1 felt the hard limit of what his group was built to do. White Guard was a private shield forged to protect Chen Xuan, crush business plots, stop crime rings, and clean up regional threats quietly from the shadows.

They could tear down Hongrui Real Estate before sunrise, erase Yan Tiehu's thugs, bury Cheng Biao's station under inspection audits, and bankrupt Shengyuan Capital's shell companies in forty-eight hours.

But pulling out a twenty-year, fourteen-province, 100 trillion yuan treason network backed by foreign military intelligence and planted inside top state offices was beyond any private guard's authority. Striking too early without absolute power would scatter deeply hidden traitors, burn records, spark bank panics, or push them to hand over the remaining defense keys in desperation.

White Guard had hit its ceiling.

"Freeze all active strikes in Yunxi County and Sha City," Number 1 ordered quietly. "Keep silent watch on Old Street, the quarry tunnel, Cheng Biao, Zhou Quan, Deng Yongchang, and the Sha City hotel middleman. Alert no targets. Touch no servers until further orders."

"Understood," Number 11, Number 34, and Number 73 answered in quiet unison.

Number 1 walked alone to the far corner, stopped before a dark steel cabinet, and lifted a single-line secure phone connected to only one receiver in the world. He pressed the single blank key.

Two hundred kilometers east in Yunxi County, inside the quiet third-floor bedroom of Building 2 at Fuxing New Village, the dark phone on the nightstand vibrated once.

Chen Xuan opened his eyes in the dark, his gaze clear and untouched by sleep. With calm, unhurried fingers, he lifted the phone to his ear.

"Speak," Chen Xuan said softly.

Across the secure line, Number 1's voice came through, steady as always, yet heavier than Chen Xuan had heard it in many years.

"White Guard has hit its ceiling," Number 1 said quietly. "You need to see what we found."
