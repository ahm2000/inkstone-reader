# Chapter 25: What the Detail Cannot Do

Late Sunday night in the Detail's courtyard, Chief An Zhiyuan was reading, as he read anything important, on paper, with a pot of tea at his elbow and his glasses on the end of his nose.

Tonight's report was one page long, from Gong Yi, and Chief An had already read it four times!

*A girl, thirteen, calls herself Tang Xiaoyu. Stood outside my shop four nights. Says her father is Tang Dawei, a laborer at Tiancheng Demolition on Dongqiao Road. The Boss fed her, took her to the station, and brought her home on Saturday. She sleeps in his sister's room.*

Chief An set the page down and looked at his own hands, which ached the way a man's hands ache when he is holding them still.

He knew exactly how long the job would take: one call to the Provincial Labor Department, one search of hospital records, and one request for the Tiancheng payroll.

A quarter of an hour at most, and the whole Detail would know where Tang Dawei worked, where he slept and where he was right now!

After forty years in this work, Chief An feared the worst. He had no proof, and he would not say the word aloud! But what father who was only "late" leaves a thirteen-year-old at a noodle shop for four nights?

So why not do it?

There were three reasons, and Chief An hated every one of them!

First, the Boss's own first rule said the ordinary road comes first.

Second, the Detail followed the stay-ordinary order the strictest way of all, which meant nothing around the Boss could be changed or helped by them unless he gave a signal.

The reason was simple. If the Detail solved this in fifteen minutes, it would no longer be the Boss's way of helping a child but the Detail's way, and a girl who had already been hurt by people deciding things over her head did not need that. Would she feel helped, or handled?

Third, and this one was the hardest, the Boss might still say nothing, and they would have to stand by and watch!

The courtyard gate creaked, and in came Mo Lin, Number Nine of the Detail, a man of few words in a gray jacket, with a phone in his hand.

"Chief, the Boss sent a message at twelve minutes past eight tonight, to Attorney Bai, with a copy to your private line," he said, holding out the screen.

*Attorney Bai, sorry to trouble you on a Sunday. Could you make a normal inquiry, lawfully, no shortcuts? A laborer named Tang Dawei, who worked for Tiancheng Demolition on Dongqiao Road. Please check only what any citizen's lawyer could check. Thank you.*

Chief An read it slowly, and two phrases stayed in his mind: a normal inquiry, and lawfully, no shortcuts.

The Boss had not asked the Detail to do anything beyond that, so a normal inquiry was all the Detail would do, and the corner of the old man's mouth lifted.

"Level Three!"

Level Three meant a professional stepping in under a real, valid identity, the way any family can hire one. Attorney Bai was a real lawyer with a real license, so he was the smallest door the Boss could knock on. An entrustment is a signed letter that lets a lawyer act for the family, and with one, a lawyer could ask the Labor Inspection office for a worker's insurance and injury records, exactly as any lawyer in the county could. Out of all the doors in the world, the Boss had picked the smallest one!

"He didn't say 'Chief.' He said 'normal,'" said Mo Lin dryly. "I think that was the whole point."

"Mm," said Chief An.

The gate opened again, and Attorney Bai Qingzhou, sixty, silver-haired, in a charcoal coat, walked in with a leather folder and no hurry at all.

He took the phone, read the message, and nodded. "I will go to Yunxi County on Wednesday morning," he said.

"Wednesday? Not Monday, when the office opens?" asked Mo Lin.

"A lawyer needs the signed entrustment of the girl's temporary caretaker first, and by mail it reaches me on Tuesday. Then I check every form twice, because if I go with one paper missing, I must go twice, and I do not intend to waste anybody's time."

"You'll print the request here?" Mo Lin glanced at the corner of the courtyard, where a sleek gray machine hummed on a shelf. "Chief's printer is fast, and every copy is perfect."

"That is exactly the trouble," said Bai. "Perfect copies would look like Detail paperwork, and a clerk might wonder who sent me. I will use an ordinary office copier, with its ordinary gray streak, like everyone else."

Mo Lin made a small, disbelieving sound, because in his experience, anyone who chose the slow door while a fast one stood open either had something to prove or did not understand how doors worked.

"A streak is a risk, Chief! The courtyard printer could print it clean."

"Nine!" said Chief An.

"Yes, Chief."

"Step away from the printer."

"I haven't touched it," said Mo Lin.

"You are standing in front of it," said Chief An.

Mo Lin stepped aside with total dignity!

Attorney Bai tucked the folder under his arm and, for the first time that night, smiled.

"Please do not worry about me. I once sat for years in the back row of the provincial court, waiting for a judge to finish his tea, and I am the most patient man in Lanshui. I will hand the request through the front window and wait in line like everyone else."

Nobody laughed.

Was he joking? They only stared at him!

Attorney Bai had stood before the highest benches in the province, and the last time he stood in line at a ticket window was long before most of them had joined the Detail, yet he said it the way another man says he will walk to the corner shop.

"The first answer?" asked Chief An quietly.

"Ready by Wednesday." Bai picked up his coat. "Not by any shortcut, Chief. I know exactly what the clerk needs, and I will bring all of it the first time. I will take a number, and I will wait."

When the gate closed behind him, Mo Lin cleared his throat. "Chief, what if the answer is the one you think it is?"

Silence.

Chief An poured two cups of tea, and neither of them drank.

"Then somebody will have to tell a thirteen-year-old girl," he said at last. "And it will not be us."

Mo Lin looked at the two full cups and, for once, had nothing to say.
