# Chapter 25: The Cell Door

In the detention corridor, Chen Mingde sat on the concrete bench, folding and unfolding a paper cup.

When the steel door swung open, he rose out of habit, the same way he stood to greet customers at his shop.

"Mr. Chen Mingde." Mai Zhenhua took the old man's hand in both of his and bowed.

"We held you wrongly and damaged your good name. I apologize to you, your wife, and your daughter in Wuhan."

Chen Mingde shook his head gently. "Director, I'm only a hardware shopkeeper. I'm nobody important."

"You are to me." Mai Zhenhua turned to the officers lined along the wall, keeping his voice level. "Every officer who worked this corridor since Friday will submit a written statement by noon detailing who gave which order. Anyone who lies is finished. The next time a citizen is held like this, the officer who signed the order sits on that bench within the hour, and I will lock the door myself."

Once Mai Zhenhua stepped ahead to escort Chen Mingde toward the front lobby, Zhong Hai caught Wei Kun alone at the mouth of the corridor, his hands twitching at his sides.

"Wei Kun," he whispered, pale to the lips. "Before anyone else asks, I have to tell you myself. Yesterday I sent Lei Bo the timestamp you wrote. Seven-fifty. They offered sixty thousand yuan for Zhong Tiantian's heart surgery."

Wei Kun's face went through shock, then a deep, quiet hurt. His right hand clenched into a fist, and Zhong Hai squeezed his eyes shut and waited.

The fist trembled, then slowly opened.

"Does Zhong Tiantian go into surgery on Thursday?"

"Thursday," Zhong Hai whispered.

"Then go sit with him," Wei Kun said roughly. "And don't speak to me today."

Zhong Hai bowed his head low, turned, and hurried down the hall.

* * *

The minute Mai asked for the key, Chen Xuan had texted his father: *He is coming out.* At 6:20, the glass doors opened and Chen Mingde stepped out into the morning air with a gray blanket draped over his shoulders.

Chen Xuan stood up so fast his thermos rolled down a step.

"Second Uncle." His voice was tight with relief.

"Brother!" Chen Jianguo shouted from the curb. He ran faster than he had in twenty years, catching his brother in a fierce embrace.

Wu Lanying wept silently, pressing both hands over her mouth.

Chen Xuan stepped in and wrapped his arms around his uncle, his eyes reddening as he held on tightly.

"I'm sorry we kept you waiting so long, Second Uncle. I'm just glad you're out."

"Chen Xuan." Chen Mingde's big, rough hand patted the back of his shoulder. "You came. That was all I needed."

Wu Lanying pulled them all together, and for a long minute nobody spoke.

Chen Jianguo's voice shook with relief. "Heaven has eyes."

Chen Mingde shook his head softly. "It was luck. A good director, an early car. I didn't do anything at all."

Wu Lanying dabbed her eyes and touched Chen Xuan's cool cheek. "Chen Xuan, you've been sitting out here since five."

"I couldn't sleep anyway," Chen Xuan said with a warm, tired smile. "Wanted to be the first one to greet him."

At 6:35, Liu Meifang hurried up the street carrying a steel pot of soup and pressed a lid into Chen Mingde's hands.

"Drink! It's still hot! And if anyone says one more word against my brother-in-law, I brought a ladle."

She pressed a second lid into Chen Xuan's hands. "Drink, you too! Look how thin your wrists are."

He took a sip. Mai Zhenhua stood at the top of the steps, and Chen Xuan looked up at him.

"Director Mai. Thanks for opening the door."

Mai Zhenhua looked at the calm, ordinary young man beside Chen Mingde. Just a logistics clerk, he told himself, yet someone had filed nineteen pages at 11:44 last night.

"It was our duty," Mai Zhenhua replied stiffly. Right then, his phone buzzed in his coat pocket. One glance at the screen drained the color from his face.

*General Office, Provincial Department of Public Security.*

Since Friday night, when masked men had stood in every office and nobody could say who sent them, every senior official in Yunxi had been afraid of his own phone. Mai Zhenhua turned away from the steps to answer. His hand was not steady. "Yes, sir."

"Yes. He has been released, sir. He's with his family right now. Soup, sir. His sister-in-law brought him soup."

At the glass doors, Wei Kun stood holding his cap. Chen Xuan caught his eye and gave him a respectful nod.

"Thank you for the hot water, Officer Wei. My uncle told me. It meant a great deal."

Wei Kun flushed to his ears and found himself smiling.

Back inside his sedan, Mai Zhenhua thought of the matte-black card in his bedside drawer at home. He had tried to throw it away twice and could not.

He typed the young man's name into the police terminal.

Chen Xuan, twenty-nine, born in Yunxi. Honest, quiet, spotless record. Ordinary in every way.

A slow chill crept down Mai Zhenhua's spine. That calm manner. The timing. A call from the provincial office before breakfast, all over a hardware shopkeeper.

His thumb hovered over a gray button marked *Restricted*. After a long beat, he locked the screen.

A wise man does not open a door when he cannot bear to see what stands behind it.
