# Chapter 212: Hou Liangping's Lead

Late Wednesday morning, pale December sunlight slanted through the frosted blinds of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau in Jingzhou. On the corkboard at the back of the room, red strings linked three coastal logistics shell companies to a cluster of shipping manifests stamped for Shanghai Port.

Hou Liangping stood by the window with his phone pressed to his ear, a steaming mug of strong black tea resting on the sill. It was 10:45 AM Beijing time (+8:00 GMT), and his wife Zhong Xiaoai had just stepped out of a briefing at the Central Discipline Commission in the capital.

"Did you remember to wear the gray wool scarf I packed?" Zhong Xiaoai asked over the line, her voice crisp and warm at the same time. "The weather forecast says Jingzhou dropped below freezing last night."

"I have it right here on the coat rack, Director Zhong," Hou Liangping replied with an easy grin. "Are you calling as my wife or inspecting my winter uniform?"

"Both," she said, laughing softly before her tone turned serious. "Liangping, I am only giving you a three-minute window before my next meeting. Our financial tracking unit in Beijing flagged a dirty transfer bouncing out of Handong's coastal freight accounts toward Shanghai. My father always says small dockside rats chew through the thickest cables when everyone only watches the big towers. Keep your eyes open and do not rush ahead without proper paperwork."

"Tell Elder Zhong I heard him loud and clear," Hou Liangping said. "And do not skip lunch again."

He ended the call, slipped his phone into his jacket pocket, and walked back to the long wooden conference table where Chen Hai and Chief Procurator Ji Changming were bent over a stack of bank wire printouts.

Chen Hai, the former Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief and Hou Liangping's oldest classmate, tapped a yellow highlighter against a line of bold digits.

"This is the missing link," Chen Hai said, his voice steady and focused. "Two days ago, a Jingzhou trading shell called Yongshun Marine Supplies wired 500k yuan through two fake equipment invoices. The money bounced through a sub-branch in Suzhou and landed in a private wealth account registered to the brother-in-law of Captain Lu, a senior maritime inspection officer stationed at Shanghai Port Terminal Six."

Hou Liangping leaned over the printout, his eyes narrowing. "Who controls Yongshun Marine Supplies?"

"We pulled the beneficial ownership registry and the corporate tax filings," Chen Hai answered, sliding a second sheet across the table. "Every trail leads straight back to Tianhai International. Specifically, their regional operations director, Qian Weidong."

Ji Changming adjusted his wire-rimmed glasses and frowned as he studied the shipping schedules beside the bank records. As Handong's Chief Procurator, he was always the most cautious voice in the room.

"Tianhai International is a major coastal logistics player," Ji Changming said slowly. "Why would Qian Weidong risk wiring 500k yuan across provincial lines to bribe a single inspection captain at Terminal Six?"

"Because Tianhai International just lost a flagship European export contract to Shanghai's Haiyue Group," Hou Liangping said, pointing to a red circle on the port manifest. "Look at the departure log. Haiyue Group's main cargo vessel, the Haiyue Star, is scheduled to finish loading at Terminal Six this evening and clear the outbound channel before 9:00 PM to catch the winter tide and meet its buyer window in Zurich (+1:00 GMT)."

Chen Hai nodded grimly. "If Captain Lu invents a technical violation and detains the Haiyue Star tonight, Haiyue Group misses its delivery window by at least three days. That triggers an 80 million yuan penalty clause and gives Qian Weidong an opening to steal the European client back."

Ji Changming sat back in his chair, drumming his fingers lightly against his leather folder. "The evidence on the 500k yuan transfer is solid, Liangping, and the motive makes complete sense. However, Terminal Six is in Shanghai, not Handong. You cannot walk onto another municipality's port and slap handcuffs on a maritime officer without joint jurisdiction. If we break procedure, Tianhai International's lawyers will tear the case apart by Friday."

"I would never give you a sloppy case file, Chief Procurator," Hou Liangping replied with a confident smile. "Remember how Secretary Tian Guofu mentioned cross-border freight tips in the Standing Committee this morning? Our bureau already transmitted the verified bank wire receipts and the timestamped call logs between Qian Weidong and Captain Lu to the Shanghai Maritime Safety Administration and the joint discipline liaison office."

Ji Changming raised an eyebrow. "And they approved a joint strike?"

"Their regional commissioner is reviewing the warrant right now," Hou Liangping said, checking his watch. "If Qian Weidong is bold enough to pay 500k yuan for a single night's detention, he will be sitting right inside Captain Lu's dock office tonight to make sure the ship stays locked down. Catch them together over the fake inspection order, and we do not just stop a dirty trade ambush. We roll up the entire Handong-to-Shanghai port bribery chain in one clean sweep."

Chen Hai stood up and handed Hou Liangping a sealed evidence pouch containing the certified bank records. "Watch your back down by the docks, Liangping. The December fog at Pudong Port gets thick after sunset, and Qian Weidong has desperate friends."

Ji Changming picked up his red pen, paused for two seconds, and signed his crisp signature across the cross-provincial authorization form.

"Go," Ji Changming said, looking up over his glasses. "Keep the chain of evidence airtight, coordinate strictly with Shanghai's maritime leadership, and bring me a case that nobody in Jingzhou or Shanghai can bury."

Hou Liangping tucked the authorization form into his briefcase, grabbed his dark wool coat and the gray scarf Zhong Xiaoai had packed, and headed straight out into the crisp winter air toward the high-speed railway station.
