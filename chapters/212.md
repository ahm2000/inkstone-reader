# Chapter 212: Hou Liangping's Lead

In the bustling command room of the Handong Provincial Anti-Corruption Bureau, large magnetic whiteboards stretched across the entire eastern wall, covered in complex banking diagrams, corporate hierarchies, and port surveillance photographs.

Hou Liangping, Chief of the Anti-Corruption Bureau, stood before the central board holding a blue dry-erase marker. His dark hair was slightly rumpled, his shirt sleeves rolled neatly up to his elbows, and his sharp, perceptive eyes tracked a series of thick red lines connecting offshore bank accounts in the Cayman Islands to local maritime inspection bureaus along the eastern coast.

Around him, senior investigators Lu Yike, Chen Hai, and Lin Huafeng worked with rapid, rhythmic focus at their computer terminals, cross-referencing confiscated corporate files against federal customs databases and provincial tax registries.

Chief Procurator Ji Changming walked into the room carrying a porcelain mug of hot tea. He stopped beside Hou Liangping, watching the younger prosecutor draw a firm circle around a corporate logo labeled *Tianhai International Shipping Group*.

"Liangping," Ji Changming said in a low, measured voice. "The standing committee meeting concluded twenty minutes ago. Secretary Sha Ruijin and Secretary Li Dakang have granted our task force full authority to proceed. What did the forensic analysis of the Changbai hard drives reveal?"

Hou Liangping turned around, a sharp, confident glint shining in his dark eyes as he placed the marker on the metal tray.

"Procurator Ji, the Changbai seizure was nothing short of a tactical masterpiece," Hou Liangping said, his voice brimming with professional admiration. "The mysterious task force that dismantled the northern bunker preserved every single byte of financial data without corruption. In the secondary ledgers, our forensic accountants uncovered an off-the-books slush fund known as the Southern Harbor Channel, totaling 180 million yuan."

"Where did the funds flow?" Ji Changming asked, his brow furrowing as he leaned closer to the board.

"Directly into private accounts controlled by Qian Weidong, the regional director of Tianhai International," Hou Liangping explained, pointing his pen at a series of encrypted bank transactions. "For the past five years, Qian Weidong served as Seven-Harbor's regional enforcer in the Yangtze River Delta and Handong waters. Whenever an honest competitor won a legitimate port tender, Qian Weidong deployed payoffs to corrupt maritime safety inspectors. The inspectors would invent fictitious environmental or mechanical violations, impounding the competitor's vessels at the pier until shipping deadlines expired and contracts were forfeited."

Lu Yike turned around in her swivel chair, holding up a printed bank telegraphic transfer record.

"Director Hou, Procurator Ji," Lu Yike reported with urgent clarity. "We just intercepted an emergency wire transfer executed forty minutes ago from Qian Weidong's personal account in Macau. Five hundred thousand yuan was wired directly to the offshore account of Captain Lu, the Chief Safety Inspector at Pudong Port Terminal Six."

Chen Hai looked up from his monitor, tapping his keyboard to pull up Captain Lu's background file. "Captain Lu has held the Terminal Six inspection post for four years. Over that time, seven independent shipping firms have filed formal administrative complaints against him, alleging arbitrary detention of cargo. Every complaint was quietly suppressed by local port officials who had personal ties to the old Shanshui Group syndicate."

Ji Changming's face hardened with stern disapproval. "Terminal Six is the primary docking terminal for the new northern coastal feeder vessels. Which vessel is berthed there right now?"

"The *Haiyue Star*," Hou Liangping answered, his expression turning cold and serious. "It is the flagship container vessel of Haiyue Group, carrying 50 million yuan worth of precision electronic components and refrigerated agricultural goods scheduled for export to Qingdao on tonight's high tide. If the vessel is delayed beyond midnight, Haiyue Group faces devastating liquidated damage claims, and the entire new coastal route will be thrown into chaos."

"Qian Weidong is deliberately sabotaging President Qin Yuhan's operations to force Haiyue into a predatory sub-contracting agreement," Ji Changming noted with deep anger. "He thinks he can operate with the same arrogance the Zhao family once used in Jingzhou before the sweep."

"He forgets that Handong is no longer a playground for corrupt syndicates," Hou Liangping said, walking over to his desk and picking up his dark suit jacket. He slipped his arms into the sleeves, adjusted his gold national badge on his lapel, and picked up his leather warrant folder. "The era of corrupt inspectors shaking down honest businesses ends tonight."

"What is your immediate tactical plan, Liangping?" Ji Changming inquired.

"I am taking Lu Yike and our rapid-response procuratorial team directly to Shanghai Port Terminal Six," Hou Liangping declared with absolute resolve. "We have already coordinated with Commissioner Shen Bo of the Shanghai Maritime Safety Administration and Captain Zhou Ming of State Security. We will catch Captain Lu with the bribe money on his desk, revoke his authority, and ensure the *Haiyue Star* clears the harbor without a minute of delay."

Ji Changming nodded with deep pride, clapping a firm hand on Hou Liangping's shoulder.

"Go, Liangping," Ji Changming said. "Uphold the law, protect legitimate enterprise, and show the public that the nation's trade gates belong to the people."
