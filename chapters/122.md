# Chapter 122: The Husband's Lucky Map

Tuesday evening arrived with a cool autumn breeze drifting off the Huangpu River. The front door of Emerald Riverside Villa clicked open at 7:30 PM, and Qin Yuhan stepped into the foyer looking thoroughly drained.

Her day on the 38th floor of Haiyue Tower had been an endless string of arguments. She slipped off her black heels beside the shoe cabinet, rubbed her aching temples, and walked into the warm living room carrying a thick blue folder packed with real estate maps and port shipping reports.

On the plush fabric sofa facing the glass balcony and the glowing skyline of Lujiazui, Chen Xuan was lounging comfortably against two soft cushions in his gray cotton pajamas. In his left hand, he held a crisp red Fuji apple. In his right hand, a small paring knife glided smoothly around the fruit, peeling off a single unbroken red ribbon of skin.

On the low glass coffee table in front of him sat his dented green metal thermos and the Tuesday evening edition of the Shanghai Municipal Economic Daily.

"You look like you fought three boardrooms at once," Chen Xuan said, sitting up and offering her the freshly peeled apple. "Sit down. I simmered ginger pear soup on the stove. Give me one minute."

Before Qin Yuhan could protest, he padded into the kitchen and returned with a steaming porcelain bowl of pale golden soup fragrant with rock sugar and sliced autumn pear. She took a careful spoonful, feeling the gentle warmth soothe her dry throat.

"Director Zhao and Qin Haoran are impossible," Qin Yuhan sighed, setting the blue folder onto the coffee table and spreading out a large coastal shipping and warehouse map. "They brought a full valuation team to the 38th floor today. They want Haiyue Group to invest 300 million yuan to acquire an old riverside warehouse complex in the northern dock zone. They claim the city is expanding freight capacity there and that the land will double in value within two years."

Chen Xuan leaned forward with a lazy stretch, resting his elbows on his knees as he looked down at the colorful map. "300 million yuan is a lot of braised pork ribs. Do you trust Director Zhao's numbers?"

"Not for a second," Qin Yuhan said, tapping the northern dock zone with her pen. "The lease history looks messy, and the seller is an offshore shell company that refuses to disclose its actual owners. Even worse, my own logistics team is divided over which eastern terminal route will link best to the new Handong provincial freight corridor. If I cannot counter Director Zhao's proposal with solid facts at tomorrow morning's board meeting, the older shareholders might side with him."

Chen Xuan yawned lightly and reached over to pick up the Tuesday evening newspaper from the coffee table. He flipped smoothly to page four and pointed a finger at a small rectangular box tucked into the bottom corner beneath the shipping index.

"Wife, you work too hard to read the boring pages," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile. "Look at this little notice right here. It just came out this afternoon."

Qin Yuhan leaned closer, her eyes scanning the dense black print. Within three seconds, her breath caught.

It was an official public announcement from the Shanghai Municipal Planning and Environmental Bureau. Starting next quarter, the entire northern dock strip was being rezoned into an ecological riverside park and public greenbelt. All commercial warehouse expansion permits in that zone were immediately frozen, and existing freight yards would be required to relocate within eighteen months.

Qin Yuhan stared at the paper in disbelief. "If Haiyue Group spent 300 million yuan buying those warehouses tomorrow, we would be stuck with unusable land before the ink dried on the contract. Director Zhao and Qin Haoran either failed to check the municipal bulletin, or they are intentionally trying to dump a dead asset onto the company."

"Probably the second one," Chen Xuan remarked casually, taking a sip of tea from his green thermos. "People who rush you to sign a 300 million yuan check before lunch usually have something to hide."

Qin Yuhan folded the page carefully and tucked it straight into her blue folder, her eyes shining with sudden relief. That single newspaper clipping was enough to crush Director Zhao's scheme in five minutes flat.

Then she glanced back down at the shipping map. "What about the eastern corridor? My analysts gave me three possible transfer terminals, and every report contradicts the others."

Chen Xuan picked up her red ballpoint pen, twirled it once between his fingers, and drew a neat red circle around a quieter deep-water terminal southeast of the main docks. From there, he drew a clean straight line heading west toward Jingzhou in Handong Province.

"If you ask a quiet logistics clerk who stares at freight charts all day, pick this one," Chen Xuan said lightly. "The main northern bridges are always choked with container trucks in autumn, and the toll fees eat up the margin. This eastern terminal connects directly to the newly upgraded rail spur. Fewer delays, lower fuel costs, and clean customs clearance. Call it your husband's lucky map."

Qin Yuhan studied the red line for a long moment. The more she looked at the road junctions and rail connections he had casually traced, the more obvious the logic became. Her expensive consulting team had missed the simplest, most efficient path on the entire page.

"How does a clerk who naps at his desk notice things my directors miss?" she asked softly, leaning her head briefly against his shoulder.

"Simple," Chen Xuan grinned, tapping the side of his thermos. "I drink good tea, I clock out at 4:55 PM, and my brain is not fried by meetings."

Qin Yuhan laughed, feeling lighter than she had all week. She took his hand and squeezed it gently. "Since my husband just saved Haiyue Group 300 million yuan, you have to come with me to the Waldorf Bund Gala tomorrow evening. Otherwise, I will be stuck listening to boring heirs trying to pitch me business deals all night."

"Deal," Chen Xuan agreed at once. "Just make sure the hotel buffet has decent pastries."
