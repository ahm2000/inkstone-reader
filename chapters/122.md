# Chapter 122: Invitation to the Handong Provincial Forum

Monday morning at Hengxin Logistics began with the hum of printers and the cheerful clatter of tea mugs.

Ever since the port customs sweep cleared out the crooked middlemen, the office had breathed easier.

By nine-twenty, Chen Xuan sat at his corner desk, checking cold-chain receipts with a blue pen.

Suddenly, the glass door to the general manager's office swung open.

Sun Haoran stepped out holding a stiff cream-and-gold envelope in both hands, his round face flushed with excitement.

"Everyone stop for two minutes," Sun Haoran called out. "Hu Dewei, Zhang Pengfei, Chen Xuan, come to the meeting table. Bring your pens."

Hu Dewei paused over his teacup, squinting at the gold seal.

Around the scarred meeting table, Sun Haoran laid the invitation flat so everyone could see the bright red stamp of the Provincial Commerce Office at the bottom.

"We just got invited to next week's Handong Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum," Sun Haoran announced, grinning broadly. "Not as outside visitors buying tickets. As an official guest company from Shanghai."

Zhang Pengfei whistled softly, leaning over the table to squint at the gold lettering. "General Manager Sun, is that real? Last year only the big port groups with fifty trucks got invited to Jingzhou City."

"It is completely real," Sun Haoran said, wiping sweat from his forehead with a tissue. "The provincial office reviewed every customs record in Shanghai after last week's sweep. Because Hengxin Logistics caught that port overcharge early and kept the cleanest shipping books in our district, the commerce office put our name on the model-company list. They gave us two official guest passes."

For a heartbeat, pride shone in Sun Haoran's eyes.

"Two passes," Sun Haoran muttered, tapping his knuckles on the table. "I have to go myself, of course. The deputy governor will sit in the front row, and the big provincial freight chairmen will be shaking hands in the hall. If Hengxin Logistics does not show up, it looks disrespectful. I need one steady person from our team to travel north with me, carry the company files, and sit through the three days of meetings."

The moment Sun Haoran said "three days of meetings," Hu Dewei's cheerful expression shifted into heavy, heroic exhaustion.

Hu Dewei sighed loudly and pressed two fingers to his temple. "General Manager Sun, this is a huge honor for Hengxin. Honestly, I would love to carry your briefcase to Jingzhou City myself. The trouble is, next week is right when our south warehouse lease comes up for renewal, and three local tire suppliers want me to inspect their storage yards in person."

Sun Haoran blinked. "Hu Dewei, the warehouse lease does not expire until the end of the quarter."

"Better to lock the landlord down early before he raises the rent," Hu Dewei said smoothly, waving a hand as if carrying the whole company on his shoulders. "And you know my lower back acts up whenever I sit on a train for more than two hours. Four hours on the express line to Jingzhou City, plus sitting on hard hotel chairs listening to speeches from nine to five? I would be hobbling around the banquet hall like an old man, embarrassing Hengxin in front of the provincial leaders."

Before Sun Haoran could reply, Zhang Pengfei jumped in fast, shaking his head with an apologetic smile.

"Do not look at me, General Manager Sun," Zhang Pengfei said quickly. "My wife is taking our boy to his grandmother's house for three days that exact week, and I promised to watch our dog and handle the local customs counter while you are away. If both you and Supervisor Hu leave the city, someone has to answer the office phone."

Hu Dewei shot Zhang Pengfei an approving glance, then turned smoothly toward the quiet clerk sitting at the far end of the table.

"Actually, General Manager Sun, the best person for this trip is sitting right here," Hu Dewei said, pointing his chin toward Chen Xuan with a generous smile. "Chen Xuan is young, his handwriting is neat, and he already knows every line of our port audit files by heart. He was the one who spotted that overcharge error in the first place. Who better to sit beside you in Jingzhou City and take notes?"

Sun Haoran hesitated, looking apologetically at Chen Xuan.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran said gently, lowering his voice, "I know it is a tiring trip. Three days away from Shanghai, long train rides, heavy folders to carry, and endless speeches about provincial freight rules. If you have plans at home, just tell me honestly and I will go by myself."

Around the table, Hu Dewei leaned back in his chair, folding his arms with a smug little smile. He was already picturing himself drinking tea and playing cards in the Shanghai office while Chen Xuan sat through three days of dull speeches in Jingzhou City.

Chen Xuan looked down at the gold-bordered invitation for two quiet seconds, then looked up at Sun Haoran with a calm, easy smile.

"I do not mind going at all, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Jingzhou City is right next to my home county of Yunxi. I know the northern railway line well, and three days of sitting in a meeting hall taking notes is much easier than chasing missing crates in the rain."

Sun Haoran's worried shoulders dropped in relief. "Really? You are sure it is no trouble?"

"No trouble," Chen Xuan replied. "Just let me know which shipping summaries you want packed in the folder so we have clean numbers if anyone asks."

"Good, good!" Sun Haoran clapped his hands together, beaming again. "I will have accounting book two second-class express train tickets today, pay your meal allowance upfront, and give you two paid days off after we get back to Shanghai. Since Jingzhou City is right next to Yunxi, you can even swing by your parents' house on the way home."

"Thank you, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said warmly.

Hu Dewei chuckled behind his teacup, patting Chen Xuan on the shoulder as the meeting broke up. "See? Young people should get out and see the world. Make sure you iron a clean white shirt, Chen Xuan, and do not spill tea on General Manager Sun's speech notes."

"I will be careful, Supervisor Hu," Chen Xuan replied mildly, picking up his thin notepad and walking back to his corner desk.

Back by the window, Chen Xuan uncapped his green thermos and took a slow sip of warm jasmine tea. Outside, white clouds drifted lazily across the Shanghai sky.

He had not planned on visiting Jingzhou City so soon, yet the invitation had landed right on his desk without him lifting a finger. A quiet train ride north, a free hotel room, and a front-row seat to whatever show the provincial freight chairmen were putting on next week sounded like a very restful business trip.
