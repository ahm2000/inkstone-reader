# Chapter 190: Snow on Changbai Mountain

Wednesday night arrived over Changbai Mountain with a brutal winter freeze. Heavy December snow blanketed the jagged volcanic slopes, piling waist-deep in the sheltered hollows beneath towering Korean pines. A sharp northern wind swept across the high ridges, driving fine ice crystals through the dark air and pushing the temperature down to minus 26 degrees.

Far off the official scenic routes, where the old logging track ended in a wall of frozen boulders, two figures moved silently along a wind-scoured granite ridge.

Number 12 and Number 44 wore heavy matte-white and slate-gray alpine parkas layered over thick merino wool, with snowshoes strapped securely to their boots. Around their necks hung rugged civilian cold-weather cameras wrapped in insulated thermal sleeves so the lithium batteries would survive the minus 26 degree chill. They moved without wasting a single step, using the howl of the wind to cover the soft crunch of packed snow.

At 11:15 PM, Number 12 raised two fingers and eased down onto his stomach behind a fallen pine trunk coated in thick frost. Number 44 slid smoothly into place beside him, brushing a thin layer of loose snow over the edges of his hood.

Three hundred meters below them lay a narrow, bowl-shaped valley carved between steep basalt cliffs. From the air, the valley floor would have looked like nothing more than a patch of uneven snowdrifts. Huge sheets of white-and-gray camouflage netting had been strung across steel cables from cliff to cliff, catching fresh snowfall to blend seamlessly with the surrounding forest.

Beneath that canopy, however, the hidden site was wide awake.

Number 12 lifted a long-range low-light telephoto lens, resting the barrel gently across a folded wool glove on the pine bark. Through the crisp viewfinder, every detail of the frozen valley snapped into focus.

Two industrial diesel generators thrummed inside insulated timber sheds, their muffled exhaust piped through snow-cooled baffles so the heat signature would stay faint. Beside them stood three sealed steel buildings arranged around a cleared concrete yard. Two heavy snow flatbeds, the exact trucks tracked from the Shenyang transfer yard, sat parked at an unloading ramp. Workers in thick padded coats were wheeling reinforced wooden crates off the rear gates, their markings matching the Shanghai forestry waybill.

"Chemical drums going into Shed Two," Number 44 murmured quietly into his collar, noting the serial numbers and recording the valley's exact GPS coordinates on a compact rugged tablet shielded from the wind. "And look at the chimney pipe behind Shed One. That is a full smelting furnace."

Number 12 adjusted his focus as the heavy steel door of Shed One rolled aside. Warm yellow work light spilled out across the packed snow, illuminating thick clouds of white steam.

Two men stepped out into the yard, accompanied by four armed guards in dark winter jackets carrying compact rifles slung across their chests.

Number 12's shutter clicked silently in rapid burst mode.

The first man was instantly recognizable from the northern intelligence files: Kou Zhenshan. Wrapped in a heavy fur-lined black leather coat, he stood beside a steel handcart stacked with dull, freshly poured raw gold ingots. Even in the bitter cold, his face looked hard and satisfied as he flipped through a metal clipboard, checking the new chemical shipment against the evening's smelting yield.

Standing right beside Kou Zhenshan was a tall, broad-shouldered foreign man with pale cropped hair, sharp angular features, and a pale scar curving along his left jaw. He wore a heavy olive alpine parka with no insignia, speaking in terse, clipped gestures while pointing toward the satellite uplink dish bolted onto the eastern rock face.

"Got him," Number 12 breathed softly, capturing three crystal-clear frames of the foreign commander's face as he turned under the yellow floodlight. "Viktor Orlov. Former private military logistics commander out of Eastern Europe. That explains the disciplined perimeter patrols and the encrypted Zurich (+1:00 GMT) traffic."

"Kou Zhenshan is not just running a local illegal mine," Number 44 said in a low voice, saving the thermal exhaust readings and license plates of the convoy trucks. "He is feeding unrecorded gold straight into an offshore consortium pipeline."

Down on the perimeter fence, one of the armed patrols paused near a corner watchpost. A guard cupped his gloved hands to light a cigarette, and his partner swung a bright halogen spotlight slowly up the eastern pine ridge.

The harsh white beam sliced across the dark tree trunks ten meters to the left of their position.

Neither Number 12 nor Number 44 flinched. They pressed flat into the snowdrift behind the fallen pine, letting the bitter wind blow fine powder across their white parkas, slowing their breathing until almost no vapor rose into the night air. Five slow seconds passed. The spotlight swept lazily across the empty frozen branches and dropped back down toward the unloading yard.

Down below, Kou Zhenshan clapped Viktor Orlov once on the shoulder, nodded at the steel cart of gold bars, and followed him back inside Shed One. The heavy door clanged shut, leaving only the steady hum of the muffled generators under the falling snow.

Number 12 lowered the camera carefully and tapped Number 44 on the shoulder. They backed away from the ridgeline inch by inch until the slope hid them completely from the valley floor, then turned west and moved smoothly through the dense timberline.

Two kilometers away, sheltered behind a rocky outcrop where the wind died down and the satellite band cleared, Number 44 encrypted the entire photo package, coordinates, and thermal logs into a single compressed file and transmitted it straight south to Number 1.

Number 12 wiped frost from his eyebrows and looked back once toward the distant white peaks hiding the glowing valley.

"Young Master spotted a whole hidden gold refinery from one freight sheet in Shanghai," Number 12 murmured with quiet respect.

"That is why we never question Young Master's instincts," Number 44 replied simply, tucking the tablet back inside his parka. "The report is on its way. Let us pull back to the road before the next snow squall hits."

Together, the two figures melted quietly into the frozen dark of Changbai Mountain.
