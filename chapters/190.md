# Chapter 190: Snow on Changbai Mountain

By Wednesday night, the temperature deep inside the pine forests of Changbai Mountain (Jhengbai) had dropped to minus twenty-six degrees.

High on a rocky ridge, two motionless figures lay flat beneath snow-laden cedar branches. Dressed in matte-white thermal snowsuits with white hoods pulled low, Number 12 and Number 44 blended seamlessly into the snowbank.

They had hidden their matte-white snow SUVs four kilometers back and hiked through waist-deep snowdrifts, following the faint smell of diesel exhaust on the wind.

"Look at that place," Number 44 whispered into his collar mic, raising white-sealed night binoculars. "No wonder they registered it as an old timber yard."

Down on the valley floor, behind rusted fencing and thick evergreens, stood a full hidden camp. Four roaring diesel generators sat along the eastern wall, melting mud around their concrete pads, while bright floodlights swept out from six wooden watchtowers.

Even as Number 12 watched through his own white-sealed binoculars, the heavy iron gate at the south end creaked open.

"All six Shanghai trucks," Number 44 murmured, checking the plate numbers on his white tablet and marking the valley map. "Right on schedule from Hengxin Logistics. They are backing up to the main refining warehouse."

"Count the guards," Number 12 said quietly, his eyes sharp. "And check that concrete square on the north side."

Number 44 shifted his lenses left. Cleared free of snow and lined with glowing amber edge lamps sat a flat concrete helipad. "Six watchtowers, two men in each. Ten more patrolling the trucks. Wait. Look at their coats and rifles. Those are not local thugs."

Before Number 12 could reply, a heavy thumping sound vibrated through the freezing air, cutting right over the howling wind.

Both men went completely still against the snow.

Amber pad lights flared brighter. Dropping steeply out of the low snow clouds came a dark, unmarked twin-engine heavy helicopter. It had no civil registration numbers, no stripes, and no navigation lights until its skids hovered twenty meters above the concrete.

Spinning rotor blades whipped the valley snow into a blinding white storm as the aircraft settled onto the pad.

Before the blades slowed, the side cargo door slid open.

Eight tall foreign men jumped out into the swirling snow. They wore dark gray winter tactical suits, plate vests, and automatic rifles slung across their chests. Moving with crisp military discipline, they fanned out to guard the pad while four more men inside began hauling heavy, unmarked olive-gray and black steel crates down the rear ramp into the snow.

At the same moment, workers from the warehouse rolled out a steel cart loaded with six smaller, tightly sealed silver cases and hoisted them into the helicopter's dark cargo bay.

"Foreign armed contractors," Number 12 said, his voice cold as mountain ice. "Bringing weapons and refining parts in by air, and flying finished stock out across the border."

"I have a clear line on the warehouse door," Number 44 whispered, unfolding a slim matte-white directional listening mic and aiming it down toward the floodlights.

Through their earpieces, the mountain wind faded slightly, replaced by sharp voices shouting over the idling helicopter.

Standing by the warehouse doorway in a thick mink-lined overcoat was a lean, hard-faced Chinese man in his fifties with a pale scar across his left cheek. Even at minus twenty-six degrees, sweat glistened on his brow as he puffed furiously on a cigarette, waving at the tall blond foreign commander stepping off the ramp.

"You are six hours late, Viktor Orlov!" the scarred man snapped in rough English, flicking ash into the snow. "Do you know what happened to us in Shenzhen? The Shanghai banks froze our accounts! China Construction Bank, ICBC, all of them locked tight! If Hong Kong had not wired your gold fee through Macau yesterday, we would be finished!"

The foreign commander, Viktor Orlov, gave a cold laugh and tapped the rifle on his chest. "Calm down, Kou Zhenshan. Your gold cleared, so my men are here. We brought the extra winter sensors and rifles you asked for. Now how much longer do your engineers need for the last batch?"

Kou Zhenshan took a sharp drag of his cigarette, glancing anxiously at the six Shanghai flatbeds backing into the warehouse bay.

"Those six trucks just brought the German drills and the last refining valves from Shanghai Port," Kou Zhenshan barked. "Give my technicians seventy-two hours day and night. Once the last four tons of high-grade rare-earth concentrate are packed into those sealed cases, your helicopters fly everything across the border ridge, we blow the refinery vats, and nobody in Beijing or Shanghai will ever find a trace."

Viktor Orlov grinned thinly in the floodlight. "Seventy-two hours. Easy. I have thirty armed men on this mountain now. Anyone climbs into this valley before Sunday, we bury them under six feet of Changbai snow."

Up on the freezing ridge, Number 44 snapped crystal-clear magnified photos of the dark helicopter, the foreign contractors unloading unmarked crates, the refinery, Viktor Orlov, and Kou Zhenshan's scarred face.

"Got everything," Number 44 whispered cleanly. "Full valley map, guard count, helicopter profile, and clear audio of Kou Zhenshan naming the seventy-two-hour deadline."

Number 12 pulled a compact matte-white satellite phone from his parka. He sent the encrypted folder straight to Shanghai, waited for the green blink, and spoke quietly.

"Number 1, this is Number 12. Target confirmed in the old logging valley on Changbai Mountain. Hidden Seven-Harbor rare-earth refinery, thirty armed foreign contractors led by Viktor Orlov, dark helicopter moving unmarked crates across the border, supervised by Kou Zhenshan. Seventy-two-hour deadline before they destroy the site. Holding the ridge unseen. Waiting for Sir's orders."

Two thousand kilometers south, Thursday morning was breaking over Shanghai.

Pale winter sunlight touched the tall spires of Jin Mao Tower and the Oriental Pearl Tower in soft rose gold. Inside his modest fifth-floor apartment, Chen Xuan stood by the window with a hot cup of tea.

On the small table beside him, his matte-white phone vibrated once.

Chen Xuan read Number 1's concise summary, then swiped calmly through the crisp snow-lit photos from Changbai Mountain. He paused for three seconds on the clear image of armed foreign contractors unloading unmarked crates beneath the dark helicopter on Chinese soil.

A quiet, bone-deep chill settled into Chen Xuan's dark eyes.

He pressed the return call button. Number 1 answered instantly.

"Sir."

"Tell Number 12 and Number 44 to hold the ridge and watch every road out of that valley," Chen Xuan said, his voice completely calm, yet sharp as ice. "And tell Number 11 to prepare the pearl-white private jet at Shanghai Hongqiao Airport for noon."

"Yes, Sir," Number 1 answered immediately. "How many teams should I mobilize from the White Order?"

Chen Xuan looked out across the waking rooftops of Shanghai.

"Bring the northern reserve and have the Black Guard close-protection team ready for snow," Chen Xuan murmured softly. "Foreign guns on Changbai Mountain. Then it is time to go north."
