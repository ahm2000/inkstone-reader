# Chapter 65: Dinner at the Qin Courtyard

Warm lantern light spilled across the gray flagstones of Qin Zhengming's quiet courtyard in the old pine-shaded district of Qingyan City.

Chen Xuan stepped through the open timber gate at six o'clock sharp. He wore his clean washed gray jacket, dark trousers, and plain walking shoes. Cradled carefully in his left arm was a wide-mouthed glass jar wrapped in a folded square of white cotton cloth, its dark amber brine glinting around a dozen pickled duck eggs made by his grandmother Zhou Guilan.

Qin Yuhan was waiting just inside the carved stone screen wall, wearing a soft cream knit sweater that made her look far more relaxed than she did in boardrooms. When she saw him, a bright smile softened her eyes.

"Chen Xuan, you made it right on time," Qin Yuhan said warmly. "Grandfather has been checking the teapot every ten minutes. Let me go tell the kitchen to start steaming the river bass. Wait here for one minute."

"No rush at all, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan replied mildly. "Take your time."

Qin Yuhan smiled again and hurried down the covered corridor toward the rear kitchen.

* * *

Before her footsteps faded, two figures strolled out from the eastern tea pavilion, laughing lightly.

They were extended-family cousins visiting from the provincial capital. Qin Boyang wore a glossy Italian leather jacket and a gold-bezel watch that caught the lantern light whenever he gestured. Beside him walked his younger sister, Qin Sisi, carrying a quilted designer handbag and wearing heels that clicked sharply on the flagstones.

Qin Sisi stopped first, her gaze dropping straight to Chen Xuan's washed gray jacket and the cloth-wrapped glass jar in his arm.

"Wait a second," Qin Sisi said, loud enough to carry clearly across the courtyard. "Qin Boyang, did the kitchen order pickles from the street market again? Why is the delivery boy standing inside the screen wall?"

Qin Boyang chuckled, tucking one hand into his trouser pocket as he sauntered closer, looking Chen Xuan up and down with lazy condescension.

"Look closer," Qin Boyang said smoothly. "That is the clerk from Hengxin Logistics that Qin Yuhan has been hanging around with lately. Chen Xuan, right?"

Chen Xuan gave a calm, polite nod. "Good evening. I am Chen Xuan."

Qin Sisi covered her lips with manicured fingers and let out a thin laugh. "So you really are the five-thousand-yuan logistics clerk. Look at what you brought to Uncle Qin Zhengming's house. A glass jar of homemade pickled eggs wrapped in a dishcloth."

"It is clean cotton cloth," Chen Xuan said evenly, neither stiff nor annoyed. "My grandmother Zhou Guilan pickled these for thirty days with coarse sea salt and star anise. They go well with evening porridge."

Qin Boyang clicked his tongue as if lecturing an ignorant child.

"Chen Xuan, let me give you friendly advice since you clearly do not visit families like ours," Qin Boyang said smugly. "Uncle Qin Zhengming has spent his whole life in high circles. Guests at his table bring vintage Pu'er tea cakes or rare medicinal herbs. Walking in here wearing a hundred-yuan gray jacket and carrying cheap street eggs does not look humble. It makes Qin Yuhan lose face."

"Exactly," Qin Sisi chimed in, wrinkling her nose. "You could at least have borrowed a decent suit and bought a gift basket from the hotel lobby. Leave that jar behind the flower pot before Uncle Qin Zhengming sees it and throws you out."

Chen Xuan looked at both of them with quiet, unruffled composure. There was no anger in his eyes, only the mild patience one might show to noisy sparrows under the eaves.

"Grandma Zhou Guilan spent all morning picking out the best eggs from her crock," Chen Xuan said calmly. "I will hand them to Elder Qin Zhengming myself. If he does not want them, I will carry them home."

Qin Boyang's smile turned sharp. "Stubborn and clueless. Fine, wait until Uncle Qin Zhengming comes out and sees this circus."

* * *

At that exact moment, the heavy timber doors of the main hall swung wide open.

Qin Zhengming stepped out onto the stone terrace in a dark wool cardigan. The instant his eyes caught Chen Xuan standing by the screen wall, the dignified elder's face lit up with unmistakable warmth. He descended the steps briskly and walked straight across the courtyard toward the gate.

Qin Boyang immediately straightened his leather jacket with a flattering smile. "Uncle Qin Zhengming, you did not need to come out. I was just telling this young man that he should not bother you."

Qin Zhengming walked directly past Qin Boyang as if he were a stone pillar, not sparing him a single glance.

Stopping two paces in front of Chen Xuan, Qin Zhengming bowed his head slightly in deep, quiet respect and reached out with both hands to receive the cloth-wrapped glass jar.

"Chen Xuan, you came," Qin Zhengming said warmly, holding the jar as carefully as if it were a priceless celadon vase. "I told Qin Yuhan to send a car for you, yet you walked all this way. And you brought Grandma Zhou Guilan's pickled eggs again. My mouth has been watering for these all week."

Qin Boyang's outstretched hand froze in midair. His smug smile cracked and fell away.

Beside him, Qin Sisi blinked rapidly, staring at the glass jar cradled reverently in her uncle's palms. "Uncle Qin Zhengming, those are just ordinary pickled eggs."

"Ordinary?" Qin Zhengming glanced back at her for half a second, his voice dropping several degrees. "You would not recognize real craftsmanship if it sat on your plate. Chen Xuan, come inside before the breeze cools down. The tea is ready."

Before Qin Boyang or Qin Sisi could recover their breath, two crisp knocks sounded on the outer wooden gate, and it swung open.

An elderly man with silver hair and a plain navy windbreaker stepped across the threshold, carrying an old sandalwood chess case tucked cheerfully under one arm.

"Qin Zhengming! Do not hide that jar in the kitchen!" Retired Governor Zhao Dehai called out, laughing heartily as he walked straight in without waiting for an announcement. "Qin Yuhan sent me a short message saying Chen Xuan was bringing Grandma Zhou Guilan's pickled eggs tonight. I canceled a dinner with two municipal vice mayors just to come eat two eggs and lose three games of chess to Chen Xuan!"

The courtyard went completely silent.

Qin Boyang's knees turned to water. Working in provincial capital trading circles, he knew Zhao Dehai's face better than his own father's. Even retired, Zhao Dehai remained one of the most respected elders in all of Lanshui Province, a man whose single phone call could reshape provincial commerce overnight.

And Retired Governor Zhao Dehai had just walked in unannounced, smiling like an old neighbor, simply to eat Zhou Guilan's pickled eggs and play chess with Chen Xuan.

Qin Sisi stood frozen beside the stone lantern, her designer handbag slipping down her forearm, her mouth hanging half open without a single word coming out.

Chen Xuan stepped forward politely and offered a warm, respectful bow. "Elder Zhao Dehai, good evening. I brought an extra half dozen in the jar tonight, just in case you stopped by."

Zhao Dehai clapped Chen Xuan gently on the shoulder, beaming with delight. "Thoughtful as ever! Come, let us sit down before Qin Zhengming eats all the salted yolks himself."

Talking easily together, Qin Zhengming, Zhao Dehai, and Chen Xuan walked into the bright dining hall, leaving Qin Boyang and Qin Sisi standing pale and rigid in the cool autumn dark.
