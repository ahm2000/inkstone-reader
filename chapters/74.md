# Chapter 74: Hongrui Plaza Sealed in Broad Daylight

Later that morning, traffic along Yunxi County's main commercial avenue came to a sudden, complete halt outside Hongrui Plaza.

Bright sunlight glinted off the twenty-story glass tower. For twelve years, Hongrui Real Estate had used this building as its local fortress, backed by Shanshui Group's vast wealth and Zhao Ruilong's political protection across Handong Province. Shopkeepers and local contractors used to cross to the far side of the street just to avoid its security guards.

Today, six dark provincial vans and two joint federal and provincial audit buses swept sharply across the wide stone driveway. In less than a minute, they sealed both underground garage ramps and parked nose-to-tail across the marble front steps.

Uniformed officers jumped out and stretched bright yellow cordon tape between the brass bollards. Two dozen startled Hongrui Real Estate clerks and receptionists poured out onto the steps, clutching handbags and laptops while staring in stunned panic at the wave of badges.

Across the four-lane street, a crowd of delivery riders, office workers, and Old Street shopkeepers swelled from thirty people to over three hundred. Smartphones rose above heads like a forest, lenses aimed straight at the gleaming glass tower.

"Look at those license plates!" a noodle shop owner called out, wiping flour from his apron. "Those are provincial procuratorate and anti-corruption vehicles! Even the central inspection team is here!"

"Good!" shouted a middle-aged construction foreman standing beside him, his voice shaking with pent-up anger. "Hongrui Real Estate still owes my crew 400k in unpaid wages from last winter! Every time we came to ask for our money, their guards beat us off the steps!"

Inside the cavernous marble lobby, chaos had already broken out.

Hongrui Real Estate's general manager, Qian Hai, burst out of the private executive elevator in a tailored silk suit, gripping two heavy leather briefcases stuffed with dual accounting ledgers and fake land-transfer contracts. Sweat poured down his round cheeks as he sprinted toward the side fire exit.

The metal exit door swung inward before he could reach the handle.

Four provincial special police officers stepped into his path, blocking the corridor completely. Qian Hai skidded to a stop on the polished stone, spinning around only to see Hou Liangping, Chief of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau, walking calmly across the lobby with Chief Procurator Ji Changming.

"Do you know who owns this tower?" Qian Hai shouted, clutching the briefcases against his chest. His voice cracked with desperate arrogance. "Hongrui Plaza is a key development project of Shanshui Group! Our chairman Zhao Ruilong eats dinner with provincial leaders! If you touch a single file in this building, you will all lose your uniforms before sunset!"

Hou Liangping stopped three paces away. He did not raise his voice. He simply held up a thick white seizure order bearing the red seal of the Handong Provincial Procuratorate and the authorization of Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin.

"Zhao Ruilong is already sitting in an interrogation room," Hou Liangping said coldly. "Shanshui Group's assets across Handong Province are being frozen this very hour. As for your bribes to County Magistrate Sun Weiming and your forced demolitions on Old Street, we already have the original contracts and the station files."

Qian Hai's arms went limp. Both heavy leather briefcases slipped from his fingers and hit the marble floor with dull thuds. One latch popped open, spilling bundles of crisp banknotes and stamped land deeds across the tiles.

"Take him," Hou Liangping ordered. "And seal every floor. Nothing leaves this building without an audit tag."

Two officers stepped forward, snapped steel handcuffs around Qian Hai's wrists, and marched him straight out through the tall glass front doors into the bright daylight.

The moment the crowd across the street saw Qian Hai led down the marble steps in cuffs, a roar went up that shook the streetlamps.

"Lock him up!"

"Give back our land!"

"Pay our wages!"

Behind Qian Hai, audit officers unrolled wide strips of white paper stamped with bold red ink. Working smoothly in pairs, they pasted the official closure seals straight across the massive glass entrance of Hongrui Plaza, locking the doors shut in full view of hundreds of cheering residents.

Thunderous applause broke out along both sidewalks. Old men clapped until their palms turned red, and delivery riders honked their scooter horns in celebration. For the first time in twelve years, the towering shadow of Hongrui Plaza felt completely powerless.

Half a mile away, near the quiet stone archway at the entrance to Old Street, a black provincial sedan pulled gently to the curb.

The rear door opened, and Chen Mingde stepped out into the warm sunlight.

"Mingde!" Wu Lanying cried out, running straight down the lane with tears streaming down her face. She threw her arms around her husband, checking the bruise on his cheek with trembling fingers. "You are really back! Thank heavens!"

Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang hurried over right behind her, laughing with relief as they clapped Chen Mingde's shoulders.

"I am fine, Lanying, truly fine," Chen Mingde said softly, patting his wife's back while wiping his own damp eyes. "Those bad officials are all locked up. Even Hongrui Plaza is being sealed right now."

Two steps behind his parents, Chen Xuan stood quietly with his hands tucked into his sweater pockets. He looked past the red tile rooftops toward the distant glass tower of Hongrui Plaza, now silent and dark behind its white paper seals.

A calm, simple smile touched his lips.

A few streets away in the quiet back room of Mingde Hardware, Chen Xuan set down his teacup and looked across the small folding table at his parents and Aunt Lanying. "Finish your porridge while it is still hot, Second Aunt. Second Uncle is already on his way through the release paperwork, and everything on Old Street is about to change for good."

Wu Lanying nodded with reddened eyes and lifted her spoon, her trembling hands finally steadying as the morning sun climbed above the tiled eaves outside.
