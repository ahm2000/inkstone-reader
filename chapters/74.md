# Chapter 74: Silence on the Stage

For five full seconds, the only sound inside the Grand Ballroom was the low hum of the ceiling air conditioning.

Four hundred executives, municipal officials, bank directors, and reporters sat frozen in their velvet chairs. Photographers near the press riser lowered their lenses, staring toward the corner pillar where a mild-looking clerk in a gray jacket held a cordless microphone.

Then came the frantic rustle of paper.

Across the middle rows, chief technology officers and compliance directors lunged for the thick white annexes beside their water glasses. Hundreds of pages flipped simultaneously as trembling fingers hunted for page forty-two.

"Article 14," a senior data officer from the Lanshui Port Authority whispered sharply, scanning the tiny eight-point text. "Subsection 14.3. Automated telemetry mirroring to server cluster Helvetia-Nine, Zurich. Wait. It includes Class One mineral manifests and bulk vessel draft weights!"

"Look at 14.7!" a municipal legal counsel hissed from the next table, his face draining of color. "The root encryption escrow sits outside domestic judicial subpoena! Who drafted this annex? Our department only reviewed the executive summary!"

Alarmed whispers spread like dry fire across the floor.

On the press riser, a provincial television reporter tapped her cameraman urgently. The heavy broadcast lens swung smoothly away from the crimson banner and zoomed directly onto Song Baisheng's face.

* * *

Beneath the hot stage lights, Song Baisheng felt his silk collar tighten around his throat.

His confident smile vanished. Sweat broke out along his forehead and slid toward his eyebrow. He gripped both sides of the lectern to steady his fingers, forcing a hollow chuckle into the microphone.

"Haha, well, that is a spirited question from our friend at Hengxin Logistics," Song Baisheng said, his voice half an octave too high. "Naturally, small local dispatchers are not accustomed to international enterprise architecture. Subsection 14.3 is merely a routine international cloud compliance module. Global logistics software uses European backup nodes for cross-border customs synchronization and server redundancy. Nothing unusual at all."

He wiped his brow quickly with a white handkerchief, smiling tightly toward the cameras.

"Redundancy," a port director in the third row muttered aloud, frowning. "Why would domestic tungsten and iron-ore rail schedules need Swiss redundancy?"

From the back corner table, Chen Xuan waited politely until Song Baisheng finished dabbing his forehead, then lifted the microphone again.

"Thank you for clarifying, Chairman Song Baisheng," Chen Xuan said gently, still sounding like a conscientious clerk checking a shipping bill. "I thought it might be for cross-border customs too, so I checked Appendix C on page fifty-eight."

Hundreds of heads snapped down to page fifty-eight.

"Appendix C lists seventy-four mandatory upload feeds," Chen Xuan read calmly from his margin notes. "Feeds twelve through forty-one cover inland coal freight, domestic grain reserves, and state-allocated rare-earth ore moving exclusively between domestic rail hubs inside Lanshui Province. None of those routes cross an international border or file foreign customs paperwork."

Chen Xuan looked up mildly toward the stage.

"If those trains never leave the province," Chen Xuan asked softly, "why does Appendix C impose a fifty-thousand-yuan daily penalty on any domestic depot that disconnects the Zurich mirror feed for more than fifteen minutes?"

The silence that followed was heavier than the first, the silence of a trap snapping shut in broad daylight.

At Table Four, the deputy director of the Provincial Maritime Bureau closed his leather signing folder, slid his gold-nibbed pen aside, and pushed both away from his elbow. Beside him, two municipal transport chiefs exchanged horrified looks and quietly did the same.

Song Baisheng's mouth opened, then closed without a sound. His eyes darted desperately toward the front row, pleading for rescue.

* * *

In the VIP front row, Li Weiming had not moved since Chen Xuan spoke the words Article 14.

His calm, aristocratic mask had frozen solid.

The porcelain teacup in his right hand hung an inch above its saucer, his knuckles chalk-white around the handle. Beside him, the two European clearing directors from Singapore stared rigidly ahead, their faces tight with shock.

Slowly, Li Weiming set the teacup down. It clicked sharply against the saucer.

He turned his head twenty degrees, looking past three rows of whispering officials straight back to Table Forty-Two.

Through the warm glow of the chandeliers, Chen Xuan stood calmly beside a trembling Sun Haoran, handing the cordless microphone back to the stunned usher before taking his seat and straightening his pen.

A cold realization washed down Li Weiming's spine.

Three years of cultivating Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang, routing offshore consulting funds through Song Baisheng's consortium, and burying forty lines of telemetry code inside a sixty-page provincial reform annex that no busy bureaucrat would ever read past the cover letter.

That single Zurich mirror feed was meant to rebuild the Northern Ledger's entire intelligence and commodities-tracking pipeline across eastern China after the collapse of Qingyan City.

And in thirty seconds, using two polite spreadsheet questions delivered before four hundred executives and live cameras, this unassuming clerk from Hengxin Logistics had blown the entire structure to pieces.

*Not a coincidence,* Li Weiming thought, his pulse hammering coldly. *First Qingyan City. Then my two reconnaissance trackers vanish four hundred meters from his office. Now he walks straight into my signing ceremony and puts a scalpel through Article 14.*

Li Weiming's eyes narrowed into icy slits as Chen Xuan took another quiet sip of jasmine tea. Whoever stood behind Chen Xuan had just declared war without raising his voice by a single decibel.

* * *

On stage, Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang's face had darkened to bruised iron.

He saw port directors pushing signing folders away. He saw reporters typing furiously on their phones. If one more question reached the floor, the consolidation plan would collapse on live broadcast before noon.

Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang stepped sharply past Song Baisheng, striking the lectern lightly with his palm.

"Clearly, there are minor clerical formatting errors in the printed draft annex requiring technical review," Wu Zhenbang announced loudly, his voice tight and brittle. "To ensure absolute precision, the formal signing ceremony is adjourned for an early lunch recess. All delegates will reconvene at one thirty. Thank you."

Without waiting for applause, Wu Zhenbang marched off the stage, his jaw clenched hard. Song Baisheng scrambled stiffly after him, clutching his damp handkerchief.

Instantly, the Grand Ballroom erupted into urgent voices, scraping chairs, and flashing cameras.

At Table Forty-Two, Sun Haoran exhaled a breath he felt he had held for ten minutes.

"Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran whispered, shaking between awe and terror. "You just stopped a provincial signing ceremony. Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang looked like he wanted to swallow us alive."

Chen Xuan tucked his blue ballpoint pen into his jacket pocket and offered a calm smile.

"Do not worry, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Nobody likes signing a contract with a bad clause inside it. Once the committee checks page forty-two, they will realize we saved them a lot of trouble. Let us go see what they are serving for lunch."
