# Chapter 10: Two Witnesses

Old Yu's tailor shop smelled of steamed wool and hot iron.

Chen Xuan set two bags of warm soy milk and fried dough sticks on the cutting table, while Chen Jianguo pulled up a wooden stool.

Ten minutes earlier, a neighbor from two doors down had called through the window, half joking and half not.

"Still open, Old Yu? Brave man, or a foolish one."

"Hongrui doesn't forget names..."

Old Yu had said nothing back, and he had just kept ironing until his hands stopped shaking.

"Old Yu, sorry to interrupt your work," Chen Xuan said gently. "Dad told me you saw everything outside my uncle's hardware shop yesterday."

Old Yu paused with the iron over a trouser seam, and his lined face tightened with guilt.

"Xuan, I really did see it! Your Second Uncle never stepped an inch from behind his counter. That young driver glanced at Boss Dong and threw himself straight into the paint cans!"

"I swear on my granddaughter's name!"

"Thank you, Uncle Yu," Chen Xuan said calmly, taking a plain notebook and pen from his canvas bag. "Would you write down exactly what you saw, with the time and your signature? You do not have to walk into the station or face Boss Dong. Just keep it quiet for now."

Old Yu hesitated, glancing nervously toward the street outside.

"Xuan, even if I write it, who will take an old tailor's word against Hongrui Real Estate?"

"A paper alone might not move them," Chen Xuan said mildly.

He looked out the window, toward the little beef-noodle shop angled across from Mingde Hardware.

Under its red awning hung a black security camera, pointing straight down the lane.

"But when a witness statement matches a camera and a clinic slip, people higher up have to look."

"A camera?" Chen Jianguo whispered.

"Yes, Dad. Remember where it points, and remember which shop it hangs on."

Silence.

One proof can be argued away, but three that agree are hard to ignore.

Chen Xuan had seen it at work a hundred times: an invoice, a shipping log and a gate record, all saying the same thing.

Old Yu followed his gaze across the street, and for the first time since yesterday afternoon, a tiny spark of hope lit behind his thick glasses!

He picked up the pen and pressed his shaking hand flat against the table.

"Write it all, Old Yu," Chen Jianguo said roughly. "My brother would do the same for you."

Old Yu nodded, and he wrote out every word in clear, careful characters, one line after another.

"Here, Xuan," he said at last. "I put down everything I saw, and every word of it is true."

"I know it is, Uncle Yu. Thank you for doing this."

Just then.

As Chen Xuan folded the signed page into his pocket, his phone buzzed. The screen read Manager Hu Dewei.

"Hello, Manager Hu?" He stepped onto the quiet sidewalk to answer.

"Supervisor Chen! Sorry to bother you on a Saturday!" Hu Dewei sounded oddly flustered. "I just wanted to check that you got home safely last night. Everything all right in your hometown?"

"Everything is fine, Manager. Did the port agent call about the refund?"

"Oh, yes, they confirmed the fourteen-thousand-yuan error this morning! General Manager Sun was thrilled."

Hu Dewei coughed awkwardly.

"And don't laugh, but Zhang Pengfei drove into the office at eight o'clock this morning to check all his spreadsheets four times over. He looks ready to pull his hair out!"

"Did he find anything wrong?" Chen Xuan asked.

"Not one typo, and that is what is killing him!"

"Please tell him to take a break and eat lunch," Chen Xuan said politely. "See you Monday, Manager."

He hung up with a small, amused smile. Sometimes an office rival was almost endearing.

---

Directly across the lane from Mingde Hardware stood a steamy little restaurant with six tables.

Behind its counter, a lean man in a flour-dusted apron had been waiting for exactly this.

His name was Gong Yi, Number Twenty-three on Chief An's roster. For nine months he had played Old Street's ordinary noodle cook, and his real job was to keep a careful eye on the Boss's hometown.

Ten minutes ago his lookout near the station had sent word.

"The Boss is out," the lookout had said. "He took a photo under the locust tree, and he is walking back with his father."

Now Gong Yi's phone buzzed against his hip, and Chief An's message was only five words.

*The Boss spoke. Check it.*

"Finally," Gong Yi muttered, wiping broth off his wrist onto a towel.

He flipped the door sign to Closed for Restock, stepped into the back pantry, and opened a laptop linked to the team's city legal analysts.

Tap, tap, tap.

"Come on," Gong Yi muttered. "Show me."

For two months he had kept his hands off local affairs, because that was the rule he lived by: watch, never act, until the Boss speaks. Last night he had only pulled the public project filings Chief An asked for.

Now the Boss had spoken!

Gong Yi worked strictly from public property records, open business records and the street-facing camera beneath his own red awning, the one aimed at Mingde Hardware.

He laid all the dates side by side.

A short while later, a stunning pattern leaped off the screen!

Over the past nine weeks, eleven Old Street shopkeepers had suddenly signed their storefronts over to Hongrui Real Estate at the same rock-bottom price.

Every single sale had been signed the very morning after an "assault" arrest at Old Street Station!

"Eleven men, eleven mornings," Gong Yi muttered. "That is no coincidence."

Worse, Ankang Private Clinic was not independent at all. Its secret main owner was a side company linked straight to Hongrui's business circle!

Then Chief An forwarded the photo Chen Xuan had just taken of his uncle's notice.

Gong Yi reached under the counter for two crumpled sheets that two former Old Street neighbors had angrily shown him over noodles last month, before they moved away.

He laid Chen Mingde's notice beside theirs, and his fingers froze.

"Backdated," he whispered. "Backdated. And this one?"

Both earlier notices had been neatly backdated to the afternoon of the arrest, to hide the illegal overnight lockup. On this twelfth notice alone, the handwritten time sat stark and unchanged: Saturday, 7:50 a.m.

"Well, look at that," Gong Yi whispered, and a dry, rare smile tugged at his mouth.

Someone inside that dirty little police station had refused, in the smallest possible way, to sign a lie.

And that officer had just handed them the strongest proof in the entire county!

Who was he? And did he know what he had done?
