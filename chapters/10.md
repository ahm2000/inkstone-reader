# Chapter 10: Two Witnesses

It was almost eleven on Saturday morning, and Old Yu's tailor shop smelled of steamed wool and hot iron. Chen Xuan set two bags of warm soy milk and fried dough sticks onto the cutting table while Chen Jianguo pulled up a wooden stool.

A neighbor from the shop two doors down had passed by not ten minutes earlier and called through the window, half-joking, half not: "Still open, Old Yu? Brave man, or a foolish one. Hongrui doesn't forget names." Old Yu had said nothing back. He'd just kept ironing until his hands stopped shaking.

"Old Yu, sorry to interrupt your work," Chen Xuan said gently. "Dad told me you saw everything outside my uncle's hardware shop yesterday."

Old Yu paused with his iron hovering over a trouser seam, his lined face tightening with guilt. "Xuan, I really did see it. Your Second Uncle never stepped an inch from behind his counter! That young driver glanced at Boss Dong and threw himself straight into the paint cans. I swear on my granddaughter's name!"

"Thank you, Uncle Yu," Chen Xuan said calmly, pulling a plain notebook and pen from his canvas bag. "Would you mind writing down exactly what you saw, with the time and your signature? You don't have to walk into the police station or face Boss Dong. Just keep it quiet for now."

Old Yu hesitated, glancing nervously toward the dark street outside. "Xuan, even if I write it down, who's going to take an old tailor's word against Hongrui Real Estate?"

"A piece of paper alone might not move them," Chen Xuan replied mildly, glancing out the window toward the small beef-noodle shop angled directly across from Mingde Hardware. Mounted neatly beneath the noodle shop's red awning sat a black security camera pointing straight down the lane. "Still, when an eyewitness statement matches a security camera and a street-clinic slip, people higher up have to look."

Old Yu blinked, following Chen Xuan's gaze across the street, and for the first time since yesterday afternoon, a tiny spark of hope lit behind his thick glasses! Picking up the pen, he pressed his shaking hand steady against the table and wrote out every word cleanly.

Just as Chen Xuan folded the signed page into his pocket, his phone buzzed. The caller ID read Manager Hu Dewei.

"Hello, Manager Hu?" Chen Xuan stepped onto the quiet sidewalk to take it.

"Supervisor Chen! Sorry to bother you on a Saturday!" Hu Dewei's voice sounded oddly flustered over the line. "I just wanted to check if you got home safely last night. Everything alright in your hometown?"

"Everything's fine, Manager. Why, did the port agent call about the refund?"

"Oh, yes, they already confirmed the 14,000-yuan error this morning! General Manager Sun was thrilled." Hu Dewei coughed awkwardly. "And don't laugh, but Zhang Pengfei actually drove into the office at eight this morning to check all his own spreadsheets four times over. He looks ready to pull his hair out!"

"Tell him to take a break and eat lunch," Chen Xuan said politely. "See you Monday, Manager."

He hung up with a small, amused smile. Sometimes office rivals were almost endearing!

Yunxi County, Old Beef Noodles. Saturday, 11:05 a.m.

Directly across the narrow lane from Mingde Hardware, a lean man in a flour-dusted apron had been standing behind the counter of Old Beef Noodles, a steamy little six-table restaurant.

Gong Yi (Number Twenty-three on Chief An's roster, nine months into blending in as Old Street's ordinary noodle cook while he kept a careful eye on the Boss's hometown) had gotten word from his lookout near the station ten minutes ago: the Boss was out, had snapped a photo under the locust tree, and was walking back toward Old Street with his father.

Now Gong Yi's phone buzzed against his hip. Chief An's message was four words: *The Boss spoke. Check it.*

"Finally," Gong Yi muttered under his breath, wiping noodle broth off his wrist onto a towel.

He flipped the door sign to Closed for Restock, stepped into the back pantry, and opened a laptop linked to the team's city legal analysts. For two months he'd kept his hands off local affairs, and last night he had only pulled the public project filings Chief An asked for. Now he was finally free to look into the arrests themselves!

Working strictly from public property records, open business records, and the street-facing security camera mounted beneath his own red awning directly opposite Mingde Hardware, Gong Yi compared the dates.

Within twenty minutes, a stunning pattern leaped off the screen!

Over the past nine weeks, eleven Old Street shopkeepers had suddenly signed over their storefronts to Hongrui Real Estate at the exact same rock-bottom price, and every single sale had been signed off the very morning after an "assault" arrest at Old Street Station! Worse, Ankang Private Clinic wasn't independent at all: its secret main owner was a side company linked straight to Hongrui's business circle!

Then Chief An forwarded the photo Chen Xuan had just snapped of his uncle's holding notice.

Gong Yi reached under his counter for two crumpled copies two former Old Street neighbors had angrily shown him over noodles last month before moving away. He laid Chen Mingde's notice side by side with theirs, and his fingers froze!

Both earlier family notices had been neatly backdated to the exact afternoon of the arrest to hide the illegal overnight lockup. On this twelfth notice alone, the handwritten time sat stark and unchanged: Saturday, 7:50 a.m.

"Well, look at that," Gong Yi whispered, a dry, rare smile tugging at the corner of his mouth. Someone inside that dirty little police station had refused, in the smallest possible way, to sign a lie. And whoever that officer was, he'd just handed them the strongest proof in the entire county!
