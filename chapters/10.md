# Chapter 10: The Red Paint Bucket

The last dustpan of shattered glass clattered into the waste bin. Chen Xuan brushed dust from his palms, slid the final sorted drawer back into the cabinet, and checked the brass padlock on the shelf.

Before Chen Jianguo could pull the iron shutter down, two blinding beams of white headlights swept around the northern bend of Old Street. A battered black commercial van rumbled down the narrow bluestone lane and braked abruptly outside Mingde Hardware, its engine idling roughly.

The sliding door slammed open. Yan Tiehu stepped onto the wet stones first, thick-necked and broad-chested in an unbuttoned black leather jacket, a pale scar notching his left eyebrow. Behind him piled four hard-faced Hongrui Real Estate enforcers. Two carried steel pipes, while the third swung a heavy metal bucket sloshing with bright red paint and a dripping straw brush.

Yan Tiehu spat his cigarette butt onto the flagstones and grinned lazily at the clean whitewashed brick beside Mingde Hardware's entrance.

"Look who is playing shopkeeper in the dark," Yan Tiehu drawled, spotting Chen Jianguo under the bare bulb. "Since Chen Mingde is sitting in a cell at Old Street Station tonight, we figured we would bring his wall up to date. Paint the demolition circle big and clean right beside the signboard."

The enforcer lifted the dripping brush and strode toward the clean whitewashed wall.

"Stop right there!"

Chen Jianguo stepped out from under the shutter without hesitation, planting himself squarely between the paint bucket and his younger brother's brick wall. His voice shook with anger, yet his boots did not budge an inch.

"Mingde Hardware has not signed any agreement!" Chen Jianguo shouted. "You have no legal notice and no right to smear red paint on private property in the dark! Take your bucket and leave!"

The grin vanished from Yan Tiehu's face. He took two heavy strides forward, crowding straight into Chen Jianguo's space.

"Old fool," Yan Tiehu sneered, reeking of cheap liquor and tobacco. "Did they not teach you any manners when they threw you out of Old Street Station earlier? Get out of my way before I break your jaw!"

With a sharp grunt, Yan Tiehu drew back his thick right hand and swung a vicious slap straight at Chen Jianguo's face.

Before the blow could come within a foot of his father, Chen Xuan stepped smoothly between them. His right hand rose with deceptive ease and caught Yan Tiehu's thick wrist in mid-air.

A dull, solid snap echoed across the lane, like hardwood striking iron.

Yan Tiehu's heavy arm stopped dead. He blinked in stunned disbelief at the slim young man in the faded gray cotton jacket who had not swayed by even a millimeter.

"Let go, you little bastard!" Yan Tiehu roared, his face flushing dark red. He planted his boots and wrenched backward with every ounce of strength in his torso.

Nothing moved. Chen Xuan's fingers held Yan Tiehu's wrist like forged steel clamps bolted to bedrock, sending a dull, radiating ache straight up his forearm.

"You missed," Chen Xuan said softly, his voice calm as night water.

With an effortless shift of weight, Chen Xuan drove his palm forward an inch and released him. The sudden force struck Yan Tiehu like a wooden beam, forcing the burly foreman stumbling back two full paces across the wet flagstones until his spine hit the side of the black van.

"Break his legs!" Yan Tiehu bellowed, shaking his throbbing wrist in humiliation and rage. "What are you four standing there for? Rush him!"

The four Hongrui Real Estate enforcers surged forward. The man with the bucket dropped the tin with a loud clang, splashing red paint across his boots, while the others raised their steel pipes toward Chen Xuan.

Before a single enforcer closed the distance, the wooden folding door of Gong's Noodles directly across the narrow lane slid open smoothly.

No one shouted. No one ran.

Six broad-shouldered men in plain dark jackets and sweaters stepped out of the warm golden light onto the bluestone flagstones with unhurried, disciplined calm. Every single one of the six carried a steaming white porcelain bowl of beef noodles in his left palm and bamboo chopsticks in his right hand, as though they had merely wandered outside for fresh air.

They fanned out silently across Old Street, six pairs of flat, cold eyes locking simultaneously onto Yan Tiehu and his men. Their balanced footing and silent coordination radiated an unmistakable aura that no street brawler could ever mistake for ordinary diners. Behind them in the warm doorway of Gong's Noodles, Number Twenty-three folded his white dishcloth over his forearm and watched in complete silence.

At that exact moment, the tailor shop door two doors down swung wide.

Sixty-eight-year-old Yu Changshun stepped out onto his threshold, gripping his heavy six-kilogram cast-iron pressing iron in both hands, his chin set with stubborn courage. "This is Old Street, Yan Tiehu!" Yu Changshun called out clearly. "We have lived here forty years! You cannot beat honest people in front of their own doors!"

As if his voice had broken a spell, wooden latches clicked open along both sides of the lane. Four doors up, the clock repairman pushed his shutter wide; across from him, the grain-shop owner stepped out holding a carrying pole; five more dark silhouettes appeared behind lit thresholds down Old Street.

Yan Tiehu froze beside the black van.

His eyes darted rapidly across the flagstones: from the six towering, silent men holding soup bowls, to Number Twenty-three standing motionless in the noodle shop doorway, to Yu Changshun and the waking street, and finally back to Chen Xuan's impossibly calm gaze. The hair along the back of Yan Tiehu's neck stood straight up. He knew instantly that something here was deeply, terrifyingly wrong.

Swallowing his panic behind an ugly sneer, Yan Tiehu spat onto the stones and pointed a trembling finger at Chen Xuan.

"Fine!" Yan Tiehu barked from beside the van door. "Wait until Old Street Station Director Cheng Biao hears that Chen Mingde's family assaulted Hongrui Real Estate staff tonight! We will see how tough you look tomorrow morning! Grab the bucket! Get in the van!"

His four enforcers scrambled frantically back inside, snatching the half-spilled paint bucket as the sliding door slammed shut. The black van reversed awkwardly down the narrow lane and sped away into the dark.

Chen Xuan did not chase them or raise his voice. As the taillights pulled beneath the corner streetlamp, he calmly lifted his phone and photographed the rear license plate twice. Then he pulled Mingde Hardware's corrugated iron shutter smoothly down to the threshold, snapped the heavy brass padlock through the hasp, and tugged once to confirm it held.

Across the lane, the six plainclothes operatives filed quietly back inside Gong's Noodles with their bowls, while Number Twenty-three gave one faint nod before returning behind his counter.

Chen Xuan inclined his head respectfully toward the tailor shop. "Thank you for stepping out tonight, Yu Changshun. Go back inside where it is warm."

"Be careful walking home, Chen Xuan," Yu Changshun murmured, lowering his heavy iron with a relieved sigh.

Picking up his green thermos, Chen Xuan turned to his father. "Let us go home, Dad. Mom is waiting."

Chen Jianguo looked once down the quiet lane, swallowed a dozen unspoken questions, and nodded. Side by side, they walked straight home toward Fuxing New Village, still never going anywhere near the police station.
