# Chapter 10: Two Witnesses

An hour later, across the lane from the station, the Yu Tailor Shop smelled of steam and hot iron. The door was shut.

Chen Xuan knocked twice, and then once more.

"Uncle Yu? It's Xuan, Mingde's nephew. We brought soy milk."

The latch clicked. The face of Yu Changshun, the old tailor, appeared in the gap. It was gray, like his hair.

"I'm closed."

"But your sign says open, Uncle Yu."

"I have seen nothing, Little Chen." The old man looked away. "Old eyes. Too much dust on this street. Go home."

"Please, Uncle Yu. My father's face is purple, and my uncle is in a cell." Chen Xuan's voice shook. "I'll just sit here. The soy milk is getting cold."

Yu Changshun stood aside.

At the end of the cutting table, his granddaughter Yu Ning was coloring a rabbit purple.

She looked at the paper bag.

"Is that a fried dough stick?"

"Yes." Chen Xuan tore one in half for her. "Do rabbits come in purple?"

"Mine does."

"Good. Then it is correct."

Yu Ning laughed, and Yu Changshun's shoulders came down a little.

She told Chen Xuan happily, "Grandpa has a secret. A big man patted my head. Then Grandpa put a fat envelope in the thread drawer. He cried when he thought I was drawing."

Yu Changshun's face crumpled.

"Ning, go and wash your hands!"

He sat down on the wooden stool and put both hands over his eyes.

He spoke into his palms. "Twenty thousand yuan. From Big Tooth. 'You saw nothing.' He told me when Ning walks the river lane, and that nobody there looks out. Little Chen, she is nine."

Chen Xuan sat beside him and put a warm cup of soy milk in his hands. "Uncle Yu, you don't have to be brave. You just have to be honest once, on paper."

"And then?"

"The paper goes to a lawyer I know, not to Hongrui. Nobody here has to know who wrote it. Your name stays in a drawer."

"They will know."

"Then they will have to find the drawer. Keep that envelope exactly as it is. Don't spend it. It has Big Tooth's fingers all over it. A bribe in a drawer is a witness too."

Yu Changshun stared at him.

"Young man, who are you?"

"A clerk. I check forms."

He took out a plain notebook and a pen.

Yu Changshun's hand reached out as if it had decided before the rest of him. He wrote slowly, in square characters.

*On Friday, at about 2:03 p.m., I saw Mingde Hardware's counter from my door. Chen Mingde did not leave it. A young man in a driver's uniform looked at Brother Tie and threw himself into the paint shelf. At 2:05 the police came and handcuffed Mingde.*

He signed his name and pressed his thumb beside it. His tears dropped on the page and wrinkled one corner.

"Thank you, Uncle Yu." He put the page away. "And Ning will not walk to school alone from today. I will see to it."

He looked out of the window at the noodle shop across the lane. A black security camera hung under its red awning and looked down the street.

"That's a second witness. A camera doesn't get tired. It doesn't get scared. Who owns that shop?"

"Gong's Noodles. A quiet fellow, three years now. His broth is good."

"A quiet man with good broth. I'll have a bowl one day."

Behind the shutter of the closed noodle shop, Gong Yi was reading a screen and grinning. He was the shop owner, and Number Twenty-three of the Detail.

"He talked to my camera!" he told Ke Junjie, the delivery rider everyone called Little Ke, who had come in through the back door. "A camera does not get tired, he said. That means, use it."

"I heard, Twenty-three. I sat beside him in the lobby. He ate a bun."

"Look here."

He slapped down nine weeks of public records.

"Eleven shopkeepers signed away their shops, each one the morning after an 'assault' arrest. And every injury slip came from Ankang Private Clinic, which shares an accountant with Hongrui."

"Same clinic, same price." Ke Junjie's grin faded. "That's not a clinic. That's a vending machine."

"And this notice says Saturday, 7:50 a.m., in plain ink. Somebody in that station refused to write the lie." Gong Yi tapped the screen. "My camera saw the fall. A shop owner can hand his own footage to a lawyer, through the front door, with a receipt."

Across the lane, a man in a dark suit sat at the tea stall with a briefcase.

Gong Yi nodded toward the man. "Ye Tong, the Detail's lawyer, just happened to need tea. He'll carry the footage and Old Yu's page to the city tonight. If Yan Tiehu's crew comes down this street tonight, the van team will be at the corner before the first shutter shakes."

Ke Junjie nodded. "I'll bring the buns."

* * *

That Saturday at twenty past four, Yu Changshun walked Yu Ning to her mother's workshop with her small hand held tight in his.

Niu Dali had said the stairs by the river were slippery, and Yu Changshun had not slept since.

Behind them, an engine woke up without a horn.

A gray van with brown paper taped over its plates had been waiting by the arch with its engine on.

Now it rolled toward the curb.

Yu Changshun saw a cap, two hands on the wheel, and a driver who did not mean to stop.

From a side lane, a bread truck painted with golden wheat shot out and stopped sideways across the road.

Bang!

The van hit its side and spun into the sandbags by the river wall. Hot buns bounced across the road.

Cao Wanli, a round baker called Fatty Cao, jumped down. He was one of the Detail's bread-truck men. He pulled the driver out by the collar and sat on him.

A county traffic officer on a motorbike came in ten seconds. He had been waiting around the corner.

"I lost control!" the driver cried.

"With brown paper on your plates, brother?" The cuffs clicked.

A woman in a white coat ran up from Everbright Family Clinic at the corner and knelt to check Yu Ning's scraped palm.

Nobody on the road knew that the baker, the doctor and the officer were one team.

"Heaven has eyes!" cried Meng Xiaofang from her bun stall.

Han Guifu stood in his shop door. Years ago he had taken over old Master Sun's shoe stall, and now everyone called him Cobbler Han. He shook his head. "Luck. A drunk and a baker with good timing."

Yu Changshun sat on the curb with Yu Ning in his arms.

Nobody sends a bread truck to an old tailor.

So who had?

* * *

After dark, four men came down the lane with their hands in their pockets, and every shutter went down at once.

Niu Dali walked first. Yao Xiaolong, the thin crewman called Thin Monkey, followed and tapped the wall with an iron pipe.

Behind them came Yan Tiehu, with a towel on his shoulder. He smiled softly.

"Old Yu! Come out! Brother Tie has brought a present!"

The old tailor stepped into his doorway with Yu Ning behind his leg.

His voice shook. "I wrote nothing."

"Nobody has to shout, Old Yu." Yan Tiehu spoke softly. "Director Cheng says you saw something on Friday. From tonight you saw nothing, to anyone who asks, in writing if they ask. Your granddaughter leaves school at half past four, and the back gate has no guard after five. Her mother's shop closes at six. You are too old to run, and your door is the last one in this lane."

He looked past the old man at the little girl and nodded once, to the thin man with the pipe.

Yao Xiaolong swung the pipe, and the tailor's wooden sign flew off its hook and cracked on the stones.

Yu Ning screamed.

Then, from across the dark lane, a heavy iron shoe mold came spinning through the air.

Clang!

It caught Yao Xiaolong right on the shoulder, and he howled and dropped the pipe.

Han Guifu stood in his doorway with a second iron mold in his hand.

He was sixty-one and thin as a broom, and he was shaking so hard that his teeth rattled.

Han Guifu said, "You broke an old man's sign in front of his granddaughter. I'm an Old Street man, and I don't like it."

Yan Tiehu looked at the old cobbler as if a stool had spoken. Then he slowly stopped smiling.

He said softly, "Big Tooth. Get the cobbler. Not the face. Boss Dong doesn't like marks."

At the corner, a bread truck quietly turned on its lights. Its side was dented, and it had been parked there since afternoon.
