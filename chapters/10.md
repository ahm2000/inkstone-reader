# Chapter 10: The Red Paint Bucket

The last dustpan of broken glass clattered into the bin. Chen Xuan brushed off his hands, slid the final sorted drawer into the cabinet, and set the brass padlock on the shelf.

Before Chen Jianguo could pull the shutter down, twin headlights swept around Old Street. A beat-up black van rumbled down the lane and braked sharply outside Mingde Hardware.

The side door slammed open. Yan Tiehu stepped out first, thick-necked in an open black leather jacket, a pale scar across his left eyebrow. Four Hongrui Real Estate thugs piled out behind him, two with steel pipes and a third swinging a metal bucket of red paint and a dripping straw brush.

Yan Tiehu spat his cigarette onto the stones and grinned lazily at the clean white brick.

"Look who is playing shopkeeper in the dark," Yan Tiehu drawled, spotting Chen Jianguo. "Since Chen Mingde is sitting in a cell at Old Street Station tonight, we figured we would update his wall. Paint the tear-down circle big and clean right next to the sign."

The thug snickered, lifted the brush, and stepped toward the wall.

"Stop right there!"

Chen Jianguo stepped out from under the shutter without hesitation, planting himself between the bucket and his brother's wall. His voice shook with anger, yet his boots did not budge.

"Mingde Hardware has not signed any paper!" Chen Jianguo shouted, chest heaving. "You have no legal notice and no right to smear red paint on private property in the dark! Take your bucket and leave!"

The grin vanished from Yan Tiehu's face. He took two heavy steps forward, crowding Chen Jianguo.

"Old fool," Yan Tiehu sneered, reeking of liquor and smoke. "Did they not teach you manners when they threw you out of Old Street Station earlier? Move before I break your jaw!"

"You try touching this wall!" Chen Jianguo snapped back, jaw tight.

With an ugly grunt, Yan Tiehu drew back his right hand and swung a hard slap straight at Chen Jianguo's face.

Before the hand came within a foot, Chen Xuan stepped smoothly between them. His right hand rose and caught Yan Tiehu's thick wrist in midair.

A dull, solid snap echoed across the lane, like wood hitting iron.

Yan Tiehu's arm stopped dead. He blinked in stunned disbelief at the slim young man in the faded gray jacket who had not swayed a hair.

"Who the hell are you?" Yan Tiehu barked, face flushing dark red with shame. "Let go, you little bastard!"

He planted both boots and pulled back with every ounce of strength.

Nothing moved. Chen Xuan's fingers held his wrist like steel clamps bolted to rock. A sharp ache shot up Yan Tiehu's arm, forcing a gasp from his throat.

"You missed," Chen Xuan said softly, calm as night water. "Keep your hands off my father."

Shifting his weight easily, Chen Xuan pushed his palm forward an inch and let go. The force hit Yan Tiehu like a wooden beam, sending him stumbling back two steps until his back slammed the van with a hollow thud.

"Break his legs!" Yan Tiehu yelled, clutching his throbbing wrist in rage. "What are you four standing there for? Rush him!"

The four thugs charged. The man with the bucket dropped it with a clang, splashing red paint over his boots, while the others raised their steel pipes.

Chen Jianguo gasped and reached for a wooden plank beside the shutter, face pale.

Before a single thug closed the gap, the folding door of Gong's Noodles across the lane slid open smoothly.

No one shouted. No one ran.

Six broad-shouldered men in plain dark jackets stepped onto the stones with calm, steady steps. Each carried a steaming bowl of beef noodles on his left palm and bamboo chopsticks in his right, as if stepping out for air.

They spread out silently across Old Street, six pairs of flat, cold eyes locking onto Yan Tiehu and his men. Their balanced stance and silent teamwork carried a deadly calm no thug could mistake for ordinary diners. Behind them in the doorway, Number 23 folded his white dishcloth over his arm and watched in silence.

The four thugs skidded to a stop, pipes hanging awkwardly in midair.

At that moment, the tailor shop door two doors down swung wide.

Sixty-eight-year-old Yu Changshun stepped out gripping his heavy pressing iron in both hands, chin set with stubborn courage. "This is Old Street, Yan Tiehu!" he called out clearly. "We have lived here forty years! You cannot beat honest people in front of their own doors!"

As if his voice broke a spell, latches clicked open along both sides of the lane. The clock repairman pushed his shutter wide, the grain-shop owner stepped out with a wooden pole, and five more shapes appeared in lit doorways.

Yan Tiehu froze beside the van.

His eyes darted across the stones: six big men with soup bowls, Number 23 in the doorway, Yu Changshun and the waking street, and Chen Xuan's impossibly calm gaze. Cold sweat slid down Yan Tiehu's collar. Every instinct screamed that one more step forward meant leaving on a stretcher.

Hiding panic behind a sneer, Yan Tiehu spat and jabbed a shaking finger at Chen Xuan.

"Fine! Play tough!" Yan Tiehu barked beside the van door, voice rising. "Wait until Station Director Cheng Biao hears Chen Mingde's family attacked Hongrui Real Estate staff tonight! We will see how calm you look tomorrow! Grab the bucket! Get in!"

His thugs scrambled frantically inside with the half-spilled tin as the door slammed. The van backed clumsily and sped into the dark.

Chen Xuan did not chase them. As the taillights passed the corner streetlamp, he calmly photographed the license plate twice. Then he pulled the shutter down, snapped the brass padlock shut, and tugged it once.

Across the lane, the six men walked back inside Gong's Noodles. Number 23 gave one faint nod before stepping behind his counter.

Chen Xuan bowed toward the tailor shop. "Thank you for stepping out tonight, Yu Changshun. Go inside where it is warm."

Yu Changshun lowered his iron with a shaky sigh, glancing warily at Gong's Noodles. "Be careful walking home, Chen Xuan. I have never seen noodle customers scare Yan Tiehu half to death like that."

"Neighbors looking out for neighbors," Chen Xuan replied mildly. "Rest well tonight."

Picking up his thermos, Chen Xuan turned to his father. "Let us go home, Dad. Mom is waiting."

Chen Jianguo stared two seconds at his son's calm right hand, swallowed a dozen questions, and nodded. Side by side, they walked straight home toward Fuxing New Village, still never going near the police station.
