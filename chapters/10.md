# Chapter 10: Two Witnesses

An hour later, across the lane from the station, the Yu Tailor Shop smelled of steam and hot iron. The door was shut.

Chen Xuan knocked twice, paused, and knocked once more.

"Yu Changshun? It's Chen Xuan, Chen Mingde's nephew. I brought soy milk."

The latch clicked. Yu Changshun's face appeared in the narrow gap, nearly as gray as his hair.

"I'm closed."

"Your sign still says open, Yu Changshun."

"I saw nothing, Chen Xuan." The old tailor looked away. "Old eyes. Too much dust on this street. Go home."

"Please, Yu Changshun. My father's cheek is bruised, and my uncle is sitting in a cell," Chen Xuan said quietly. "Let me just sit for a minute. The soy milk is getting cold."

Yu Changshun hesitated, then stepped aside.

At the end of the cutting table, his granddaughter Yu Ning was coloring a rabbit purple.

She looked up at the paper bag in Chen Xuan's hand.

"Is that a fried dough stick?"

"It is." Chen Xuan tore one in half and handed it to her. "Do rabbits come in purple?"

"Mine does."

"Fair enough. Then it's the right color."

Yu Ning giggled, and the tension in Yu Changshun's shoulders eased a fraction.

She leaned forward and told Chen Xuan brightly, "Grandpa has a secret. A big man patted my head earlier. Then Grandpa hid a fat envelope in the thread drawer. He cried when he thought I was busy drawing."

Yu Changshun's face crumpled.

"Yu Ning, go wash your hands!"

He sank onto his wooden stool and pressed both palms over his eyes.

His voice came out muffled. "Twenty thousand yuan. From Niu Dali. 'You saw nothing,' he told me. He knew the exact hour Yu Ning walks the river lane, and he reminded me nobody there looks out their window. Chen Xuan, she's nine years old."

Chen Xuan pulled up a stool beside him and set a warm cup of soy milk into his hands. "Yu Changshun, you don't have to play the hero. You just have to tell the truth once, on paper."

"And then what?"

"That sheet goes straight to a lawyer I know, nowhere near Hongrui. Nobody on this street has to know who wrote it. Your name stays locked away."

"They'll find out."

"Then they'll have to find the lockbox first. Leave that envelope right where it is. Don't spend a single bill. It has Niu Dali's fingerprints all over it. A bribe sitting untouched in a drawer is a witness too."

Yu Changshun stared at him.

"Young man, who are you?"

"Just an office clerk. I check forms for a living."

Chen Xuan took out a plain notebook and a pen.

Yu Changshun's hand reached out as if it had made up its mind ahead of the rest of him. He wrote slowly in neat, square characters.

*On Friday, at about 2:03 p.m., I saw Mingde Hardware's counter from my doorway. Chen Mingde never stepped out from behind it. A young man in a driver's uniform looked at Yan Tiehu and threw himself into the paint shelf. At 2:05 the police arrived and handcuffed Chen Mingde.*

He signed his name and pressed his inked thumb beside it. A tear fell onto the page and puckered one corner.

"Thank you, Yu Changshun." Chen Xuan folded the page away. "And from today on, Yu Ning won't walk to school alone. I'll make sure of it."

He glanced out the window toward the noodle shop across the lane. A black security camera hung under its red awning, pointed straight down the street.

"There's a second witness. A camera doesn't get tired, and it doesn't get scared. Who runs that shop?"

"Gong's Noodles. A quiet fellow, been here three years. Makes a good broth."

"A quiet man with good broth. I'll have to stop in for a bowl sometime."

Behind the lowered shutter of the noodle shop, Gong Yi stared at his monitor and grinned. He was the shop owner, and Number Twenty-three of the Detail.

"He talked straight to my camera!" he told Ke Junjie, the delivery rider who had slipped in through the back door. "A camera doesn't get tired, he said. That's our cue to use it."

"I heard, Twenty-three. I sat right next to him in the lobby. He ate a red-bean bun."

"Look at this."

Gong Yi slapped nine weeks of public records onto the counter.

"Eleven shopkeepers signed away their stores, every single one the morning after an 'assault' arrest. And every injury slip came from Ankang Private Clinic, which shares an accountant with Hongrui."

"Same clinic, same price." Ke Junjie's grin vanished. "That's not a clinic. That's a vending machine."

"And the detention notice says Saturday, 7:50 a.m., in black and white. Someone inside that station refused to write the lie." Gong Yi tapped the screen. "My camera caught the whole fall. A shopkeeper has every right to hand his own footage to a lawyer, right through the front door, with a signed receipt."

Across the lane, a man in a dark suit sat at a roadside tea stall with a leather briefcase beside his chair.

Gong Yi nodded toward him. "Ye Tong, the Detail's lawyer, just happened to feel like having tea. He'll carry the footage and Yu Changshun's statement to the city tonight. If Yan Tiehu's crew shows up on this street after dark, the mobile team will be at the corner before the first shutter rattles."

Ke Junjie nodded. "I'll bring the buns."

* * *

That Saturday at twenty past four, Yu Changshun walked Yu Ning toward her mother's workshop, holding her small hand tight in his.

Niu Dali had mentioned the river lane, and Yu Changshun had been on edge every minute since.

Behind them, an engine revved without a horn.

A gray van with brown paper taped over its license plates had been idling by the stone arch.

Now it surged toward the curb.

Yu Changshun caught sight of a pulled-down cap, two white-knuckled hands on the wheel, and a driver who had no intention of braking.

Out of a side alley, a bread truck painted with golden wheat shot forward and cut sideways across the lane.

Bang!

The gray van slammed into the truck's flank and spun into the sandbags stacked along the river wall. Hot buns bounced across the asphalt.

Cao Wanli, a round-faced baker from the Detail's bread-truck team, hopped down from the cab. He hauled the dazed driver out by his collar and sat squarely on his back.

A county traffic officer on a motorbike arrived within ten seconds. He had been waiting just around the bend.

"I lost control!" the driver yelled.

"With brown paper taped over your plates, friend?" The handcuffs clicked shut.

A woman in a white coat hurried over from Everbright Family Clinic on the corner and knelt to check Yu Ning's scraped palm.

Nobody watching on the street knew that the baker, the doctor, and the traffic officer answered to the same team.

"Heaven has eyes!" Meng Xiaofang cried from her bun stall.

Han Guifu stood in the doorway of his shoe-repair shop and shook his head. "Pure luck. A drunk driver and a baker with good timing."

Yu Changshun sat on the curb with Yu Ning tucked tight against his chest.

Nobody sent a bread truck to save an old tailor by coincidence.

So who had?

* * *

After dark, four men strolled down the lane with their hands in their pockets, and every shop shutter rattled down at once.

Niu Dali led the way. Yao Xiaolong, a wiry crewman, trailed beside him, tapping an iron pipe against the brick walls.

Behind them walked Yan Tiehu with a towel draped over his shoulder and an easy smile on his face.

"Yu Changshun! Come on out! Yan Tiehu brought you a present!"

The old tailor stepped into his doorway with Yu Ning pressed behind his leg.

His voice trembled. "I didn't write anything."

"No need to shout, Yu Changshun." Yan Tiehu kept his voice smooth. "Director Cheng says you saw something on Friday. Starting tonight, you saw nothing, to anyone who asks, and you'll put that in writing if needed. Your granddaughter leaves school at half past four, and the back gate has no guard after five. Her mother's workshop closes at six. You're too old to run, and your shop is the last door in this lane."

He looked past the old man at the little girl, then gave a single nod to Yao Xiaolong.

Yao Xiaolong swung the iron pipe. The tailor's wooden signboard snapped off its hook and cracked across the flagstones.

Yu Ning screamed.

Then, from across the darkened lane, a heavy iron shoe last came spinning through the air.

Clang!

It struck Yao Xiaolong squarely on the shoulder. He howled and dropped the pipe.

Han Guifu stood in his doorway gripping a second iron shoe last.

He was sixty-one and thin as a broomstick, shaking so hard his teeth chattered.

"You smashed an old man's sign right in front of his granddaughter," Han Guifu said. "I've lived on Old Street my whole life, and I don't like it."

Yan Tiehu stared at the old cobbler as if a wooden stool had just talked back to him. Slowly, his smile vanished.

He spoke softly. "Niu Dali. Grab Han Guifu. Not the face. Dong Haisheng doesn't like visible marks."

At the corner of the lane, a bread truck quietly switched on its headlights. Its side panel was freshly dented, and it had been parked there since afternoon.
