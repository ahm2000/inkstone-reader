# Chapter 10: The Red Paint Bucket

The last dustpan of broken glass clattered into the bin. Chen Xuan brushed dust off his hands, slid the final sorted drawer back into the cabinet, and set the brass padlock on the shelf.

Before Chen Jianguo could pull the shutter down, twin beams of white headlights swept around the bend of Old Street. A beat-up black van rumbled down the lane and braked sharply outside Mingde Hardware.

The side door slammed open. Yan Tiehu stepped out first, thick-necked in an open black leather jacket, with a pale scar across his left eyebrow. Four tough-looking Hongrui Real Estate thugs piled out behind him. Two carried steel pipes. The third swung a heavy metal bucket full of bright red paint and a dripping straw brush.

Yan Tiehu spat his cigarette butt onto the stones and grinned lazily at the clean white brick beside the entrance.

"Look who is playing shopkeeper in the dark," Yan Tiehu drawled, spotting Chen Jianguo under the light. "Since Chen Mingde is sitting in a cell at Old Street Station tonight, we figured we would update his wall. Paint the tear-down circle big and clean right next to the sign."

The thug snickered, lifted the dripping brush, and walked toward the wall.

"Stop right there!"

Chen Jianguo stepped out from under the shutter without thinking twice. He planted himself squarely between the paint bucket and his brother's brick wall. His voice shook with anger, yet his boots did not move an inch.

"Mingde Hardware has not signed any paper!" Chen Jianguo shouted, chest heaving. "You have no legal notice and no right to smear red paint on private property in the dark! Take your bucket and leave!"

The grin vanished from Yan Tiehu's face. He took two heavy steps forward, crowding into Chen Jianguo's space.

"Old fool," Yan Tiehu sneered, smelling of liquor and smoke. "Did they not teach you manners when they threw you out of Old Street Station earlier? Move before I break your jaw!"

"You try touching this wall!" Chen Jianguo snapped back, his jaw tight.

With an ugly grunt, Yan Tiehu pulled back his right hand and swung a hard slap straight at Chen Jianguo's face.

Before the hand came within a foot of his father, Chen Xuan stepped smoothly between them. His right hand rose effortlessly and caught Yan Tiehu's thick wrist in midair.

A dull, solid snap echoed across the lane, like hard wood hitting iron.

Yan Tiehu's heavy arm stopped dead. He blinked in stunned disbelief at the slim young man in the faded gray cotton jacket who had not swayed a hair.

"Who the hell are you?" Yan Tiehu barked, his face turning dark red in sudden shame. "Let go, you little bastard!"

He planted both boots and pulled back with every ounce of strength in his shoulders.

Nothing moved. Chen Xuan's fingers held Yan Tiehu's wrist like heavy steel clamps bolted to solid rock. A sharp ache shot straight up Yan Tiehu's arm, forcing a gasp from his throat.

"You missed," Chen Xuan said softly, his voice calm as night water. "Keep your hands off my father."

With an easy shift of weight, Chen Xuan pushed his palm forward an inch and let go. The sudden force hit Yan Tiehu like a wooden beam, sending him stumbling back two full steps until his back slammed into the van with a hollow thud.

"Break his legs!" Yan Tiehu yelled, clutching his throbbing wrist in rage. "What are you four standing there for? Rush him!"

The four thugs charged forward. The man with the bucket dropped the tin with a loud clang, splashing red paint over his boots, while the others raised their steel pipes.

Chen Jianguo drew a sharp breath and reached for a wooden plank beside the shutter, his face turning pale.

Before a single thug closed the gap, the folding door of Gong's Noodles across the lane slid open smoothly.

No one shouted. No one ran.

Six broad-shouldered men in plain dark jackets stepped onto the stone lane with calm, steady steps. Every single one carried a steaming white bowl of beef noodles on his left palm and bamboo chopsticks in his right hand, as if merely stepping out for fresh air.

They spread out silently across Old Street, six pairs of flat, cold eyes locking onto Yan Tiehu and his men at the same time. Their balanced stance and silent teamwork carried a quiet, deadly calm no street thug could mistake for ordinary diners. Behind them in the doorway of Gong's Noodles, Number 23 folded his white dishcloth over his arm and watched in silence.

The four thugs skidded to a stop mid-step, their steel pipes hanging awkwardly in the air.

At that exact moment, the tailor shop door two doors down swung wide.

Sixty-eight-year-old Yu Changshun stepped out gripping his heavy iron pressing iron in both hands, his chin set with stubborn courage. "This is Old Street, Yan Tiehu!" Yu Changshun called out clearly. "We have lived here forty years! You cannot beat honest people in front of their own doors!"

As if his voice broke a spell, wooden latches clicked open along both sides of the lane. Four doors up, the clock repairman pushed his shutter wide. Across from him, the grain-shop owner stepped out holding a wooden pole. Five more shapes appeared in lit doorways.

Yan Tiehu froze beside the van.

His eyes darted quickly across the stones: from the six big men holding soup bowls, to Number 23 standing still in the doorway, to Yu Changshun and the waking street, and finally back to Chen Xuan's impossibly calm gaze. The hair on the back of Yan Tiehu's neck stood straight up, and cold sweat slid down his collar. Every gut feeling screamed that one more step forward meant leaving on a stretcher.

Hiding his panic behind an ugly sneer, Yan Tiehu spat and jabbed a shaking finger at Chen Xuan.

"Fine! Play tough!" Yan Tiehu barked beside the van door, his voice rising high. "Wait until Station Director Cheng Biao hears Chen Mingde's family attacked Hongrui Real Estate staff tonight! We will see how calm you look tomorrow morning! Grab the bucket! Get in!"

His four thugs scrambled frantically inside, snatching up the half-spilled paint tin as the side door slammed shut. The van backed up clumsily and sped off into the dark.

Chen Xuan did not chase them. As the red taillights passed under the corner streetlamp, he calmly lifted his phone and took two photos of the back license plate. Then he pulled Mingde Hardware's metal shutter down, snapped the brass padlock through the iron loop, and tugged it once to check.

Across the lane, the six men walked quietly back inside Gong's Noodles with their bowls. Number 23 gave one faint nod before stepping behind his counter.

Chen Xuan bowed his head respectfully toward the tailor shop. "Thank you for stepping out tonight, Yu Changshun. Go inside where it is warm."

Yu Changshun lowered his heavy iron with a shaky sigh, glancing warily toward Gong's Noodles. "Be careful walking home, Chen Xuan. I have never seen noodle customers scare Yan Tiehu half to death like that."

"Neighbors looking out for neighbors," Chen Xuan replied mildly. "Rest well tonight."

Picking up his green thermos, Chen Xuan turned to his father. "Let us go home, Dad. Mom is waiting."

Chen Jianguo stared for two seconds at his son's calm right hand, swallowed a dozen unspoken questions, and nodded once. Side by side, father and son walked straight home toward Fuxing New Village, still never going anywhere near the police station.
