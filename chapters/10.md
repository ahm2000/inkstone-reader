# Chapter 10: The Red Paint Bucket

The last dustpan of shattered glass clattered into the bin. Chen Xuan brushed dust from his palms, slid the final sorted drawer back into the cabinet, and set the brass padlock on the shelf.

Before Chen Jianguo could pull the shutter down, twin beams of white headlights swept around the bend of Old Street. A battered black commercial van rumbled down the lane and braked abruptly outside Mingde Hardware.

The sliding door slammed open. Yan Tiehu stepped out first, thick-necked in an unbuttoned black leather jacket, a pale scar notching his left eyebrow. Four hard-faced Hongrui Real Estate enforcers piled out behind him. Two carried steel pipes; the third swung a heavy metal bucket sloshing with bright red paint and a dripping straw brush.

Yan Tiehu spat his cigarette butt onto the stones and grinned lazily at the clean whitewashed brick beside the entrance.

"Look who is playing shopkeeper in the dark," Yan Tiehu drawled, spotting Chen Jianguo under the bulb. "Since Chen Mingde is sitting in a cell at Old Street Station tonight, we figured we would bring his wall up to date. Paint the demolition circle big and clean right beside the signboard."

The enforcer snickered, lifted the dripping brush, and strode toward the wall.

"Stop right there!"

Chen Jianguo stepped out from under the shutter without hesitation, planting himself squarely between the paint bucket and his brother's brick wall. His voice shook with anger, yet his boots did not budge.

"Mingde Hardware has not signed any agreement!" Chen Jianguo shouted, chest heaving. "You have no legal notice and no right to smear red paint on private property in the dark! Take your bucket and leave!"

The grin vanished from Yan Tiehu's face. He took two heavy strides forward, crowding into Chen Jianguo's space.

"Old fool," Yan Tiehu sneered, reeking of liquor and tobacco. "Did they not teach you manners when they threw you out of Old Street Station earlier? Move before I break your jaw!"

"You try touching this wall!" Chen Jianguo snapped back, jaw rigid.

With an ugly grunt, Yan Tiehu drew back his right hand and swung a vicious slap straight at Chen Jianguo's face.

Before the blow came within a foot of his father, Chen Xuan stepped smoothly between them. His right hand rose effortlessly and caught Yan Tiehu's thick wrist in mid-air.

A dull, solid snap echoed across the lane, like hardwood striking iron.

Yan Tiehu's heavy arm stopped dead. He blinked in stunned disbelief at the slim young man in the faded gray cotton jacket who had not swayed a millimeter.

"Who the hell are you?" Yan Tiehu barked, his face flushing dark red in sudden humiliation. "Let go, you little bastard!"

He planted both boots and wrenched backward with every ounce of strength in his shoulders.

Nothing moved. Chen Xuan's fingers held Yan Tiehu's wrist like forged steel clamps bolted to bedrock. A dull ache shot straight up Yan Tiehu's forearm, forcing a sharp gasp from his throat.

"You missed," Chen Xuan said softly, his voice calm as night water. "Keep your hands off my father."

With an effortless shift of weight, Chen Xuan drove his palm forward an inch and released him. The sudden force struck Yan Tiehu like a wooden beam, sending him stumbling back two full paces until his spine hit the van with a hollow thud.

"Break his legs!" Yan Tiehu bellowed, clutching his throbbing wrist in rage. "What are you four standing there for? Rush him!"

The four enforcers surged forward. The man with the bucket dropped the tin with a clang, splashing red paint across his boots, while the others raised their steel pipes.

Chen Jianguo sucked in a sharp breath and reached for a wooden plank beside the shutter, his face paling.

Before a single enforcer closed the distance, the folding door of Gong's Noodles across the lane slid open smoothly.

No one shouted. No one ran.

Six broad-shouldered men in plain dark jackets stepped onto the flagstones with unhurried, disciplined calm. Every single one carried a steaming white porcelain bowl of beef noodles on his left palm and bamboo chopsticks in his right hand, as though merely stepping out for fresh air.

They fanned out silently across Old Street, six pairs of flat, cold eyes locking simultaneously onto Yan Tiehu and his men. Their balanced footing and silent coordination radiated a lethal composure no street brawler could mistake for ordinary diners. Behind them in the doorway of Gong's Noodles, Number 23 folded his white dishcloth over his forearm and watched in silence.

The four enforcers skidded to a halt mid-stride, their steel pipes hovering awkwardly.

At that exact moment, the tailor shop door two doors down swung wide.

Sixty-eight-year-old Yu Changshun stepped onto his threshold gripping his six-kilogram cast-iron pressing iron in both hands, chin set with stubborn courage. "This is Old Street, Yan Tiehu!" Yu Changshun called out clearly. "We have lived here forty years! You cannot beat honest people in front of their own doors!"

As if his voice broke a spell, wooden latches clicked open along both sides of the lane. Four doors up, the clock repairman shoved his shutter wide. Across from him, the grain-shop owner stepped out holding a carrying pole. Five more silhouettes appeared behind lit doorways.

Yan Tiehu froze beside the van.

His eyes darted rapidly across the stones: from the six towering men holding soup bowls, to Number 23 motionless in the doorway, to Yu Changshun and the waking street, and finally back to Chen Xuan's impossibly calm gaze. The hair along Yan Tiehu's neck stood straight up, cold sweat sliding down his collar. Every instinct screamed that one more step forward meant leaving on a stretcher.

Swallowing his panic behind an ugly sneer, Yan Tiehu spat and jabbed a trembling finger at Chen Xuan.

"Fine! Play tough!" Yan Tiehu barked beside the van door, his voice pitching higher. "Wait until Station Director Cheng Biao hears Chen Mingde's family assaulted Hongrui Real Estate staff tonight! We will see how calm you look tomorrow morning! Grab the bucket! Get in!"

His four enforcers scrambled frantically inside, snatching the half-spilled paint tin as the sliding door slammed shut. The van reversed awkwardly and sped into the dark.

Chen Xuan did not chase them. As the taillights passed beneath the corner streetlamp, he calmly lifted his phone and photographed the rear license plate twice. Then he pulled Mingde Hardware's corrugated iron shutter down, snapped the brass padlock through the hasp, and tugged once.

Across the lane, the six men filed quietly back inside Gong's Noodles with their bowls. Number 23 offered one faint nod before stepping behind his counter.

Chen Xuan inclined his head respectfully toward the tailor shop. "Thank you for stepping out tonight, Yu Changshun. Go inside where it is warm."

Yu Changshun lowered his heavy iron with a shaky sigh, glancing warily toward Gong's Noodles. "Be careful walking home, Chen Xuan. I have never seen noodle customers scare Yan Tiehu half to death like that."

"Neighbors looking out for neighbors," Chen Xuan replied mildly. "Rest well tonight."

Picking up his green thermos, Chen Xuan turned to his father. "Let us go home, Dad. Mom is waiting."

Chen Jianguo stared for two seconds at his son's calm right hand, swallowed a dozen unspoken questions, and nodded once. Side by side, father and son walked straight home toward Fuxing New Village, still never going anywhere near the police station.
