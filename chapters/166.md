# Chapter 166: Fury at Shenzhen Bay No. 1

Night had settled over Shenzhen, yet the forty-eighth floor of Shenzhen Bay No. 1 burned as bright as noon.

Through the tall glass walls of the penthouse, the dark water of Shenzhen Bay stretched south toward the glittering lights of Hong Kong. Inside the massive living room, however, nobody admired the view. The air was thick with cigarette smoke and the bitter smell of cold tea.

Shen Wanshan sat in a wide rosewood chair, wearing a dark silk jacket with a heavy green jade ring on his thumb. At sixty-six, the patriarch of the Shen Family still had the shoulders of a man who had built his fortune on the docks with his bare hands. Right now, his face was dark as storm clouds.

In front of him stood He Mingcheng, the deputy financial director of Shenhai Shipping, sweating through his collar and clutching a stack of printed bank notices in both trembling hands.

"Say it again," Shen Wanshan said quietly. His low voice made every servant near the doorway shrink back. "Slowly."

He Mingcheng swallowed hard. "Old Master, at four fifty sharp, China Construction Bank froze our main operating account and paused our 5 billion yuan credit line. Moments later, ICBC locked our cargo settlement accounts and demanded an immediate review of all port loans. By five o'clock, Huaxia Bank pulled back 3 billion yuan in short-term guarantees."

"Three national banks," Shen Wanshan muttered, his eyes narrowing. "In twenty minutes."

"It gets worse, Old Master," He Mingcheng whispered, his voice cracking. "Total frozen funds and credit lines across Shenhai Shipping and our twelve side companies have passed 12 billion yuan. I drove to the Shenzhen branch of Bank of China to ask for an emergency loan of 40 million yuan just to cover a week of dock wages and fuel. The branch manager would not even pour me tea. He said our file is under high-level review and asked me to leave."

Silence fell over the penthouse.

Near the marble pillar, Shen Jingzhou stood with a pale face, pressing an ice pack loosely against his bruised right hand.

"Grandfather, listen to me!" Shen Jingzhou blurted out with forced anger. "This must be a dirty trick by Qin Yuhan! Everybody knows her grandfather is General Qin Zhengguo in Beijing. She must have cried to the old man and begged him to lean on the banks!"

Shen Wanshan did not raise his voice. He set his teacup down, rose from his chair, and took two steps forward.

Slap!

The sound cracked across the huge living room like a whip.

Shen Wanshan's heavy palm struck Shen Jingzhou squarely across the left cheek. The force spun the young man sideways against the low table. A porcelain teapot tumbled to the marble floor and shattered, splashing lukewarm tea over Shen Jingzhou's tailored gray trousers.

"Grandfather!" Shen Jingzhou gasped, dropping onto one knee and clapping a hand over his burning cheek. Blood pooled at the corner of his mouth. "Why did you hit me? I only tried to take back our routes at Pier Nine!"

"Shut your useless mouth!" Shen Wanshan barked, his chest heaving with cold fury. "General Qin Zhengguo? You idiot! I have done business in this country for forty years. General Qin is a retired soldier who guards his family name cleaner than glass! He would never lift a phone over a shipping fight, and even if he did, he could not make China Construction Bank, ICBC, and Huaxia Bank move together in twenty minutes!"

He pointed a shaking finger down at his kneeling grandson.

"Do you know what it takes to lock 12 billion yuan across three national banks in twenty minutes without a single warning reaching my desk? Not one friend in Futian CBD answered my phone tonight! Even Deputy Director Ma turned his phone off! We did not step on Haiyue Group's toes, Jingzhou. You kicked an iron mountain in the dark!"

Shen Jingzhou stared up in shock, all arrogance draining from his eyes. Beside the pillar, veteran steward Zhou Dequan hurried over with a clean towel, careful not to help the young master up without a nod.

Shen Wanshan walked to the window and took a cigarette from a silver box. Zhou Dequan clicked a brass lighter at once. Shen Wanshan took a long drag and blew a gray cloud against the glass.

"Tell me everything that happened at Pier Nine," Shen Wanshan ordered. "Every word. Every face. Leave out one detail, and I will ship you to the northern coal yards tomorrow."

Shen Jingzhou wiped the blood from his lip. His voice was small now, stripped of swagger.

"Nothing unusual happened at first," Shen Jingzhou stammered. "Qin Yuhan refused to sign over the 30% route share, so I told Chief Inspector Lu Guobin to lock Pier Nine for seven days. That was all!"

"Who else was there?" Shen Wanshan turned slowly, his eyes sharp as knives.

Shen Jingzhou hesitated. "Just one nobody. A young man in a cheap white shirt and dark pants. He carried a cloth folder and a scratched green thermos. He said he worked at some tiny Shanghai office called Hengxin Logistics and that six of their containers were on the Haiyue Star."

"What did he say?" Shen Wanshan asked, his voice dropping.

"He drank tea right in front of my guards," Shen Jingzhou whispered, a chill running down his spine as he remembered those flat, calm eyes. "He told me I had until five o'clock to open the gate because he did not like working overtime."

Shen Wanshan froze. The glowing tip of his cigarette burned slowly toward his fingers.

"Five o'clock," Shen Wanshan repeated softly.

"Yes, Grandfather," Shen Jingzhou stammered. "At ten to five, He Mingcheng called to say the banks froze everything. Then the port chief ran down in person, suspended Lu Guobin, and unlocked the gate. Police took Lu away at five."

Heavy quiet gripped the penthouse. He Mingcheng's face turned as pale as the notices in his hands.

Shen Wanshan crushed his cigarette into a crystal ashtray. All loud anger left his face, replaced by something colder and far more dangerous.

"A clerk from Hengxin Logistics," Shen Wanshan murmured. "With a scratched green thermos. Setting a five o'clock deadline for three national banks."

"Old Master," Zhou Dequan asked carefully, "should we send men to watch Qin Yuhan's hotel near MixC Mall?"

"No!" Shen Wanshan snapped. "Nobody goes within a kilometer of that young man tonight. If my men touch him before we know who stands behind him, the Shen Family will not see next month."

He turned toward the dark bay where Hong Kong glowed across the water.

"Zhou Dequan," Shen Wanshan said gravely. "Call Hong Kong on the private line. Tell the Seven-Harbor Consortium that Shenzhen has a serious problem, and ask them to send someone senior across the bridge tonight."

Zhou Dequan's eyes widened. "The Seven-Harbor Consortium? Old Master, they will demand a bigger cut of the northern shipments."

"If we do not call them in," Shen Wanshan replied grimly, "there will be no Shen Family left to take any cut at all. Make the call."
