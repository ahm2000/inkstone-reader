# Chapter 166: Fury at Shenzhen Bay No. 1

A cold late November wind rattled the floor-to-ceiling glass of the top penthouse at Shenzhen Bay No. 1. Far below, the dark water of the bay mirrored the bright towers of Nanshan and the distant lights of Hong Kong across the strait. Inside the marble living room, the air was hot enough to choke a man.

Crash.

A thin porcelain teacup shattered against the slate wall, scattering wet tea leaves and white shards across the imported wool rug. Shen Wanshan stood behind his rosewood desk, his chest heaving and his face dark with anger. At sixty-two, the patriarch of the Shen Family rarely lost his temper in front of his own household, yet tonight his knuckles were white against the edge of the desk.

"200 million yuan in port storage leases," Shen Wanshan barked, his voice sharp as broken glass. "Gone in one afternoon! And not just that. Three local shipping brokers froze our credit lines before sunset. Do you know what the vice president of the Shenzhen Port Bank just told me on the phone? He told me to settle our own mess before asking for another cent!"

Standing six feet away with his head bowed, Shen Jingzhou clenched his jaw. A faint red mark still sat on his cheek from the slap his father had given him ten minutes earlier. "Dad, it was not my fault alone," he muttered, his voice tight with resentment. "Qin Yuhan refused to budge an inch. I offered her a clean partnership on the southern shipping corridor, and she threw the draft back in my face!"

"A clean partnership?" Shen Wanshan laughed coldly, stepping around the desk. "You tried to swallow sixty percent of Haiyue Group's southern freight channel for thirty cents on the dollar, and you insulted her in a public banquet hall! Did you forget who her grandfather is? Elder Qin Zhengguo may be retired, but half the old generals in the southern theater still call him Sir!"

"Retired means out of power!" Shen Jingzhou shot back, his eyes flashing with spite. "Every business circle in Shenzhen knows Elder Qin lives quietly in Shanghai and never touches Haiyue's books. Qin Yuhan was just putting on a brave front! And do you know who sat next to her the entire time, smirking like a fool while drinking warm water out of a dented green thermos?"

Shen Wanshan paused, frowning. "Her husband?"

"Yes! Chen Xuan!" Shen Jingzhou spat the name out as if it tasted sour. "I had my assistant pull his file this morning. He is a junior logistics supervisor on the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics in Shanghai! No shares, no title, no family background, nothing! He sat there eating braised beef while my security guards waited outside, and he had the nerve to tell me my wine tasted sour!"

Shen Wanshan stared at his son for a long moment, his anger cooling into a heavier, sharper suspicion. "If he is just a quiet Shanghai clerk and Elder Qin did not make a single phone call, then explain this to me, Jingzhou. Why did three Shenzhen banks and two customs clearance agencies suddenly turn ice cold toward the Shen Family within four hours of your dinner?"

Shen Jingzhou opened his mouth, then stopped. His face paled slightly. "Maybe Haiyue Group has a hidden partner in Guangdong. Or maybe Qin Yuhan pulled strings with Shanghai bankers. It cannot be that clerk. A man who carries a 15-yuan tin thermos does not move 800 million yuan in port credit!"

"I do not care who moved it," Shen Wanshan said grimly, walking to the tall glass window and staring out across the dark bay. "What matters is that our cash flow is squeezed. We have 500 million yuan in short-term warehouse bonds maturing next month. If the Shenzhen banks do not roll them over, the Shen Family's shipping arm will crack right down the middle."

He stood silent for ten seconds, weighing his pride against survival. Then he reached into his silk jacket, pulled out an encrypted satellite handset, and dialed a Hong Kong number he had not touched in eight months.

The line rang twice before clicking open. Soft classical piano music drifted faintly from the other end, followed by a calm, unhurried Cantonese-accented Mandarin voice.

"Old Shen," the voice said smoothly. "It is past 10:30 PM. You only call this line when your house is on fire."

Shen Wanshan straightened his spine, his tone dropping immediately from tyrant to junior partner. "Director Leung, I apologize for disturbing your rest. We hit a hard wall in Shenzhen today. Haiyue Group from Shanghai moved into our southern freight route, and someone behind them quietly jammed our local bank lines."

On the other end, Victor Leung, the core decision-maker of the Seven-Harbor Consortium in Hong Kong, let out a soft, amused chuckle. "Haiyue Group? Qin Yuhan's little company from Pudong? Old Shen, the Seven-Harbor Consortium handed you three Shenzhen berths and 300 million yuan in offshore trade liquidity so you could hold the Pearl River gate for us. Now a young woman from Shanghai has you calling Hong Kong in a panic?"

Sweat beaded on Shen Wanshan's forehead. "Director Leung, she is stubborn, and the local banks suddenly stopped answering my calls. If Haiyue takes our southern corridor, Seven-Harbor's own transshipment schedule through Shekou will be exposed to mainland audits."

The piano music on the other end paused. When Victor Leung spoke again, the amusement was gone, replaced by crisp, icy precision.

"That is the only sentence you said tonight that matters," Victor Leung said quietly. "Nobody touches our Shekou transshipment channel. Not Haiyue Group, and not whichever Shanghai banker she hired. Keep your son locked inside the house and stop making noise."

"Yes, Director Leung," Shen Wanshan breathed, relief and fear tangling in his chest. "What should I prepare?"

"Prepare a pot of good Pu-erh tea," Victor Leung replied. "I am sending Lin Zhaorong across the border tonight. He will be at Shenzhen Bay No. 1 before midnight. By Thursday night, Qin Yuhan will sign over her southern route with both hands, or Haiyue Group will learn what happens when offshore capital closes the door."

The line went dead. Shen Wanshan slowly lowered the phone, a cold smile returning to his thin lips as he turned back to his shaken son.

"Clean up the floor, Jingzhou," Shen Wanshan said softly. "Hong Kong is sending a butcher."
