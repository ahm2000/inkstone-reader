# Chapter 67: The High-Speed Train to Lanshui

Monday morning broke crisp and clear over Qingyan High-Speed Railway Station, with pale autumn sunlight slanting across the glass canopy above Platform Four.

Chen Xuan arrived at seven thirty in his washed gray jacket and clean knit sweater, carrying a faded canvas backpack. Tucked into the side pocket was his scratched green thermos, and looped around his wrist was a warm cloth pouch.

Inside sat six freshly boiled tea eggs and three sesame flatbreads that Liu Meifang had insisted on packing at six in the morning, refusing to let him board a train hungry.

Near the departure board, General Manager Sun Haoran stood in his finest pressed navy suit, his leather briefcase gripped tightly against his thigh. Beside him, Manager Hu Dewei wore a slightly too-tight striped tie and paced in small circles, checking the cream-colored invitation envelope for the fourth time.

"Chen Xuan! Over here!" Hu Dewei called out, his voice pitched higher than usual. "Thank goodness you are early. I worried the morning bus might hit traffic near East Avenue."

"Good morning, General Manager Sun Haoran. Good morning, Manager Hu Dewei," Chen Xuan greeted them with an easy smile. "The bus was on time. Mom boiled some tea eggs before I left, so we have breakfast for the ride."

Sun Haoran relaxed slightly and chuckled, though a faint line of tension lingered between his brows. "Your mother always thinks of everything, Chen Xuan. Boarding for Train G1428 to Lanshui City opens in ten minutes. Let us get in line."

* * *

Ten minutes later, the sleek white high-speed train glided out of Qingyan Station, leaving behind the green hills of the county as the rails curved north toward the provincial capital.

Inside Car Seven, second-class seating hummed with quiet conversation and the steady rhythm of steel wheels. Sun Haoran took the window seat, Chen Xuan sat in the middle, and Hu Dewei took the aisle seat, his knees bouncing nervously against the tray table.

Before the train reached cruising speed, Hu Dewei pulled a printed brochure from his briefcase and leaned inward.

"General Manager Sun Haoran, I stayed up until midnight checking the guest list for this Lanshui Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum," Hu Dewei whispered, wiping sweat from his temple despite the cool cabin air. "My hands are still cold thinking about it."

"It is a provincial forum, Hu Dewei," Sun Haoran said, straightening his collar. "Of course the big companies will attend."

"Not just big companies," Hu Dewei muttered anxiously, tapping the glossy page. "Look at these names. Lanshui Port Authority. Wanfeng Freight Group, with over three thousand heavy trucks and four coastal terminals. Two provincial banks, plus an overseas equity delegation from Europe. And then there is Hengxin Logistics, a county fleet with forty-two trucks and one rented dispatch yard."

Sun Haoran's fingers tightened on his briefcase clasp. Even though he tried to project steady leadership, the scale of the roster weighed visibly on his shoulders.

"We did not sneak in, Hu Dewei," Sun Haoran replied steadily, though his voice lacked its usual boom. "The provincial federation sent us an official gold-embossed invitation. They named Chen Xuan as our regional freight representative because of the Haiyue Group contract and Yunxi Old Street's smooth reopening."

"I am proud of Chen Xuan, truly," Hu Dewei sighed, slumping back against the headrest. "I just worry those provincial tycoons will look straight through us, or treat us like country bumpkins who wandered into the wrong hall."

Chen Xuan listened quietly. Reaching into the cloth pouch on his lap, he took out two warm tea eggs, tapped their shells gently against the tray table, and handed them over with clean napkins.

"Have an egg while they are warm, General Manager Sun Haoran, Manager Hu Dewei," Chen Xuan said mildly. "Mom steeped these overnight with star anise and black tea."

Hu Dewei blinked and accepted the warm egg. "Thank you, Chen Xuan. Are you not nervous at all?"

"Not really," Chen Xuan replied honestly, unscrewing his scratched green thermos. A light fragrance of chrysanthemum tea drifted across their row as he poured a small cup. "We are going to listen to the speeches, take neat notes on the new freight policies, and represent Hengxin Logistics politely. Nobody can fault us for doing our job properly."

Sun Haoran smiled genuinely, peeling his egg with practiced fingers. "Chen Xuan has the steadiest nerves in the company. Listen to him, Hu Dewei. Keep our heads up, stay polite, and learn what we can. Besides, the forum hotel covers our meals for three days."

"The invitation did mention a full provincial buffet," Chen Xuan added lightly, his eyes crinkling with quiet amusement. "Mom told me to eat properly while I am away."

Hu Dewei let out a relieved laugh and took a bite of his egg. "Well, if Liu Meifang gave orders, we cannot disappoint her. These tea eggs really are delicious."

* * *

Across the narrow aisle and two rows ahead, a slender commuter in a plain charcoal windbreaker and rimless spectacles sat by the window with an unread financial newspaper resting across his lap.

To every passenger in Car Seven, he looked like an ordinary accountant heading to Lanshui City for a quarterly audit. He wore scuffed black loafers, kept a modest laptop bag between his feet, and never once glanced back toward Chen Xuan's row.

Yet his breathing stayed completely even, his hands rested loose beside his knees, and his reflection in the dark window tracked every traveler walking down the aisle. Whenever a conductor pushed a beverage cart past or a passenger reached for an overhead rack, his eyes read posture and hidden contours in a fraction of a second.

He was an unseen operative of the permanent Zero Guard team, moving silently inside the invisible fifty-meter ring that never left Chen Xuan's side.

Two carriages back, in the quiet rear corner of Car Five, Number Nine sat alone beside the window in a dark coat, his posture straight as a winter pine.

A matte-black phone vibrated once on his tray table. Number Nine picked it up without changing expression and read the encrypted line sent ahead by Number Seven and Number Nineteen from the provincial capital.

*Lanshui International Convention Hotel perimeter mapped. Foreign clearing executives checked into the twenty-eighth floor last night. Song Baisheng and Li Weiming arrived thirty minutes apart. Outer ring secure.*

Number Nine deleted the message with one thumb stroke and gazed out at the rushing autumn fields. Number One's order was clear: do not obstruct Chen Xuan's trip, do not alarm his colleagues, and let every hidden player in Lanshui City step into the light on their own feet.

Back in Car Seven, the train's digital display flashed three hundred kilometers per hour as the silver ribbon of the Lanshui River appeared on the northern horizon.

Chen Xuan finished peeling his own tea egg, took a slow, contented sip from his green thermos, and watched the distant skyline of Lanshui City rise calmly through the morning haze.
