# Chapter 145: The Secret Kept by the Lake

Half an hour later, the small purple clay teapot sat empty beside the cooling charcoal stove.

Chen Xuan stood up from his stone stool, screwed the lid snugly onto his dented green metal thermos, and tucked it under his arm.

"Please stay seated, Elder Song," Chen Xuan said warmly as the old man started to rise. "The lake breeze is picking up, and the stone steps are slick with fallen leaves. I should head back to the airfield now if I want to make it home to Shanghai before 3:00 PM."

Elder Song settled back onto his stool with a fond smile, his bright ninety-year-old eyes crinkling. "Right, right! You still have to finish your Saturday warehouse check so your wife does not lecture your general manager on Monday! Go on, young friend. I already rinsed the second teacup and tucked it away inside my wicker basket. Anyone who climbs up here later will only see one old man enjoying the view."

Chen Xuan gave a respectful nod. "Take good care of your health this winter, Elder Song. Keep the charcoal stove close when the snow comes."

"I will," Elder Song replied gently. "Safe travels home, young friend."

Chen Xuan turned and walked unhurriedly down the leaf-strewn stone steps toward the bamboo grove, where Number 7 waited quietly beside the plain black sedan. Within minutes, the car slipped smoothly down the mountain road and vanished beyond the autumn pines.

An hour later, the quiet peace of the Western Hills footpath was broken by a pair of heavy, energetic boots crunching briskly through the fallen maple leaves.

"Old Song! Are you still sitting up here freezing your ears off?" a booming, cheerful voice called out from the bend in the trail.

Elder Song smiled and reached down to add two fresh lumps of charcoal to the small clay stove. A moment later, retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo strode into the pavilion wearing a thick olive wool coat. Under one arm he carried a rolled wooden chessboard, and in his other hand swung a red cloth box of aged Pu-erh tea.

"You always walk like you are still inspecting an artillery regiment," Elder Song teased, gesturing to the stone stool across from him. "Sit down and catch your breath."

Elder Qin dropped onto the stool with a hearty laugh, then paused. His sharp veteran eyes swept across the stone table, lingering on the fresh gray ash in the stove and the warm dampness along the spout of the purple clay teapot.

"Aha!" Elder Qin leaned forward, lowering his voice to a rumbling whisper. "The outer gatehouse was unmanned until noon, and your kettle is still warm. Do not try to fool an old comrade, Song. Did the holder of the Black Order really come up here this morning?"

Elder Song picked up his single white porcelain cup and took a slow, unhurried sip of tea. "I have no idea what you are talking about, Lao Qin. An old man cannot enjoy a quiet morning by the lake without you sending out a scouting report?"

"Come off it!" Elder Qin slapped the stone table with a grin. "Half the cabinet is still talking about how cleanly that 5 trillion yuan credit gap got sealed and how those foreign short-sellers got sent packing before lunch. I am not asking for state secrets, Song! Just tell me one thing. What kind of towering, iron-willed titan sits behind that Black Order? Is he an old hidden master from overseas, or some brilliant strategist with hair whiter than ours?"

Elder Song chuckled softly, his eyes twinkling with quiet amusement as he remembered his solemn vow. "Even if someone had sat across from me this morning, Lao Qin, my lips are sealed forever. All I will tell you is that this country is in very safe hands when calm people prefer quiet, unpretentious lives."

Elder Qin let out a loud, good-natured snort and shook his head. "Stubborn as a mule! Seventy years of friendship and you will not even give me a tiny hint."

Grumbling cheerfully, Elder Qin unrolled the wooden chessboard across the stone table and began clacking the heavy red and black pieces into place. "Fine, keep your grand mysteries. Let us play a round instead. I need to sharpen my openings before I fly back south next week."

"Still losing to that young fellow in Shanghai?" Elder Song asked with a knowing smile.

Elder Qin's eyebrows shot up immediately, half indignant and half deeply proud. "Do not even get me started on my granddaughter's husband! Yuhan is a brilliant company president who runs half a skyscraper, and who did she marry? A lazy 12,000-yuan logistics clerk who clocks out at 4:55 PM on the dot every single day!"

"Sounds like a sensible young man," Elder Song remarked mildly, moving a red cannon forward.

"Sensible? The boy carries a dented 15-yuan green metal thermos everywhere he goes, drinks cheap jasmine tea, and looks half asleep when he sits across the chessboard!" Elder Qin complained fondly, moving his black horse to block. "Yet somehow he beats me three games out of five without even breaking a sweat! And the worst part is I cannot even stay mad at him, because the second the game ends he walks into the kitchen and cooks the best braised pork ribs I have ever tasted in my life."

Midway through lifting his porcelain teacup, Elder Song's hand paused for half a second.

A dented 15-yuan green metal thermos. Cheap jasmine tea. A lazy logistics clerk in Shanghai who loves cooking braised pork ribs for his wife.

Elder Song's sharp ninety-year-old mind stayed calm, never letting a flicker of surprise cross his face. He did not know the boy's name, and he would never ask. Whether it was the funniest coincidence in the world or something far more remarkable, his promise by the lake stood like iron.

Slowly, Elder Song set his teacup down and burst into a long, hearty laugh.

"What is so funny?" Elder Qin asked, blinking in confusion over the chessboard. "I am telling you my granddaughter's husband is too relaxed for his own good!"

"Nothing at all, Lao Qin," Elder Song chuckled warmly, moving his chariot across the river line. "Just listen to an old friend. Treasure that grandson-in-law of yours, eat all the pork ribs he cooks for you, and never let him go."

Elder Qin scratched his gray head, baffled, while the late autumn breeze carried Elder Song's cheerful laughter across the sunlit lake.
