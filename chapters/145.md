# Chapter 145: The Four-Star Bow

Half an hour later, the small purple-clay teapot sat empty beside the cooling brazier.

Chen Xuan stood up from the stone stool and picked up his green thermos.

"Please stay seated, Elder Song," Chen Xuan said warmly. "The stone steps are cool, and the breeze off the water is picking up. Keep your strength for lunch."

Elder Song smiled fondly and settled back onto the stool. "Alright, alright. I will take advantage of being ninety years old today."

"Take good care of your health," Chen Xuan said, bowing slightly from the waist. "Thank you for the tea."

"Go home, young man," Elder Song replied softly, his bright eyes resting on Chen Xuan's calm face one last time. "Go back to your quiet streets and your peaceful weekends. This country owes you a good night's sleep."

Chen Xuan nodded once, turned, and walked unhurriedly down the stone steps of the pavilion.

His worn canvas shoes tapped evenly along the gray flagstones.

Fifty paces below the pavilion, four-star General Qin Zhengguo was still standing beside the ancient pine tree, exactly where he had waited for the past forty minutes.

Hearing steady footsteps approaching from the pavilion, Qin Zhengguo stepped squarely onto the center of the stone path.

General Qin Zhengguo stopped five paces in front of Chen Xuan.

He was a man who had commanded hundreds of thousands of troops, stood firm through border storms, and walked into the highest halls of the capital without ever bending his spine for anyone. Three weeks ago, he had stared down corrupt theater officers with a lit cigarette and iron eyes.

Yet now, on this quiet stone path in the Western Hills, the battle-hardened four-star general brought his heels together with a crisp, quiet click. He straightened his broad back to its full height, looked straight at Chen Xuan for one clear heartbeat, and then bent deeply from the waist in a long, silent bow of pure respect.

He did not speak a word. He did not ask a question. There was no mention of Qin Yuhan, no mention of Yunxi County, and no mention of the forty ministries swept clean in a single week.

That deep, silent bow said everything a soldier could ever say to the man who had saved the country's backbone without asking for a medal or a name.

Chen Xuan stopped cleanly on the flagstones. His calm eyes softened with genuine respect for the old soldier. He returned a polite, unhurried nod, waited until the general's shoulders began to rise, and then stepped smoothly past him on the left side of the path.

"Take care of Elder Song, General Qin," Chen Xuan said quietly as he passed. "The wind by the lake is turning cool."

"I will, sir," Qin Zhengguo answered quietly.

Chen Xuan walked on down the slope toward the cedar grove where Number 7 and the black sedan waited.

As General Qin Zhengguo straightened his tall frame from the bow, he turned slightly to watch his guest depart down the sunlit flagstones.

At that exact moment, a bright shaft of late-morning sunlight broke through the pine branches and fell straight across Chen Xuan's right hand.

It lit up the faded green paint, the two small dents near the base, and the worn silver scratches running down the side of a cheap fifteen-yuan metal thermos swinging casually from Chen Xuan's fingers.

General Qin Zhengguo froze completely.

His broad shoulders locked stiff as iron. His jaw slackened slightly. The battle-hardened general who had never blinked under live artillery stood utterly motionless on the stone path, staring at that green thermos as if lightning had just struck right between his boots.

In a single blinding flash, a memory from two weeks ago slammed straight into his mind.

He heard his son Qin Zhengming's laughing voice over a Sunday family call from Shanghai. He heard his granddaughter Qin Yuhan's amazed voice right after it, telling her grandfather about the strangest, calmest young office clerk she had ever met at Shanghai Port.

A quiet young man in a cheap dark windbreaker.

A young man who could make a corrupt port customs chief turn pale in five minutes with one quiet look, yet rode the public bus home, never missed leaving work at 4:55 on Fridays, and carried a battered, scratched green metal thermos everywhere he went.

*"If Grandfather heard that story,"* Qin Zhengming had joked on the phone, *"he would want to invite that young man to the capital for tea."*

Qin Zhengguo's throat went completely dry. Not a single sound came out of his mouth.

Down below in the gravel courtyard, Chen Xuan reached the black sedan. Number 7 opened the rear door smoothly and bowed his head. Chen Xuan climbed inside, setting his green thermos neatly beside him on the seat. The door shut with a quiet thud, and the sedan pulled smoothly out through the cedars onto the north garden road.

Fifty meters ahead along the shaded lane, an unmarked dark van rolled silently out from beneath the pines, sliding smoothly into the lead without a horn or a word.

General Qin Zhengguo stood frozen beside the pine tree until even the faint hum of the sedan's engine vanished into the quiet hills.

Slowly, mechanically, the four-star general reached into his pocket, pulled out his rumpled pack of cigarettes, and realized his weathered fingers were trembling slightly as he tapped one out. He did not even light it. He just stood there holding the unlit cigarette, staring down the empty stone path in stunned, speechless silence.

"General Qin?"

A soft, raspy voice drifted down from the pavilion fifty paces above.

Qin Zhengguo blinked hard, pulled himself together with a slow breath, and walked back up the stone path to the pavilion.

Elder Song sat calmly at the round stone table, looking out over the bright lake with a peaceful smile. He glanced up at Qin Zhengguo's stunned face and chuckled softly.

"What is the matter, Zhengguo?" Elder Song asked with a knowing twinkle in his old eyes. "You look like you just saw a ghost walk down the hill."

Qin Zhengguo stopped beside the stone table, swallowed hard, and stared down at the two empty porcelain cups. For a long moment, the four-star general did not know whether to laugh or shake his head in disbelief.

"Elder Song," Qin Zhengguo said in a hoarse, quiet voice, "did he mention where he works?"

"I did not ask," Elder Song replied serenely, resting his thin hands on the warm clay teapot. "And neither should you, Zhengguo. Remember only two things: keep Yunxi County clean, and never let anyone bother an ordinary young man who wants to leave work at 4:55 on Fridays."

Qin Zhengguo stood straight by the lake, looked down once more toward the winding mountain road below, and let out a long, low breath filled with pure awe.

"Yes, Elder Song," the four-star general murmured softly. "I understand completely."
