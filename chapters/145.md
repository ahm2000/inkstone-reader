# Chapter 145: The Secret Kept by the Lake

Half an hour later, the small purple clay teapot sat empty beside the cooling charcoal stove.

Chen Xuan stood up from his stone stool, screwed the lid snugly onto his dented green metal thermos, and tucked it under his arm.

"Please stay seated, Elder Song," Chen Xuan said warmly as the old man started to rise. "The lake breeze is picking up, and the stone steps are slick with fallen leaves. I should head back to the airfield now if I want to make it home to Shanghai before 3:00 PM."

Elder Song settled back onto his stool with a fond smile, his bright ninety-year-old eyes crinkling. "Right, right! You still have to finish your Saturday warehouse check so your wife does not lecture your general manager on Monday! Go on, young friend. I already rinsed the second teacup and tucked it away inside my wicker basket. Anyone who climbs up here later will think I spent the morning talking to the water birds."

"Take good care of your health, Elder Song," Chen Xuan said, bowing slightly with sincere respect. "Dress warm when the winter snow arrives next month."

"I will, young friend. Safe journey home."

Chen Xuan turned and walked unhurriedly down the leaf-strewn flagstone path. At the gravel clearing below, Number 7 stood beside the plain black sedan and opened the rear door immediately.

"Back to the airfield, Young Master?" Number 7 asked quietly.

"Yes. Let us head home."

The black sedan glided smoothly down the winding mountain road and passed beneath the carved stone archway. Five minutes after the car disappeared into the valley below, the distant barrier posts quietly resumed their regular watch.

Twenty minutes later, brisk, heavy footsteps echoed up the flagstone path toward the lakeside pavilion.

A tall, broad-shouldered old man with silver crew-cut hair and a thick military-style wool overcoat strode around the bend, his cheeks rosy from the steep late November climb. In his left hand, he carried a polished wooden box of aged Pu-erh tea.

Retired four-star general Elder Qin Zhengguo stepped into the pavilion, set the tea box down with a solid thump on the stone table, and narrowed his sharp eyes at his old comrade.

"Old Song!" Elder Qin boomed cheerfully, dropping onto the stone stool Chen Xuan had vacated only minutes earlier. "Do not try to play innocent with me! I heard you cleared the entire north ridge before dawn and turned off every camera on the mountain! Tell me the truth. Did you actually meet the holder of the Black Order up here this morning?"

Elder Song did not even blink. Calmly, he poured himself a fresh cup of plain hot water from the small kettle and took a leisurely sip.

"The autumn air is crisp today, Old Qin," Elder Song replied serenely. "An old man is not allowed to enjoy one quiet morning by the lake without filing a battle report with you?"

Elder Qin leaned forward eagerly, resting both elbows on the stone table. "Come on, we have been comrades for sixty years! You do not shut down the whole Western Hills just to watch ducks! That anonymous benefactor gifted 300 trillion yuan to the nation without asking for a single medal. Even Premier Gu Chengshan has been pacing his office wondering who he is! Just give me one tiny hint. Is he some legendary gray-haired patriarch? A hidden financial titan from overseas?"

Elder Song chuckled softly, setting his cup down with an expression as immovable as an ancient pine.

"Old Qin, you know the rules better than anyone," Elder Song said gently, though his tone left zero room for debate. "Some people carry mountains on their shoulders precisely because nobody knows their faces. If a benefactor wishes to stay unseen, our only duty is to guard that peace and stop asking questions."

Elder Qin stared at his old friend for three seconds, then snorted with a grin. "Stubborn as a granite boulder, just like fifty years ago! Fine, keep your grand secret. I will not pry another word out of you."

Shaking his head, Elder Qin opened his wooden tea box and tossed a pinch of dark Pu-erh leaves into the rinsed pot. As earthy steam rose between them, his fierce expression melted into affectionate exasperation.

"Speaking of people who refuse to chase fame," Elder Qin grumbled fondly, "you should see the grandson-in-law I ended up with in Shanghai."

Elder Song smiled warmly. "Oh? Your brilliant granddaughter Yuhan got married, did she not? What is he like?"

"Do not get me started!" Elder Qin waved a big hand, though anyone could hear the fondness beneath his rough voice. "My granddaughter runs the entire Haiyue Group without breaking a sweat, and who does she pick? A lazy 12,000-yuan logistics clerk! The kid has zero ambition, clocks out on the dot at 4:55 PM every afternoon, and carries a dented 15-yuan green metal thermos everywhere!"

Elder Song paused for half a second with his teacup halfway to his lips, a tiny flicker of amusement crossing his eyes before he smoothly took a sip. Surely half the working men in China owned a cheap green metal thermos.

"Sounds like a steady, grounded young man," Elder Song remarked with a mild smile.

"Too grounded!" Elder Qin scoffed, grinning despite himself. "The shameless boy refuses every executive post I offer him, spends his evenings cooking braised pork ribs so delicious my mouth waters thinking about them, and beats me three games in a row at chess every time I visit! An old four-star general losing to a logistics clerk! Tell me, Old Song, where is the justice in that?"

Elder Song threw his head back and laughed warmly across the sunlit lake. "If he makes your granddaughter happy and cooks ribs that good, Old Qin, you should count yourself the luckiest grandfather in Beijing."

"Hmph, I suppose he treats Yuhan like treasure," Elder Qin admitted, his gruff face softening as he lifted his teacup. "Still, next time I fly south, I am bringing my best chess manual."

By the quiet Western Hills lake, the two old comrades laughed and drank their tea in the late autumn sun, neither realizing for a single moment that the mysterious benefactor of the nation and the lazy Shanghai clerk were the exact same young man currently flying southward through the clouds, deciding whether to add rock sugar or star anise first to tonight's braised pork ribs.
