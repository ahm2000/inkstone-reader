# Chapter 147: Lanterns on Old Street

Rows of red silk lanterns swayed along both sides of Old Street, casting a warm glow over the worn gray flagstones. Golden ginkgo leaves drifted past the stone archway, mingling with the rich scent of roasted chestnuts, dried red dates, and toasted sesame oil from the corner noodle shop.

Just down the lane inside Mingde Hardware, the brass door chime rang brightly as two carpenters picked out heavy iron hinges and a neighbor counted copper pipe fittings at the wooden counter.

"Take two extra rubber washers, Little Li," Chen Mingde called out cheerfully as he weighed steel nails on his old metal scale. "Cold late autumn nights make outdoor pipes drip. No charge for the washers."

"Thank you, Uncle Mingde," the young man grinned, tucking the wrapped parcel under his arm. "Everybody in the county says your copper fittings last ten years longer than the big store by the train station."

Behind the counter, Wu Lanying tied a neat bow around a bundle of twine with a proud smile. Business had been steadier and livelier than ever since the street was repaved and the neighborhood settled into peaceful prosperity.

The brass chime rang again. Wu Lanying looked up and her face lit up at once.

"Xuan! Yuhan!" she cried, stepping around the counter with open arms. "Why did you not call from the station? Your father could have walked down to carry your bags."

"We only brought one small overnight bag, Mom," Chen Xuan laughed, setting a box of Shanghai osmanthus pastries on the counter. "The high-speed train was quick, and Yuhan wanted to walk through the archway and see the lanterns."

Qin Yuhan wore a soft camel wool coat and a cream scarf. Her cheeks were rosy from the cool autumn air as she hugged Wu Lanying warmly. "Auntie, Uncle, we missed you both. The street looks so lively today."

"Look how thin your jacket is, Xuan," Wu Lanying scolded gently, patting her son's shoulder before turning right back to Qin Yuhan with a beaming smile. "Come inside and sit by the kettle, Yuhan. I stewed red date and ginger tea this morning. Wait until you taste it."

Chen Mingde wiped his hands on his canvas apron, chuckling happily. "Go take a slow walk down Old Street while the sun is still up. Your mother has been planning tonight's dumplings since Thursday. Dinner will be ready by the time the lanterns light up."

Hand in hand, Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan stepped back out onto the cobbled lane.

Old Street hummed with relaxed weekend warmth. Neighbors sat on low wooden stools shelling peanuts outside their doorways, children chased drifting ginkgo leaves under the eaves, and steam rose from bamboo baskets outside the bun shop.

Halfway down the lane, a cheerful middle-aged uncle in a faded blue vest scooped hot chestnuts from a bubbling iron pan of black sand. Seeing them approach, he grinned widely and scooped an extra handful into a brown paper bag.

"Young Chen, you finally remembered the road home," the chestnut vendor teased warmly, handing the hot bag straight to Qin Yuhan. "Take these, Miss Qin. Fresh out of the pan. Look at your husband, he has put on healthy color since you started looking after him."

"Thank you, Uncle Wang," Qin Yuhan smiled, peeling a warm chestnut and popping half into Chen Xuan's mouth. "He still drinks tea out of the same dented thermos every day."

"Good habits last a lifetime," Uncle Wang chuckled, waving away Chen Xuan's attempt to pay for the extra scoop. "Go on, enjoy the breeze."

A few shops further along, a college-aged girl sweeping fallen maple leaves outside the small bookstore paused and leaned on her broom with a bright grin. "Brother Chen, back for Auntie Wu's dumplings again? Save me two if you have leftovers."

"Only if you sweep all the way to the corner," Chen Xuan joked back easily.

Beside the corner tea stall, a delivery rider resting on his electric scooter raised a paper cup in a casual salute and laughed as he chatted with an elderly woman sorting dried tangerines.

Chen Xuan met their eyes briefly and felt a quiet warmth in his chest. Every one of them carried zero stiffness, zero sharp tension, and zero trace of a guard's posture. They were simply living, breathing neighbors of Old Street, chatting with him like real elders and younger siblings.

Across the narrow lane, a quiet man in a plain corduroy jacket paused outside the grocery shop with a bottle of soy sauce in his hand. He was one of the White Guard watchers assigned to keep an eye on the county. He glanced down at his phone screen, where the latest background checks on the chestnut vendor, the bookstore student, and the delivery rider sat open.

Every single family record, tax filing, and school history was spotless, logical, and airtight. There was not a single flaw to report, leaving the White Guard watcher with only a faint, puzzled frown and no reason at all to interfere. He tucked his phone away and walked quietly down the lane.

As dusk settled over Yunxi County, the red silk lanterns flickered on one after another, bathing the cobblestones in a soft golden glow.

Qin Yuhan leaned her head lightly against Chen Xuan's shoulder as they turned back toward Mingde Hardware. "Every time we visit Old Street, my shoulders just drop," she murmured happily. "Everyone here feels like family."

"Because it is home," Chen Xuan said softly, tightening his hand around hers. "Come on. If we are late for Mom's dumplings, she will lecture me until tomorrow morning."

Laughing together under the warm autumn lanterns, they walked back toward the bright doorway of the little hardware shop.
