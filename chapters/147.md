# Chapter 147: Lanterns on Old Street

By Saturday afternoon, rows of red winter lanterns glowed along both sides of Old Street in Yunxi County, Handong Province. The crisp dusk air smelled of roasted chestnuts, dried red dates, and hot sesame oil drifting from the noodle shop near the stone archway.

Inside Mingde Hardware, the shop bell chimed as two carpenters picked out door hinges and a neighbor counted copper pipe fittings at the counter.

"Take two extra rubber washers, Little Li," Chen Mingde called out cheerfully as he weighed steel nails on his scale. "Winter frost makes outdoor pipes leak. No charge for the washers."

"Thank you, Uncle Mingde!" the young man grinned, tucking the bundle under his arm. "Everybody says your copper fittings last ten years longer than the big store by the station."

Behind the counter, Wu Lanying wrapped the nails neatly in brown paper. Warm laugh lines sat around her eyes now that the gangsters who once harassed Old Street were gone.

By the iron stove at the back, Chen Xuan sat on a low stool beside Qin Yuhan while his mother, Liu Meifang, peeled two tangerines. Wearing a soft beige sweater instead of her business suits, Qin Yuhan held an enamel mug of red date tea with both hands, her cheeks rosy from the fire. Across from them, Chen Jianguo sipped his tea and watched the shop floor with pride.

"Look at your Uncle Mingde," Chen Jianguo chuckled warmly. "He has not stopped smiling since eight this morning. Even his sore knee stopped bothering him once those bullies cleared out of Yunxi County."

"Good business and an honest street are the best medicine," Liu Meifang said happily, handing the sweetest tangerine half to Qin Yuhan first before giving the other half to Chen Xuan. "Yuhan, eat more fruit. You work so hard running that big office in Shanghai. When Chen Xuan told me on the phone that he turned down a 50,000 yuan manager job this week, I was worried you would be angry with him for being so lazy!"

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, her dark eyes curving into bright crescents as she bumped Chen Xuan's shoulder playfully. "Mom, how could I be angry? I earn plenty for both of us at Haiyue Group. If he became an executive and worked overtime every night, who would have hot radish soup waiting for me when I come home at six? Honestly, I was relieved when he told me he turned it down."

Chen Xuan popped a slice of tangerine into his mouth with an easy grin. "See, Mom? My wife appreciates my priorities. A corner office cannot compete with my kitchen apron."

Liu Meifang laughed out loud and patted Qin Yuhan's hand. "You spoil him too much, Yuhan! Still, your father and I never asked for fancy titles. Seeing the two of you happy and eating hot meals together is worth more than all the gold in the province."

Just then, a truck horn beeped twice outside the shop doors.

A white delivery van pulled up to the curb. Wang Lei hopped out in a dark jacket, carrying two woven bags bulging with apples and pears.

"Uncle Mingde! Aunt Lanying! Look what came off the orchard truck!" Wang Lei called out cheerfully, pushing open the shop door.

"Wang Lei, I told you last week to stop spending money bringing fruit every Saturday!" Wu Lanying scolded gently as she came around the counter to take a bag. "You have fuel and van payments to save up for."

"My van payments are three months ahead of schedule, Aunt Lanying!" Wang Lei grinned proudly, setting the second bag by the stove before spotting Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan. "Brother Chen! Yuhan! You came home for the weekend!"

"Good to see you, Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said warmly as Wu Lanying poured hot tea for the driver. "How are the highway freight runs treating you this month?"

"Steady as a rock!" Wang Lei said happily, blowing on his tea. "Ever since the county cleared out those crooked checkpoints and fixed the freight rules, nobody skims half my fee anymore. I run two clean loads to the provincial capital every week and still make it home for dinner with my mom."

Chen Mingde clapped Wang Lei on the shoulder. "You keep that van shining like a mirror. Put the truck in the back alley and stay for dinner tonight. We are setting up the big table in the courtyard."

"I never say no to Aunt Lanying's cooking!" Wang Lei laughed. "Let me first run two bags of pears down to Auntie Tian and Master Yu. They are coming over for supper too, right?"

"We will walk down with you and call them over," Chen Xuan said, standing up and offering a hand to Qin Yuhan.

They stepped out together onto the slate street as shopkeepers called out greetings under the red lanterns.

Three doors down by the dried goods stall, Auntie Tian was tying up her awning while Yu Changshun stood beside her in a blue coat holding a brass teapot. Talking with them in a dark coat and gray scarf was Director Wei Kun.

"Auntie Tian, your dried persimmons are sweeter every winter," Wei Kun smiled, paying for a paper sack. "My wife reminded me twice at lunch not to come home empty handed."

"Director Wei, you are always welcome on Old Street!" Auntie Tian beamed, tucking two extra persimmons into his sack. "Ever since you repaved the drainage stones and hung these red lanterns, families from the new district walk over here every evening. Our shops have never been safer or busier!"

"Honest folks deserve a clean street," Yu Changshun nodded warmly before spotting Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan approaching. "Look, Chen Xuan and his wife are here to call us to supper!"

Wei Kun turned around, stepping forward with quiet respect and a warm smile. "Good evening, Chen Xuan. Good evening, Miss Qin. Back in Yunxi County for a family weekend?"

"Good evening, Director Wei," Chen Xuan replied politely. "We came to join my parents and Uncle Mingde for dinner. Why not join our table for a bowl of hot soup before heading back?"

"I never turn down a lively Old Street dinner," Wei Kun chuckled warmly. "Half a bowl of hot soup with Uncle Mingde sounds wonderful."

Ten minutes later, around the big table in the courtyard behind Mingde Hardware, Wu Lanying and Liu Meifang set down a clay pot of pork ribs and radish, braised fish, and crispy peanuts. Chen Mingde, Chen Jianguo, Yu Changshun, Auntie Tian, Wang Lei, and Wei Kun raised their tea mugs and soup spoons amid laughter, while Qin Yuhan happily squeezed Chen Xuan's hand under the table in the lantern glow.
