# Chapter 147: Lanterns on Old Street

By Saturday afternoon, red winter lanterns were already lit along both sides of Old Street in Yunxi County.

The cold dusk air smelled of roasted chestnuts, sweet dried dates, and hot sesame oil from the noodle shop near the stone archway. Paper tassels swayed softly under the eaves of the old wooden shops, turning the gray slate road a warm, gentle gold.

Inside Mingde Hardware, the shop bell chimed every few minutes. Two carpenters from the east village were picking out brass door hinges, while a young homeowner counted copper pipe fittings at the wooden counter.

"Take two extra washers, Little Li," Chen Mingde called out cheerfully as he weighed a scoop of steel nails on the old brass scale. "Winter frost makes outdoor pipes leak. No charge for the washers."

"Thank you, Uncle Mingde!" the young man said with a bright grin. "Everybody on the street says your fittings last ten years longer than the big mall down by the station."

Behind the counter, Wu Lanying wrapped the nails in brown paper and tied them neatly with red string. Her face had soft laugh lines around the eyes that had not been there a few months ago, when rough men had threatened to shut the street down.

By the small coal stove near the back wall, Chen Xuan sat on a low wooden stool, peeling a bright orange tangerine for his mother, Liu Meifang. Across from him, Chen Jianguo sipped hot tea from an enamel mug and watched the busy shop floor with quiet pride.

"Look at your uncle," Chen Jianguo murmured, nodding toward the counter with a small smile. "He has not stopped smiling since nine this morning. Even his bad knee stopped aching once those bullies cleared out of Yunxi."

"Good business is the best medicine," Liu Meifang said warmly, taking half the tangerine from Chen Xuan. "Xuan, did you really turn down a manager job in Shanghai? Your cousin Chen Hao texted me last night asking if you bumped your head."

Chen Xuan smiled easily and handed the other half of the tangerine to his father. "I did not bump my head, Mom. Managers work weekends. If I took that job, I would be sitting in a meeting room right now instead of eating tangerines by Uncle Mingde's stove."

Liu Meifang laughed softly and patted his wrist. "Listen to him. Well, your father and I never asked you to chase big titles. As long as you eat on time, sleep well, and stay safe, that is enough for us."

Just then, a gentle horn sounded twice outside the shop door.

A clean white delivery van pulled smoothly to the curb across from the hardware store. The side door slid open, and Wang Lei hopped out in a thick dark work jacket, his cheeks rosy from the winter wind. He carried two heavy woven bags bulging with red apples and sweet yellow pears.

"Uncle Mingde! Aunt Lanying! Look what just came off the morning truck from the mountain orchard!" Wang Lei called out, stepping into the warm light of the shop.

"Wang Lei, you rascal, I told you not to spend your money bringing fruit every week!" Wu Lanying scolded gently, though she hurried around the counter to take one of the bags. "You have fuel and van payments to worry about."

"Payments are already way ahead of schedule, Aunt Lanying!" Wang Lei grinned widely, setting the second bag down by the stove. He spotted Chen Xuan and his face lit up even brighter. "Brother Chen! You came back for the weekend!"

"Good to see you, Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, standing up to pour him a cup of hot tea. "How are the freight routes this month?"

"Steady as a rock," Wang Lei said happily, taking the warm cup with both hands. "Ever since the county cleared out those crooked road checkpoints and fixed the freight rules, nobody tries to skim half my fee anymore. I run two clean loads to Shanghai every week, and I still get home in time for supper with my mom."

Chen Mingde stepped over, wiping his hands on his dark apron, and clapped Wang Lei on the shoulder. "That van of yours shines like a brand-new mirror. Stay for dinner tonight. Your Aunt Lanying is stewing pork ribs with radish."

"I would never say no to Aunt Lanying's ribs," Wang Lei laughed. "Let me first drop a bag of pears over at Auntie Tian's dried-goods stall and Master Yu's repair shop. Auntie Tian saved me two jars of salted plums last week."

"I will walk out with you," Chen Xuan said, slipping his hands into his gray jacket pockets. "It is nice outside before the wind picks up."

They stepped out onto the warm-lit slate of Old Street. Neighbors nodded and called out friendly greetings from doorway to doorway.

Three doors down, under a string of glowing red lanterns, Auntie Tian was weighing dried red dates and roasted melon seeds for two smiling schoolgirls. Standing right beside her wooden stall was Yu Changshun in his faded blue cotton coat, holding a freshly mended kettle, and beside him stood Director Wei Kun.

Wei Kun wore no badge and no stiff formal suit today. He was dressed in a plain dark wool coat with a gray scarf, looking like any ordinary neighbor out for an evening stroll.

"Auntie Tian, your dried persimmons are sweeter every winter," Wei Kun said with an easy, genuine smile, paying for a small paper sack with crisp bills. "My wife asked me twice not to come home without a pound."

"Director Wei, you are too kind!" Auntie Tian laughed, tucking two extra persimmons into the sack. "Ever since you sorted out the street drainage and put up these winter lanterns, even folks from the new district walk over here after supper. Business has doubled!"

"That is because Old Street takes care of its own," Yu Changshun said warmly, nodding his gray head. "People come where they feel welcome and safe. No shouting, no rough men kicking over baskets."

Wei Kun's eyes brightened as he spotted Chen Xuan and Wang Lei walking up. He did not stiffen or make a fuss that would draw curious stares from the neighbors. Instead, he stepped forward with quiet, natural respect and gave Chen Xuan a warm nod.

"Chen Xuan," Wei Kun said gently. "Home for the weekend?"

"Good evening, Director Wei," Chen Xuan replied politely. "Old Street looks very lively tonight."

"The county committee kept the old stone paving just as the shopkeepers asked," Wei Kun said, his voice carrying calm satisfaction. "The new power lines are tucked neatly under the eaves, and the patrol station at the north gate stays open all night. Nobody is going to trouble these families again."

"We all owe you a big thank you, Director Wei," Wang Lei chimed in earnestly, handing a bag of mountain pears to Auntie Tian and another to Yu Changshun.

"No need to thank me," Wei Kun said softly, his gaze resting on Chen Xuan for a brief, grateful second before turning back to the street. "I only did the job I was supposed to do. Enjoy your dinner with your family, Chen Xuan."

"Take care on the road, Director Wei," Chen Xuan replied.

Wei Kun smiled, tipped his head to Auntie Tian and Yu Changshun, and strolled unhurriedly toward the south archway, greeting two old men playing chess under a porch lamp along the way.

Chen Xuan stood beside the dried-goods stall for a quiet moment, listening to Auntie Tian tease Wang Lei about finding a girlfriend while Yu Changshun chuckled beside them. Down the street, warm yellow light spilled from the doorway of Mingde Hardware, where Liu Meifang was calling everyone in for hot soup.

Fifty meters back by the darkened corner near the old willow tree, two still shadows stood half-hidden beyond the lantern glow, watching the peaceful street in total silence before melting quietly into the dusk.

Chen Xuan did not look back. He picked up a warm paper bag of roasted chestnuts from Auntie Tian's stall, paid her with a smile, and walked slowly back toward his family. For the first time in a long winter, Old Street felt completely at peace.
