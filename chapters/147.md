# Chapter 147: Lanterns on Old Street

By Saturday afternoon, red winter lanterns glowed along both sides of Old Street in Yunxi County. The cold dusk air smelled of roasted chestnuts, dried dates, and hot sesame oil from the noodle shop near the stone archway.

Inside Mingde Hardware, the shop bell chimed as village carpenters picked out brass hinges and a young homeowner counted copper pipe fittings at the counter.

"Take two extra washers, Little Li," Chen Mingde called out cheerfully as he weighed steel nails on the brass scale. "Winter frost makes outdoor pipes leak. No charge for the washers."

"Thank you, Uncle Mingde!" the young man grinned. "Everybody says your fittings last ten years longer than the big mall by the station."

Behind the counter, Wu Lanying wrapped the nails in brown paper. Soft laugh lines sat around her eyes now that the rough men who once threatened the street were gone.

By the small coal stove in back, Chen Xuan sat on a low wooden stool while his mother, Liu Meifang, peeled him a tangerine. Across from him, Chen Jianguo sipped hot tea from an enamel mug and watched the busy shop floor with pride.

"Look at your uncle," Chen Jianguo murmured with a smile. "He has not stopped smiling since nine this morning. Even his bad knee stopped aching once those bullies cleared out of Yunxi."

"Good business is the best medicine," Liu Meifang said warmly, handing him half the tangerine. "Xuan, did you really turn down a manager job in Shanghai? Your cousin Chen Hao texted me last night asking if you bumped your head."

Chen Xuan smiled easily and took it. "I did not bump my head, Mom. Managers work weekends. If I took that job, I would be sitting in a meeting room right now instead of eating tangerines by Uncle Mingde's stove."

Liu Meifang laughed softly and patted his wrist. "Your father and I never asked you to chase big titles. As long as you eat on time, sleep well, and stay safe, that is enough for us."

Just then, a horn sounded twice outside.

A clean white delivery van pulled to the curb. Wang Lei hopped out in a dark work jacket, carrying two heavy woven bags bulging with red apples and yellow pears.

"Uncle Mingde! Aunt Lanying! Look what just came off the mountain orchard truck!" Wang Lei called out, stepping into the shop.

"Wang Lei, I told you not to spend money bringing fruit every week!" Wu Lanying scolded gently, taking one bag. "You have fuel and van payments to worry about."

"Payments are way ahead of schedule, Aunt Lanying!" Wang Lei grinned, setting the second bag by the stove before spotting Chen Xuan. "Brother Chen! You came back for the weekend!"

"Good to see you, Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, as Wu Lanying poured him hot tea. "How are the freight routes this month?"

"Steady as a rock," Wang Lei said happily. "Ever since the county cleared out those crooked road checkpoints and fixed the freight rules, nobody skims half my fee anymore. I run two clean loads to Shanghai every week and still get home for supper with my mom."

Chen Mingde clapped Wang Lei on the shoulder. "That van shines like a mirror. Stay for dinner tonight. Your Aunt Lanying is stewing pork ribs with radish."

"I would never say no to Aunt Lanying's ribs," Wang Lei laughed. "Let me first drop a bag of pears at Auntie Tian's dried goods stall and Master Yu's repair shop. Auntie Tian saved me two jars of salted plums last week."

"I will walk out with you," Chen Xuan said.

They stepped onto the slate road as neighbors called out friendly greetings.

Three doors down under the red lanterns, Auntie Tian was weighing red dates for two schoolgirls. Beside her stall stood Yu Changshun in his blue coat holding a mended kettle, and next to him stood Director Wei Kun in a plain dark wool coat and gray scarf.

"Auntie Tian, your dried persimmons are sweeter every winter," Wei Kun smiled, paying for a paper sack. "My wife asked me twice not to come home without a pound."

"Director Wei, you are too kind!" Auntie Tian laughed, tucking two extra persimmons into the sack. "Ever since you fixed the street drainage and put up these winter lanterns, folks from the new district walk over here after supper. Business has doubled!"

"Old Street takes care of its own," Yu Changshun said warmly. "People come where they feel safe. No rough men kicking over baskets anymore."

Spotting Chen Xuan and Wang Lei, Wei Kun stepped forward with quiet respect and gave Chen Xuan a warm nod.

"Chen Xuan," Wei Kun said gently. "Home for the weekend?"

"Good evening, Director Wei," Chen Xuan replied politely. "Old Street looks lively tonight."

"The county committee kept the stone paving just as the shopkeepers asked," Wei Kun said calmly. "The new power lines are tucked under the eaves, and the north gate patrol station stays open all night. Nobody will trouble these families again."

"We all owe you a big thank you, Director Wei," Wang Lei said, handing mountain pears to Auntie Tian and Yu Changshun.

"No need to thank me," Wei Kun said softly, glancing gratefully at Chen Xuan. "I only did my job. Enjoy dinner with your family, Chen Xuan."

"Take care on the road, Director Wei," Chen Xuan replied.

After Wei Kun strolled toward the south archway, Chen Xuan stood by the stall while Auntie Tian teased Wang Lei about finding a girlfriend and Liu Meifang called everyone in for soup.

Fifty meters back near the old willow tree, unnumbered Black Guard figures in plain black gear watched the street in total silence before melting into the dusk.

Chen Xuan bought a warm bag of roasted chestnuts from Auntie Tian and walked back to his family through the peaceful winter evening.
