# Chapter 191: The High Council in Hong Kong

Thick blue smoke drifted beneath the crystal chandelier of the sixty-second floor penthouse in Central, Hong Kong.

Outside the floor-to-ceiling windows, gray Friday clouds hung low over Victoria Harbour.

Inside the private dining room, however, the air was hot enough to ignite.

"Fourteen years of banking, gone before breakfast!" Marcus Vance shouted, slapping a stack of printed bank notices onto the dark mahogany table. His face was flushed crimson, and his gold watch shook as he jabbed at the red-stamped pages. "140 billion yuan! Locked tight! Every private vault in Zurich and every clearing line in Geneva froze overnight because China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank filed a joint fraud hold at the exact same minute!"

Across the table, Victor Leung did not flinch.

"Lower your voice, Marcus," Victor Leung said evenly, exhaling a slow stream of cigar smoke. "Screaming at paper will not open Zurich."

"Easy for you to sound calm!" Marcus Vance snapped, sinking back into his leather chair and grabbing his whiskey glass. "Half of my European partners have been calling since six this morning! They want to know how four mainland state banks suddenly learned the exact account numbers of shell companies that were never written on public books!"

At the far end of the mahogany table, Hao Zhenhai let out a sharp, humorless laugh.

"You think Zurich is our worst problem?" Hao Zhenhai said, his voice rough from smoke. "Open your eyes, Marcus. What happened in Shenzhen two days ago broke our spine."

He slid a stack of port manifests across the polished wood.

"The Shen Family is finished," Hao Zhenhai continued grimly. "Shenzhen Pier Nine is under full customs guard. 12 billion yuan in cargo, cranes, and warehouse contracts vanished in one afternoon. Old Man Shen is in a detention room, and his son could not even reach Shekou before patrol cars boxed him in. Our southern gateway is dead."

Heavy silence settled over the room, broken only by the clink of ice in Marcus Vance's glass.

Seven years ago, seven powerful harbor families had formed the Seven-Harbor Consortium to control private shipping lanes and offshore lending. One member sat in prison in Sha Province, and two had vanished after Shanghai Port fell.

Victor Leung leaned forward, his eyes cold behind the curling smoke.

"We lost Shanghai Port, Sha City, the Shen Family in Shenzhen, and now Zurich," Victor Leung said quietly. "Do either of you believe four disasters happened by pure luck in one month?"

"Of course not!" Marcus Vance muttered, wiping sweat from his forehead. "Someone is tearing us apart faster than our lawyers can file an appeal."

"Then listen," Victor Leung ordered, his tone hard enough to make both men stiffen. "As long as the Changbai Mountain vault stands untouched, we are not dead. Every gold bar from Macau, every original land deed, and every private ledger naming officials who owe us favors sits inside that mountain fortress in Jhengbai. Even if the Four National Banks freeze Europe, Changbai Mountain can buy our way back."

Hao Zhenhai took a long drag, nodding slowly. "Victor is right. Nobody outside this room knows the Changbai Mountain tunnel map. Our guards have six months of supplies, and the roads freeze solid next week. Even State Security cannot march through those pines without our lookouts spotting them twenty miles out."

Just as Marcus Vance exhaled a small breath of relief, a soft knock sounded at the oak double doors.

"Come in," Victor Leung called sharply.

Simon Kwok stepped inside. He carried a slim black folder in both hands as if it burned his fingers.

"Mr. Leung, Mr. Vance, Mr. Hao," Simon Kwok said nervously, hurrying to the head of the table. "An urgent report just arrived from the night supervisor at Changbai Mountain."

Victor Leung frowned, setting his cigar on the tray. "Speak clearly, Simon. Did the winter supply truck stall on the highway?"

"No, sir," Simon Kwok swallowed hard and spread six glossy photographs across the table. "Motion cameras on the north pine ridge above the vault triggered at two in the morning two nights ago. Our guards swept the snow at dawn with dogs and found nothing. No footprints, no broken branches, not even a cigarette butt."

Hao Zhenhai scoffed. "Then it was a wild boar or a deer. Why waste our time?"

"Look closely at the third photograph, Mr. Hao," Simon Kwok whispered.

Hao Zhenhai picked up the glossy print beneath the chandelier light. The scoff died instantly on his face.

Captured in grainy night vision among the snow-dusted pines stood three tall figures. They wore plain, heavy white winter coats that blended smoothly into the frost on the ridge. One of them held a compact matte-white rangefinder, aiming it straight down toward the hidden air shaft of the Changbai Mountain vault.

"What the hell is this?" Marcus Vance stood up so fast his chair scraped loudly across the marble floor. He snatched another photo. "White coats? White gear? That is not local police! That is not border patrol!"

Hao Zhenhai's cigarette burned down to the filter between his frozen fingers until the heat stung his skin. He dropped it hastily into an ashtray, his eyes wide with dread.

"White," Hao Zhenhai breathed, his voice cracking. "At Shanghai Port, our night manager swore a white sedan sat outside the gate before customs raided us. At Shenzhen Pier Nine, a dockworker saw men in white jackets walking past the cranes ten minutes before police arrived. Now they are standing on top of Changbai Mountain!"

Victor Leung stared down at the photograph for a long, silent moment. His calm mask finally cracked, replaced by cold fury. He picked up his Cuban cigar and crushed it straight into the crystal tray until sparks scattered across the glass.

"They did not stumble onto Changbai Mountain by accident," Victor Leung said, his voice low and venomous. "Someone on the mainland has been hunting us from Shanghai to Sha City to Shenzhen, and now they are measuring the doors of our last vault."

He snapped his fingers at the trembling aide.

"Simon! Bring me every leaked hotel log, conference guest list, flight record, and port camera still we paid for over the last month. Put everything from Shanghai Port, Sha City, and Shenzhen on this table within thirty minutes!"

"Right away, Mr. Leung!" Simon Kwok stammered, nearly running for the door.

Marcus Vance sank back into his chair, pouring more whiskey with an unsteady hand. "And what if we find whoever leads these white-coated scouts, Victor? If they can move all Four National Banks in one morning, who are we fighting?"

Victor Leung walked to the tall glass window, gazing out over the darkening waters of Victoria Harbour.

"No matter how big an organization is, Marcus, someone at the center gives the orders," Victor Leung said softly, his jaw tight. "Find that person on the mainland before they reach Changbai Mountain, and cut them down. Tonight."
