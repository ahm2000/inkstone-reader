# Chapter 191: The High Council in Hong Kong

Cold December rain streaked the floor-to-ceiling glass of the sixty-second floor in Central, Hong Kong (+8:00 GMT). Below the tower, gray mist swallowed Victoria Harbour, turning the cargo ferries into faint white smudges on dark water.

Inside the private boardroom of the Seven-Harbor Consortium, the air was warm, yet none of the five men seated around the long mahogany table looked comfortable. Across the far wall, three wide financial screens glowed with red columns. Every number represented a broken shipping line, a frozen credit facility, or a seized mainland asset.

Kou Zhenshan slapped a thick blue folder onto the polished wood. His face was flushed, and his knuckles trembled slightly as he leaned forward.

"Three bonded warehouses in Shenzhen were sealed at 6:00 AM," Kou Zhenshan said, his voice tight. "Customs auditors, banking regulators, and provincial police walked in together. They froze 420 million yuan in leveraged cargo credit before my managers even finished their morning tea. Not one of our local contacts gave us a five-minute warning."

Across the table, Alan Kwok rubbed his temples. "It is worse in Handong Province. Since Zhao Ruilong, Gao Yuliang, and Ding Yizhen fell, the entire provincial network has been scraped clean. Secretary Sha Ruijin and Li Dakang blocked every shell company linked to Shanshui Group. Our bid for the Jingzhou port hub was rejected without a hearing, and the Luzhou logistics corridor is completely shut to us."

"That is not all," Viktor Orlov said calmly from the end of the table. The tall Eastern European banker tapped his laptop, which was synced with trading desks in Zurich (+1:00 GMT). "During the overnight session, Aegis Meridian quietly bought out three of our distressed debt tranches in Shanghai. At the same time, they extended a fresh credit line to Haiyue Group. Haiyue has already signed four major freight contracts that belonged to us last month."

Marcus Tan stared at the glowing red wall. "Add Shenzhen, Handong, and Shanghai together. We have lost 1.8 billion yuan in seven days. If the banks in Singapore see these numbers on Monday morning, they will call in our collateral before lunch."

At the head of the table, Victor Leung said nothing for a long moment. He lifted a dark clay teapot and poured a thin stream of hot oolong tea into his porcelain cup. His movements were slow and deliberate, though a faint white line showed around his tightly pressed lips.

"Panic is for amateurs," Victor Leung said softly. He set the teapot down with a quiet click. "Handong was already damaged when Zhao Ruilong got greedy. Shenzhen is a regulatory squeeze, painful but survivable. What worries me is Shanghai. Someone in Shanghai is cutting our legs out from under us with surgical timing."

Kou Zhenshan reached into his folder and pulled out a glossy printed photograph, sliding it down the table toward Victor Leung.

"My freight brokers in Pudong spent three days tracing who leaked our altered bills of lading to the port authority," Kou Zhenshan said. "Every time we tried to pressure Haiyue Group or delay their winter shipments, the paperwork got rerouted cleanly around our bottlenecks. The trail kept dying at a single logistics desk on the twelfth floor of Hengxin Logistics."

Victor Leung picked up the photograph. It was a slightly grainy street-camera frame taken outside a subway station in Pudong during a cold rain. It showed a young man in his late twenties wearing a simple gray wool coat and carrying a dented green metal thermos in one hand. His posture was completely relaxed, almost lazy, as if he had nowhere urgent to be.

"A clerk?" Alan Kwok asked, leaning over to squint at the print. "You are telling us a modest freight clerk is coordinating against Seven-Harbor?"

"Look at the second page," Kou Zhenshan said darkly.

Victor Leung turned to the stapled background sheet. His eyes narrowed. There was a basic employment entry at Hengxin Logistics, an address at Emerald Riverside Villa, and almost nothing else. Every deeper municipal registry check their paid informants had run came back empty, blocked by a blank seal with no issuing office listed.

"A man earning 12,000 yuan a month does not live at Emerald Riverside Villa," Viktor Orlov murmured, his pale eyes sharpening. "And he does not have a ghost file that shuts down our best mainland data brokers."

"He is married to Qin Yuhan, the president of Haiyue Group," Kou Zhenshan said. "Publicly, everyone in Pudong thinks he is just a quiet husband who clocks out at 4:55 PM and buys groceries. I do not buy it for a second. Look at the timeline. Two days after our Shanghai brokers threatened Haiyue's supply chain, Aegis Meridian crushed our bond positions. This clerk is either an off-books courier for Aegis Meridian or the hidden liaison carrying their private authorization codes."

Victor Leung tapped the edge of the photograph against the mahogany table. The rain drummed harder against the window glass.

"Our northern stronghold at Changbai Mountain holds the master ledgers and the offshore clearing servers," Victor Leung said evenly. "If whoever is pulling strings in Shanghai turns their attention north before we finish moving the accounts out of Hong Kong, we lose everything. Not 1.8 billion. Everything."

"So we pull back to Changbai?" Marcus Tan asked.

"We move the remaining liquidity north by Sunday night," Victor Leung replied. "And before that, we cut the thread in Shanghai. If this young man is the courier between Haiyue Group and Aegis Meridian, removing him will blind their local operations long enough for us to reset the board."

Kou Zhenshan's eyes glinted. "Do you want me to call our local enforcers in Pudong?"

"No," Victor Leung said coldly. "Local thugs talk when the police lean on them. I want outsiders who enter clean, finish fast, and vanish before dawn. Clear the room, Zhenshan. Call the Macau broker and tell him I have an 8 million yuan job for Kieran Cole at noon."

Alan Kwok and Marcus Tan exchanged an uneasy glance, gathered their tablets, and hurried out. Victor Leung remained by the window, staring down through the December rain at the gray harbor, holding the photo of the man with the green thermos in his hand.
