# Chapter 12: Three Options

An Zhiyuan, Chief of the Detail, sat in a quiet courtyard in the east of Qingyan City with a sheet of clean paper on his desk. On it he had written two lines in his neat, old-fashioned hand.

*Option One.*

*Option Two.*

He looked at them for a long time, and then he wrote a third.

The speaker crackled. "Chief, this is Twenty-three." It was Gong Yi, the noodle-shop owner. "I have the full count."

"Money first."

"Yunxi approved six hundred forty million yuan to move Old Street and the three blocks behind it, about 1,100 households and shops. At least one hundred ninety million never reached a single family. It went to three companies: Hongrui, Ankang Private Clinic, and a demolition firm owned by Cheng Biao's brother-in-law."

An Zhiyuan's pen stopped.

"The clinic that wrote the injury slip."

"The very one. The slip says the driver was treated at ten to two. Old Yu saw him throw himself into the paint shelf at 2:03, and the arrest came at five past."

"Treated thirteen minutes before he was hurt." An Zhiyuan let out one dry breath that was almost a laugh. "A very fast doctor."

"One more thing." Gong Yi's voice softened. "Last night Wei Kun took a cup of hot water to the old man in the cell. Cheng Biao slapped him for it, and hinted at his wife in the hospital. This morning Cheng sent a cup of poisoned tea to Wei Kun by Lei Bo's hand. Our records clerk spilled it, and the tube is saved."

"Who told Cheng what time Wei wrote?"

"Zhong Hai, Wei's desk neighbor. His boy has a bad heart, and Cheng offered a loan of sixty thousand. He has not eaten since."

"And Old Yu?"

"A van tried to run him down by the river arch at 4:20. Our bread truck stopped it, and the hired driver is talking to the police."

Gong Yi added, "Cheng Biao ran the Boss's name this afternoon. He found a boring clerk."

An Zhiyuan nodded. "His ordinary file is real and boring. Honest, simple, no debts. Cheng Biao already looked. So will others."

"And his secret file, Chief?"

"It lives where nobody below the top can open it. If anyone tries, the screen says one thing. Permission denied. Then the highest level asks them why they looked."

An Zhiyuan looked at his three options and added a smaller line under them: *Wei Kun's wife.*

Gong Yi spoke up unasked. "Apartment 302, a safe flat, is ready for her. Nurse Qiao will 'bump into' her at the pharmacy and find her a bed."

An Zhiyuan said, "Bai."

By the window, Bai Qingzhou, Number Eleven, the Detail's lawyer, stood and bowed. He held a thin black folder.

"Whenever you give the word, Chief."

"Not mine." An Zhiyuan picked up his phone. "His."

* * *

In Room 601, in Yunxi, Chen Hao spoke up from the sofa.

"Big brother, you've looked at that phone for ten minutes."

"I keep thinking of Second Uncle." Chen Xuan's eyes were red. "Is he cold in there? Is he scared?"

In the kitchen, Liu Meifang packed a steel thermos with pork-rib soup, crying a little into the ribs.

She told nobody in particular, "This is for your uncle. He's fifty-two. He can't eat cell food." She pointed the ladle at Chen Xuan. "And you, you boy, you're too thin. Eat."

The phone buzzed. "Work." Chen Xuan mumbled it and took the phone to the balcony.

An Zhiyuan's voice came low. "Boss. Three options."

"Go ahead, Chief."

"One. Our lawyer files a full complaint at the city Discipline Commission tonight. That is the Party's watchdog office, and it investigates officials. The complaint has the clinic slip, the honest notice, Old Yu's statement, the street footage and the money trail. Your uncle could be home by morning."

Chen Xuan held the railing with his free hand. "Home by morning." He said it very low.

"Two. We wait and hand it to the provincial team on Wednesday, when the inspection was due. Four more nights in that cell."

"No. Not four more nights."

"Three. I make one call tonight. A man in the provincial capital would break open Yunxi's whole police bureau by breakfast. It's the fastest. It's also the loudest. A dozen shopkeepers and one honest duty officer would be lost in the noise."

"I'd choose the first, Boss." An Zhiyuan paused. "One more thing. Two of Yan Tiehu's men got away tonight: his black-van driver and a man called Abacus. Abacus keeps Brother Tie's debt book."

"The first one. Through the front door, with a receipt."

"Yes, Boss."

"Chief An. I want a little of the third. Not the whole bureau. Just Yan Tiehu's crew." Chen Xuan said it the way he might order tea. "Find Abacus and his book, and the black-van driver, tonight."

"By what charge, Boss?"

"All of them. Beating. Wrecking shops. Taking money. Threatening a child. They hit an old man in the street, and then they went to a tailor's door with a pipe. That's twice. Charge the whole crew tonight, every man, by the law. Whoever held the pipe is charged with assault with a weapon and with threatening a child. Not ten days. Years. Tell him that in the van, before he speaks to anyone."

An Zhiyuan heard no shout in it. That was what made it hard to forget.

"It'll be done, Boss. And about today: the street calls the tea and the van luck. Cheng says Wei Kun has a guardian angel."

Chen Xuan put his free hand over his face.

"Hmm. Luck and an angel. Good. Let them think so. Thank Old Ding and the bread man for me."

"They will be told, Boss."

"And Officer Zhong Hai, Boss? The man who sold the hour."

Chen Xuan's hand came down.

"He sold a friend for a sixty-thousand-yuan loan, and a tired man almost drank poison for it. He will be charged on Monday."

"And the boy, Tiantian?"

"The boy sold nobody. Keep a bed on the fourth floor of People's Hospital. Tell the father nothing. If he walks to Wei Kun's window before Monday and says it himself, I will think again."

"Yes, Boss."

"And Chief An. Please. A hot meal and a blanket for my second uncle, through the family window, like any relative. My mother has soup. And nobody touches Officer Wei Kun. His wife's ward. His house loan. His desk. He passed a cup of water through the bars. He told the truth on a form when it was easier not to. Pay that back ten times over."

"And Old Yu, Boss?"

"Ning's road stays watched until she's grown. He stood in his door with a pipe in front of him. That's not small."

An Zhiyuan's throat tightened.

Three ways to bring down a county's leaders lay on his desk, and Chen Xuan had spent half his answer on soup and a blanket.

"The beef noodles will be hot, Boss." His voice broke a little. "With an extra egg."

"Thank you, Chief." Chen Xuan's voice broke too. "Really."

He hung up and found Chen Yuan in the doorway with his green thermos in her hands.

Chen Yuan held it out. "Here. It was cold. I heated it."

"Thanks, Yuan."

"Don't say anything, Brother. Just drink it."

She pushed it into his hand and walked away fast, wiping her nose on her sleeve.

Chen Hao leaned out of the kitchen. "Big brother, why are you smiling?"

Chen Xuan grinned. "I'm early for Mom's soup."

* * *

In the marble lobby of the Municipal Discipline Commission, a lone clerk named Qian Yu sat behind a long desk with instant noodles and a crossword. For two years of nights he had put complaints in a drawer to wait for Monday.

A man in a gray suit crossed the lobby like a person with all the time in the world. Qian Yu stood up so fast that noodles splashed onto his tie.

Bai Qingzhou spoke kindly. "Please sit. Finish your dinner. I wouldn't like to cause a stain."

"No, no, sir. How can I help?"

"A complaint."

He laid a thin black folder on the desk with both hands and pushed it forward with a little bow.

"Nineteen pages, in order. I'd like a signed receipt, a stamp and the time."

Qian Yu stopped chewing and read the names circled in red. Three companies. Then two officials.

Cheng Biao, who ran public order for the county police.

Lu Wenzhe, Executive Deputy Magistrate of Yunxi County.

His voice was very thin. "Sir, is this real?"

"I'm afraid it's quite real."

Bai Qingzhou smiled, and the smile made the young man's stomach turn over.

"I'd wake somebody up. If I were you."

Qian Yu looked at the clock: 11:44 p.m.

He picked up the phone.

Which door was he going to knock on at midnight?
