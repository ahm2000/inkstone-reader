# Chapter 12: Three Doors in the Night

At eleven o'clock, a cold drizzle kept the side lanes of Yunxi County empty. Across three parts of town, three doors opened without a sound.

The first door stood behind Golden Wheat Bakery, diagonally across Old Street from Mingde Hardware. Number 19 knelt in dark gloves beside the steel back door. Two thin metal picks turned the brass padlock with a soft click.

Inside the storage room, a camera recorder box blinked green beside the fuse box. Through the thin wooden ceiling, quiet voices drifted down from the apartment upstairs.

"Did you see them drag Old Chen Mingde into that police van?" a woman asked anxiously. "He has run that shop for twenty years. He would not shove a stray dog, let alone a grown man."

"Lower your voice," a man muttered back, his chair scraping the floor. "Everyone on Old Street knows that fall was fake. Still, nobody is crazy enough to hand camera video to Cheng Biao. Hongrui Real Estate's boys would smash our ovens before sunrise."

"Poor Wu Lanying," the woman whispered. "What can they do?"

"Nothing," the man said bitterly. "Sign the paper and pray they leave us alone."

The television clicked off, and footsteps faded toward the bedroom.

Number 19 used a small penlight to unplug the recorder's cable, paused the recording, and plugged in his pocket copying device. A clean, unedited video clip from two zero one rolled across the small screen.

The camera angle was clear. Mingde Hardware's sign sat in the top left corner. Chen Mingde stepped out carrying a heavy wooden crate of metal pipe joints with both hands. Two full meters separated him from Ma Er. Ma Er glanced up the lane, planted his left heel on the wet curb, and threw himself backward like a bad actor. Chen Mingde's face went pale as he set the crate down and hurried over to check on the stranger.

Number 19 pulled out the real hard drive, sealed it in a protective pouch with a proof tag, and slid an exact copy drive into the empty slot. Five minutes after walking in, he slipped back into the alley and snapped the padlock shut.

Two kilometers east, the second door waited at the end of a basement hallway under Yunxi People's Hospital.

Number 27 walked down the hall in a gray repair coat, wearing a faded worker badge and carrying a scuffed toolbox. Halfway down the corridor, the stairwell door swung open. Number 27 stepped calmly aside as a tired night worker walked past with a cigarette in one hand and a phone pressed to his ear.

"It made zero sense," the worker grumbled into his phone without even looking at Number 27. "Deputy Director Fang Zhihua marched into the scan room at half past two, pushed me off the computer, and locked a normal head scan behind his private password. That scan was cleaner than a baby's, not even a bump. When I asked if the files got mixed up, he looked ready to fire me on the spot."

The outside door clicked shut behind him.

Number 27 walked straight to the Hospital Scan Server Room, swiped a copied keycard across the reader, and stepped inside.

Kneeling beside the main storage rack, he plugged a read-only cable into the port and typed in Ma Er's patient number. Twelve seconds later, the original hospital X-ray file popped up on his small screen.

The head scan taken at two thirty-one showed zero bleeding, zero broken bone, zero swelling, and zero neck injury. Three lines below sat the locked computer record: at two thirty-four, Deputy Director Fang Zhihua used his own login to block the normal doctor's check, pasted in a pre-written injury report from a flash drive, and hid the folder so nurses could not open it.

Number 27 saved the original head scan, the computer record, and the official server clock proof onto two locked drives. He checked the time stamps down to the second, wiped the port edge clean, and slipped out thirty seconds before the elevator chimed upstairs.

The third door was not locked at all. It was a greasy plastic curtain hanging over a basement mahjong parlor behind the old grain depot in the south district.

Inside, plastic tiles clacked across four cramped tables. At the corner table, Ma Er sat hunched over crisp cash with a cigarette between his fingers and a warm beer at his elbow. He wore no bandage and no neck brace.

"Hold on, Ma Er," a skinny player across from him said, squinting at the neat bills. "Last week you owed the noodle stall forty yuan. Where did you get fresh bank-wrapped bills?"

Ma Er barked a greasy laugh and blew smoke toward the bare light bulb. "Some men work with their backs. Smart men use their heads. One tumble on Old Street paid better than three months of hauling cement."

"That hardware shop?" a second player asked, leaning in. "Old Chen Mingde? Word is you told the police he cracked your skull."

"Cracked my skull!" Ma Er snorted, slapping his own forehead with a wide grin. "Do I look like my head is hurt? That old fool never came within two steps of me. Still, the second I hit the curb and yelled, Yan Tiehu's car was waiting, and the hospital doctor already had my injury paper warm on his desk."

The third player whistled softly, eyeing Ma Er's jacket with uneasy envy. "Five thousand for one fall? You are not worried the police will check?"

"The police?" Ma Er scoffed, patting a thick brown envelope inside his fake-leather jacket. "Station Director Cheng Biao locked him up before my tea even got cold. Who in Yunxi County argues with Hongrui Real Estate?"

Two tables away, sitting alone in a dark cap over a plate of untouched peanuts, Number 42 kept his eyes down and listened without moving a muscle.

Twenty minutes later, Ma Er shoved his stool back and wandered down the narrow hall toward the restroom.

Beside the sink, Number 42 brushed past Ma Er's right shoulder as the drunk man fumbled with his belt. The touch lasted less than half a second.

"Watch where you are going, blind fool," Ma Er slurred, glaring before kicking the stall door shut.

Number 42 said nothing. Standing in the dim entrance, he opened the lifted brown envelope with gloved fingers. Inside sat fifty crisp hundred-yuan bills held by a Yunxi Rural Commercial Bank paper strap stamped ten thirty that morning. He took clear photos of the bill numbers and the bank stamp, then tucked the cash back inside.

In his other hand sat Ma Er's cheap phone, unlocked. Number 42 opened the built-in call recorder and played the forty-eight-second recording from one fifty-one through a single earbud.

Yan Tiehu's rough voice came through sharp and clear. He told Ma Er to fall cleanly outside Mingde Hardware, climb into the white car, and collect five thousand yuan as soon as Deputy Director Fang Zhihua stamped the hospital sheet.

Number 42 copied the original voice file, the phone call details, and the phone's ID number onto his pocket drive in nine seconds.

When the stall latch rattled and Ma Er stumbled out wiping his hands on his pants, Number 42 stepped smoothly past him toward the stairs. A second light brush slipped both the envelope and the phone back into Ma Er's pockets without the man feeling a thing.

Minutes later, Ma Er swaggered back out into the drizzle, humming off-key toward his rented room near the freight yard. Fifty paces behind him, Number 42 melted into the dark shadows and tapped his earpiece twice to report that all three doors were closed and clean.
