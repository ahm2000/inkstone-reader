# Chapter 12: Three Doors in the Night

At eleven o'clock, a cold drizzle kept Yunxi County's side lanes empty. Across three districts of the county seat, three doors opened without a sound.

The first door stood behind Golden Wheat Bakery, diagonally across Old Street from Mingde Hardware. Number Nineteen knelt in matte gloves beside the steel service door. Two slender tension tools turned the brass padlock with a soft click.

Inside the storage room, a digital video recorder blinked green beside the breaker box. Through the thin ceiling floorboards, muffled voices drifted down from the apartment above.

"Did you see them drag Old Chen Mingde into that police van?" a woman asked anxiously. "He has run that shop for twenty years. He would not shove a stray dog, let alone a grown man."

"Lower your voice," a man muttered back, his chair scraping. "Everyone on Old Street knows that fall was staged. Still, nobody is crazy enough to hand camera footage to Cheng Biao. Hongrui Real Estate's boys would smash our ovens before sunrise."

"Poor Wu Lanying," the woman whispered. "What can they do?"

"Nothing," the man said bitterly. "Sign the paper and pray they leave us alone."

The television clicked off, and footsteps faded toward the bedroom.

Number Nineteen unplugged the recorder's network cable by penlight, paused the write cycle, and linked his handheld forensic duplicator. An unedited clip from two zero one scrolled cleanly across the miniature screen.

The framing was exact. Mingde Hardware's sign sat in the upper left corner. Chen Mingde stepped out carrying a heavy crate of pipe elbows with both hands. Two full meters separated him from Ma Er, who glanced up the lane, planted his left heel on the wet curb, and threw himself backward with theatrical force. Chen Mingde's face went pale as he set the crate down and hurried over to check on the stranger.

Number Nineteen pulled the original two-terabyte drive, sealed it in an anti-static pouch with a cryptographic hash tag, and slotted an identical pre-cloned drive into the bay. Five minutes after entering, he slipped back into the alley and snapped the padlock shut.

Two kilometers east, the second door waited at the end of a basement corridor beneath Yunxi People's Hospital's radiology wing.

Number Twenty-seven approached in a gray utility coat with a faded maintenance badge and a scuffed toolbox. Halfway down the hall, the stairwell door opened. Number Twenty-seven stepped calmly aside as a tired night technician walked past with a cigarette in one hand and a phone pressed to his ear.

"It made zero sense," the technician grumbled into the handset without glancing at Number Twenty-seven. "Deputy Director Fang Zhihua marched into the booth at half past two, pushed me off the terminal, and locked a routine cranial CT behind his private override. The scan was cleaner than a newborn's, not even a bruise. When I asked if the files got mixed up, he looked ready to fire me on the spot."

The courtyard door clicked shut behind him.

Number Twenty-seven walked straight to the Imaging Archive Server Room, swiped a cloned magnetic card across the reader, and stepped inside.

Kneeling beside the primary DICOM array, he plugged a hardware write-blocker into the maintenance port and queried Ma Er's intake number. Twelve seconds later, the raw imaging packet appeared.

The cranial CT recorded at two thirty-one showed zero hematoma, zero fracture, zero swelling, and zero cervical displacement. Three lines below sat the immutable server audit log: at two thirty-four, Deputy Director Fang Zhihua's credentials had overridden the radiologist sign-off flag, attached a boilerplate text file from an external flash drive, and sealed the folder from nurse-station viewing.

Number Twenty-seven extracted the raw DICOM package, audit journal, and clock synchronization certificate onto dual encrypted drives, verifying the digital signatures to the millisecond. He wiped the port edge clean and exited thirty seconds before the elevator chimed upstairs.

The third door was not locked at all. It was a grease-stained vinyl curtain over a basement mahjong parlor behind the old grain depot in Yunxi's south district.

Inside, plastic tiles clacked over four cramped tables. At the corner table, Ma Er sat hunched over crumpled bills with a cigarette between his fingers and a warm beer at his elbow. He wore no bandage and no neck brace.

"Hold on, Ma Er," a skinny player across from him said, squinting at the crisp cash. "Last week you owed the noodle stall forty yuan. Where did you get bank-strapped bills?"

Ma Er barked a greasy laugh, puffing smoke toward the bulb. "Some men work with their backs. Smart men use their heads. One tumble on Old Street paid better than three months of hauling cement."

"That hardware shop?" a second player asked, leaning in. "Old Chen Mingde? Word is you told the police he cracked your skull."

"Cracked my skull!" Ma Er snorted, slapping his own forehead with a wide grin. "Do I look concussed? That old fool never came within two steps of me. Still, the second I hit the curb and yelled, Yan Tiehu's hatchback was waiting, and the hospital doctor already had my injury sheet warm off the desk."

The third player whistled softly, eyeing Ma Er's jacket with uneasy envy. "Five thousand for one fall? You are not worried the police will check?"

"The police?" Ma Er scoffed, patting a thick kraft envelope inside his imitation-leather jacket. "Station Director Cheng Biao locked him up before my tea cooled. Who in Yunxi County argues with Hongrui Real Estate?"

Two tables away, sitting alone in a dark peaked cap over untouched peanuts, Number Forty-two kept his eyes down and listened without stirring.

Twenty minutes later, Ma Er shoved his stool back and wandered down the narrow hallway toward the washroom.

Beside the sink, Number Forty-two brushed past Ma Er's right shoulder as the drunk man fumbled with his belt. The contact lasted less than half a second.

"Watch where you are going, blind fool," Ma Er slurred, glaring before kicking the stall door shut.

Number Forty-two said nothing. In the dim vestibule, he opened the lifted kraft envelope with gloved fingers: fifty crisp hundred-yuan notes bound in a Yunxi Rural Commercial Bank strap stamped ten thirty that morning. He photographed the serial bands and teller stamp cleanly, then tucked the bills back.

In his other hand sat Ma Er's cheap smartphone, unlocked. Number Forty-two opened the built-in call recorder and played the forty-eight-second file from one fifty-one through a single earbud.

Yan Tiehu's gravelly voice came through sharp and clear, telling Ma Er to fall cleanly outside Mingde Hardware, climb into the white hatchback, and collect five thousand yuan once Deputy Director Fang Zhihua stamped the hospital sheet.

Number Forty-two mirrored the raw audio, carrier metadata, and device ID onto his pocket drive in nine seconds.

When the stall latch rattled and Ma Er stumbled out wiping his hands on his trousers, Number Forty-two stepped smoothly past him toward the stairs. A second feather-light brush returned both envelope and phone to Ma Er's pockets without the man blinking.

Minutes later, Ma Er swaggered back into the drizzle, humming off-key toward his rented room near the freight yard. Fifty paces behind him, Number Forty-two melted into the dark eaves and tapped his earpiece twice to report all three doors closed and clean.
