# Chapter 12: Three Options

Qingyan City, the quiet eastern courtyard. Saturday, 8:40 p.m.

"Chief, this is Twenty-three. Everything is checked."

Chief An Zhiyuan sat upright beneath his brass desk lamp. Beside the encrypted speaker lay a clean sheet of white paper bearing two lines in his neat handwriting: Option One. Option Two.

"Go ahead, Gong Yi."

"First, the Old Street relocation fund," Gong Yi reported crisply, the faint clink of a tea mug sounding in the background of his noodle shop. "Hongrui Real Estate offered shopkeepers 3,800 yuan per square meter when real Old Street shops sell for 8,000. To cover the gap, Yunxi County approved a 640-million-yuan relocation fund. I traced the public payments and open business records. At least 190 million never went to residents. It flowed into three companies: Hongrui Real Estate, a demolition company registered to Deputy Police Director Cheng Biao's brother-in-law, and Ankang Private Clinic."

Chief An's pen paused mid-stroke. "The clinic that wrote the injury note?"

"The very same, Chief. Attorney Bai's legal assistant pulled the clinic's patient billing receipt through a normal legal records request: it shows Boss Dong's driver was treated at 1:50 p.m., fifteen minutes before the 2:05 arrest, and no official police doctor ever checked him. Meanwhile, my noodle shop's front street camera across from Mingde Hardware caught the whole stunt: the driver threw himself into the paint shelves while the Boss's uncle never moved from behind his counter."

"Clean proof," Chief An murmured approvingly. "Continue."

"Second, property records show eleven Old Street shopkeepers signed over their shops to Hongrui over the past nine weeks, every single one the morning after an assault arrest signed by Cheng Biao. I compared the Boss's photo against copies kept by two of those former shopkeepers. Their earlier notices were all neatly backdated to hide illegal overnight lockups. Only the Boss's uncle's notice carries a truthful 7:50 a.m. time, written by duty officer Wei Kun. Even better, two off-duty auxiliary cops stopped by my noodle shop for lunch today and grumbled that Wei Kun actually sneaked hot water through the cell bars to the old hardware man last night."

"Hot water," Chief An repeated softly, a flicker of genuine warmth crossing his stern face.

"Third, the political clock," Gong Yi finished dryly. "County Party Secretary Fan Weiguo signed the buyout approvals, though Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu Wenzhe controls the project money. County Magistrate Tao Shu skipped their dinner last night with a fake cold. And the Provincial Inspection Team arrives in Yunxi next Wednesday. Every piece came cleanly from public records, neighbor copies, legal requests, and street video. Nobody knows we're here."

"Well done, Gong Yi. Keep your apron on and wait for my word."

Chief An clicked the line off and gazed down at his handwritten sheet.

With forty years behind him, Chief An knew three different ways to end this mess before sunrise. One quiet phone call to a top provincial leader, and Yunxi's county police bureau would be flipped inside out before breakfast! Yet that would be loud, sloppy, and heavy-handed. Worse, turning a local land grab into a political earthquake would crush ordinary shopkeepers and bury one honest beat cop in the rubble.

Above all, Chief An knew his Boss. Chen Xuan didn't want vengeance. He wanted his uncle treated fairly, strictly under the law.

Picking up his phone, Chief An typed a crisp, clear briefing instead of a bloated essay, laying out the verified facts first, followed by three clean paths.

Yunxi County, Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. 9:20 p.m.

"Brother, if you stare at your phone any longer, it's going to turn into a brick," Chen Hao teased from the far end of the sofa, thumbs flying across a mobile game.

"Just reading a work note," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

In the kitchen, his mother was scrubbing the same soup bowl for the third time to keep her hands busy. Chen Xuan scrolled calmly through Chief An's short messages.

Message one laid out the hard proof: the fake 1:50 p.m. clinic slip, the eleven forced buyouts, the 190 million yuan quietly taken from the relocation fund, the street camera video showing the staged fall, and the Provincial Inspection Team arriving Wednesday.

Message two laid out the choices:
*Option 1: Our legal team delivers a formal, proof-backed complaint straight to Qingyan's city anti-corruption office tonight (the Municipal Discipline Inspection Commission), with copies to the city prosecutor and city police chief. With provincial inspectors arriving Wednesday, they'll be forced to act within hours. Uncle should be home by dawn.*
*Option 2: Wait until Wednesday and hand everything to the provincial team directly, leaving Uncle inside three more days.*
*Option 3: Have a senior leader step in directly tonight. Fastest, but loud and outside normal rules.*
*We recommend Option 1. Note: Duty officer Wei Kun wrote the true 7:50 a.m. time and brought Uncle hot water last night.*

Chen Xuan didn't hesitate for five seconds. He saw the pressure point right away!

With provincial anti-corruption inspectors arriving in four days, Qingyan's city leaders couldn't dare hide clear, proof-backed wrongdoing, especially when Old Yu's signed eyewitness statement matched the street video and the clinic's impossible 1:50 p.m. time! Clean paperwork aimed right at the inspection deadline would break the whole cover-up open without bending a single rule!

And as for duty officer Wei Kun, a quiet cop with nothing to gain had chosen to write one honest time and hand an old man a cup of hot water when nobody was watching. A small, genuine warmth rose in Chen Xuan's chest. Wherever you went in this world, there was always someone quietly doing the right thing, wasn't there?

He tapped back two short messages with his thumb:
*Option 1. Add Old Yu's eyewitness statement too. I'll send a photo of it now. Everything strictly by the rules, no shortcuts, nobody hurt.*
*Also, please send Second Uncle a hot meal and a blanket tonight through the regular station window. He's fifty-two and concrete gets cold. And make sure nobody takes revenge on Officer Wei Kun. He did the right thing.*

He snapped a clean photo of Old Yu's handwritten statement, sent it off, and set his phone down on the coffee table.

"Whoa, Brother, why are you smiling like that?" Chen Hao squinted suspiciously over his phone screen.

"Am I smiling?"

"You look like you just won the lottery!"

"Must be Mom's pork-rib soup."

Back in the quiet Qingyan courtyard, Chief An read both messages once, then let out a slow breath.

Three options on the table, any one of which could shatter a county's leadership before morning, and what did the Boss spend half his message worrying about? A hot bowl of noodles, a blanket for his uncle, and protecting one honest beat cop!

Chief An's throat felt oddly tight, and a rare, gentle smile softened his lined face.

"Gong Yi," he spoke into the speaker, "cook a big bowl of beef noodles with an extra fried egg, pack a thick clean blanket, and drop them off at the station's family delivery window yourself, still wearing your apron. Strictly by station rules. And pass the word: nobody touches Officer Wei Kun. If anyone tries to blame him, they answer to me."

"Already boiling the broth, Chief," Gong Yi replied smoothly.

"Attorney Bai, please step in."

The study door opened smoothly. Attorney Bai Qingzhou, Number Eleven on the roster, stepped inside wearing a neat dark gray suit and carrying a slim black leather folder. A famous sixty-year-old courtroom lawyer whose name alone made city legal departments sit up straight, Bai Qingzhou looked as pleased as a man walking into his favorite concert hall.

"Everything is checked and ready, Chief," Bai Qingzhou said crisply, tapping the folder. "Nineteen pages, organized cleanly: the 1:50 p.m. private clinic slip, the honest 7:50 a.m. holding notice, the neighbor's eyewitness statement, the street video showing the staged fall, and the 190-million-yuan fund trail. I'll file it myself at the city Discipline Commission's night complaint desk."

"Through the front door, Bai," Chief An reminded him. "Signed receipt, full legal rules, nothing irregular."

Bai Qingzhou smiled, his eyes gleaming behind silver-rimmed glasses. "Naturally, Chief. When the law itself is sharp enough to cut iron, why would anyone bother swinging a club?"
