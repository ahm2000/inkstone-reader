# Chapter 12: Three Doors in the Night

At eleven o'clock, a cold drizzle kept Yunxi County's side lanes empty. Across three parts of town, three doors opened without a sound.

The first door stood behind Golden Wheat Bakery, diagonally across Old Street from Mingde Hardware. Number 19 knelt in dark gloves beside the steel back door. Two thin picks turned the brass padlock with a soft click.

Inside the storage room, a camera recorder blinked green beside the fuse box. Through the thin ceiling, quiet voices drifted down from upstairs.

"Did you see them drag Old Chen Mingde into that police van?" a woman asked anxiously. "Twenty years in that shop. He would not shove a stray dog, let alone a grown man."

"Lower your voice," a man muttered, chair scraping. "Everyone on Old Street knows that fall was fake. Still, nobody is crazy enough to hand video to Cheng Biao. Hongrui Real Estate's boys would smash our ovens before sunrise."

"Poor Wu Lanying," the woman whispered. "What can they do?"

"Nothing," the man said bitterly. "Sign the paper and pray they leave us alone."

The television clicked off, and footsteps faded toward the bedroom.

Number 19 used a penlight to unplug the recorder, paused it, and connected his pocket copier. A clean clip from two zero one rolled across the small screen.

The angle was clear. Mingde Hardware's sign sat top left. Chen Mingde stepped out carrying a heavy crate of pipe joints with both hands, two meters from Ma Er. Ma Er glanced up the lane, planted his heel on the wet curb, and threw himself backward like a bad actor. Chen Mingde paled, set the crate down, and hurried to check on the stranger.

Number 19 pulled the real hard drive, sealed it in a pouch with a proof tag, and slid an exact copy drive into the slot. Five minutes after entering, he slipped back into the alley and snapped the padlock shut.

Two kilometers east, the second door waited at the end of a basement hallway under Yunxi People's Hospital.

Number 27 walked down the hall in a gray repair coat, faded badge, and scuffed toolbox. Halfway down, the stairwell door opened. Number 27 stepped aside as a tired night worker walked past with a cigarette and a phone to his ear.

"It made zero sense," the worker grumbled without glancing at Number 27. "Deputy Director Fang Zhihua marched into the scan room at half past two, pushed me off the computer, and locked a normal head scan behind his password. Cleaner than a baby's, not even a bump. When I asked if files got mixed up, he looked ready to fire me."

The outside door clicked shut behind him.

Number 27 walked straight to the Hospital Scan Server Room, swiped a copied keycard, and stepped in.

Kneeling beside the main rack, he plugged a read-only cable into the port and typed Ma Er's patient number. Twelve seconds later, the original X-ray file appeared.

The head scan taken at two thirty-one showed zero bleeding, zero broken bone, zero swelling, and zero neck injury. Three lines below sat the locked log: at two thirty-four, Fang Zhihua used his login to block the normal check, pasted a pre-written injury report from a flash drive, and hid the folder from nurses.

Number 27 saved the original scan, log, and official server clock proof onto two locked drives, checked the time stamps to the second, wiped the port clean, and slipped out thirty seconds before the elevator chimed upstairs.

The third door was not locked at all, just a greasy plastic curtain over a basement mahjong parlor behind the old grain depot in the south district.

Inside, plastic tiles clacked across four cramped tables. At the corner table, Ma Er sat hunched over crisp cash with a cigarette and warm beer, wearing no bandage and no neck brace.

"Hold on, Ma Er," a skinny player said, squinting at the bills. "Last week you owed the noodle stall forty yuan. Where did you get fresh bank-wrapped bills?"

Ma Er barked a greasy laugh, blowing smoke toward the bulb. "Some men work with their backs. Smart men use their heads. One tumble on Old Street paid better than three months hauling cement."

"That hardware shop?" a second player leaned in. "Old Chen Mingde? Word is you told police he cracked your skull."

"Cracked my skull!" Ma Er snorted, slapping his forehead with a wide grin. "Do I look hurt? That old fool never came within two steps of me. Still, the second I hit the curb and yelled, Yan Tiehu's car was waiting, and the doctor already had my injury paper warm on his desk."

The third player whistled softly with uneasy envy. "Five thousand for one fall? Not worried police will check?"

"The police?" Ma Er scoffed, patting a brown envelope inside his fake-leather jacket. "Station Director Cheng Biao locked him up before my tea got cold. Who in Yunxi County argues with Hongrui Real Estate?"

Two tables away, alone in a dark cap over untouched peanuts, Number 42 kept his eyes down and listened without moving.

Twenty minutes later, Ma Er shoved his stool back and wandered down the narrow hall to the restroom.

Beside the sink, Number 42 brushed past Ma Er's right shoulder as the drunk man fumbled with his belt. The touch lasted half a second.

"Watch where you are going, blind fool," Ma Er slurred, glaring before kicking the stall door shut.

Number 42 said nothing. Standing in the dim entrance, he opened the lifted brown envelope with gloved fingers. Inside sat fifty crisp hundred-yuan bills bound by a Yunxi Rural Commercial Bank strap stamped ten thirty that morning. He photographed the bill numbers and bank stamp, then tucked the cash back.

In his other hand sat Ma Er's cheap phone, unlocked. Number 42 opened the built-in call recorder and played the forty-eight-second recording from one fifty-one through one earbud.

Yan Tiehu's rough voice came through clear, telling Ma Er to fall cleanly outside Mingde Hardware, climb into the white car, and collect five thousand yuan once Fang Zhihua stamped the hospital sheet.

Number 42 copied the original voice file, call details, and phone ID onto his pocket drive in nine seconds.

When the stall rattled and Ma Er stumbled out wiping his hands, Number 42 stepped smoothly past toward the stairs. A second light brush slipped envelope and phone back into Ma Er's pockets unnoticed.

Minutes later, Ma Er swaggered into the drizzle, humming off-key toward his rented room near the freight yard. Fifty paces behind, Number 42 melted into the shadows and tapped his earpiece twice to report all three doors closed and clean.
