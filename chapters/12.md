# Chapter 12: Three Options

In the quiet courtyard in the east of Qingyan City, Chief An Zhiyuan had a sheet of clean paper on his desk and two lines written on it in his neat, old-fashioned hand.

*Option One.*

*Option Two.*

He looked at them for a long time, and then he wrote a third.

"Chief, this is Twenty-three," said the speaker. "I have the full count."

"Money first."

"Yunxi approved six hundred forty million yuan to move Old Street. At least one hundred ninety million never reached a single family. It went to three companies: Hongrui, a demolition firm owned by Cheng Biao's brother-in-law, and Ankang Private Clinic."

Chief An's pen stopped.

"The clinic that wrote the injury slip."

"The very one. The slip says the driver was treated at ten to two. Old Yu saw him throw himself into the paint shelf at 2:03, and the arrest came at five past."

"Treated thirteen minutes before he was hurt." Chief An let out one dry breath that was almost a laugh. "A very fast doctor."

"The best in the county, Chief. It heals people in advance."

"One more thing." Gong Yi's voice softened. "Last night Wei Kun carried a cup of hot water to the old man in the cell, and Cheng Biao slapped him for it, and hinted at his wife's maternity ward. This morning Cheng sent a cup of poisoned tea to Wei Kun by Lei Bo's hand. Our records clerk spilled it, and the tube is saved."

"Who told Cheng what time Wei wrote?"

"Zhong Hai, Wei's desk neighbor. His boy has a bad heart, and Cheng offered a loan of sixty thousand. He has not eaten since."

"And Old Yu?"

"A van tried to run him down by the river arch at 4:20. Our bread truck stopped it, and the hired driver is singing in a cell."

"Also, Cheng Biao ran the Boss's name this afternoon," said Twenty-three. "He found a boring clerk."

"His ordinary file is real and boring," said Chief An. "Honest, simple, no debts. Cheng Biao already looked. So will others."

"And the other one?"

"The other one lives where nobody below the top can open it. If anyone tries, the screen says one thing. Permission denied. Then the highest level asks them why."

Chief An looked at his three options and added a smaller line under them: *Wei Kun's wife.*

"Apartment 302 is made up," said Twenty-three, unasked. "Nurse Qiao will 'bump into' her at the pharmacy."

Chief An nodded. What he was about to order was already in place.

"Bai," he said.

By the window, a neat man in silver-rimmed glasses stood up and bowed a little.

Bai Qingzhou, Number Eleven, the Detail's lawyer, held a thin black folder.

"Whenever you give the word, Chief."

"Not mine." Chief An picked up his phone. "His."

* * *

In Room 601, in Yunxi, Chen Hao spoke up from the sofa.

"Brother, you've stared at that phone for ten minutes."

"Mm," said Chen Xuan.

In the kitchen, Liu Meifang was packing a steel thermos with pork-rib soup, and she was crying a little into the ribs.

"For your uncle," she told nobody. "He is fifty-two. He cannot eat cell food."

The phone buzzed, and Chen Xuan took it to the balcony.

"Boss. Three options," said Chief An.

"Go ahead, Chief."

"One. Our lawyer files a full complaint at the city Discipline Commission tonight, with the clinic slip, the honest notice, Old Yu's statement, the street footage and the money trail. Your uncle could be home by morning."

"Mm."

"Two. We wait, and hand it to the provincial team on Wednesday. Three more nights in that cell."

"Hmm."

"Three. I make one call tonight. A man in the provincial capital would open Yunxi's police bureau like a tin can by breakfast. It's the fastest. It's also the loudest. A dozen shopkeepers and one honest duty officer would be buried in the noise."

"I'd choose the first, Boss," said Chief An. "One more thing. Two of Hu's men got away tonight: his black-van driver, and a man called Abacus. Abacus keeps Brother Hu's debt book."

"The first one," said Chen Xuan. "Through the front door, with a receipt."

"Yes, Boss."

"Chief An. I want a little of the third."

A pause.

"A little of the third?"

"Brother Hu's crew." Chen Xuan's voice stayed as flat as a man ordering tea. "Find Abacus and his book, and the black-van driver, tonight."

"By what charge, Boss?"

"By all of them. Beating, wrecking shops, taking money, threatening a child." "They hit an old man in the street, and then they went to a tailor's door with a pipe. That's twice. A man who hurts the weak once may learn. A man who does it twice has chosen. Finish the crew tonight, every one. By the law, not outside it. And wherever they are hiding, tell them one thing from me. Whoever held the pipe, I know which hand."

Chief An had guarded this young man for years and never heard that voice. Flat, and complete.

"It will be done, Boss," he said quietly. "About today: the street calls the tea and the van luck. The station says a city bureau protects them. Cheng says Wei Kun has a guardian angel."

Chen Xuan put his free hand over his face.

"Hmm. Luck, a bureau, an angel. Old Ding and the bread man did well. Is the bread good?"

"The best in the county, Boss."

"Oh, nice."

"And Officer Zhong Hai, Boss? The man who sold the hour."

Chen Xuan's hand came down, and his voice went flat.

"He sold a friend for sixty thousand yuan, and a tired man almost drank poison for it. The law reads his name on Monday."

"And the boy, Tiantian?"

"The boy sold nobody. Keep a bed on the fourth floor of People's Hospital. Tell the father nothing. If he walks to Wei Kun's window before Monday and says it himself, we will see."

"Yes, Boss."

"And Chief An."

"Yes?"

"A hot meal and a blanket for my second uncle. Through the family window, like any relative. My mother has soup. And nobody touches Officer Wei Kun. Whatever he needs, his wife's ward, his mortgage, his desk, he gets. He passed a cup of water through the bars, and he told the truth on a form when it was easier not to. Pay that back ten times over."

"And Old Yu, Boss?"

"Ning's road stays watched until she's grown. He stood in his door with a pipe in front of him. That's not small."

The old man's throat tightened.

Three ways to bring down a county's leaders lay on his desk, and the Boss had spent half his answer on soup and a blanket.

"The beef noodles will be hot, Boss," said Chief An, and his voice broke a little. "With an extra egg."

"Mm. Thanks, Chief."

He hung up and found Chen Yuan standing in the doorway with his green thermos in her hands.

"Here," she said. "It was cold. I heated it."

"Thanks, Yuan."

"Don't say anything. Just drink it."

She pushed it into his hand and walked away fast, wiping her nose on her sleeve.

Chen Hao leaned out of the kitchen.

"Brother, why are you smiling like you won the lottery?"

"Is it Mom's soup?"

"It's not even served yet."

"Then I'm early," said Chen Xuan.

* * *

In the marble lobby of the Municipal Discipline Commission, a lone clerk named Qian Yu sat behind a long desk with instant noodles and a crossword. In two years of night duty, he had put three hundred complaints in a drawer to wait for Monday.

A man in a gray suit and silver-rimmed glasses crossed the lobby like a person with all the time in the world, and Qian Yu stood up so fast that noodles slapped onto his tie.

"Please sit," said Bai Qingzhou kindly. "Finish your dinner. I wouldn't like to cause a stain."

"No, no, sir. How can I help?"

"I'd like to file a complaint."

He laid a thin black folder on the desk with both hands and pushed it forward with a little bow.

"Nineteen pages, in order. I'd like a signed receipt, a stamp and the time."

Page one was a clinic slip, and page three a map of payments with three company names circled in red.

He stopped chewing and read the names in the margin.

Cheng Biao, who ran public order for the county police.

Lu Wenzhe, Executive Deputy Magistrate of Yunxi County.

"Sir," he said, and his voice was very thin. "Is this. Is this real?"

"I'm afraid it's quite real."

Bai Qingzhou smiled, and the smile made the young man's stomach turn over.

"I'd wake somebody up," he said. "If I were you."

Qian Yu looked at the clock: 11:44 p.m.

He picked up the phone.

Which door was he going to knock on at midnight?
