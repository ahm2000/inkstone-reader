# Chapter 12: Beyond Number One

General Qin Zhengguo looked at the ten digits on his notepad for two seconds. Then he picked up the red receiver and dialed.

One ring. Two. A flat voice with no feeling in it answered without a greeting.

"State your clearance and purpose. This line does not exist."

"General Qin Zhengguo, Supreme Military Command." He kept his voice hard. "A founding elder gave me this Black Pine relay. An Absolute Order has locked down Lanshui, and mute masked men are standing on my deck. I know what Black Pine is, and this doesn't look like your usual protocol. Put me through to your top desk right now."

"Hold. Voiceprint check. Do not speak."

Ten seconds of static. Across the deck, the ten masks watched him and did not move. The chief of staff stood five steps back, gripping a clipboard in both hands.

A young officer hurried over with a note. A wing commander at the edge of the air shell over Lanshui had seen black jets with no markings ahead of him. He asked for permission to engage.

The general wrote one word on the note and handed it back. *Hold.*

The line clicked twice. A sharper voice came on.

"Who gave you an unlisted Black Pine relay?" The man was a senior commander in the Black Pine Guard. "If your voiceprint had failed, this line would have burned out your console. Say what you want, and be quick."

Nobody had spoken to a four-star general like that in twenty years. Qin Zhengguo swallowed his pride.

"Give me a straight answer! Did the Black Pine numbers lock down Yunxi and walk into the capital compound, or is there another force sitting above you?!"

The man gave a short, bitter laugh. "Us? General, do we look like fools? We did not do this. We were ordered out of the ten-kilometer zone ourselves. Our armed teams were pulled back to the line. Those living among civilians were told to watch and touch nothing. Our own consoles are locked, and the masks allowed this one relay to open for your call. The force in Yunxi is not Black Pine. It is above us, and we have no clearance even to see it."

The general felt the floor shift under him. He had spent his life at the top of the chain. Now he had found a chain that ran over his head.

"Above you? Then who commands them?"

"Nobody on my roster. Stay on the line. I am sending your call to the only desk that knows what lit up tonight. If he refuses you, you are on your own."

Three more clicks passed through three more relays. Then the static was gone, and the silence on the line felt older and heavier.

* * *

In a quiet courtyard in Qingyan City, Number One sat at his desk beside the cracked saucer. The black directive card still lay where the Zero Guard had put it. The great wall map behind him was dead. Five masked figures stood along the walls of his study. They had allowed this one call, and they heard every word.

He knew what had turned the whole country red. His own Black Pine perimeter had failed twice in one evening, letting a county thug strike the Boss's father and walk right up to his mother's doorstep. The Boss had bypassed Black Pine completely to wake the Zero Guard, audit the failure, and root out every layer behind that thug. Number One knew Chen Xuan did not want a war.

But a general with loaded missiles could not read Chen Xuan's mind. One nervous officer might fire. One foreign power might misread the lockdown. A slap could grow into a war.

So Number One answered.

"Number One," he said calmly.

General Qin Zhengguo drew a breath. "Number One, this is General Qin Zhengguo. Ten masked operatives stand on my deck. Every senior leader in the capital, the provinces and the cities has the same silent watch around him. Every theater is at full readiness. Fighter wings hold a ring over Lanshui. Foreign watch rooms are already reacting." He gripped the receiver until his palm hurt. "Your man says Black Pine did not launch this. Tell me the truth. What happened in Yunxi, who are these people, and is this country going to war?"

"Take a breath, General. This country is not going to war." Number One spoke slowly. "Hold your theaters where they are. Keep your air wings at the outer edge. Let no army or police unit cross into Yunxi."

"Then why are they on my deck? Why is every missile battery and jammer hot over a small county town? Can you tell them to stand down before foreign powers misread it?"

Number One let out a long breath. He sounded tired.

"No, General. Listen. The people on your deck are the Zero Guard. I hold no key to them. Black Pine has been taken off duty. We stand under the same watch as you. Five of them are in my study now. While they are awake, I cannot move one man or give one order."

"Even the Black Pine Guard was dismissed?!" The general's palm turned ice-cold on the receiver. He had spent forty years believing the Black Pine numbers were the absolute ceiling of covert power in this nation, yet now their own head sat under five mute masks.

"I do not know how many they are. I do not know what weapons they have armed in that zone. I do not know how long they will watch. When Zero wakes, the whole country must simply stay ready until it is over."

"My officers are asking me why they must obey ten silent men," the general said. "What do I tell them?"

"Tell them the order sits above you, and above me. A soldier understands that."

"And the world outside? Washington and Moscow will want an answer by morning. What do I tell my foreign ministry?"

"Tell them it is an internal security exercise. Tell them nothing more. They will not like it, but they will not shoot at a county town." Number One paused. "The safest thing your missiles can do tonight is sit still."

"What could make him wake them?" the general whispered. His throat was dry. "What is in Yunxi?"

"Some blind, foolish people in a county town crossed a line." Number One's voice went hard as winter iron. "I hope it ends quickly and quietly. Because while the Zero Guard is awake, nobody on earth can tell them to stand down except him."

The line fell quiet.

General Qin Zhengguo set the receiver on its cradle. Behind him, the ten masks had not moved a finger.

"General?" the chief of staff whispered. "What are your orders?"

The general looked at the small red dot called Yunxi on the great war map. For forty years, the dots on that map had obeyed him. This one did not.

"Every theater stays at full readiness," he said hoarsely. "Every outside unit stays out of Lanshui. Nobody speaks. Nobody moves. We wait."

The chief of staff wrote it down. His pen shook so much that he had to write it twice.

* * *

Across the capital and the provinces, the highest leaders in the country sat over cold, untouched tea under the eyes of the black masks. Every lever of power they owned was useless for one night. Nobody dared to lift a cup, and nobody dared to ask the masks a question.

In Room 601 of Building 2, Chen Xuan turned off the bathroom tap and dried his hands on a clean towel. He walked to his bed and lay down. Within a minute, he was asleep.
