# Chapter 12: Three Doors in the Night

By eleven o'clock, the rain over Yunxi County had settled into a cold, steady drizzle that kept every shuttered lane empty. Across three districts of the county seat, three quiet doors opened without a sound.

The first door stood at the rear of Golden Wheat Bakery, diagonally across the wet cobblestones of Old Street from Mingde Hardware.

The alley behind the bakery smelled of damp coal ash, yeast, and sweet red-bean paste cooling inside. Number Nineteen stepped out of the shadow of a drainpipe in a dark rain poncho and matte gloves. Upstairs, a television murmured behind a curtained window before clicking off.

Number Nineteen knelt beside the steel service door. Two slender tension tools entered the twenty-yuan brass padlock and turned with a soft click, leaving not a scratch.

Inside the flour-dusted storage room, a wall-mounted digital video recorder blinked green beside the breaker box, its cable running straight to the wide-angle high-definition camera under the second-floor awning facing Old Street.

By the amber glow of a penlight, Number Nineteen disconnected the network cable, checked the timestamp, and paused the write cycle. On his handheld forensic duplicator, an unedited clip from two zero one scrolled past in miniature.

The framing was unmistakable. Mingde Hardware's painted wooden sign stood clearly in the upper left quadrant. Chen Mingde emerged through the doorway carrying a heavy crate of pipe elbows with both hands. A full two meters separated him from Ma Er, who glanced quickly up the street, planted his left heel awkwardly on the wet curb, and flung himself backward with theatrical force. Chen Mingde did not even drop the crate until he rushed forward to see whether the stranger was hurt.

Number Nineteen popped the original two-terabyte drive from its bay, sealed it inside an anti-static evidence pouch with a printed cryptographic hash tag, and slotted an identical pre-cloned drive into the recorder. The green light resumed its steady blink. Five minutes after entering, Number Nineteen slipped back into the rain, snapped the padlock shut, and vanished into the mist without leaving a boot print in the flour.

Two kilometers to the east, the second door sat at the end of a tiled basement corridor beneath the radiology wing of Yunxi People's Hospital.

The corridor smelled sharply of floor bleach and damp concrete. Fluorescent tubes buzzed overhead at half power. One floor above, the lone night technician had stepped out to the courtyard for a cigarette, leaving the stairwell quiet.

Number Twenty-seven walked down the corridor in a gray utility coat with a faded electrical maintenance badge and a scuffed toolbox. At the heavy fire door labeled Imaging Archive Server Room, he slid a cloned magnetic card across the reader, heard the dry click of the solenoid, and stepped inside.

Cool air washed over him from two humming server racks. Kneeling beside the primary DICOM storage array, Number Twenty-seven plugged a hardware write-blocker into the maintenance port and queried the radiology database under Ma Er's intake number.

Within twelve seconds, the raw imaging packet appeared on his screen. Unlike the printed summary attached to Deputy Director Fang Zhihua's pre-typed concussion report, the cranial CT scan recorded at two thirty-one that afternoon showed zero hematoma, zero cranial fracture, zero soft-tissue swelling, and zero cervical displacement. The skull and brain tissue were completely blank of injury.

Even more damning sat three lines below the scan in the immutable server audit log. At two thirty-four, Deputy Director Fang Zhihua's credentials had manually overridden the automated radiologist sign-off flag, attaching a boilerplate text file imported from an external flash drive and sealing the folder from standard nurse-station viewing.

Number Twenty-seven extracted the raw DICOM package, the server audit journal, and the system clock synchronization certificate onto dual encrypted drives, verifying the digital signatures down to the millisecond. He disconnected the write-blocker, wiped the port edge with a dry cloth, and exited into the bleach-scented hallway thirty seconds before the elevator chimed upstairs.

The third door was not locked at all. It was a grease-stained red vinyl curtain hanging over a basement mahjong parlor tucked behind the old grain depot in Yunxi's south district.

Through the heavy curtain drifted the clatter of plastic tiles, the scrape of wooden stools, and a cloud of cheap cigarette smoke. Inside, four cramped tables sat beneath bare yellow bulbs. At the corner table farthest from the stairs, Ma Er hunched over a stack of crumpled bills, a lit cigarette wedged between stained fingers and a warm bottle of beer beside his elbow.

There was no bandage on his head and no neck brace around his throat. He laughed loudly as he slapped a tile onto the green baize, bragging to the three bleary-eyed men across from him that a single tumble on Old Street paid better than three months of hauling cement.

Two tables away, sitting alone in the dim corner with a bowl of untouched peanuts and a dark peaked cap pulled low over his eyes, sat Number Forty-two.

He did not look directly at Ma Er. He listened patiently as Ma Er drank another mouthful of beer, reached inside his imitation-leather jacket, and patted a thick kraft envelope tucked into his breast pocket with a smug grin.

Twenty minutes later, Ma Er stood up from the table and headed down the narrow cinderblock hallway toward the parlor's cramped washroom.

He never noticed the quiet man who stepped out of the shadow beside the sink.

In a passage barely a meter wide, Number Forty-two brushed past Ma Er's right shoulder as the drunk man fumbled with his belt. The contact lasted less than half a second, light as a stray draft. Ma Er muttered a slurred complaint, shouldered into the stall, and kicked the wooden door shut.

Outside in the dim vestibule, Number Forty-two opened the kraft envelope with gloved fingertips. Inside sat fifty crisp hundred-yuan notes bound with a bank strap bearing the stamp of Yunxi Rural Commercial Bank, dated ten thirty that morning. Number Forty-two photographed the serial number bands and the teller stamp in crisp resolution, then slipped the bills neatly back inside.

His other hand held Ma Er's cheap smartphone, lifted from the outer coat pocket in the same half-second brush. Ma Er did not even use a screen lock.

Number Forty-two opened the budget handset's built-in call recorder. Sitting at the top of the afternoon log was a forty-eight-second recording from one fifty-one. Yan Tiehu's gravelly voice came through clearly over the wired earbud: instructing Ma Er to fall cleanly outside Mingde Hardware, wait for the white hatchback, and collect the five thousand yuan once Deputy Director Fang Zhihua stamped the hospital sheet.

Number Forty-two mirrored the raw audio file, carrier metadata, and device identifier onto his pocket drive in nine seconds flat.

When Ma Er stumbled out of the stall, wiping his hands on his trousers, Number Forty-two was already stepping past him toward the stairs. A second feather-light brush returned both the untouched envelope and the warm handset to Ma Er's pockets without the man feeling a thing.

Minutes later, Ma Er swaggered back up into the drizzle, humming an off-key tune toward his rented room near the freight yard. Fifty paces behind him, blending seamlessly into the dark eaves, Number Forty-two followed like a silent shadow, tapping his earpiece twice to report to Riverside Road that all three doors were closed and clean.
