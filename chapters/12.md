# Chapter 12: Three Options

In the quiet eastern courtyard in Qingyan City, Chief An's secure line rang.

"Chief, this is Twenty-three."

Chief An Zhiyuan sat up and set his tea aside.

On the desk lay a clean sheet of paper with two lines in his own neat hand: *Option One. Option Two.*

"Go ahead, Gong Yi. Close your front door first, because I can hear customers."

"Already closed, Chief. The restock sign is up." A pot clinked behind him, so he had clearly answered in his apron. "I have two things for you, and one thing that made me laugh behind my own counter!"

"Start with the laugh. I could use one."

"Two off-duty auxiliary officers came in for noodles, and one said, loud enough for half the street, that duty officer Wei Kun passed a cup of hot water through the cell bars to the old hardware man last night. The other said he would be written up for it. The first said, 'Then write him up! It was cold. Director Cheng would let the old man freeze and call it discipline!' And neither of them knew the cook was listening for the Boss."

"Hot water," Chief An repeated, and his stern face eased.

A cup of hot water, in that place!

"Keep that officer's name close. Now the other two."

"First, the money. Yunxi County approved a relocation fund of six hundred forty million yuan for Old Street. I pulled the public payment records, nothing a library card could not find, and at least one hundred ninety million never reached a single family! It went to three companies: Hongrui, a demolition firm registered to Deputy Director Cheng Biao's brother-in-law, and Ankang Private Clinic."

Chief An's pen stopped over the page. "The clinic that wrote the injury note?"

"The very one. Attorney Bai's assistant pulled the billing receipt through an ordinary records request. The driver was treated at ten to two, and his own arrest was not logged until five past. No police doctor ever saw him."

"Clean proof!" Chief An said. "Go on."

"Second, the holding notices. Two earlier families gave me their copies, and both were backdated to the afternoon of the arrest, to hide an illegal overnight lockup. Only one notice tells the truth, the Boss's uncle's, with seven-fifty in the morning in Wei Kun's own hand!"

"So he would not sign the lie?"

"It looks that way, Chief. And the clock is running, because Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu controls the money, and the provincial inspection team lands next Wednesday. Every scrap came off public shelves, so nobody knows we looked."

"Well done! Keep the apron on and wait for my word."

Chief An clicked the line dark and looked at his two empty headings.

He had forty years behind him, and he knew three ways to end this before sunrise.

One call to the right man in the provincial capital would turn Yunxi's police bureau inside out by breakfast.

It would also be loud and clumsy, and it would bury a dozen shopkeepers and one honest beat cop along with the men who deserved it.

After all, a chief who acts without his Boss is only a man with a phone, and Chief An knew his Boss.

Chen Xuan wanted no revenge, only fair treatment for his uncle by the book, because the book should work for people like his uncle too.

So Chief An typed the way he would speak if the Boss sat across the desk.

---

Back in Yunxi County, in Room 601, Chen Xuan's phone buzzed.

"Brother, if you stare at that phone any harder, it will turn into a brick!" Chen Hao said from the sofa, thumbs flying over a mobile game.

"Just a work message," Chen Xuan said.

In the kitchen, his mother scrubbed the same soup bowl for the third time, only to keep her hands busy.

Chen Xuan read the note the way he read a stack of invoices, once for the shape and once for the parts that mattered.

The first part listed what Gong Yi had found, from the clinic slip timed too early to the missing money.

The second part was short, and it sounded like Chief An talking.

*Boss, here is how I see it. Option One: our lawyer files a full complaint with the city anti-corruption office tonight, with copies to the prosecutor and the police chief. Inspectors land Wednesday, so that forces their hand within hours, and your uncle could be home by morning.*

*Option Two: we wait and hand everything to the provincial team on Wednesday. That means three more nights in that cell.*

*Option Three: I make one call tonight, and a career ends before dawn. It is fastest and loudest. I would pick the first.*

*One more thing. Officer Wei Kun wrote the true time, and he brought your uncle hot water when nobody told him to.*

Chen Xuan did not need five seconds.

With the provincial inspectors four days away, the city's leaders would never dare to sit on proof this clean.

A clean paper aimed at that deadline would crack the cover-up open without anyone bending a rule.

As for the other two, one cost his uncle three more nights, and the other made a loud mess for a dozen shopkeepers.

Then he thought of the duty officer, who had every reason to sign what he was told and nothing to gain by doing right.

Wei Kun had written one honest hour anyway, and carried water to an old man nobody important would ever thank him for helping.

Something warm moved in Chen Xuan's chest.

There was always somebody, somewhere, quietly doing right when no one watched.

He typed back with his thumb: *Let's go with the first one. Could you add Old Yu's eyewitness statement too? I will send a photo now. All by the book, no shortcuts, and nobody gets hurt.*

A second message followed before he set the phone down: *Also, could someone send Second Uncle a hot meal and a blanket tonight, through the regular visitor window? He is fifty-two, and that floor is concrete. And please make sure nobody takes it out on Officer Wei Kun. He did right by a stranger when it would have been easier not to.*

He photographed Old Yu's page and sent it.

Sent.

Then he laid the phone face down on the coffee table.

"Why are you smiling like that?" Chen Hao squinted at him.

"Am I smiling?"

"You look like you won the lottery!"

"Must be Mom's soup."

Back in the courtyard, Chief An read both replies twice.

Hmm?

Three ways to end a county's leadership by morning lay on his desk, and what had the Boss spent half his reply on?

A hot meal. A blanket. One beat cop's safety.

Chief An's throat went tight, and a rare, gentle smile crossed his lined face.

"Gong Yi!" he said into the speaker. "Cook a big bowl of beef noodles with an extra egg, and carry it and a thick blanket to the station's family window yourself, apron and all. Go through the front door like anyone's relative. And pass the word down that nobody touches Officer Wei Kun!"

"The broth is already boiling, Chief!" Gong Yi said, and Chief An could hear the smile in it.

"Attorney Bai. Your turn."

Knock, knock.

The study door opened, and Bai Qingzhou, Number Eleven on the roster, walked in with a slim black folder, looking very pleased with himself.

"Everything is ready, Chief," he said, tapping the folder. "Nineteen pages, in order: the clinic slip, the honest holding notice, the neighbor's signed statement, the street footage and the fund trail. I will file it myself at the city Discipline Commission's night desk!"

"Through the front door, Bai, with a signed receipt and every rule followed. Nothing that makes a clerk raise an eyebrow."

Bai's eyes gleamed behind his silver-rimmed glasses. "Naturally, Chief. When the law is sharp enough to cut iron, who needs a club?"

At the city Discipline Commission, at the night desk in a marble lobby, a young clerk was eating instant noodles and expecting a quiet night.

Was he ready for what walked in?
