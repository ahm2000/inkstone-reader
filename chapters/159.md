# Chapter 159: Landing in Shenzhen

Inside the quiet business class cabin of the flight to Shenzhen, the midday sun shone brightly against the oval window beside Qin Yuhan's seat. She had changed into a crisp charcoal blazer over a white silk blouse, her dark hair pinned neatly back, looking every inch the composed president of Haiyue Group.

Beside her, Chen Xuan leaned back comfortably in the wide leather seat, wearing his plain navy windbreaker with his canvas backpack tucked neatly under the footrest. He looked out the window at the drifting white clouds and sighed dramatically.

"A regular logistics clerk sitting in business class," Chen Xuan murmured, shaking his head with mock solemnity. "If Manager Hu sees this boarding pass, he might accuse me of embezzling the office stationery budget."

Qin Yuhan glanced over from her tablet, her lips curving into a fond smile. "I paid for the upgrade myself, not Hengxin Logistics. Besides, you are traveling as my husband and official logistics escort. I cannot have you cramped in the back row."

"In that case, I will perform my escort duties seriously," Chen Xuan said. Just as the flight attendant set down a warm cup of oolong tea and a small porcelain bowl of honey-roasted cashews on Qin Yuhan's tray table, Chen Xuan smoothly reached over and plucked two cashews from her bowl.

Qin Yuhan caught his wrist on the second reach, raising one arched eyebrow. "Is stealing my snacks part of your official duties?"

"Quality inspection," Chen Xuan replied without blinking. "Very important in logistics. Gotta make sure the cargo is fresh."

She laughed softly, giving up and sliding the whole small bowl closer to the armrest between them. "Eat as much as you want. Just do not spill crumbs on my shipping contract."

She tapped her tablet screen, pulling up the digital scans of the Haiyue Star's customs release forms and Pier Nine's berth log. Even though she looked calm, Chen Xuan noticed the faint crease between her brows as she reread the European client's penalty clause.

"You do not need to memorize every paragraph," Chen Xuan said gently, taking a cashew. "If the customs seal and port authority release are both stamped, the yard operator has zero legal right to hold your containers. A private stevedoring firm cannot overrule customs."

Qin Yuhan looked at him in mild surprise. "That is actually the exact argument our legal team is preparing. How do you know the difference between a customs yard seal and a private berth hold?"

"You hear a lot of shouting in the logistics office at Hengxin," Chen Xuan said effortlessly, giving her a crooked grin. "Half my job is listening to warehouse dispatchers complain about port managers."

Qin Yuhan chuckled and shook her head, completely satisfied with the answer. She rested her hand briefly over his on the armrest. "Whatever happens at Pier Nine today, I am really glad you came with me, Xuan."

"Where my wife goes, I go," he said lightly, squeezing her fingers. "Now close your eyes for thirty minutes. You barely slept last night."

The flight touched down smoothly at Shenzhen Bao'an International Airport in the early afternoon. As they stepped out of the glass terminal doors toward the passenger pickup lane, a mild, humid coastal breeze greeted them, a welcome contrast to Shanghai's chilly late November air.

The arrival curb buzzed with travelers, rolling suitcases, and honking shuttles. Passing right beside them, a cheerful middle-aged uncle in a faded blue polo shirt pushing a luggage trolley caught Chen Xuan's eye and smiled warmly.

"Just flew down from Shanghai, young man?" the uncle asked in a relaxed southern accent. "Wait until you feel the breeze near the docks. Much nicer than the cold wind up north, right?"

"Definitely nicer, Uncle," Chen Xuan replied with an easy laugh. "Take care of your knees in this humidity."

"Always do!" the uncle chuckled, rolling his trolley unhurriedly toward the taxi stand.

Ten steps further down the curb, a young woman in white sneakers holding a cup of iced milk tea glanced up from her phone and flashed Chen Xuan a bright, neighborly grin as she stepped aside to let their luggage cart pass. Near the rideshare pillar, a tanned driver leaning against a silver sedan lifted his chin in a casual nod and joked about the afternoon traffic heading toward the port.

Not a single one of them looked stiff, hurried, or out of place. They blended completely into the everyday pulse of the busy airport, warm and natural as old neighborhood friends.

Qin Yuhan watched with amused curiosity as they waited by the curb. "Xuan, how is it that everywhere we go, complete strangers start chatting with you like they have known you for ten years?"

"Must be my friendly face," Chen Xuan said modestly, adjusting the strap of his backpack. "People can tell I am easy to talk to."

Before Qin Yuhan could tease him back, a sleek black business MPV glided up to the curb and stopped neatly in front of them. The sliding door opened, and a lean man in his late thirties stepped out quickly. It was Lin Jian, Haiyue Group's southern operations manager, accompanied by two company lawyers carrying thick folders.

"President Qin," Manager Lin Jian greeted respectfully, taking her rolling suitcase and loading it into the trunk. He turned politely to Chen Xuan with a warm nod. "Mr. Chen, thank you for making the trip down with President Qin."

"Just call me Chen Xuan," he replied easily, climbing into the back row beside his wife while Manager Lin Jian took the front passenger seat.

As the MPV merged onto the coastal expressway heading toward Pier Nine, Manager Lin Jian's expression grew serious again. "President Qin, I sent our customs broker back to Gate Three an hour ago with the full port authority verification. The Shen Family's dock manager refused to even look at the folder. He told our broker that unless Haiyue Group signs the 12 million yuan surcharge agreement today, the yard stays locked until Monday."

Qin Yuhan's gaze turned cool and resolute. "They are openly testing whether Haiyue will blink first. Drive straight to Gate Three, Manager Lin Jian. Let us go meet this dock manager in person."

Beside her, Chen Xuan unscrewed the cap of his green thermos, took a calm sip of warm jasmine tea, and watched the towering red-and-white gantry cranes of Shenzhen Pier Nine rise steadily against the hazy coastal sky.
