# Chapter 118: A Game of Chess with the Retired General

After the housekeeper brewed a fresh pot of Chen Xuan's Yunnan red tea and poured it into white porcelain cups, Elder Qin Zhengguo took one slow sip and nodded approvingly. The tea was smooth, warm, and rich without a hint of bitterness.

Still, as a retired four-star general who had doted on Qin Yuhan since she was a little girl studying before Fudan University, Elder Qin Zhengguo was curious to test this famous "lazy Shanghai clerk" who had somehow won his favorite granddaughter's heart.

The old general stood up from his armchair and pointed his walnut cane toward the stone table beneath the ancient pomegranate tree in the sunny center of the courtyard. On top of the stone table sat a heavy rosewood Chinese chess board with polished brass-inlaid pieces.

"Chen Xuan," Elder Qin Zhengguo called out in his deep, resonant voice. "Your Aunt Lin tells me you spend half your day reading newspapers at a shipping desk in Shanghai and clock out at four fifty-five every afternoon. Come over here and set up the pieces. Let me see if a young man with so much free time knows how to move a horse and a cannon."

Around the courtyard veranda, the Beijing relatives immediately perked up and gathered around the stone table with eager grins.

Qin Haoran folded his arms and whispered loudly to Second Aunt Zhou Meilin, "Watch this, Mom. Grandfather was the undefeated chess champion of the northern command for fifteen years. Even retired ministry chiefs lose to him in twenty minutes. This twelve-thousand-yuan logistics clerk is going to get crushed in ten moves!"

Lin Shuyin tugged anxiously at Qin Yuhan's sleeve. "Yuhan, tell Chen Xuan to decline politely! Your grandfather hates players who blunder without thinking. If Chen Xuan loses in five minutes, the whole courtyard will laugh at us!"

Qin Yuhan looked at Chen Xuan, who was already pulling up a stone stool across from Elder Qin Zhengguo with an easy smile.

"Do not worry, Mom," Qin Yuhan said softly, a hint of playfulness in her cool eyes. "Even if Chen Xuan loses in five minutes, he will probably just ask Grandfather for another plate of melon seeds."

At the stone table, Elder Qin Zhengguo took the black pieces and waved a hand. "You are a guest on your first trip to Beijing. Take red and move first."

"Thank you, Grandfather," Chen Xuan said politely.

Elder Qin Zhengguo pushed his central pawn forward with military precision, his eyes turning sharp as he set up his classic "Iron Horse Northern Formation," an aggressive double-chariot trap that had crushed dozens of ambitious young officers over the years.

Across the stone board, Chen Xuan did not frown, sweat, or stare intensely at the lines. He leaned back casually, yawned slightly in the warm mid-morning sun, took a sip of Yunnan red tea, and moved a single red cannon with two fingers.

"By the way, Grandfather," Chen Xuan chatted lazily as he slid a horse across the river line, "the morning markets in Beijing sell scallions three fen cheaper a bundle than the market near Emerald Riverside Villa in Shanghai, though your winter cabbages up here look much sweeter."

Qin Haoran snorted from the sidelines. "Look at him talking about cabbages and scallions while Grandfather's left chariot is already crossing the river! He has no idea his right flank is wide open!"

Elder Qin Zhengguo did not answer the cabbage comment. He slammed his heavy black chariot down onto Chen Xuan's bottom rank with a crisp snap, preparing to call a deadly double-cannon check on the next turn.

Chen Xuan did not even pause. While picking up a roasted peanut from the side saucer and peeling the red skin off for Qin Yuhan, who stood right beside his shoulder, he used his left hand to nudge a quiet red pawn one step sideways.

Move twelve. Move fourteen. Move fifteen.

Every move Chen Xuan made looked completely lazy, slow, and harmless, like an ordinary clerk strolling home after clocking out at four fifty-five. Yet somehow, each casual step blocked an intersection by half an inch.

On the sixteenth move, Chen Xuan popped the peeled peanut into Qin Yuhan's hand, picked up his unassuming red horse, and set it down gently behind the river wall.

"Checkmate, Grandfather," Chen Xuan smiled warmly, reaching for the teapot to top up the old general's porcelain cup. "Your tea is getting cool."

Dead silence fell over the courtyard under the pomegranate tree.

Qin Haoran leaned forward so fast his silk tie swung over his shoulder, his mouth falling wide open. Second Aunt Zhou Meilin blinked three times at the board, while Qin Zhengming gasped out loud.

On the rosewood board, Elder Qin Zhengguo's two fierce black chariots and two cannons were locked cleanly in the middle of the river, unable to retreat a single square, while Chen Xuan's lone red horse and central pawn held the black general pinned cold in the palace without taking a single piece by force!

Sixteen moves! Not one fierce clash, not one loud shout, just sixteen smooth, effortless steps that turned a four-star general's sharpest battlefield formation into a harmless box!

Elder Qin Zhengguo stared down at the rosewood board for five full seconds, his thick silver eyebrows twitching. Then the retired commander threw his head back and burst out laughing so loudly that the sparrows in the locust trees flew up into the blue sky.

"Good! What a slick sixteen-move net!" Elder Qin Zhengguo slapped his knee and pointed a finger at Chen Xuan, his eyes shining with genuine delight. "Lazy boy, where did a Shanghai logistics clerk learn to trap a heavy chariot like that while talking about market cabbages?"

"From my Second Uncle Chen Mingde on Old Street in Yunxi County, Grandfather," Chen Xuan replied honestly with a grin. "He plays on a wooden stool outside his hardware shop every evening before dinner. If you do not watch his little pawns, he makes you wash the dishes."

Qin Yuhan covered her smile with the back of her hand as her grandfather roared with laughter again. Around the table, Qin Haoran's face turned as red as the silk lanterns overhead.
