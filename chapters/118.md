# Chapter 118: A Game of Chess with the Retired General

After Auntie Zhou brewed a fresh pot of Chen Xuan's Yunnan red tea and poured it into white porcelain cups, Elder Qin Zhengguo took one slow sip and nodded approvingly. The tea was smooth, warm, and rich without a hint of bitterness, leaving a sweet aftertaste that suited the crisp autumn morning.

Still, as a retired four-star general who had doted on Qin Yuhan since she was a little girl running around this very courtyard, Elder Qin Zhengguo was curious to test this easygoing Shanghai husband who had somehow won his favorite granddaughter's heart.

The old general stood up from his armchair and pointed his walnut cane toward the stone table beneath the ancient pomegranate tree in the sunny center of the courtyard. Heavy red pomegranates hung among yellowing autumn leaves overhead. On top of the stone table sat a heavy rosewood Chinese chess board with polished brass-inlaid pieces.

"Chen Xuan," Elder Qin Zhengguo called out in his deep, resonant voice. "Shuyin tells me you spend half your day reading the newspaper and drinking tea. Come sit down. Let us see if your mind is as lazy as your feet."

Lin Shuyin and Second Aunt stepped out onto the wooden veranda to watch, both wearing knowing smiles. Qin Zhengming followed behind them with his hands tucked calmly into his jacket pockets, curious to see how his son-in-law would hold up. Qin Wenbo leaned against a red pillar and whispered loudly enough for everyone to hear, "Grandpa has beaten half the retired commanders in the Beijing military compound. Last month a provincial chess champion came to visit and lost in twenty minutes. Chen Xuan is going to be swept off the board in ten moves."

Qin Yuhan frowned slightly and walked over beside Chen Xuan. "Grandpa loves aggressive openings. Just play for fun and do not feel pressured."

"I never feel pressured when there is good tea nearby," Chen Xuan laughed. He carried the teapot and two porcelain cups out to the stone table, poured a steaming cup for Elder Qin first, and sat down across from him with an easy smile. "Grandpa, please take the red pieces and make the first move."

Elder Qin snorted warmly and immediately pushed his central cannon straight into position. From the very first turn, the old general played like a veteran commander leading an armored division across an open plain. His chariots thundered down the flanks, his horses leaped forward in sharp diagonal strikes, and his pieces snapped onto the rosewood board with crisp, loud clicks.

On the veranda, Second Aunt crossed her arms and smirked. "Look at that momentum. Chen Xuan can barely keep up."

Indeed, Chen Xuan looked completely unhurried. He rested one elbow on the stone table, took a relaxed sip of tea, and moved his black pieces almost lazily. He did not rush to counterattack. Instead, he stepped a horse back to guard a quiet corner, nudged a foot soldier forward by one square, and even seemed to leave his left flank wide open.

Elder Qin narrowed his eyes, spotting the apparent opening. His fingers hovered over his red chariot for three seconds, then he smiled triumphantly and drove the chariot straight across the river, capturing a black horse.

"Aha! Careless, boy!" Elder Qin boomed. "In war and in chess, you never leave a flank unguarded!"

"Grandpa's eyes are sharp," Chen Xuan said mildly. Without changing his expression, he gently slid a black cannon behind his own defense line, then tapped a small foot soldier forward.

At first, nobody on the veranda noticed anything special. Qin Wenbo even chuckled, thinking Chen Xuan was making desperate random moves just to delay the end.

Three turns later, however, Elder Qin's triumphant smile faded. He leaned closer over the board, his thick eyebrows knitting together. That red chariot he had proudly driven across the river was suddenly cut off from retreat. Every square he tried to move to was quietly covered by a black horse or a hidden cannon.

Elder Qin tried to force a breakthrough down the middle, sacrificing a foot soldier to open a lane. Chen Xuan did not panic or rush to strike back. He simply shifted his advisor, moved his silent chariot one space to the side, and closed the lane like a heavy wooden gate swinging shut.

The courtyard fell completely silent. Even the rustle of autumn leaves in the pomegranate tree sounded loud against the stillness.

Ten minutes later, Elder Qin stared down at the rosewood board in sheer disbelief. Without a single loud slam or flashy charge, Chen Xuan's lone horse and quiet cannon had locked the red general tight inside the palace. There was not a single legal escape left.

"Checkmate," Chen Xuan said softly, reaching out to refill Elder Qin's teacup before the tea could cool.

On the veranda, Qin Wenbo's jaw dropped open. Second Aunt blinked twice, unable to believe what she had just witnessed. Lin Shuyin stared at her son-in-law in stunned silence, while Qin Zhengming let out a quiet, surprised laugh.

Elder Qin sat frozen for three full seconds, tracing the lines of the board with one finger. Suddenly, he slapped his thigh and burst into a booming, joyful laugh that echoed across the courtyard.

"Good! Very good!" Elder Qin roared, his eyes shining with genuine delight. "You play like flowing water, boy! No greed, no hurry, no wasted motion. You let me march straight into my own cage and poured my tea while doing it!"

"Grandpa went easy on me," Chen Xuan said modestly, smiling as he began gathering the pieces. "Your opening attack nearly broke my center."

"Do not flatter an old soldier!" Elder Qin waved him off, already resetting his red pieces with eager energy. "Set them up again. Best two out of three!"

They played a second round under the warm autumn sun. This time Elder Qin played with full caution and decades of hard-earned discipline, yet Chen Xuan still guided the game with effortless calm, winning the second match cleanly while keeping Grandpa's teacup full.

By the time lunch was called, Elder Qin was beaming from ear to ear, patting Chen Xuan's shoulder as if he had found a priceless treasure. Neither the old general nor anyone in the family had the slightest idea who Chen Xuan truly was. To Elder Qin, he was simply a rare, calm young man with a steady head, warm hands, and the best chess sense he had seen in twenty years.
