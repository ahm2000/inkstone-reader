# Chapter 40: The Founding Elder's Phrase

At a quarter to two on Monday morning, the old black phone in General Qin Zhengguo's side room let out a low, steady ring along the direct copper wire leading west toward the Western Hills.

Colonel Ye Chenglin stood beside the switch box, holding his breath as the first ring stretched into the second, then the third, then the fourth.

On the fifth ring, the phone clicked open.

"Qin Zhengguo," a dry, unhurried, ninety-year-old voice said from the other end, carrying the faint crackle of old copper wire and the soft sound of someone blowing out cigarette smoke. "You always were an impatient boy. Most men wait until sunrise to disturb an old man's tea."

Qin Zhengguo's stiff shoulders eased slightly, though his face stayed hard as iron. "Elder Song. Forgive the late hour. Are you safe?"

A grim, dry laugh rolled over the wire, sounding like dry pine needles scraping stone. "Safe? I am sitting by my window with a pot of bitter tea and a cigarette my doctors banned twenty years ago. Outside my window, ten young men in plain black masks are standing in my yard like iron posts. Not one of them has coughed or shifted his boots in forty minutes."

Qin Zhengguo's breath paused for a split second. "Ten guards, Elder Song? They put five outside my doors at the Central Military Commission."

"Ten for a retired ninety-year-old who can barely climb two flights of stairs," Elder Song said with another quiet, chilling laugh. "At least Zero Guard still remembers how to show proper respect to an old man who helped write their charter. Tell me, Qin Zhengguo, how many years has it been since Zero walked the streets of this capital?"

"Thirty-six years, Elder Song," Qin Zhengguo answered evenly. "Before I even wore a division commander's badge."

"Thirty-six years," Elder Song murmured, pausing while the faint crackle of burning tobacco carried over the wire. "Back then, half the men who watched them march through the snow swore they would never see those masks again. Look at us now. All four of my red phones are sitting on silent hold, and I guess yours are the same."

"Every single one," Qin Zhengguo said, leaning one hand against the phone box. "I reached Number 1 on the vault line ten minutes ago. Black Pine Guard has been stood down and placed under Zero Guard's watch. Number 1 refuses to give even a short status report without the founding override phrase."

Elder Song went silent for five full seconds. When he spoke again, the light amusement was gone, replaced by a heavy, serious weight pressing straight through the earpiece.

"Qin Zhengguo," Elder Song said slowly, "you are not calling to ask me to get in Zero Guard's way, are you?"

"Never," Qin Zhengguo replied without a second of doubt, his jaw setting hard. "Zero Guard is acting under an Absolute Order. Nobody in the army will lift a finger against them. I already ordered my guards to stand down. I am calling because I command the armed forces of this republic, Elder Song. If an enemy reached deep enough to wake Zero Guard tonight, I need to know whether our national defense is bleeding."

"And what if the answer burns men whose hands you shook at banquets for thirty years?" Elder Song asked sharply, his voice cutting like a winter wind. "What if the rot sits right next to you? Can you keep your spine straight?"

Qin Zhengguo stared straight ahead without blinking. "I swore my oath to the republic, Elder Song. Not to any man's seat. If a general under my roof sold out this country, I will hold the rifle myself."

Across the line, Elder Song let out a long, slow breath of smoke.

"Good," Elder Song said softly. "You still sound like the young officer who refused to pull back at the southern pass. Listen closely, Qin Zhengguo. I will say this once, and you will never write it down."

"I am listening, Elder Song."

"The iron pine remembers the snow of forty-nine; open the ledger for the watchman."

Qin Zhengguo locked every single word into his memory. "Thank you, Elder Song. Take care of your health. I will come visit the Western Hills once the sun is up."

"Bring better cigarettes than the ones my nurses hide from me," Elder Song said dryly. "Go hear the truth, Qin Zhengguo. And brace yourself."

The copper line clicked dead.

Qin Zhengguo did not waste a single second. He strode back into his study, Ye Chenglin trailing two steps behind in tense silence, and lifted the plain black vault phone from his desk. He tapped a fresh cigarette between his fingers, lit it with a quick flick of his lighter, and spoke straight into the mouthpiece.

"Number 1. Are you still on the line?"

"Line open," Number 1's calm, gravelly voice answered right away.

Qin Zhengguo took one drag, blew the smoke aside, and spoke with clear, steady weight. "The iron pine remembers the snow of forty-nine; open the ledger for the watchman."

Dead silence swallowed the line.

One second passed. Two seconds. Three seconds. Then came a sharp double click from Zero Guard's listening line, checking the founding override phrase and opening a narrow window of clearance.

"Code accepted," Number 1 said, his voice stripped of feeling, cold and flat as stone. "Under charter rules, I am allowed to give you a short status report only. No target names, no places, and no mission details."

"Speak," Qin Zhengguo ordered tightly.

"Over the past twenty years," Number 1 began in a steady, heavy voice, "a deep network of insiders and foreign agents inside the republic's finance ministries, big state companies, and top offices has been steadily stealing state wealth, key reserves, and public money."

Qin Zhengguo's eyes narrowed. "How much?"

"Confirmed stolen money is over 100 trillion yuan," Number 1 stated flatly.

Qin Zhengguo's hand twitched barely an inch around the phone. Across the study, Ye Chenglin could not hear the earpiece, yet he saw his general's face suddenly harden into pale stone.

"100 trillion," Qin Zhengguo repeated in a dry whisper. "Over twenty years."

"That is only the money," Number 1 continued without pausing. "The trigger for tonight's Absolute Order is treason at the highest level. Secret navy radar plans, weak spots in the southern power grid, and troop layouts along the northern and western borders have been steadily copied, locked up, and sold to hostile foreign spy networks."

The floor seemed to tilt beneath Qin Zhengguo's boots.

Every drop of color drained from the four-star general's weathered face. Navy radar plans. The southern power grid. Border troop layouts. Those were not just bribery files. Those were the eyes, the veins, and the steel shield of the whole country, handed over to foreign knives from inside the capital's own halls.

"Zero Guard is carrying out a full sweep from top to bottom tonight," Number 1 finished quietly. "No more details are allowed. Status report complete. Closing the vault line."

The line clicked dead.

Qin Zhengguo did not move. He did not set the black phone down. He sat completely frozen in his chair, the dead phone still pressed to his ear, staring blankly at the empty air above his desk.

Between the fingers of his left hand, his forgotten cigarette burned steadily down, turning into a long stick of gray ash until the glowing red tip reached the filter and burned straight into the skin of his knuckles. He did not even flinch from the pain.

Across the smoke-filled study, Ye Chenglin stared at his general's pale, stunned face and the thin wisp of smoke rising from his motionless fingers, and a cold, bottomless dread gripped the colonel's chest.
