# Chapter 40: The Founding Elder's Phrase

At a quarter to two on Monday morning, the old black phone in General Qin Zhengguo's side room rang steadily along the direct copper wire leading west to the Western Hills.

Colonel Ye Chenglin held his breath beside the switch box as four long rings passed.

On the fifth ring, the phone clicked open.

"Qin Zhengguo," a dry, ninety-year-old voice said over the faint crackle of old copper and exhaled smoke. "You always were an impatient boy. Most men wait until sunrise to disturb an old man's tea."

Qin Zhengguo's shoulders eased slightly, though his face stayed iron. "Elder Song. Forgive the late hour. Are you safe?"

A grim, dry laugh rolled across the wire. "Safe? I am sitting by my window with bitter tea and a cigarette my doctors banned twenty years ago. Outside, ten young men in plain black masks are standing in my yard like iron posts. Not one has coughed or shifted his boots in forty minutes."

Qin Zhengguo paused a split second. "Ten guards, Elder Song? They put five outside my doors at the Central Military Commission."

"Ten for a retired ninety-year-old who can barely climb two flights of stairs," Elder Song said with a quiet, chilling laugh. "At least Black Guard still remembers proper respect for an old man who helped write their charter. Tell me, Qin Zhengguo, how many years has it been since Black Guard walked these streets?"

"Thirty-six years, Elder Song," Qin Zhengguo answered evenly. "Before I even wore a division commander's badge."

"Thirty-six years," Elder Song murmured as tobacco crackled faintly over the line. "Back then, half the men who watched them march through the snow swore they would never see those masks again. Look at us now. All four of my red phones sit on silent hold, and I guess yours are the same."

"Every single one," Qin Zhengguo said, leaning against the phone box. "I reached Number 1 on the vault line ten minutes ago. White Guard is stood down under Black Guard's watch. Number 1 refuses even a short status report without the founding override phrase."

Elder Song went silent for five full seconds. When he spoke again, all amusement was gone.

"Qin Zhengguo," Elder Song said slowly, "you are not calling to ask me to get in Black Guard's way, are you?"

"Never," Qin Zhengguo replied without hesitation. "Black Guard is acting under a Black Order. Nobody in the army will lift a finger against them. I already ordered my guards to stand down. I am calling because I command the armed forces of this republic, Elder Song. If an enemy reached deep enough to wake Black Guard tonight, I need to know whether our national defense is bleeding."

"And what if the answer burns men whose hands you shook at banquets for thirty years?" Elder Song asked sharply. "What if the rot sits right next to you? Can you keep your spine straight?"

Qin Zhengguo stared ahead without blinking. "I swore my oath to the republic, Elder Song. Not to any man's seat. If a general under my roof sold out this country, I will hold the rifle myself."

Elder Song let out a long breath of smoke.

"Good," he said softly. "You still sound like the young officer who refused to pull back at the southern pass. Listen closely, Qin Zhengguo. I will say this once, and you will never write it down."

"I am listening, Elder Song."

"The iron pine remembers the snow of forty-nine; open the ledger for the watchman."

Qin Zhengguo locked every word into memory. "Thank you, Elder Song. Take care of your health. I will visit the Western Hills once the sun is up."

"Bring better cigarettes than the ones my nurses hide from me," Elder Song said dryly. "Go hear the truth, Qin Zhengguo. And brace yourself."

The copper line clicked dead.

Qin Zhengguo strode straight back into his study, Ye Chenglin trailing in tense silence, and lifted the black vault phone. He lit a fresh cigarette and spoke into the mouthpiece.

"Number 1. Are you still on the line?"

"Line open," Number 1's gravelly voice answered at once.

Qin Zhengguo blew smoke aside and spoke with steady weight. "The iron pine remembers the snow of forty-nine; open the ledger for the watchman."

Dead silence swallowed the line.

Three seconds passed. Then came a sharp double click from Black Guard's listening line, verifying the phrase and opening a narrow window of clearance.

"Code accepted," Number 1 said, flat as stone. "Under charter rules, I am allowed a short status report only. No target names, no places, and no mission details."

"Speak," Qin Zhengguo ordered tightly.

"Over the past twenty years," Number 1 began in a heavy voice, "a deep network of insiders and foreign agents inside the republic's finance ministries, big state companies, and top offices has been steadily stealing state wealth, key reserves, and public money."

Qin Zhengguo's eyes narrowed. "How much?"

"Confirmed stolen money is over 100 trillion yuan," Number 1 stated flatly.

Qin Zhengguo's hand twitched barely an inch. Across the study, Ye Chenglin could not hear the earpiece, yet he watched his general's face harden into pale stone.

"100 trillion," Qin Zhengguo whispered dryly. "Over twenty years."

"That is only the money," Number 1 continued without pausing. "The trigger for tonight's Black Order is treason at the highest level. Secret navy radar plans, weak spots in the southern power grid, and troop layouts along the northern and western borders have been steadily copied, locked up, and sold to hostile foreign spy networks."

The floor seemed to tilt beneath Qin Zhengguo's boots.

Every drop of color drained from the four-star general's weathered face. Navy radar plans. The southern power grid. Border troop layouts. Those were not bribery files. Those were the eyes, veins, and steel shield of the country, handed to foreign knives from inside the capital's own halls.

"Black Guard is carrying out a full sweep from top to bottom tonight," Number 1 finished quietly. "No more details are allowed. Status report complete. Closing the vault line."

The line clicked dead.

Qin Zhengguo did not move or lower the dead phone. He sat frozen in his chair, staring blankly above his desk.

Between his left fingers, his forgotten cigarette burned steadily down until the glowing tip reached the filter and burned straight into his knuckles. He did not even flinch.

Across the study, Ye Chenglin stared at his general's stunned face and the thin smoke rising from his motionless hand, gripped by cold, bottomless dread.
