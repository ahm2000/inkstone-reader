# Chapter 40: The Founding Elder's Phrase

At a quarter to two on Monday morning, the old analog receiver in General Qin Zhengguo's anteroom emitted a low, steady ring along the direct copper wire leading west toward the Western Hills.

Colonel Zhao Ming stood beside the patch panel, holding his breath as the first ring stretched into the second, then the third, then the fourth.

On the fifth ring, the receiver clicked open.

"Qin Zhengguo," a dry, unhurried, ninety-year-old voice said from the other end, carrying the faint crackle of ancient copper and the soft rustle of someone exhaling cigarette smoke. "You always were an impatient boy. Most men wait until sunrise to disturb an old man's tea."

Qin Zhengguo's stiff shoulders eased slightly, though his face remained carved in iron. "Elder Song. Forgive the hour. Are you safe?"

A grim, dry chuckle rolled across the wire, sounding like dead pine needles scraping stone. "Safe? I am sitting by my window with a pot of bitter tea and a cigarette my doctors forbade twenty years ago. Outside my window, ten young men in matte-black masks are standing in my courtyard like iron pillars. Not one of them has coughed or shifted his boots in forty minutes."

Qin Zhengguo's breath paused for a fraction of a second. "Ten sentries, Elder Song? They placed five outside my doors at the Central Military Commission."

"Ten for a retired ninety-year-old who can barely climb two flights of stairs," Elder Song said with another quiet, chilling laugh. "At least Zero Guard still remembers how to show proper courtesy to an old man who helped ink the charter. Tell me, Qin Zhengguo, how many years has it been since Zero walked the streets of this capital?"

"Thirty-six years, Elder Song," Qin Zhengguo answered evenly. "Before I even wore a division commander's badge."

"Thirty-six years," Elder Song murmured, pausing while the faint crackle of burning tobacco carried over the wire. "Back then, half the men who watched them pass through the snow swore they would never see those masks again. Look at us now. All four of my red lines are sitting in silent hold, and I assume yours are the same."

"Every single one," Qin Zhengguo said, leaning one hand against the communications cabinet. "I reached Number 1 on the vault line ten minutes ago. Black Pine Guard has been stood down and placed under Zero monitoring. Number 1 refuses to issue even a basic status acknowledgment without the founding-era override phrase."

Elder Song went silent for five full seconds. When he spoke again, the mild amusement had vanished, replaced by a heavy, solemn weight pressing straight through the earpiece.

"Qin Zhengguo," Elder Song said slowly, "you are not calling to ask me to interfere with Zero Guard, are you?"

"Never," Qin Zhengguo replied without hesitation, his jaw setting hard. "Zero Guard operates under an Absolute Order. Nobody in the military will raise a finger against them. I already ordered my guards to stand down. I am calling because I command the armed forces of this republic, Elder Song. If an enemy reached deep enough to wake Zero Guard tonight, I need to know whether our national defense is bleeding."

"And what if the answer burns men whose hands you shook at banquets for thirty years?" Elder Song asked sharply, his voice cutting like a winter wind. "What if the rot sits next to you? Can you keep your spine straight?"

Qin Zhengguo stared straight ahead, unblinking. "I swore my oath to the republic, Elder Song. Not to any man's chair. If a general under my roof sold out this country, I will hold the rifle myself."

Across the line, Elder Song let out a long, slow breath of smoke.

"Good," Elder Song said softly. "You still sound like the young regimental commander who refused to retreat at the southern pass. Listen carefully, Qin Zhengguo. I will say this once, and you will never write it down."

"I am listening, Elder Song."

"The iron pine remembers the snow of forty-nine; open the ledger for the watchman."

Qin Zhengguo committed every syllable to memory. "Thank you, Elder Song. Take care of your health. I will visit the Western Hills once the sun comes up."

"Bring better cigarettes than the ones my nurses hide from me," Elder Song said dryly. "Go hear the truth, Qin Zhengguo. And brace yourself."

The copper line clicked dead.

Qin Zhengguo wasted not a second. He strode back into his study, Zhao Ming trailing two steps behind in tense silence, and lifted the matte-black vault handset from his desk. He tapped a fresh cigarette between his fingers, lit it with a quick flick of his lighter, and spoke straight into the mouthpiece.

"Number 1. Are you still on the line?"

"Channel active," Number 1's gravel-calm voice answered immediately.

Qin Zhengguo inhaled once, blew the smoke aside, and spoke with deliberate clarity. "The iron pine remembers the snow of forty-nine; open the ledger for the watchman."

Dead silence swallowed the circuit.

One second passed. Two seconds. Three seconds. Then came a sharp, double mechanical click from Zero Guard's monitoring bridge, verifying the founding-era override phrase and releasing a narrow clearance.

"Override authenticated," Number 1 said, his voice stripped of emotion, cold and flat as granite. "Under charter protocol, I am authorized to deliver a status acknowledgment only. No names of active targets, no locations, and no operational details."

"Speak," Qin Zhengguo ordered tightly.

"Over the past twenty years," Number 1 began in a steady, devastating cadence, "a deeply embedded domestic and foreign collusion network inside the republic's financial ministries, state enterprises, and senior administrative tiers has systematically drained state assets, strategic reserves, and sovereign capital."

Qin Zhengguo's eyes narrowed. "How much?"

"Confirmed verified outflow exceeds 100 trillion yuan," Number 1 stated flatly.

Qin Zhengguo's hand twitched imperceptibly around the handset. Across the study, Zhao Ming could not hear the earpiece, yet he saw his general's face suddenly harden into pale stone.

"100 trillion," Qin Zhengguo repeated in a hoarse whisper. "Over twenty years."

"That is only the financial ledger," Number 1 continued without pause. "The trigger for tonight's Absolute Order is treason of the highest strategic tier. Classified naval radar architecture, southern power-grid load vulnerabilities, and northern and western border-garrison deployment secrets have been systematically copied, encrypted, and sold to hostile overseas intelligence syndicates."

The room seemed to tilt beneath Qin Zhengguo's boots.

Every trace of color drained from the four-star general's weathered face. Naval radar. The southern power grid. Border-garrison deployments. Those were not mere corruption files. Those were the eyes, the veins, and the steel shield of the entire nation, handed over to foreign knives from inside the capital's own halls.

"Zero Guard is executing a full root-and-branch sweep tonight," Number 1 concluded quietly. "No further disclosure is authorized. Status acknowledgment complete. Closing vault circuit."

The line clicked dead.

Qin Zhengguo did not move. He did not set the black handset down. He sat completely frozen in his chair, the dead receiver still pressed beside his ear, staring blankly at the empty air above his desk.

Between the fingers of his left hand, his forgotten cigarette burned steadily downward, turning into a long column of gray ash until the glowing red ember reached the filter and scorched straight into the skin of his knuckles. He did not even flinch from the burn.

Across the smoke-choked study, Zhao Ming stared at his general's ashen, stunned face and the thin wisp of singed skin rising from his motionless fingers, and a cold, bottomless dread gripped the colonel's chest.
