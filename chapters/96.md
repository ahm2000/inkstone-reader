# Chapter 96: Back to the Lazy Life

On a crisp early autumn Tuesday morning, a pearl-white private jet cruised quietly through the high clouds toward Shanghai Hongqiao International Airport.

Inside the warm cabin, Chen Xuan leaned back in a wide leather chair with his worn dark blue canvas bag resting beside his sneakers. On the low wooden table in front of him sat his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos, its cap unscrewed so the clean scent of hot jasmine tea drifted gently through the cabin.

Across the aisle, Number 7 sat upright in a crisp white suit, holding a slim white tablet in both hands.

"Young Master," Number 7 reported in a quiet, respectful voice, "Number 1 sent the morning summary from Beijing and Handong Province just before takeoff. In Jingzhou City and Yunxi County, Secretary Sha Ruijin and Director Hou Liangping have taken full control of the provincial review. Chief Procurator Ji Changming has frozen the remaining shell accounts tied to Shanshui Group."

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea and nodded lightly. "Good. Those upright officials know how to finish a job once the road is cleared. Did anyone in Handong or Beijing trace anything back to me?"

"Not a single trace, Young Master," Number 7 replied smoothly. "Every document passed through blind channels. Even the highest offices in Beijing only refer to the sender as the holder of the Black Order or the anonymous benefactor. No one outside our guard has any clue who you are."

"Keep it that way," Chen Xuan said, stretching his shoulders until his joints popped softly. "What about my parents in Yunxi County?"

"Mr. Chen Jianguo and Mrs. Liu Meifang are both in good spirits, Young Master," Number 7 said with a faint nod. "Acting Director Wei Kun increased regular patrols along Old Street and around Fuxing New Village. Their hardware shop is peaceful, and Mrs. Liu spent yesterday afternoon pickling fresh autumn chilies for you to bring back."

Chen Xuan glanced down at the red plastic bag tucked securely next to his canvas bag. Inside sat a heavy glass jar packed to the brim with bright red pickled chilies floating in clear brine. Just looking at it made him smile.

"My mom always thinks I starve in Shanghai," Chen Xuan murmured, shaking his head fondly. "Anything else on the tablet, Number 7?"

"Only a logistical question, Young Master," Number 7 said, tapping the white screen. "The Shanghai branch of the White Guard has three white sedans ready at the private hangar. Should we arrange a direct escort to Lujiazui, or would you prefer to stop by one of your riverfront residences first?"

Chen Xuan waved his hand immediately. "Cancel the sedans. One plain, unmarked black car is plenty, and have the driver drop me two blocks away from the Hongqiao subway station."

Number 7 paused for half a second, his composed expression flickering with quiet bewilderment. "The subway, Young Master? Rush hour on Line 2 is quite crowded."

"If I ride in a convoy to Lujiazui, the whole office will stare," Chen Xuan said, screwing the metal cap back onto his green thermos. "Besides, if I do not press my thumb on the attendance scanner before 9:00 AM, Supervisor Hu Dewei will dock me 50 yuan."

Number 7 blinked slowly. Across the world, men in tailored suits waited anxiously for a single unsigned instruction from the White Order, and private banks managed accounts worth 300 trillion under Chen Xuan's silent authority. Yet here sat the master of it all, calculating subway transfer times to protect a 50-yuan attendance bonus.

"Young Master," Number 7 ventured carefully, "Hengxin Logistics could be purchased outright before lunch if Supervisor Hu is being difficult."

"And ruin my favorite lazy job?" Chen Xuan laughed, shaking his head. "Where else can I earn 12,000 yuan a month, drink jasmine tea all afternoon, chat with friendly coworkers, and clock out at 4:55 PM on the dot without a single soul asking me to sign a billion-yuan contract? Fifty yuan buys three bowls of scallion oil noodles and two salted duck eggs, Number 7. I am not handing that to Hu Dewei."

A faint, respectful smile touched the corner of Number 7's lips. "Understood, Young Master. I will dismiss the white convoy and have a plain car wait at the south gate."

Twenty minutes later, the jet touched down smoothly on the tarmac at Hongqiao. Outside the cabin door, a cool early autumn breeze brushed across Chen Xuan's face, carrying the faint dry scent of golden ginkgo leaves curling along the airport avenue.

He slung his faded blue canvas bag over one shoulder, lifted the red plastic bag of pickled chilies in his left hand, and tucked his dented green thermos into his right.

At the bottom of the boarding stairs, a silent White Guard driver in a plain gray jacket held open the rear door of an simple black sedan.

"Welcome back to Shanghai, Young Master," the driver said softly, bowing his head.

"Thanks," Chen Xuan replied easily, sliding into the back seat. "Drop me by the corner bakery near the subway entrance. Keep it quiet and go grab yourself a warm breakfast after."

"Yes, Young Master."

Fifteen minutes later, the plain sedan paused briefly beside a row of yellowing ginkgo trees two blocks from Metro Line 2. Chen Xuan hopped out, blended effortlessly into the stream of commuters in light autumn jackets, and headed down the escalator into the station.

On the crowded train rattling toward Lujiazui, nobody gave the relaxed young man in the 20-yuan white shirt a second glance. Leaning casually against the silver handrail with his green thermos and his mother's jar of chilies, Chen Xuan let out a contented breath.

Handong Province was settled, the corrupt tigers were behind bars, and his parents were safe. Now it was finally time to return to his favorite role in the world: an simple, carefree office worker.
