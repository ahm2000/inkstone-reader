# Chapter 96: Back to the Lazy Life

An hour later, that storm still lay out of sight as a pearl-white private jet cruised quietly above the clouds toward Shanghai Hongqiao International Airport.

Inside the warm cabin, Chen Xuan leaned back in a wide leather chair with his worn dark blue canvas bag resting beside his sneakers. On the low wooden table in front of him sat his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos, its cap unscrewed so the clean scent of hot jasmine tea drifted gently through the air.

Across the aisle, Number 7 sat in a relaxed cream knit sweater and dark trousers, holding a slim tablet in both hands.

"Young Master," Number 7 reported in a quiet, respectful voice, "Number 1 sent the morning summary from Beijing and Handong Province just before takeoff. In Jingzhou City and Yunxi County, Secretary Sha Ruijin, Secretary Li Dakang, and Director Hou Liangping have taken full control of the provincial cleanup. The fabricated cases against the old street shops have been formally thrown out, and Shanshui Group's illegal land deals and frozen loans are under full court review."

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of jasmine tea and let out a comfortable sigh. "Good. Once the right people have the real ledgers in front of them, they know how to do their jobs. How are the banks holding up?"

"Completely steady, Young Master," Number 7 replied with a faint smile. "The regional credit lines opened smoothly at 9:00 AM yesterday, and small business owners in Jingzhou are already back to drawing normal payroll loans. The panic is over."

"That is all I wanted to hear," Chen Xuan said, screwing the metal cap back onto his thermos. "Tell Number 1 he can take a breather too. Nobody needs to stay up all night watching spreadsheets anymore."

Number 7 hesitated for a brief second before lowering his tablet. "Young Master, Beijing's financial desk is still trying to figure out who stepped in behind the scenes. Number 1 asked if you have any further instructions for them."

"None at all," Chen Xuan said lazily, stretching his arms behind his head. "Let them focus on fixing the local rules so greedy officials cannot bully honest shopkeepers again. As for me, I have a much bigger crisis waiting in Shanghai."

Number 7 blinked in surprise, sitting up a fraction straighter. "A crisis in Shanghai, Young Master? Should I alert the local team right away?"

Chen Xuan glanced at the small screen of his single regular smartphone and tapped it twice. "Look at the time. It is already 7:25 AM. If we touch down at Hongqiao at 7:50 AM and the morning traffic on Yan'an Elevated Road backs up, I am going to be late for my shift on the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics."

Number 7 stared at him in stunned silence.

"Do you know what happens if I swipe my badge at 9:01 AM?" Chen Xuan asked with complete seriousness. "I lose my 200-yuan perfect attendance bonus for the entire month. That is enough money for four big platters of pork ribs and two bags of good jasmine tea."

Number 7 pressed his lips together to keep from laughing out loud. Only Young Master could steady an entire province's financial turmoil before breakfast and then fret over a 200-yuan attendance bonus like a junior clerk watching every penny.

"Rest assured, Young Master," Number 7 said smoothly. "The flight crew already secured priority landing clearance, and a regular sedan is waiting outside the private gate. You will reach Pudong with at least thirty minutes to spare."

"Now that is what I call good service," Chen Xuan said happily.

Right then, his single smartphone buzzed on the table. A message from Qin Yuhan popped up on the screen.

"Did your branch visit finish early? Do not forget to eat a proper breakfast before going to the office. Also, Grandpa called last night and wants a chess rematch this weekend, so start practicing."

A warm smile spread across Chen Xuan's face. His wife still thought he had spent two days helping a cousin sort out a warehouse dispute outside the city. He tapped out a quick reply promising to bring home fresh lotus root and pork ribs for dinner at Emerald Riverside Villa, then pocketed his phone.

Forty minutes later, the jet touched down smoothly at Hongqiao. Chen Xuan slung his canvas bag over one shoulder, tucked his dented green thermos under his arm, and stepped into the brisk autumn air. Golden ginkgo leaves tumbled across the curb.

He had Number 7 drop him off two blocks from Hengxin Logistics so he could walk the rest of the way like any regular commuter.

At the corner breakfast cart, a plump middle-aged uncle in a faded apron spotted him and grinned brightly. "Hey, young Chen! Missed you the past two days. Two scallion pancakes and a cup of warm soy milk, same as always?"

"You read my mind, Uncle Wang," Chen Xuan laughed, scanning the code on his phone to pay eight yuan. "Make sure you put extra sesame on mine."

"Coming right up! You look rested. Did you finally sneak a proper nap?"

Beside the cart, a young delivery rider in a yellow windbreaker leaned against his electric scooter and chuckled. "Brother Chen, your wife's company just ordered fifty boxes of autumn mooncake gift sets from our depot. Half the drivers are fighting over the route because the tips are great."

"Tell them to drive carefully around the intersection," Chen Xuan teased back, biting into the crispy pancake. "The traffic lights there turn red faster than my manager changes his mind about lunch."

Down the block, seated inside the parked sedan before pulling away, Number 7 watched the cheerful street scene in his rearview mirror. Every time Young Master walked through Shanghai, friendly uncles and easygoing delivery riders seemed to gather around him like old neighbors.

Earlier in the week, Number 7 had run quiet background checks on several of those regulars. Every single file had come back spotless, logical, and airtight. Number 7 shook his head with a faint, puzzled frown and drove quietly into the morning traffic.

At 8:42 AM, Chen Xuan stepped out of the elevator onto the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics, swiped his employee card with a cheerful beep, and walked to his sunny corner desk.

His perfect attendance bonus was safe. He unscrewed his green thermos, took a warm sip of tea, and leaned back in his creaky swivel chair with a contented grin. Nothing beat the quiet joy of a relaxed morning at work.
