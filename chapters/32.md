# Chapter 32: The Verdict on Old Street

At nine on Wednesday morning, in the Party Committee Building's main meeting hall, the provincial team was about to read its first findings aloud, and every leader in the county had come to hear whether they'd still have a chair by lunch.

Gao Zhengyi, the team leader, stood at the front with a thin folder. He did not look at anyone. An inspection team can end a career with one page, so nobody in the hall dared to breathe until he started to speak.

"The team's preliminary findings," Gao Zhengyi said evenly. "One. Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu Wenzhe is placed under formal investigation."

A cold ripple ran through the room!

"Formal investigation" meant the case was officially opened. Lu Wenzhe could not leave the county, could not sign a single paper, and the Discipline Commission could send his file on to the prosecutors if the facts held. He was not in the hall. Everyone knew exactly why.

"Two. The Old Street project is frozen. All eleven buyout contracts are void. The shop owners may reclaim their property."

A murmur broke out and died at once!

Somebody near the back covered his mouth. Eleven families, eleven shutters, and a mall that would now never be built!

"Three. Deputy Police Director Cheng Biao is removed from the police bureau's Party Committee, pending investigation."

That committee was the bureau's real decision core. Without a seat on it, Cheng Biao could still wear the uniform, but he couldn't decide a single thing anymore. His career was over; only the paperwork hadn't caught up yet.

"Four. Hongrui Real Estate is placed under audit. Auditors found a 300-million-yuan private loan from a man named Huang. When the auditors phoned him, a soft voice said the loan had been 'closed' and offered to send the papers. The auditors have not yet decided whether to be relieved."

Nobody laughed.

In Seat One, Fan Weiguo sat very straight. His face showed nothing, but inside he was counting: three years of his signature on approval sheets, and not one yuan in his own pocket. Would that be enough?

In Seat Two, Tao Shu wrote nothing and watched everything. In Seat Three, Kang Guangming gave one small nod, the nod of a man who had said "I told you so" to nobody. In Seat Four, Shao Wenhua studied his notebook.

"Five," Gao Zhengyi said, and turned a page. "Magistrate Tao Shu will lead a county rectification team, a team set up to fix the county's mistakes, to correct the Old Street problems, under the supervision of Secretary Fan Weiguo."

Fan Weiguo's jaw tightened! Was this a rescue, or a leash?

Think about it! He would now oversee the man who wanted his chair, while that man cleaned up the mess made under Fan Weiguo's own signature. It was the politest trap anyone had ever built!

In the corridor afterward, Gao Zhengyi stopped beside him.

"Secretary Fan. The city Party Secretary will talk to you on Friday."

"Talk to me," Fan Weiguo repeated. What would be said, and what would be written down?

In Party language, "to be talked to" sounds like a chat over tea. In practice, it is a recorded warning that stays in your file for the rest of your career. Fan Weiguo kept his seat. For now. He walked down the stairs with the light feeling of a man who has just missed a fall.

By three that afternoon, the Public Security Bureau's auditorium was full, every uniform pressed.

A commendation meeting is not only a ceremony. In the police world, it is also a signal, and everyone sitting in the third row knew how to read it. The first sign was on the platform: next to Director Mai Zhenhua sat Shao Wenhua, the head of the county Organization Department, the office that decides who gets which post.

"Why is he here?!" a young officer whispered. "He never comes to ours!"

"Only one kind of meeting needs a man from the Organization Department," an old inspector whispered back. "Appointments."

Mai Zhenhua stood. "Collective Third-Class Merit to Old Street Police Station, for correcting its own record. Individual Third-Class Merit to Wei Kun."

The hall clapped, loud and warm! Wei Kun walked up, round-faced and stiff, in a freshly ironed uniform.

Merit comes in three classes, and the higher the class, the harder the deed. Third class is the lowest, but it still counts toward promotion, and a man could earn it and still go home to dinner.

Wei Kun had expected a punishment. For the cell, for the forty hours, for everything he had admitted. He had not expected a red certificate. He thought about a paper cup of hot water, which nobody in this hall knew he had poured. Tonight his wife would ask him, as she did every night, whether it was a good day.

He already knew his answer.

"After research and decision by the bureau Party Committee," Mai Zhenhua read on, "Instructor Qiu Guoqiang of Old Street Police Station is named Acting Director of the station."

Another round of applause! Qiu Guoqiang, back from his "training course," rose from the front row and bowed.

But it was not yet real. In a county, a post is not filled the moment it is read aloud. First comes the document. Then comes a three-day public notice, posted on the board, so that anyone with a complaint can speak. Only on Saturday would Qiu Guoqiang truly sit in the Director's chair.

And that chair would open a chain. The station's deputy would move up to Instructor, a squad leader would become deputy, and somewhere in the back row, an auxiliary officer, a contract officer without a gun or promotion, was already hoping his name was on the list.

Mai Zhenhua closed his folder. Shao Wenhua gave him one slow, approving nod.

That was the whole message of the afternoon: the county had decided who would be trusted.

By the end of the day, the count was simple: one official under investigation, eleven contracts void, one project frozen, one company audited, one duty officer who wrote a true time on a form.

A little after six, back in his own office, Tao Shu sat back in his leather chair and let himself smile at last.

He had waited eight years! Tonight he led the rectification team. Fan Weiguo still held the seat, but Tao Shu now held the keys to every file, and a man who holds the files does not need to rush.

In his bottom drawer lay a thick folder labeled with one name: *Fan*. He had been filling it for years. He patted the drawer once, fondly.

A knock sounded. His aide came in with a thin white envelope held in two fingers.

"Magistrate Tao. This came to the front desk this afternoon. There is no return address and no name."

"What is it?"

"A letter, signed by nobody." The aide swallowed. "It mentions you."

Tao Shu's smile stayed on his face. It just stopped meaning anything.
