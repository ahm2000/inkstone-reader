# Chapter 32: Five Pillars Sealed

At thirty minutes past midnight, Yunxi County lost its voice without a single siren sounding.

Across the town's five main offices of power, the takeover happened in the same minute in total quiet. Unmarked black cars pulled up outside the County Party Committee compound, the People's Congress hall, the County Court and Prosecutor's Office, the Public Security Bureau, and the Yunxi Military Garrison. Silent figures in plain black gear and smooth full masks stepped out together. At every gate, a blank black card flashed once before the night duty officer, turning armed guards pale and speechless. Within ninety seconds, every phone line, radio link, and cell tower in Yunxi County stopped letting calls out, and the garrison's own command link to the province went dead at 00:30.

Inside the County Leadership Courtyard, County Magistrate Sun Weiming jolted awake as his wife, Xu Fang, shook his shoulder in the dark.

"Sun Weiming, wake up," Xu Fang whispered sharply, fingers ice-cold on his arm. "The yard lights went out, and the red phone in the study clicked dead."

Sun Weiming sat up, heart pounding. Grabbing a sweater, he hurried into the dark study and snatched up the red private phone. Dead silence.

"Did the switchboard blow a fuse?" Sun Weiming muttered, slamming it down. He grabbed his private cell and tapped speed-dial for the provincial deputy secretary.

No service.

His brow knotted. He pulled out his secret phone kept only for Hongrui Plaza and the provincial capital. Zero bars. He jabbed redial three times, jaw tightening with every failed try.

"He Ping!" Sun Weiming barked, yanking open the study door. "He Ping, get in here! What is wrong with the phone lines?"

Nobody answered.

Sun Weiming hurried down the hall and pulled open the front door. Cold night air hit his face, and his shout died in his throat.

Inside the porch rail, his night guard, He Ping, stood pressed flat against the brick pillar. His rifle hung untouched at his side, his face the color of wet ash. Without speaking, he lifted one shaking finger and pointed toward the steps.

Three paces beyond the iron gate stood two motionless figures in plain black gear and smooth, blank full masks. No badges sat on their chests; no guns were raised. They faced the house in complete stillness behind dark eye shields.

Sun Weiming's knees nearly gave out. He dragged He Ping inside and slammed the door, needing two shaky tries to throw the brass lock.

"Who are they?" Sun Weiming hissed, his whisper cracking. "Province Discipline Inspectors? Military police? Why did you let them inside the yard?"

He Ping swallowed hard. "County Magistrate Sun Weiming, I did not let them in. The main gate guards stepped aside without a word. One of them walked right up, held a black card two inches from my eyes, and told me not to make a sound or step past the rail. My legs just locked up."

"What was on the card?" Sun Weiming demanded, grabbing He Ping's collar. "Speak clearly!"

"Nothing," He Ping whispered, eyes glassy with fear. "No stamp, no office name, just a thin white line that lit up and vanished. County Magistrate Sun Weiming, I served six years in the border guard. Those men are not from the province."

Sun Weiming let go and stumbled back into the study. Behind him, Xu Fang crept to the doorway, clutching her robe. "Sun Weiming, what is happening? Who is on our steps? Are we in trouble?"

"Go back to the bedroom and lock the door," Sun Weiming snapped, his voice shaking. He jammed a cigarette between his lips and clicked his lighter three times before it caught, his trembling fingers dropping gray ash onto his sweater.

"Try the bureau line," Sun Weiming muttered, grabbing the desk phone and pounding Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong's home number.

Not even a dial tone.

Two streets away, inside the Public Security Bureau family block, Chief Liang Guodong was already awake and soaked in cold sweat.

His private police phone had buzzed once at 00:30 with a short message reading network suspended, then went dark. Now Liang Guodong paced his living room in an unbuttoned uniform jacket, one cigarette burning between his fingers while another smoldered forgotten in the ashtray.

By the front door stood his night duty officer, Ma Jun, pale and stiff, one hand hovering near an empty holster.

"Try it again!" Liang Guodong snarled in a raw whisper, shoving his spare satellite phone toward the window. "Hold it against the glass! A satellite phone does not use Yunxi's cell towers!"

Ma Jun pressed the call button against the cold pane and shook his head, lips bloodless. "Still blocked, Chief Liang Guodong. The signal bar will not even search. Every channel is cut off."

Liang Guodong threw the phone onto the sofa. "Cut off? Who has the gear to block a whole county's cell towers, police radios, and satellite phones at once without tripping a single alarm from the province?"

Ma Jun lowered his voice to a breath. "Chief Liang Guodong, I looked down from the stairwell two minutes ago. Two masked men in plain black gear are standing outside our entrance. Before the radio died, the Bureau main gate guard pressed his button for half a second. He said the Military Garrison, the County Court, the Prosecutor's Office, and the Party Committee compound all went dark together."

Liang Guodong froze. His cigarette burned down to the filter and stung his fingers before he flicked it into the ashtray.

"All five pillars," Liang Guodong breathed, all color draining from his face. "Even the Military Garrison? Nobody walks through a garrison gate without a direct order from the very top."

"Chief Liang Guodong," Ma Jun whispered, voice shaking, "what happened tonight in Yunxi? Did Chairman Deng Yongchang's men go too far at the pier? Or is this about the man Old Street Station locked up?"

Liang Guodong's head snapped around, eyes bloodshot with panic. "Keep your mouth shut about Old Street Station! Do not say Deng Yongchang's name or anyone else's name in this room! Not one more word!"

He grabbed his desk phone one last time, slamming the hook up and down in frantic silence, then shoved it away and stared out at the frosted glass. Outside, Yunxi County lay completely still under a dark sky.

Across the sealed county, Sun Weiming in his smoke-filled study and Liang Guodong in his dim living room reached the same cold truth. Nobody in Yunxi could reach the provincial capital, call in favors, or buy even one hour of warning. Whoever stood silently outside their doors had not come to talk, and they had not come from inside the province.
