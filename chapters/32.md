# Chapter 32: The Verdict on Old Street

Wednesday morning. The main meeting hall of the County Party Committee Building.

The provincial inspection team was about to read out its first findings. On the platform sat Gao Zhengyi, the team leader, with the team's two deputy leaders. Below them sat Party Secretary Fan Weiguo, Magistrate Tao Shu, Discipline Commission Secretary Kang Guangming, Organization Department head Shao Wenhua, and the heads of every county bureau.

By Tuesday night, the whole county had guessed the first finding. Nobody had dared to guess the fifth.

Gao Zhengyi did not look at anyone. He opened a thin folder and waited.

After all, one page from an inspection team could end a career. Nobody in the hall dared to breathe.

"The team's preliminary findings are as follows," Gao Zhengyi said evenly. "One. Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu Wenzhe is placed under formal investigation."

Silence.

Then a cold ripple ran through the hall!

"Formal investigation" meant the case was now officially open. Lu Wenzhe could not leave the county and could not sign a single paper. If the facts held, the Discipline Commission would send his file to the prosecutors. His chair in the hall was empty, and everyone knew exactly why.

"Two. The Old Street project is frozen. All eleven buyout contracts are void, and the shop owners may take back their property."

A murmur rose, then died at once.

Eleven families. Eleven shutters. A mall that would now never be built!

"Three. Deputy Police Director Cheng Biao is removed from the Party Committee of the police bureau, pending investigation."

Hearing this, Mai Zhenhua, the county police director, did not move a muscle. The reason was simple: that committee was the bureau's real decision core. Without a seat on it, Cheng Biao could keep his uniform, but he could not decide anything. His career was over. Only the paperwork had not caught up yet.

"Four. Hongrui Real Estate is placed under audit. The auditors found a private loan of 300 million yuan from a man named Huang. When they phoned him, a soft voice said the loan had been closed and offered to send the papers."

Nobody laughed.

To put it plainly, at a clerk's pay of five thousand yuan a month, that loan was five thousand years of work. And the lender had closed it with one phone call. The auditors had not yet decided whether to be relieved.

In Seat One, Fan Weiguo sat very straight. His face showed nothing.

Three years of his signature on approval sheets! Not one yuan in his own pocket. Would that be enough? Would anyone believe it?

In Seat Two, Tao Shu wrote nothing and watched everything. In Seat Three, Kang Guangming gave one small nod, the nod of a man who had said "I told you so" to nobody. In Seat Four, Shao Wenhua studied his notebook.

"Five," Gao Zhengyi said, and turned a page. "Magistrate Tao Shu will lead a county rectification team to correct the Old Street problems, under the supervision of Party Secretary Fan Weiguo."

Fan Weiguo's jaw tightened! Was this a rescue, or a leash?

A rectification team is a team set up to fix a county's own mistakes. Fan Weiguo would now supervise the man who wanted his chair, while that man cleaned up a mess made under Fan Weiguo's own signature. It was the politest trap anyone had ever built!

Half an hour later, in the corridor.

"Five findings in ten minutes..."

"Lu Wenzhe is finished."

"Cheng Biao too..."

"And Secretary Fan? Still in his chair..."

"For now..."

Gao Zhengyi stopped beside Fan Weiguo.

"Secretary Fan. The city Party Secretary will talk to you on Friday."

"Talk to me," Fan Weiguo repeated. What would be said? What would be written down?

In Party language, "to be talked to" sounds like a chat over tea. In practice, it is a recorded warning that stays in your file for the rest of your career. Fan Weiguo kept his seat. For now. He walked down the stairs with the light feeling of a man who has just missed a fall.

---

That afternoon. The big auditorium at the county police bureau.

The hall was full, and every uniform was pressed. On the platform sat Director Mai Zhenhua, the bureau's deputy directors, and one guest nobody had expected: Shao Wenhua, head of the county Organization Department, the office that decides who gets which post.

"Why is he here?!" a young officer whispered. "He never comes to ours!"

"Only one kind of meeting needs a man from that office," an old inspector whispered back. "Appointments."

Mai Zhenhua stood and read from the list, one name after another.

"Collective Third-Class Merit to Old Street Police Station, for correcting its own record. Individual Third-Class Merit to Wei Kun."

The hall applauded, loud and warm! In the second row, a young officer who had ignored Wei Kun in the cafeteria for two straight years clapped the loudest of all.

After all, merit is what moves a police officer up. It comes in three classes, and the higher the class, the harder the deed. Third class is the lowest, but it goes into the file, and a file with merit in it is the one that gets read when posts are filled.

Wei Kun walked up, round-faced and stiff in a freshly ironed uniform.

He had expected a punishment. For the cell, for the forty hours, for everything he had admitted. He had not expected a red certificate. He thought of a paper cup of hot water that nobody in this hall knew he had poured. Tonight his wife would ask him, as she did every night, whether it was a good day.

He already knew his answer.

Seeing him stand there, the whole hall looked at Wei Kun in a new way. Yesterday he was the duty officer who had caused trouble. Today he had merit on his record.

But this was not the main event.

"The bureau Party Committee has met and voted," Mai Zhenhua read on, "Instructor Qiu Guoqiang of Old Street Police Station is named Acting Director of the station."

Another round of applause! Qiu Guoqiang, just back from his "training course," rose from the front row and bowed.

Not yet real, though. Acting means temporary, until the paperwork is final.

In a county, a post is not filled the moment it is read aloud. First comes the written document. Then comes a three-day public notice on the board, so that anyone with a complaint can speak up. Only on Saturday would Qiu Guoqiang truly sit in the Director's chair.

And that chair opened a chain. The station's deputy would move up to Instructor, a squad leader would become deputy, and in the back row an auxiliary officer, a contract officer with no gun and no hope of promotion, was already hoping to hear his own name.

Mai Zhenhua closed his folder. Shao Wenhua gave one slow, approving nod.

That was the whole message of the afternoon: the county had decided whom to trust.

The credit and the blame, counted aloud, came out simply. One official under investigation. Eleven contracts void. One project frozen. One company audited. And one duty officer who had written a true time on a form, who was not punished, but commended in front of the whole bureau.

A little after six, in the magistrate's office.

Tao Shu sat back in his leather chair and let himself smile at last.

He had waited eight years! Tonight he led the rectification team. Fan Weiguo still held the seat, but Tao Shu now held the keys to every file, and a man who holds the files does not need to rush.

In his bottom drawer lay a thick folder with one name on it: *Fan*. He had been filling it for years. He patted the drawer once, fondly.

Knock, knock.

His aide came in, holding a thin white envelope in two fingers.

"Magistrate Tao. This came to the front desk this afternoon. There is no return address and no name."

"What is it?"

"A letter, signed by nobody." The aide swallowed. "It mentions you."

Tao Shu's smile stayed on his face. It just stopped meaning anything.

Who had written it? And who had read it first?
