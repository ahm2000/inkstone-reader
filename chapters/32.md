# Chapter 32: Five Pillars Sealed

At thirty minutes past midnight, Yunxi County lost its voice without a single siren sounding.

Across the town's five centers of authority, the override unfolded in synchronized quiet. Unmarked black vehicles stopped outside the County Party Committee compound, the People's Congress hall, the County Court and Procuratorate complex, the Public Security Bureau, and the Yunxi Military Garrison. Featureless figures in matte-black gear and seamless full masks stepped out together. At every gate, a blank obsidian slate flashed once before the night duty officer, turning armed sentries pale and silent on the spot. Within ninety seconds, every external fiber trunk, microwave relay, and cellular tower serving Yunxi County sealed shut against outgoing traffic.

Inside the County Leadership Courtyard, County Magistrate Sun Weiming jolted awake as his wife, Xu Fang, shook his shoulder in the dark.

"Sun Weiming, wake up," Xu Fang whispered sharply, her fingers cold against his arm. "The courtyard lights went out, and the red phone in the study clicked dead."

Sun Weiming sat upright, his heart thudding against his ribs. He grabbed his wool cardigan and strode into the study without turning on the lamp. He snatched up the red confidential handset and pressed it to his ear. Dead silence.

"Did the switchboard trip a fuse?" Sun Weiming muttered, his voice raspy with sleep and irritation. He slammed the receiver down, grabbed his private mobile, and tapped the speed-dial for the provincial deputy secretary.

The screen flashed once: no service.

His brow furrowed deeply. He tried his unregistered phone, the line he reserved for Hongrui Plaza and the provincial capital. Zero bars. He jabbed redial three times with a stiffening thumb, his jaw tightening with each failed chime.

"He Ping!" Sun Weiming barked, yanking open the study door. "He Ping, get in here right now! What is wrong with the lines?"

Nobody answered.

Sun Weiming hurried down the hallway and pulled the front door open. Cold night air hit his face, and the shout died in his throat.

Just inside the porch rail, his night guard, He Ping, stood pressed flat against the brick pillar. His rifle hung untouched at his side, and his face had turned the color of wet ash. He did not salute or speak. He lifted one trembling finger two inches and pointed toward the steps.

Three paces beyond the iron gate stood two motionless figures in seamless matte-black gear and smooth, expressionless full masks. No badges sat on their chests. No weapons were raised. They faced the house in complete stillness behind opaque dark visors.

Sun Weiming's knees nearly buckled. He dragged He Ping inside by the sleeve and slammed the door shut, throwing the brass latch with shaking hands.

"Who are they?" Sun Weiming hissed, his whisper cracking into a dry wheeze. "Provincial Discipline Inspection? Military police? Why did you let them inside the compound?"

He Ping swallowed hard, his Adam's apple bobbing. "County Magistrate Sun Weiming, I did not let them in. The main gate guards stepped aside without a word. One of them walked up to me, held a black plate two inches from my eyes, and told me not to make a sound or step past the rail. My legs would not move."

"What was on the plate?" Sun Weiming demanded, grabbing He Ping's collar with both hands. "Speak clearly!"

"Nothing," He Ping whispered, his eyes glassy with terror. "No seal, no department name, just a white line that lit up and vanished. County Magistrate Sun Weiming, I served six years in the border guard. Those are not provincial inspectors."

Sun Weiming released him and staggered back into the study. Behind him, Xu Fang crept to the doorway, clutching her robe around her neck. "Sun Weiming, what is happening outside? Who is on our steps?"

"Go back to the bedroom and lock the door," Sun Weiming snapped, his voice trembling despite his effort to sound firm. He grabbed his pack of cigarettes, jammed one between his lips, and clicked his lighter three times before the flame caught. He sucked smoke deep into his lungs, his fingers shaking so badly that ash dropped onto his cardigan.

"Try the bureau line," Sun Weiming muttered, snatching the desk phone and hammering Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong's home extension.

Nothing. Not even a dial tone.

Two streets away inside the Public Security Bureau family block, Chief Liang Guodong was already awake, drenched in cold sweat.

His encrypted police handset had vibrated once at 00:30 with a flat alert reading network suspended, then gone dark. Now Liang Guodong paced his living room in an unbuttoned uniform jacket over his undershirt, a cigarette burning fast between his fingers while another smoldered forgotten in the ashtray.

Beside the front door stood his night duty officer, Ma Jun, pale and rigid, his hand hovering near an empty holster.

"Try it again!" Liang Guodong snarled in a raw whisper, thrusting his spare satellite phone toward the window. "Hold it against the glass! A satellite phone does not run through Yunxi's local towers!"

Ma Jun pressed the call key against the cold pane and shook his head, his lips bloodless. "Still blocked, Chief Liang Guodong. The signal indicator will not even search. It is jammed clean across every band."

Liang Guodong snatched the phone back and hurled it onto the sofa. "Jammed? Who has hardware to jam a county-wide cellular grid, encrypted police radio, and commercial satellite channels at the same minute without tripping an alert from the province?"

Ma Jun lowered his voice until it was barely audible. "Chief Liang Guodong, I looked down from the stairwell window two minutes ago. There are two masked men in matte-black gear standing outside the entrance. Before the radio died completely, the night shift at the Bureau main gate keyed his mic for half a second. He said the Military Garrison, the County Court, the Procuratorate, and the Party Committee compound all went dark together."

Liang Guodong stopped pacing dead in his tracks. The cigarette burned down toward the filter, stinging his skin before he flicked it wildly into the ashtray.

"All five pillars," Liang Guodong breathed, his face draining of color. "The Military Garrison too? Nobody walks into a garrison gate without a central order."

"Chief Liang Guodong," Ma Jun whispered, his voice shaking, "what happened tonight in Yunxi? Did Chairman Deng Yongchang's people cross a line at the pier? Or is this about the man Old Street Station locked up?"

Liang Guodong's head snapped around, eyes bloodshot with sudden panic. "Keep your mouth shut about Old Street Station! Do not say another word about Deng Yongchang or anyone else in this room!"

He grabbed his desk landline one more time, slamming the hook up and down in frantic silence, then shoved it away and stared at the frosted balcony glass. Outside, Yunxi County lay utterly still under a dark sky.

Across the sealed county, both Sun Weiming in his smoke-choked study and Liang Guodong in his dim living room reached the same chilling realization. Nobody in Yunxi could reach the provincial capital. Nobody could call for favors or buy an hour of warning. Whoever stood silently outside their doors had not come to negotiate, and they had not come from inside the province.
