# Chapter 32: Five Pillars Sealed

At thirty minutes past midnight, Yunxi County lost its voice without a single siren sounding.

Across the town's five main offices of power, the takeover happened at the exact same minute in total quiet. Unmarked black cars pulled up outside the County Party Committee compound, the People's Congress hall, the County Court and Prosecutor's Office, the Public Security Bureau, and the Yunxi Military Garrison. Silent figures in plain black gear and smooth full masks stepped out together. At every gate, a blank black card flashed once in front of the night duty officer, turning armed guards pale and speechless on the spot. Within ninety seconds, every phone line, radio link, and cell tower in Yunxi County stopped letting calls go out.

Inside the County Leadership Courtyard, County Magistrate Sun Weiming jolted awake as his wife, Xu Fang, shook his shoulder in the dark.

"Sun Weiming, wake up," Xu Fang whispered sharply, her fingers ice-cold on his arm. "The yard lights went out, and the red phone in the study clicked dead."

Sun Weiming sat straight up, his heart pounding against his ribs. He grabbed his wool sweater and hurried into the study without turning on the lamp. He snatched up the red private phone and pressed it to his ear. Dead silence.

"Did the switchboard blow a fuse?" Sun Weiming muttered, his voice rough with sleep and anger. He slammed the phone down, grabbed his private cell phone, and tapped the speed-dial for the provincial deputy secretary.

The screen flashed once: no service.

His brow knotted tightly. He pulled out his secret phone, the line he kept only for Hongrui Plaza and the provincial capital. Zero bars. He jabbed redial three times with a stiff thumb, his jaw tightening harder with every failed try.

"He Ping!" Sun Weiming barked, yanking open the study door. "He Ping, get in here right now! What is wrong with the phone lines?"

Nobody answered.

Sun Weiming hurried down the hallway and pulled the front door open. Cold night air hit his face, and the shout died right in his throat.

Just inside the porch rail, his night guard, He Ping, stood pressed flat against the brick pillar. His rifle hung untouched at his side, and his face was the color of wet ash. He did not salute or speak. He lifted one shaking finger two inches and pointed toward the steps.

Three paces beyond the iron gate stood two motionless figures in plain black gear and smooth, blank full masks. No badges sat on their chests. No guns were raised. They faced the house in complete stillness behind dark eye shields.

Sun Weiming's knees nearly gave out. He grabbed He Ping by the sleeve, dragged him inside, and slammed the door shut. His hands shook so badly he needed two tries to throw the brass lock.

"Who are they?" Sun Weiming hissed, his whisper cracking into a dry wheeze. "Province Discipline Inspectors? Military police? Why did you let them inside the yard?"

He Ping swallowed hard, his throat bobbing. "County Magistrate Sun Weiming, I did not let them in. The main gate guards stepped aside without saying a word. One of them walked right up to me, held a black card two inches from my eyes, and told me not to make a sound or step past the rail. My legs just locked up."

"What was on the card?" Sun Weiming demanded, grabbing He Ping's collar with both hands. "Speak clearly!"

"Nothing," He Ping whispered, his eyes wide and glassy with fear. "No stamp, no office name, just a thin white line that lit up and vanished. County Magistrate Sun Weiming, I served six years in the border guard. Those men are not from the province."

Sun Weiming let go of him and stumbled back into the study. Behind him, Xu Fang crept to the doorway, clutching her robe tight around her neck. "Sun Weiming, what is happening outside? Who is on our steps? Are we in trouble?"

"Go back to the bedroom and lock the door," Sun Weiming snapped. His voice shook even as he tried to sound firm. He grabbed his pack of cigarettes, jammed one between his lips, and clicked his lighter three times before the flame caught. He pulled smoke deep into his lungs, his fingers trembling so hard that gray ash dropped onto his sweater.

"Try the bureau line," Sun Weiming muttered, snatching up the desk phone and pounding Public Security Bureau Chief Liang Guodong's home number.

Nothing. Not even a dial tone.

Two streets away, inside the Public Security Bureau family block, Chief Liang Guodong was already awake and soaked in cold sweat.

His private police phone had buzzed once at 00:30 with a short message reading network suspended, then gone completely dark. Now Liang Guodong paced his living room in an unbuttoned uniform jacket over his undershirt. One cigarette burned fast between his fingers while another smoldered forgotten in the glass ashtray.

Beside the front door stood his night duty officer, Ma Jun, pale and stiff, one hand hovering near an empty holster.

"Try it again!" Liang Guodong snarled in a raw whisper, shoving his spare satellite phone toward the window. "Hold it right against the glass! A satellite phone does not use Yunxi's local cell towers!"

Ma Jun pressed the call button against the cold pane and shook his head, his lips bloodless. "Still blocked, Chief Liang Guodong. The signal bar will not even search. Every channel is cut clean off."

Liang Guodong snatched the phone back and threw it onto the sofa. "Cut off? Who has the gear to block a whole county's cell towers, private police radios, and satellite phones at the exact same minute without setting off a single alarm from the province?"

Ma Jun lowered his voice until it was barely a breath. "Chief Liang Guodong, I looked down from the stairwell window two minutes ago. There are two masked men in plain black gear standing outside our front entrance. Before the radio died for good, the night guard at the Bureau main gate pressed his radio button for half a second. He said the Military Garrison, the County Court, the Prosecutor's Office, and the Party Committee compound all went dark together."

Liang Guodong stopped pacing dead in his tracks. The cigarette burned all the way down to the filter and stung his fingers before he flicked it wildly into the ashtray.

"All five pillars," Liang Guodong breathed, every drop of blood draining from his face. "Even the Military Garrison? Nobody walks through a garrison gate without a direct order from the very top."

"Chief Liang Guodong," Ma Jun whispered, his voice shaking, "what happened tonight in Yunxi? Did Chairman Deng Yongchang's men go too far down at the pier? Or is this about the man Old Street Station locked up?"

Liang Guodong's head snapped around, his eyes bloodshot with sudden panic. "Keep your mouth shut about Old Street Station! Do not say Deng Yongchang's name or anyone else's name in this room! Not one more word!"

He grabbed his desk phone one more time, slamming the hook up and down in frantic silence, then shoved it away and stared out at the frosted balcony glass. Outside, Yunxi County lay completely still under a dark sky.

Across the sealed county, both Sun Weiming in his smoke-filled study and Liang Guodong in his dim living room reached the exact same cold truth. Nobody in Yunxi could reach the provincial capital. Nobody could call in favors or buy even one hour of warning. Whoever stood silently outside their doors had not come to talk, and they had not come from inside the province.
