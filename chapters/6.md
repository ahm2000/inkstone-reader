# Chapter 6: The Empty Chair

Gong's Noodles was the last light burning on Old Street, and it held a small mystery.

By the window stood a round table with four chairs. Three were used every night. The fourth always stayed empty, with a clean bowl and a pair of chopsticks set before it.

Nobody who walked in knew why.

Gong Yi knew.

He was a thin man in glasses and a flour-dusted apron, looking every bit like a quiet noodle seller who had never raised his voice in his life.

His real designation was Number Twenty-three of the Detail. The entire watch over Yunxi County ran out of the back of his kitchen.

Ke Junjie, the delivery rider who patrolled the lane for the Detail, dropped onto a stool with his helmet in his lap. "Eleven ways. I counted eleven ways to break that man's hand tonight. I gripped my handlebars so hard I nearly bent them."

Tian Cuilian, who ran the front of Gong's Noodles, set down a tray. "Twelve. You forgot the ladle."

"And the Boss slapped Yan Tiehu!" the cook muttered from the stove. "Right in the middle of the lane!"

Gong Yi wiped a cup. "And we stood in the doorway with our bowls and did nothing. The Boss gave no order. That is all the rules allow."

"Does he even worry about it?" Ke Junjie asked.

"Mostly he just eats."

They laughed quietly, and for a moment the kitchen felt warm.

Then Gong Yi raised one finger and flicked a small switch beside the cash register. A faint hiss came from the receiver wired to the directional mic tucked beneath the awning, aimed across the narrow square.

At a corner table outside the barbecue stall, well back from the other diners, Yan Tiehu sat with his collar unbuttoned and a towel slung around his neck. Across the plastic table sat Lei Bo, the young officer from the station's front hall. Niu Dali and Yao Xiaolong sat on the outer stools, chewing skewers and watching the lane.

Yan Tiehu leaned in, keeping his voice low. "Drink up, Officer Lei. Tonight is on Hongrui."

Lei Bo raised his glass with a quiet chuckle. "Yan Tiehu, slapping an old man in front of the whole lane is bold. The street will be talking about that for a year."

"I kept it polite. Only one hand." Yan Tiehu flexed his fingers. "Besides, Director Cheng said no arrests, so I made no arrests. Just a little red paper and paint."

"Paper only," Lei Bo murmured, grinning.

Yan Tiehu slid a red envelope across the tabletop under a stack of napkins. "Five thousand. For your trouble, and for having a bad memory when anyone asks for Yan Tiehu's name."

Lei Bo slipped it into his jacket pocket in one smooth motion. "Memory? What name?"

"Smart." Yan Tiehu nudged a second, thicker envelope across the table. "And this one is ten thousand, for Director Cheng's tea. He says no arrests. Fine. I don't need arrests. I just need the police looking the other way."

Lei Bo tucked the second envelope away and tapped his glass against Yan Tiehu's. "The station is blind in both eyes."

Yan Tiehu's smile faded first. He picked at his teeth with a wooden skewer and lowered his voice even further.

"Now for the old man's family. The sister teaches at the junior high. Tomorrow, someone from the Education Bureau asks her principal to pull her personnel file. The mother walks down six flights of stairs at six every morning, alone, past a dead bulb. I left paint on her door tonight so she knows we have the address. By next Friday she'll be afraid of her own stairwell, and the old man will sign for his brother's sake. No hands needed."

Inside the noodle shop, the cook dropped his ladle, and nobody bent to pick it up.

"One more thing," Yan Tiehu murmured across the table. "That clerk. The nephew who slapped me. When he caught my wrist, for one second I actually paused. Then I looked at him standing there and had to laugh. What can a clerk do?"

"He walked into the station carrying a blanket," Lei Bo whispered, shaking with suppressed laughter. "A blanket! Director Cheng nearly choked laughing."

Inside Gong's Noodles, Ke Junjie's knuckles turned white on his helmet.

"That is the Boss's mother on those stairs," he said tightly. "Let me break one hand. Just one."

"No." Gong Yi tapped the small recorder beside the cash register, its red light glowing steady. "Every word is on tape. Five thousand yuan, one officer, one bribe. That is roughly three weeks of a clerk's pay, and it goes straight into the file."

"Then at least let us guard the stairs," the cook said.

"Two of ours are already watching Building 2. Watching only." Gong Yi looked toward the front door. "We use the file when the Boss gives the word. For now, his order is only to record."

Tian Cuilian sighed softly. "Then let us hope he gives the word soon."

* * *

In the underground garage of Hongrui Real Estate, Dong Haisheng sat in the back seat of his black sedan with the engine shut off. The concrete bay was so quiet he could hear the ticking of his own watch.

His phone lit up on his knee, displaying a number he had never saved and knew by heart: Huang Boren, the private lender known across the county as Chairman Huang, who charged two percent interest a month.

Dong Haisheng answered with both hands. "Chairman Huang."

"Dong Haisheng." The voice was soft, almost warm, like an uncle chatting at a New Year dinner. "Have you eaten?"

"I have, thank you. I was just about to..."

"I heard the Secretary spoke tonight. No more arrests." A brief pause. "That must put you in a tight spot."

Dong Haisheng's mouth went dry.

"My three hundred million costs two percent a month. Do you know what that comes to every single day, Dong Haisheng?"

"Two hundred thousand, Chairman."

"Two hundred thousand. Your driver works four years to earn that much, and you lose it before breakfast." The voice never lost its gentleness. "On the thirtieth, Huaxin Bank reviews your 1.1 billion yuan loan. If construction hasn't started, they take everything. Then I take whatever is left of you."

"The last shops will be cleared by Monday."

"Are they cleared yet, Dong Haisheng?"

"Not yet."

"I heard about a clerk who slapped one of your men in the street. A clerk is nothing, but he made a spectacle in front of the whole block." A soft chuckle came down the line. "Your daughter is in Grade Four at the river school, isn't she? Your driver picks her up at half past four. His brother happens to owe me eight hundred thousand. I only mention it in passing. Deal with the clerk by Monday, Dong Haisheng. If you don't, I will send someone to help you, and you won't care for his methods."

Cold sweat trickled down Dong Haisheng's neck. "No need, Chairman! I will handle it!"

The line clicked dead.

Dong Haisheng stared at his hand. It trembled so badly that the phone rattled against the window glass.

He wiped his face with his sleeve and dialed another number.

"Yan Tiehu. Tomorrow, no arrests. As for the tailor and the cobbler, wear them down. No marks on their faces. And find out everything about that clerk."

"Consider it done, Dong Haisheng!"

He ended the call and sat in the dark car for a long time.

With all his money, why did he feel like the smallest man in the county?

* * *

By three in the morning, the last customer had gone.

Gong Yi stood beside the window table and looked down at the fourth chair. A fresh bowl of noodles had been set there at midnight, and it had long since gone cold.

He cleared it away, washed the bowl, and set a clean one in its place.

"Same tomorrow?" Tian Cuilian asked from the doorway.

"The same."

"Three years, and he has never once sat in that chair. He doesn't even know this shop is ours."

"He will." Gong Yi lined up the chopsticks until they lay perfectly parallel. "An empty chair is a promise. When the Boss finally walks in and sits down, I want the bowl to be hot."

Tian Cuilian smiled.

On the wall behind the counter, a small monitor displayed a single green dot circled by a ring, marking the Boss's position. It wasn't at Fuxing New Village.

It was on the corner of Old Street, right beside a crooked shop shutter.

"He's awake?" Tian Cuilian whispered.

Ke Junjie slipped in through the back door with his helmet tucked under his arm, fresh off watching the lane.

"He brought a screwdriver. Couldn't sleep, I guess. He's tightening the shutter back onto its bolt and humming to himself. Niu Dali shouted from the van, 'That wall is gravel on Monday, clerk. Save your screws.' The Boss just said, 'Evening. It hangs better straight.' Then he went right back to work. Now he's lining up the lock, taking his time."

"Does he look angry?"

"He looks like a man who wants the shutter straight before he goes back to bed."

They watched the green dot in silence, and nobody laughed.

Beneath it on the screen, a single line of text glowed.

*8:30 a.m.: goes to Old Street Station.*

Gong Yi shook his head. "A bench. I wonder what that station will do once it finds out who is sitting on it."
