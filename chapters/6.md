# Chapter 6: The Empty Chair

Yan Tiehu whistled as he steered his black van off Fuxing New Village's side street and onto the outer ring road.

He had left a wet paintbrush on a doorstep, unscrewed the hallway bulb, and taped a strip of red paper to the door. The job had been cheap and clean, and there was not a single arrest for Fan Weiguo to complain about. By morning, that clerk's mother would be jumping at her own shadow on the stairs.

He reached for a cigarette and grinned to himself. Lei Bo, Niu Dali, and Yao Xiaolong were already waiting at the barbecue stall near Old Street for their drinks and red envelopes.

Half a mile down the empty ring road, the overhead streetlamps blinked out all at once.

Yan Tiehu frowned and tapped his dashboard. "Cheap county wiring."

Before the words left his mouth, two plain black sedans slid out of a feeder lane, one gliding smoothly across the asphalt in front of his bumper and the other closing tight behind his rear doors. At the exact same second, a windowless white box truck pulled flush alongside his driver's window, cutting off the shoulder.

There were no sirens, no flashing lights, and not even the sound of a horn.

Yan Tiehu's grin vanished. Instinct screamed through his spine as he slammed the brakes and lunged under his seat for an iron pipe.

His fingers had barely grazed the cold metal when his driver's door was wrenched open from outside.

A gloved hand caught his wrist, twisted it backward until his shoulder popped, and hauled all two hundred pounds of him straight out onto the asphalt. His phone flew from his pocket, skittered across the lane, and died under the heel of a heavy black boot with a crisp crunch.

"Who the hell are you?!" Yan Tiehu roared, kicking wildly. "I'm with Hongrui! Director Cheng Biao is my boss!"

Before he could shout another word, a thick black hood dropped over his head, cutting off both the light and the air. A single heavy blow landed behind his ear, and the road went black.

* * *

When Yan Tiehu woke, he was freezing.

A bucket of ice water crashed over his head and chest, jolting him upright with a choking gasp. Heavy steel chains bit into his wrists and ankles, bolting him to an iron chair in the center of a pitch-black concrete cellar that smelled of rust and damp earth.

No lamp shone. Only a faint gray crack under a steel door twenty feet away showed him he was underground.

"Who is there?!" Yan Tiehu's voice echoed back at him, raw and shaking. "Say a name! If it's money, Dong Haisheng will pay! How much do you want?!"

Only silence answered him in the dark.

Then slow, unhurried footsteps circled behind his chair.

Yan Tiehu jerked against the chains, his breath coming in ragged gasps. In twelve years of breaking kneecaps and scaring shopkeepers on Old Street, he had never once felt this kind of cold crawling up his spine. Street rivals shouted. Police read charges. Men who said nothing at all did not plan on letting you walk out.

A cold length of steel tapped once against the back of his knuckles, right on the hand that had slapped Chen Jianguo's face.

Crack!

Yan Tiehu screamed until his throat tore, pitching forward against the chains while sweat poured down his shaved head.

"Wait! Stop! Who did I offend?!" he shrieked, tears and mucus running down his face. "Was it Old Street? Was it the construction bid in Dongqiao? Tell me a name! Please, just tell me a name!"

Another splash of freezing water hit his face.

Still nobody spoke. Still nobody gave him a name.

In the pitch dark, with his fingers broken and no idea whether the next hour would bring more cold steel or a shallow ditch, Yan Tiehu's twelve years of arrogance collapsed into pure, choking terror. He babbled every rival, every debt, every dirty job he had ever done for Dong Haisheng and Cheng Biao, begging an empty room for mercy.

He never even thought of the quiet office clerk whose father he had slapped two hours ago.

* * *

Back on Old Street, Gong's Noodles was the last light burning in the narrow lane.

By the window stood a round table with four chairs. Three were used every night. The fourth always stayed empty, with a clean bowl and a pair of chopsticks set before it.

Number Twenty-three, thin in his glasses and flour-dusted apron, wiped a teacup behind the counter. Nobody in the Black Pine Guard knew his real name or face, and the entire watch over Yunxi County ran out of the back of his kitchen.

He flicked a small switch beside the cash register. A faint hiss came from the receiver wired to the directional microphone tucked under the awning, aimed across the square toward the barbecue stall.

At a corner table outside the stall, Lei Bo tapped his empty beer glass impatiently while Niu Dali stared at his phone for the fourth time.

"Still nothing?" Lei Bo muttered, his usual smug grin thinning. "He texted twenty minutes ago saying he was turning onto the ring road with the envelopes. Why is he late?"

Niu Dali pressed redial and put the phone to his ear. A flat automated voice answered. *The number you have dialed is powered off.*

"Powered off again," Niu Dali said, his brow furrowing. "Yan Tiehu never turns his phone off. Never."

"Maybe his battery died," Yao Xiaolong offered, though he stopped chewing his skewer.

Just then, Niu Dali's own phone began to ring furiously. He snatched it up. "Hello? Did you find him?"

A frantic voice shouted through the receiver loud enough for both Lei Bo and Yao Xiaolong to hear.

"Niu Dali! Yan Tiehu's black van is sitting out on the outer ring road! The engine is still running, the headlights are on, and the driver's door is wide open! His phone is smashed flat on the asphalt, and there is blood by the wheel!"

Niu Dali's face went dead white. His skewer slipped from his fingers and hit the dirt.

"What about the road cameras?!" Lei Bo lunged across the table, grabbing Niu Dali's wrist. "Call the traffic desk!"

"I already checked through my cousin on night dispatch!" the caller gasped in sheer panic. "The whole stretch of ring road went dark for four minutes! Not a single frame! Yan Tiehu just vanished into thin air!"

Silence hit the corner table like a bucket of ice.

Lei Bo's hand shook so badly he knocked his beer bottle over, foaming across the plastic tablecloth. A man like Yan Tiehu did not get mugged. If someone could shut down county road cameras and make Hongrui's chief enforcer vanish in four minutes flat, then nobody at this table was safe.

"I have to call Dong Haisheng," Niu Dali stammered, his fingers slipping twice on the screen.

Inside Gong's Noodles, Number Fifty-six set his delivery helmet on the counter and let out a slow, cold breath.

"He was taken off the road in four minutes," Number Fifty-six murmured. "He vanished without a word or a trace. Good. That hand touched the Boss's father."

Number Sixty-eight glanced at the glowing monitor behind the counter. "And now let us see how the ones who sent him handle the quiet."

* * *

In the underground garage of Hongrui Real Estate, Dong Haisheng sat in the back seat of his black sedan with the engine shut off.

He had dialed Yan Tiehu's number six times in a row. Six times, the same cold recording told him the phone was powered off.

His pulse drummed in his ears. Two minutes ago, Niu Dali had called in a panic about the idling van on the ring road. Thirty seconds after that, Cheng Biao had called from the police station, his voice tight with sweat and anger.

"Dong Haisheng, who the hell did you people offend?!" Cheng Biao had hissed down the line. "Four minutes of wiped traffic cameras on a county ring road! That isn't a street gang! If Yan Tiehu opens his mouth to whoever took him, both of us are buried before Wednesday!"

Dong Haisheng wiped cold sweat from his forehead with the back of his sleeve. His mind raced in circles, anxiety knotting his stomach. Was it a rival developer from Qingyan City? Was it somebody from the provincial inspection team arriving early in plain clothes? Or was it something to do with Old Street?

He tried to steady his breathing, and right then his phone lit up on his knee.

The number was not saved, and he knew every digit by heart: Huang Boren, the private lender known across the county as Chairman Huang, who charged two percent interest a month.

Dong Haisheng's stomach dropped. He answered with both hands. "Chairman Huang."

"Dong Haisheng." The voice was soft, almost warm, like an uncle chatting at a New Year dinner. "Have you eaten?"

"I have, thank you, Chairman. I was just taking care of Old Street."

"I heard Fan Weiguo spoke tonight and told everyone there would be no more arrests," Huang Boren said after a brief pause. "And I also heard your head enforcer's van was just found empty on the ring road."

Dong Haisheng froze. Cold dread poured down his back. How did Chairman Huang already know?!

"My three hundred million costs two percent a month," Huang Boren continued gently. "Do you know what that comes to every single day, Dong Haisheng?"

"Two hundred thousand, Chairman."

"Two hundred thousand. Your driver works four years to earn that much, and you lose it before breakfast." The voice never rose. "On the thirtieth, Huaxin Bank reviews your 1.1 billion yuan loan. If construction hasn't started, they take everything. Then I take whatever is left of you."

"The last shops will be cleared by Monday, Chairman! I swear it!"

"Are they cleared yet, Dong Haisheng?"

"Not yet."

"Your enforcer vanishes, a clerk slaps him in front of the whole block, and Old Street is still standing." A soft chuckle came down the line. "Your daughter is in Grade Four at the river school, isn't she? Your driver picks her up at half past four. His brother happens to owe me eight hundred thousand. I only mention it in passing. Clear that street and deal with the clerk by Monday, Dong Haisheng. If you don't, I will send someone to help you, and you won't care for his methods."

The line clicked dead.

Dong Haisheng sat paralyzed in the dark sedan, his hand trembling so violently that the phone clattered against the window glass.

He was being squeezed from above by Huang Boren and hunted from the dark by whoever had just made Yan Tiehu disappear without a sound. For the first time in ten years, genuine panic gripped his chest.

He wiped his wet face with his sleeve and dialed Niu Dali with a hoarse, desperate voice.

"Yan Tiehu is gone, so you run the crew starting tomorrow!" Dong Haisheng barked, trying to hide the shake in his voice. "No arrests, Director Cheng won't cover any more arrests. Pay off or lean on the tailor and the cobbler, no marks on their faces! And put every ear you have on the street to find out who the hell took Yan Tiehu!"

"Yes, Dong Haisheng! Right away!"

Dong Haisheng dropped the phone onto the leather seat and stared out at the empty concrete pillars. With all his money, he felt like a rat trapped in a dark room with the walls closing in.

* * *

By three in the morning, the last customer had gone from Gong's Noodles.

Number Twenty-three stood beside the window table and looked down at the fourth chair. A fresh bowl of noodles had been set there at midnight, and it had long since gone cold.

He cleared it away, washed the bowl, and set a clean one in its place.

"Same tomorrow?" Number Sixty-eight asked from the doorway.

"The same."

"Three years, and he has never once sat in that chair. He doesn't even know this shop is ours."

"He will." Number Twenty-three lined up the chopsticks until they lay perfectly parallel. "An empty chair is a promise. When the Boss finally walks in and sits down, I want the bowl to be hot."

Number Sixty-eight smiled.

On the wall behind the counter, a small monitor displayed a single green dot circled by a ring, marking the Boss's position. It was not at Fuxing New Village.

It was on the corner of Old Street, right beside a crooked shop shutter.

"He's awake?" Number Sixty-eight whispered.

Number Fifty-six slipped in through the back door with his helmet tucked under his arm, fresh off watching the lane.

"He brought a screwdriver. Couldn't sleep, I guess. He's tightening the shutter back onto its bolt and humming to himself. Yao Xiaolong was sitting in the parked van looking jumpy as a cat ever since Yan Tiehu disappeared. He shouted out the window, 'That wall is gravel on Monday, clerk. Save your screws.' The Boss didn't even blink. He just said, 'Evening. It hangs better straight.' Then he went right back to lining up the lock."

"Does he look angry?"

"He looks like a man who wants his uncle's shutter straight before he goes back to bed."

They watched the green dot in silence. Somewhere across the county, Yan Tiehu was still chained in a pitch-black cellar, and Dong Haisheng was still staring at a dead phone in a cold garage.

Beneath the green dot on the screen, a single line of text glowed.

*8:30 a.m.: goes to Old Street Station.*

Number Twenty-three shook his head quietly. "A bench. I wonder what that station will do once it finds out who is sitting on it."
