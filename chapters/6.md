# Chapter 6: Thumbprints and Red Paint

Later that night, the shutter of Yu Tailor Shop was pulled halfway down. Four people sat inside around a sewing table.

Yu Changshun, the old tailor, sat at the head.

Meng Xiaofang from the bun stall sat beside him, along with Han Guifu, who had taken over Master Sun's shoe stall forty years earlier.

To everyone's surprise, Kuang Guihua from the vegetable stall was there as well. Only on Thursday, she had urged Chen Mingde to sign.

She scraped a fingernail across the tablecloth. "I was wrong. I said no shop was worth jail. Then Yan Tiehu came by my stall at noon. He mentioned that my grandson leaves the river school at four and walks the path home alone. He said, 'Sign by Monday.' That was all." Her hand trembled. "Tonight I watched a man get slapped for holding a paintbrush, and a son bend down to pick up his father's glasses."

"Yan Tiehu's van is still parked at the corner," Han Guifu muttered. "Niu Dali is out there smoking."

"Let him smoke." Yu Changshun pulled an ink pad from a drawer. "I saw it at 2:03. That driver looked straight at Yan Tiehu and threw himself into the paint cans. Chen Mingde never stepped out from behind his counter. The police pulled up at 2:05. I will tell that to anyone who asks."

He laid a printed form on the table. "This is Hongrui's offer, the paper they want every owner to sign. Three thousand eight hundred yuan a square meter. Market value is eight thousand. Less than half, for forty years of this shop."

Meng Xiaofang pressed her apron to her mouth. "My stall is eleven square meters. Forty-one thousand eight hundred yuan, for the place where I raised two children. I was going to sign on Monday."

She wiped her cheeks. "I'll speak up too. My steamer is my whole livelihood, but I saw what that driver did."

One by one, they pressed their thumbs into the ink and stamped a clean sheet of paper. A thumbprint meant only one thing: I stand by my word.

"Names can come later," Yu Changshun said. "Thumbs first."

Outside, across the lane, a delivery rider sat astride his scooter with his helmet still on. He wasn't going anywhere. He was watching too.

"But who do we even tell?" Han Guifu whispered. "The station belongs to Cheng Biao."

Nobody had an answer.

Yu Changshun spoke slowly. "That young man slapped Yan Tiehu right to his face. In two months, nobody else has dared."

"And Yan Tiehu didn't hit back," Meng Xiaofang said. "Because six men stepped out of Gong's Noodles holding their bowls."

"Lucky timing," Kuang Guihua said. For a brief moment, even Han Guifu managed a smile.

"A clerk who slaps Yan Tiehu with his father on the ground," Yu Changshun said, "is either a fool or a very dangerous man."

"Which one?" Meng Xiaofang asked.

Yu Changshun stared down at the dark thumbprints, and he did not know.

* * *

Back in Room 601, Liu Meifang opened the door and saw her husband's left cheek. She dropped the ladle.

"Who did that?"

"Nobody," Chen Jianguo muttered. "I walked into a door."

"Yan Tiehu, that thug from Hongrui, slapped him." Chen Xuan's voice was quiet, almost flat. "Right in front of the whole street."

"I will kill him!"

Liu Meifang snatched the ladle off the floor and marched toward the door. It took Chen Yuan and Chen Hao together to hold her back.

"Mom, he's twice your size!"

"Then I'll get a bigger ladle!"

Chen Hao laughed, and Wu Lanying let out a shaky laugh through her tears, easing the tension in the room just a little.

Liu Meifang pointed toward the kitchen. "Chen Xuan, cold water and a towel. Go."

Chen Xuan filled a basin, wrung out a towel, and pressed it gently against his father's swollen cheek. His jaw was tight, though his touch stayed light.

Chen Jianguo's voice came out rough. "Sorry, son. You shouldn't have had to see your old father like that."

"Does it hurt, Dad? Tell me the truth."

"Not much."

His father turned his face toward the wall.

Chen Xuan still saw that weathered hand reaching for the paintbrush. A dull ache settled in his chest.

"Well?" Chen Yuan asked from the dining table. "Two hours. Dad got slapped, the station turned you away, and what did you bring back? A thermos."

"The station said to come back tomorrow."

"Tomorrow! Big Brother, you have never won an argument in your life."

"Chen Yuan, enough." Liu Meifang didn't sound angry. She sounded afraid.

"Second Uncle is sitting in a cell, and the only man in this family with a city job says nothing."

"I'm sorry, Chen Yuan," Chen Xuan said evenly. "I just don't want to sit at home and do nothing."

"Don't apologize." Chen Yuan covered her mouth. "That's even worse."

Liu Meifang filled two bowls and set them on the table with a clack. "Two bowls. I am counting."

Chen Xuan ate the first one slowly, knowing his mother was watching. "Best ribs in the county, Mom."

"Can I go to the station with Dad tomorrow?" he asked between spoonfuls.

"To do what?" Chen Yuan snapped. "Yell at them?"

"Sit. Just sit in the lobby, so Second Uncle knows we came."

Chen Jianguo frowned. "Stay home, Chen Xuan. That's grown-up business."

"I'll sit quietly, Dad. I promise."

His father sighed and did not say no.

Chen Xuan glanced at the red welt on his father's cheek. "Dad, what time did he hit you?"

"Ten past eight. Why?"

"I want to remember it right, in case somebody asks." He said it so mildly that Chen Yuan rolled her eyes.

Then three slow knocks sounded on the front door.

Nobody moved. Liu Meifang's hand went straight for the ladle.

Chen Xuan was nearest the door, so he stepped over and opened it. He looked out into the dark stairwell.

The overhead bulb was out, and the stairs were empty.

On the doormat lay a wet red paintbrush. A strip of red paper had been taped to the door with a single character painted on it: *chai*.

Yan Tiehu had not waited for tomorrow.

"Don't touch that!" Liu Meifang cried, clutching her apron.

"It's just paint, Mom. Go sit down," Chen Xuan said evenly.

Without a word more, he picked up the wet brush, dropped it straight into the trash bin, tore the red paper off the door, and turned the deadbolt twice. His face showed nothing at all.

Chen Yuan stared at him, and for once she had nothing to say.

Chen Xuan walked into the bathroom, locked the door behind him, and turned on the tap. Alone in front of the mirror, the mild look left his face completely, and his eyes went ice-cold.

He pulled out the gray phone, bypassed Number One's channel completely, and opened a sealed zero-tier line that had remained dormant for years.

With one steady thumb, Chen Xuan himself activated the Absolute Order.

*Zero Guard. Activate. Lock down Building 2. Yan Tiehu disappears tonight.*
