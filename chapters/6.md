# Chapter 6: The Empty Chair

Behind the closed dark wooden doors of the Fuyuan Garden's private banquet room, the air was warm and smelled of good liquor and tea, and nobody at the round table glanced twice at the one empty chair.

It was 9:40 on Friday night, and the chair belonged to Tao Shu, the County Magistrate, head of Yunxi's government and second only to the man at the head of the table. An hour earlier his aide had called to apologize: the Magistrate had come down with a bad cold.

Fan Weiguo, Yunxi's County Party Secretary and the one man in the room whose word was actually final, didn't believe that cold for a second. Tao Shu had wanted his chair for eight years, and whenever a dinner smelled like trouble to come, Tao Shu's cold arrived right on schedule, leaving Fan Weiguo to sit alone at the table if anything went wrong.

Dong Haisheng, the man who'd won the contract to tear down Old Street, sat at Fan Weiguo's right with an easy smile. Across from him, Cheng Biao perched on the front third of his chair, jacket buttoned to the throat, both hands flat on his knees, and didn't so much as reach for his chopsticks until the Secretary lifted his own. Three full rungs of the government ladder stood between a deputy police director and a county's top leader, and Cheng Biao never once forgot it.

Dong Haisheng stood up first, bending slightly at the waist and holding his small liquor cup in both hands, carefully keeping his rim lower than Fan Weiguo's.

"Secretary Fan, please allow me to offer a respectful toast," Dong Haisheng said, his voice smooth and polite. "Thanks to the county's steady guidance, eleven Old Street shop owners have already signed their buyout agreements. Only three are left, including that corner hardware shop. Without the corner lot, our builders can't even open the mall's front entrance."

"I'm aware," Fan Weiguo said evenly. He raised his teacup half an inch instead of touching his liquor glass. Inside his jacket pocket, his phone lay on silent, and tonight he'd already checked it eleven times.

Seeing the Secretary's tight brow, Cheng Biao straightened his back even further and spoke in the careful, respectful tone of a lower official giving a work report.

"Secretary Fan, please rest assured," Cheng Biao said politely. "Our station has been helping keep order on Old Street. After patient talks and legal reminders, the first eleven shop owners all understood the bigger picture and agreed to sign. The corner hardware owner was brought in today over a minor fight, and I expect he will agree to sign the buyout contract by Monday."

Clink.

Fan Weiguo set his teacup gently onto its saucer.

The sound wasn't loud, yet the temperature in the private room dropped twenty degrees in an instant!

"Eleven," Fan Weiguo repeated, his voice quiet and completely flat.

He didn't raise his volume, and he didn't slam the table. He simply looked across the white tablecloth at Cheng Biao with thirty years of quiet authority behind his eyes.

"Comrade Cheng Biao," Fan Weiguo said softly, "are you telling me that the county police have been using jail cells to force shop owners into signing business contracts?"

Cold sweat broke out along Cheng Biao's collar! He half-rose from his chair at once, his smooth posture falling apart. "No, Secretary Fan, never! Every single case started from a real citizen complaint and followed normal police rules!"

"Sit down," Fan Weiguo said coldly.

Cheng Biao dropped back onto the edge of his seat like a scolded schoolboy.

Fan Weiguo's gentle expression was gone, replaced by an icy stillness that made even Dong Haisheng's stomach tighten.

"Listen to me very carefully," Fan Weiguo said, every word crisp and calm. "The provincial inspection team arrives in Yunxi next Wednesday. Five days from tonight."

Every official in the room felt the weight of those words. The provincial inspection team consisted of senior anti-corruption investigators sent straight from the provincial capital, two full levels above the county, to dig for dirty deals and hear citizen complaints. One serious scandal exposed to them could end a thirty-year career in a single afternoon!

"If even one angry family camps outside the county gate next week," Fan Weiguo continued in that same chilling, quiet voice, "if one old woman breaks down crying in front of those inspectors, I will not be the only person answering questions. Starting tomorrow morning, there will be no more arrests on Old Street. Paperwork only. No pressure, no scenes, nothing an inspector can photograph. Is my order clear?"

"Yes, Secretary Fan! Completely clear!" Cheng Biao answered at once, not daring to meet his eyes.

Fan Weiguo leaned back slowly, hiding the hard thud of his pulse behind a calm face.

Neither man across from him knew the real reason his palms were cold tonight: somebody had already sent a secret complaint letter straight to the Governor's office in the provincial capital! Yesterday an old friend up in the province had quietly warned him that leaders above were already asking questions about Old Street.

Who had sent it? One of the three shopkeepers? A reporter? Or Tao Shu, resting comfortably at home with his convenient cold? Fan Weiguo glanced darkly at the empty fourth chair.

Worse still, the low buyout price wasn't even the real time bomb! To make up the difference between Hongrui's 3,800-yuan offer and Old Street's real 8,000-yuan value, the county had set aside a 640-million-yuan relocation fund, and huge chunks of that money had quietly vanished without reaching any family. Fan Weiguo hadn't pocketed a single yuan himself, he was almost certain of that, yet his signature sat on every approval sheet. The man actually holding the purse strings and running the Old Street office was Lu Wenzhe, the Executive Deputy Magistrate who controlled Yunxi's budget.

"Secretary Fan, please don't worry on our account," Dong Haisheng said respectfully, refilling the Secretary's teacup with both hands. "Hongrui will follow every county order fully. We won't cause a single ripple before Wednesday."

Outwardly, Dong Haisheng looked calm and humble. Inside, his stomach burned with fear! He owed Huaxin Bank 1.1 billion yuan, with a loan review hitting on the thirtieth. If construction hadn't started by then, the bank could take everything he owned. Even worse, he'd secretly borrowed another 300 million yuan from a shadowy private lender called Chairman Huang, who'd already phoned three times today, never shouting, only asking gently: "Is the corner cleared yet, Boss Dong?"

Trying carefully to ease the heavy silence, Dong Haisheng added in a light, cautious tone, "Actually, Secretary, I don't expect any trouble from that hardware family. His older brother is just a retired factory worker, and his nephew is an ordinary clerk at a small logistics company in Qingyan City. We heard the nephew is coming back tomorrow morning to ask questions at the station."

Cheng Biao seized the chance to reassure his boss, offering a restrained, dutiful nod. "Just a small company clerk, Secretary Fan. We'll receive him politely and handle everything strictly by the book."

Inside, Cheng Biao sneered. A logistics clerk from the city thought he could walk into Old Street Station and lecture him? They knew exactly how to wear down ordinary clerks!

Fan Weiguo didn't answer. He pulled out his phone and stared down at the calendar screen, where next Wednesday at nine o'clock glowed circled in red.

Five days. Thirty years in government, survived by never taking one careless step. Yet tonight, for a reason he couldn't quite name, he felt like a man standing on a frozen river, listening to the ice quietly crack right under his shoes.
