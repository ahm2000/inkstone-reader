# Chapter 6: Landing in the Dark

The clouds over the southern hills broke apart ten minutes before touchdown, revealing a dark valley stitched together by thin ribbons of sodium-orange highway lamps. Through the oval window of the Gulfstream cabin, Chen Xuan watched the ridges of Lanshui and Yunxi rise out of the mist like the backs of sleeping beasts. Forty kilometers to the east lay Yunxi County, tucked against the bend of the river where his parents had lived for thirty years.

Inside the cabin, the engines shifted to a lower hum as the landing gear locked beneath the fuselage. Chen Xuan sat in the second row with his hands folded loosely in his lap, his expression as calm as still rainwater. Across the aisle, Number Seven sat upright with an encrypted tablet resting dark against his knee, while Number Nine stood near the forward bulkhead checking the door seal. Neither man spoke or glanced unnecessarily at the other. Inside the Black Pine Guard, faces were forgotten by habit, names did not exist, and no operative knew the true identity of the man beside him.

The tires kissed the wet concrete of Lanshui-Yunxi Regional Airfield with barely a tremor. Reverse thrusters roared softly, then faded as the jet taxied away from the small darkened civilian terminal toward the isolated western freight apron. A light drizzle had left the tarmac slick and black, mirroring the yellow wingtip lights in trembling streaks.

When the cabin door swung down and the airstairs unfolded onto the damp asphalt, cool mountain air rushed inside carrying the scent of wet pine needles and diesel. Chen Xuan rose without haste, folded his tailored dark wool coat neatly across the armrest, and took the faded gray cotton jacket, scuffed canvas shoulder bag, and dented dark-green steel thermos offered by Number Seven. In three quiet seconds, the man who had just crossed six hundred kilometers in a private jet vanished, leaving behind an ordinary twenty-four-year-old junior clerk returning home on a Friday night.

He descended the airstairs into the cool air. At the foot of the steps stood a lean man in a plain dark windbreaker beside an understated black domestic sedan with dusty wheel wells and local Yunxi plates. The sedan looked indistinguishable from the hundreds of aging county cars parked outside local offices and tea houses across the prefecture.

"Boss," Number Fourteen said quietly, bowing his head a fraction of an inch. As the Yunxi local branch coordinator, his voice was flat, crisp, and devoid of wasted emotion. "The runway is cleared and the flight log is sealed under routine provincial charter traffic. No local aviation staff saw the passenger manifest."

"Good," Chen Xuan replied, his voice low and unhurried.

Before Number Fourteen could continue, a dull, rhythmic rumble drifted across the far side of the western apron. Chen Xuan turned his gaze toward the iron freight gate two hundred meters away. One after another, six unmarked dark vans rolled silently out through the open gate onto the county feeder road. Their headlights stayed dipped low against the wet asphalt, their windows tinted dark, moving in tight, disciplined spacing toward Yunxi County. Inside those six vehicles sat Black Pine technical analysts, forensic accountants, legal-archive specialists, and field operatives already deployed on the order Chen Xuan had given from the air.

Chen Xuan watched the red taillights of the sixth van disappear around the bend of the poplar trees before looking back at Number Fourteen. "Give me the ground report."

"Second Uncle Chen Mingde is being held in Holding Room Three at Old Street Station," Number Fourteen said immediately, speaking in clean, measured sentences. "Our embedded operative, Number Fifty-six, is already positioned on the night shift inside that corridor. Chen Mingde is unharmed. His physical safety is guaranteed. Half an hour ago, Number Fifty-six ensured he received warm water and a clean blanket without drawing attention from the duty sergeant. No one inside Old Street Station will be permitted to raise a hand against him."

A faint breath left Chen Xuan's lips in the cool mountain air. "And the case file?"

"Already being dismantled," Number Fourteen answered. "The assault charge filed against Chen Mingde rests on two pieces of manufactured evidence: a hospital injury report claiming a fractured rib and mild concussion for a Hongrui Real Estate foreman, and a missing forty-minute gap in the municipal camera feed at the north mouth of Old Street. Our technical unit pulled the raw digital audit trail from Yunxi People's Hospital twenty minutes ago. The X-ray film attached to the report belongs to a traffic accident patient from three months ago, and the attending physician's stamp was logged after clinic hours. We are tracing the physician's bank transfers and phone records right now."

"What about the street camera?" Chen Xuan asked.

"The municipal feed was manually looped from a maintenance terminal, but two private dashcams parked near the tobacco stall and a bank ATM lens eighty meters up the lane captured the entire altercation clearly," Number Fourteen said. "The footage shows three men from Hongrui Real Estate entering Mingde Hardware first, smashing the glass counter, and shoving Chen Mingde into the display rack before claiming he struck them with a wrench. Our field team has already secured verified copies of both recordings."

Chen Xuan looked out across the dark runway, where the wind stirred ripples across thin puddles. "Keep the lid tight," he said calmly. "Do not release the evidence to Old Street Station tonight. If we hand a clean exoneration to Station Director Cheng Biao right now, he will simply drop the charge, apologize with a smile, and bury the people who paid him to sign the detention order."

"Understood, Boss," Number Fourteen said. "Number One sent the same assessment over encrypted comms five minutes ago. We are pulling the full chain behind Hongrui Real Estate, Old Street's demolition bidding, and Cheng Biao's private accounts before we move. Meanwhile, Number Twenty-three and a six-man watch team are already stationed across from Mingde Hardware on Old Street."

"Make sure Chen Mingde rests comfortably tonight," Chen Xuan said. "If anyone tries to pressure him into signing a confession or a demolition consent form inside that holding room, intervene quietly and record every word."

"Already arranged, Boss. Number Fifty-six has audio coverage on the holding corridor."

Number Seven and Number Nine stepped down the airstairs behind Chen Xuan carrying two compact aluminum cases, turning smoothly toward a dark utility vehicle waiting near the hangar shadow to establish the local relay post. Chen Xuan opened the rear door of the dusty black sedan himself, set his green thermos on the cloth seat, and slid inside.

Number Fourteen closed the door softly, took the driver's seat, and eased the sedan away from the apron without high beams. The airfield shrank into the rearview mirror, swallowed by dark hills and wet bamboo.

As the sedan turned onto the narrow road leading into Yunxi County, the faint yellow glow of the small town rose over the distant treeline. Chen Xuan rested his elbow on the window ledge, his fingers resting lightly against the cool painted metal of his thermos. Tomorrow morning, the people behind Hongrui Real Estate and Old Street Station would wake up believing Yunxi County still belonged to them. Tonight, Chen Xuan simply needed to walk up six flights of stairs and drink a bowl of his mother's soup.
