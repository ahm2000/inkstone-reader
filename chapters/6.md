# Chapter 6: Landing in the Dark

The private jet's landing gear locked with a soft thud as the plane came down toward Sha-Yunxi Regional Airfield.

Inside the cabin, Chen Xuan sat with his hands resting loosely in his lap, his face calm and still. Across the aisle, Number 7 put down his locked tablet. Near the front wall, Number 9 stood with one hand braced lightly against the door latch. Neither man looked at the other.

"How long until the cargo tarmac?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"Four minutes," Number 7 replied evenly. "Number 14 confirmed the west gate is clear. All six vans are lined up and fueled."

"And the flight log?"

"Filed as a normal local charter flight. No airport staff saw the passenger list, and the tower chief ended his shift twenty minutes ago." Number 7 lifted his eyes briefly. "Number 1 also sent a note from Shanghai. He suggests holding all proof back until we map the whole money trail."

A cold stillness settled in Chen Xuan's eyes. "Tell him I agree. Pulling one weed only teaches the roots to grow deeper."

The tires touched the wet runway with barely a bump as the jet rolled onto the quiet west tarmac.

When the cabin door opened into the cool mountain air, Chen Xuan stood up without rushing and folded his dark wool coat over the armrest. Number 7 stepped forward holding a faded gray cotton jacket, a worn canvas shoulder bag, and a dented dark-green steel thermos. In three quiet seconds, the man who had flown six hundred kilometers on a private jet was gone. In his place stood a quiet twenty-four-year-old office clerk coming home on a Friday night.

At the bottom of the stairs stood Number 14 in a plain dark jacket beside a dusty black sedan with local Yunxi plates. He bowed his head a tiny fraction.

"The runway is sealed," Number 14 said cleanly. "Local control logged an empty cargo flight. Nobody in the terminal saw you get off."

"Good," Chen Xuan answered, looking past him toward the iron cargo gate two hundred meters away.

A low rumble carried across the tarmac. Six unmarked dark vans rolled through the open gate onto the side road, headlights dipped low, moving in a tight line toward Yunxi County.

"All six teams are moving?" Chen Xuan asked as the last red taillights disappeared.

"Yes," Number 14 said. "Tech experts, money checkers, legal file trackers, and field men. Each van has its own area assigned. They will be active in Yunxi within twenty-five minutes."

Chen Xuan turned back and met Number 14's eyes. "Start with my Second Uncle. How is he doing?"

Number 14 stood a little straighter. "Chen Mingde is in Holding Room Three at Old Street Station. Our man, Number 56, is on night duty in that hall. Chen Mingde has not been hurt."

"Is he scared?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"Shaken, and mostly worried about his wife," Number 14 replied. "Two hours ago, a patrol officer pushed a blank statement sheet across the table and told him to sign it if he wanted to go home before morning. Chen Mingde refused. His hands were shaking, yet he kept repeating that he never hit anyone. Thirty minutes ago, Number 56 brought him warm water and a clean blanket without drawing attention from the sergeant on duty. No one inside that station will lay a hand on him."

A faint breath left Chen Xuan's lips, and the slight tension along his jaw eased. "And the case file?"

"Already falling apart," Number 14 said, a dry edge entering his flat voice. "The hold rests on two things: a Yunxi People's Hospital injury report claiming a cracked rib and mild head injury for a Hongrui Real Estate foreman, and a forty-minute gap in the street camera video at the north end of Old Street."

"Start with the hospital," Chen Xuan said.

"Our tech team pulled the raw computer log twenty minutes ago. The X-ray file belongs to a car crash patient treated three months ago, and the doctor's digital stamp was entered forty minutes after the clinic closed."

Chen Xuan's eyes narrowed. "Paid off, or forced?"

"We are checking his bank transfers and call logs now," Number 14 answered. "Early signs point to a payment sent through a relative's account two weeks ago, plus three calls from Hongrui Real Estate's office manager. We will have the exact amount before midnight."

"And the street camera?"

"Looped by hand from a county repair computer," Number 14 said. "They missed two parked car cameras near the tobacco stall and a bank ATM camera eighty meters up the lane. Both views are clear. Three men from Hongrui Real Estate walked into Mingde Hardware first, smashed the glass counter, and shoved Chen Mingde into the shelves before claiming he swung a wrench. Our team saved clean copies of every frame."

Chen Xuan stood silent for two seconds, his fingers resting on his steel thermos.

"Do not hand any of it to Old Street Station tonight," Chen Xuan said calmly.

Number 14 did not blink. "Keep the lid shut."

"If we drop clean proof on Station Director Cheng Biao's desk tonight, he smiles, blames a junior clerk for a paperwork mistake, signs a release slip, and warns his backers before the ink dries," Chen Xuan said, his voice level and cold. "By noon tomorrow, the hospital records disappear and the people behind Hongrui Real Estate hide behind shell companies."

"Understood," Number 14 said crisply. "Number 1 sent the same view five minutes ago. We are pulling the full chain behind Hongrui Real Estate, Old Street's tear-down bids, and Cheng Biao's private bank accounts before we strike. Number 23 and a six-man watch team are already across from Mingde Hardware."

"Keep Number 56 watching with live sound in that holding hall," Chen Xuan said. "If anyone tries to push Chen Mingde into signing a confession or a move-out paper, step in quietly and record every word."

"Already set. Every word in that room is being recorded."

Behind them, Number 7 and Number 9 walked down the stairs carrying two small metal cases and headed smoothly toward a dark van to set up the local signal post. Chen Xuan opened the back door of the dusty sedan, placed his thermos on the seat, and climbed inside.

Number 14 took the wheel and drove off without turning on the high beams.

"One more thing," Number 14 said quietly, glancing at the mirror. "Your father, Chen Jianguo, rode his electric scooter to Old Street Station four hours ago with hot tea and a sweater. They refused to let him past the gate. He stood outside for an hour before riding home."

Chen Xuan's hand stopped still on the thermos lid, his dark eyes going flat.

"He is at Fuxing New Village now?" Chen Xuan asked.

"Confirmed. Our watch team made sure no Hongrui Real Estate lookouts followed him."

"Drive straight there," Chen Xuan said quietly. "Stop two blocks south of the gate."

Number 14 nodded once. Tomorrow morning, the men behind Hongrui Real Estate and Old Street Station would wake up thinking Yunxi County still belonged to them. Tonight, Chen Xuan only needed to walk up six flights of stairs and drink a bowl of his mother's soup.
