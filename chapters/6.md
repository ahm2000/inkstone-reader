# Chapter 6: The Empty Chair

In a private banquet room at Fuyuan Garden, the round table had four chairs, and one of them stayed empty all evening.

Nobody looked at it.

It belonged to Tao Shu, the County Magistrate.

The Magistrate runs the county government, and only the Party Secretary ranks above him.

An hour earlier, his aide had called to apologize: the Magistrate had come down with a bad cold.

"A cold," Fan Weiguo said, turning his teacup slowly. "What a pity."

Fan Weiguo was Yunxi's County Party Secretary, and his word was final. He did not believe that cold for a second.

After all, Tao Shu had wanted the top chair for eight years. Whenever a dinner smelled like trouble, his cold arrived right on time.

If anything went wrong tonight, Fan Weiguo would sit with it alone!

At his right sat Dong Haisheng, the man who had won the contract to tear down Old Street.

He smiled easily and ate slowly.

Across from him, Cheng Biao perched on the front third of his chair. His jacket was buttoned to the throat, and he did not touch his chopsticks until the Secretary lifted his own.

Why so careful? The reason was simple: three full rungs of the government ladder stood between a deputy police director and the top leader of a county. Cheng Biao never forgot it, not for one minute.

Dong Haisheng stood up first. He held his small cup in both hands and kept its rim lower than the Secretary's.

A thick gold watch slid down his wrist as he bowed.

It caught the light just long enough for everyone to read the brand.

Cheng Biao noticed it and said nothing. Real estate money could buy watches and toasts, but it could not buy a phone call that got answered on a Sunday morning.

Everyone at this table knew whose could.

"Secretary Fan, allow me to offer a respectful toast," Dong Haisheng said smoothly.

"Thanks to the county's guidance, eleven Old Street shop owners have signed their buyout agreements. Only three are left, including the corner hardware shop."

"Without that corner lot, our builders cannot even open the mall's front entrance!"

"I'm aware," Fan Weiguo said evenly.

He raised his teacup half an inch instead of touching his liquor glass. His phone lay on silent in his jacket pocket, and tonight he had already checked it eleven times.

Seeing the Secretary's tight brow, Cheng Biao sat even straighter. He spoke like a lower official giving a work report.

"Secretary Fan, please rest assured," Cheng Biao said politely. "Our station has helped keep order on Old Street."

"After patient talks and legal reminders, the first eleven owners understood the bigger picture and agreed to sign. The corner hardware owner was brought in today over a minor fight, and I expect his signature by Monday."

Clink.

Fan Weiguo set his teacup on its saucer, gently.

The sound was not loud. Yet the room seemed to lose twenty degrees at once!

"Eleven," Fan Weiguo repeated.

Dong Haisheng opened his mouth. "Secretary, I..."

"Not you, Haisheng."

His voice was quiet and completely flat. He did not slam the table.

He simply looked across the white tablecloth at Cheng Biao, with thirty years of authority behind his eyes.

"Comrade Cheng Biao," he said softly, "are you telling me the county police have been using jail cells to force shop owners to sign business contracts?"

Cold sweat broke out along Cheng Biao's collar! He half rose from his chair, and his smooth posture fell apart.

"No, Secretary Fan, never!" he said. "Every case began with a real citizen complaint and followed normal police rules!"

"Every case?" Fan Weiguo asked.

"Every case, Secretary," Cheng Biao said, and his voice cracked on the last word.

"Sit down," Fan Weiguo said coldly.

"Yes, Secretary," Cheng Biao whispered, and dropped back onto the edge of his seat.

Fan Weiguo's gentle look was gone. Even Dong Haisheng's stomach tightened!

"Listen carefully," Fan Weiguo said, every word crisp and calm. "The provincial inspection team arrives in Yunxi next Wednesday. Five days from tonight."

"Five days?" Dong Haisheng echoed in a thin voice.

Silence.

Every official in the room felt the weight of it.

To put it simply, the inspection team is a group of senior anti-corruption investigators sent from the provincial capital, two full levels above the county. They come to dig for dirty deals and to hear citizens' complaints. One serious scandal in front of them could end a thirty-year career in an afternoon!

"If one angry family camps outside the county gate next week," Fan Weiguo went on, "if one old woman breaks down crying in front of those inspectors, I will not be the only person answering questions."

He paused, and nobody breathed.

"Starting tomorrow morning, there will be no more arrests on Old Street. Paperwork only. No pressure, no scenes, nothing an inspector can photograph."

"Is my order clear?"

"Yes, Secretary Fan! Completely clear!" Cheng Biao answered at once.

He did not dare to meet the Secretary's eyes.

Fan Weiguo leaned back slowly. His face stayed calm, though his pulse thudded hard.

Neither man across from him knew the real reason his palms were cold tonight.

Somebody had sent a secret complaint letter straight to the Governor's office in the provincial capital! Yesterday an old friend up in the province had quietly warned him that leaders above were already asking about Old Street.

Who had sent it? One of the three shopkeepers? A reporter?

Or Tao Shu, resting at home with his convenient cold?

Fan Weiguo glanced darkly at the empty chair.

Worse still, the low buyout price was not even the real time bomb!

Hongrui had offered thirty-eight hundred yuan, while Old Street was truly worth eight thousand. To close the gap, the county had set aside a relocation fund of six hundred forty million yuan, and huge chunks of it had quietly vanished before reaching any family.

Fan Weiguo had not pocketed a single yuan himself, he was almost certain of that. Yet his signature sat on every approval sheet.

The man actually holding the purse was Lu Wenzhe, the Executive Deputy Magistrate, who controlled Yunxi's budget and ran the Old Street office.

"Secretary Fan, please don't worry on our account," Dong Haisheng said, refilling the Secretary's teacup with both hands. "Hongrui will follow every county order. We won't cause a ripple before Wednesday."

"Good," Fan Weiguo said, and looked away.

He looked calm and humble.

Inside, his stomach burned with fear! No more arrests meant no more pressure, and without pressure, who would clear that corner?

He owed Huaxin Bank one point one billion yuan, and the loan review fell on the thirtieth. If construction had not started by then, the bank could take everything he owned!

Even worse, he had secretly borrowed another three hundred million from a private lender called Chairman Huang.

Chairman Huang had phoned three times today. He never shouted. He only asked, gently, "Is the corner cleared yet, Boss Dong?"

Trying to ease the heavy silence, Dong Haisheng added in a light tone, "Actually, Secretary, I expect no trouble from that hardware family."

"Why not?" Fan Weiguo asked, without looking up.

"His older brother is a retired factory worker. His nephew is an ordinary clerk at a small logistics company in Qingyan City."

"We heard the nephew is coming to the station tomorrow morning to ask questions."

Cheng Biao seized the chance. He gave a small, dutiful nod.

"Just a small company clerk, Secretary Fan. We will receive him politely and handle everything strictly by the book."

Inside, Cheng Biao sneered. A clerk from the city thought he could walk into Old Street Station and lecture him?

They knew exactly how to wear down ordinary clerks!

"Eat," Fan Weiguo said at last. "The food is getting cold."

Neither man touched a dish.

Fan Weiguo took out his phone and stared at the calendar screen. Next Wednesday, nine o'clock, glowed in a red circle.

Five days.

Thirty years in government, and he had survived by never taking one careless step. So why, tonight, did he feel the ice crack under his shoes?
