# Chapter 6: The Empty Chair

Gong's Noodles was the last light on Old Street, and it held a small mystery.

By the window stood a round table with four chairs. Three were used every night. The fourth was always empty, with a clean bowl and a pair of chopsticks in front of it.

Nobody who walked in knew why.

Gong Yi knew.

He was a thin man in glasses and a flour-dusted apron. He looked like a quiet noodle seller who had never raised his voice in his life.

His real title was Number Twenty-three of the Detail. The Detail's whole watch over Yunxi ran from the back of his kitchen.

Ke Junjie, a delivery rider who watched the lane for the Detail, dropped onto a stool with his helmet in his lap. "Eleven ways. I counted eleven ways to break that man's hand tonight. I held my handlebars so hard that I bent them."

Tian Cuilian, the woman at Gong's Noodles everyone called Auntie Tian, put down a tray. "Twelve. You forgot the ladle."

"And the Boss slapped Yan Tiehu!" cried the cook, whose nickname was Big Pot. "Right in the lane!"

Gong Yi wiped a cup. "And we stood in the door with our bowls and did nothing. The Boss gave no order. That is all the rules allow."

"Does he think?" Ke Junjie asked.

"Mostly he just eats."

They laughed quietly, and for a moment the kitchen felt warm.

Then Gong Yi raised one finger.

Through the open window came a man's loud, happy voice. It came from the barbecue stall across the little square.

"Drink, Officer Lei! Tonight, all on Hongrui!"

Yan Tiehu sat there with his shirt open and a towel on his neck. Across the table sat Lei Bo, the young officer from the front hall. Beside them, Niu Dali and Yao Xiaolong chewed skewers.

Lei Bo raised his glass. "Brother Tie, you are a hero! Slapping an old man in front of the whole lane! The street will talk for a year."

"I did it politely. Only one hand." Yan Tiehu held it up and flexed it. "Besides, Director Cheng said no arrests, so I made no arrests. I only put up a little paper and paint."

"Ha! Paper only!"

Yan Tiehu tossed a red envelope across the table.

"Five thousand. That's for your trouble, brother. And for your bad memory about Brother Tie's name."

Lei Bo pocketed it fast.

"Memory? What name?"

"Good boy. And this one is ten thousand, for Director Cheng's tea." Yan Tiehu slid out a second envelope, thicker than the first. "He says no arrests. Fine. I don't need arrests. I need the police blind."

Lei Bo grinned. "The station is blind in both eyes, Brother Tie."

The stall shook with laughter, and a drunk customer at the next table clapped.

Yan Tiehu stopped laughing first.

He picked his teeth with a skewer. "Now the old man's family. The sister teaches at the junior high. Tomorrow a man from the Education Bureau asks her principal for her file. The mother goes down six floors at six every morning, alone, past a dead bulb. I painted her door tonight so she knows we have the address. By next Friday she will be afraid of her own stairs, and the old man will sign for his brother. No hands needed."

In the noodle shop, the cook dropped his ladle, and nobody picked it up.

"Another thing." Yan Tiehu leaned in. "That clerk. The nephew who slapped me. I felt that wrist, and for one second I thought, hmm. Then I looked at the street, and I laughed. What can a clerk do?"

"He came to the station with a blanket!" Lei Bo said. "A blanket! The Director nearly died laughing."

The whole table roared.

In the noodle shop, Ke Junjie's knuckles went white on his helmet.

"That is the Boss's mother on those stairs," he said. "Let me break one hand. Only one."

"No." Gong Yi tapped a small recorder next to the cash register. Its red light glowed. "Every word is saved. Five thousand yuan, one officer, one bribe. That is about three weeks of a clerk's pay, and it will go in the file."

"Then at least let us guard the stairs," the cook said.

"Two of ours already watch Building 2. Watching only." Gong Yi looked at the door. "We use the file when the Boss says so. For now he has told us only to record."

Tian Cuilian sighed. "Then let us hope he says it soon."

* * *

In the underground garage of Hongrui Real Estate, Dong Haisheng sat in the back seat of his black car with the engine off. It was so quiet that he could hear his own watch.

Now his phone lit up on his knee, with a name he had never saved and always knew: Huang Boren, whom everyone called Chairman Huang. He was a loan shark who charged two percent a month.

He answered with both hands.

"Chairman Huang."

"Boss Dong." The voice was soft, almost kind, like an uncle's at a New Year table. "Have you eaten?"

"I have, thank you. I."

"I heard the Secretary spoke tonight. No more arrests." A small pause. "That must be hard on you."

Dong Haisheng's mouth was dry.

"My three hundred million costs two percent a month. Do you know how much that is each day, Boss Dong?"

"Two hundred thousand, Chairman."

"Two hundred thousand. Your driver needs four years to earn that. You lose it before breakfast." The voice stayed gentle. "On the thirtieth, Huaxin Bank reviews your 1.1 billion yuan loan. If you haven't started building, they take everything. Then I take what is left of you."

"The last shops will be cleared by Monday."

"Are they cleared yet, Boss Dong?"

"Not yet."

"I hear about a clerk who slapped a man in the street. A clerk is small. But he made a scene in front of the whole street." A soft laugh. "Your girl is in Grade Four at the river school. Your driver brings her home at half past four. His brother owes me eight hundred thousand. I only mention it. Deal with the clerk by Monday, Boss Dong. If you don't, I will send someone to help you. You will not like his help."

Sweat ran down Dong Haisheng's neck.

"No need, Chairman! I will handle it!"

The line clicked.

Dong Haisheng stared at his hand. It shook so hard that the phone rattled against the window.

He wiped his face with his sleeve and dialed another number.

"Tie. Tomorrow, no arrests. But the tailor and the cobbler? Make them tired. No marks on faces. And find out who that clerk is."

"Boss Dong, you got it!"

He hung up and sat silent for a long time.

Why did he feel like the smallest man in the car, with all his money?

* * *

By three in the morning, the last customer had gone.

Gong Yi stood at the window table and looked at the fourth chair. A fresh bowl of noodles had gone on it at midnight, and it was cold by now.

He took it away, washed it, and laid down a clean one.

"Same tomorrow?" Tian Cuilian asked from the doorway.

"The same."

"Three years, and he has never sat in that chair. He doesn't even know the shop is ours."

"He will." Gong Yi straightened the chopsticks until they lay side by side. "A chair that stays empty is a promise. When the Boss finally comes and sits, I want the bowl to be hot."

Tian Cuilian smiled.

On the wall behind him, a small screen showed one green dot with a ring around it, the Boss's own. It was not on Fuxing New Village.

It was on the corner of Old Street, beside a crooked shutter.

"He is up?" Tian Cuilian whispered.

Ke Junjie came in through the back door with his helmet under his arm. He had been watching the lane.

"He has a screwdriver. He couldn't sleep, I think. He's putting the shutter back on its bolt, and he's humming. Big Tooth shouted from the van, 'That wall is gravel on Monday, clerk. Save your screws.' He said, 'Evening. It hangs better straight.' Then he went on humming. A minute ago he stopped and wiped his face with his sleeve. Now he's fixing the lock with his tongue out, like a boy."

"Does he look angry?"

"He looks like a man who wants to go back to bed. And like a man who has been crying a little."

They watched the dot for a while, and nobody laughed.

Under it on the screen, one line of text glowed.

*8:30 a.m.: goes to Old Street Station.*

Gong Yi shook his head. "A bench. I wonder what the station will do when it finds out who is sitting on it."
