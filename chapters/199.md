# Chapter 199: Viktor Orlov's Mistake

The silence inside the underground control office became so heavy that Kou Zhenshan's ragged breathing sounded like a bellows in the cold air.

Viktor Orlov stood frozen, his thumb trembling against the red toggle of the detonator, his eyes locked on Chen Xuan's calm, smiling face. He had fought in Chechnya, in Syria, and across three private military operations in Central Africa. He had faced warlords, hardened federal prosecutors, and elite special forces commanders. Every man he had ever threatened with death had flinched, negotiated, or panicked.

Yet this young man with an old green thermos sat in the desk chair as if he were enjoying a cup of morning tea in a park pavilion.

"You think I am bluffing?" Orlov snarled, his scarred cheek twitching with rage. "You think I will not die for thirty million dollars?"

"I know you would die for thirty million dollars, Mr. Orlov," Chen Xuan replied in a mild, conversational tone. "The problem is that you are not going to die today. And you are certainly not going to blow up this mountain."

"Die, you arrogant bastard!" Orlov roared, slamming his thumb down onto the red button.

Click.

Nothing happened.

No subterranean roar, no violent tremor in the concrete floor, no shower of rock dust from the ceiling.

Orlov jammed the button a second time, then a third, hitting it furiously with both hands like a madman pounding a broken radio. "What, what did you do? Why did it not fire?"

"Because your detonator wire was severed sixteen minutes ago," Number 7 said calmly from the doorway, his hands still folded behind his white down jacket. "A mechanical shunt was installed across all thirty-two blasting charges in the mountain tunnel. Your transmitter is currently broadcasting a harmless radio wave into an empty snowbank."

Orlov's face went from pale to purple. A wave of animal desperation overtook him. He dropped the useless remote control, grabbed his automatic pistol with both hands, and pulled the trigger aimed directly at Chen Xuan's chest.

He never finished the trigger squeeze.

From the shadow just behind the doorframe, a black blur darted across the three meters of open floor.

Before Orlov's finger could complete the two-millimeter pull, a gloved hand struck the mercenary's wrist with the speed of striking lightning. The heavy steel pistol flew out of Orlov's grip, smashing into the wall and clattering across the epoxy floor.

In the same seamless motion, the black operative drove a knee into Orlov's sternum, spun behind the 220-pound fighter, and applied an effortless cross-body restraint. With a crisp, sickening snap, Orlov's right shoulder dislocated cleanly, and the massive Russian was driven face-first onto the concrete floor, pinned beneath a single black tactical boot that did not waver by a fraction of an inch.

The entire neutralization took less than 0.8 seconds.

Kou Zhenshan let out a strangled shriek, dropped to his knees behind the desk, and pressed his forehead against the floorboards, shaking so violently that his spectacles fell off his nose and skittered across the tiles.

"Spare me! Please spare me!" Kou Zhenshan sobbed into the carpet. "I only handled the books! I never killed anyone! Victor Leung told me it was all legal import-export through the Hong Kong trade channels! I will confess everything! I will give you every bank account, every offshore routing number, every ledger!"

The black operative kept his boot firmly on Orlov's spine, his plain black mask giving no sign of emotion, his dark eyes looking forward into empty space.

Chen Xuan did not even look at the floor. He picked up his green thermos, took another measured sip of jasmine tea, and looked down at Kou Zhenshan with an unhurried smile.

"Sit up, Mr. Kou," Chen Xuan said gently. "The floor is cold, and you are wrinkling your cashmere sweater."

Kou Zhenshan scrambled back onto his knees, trembling like a leaf in an autumn storm, his eyes wide with terror as he looked up at the young man. "Who, who are you? Please, tell me what you want!"

"I told you," Chen Xuan replied, tapping a finger against the desk blotter. "I am an operations clerk from Hengxin Logistics. When cargo sits in an unauthorized warehouse without proper clearance, it offends my sense of inventory order. Where are the master ledgers for the 300 tons of bullion?"

"In, in the safe behind the bookshelf!" Kou Zhenshan stammered, pointing a shaking hand toward a heavy mahogany shelving unit against the west wall. "The code is 884-219! The hard drives and the paper balance sheets are all inside! It lists every shipment from the Seven-Harbor Consortium over the last twelve years!"

"Number 7, collect the drives," Chen Xuan instructed softly.

"Yes, sir," Number 7 stepped to the bookshelf, tapped the electronic keypad, and swung open the concealed wall safe. Inside lay three black encrypted external hard drives and five thick leather-bound ledgers stamped with the gold seal of the Seven-Harbor Consortium.

Number 7 placed the items neatly into his leather folder and stepped back.

Chen Xuan glanced down at Orlov, who was groaning in muffled agony on the floor beneath the operative's boot.

"Number 12," Chen Xuan said. "Call Captain Zhou Ming and State Security. Tell them the compound has been pacified, the explosives are disarmed, and the suspects are secured. Have President Han Guoliang and President Fang Zhengming bring their armored convoys up the mountain road. The Four National Banks have an audit to conduct."

"Right away, sir," Number 12 replied, stepping out to send the coded message.

Chen Xuan stood up, adjusted his coat, and looked out through the glass window at the underground cavern where stacks of gold bullion gleamed under the industrial lamps.

"Three hundred tons of national wealth," Chen Xuan murmured softly to himself. "Stolen piece by piece by merchants who thought they could outsmart the state. Tomorrow, every ounce goes back to the people."

He picked up his green thermos, tucked it comfortably into the crook of his arm, and walked toward the vault doorway with the unhurried ease of a man finishing a pleasant morning walk.
