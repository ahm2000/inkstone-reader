# Chapter 199: Viktor Orlov's Mistake

Chen Xuan took a quiet sip of hot jasmine tea, completely ignoring the cold steel barrel of Viktor Orlov's pistol aimed at his chest from five paces away.

"You hid three hundred tons of gold in an old mine and left a trail of shell freight companies stretching from Jingzhou to Zurich," Chen Xuan said in an even, conversational tone. "Finding this tunnel was not the hard part. Getting up the snowy road without spilling my tea was much trickier."

Kou Zhenshan's hands shook so badly that the lid of his aluminum briefcase rattled against the steel desk. Trying to muster the last scraps of his old arrogance, he forced his voice steady. "Young man, I do not know which family or ministry sent you here, but you are making a terrible mistake. The Seven-Harbor Consortium has partners across three continents. Let us walk out of this valley, and you can take fifty tons of gold for yourself. Nobody ever has to know you were here."

Chen Xuan looked at him with mild amusement. "Fifty tons? Where would I even put it? My wife already complains when I buy too many bags of rice on sale."

Beside the concrete pillar, Viktor Orlov did not bother listening to Kou Zhenshan's desperate bargaining. The veteran mercenary saw his four interior guards lying unconscious on the floor and realized his twelve men outside were already gone. There was no talking his way out of a mountain trap.

If he could not leave with the gold, nobody else would either.

Orlov's scarred face twisted into a cold grin. Keeping his pistol trained on Chen Xuan with his right hand, he raised his left palm and slammed it straight down onto the big red detonator button.

Click.

The plastic switch depressed with a dull, hollow sound.

Nothing happened. No deafening roar shook the concrete walls. No dust fell from the arched ceiling. The tiny green indicator bulb simply flickered once and went dark.

Orlov froze. His eyes widened in disbelief. He slammed the red button a second time, then a third, hammering the steel box with his fist. The vault remained completely silent except for the quiet hum of the overhead lights.

Standing calmly near the entrance aisle, Number 7 spoke in a calm, respectful voice. "Young Master, the primer caps were pulled out of all six crates ten minutes ago. I left a bridge resistor on the test wire so he would feel safe until we walked in."

Cold sweat broke out across Orlov's forehead. He realized instantly that he had been dismantled before the door even opened. Fury replaced his shock. With a low snarl, he whipped his right hand up and tightened his finger on the trigger, firing point-blank at Chen Xuan's chest.

He never got the shot off.

Chen Xuan stepped forward in a motion so smooth it barely stirred the air around his jacket. His right hand snapped out, catching Orlov's wrist cleanly before the hammer could fall. With a single effortless twist, Chen Xuan turned the barrel upward, stripped the heavy pistol straight out of Orlov's grip, and tapped the inside of his elbow and the back of his knee.

It took less than a second.

Orlov, a hundred-kilogram veteran of a dozen conflicts, dropped face-first onto the concrete floor with a heavy thud. The air rushed out of his lungs in a choked gasp. Before he could even roll onto his side, Number 12 stepped over, pressed a knee briefly between his shoulder blades, and snapped heavy restraints around his wrists.

Chen Xuan ejected the magazine from the pistol, cleared the chamber smoothly with his thumb, and set the unloaded weapon neatly on the corner of Kou Zhenshan's desk. Through the entire exchange, his left hand had not even tilted. Not a single drop of hot tea had spilled from the open green thermos.

Behind the desk, Kou Zhenshan collapsed straight back into his chair as if his spine had vanished. Watching Viktor Orlov drop in under a second shattered every illusion he had left. His hands trembled uncontrollably as he pushed the aluminum case of encrypted hard drives and the entire stack of cloth-bound ledgers across the metal tabletop.

"Take them," Kou Zhenshan whispered, his voice cracking completely. "Every drive, every bank key, every paper ledger from the last ten years is right there. None of it has been burned or wiped. Just keep me alive. I will sign any confession the prosecutors want."

Number 7 and Number 12 walked up to the desk, quickly checking the drive seals and flipping through the index pages of the ledgers.

"Young Master, all twelve offline drives and every original paper ledger are intact," Number 7 reported clearly. "The vault manifest matches our count of three hundred tons down to the last crate."

"Young Master, Orlov and all four interior guards are secured," Number 12 added. "Number 44 is moving the outer sentries from the generator shed into the front holding bay so the handover team finds everyone together."

Chen Xuan nodded slightly. He pulled a plain sheet of white paper and a black pen from Kou Zhenshan's desk drawer, wrote four neat lines in steady handwriting, and folded the sheet once. He placed the note on top of the main ledger stack and weighted the corner down with a small loose gold bar sitting beside the scales.

"Do not leave any trace of our visit," Chen Xuan told them, screwing the lid back onto his thermos. "Signal the convoy waiting at the county airfield. We will be off the ridge before their headlights reach the first bend."

"Yes, Young Master," Number 7 and Number 12 replied together.

Chen Xuan turned calmly and walked back toward the open steel door, leaving Kou Zhenshan staring numbly at the silent vault as the cold mountain air drifted across the concrete floor.
