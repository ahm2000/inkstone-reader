# Chapter 196: The Snow Valley of Changbai

Early December had turned the northern foothills of Changbai Mountain into a silent expanse of white and dark pine. Heavy snow drifted steadily through the night air, coating the spruce branches and burying the old logging roads under knee-deep drifts. Outside, the temperature sat at minus 16 degrees Celsius, cold enough to bite through bare skin in minutes.

Deep in a sheltered basin between two timbered ridges, a private airstrip had been plowed clean hours earlier. Amber runway markers glowed dimly through the falling snow. At 9:40 PM, the sleek white jet from Shanghai broke through the low winter clouds, touched down smoothly on the packed strip, and rolled to a quiet stop near the treeline.

The cabin door hissed open and the airstair unfolded. A plume of warm cabin air met the freezing mountain wind.

Chen Xuan stepped out onto the top stair wearing a plain dark down jacket, a knit scarf, and heavy winter boots. In his left hand he carried his dented green thermos. Number 7 stepped out right behind him in a thick dark parka, scanning the dark treeline briefly before gesturing down the stairs.

Chen Xuan paused for a second to let his eyes adjust to the pale glare of the snow, then pulled his phone from his coat pocket. A new message sat on the screen from Qin Yuhan back in Shanghai.

"Did your logistics team reach the northern warehouse? Remember to keep your scarf zipped up. The weather report says Jilin is freezing tonight. Do not stay up too late."

Chen Xuan smiled faintly. Even from two thousand kilometers away, his wife still worried about him catching a chill on a routine freight inspection. He typed a short reply with his thumb.

"Just landed. Wearing the thick jacket you packed. I will be back in Pudong in time for lamb hot pot tomorrow."

He slipped his phone back into his pocket and walked down the stairs, with Number 7 following two steps behind. Waiting beside two dark off-road SUVs with engines idling were two men dressed in practical winter parkas, wool hats, and insulated boots. They looked like seasoned local mountain guides getting ready for a late winter supply run.

As Chen Xuan stepped onto the packed snow, both men inclined their heads respectfully.

"Young Master," Number 12 said calmly, his breath curling white in the cold air.

"Young Master," Number 44 echoed.

"No need to stand out in the wind," Chen Xuan said, walking toward the lead SUV. "Get in. Tell me what we have up the ridge."

He climbed into the back seat and placed his green thermos in the cup holder. Number 7 took the front passenger seat while Number 12 slid behind the wheel. Number 44 climbed into the second vehicle behind them. The SUVs pulled away from the strip, snow chains biting steadily into the narrow forest track as the white jet behind them dimmed its lights and settled quietly against the pines.

Inside the warm cabin, Number 7 turned slightly and handed back a slim tablet showing a thermal terrain map of the northern valley.

"Young Master, the target sits three kilometers ahead inside an abandoned mining tunnel dug in the late seventies," Number 7 reported clearly. "Kou Zhenshan spent four years reinforcing the main shaft with steel blast doors, backup diesel generators, and an independent satellite dish hidden under pine netting."

Chen Xuan glanced at the map without much expression. "And the cargo inside?"

"Confirmed by our two days of thermal and seismic checks, Young Master," Number 7 replied. "Three hundred tons of physical gold bars, moved out in small batches through shell freight companies over the past five years. Alongside the gold, Kou Zhenshan keeps the offline hard drives and original cloth-bound ledgers for the entire Seven-Harbor Consortium. Those records link their domestic front accounts directly to the Aegis Meridian transfers in Zurich."

"Good," Chen Xuan said softly. "Without those drives, some of their overseas lawyers might still drag out the banking freezes for months. With the physical gold and the paper trail both in hand, the case closes cleanly."

From the driver's seat, Number 12 kept his eyes on the winding snow track and spoke calmly. "Young Master, Viktor Orlov is running security inside the valley. He brought sixteen former foreign mercenaries with him. Twelve are stationed along the outer ridge and treeline with thermal scopes and automatic rifles. Four more stay inside the vault chamber with Kou Zhenshan."

"Orlov is careful," Chen Xuan remarked, unscrewing the lid of his green thermos. The gentle scent of hot jasmine tea filled the warm cabin. "Did he rig the tunnel entrance?"

"Yes, Young Master," Number 7 answered. "Our scout drones spotted heavy industrial cables leading from steel crates bolted to the concrete entrance beams. He wired demolition charges across the tunnel mouth. If an armed team tries to storm the valley, he intends to collapse the shaft and bury the gold and records under ten thousand tons of granite."

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of hot tea. "A clever threat if you are dealing with someone who knocks loudly on the front door. What about the local authorities?"

"No hikers or forestry workers are anywhere near this valley because of the storm," Number 7 said. "Meanwhile, the State Security handover team and the four presidents of the Four National Banks landed at the Changbai county airfield forty minutes south of here. They are waiting on standby for our signal. Once the perimeter is quiet and the charges are dead, they can drive straight up the main road."

The SUVs climbed higher into the dark pines. Through the windshield, the snow thickened, swirling across the yellow headlights. Far up the slope, a faint dark scar in the rocky mountainside marked the entrance to the old mine.

Number 12 eased the vehicle to a stop behind a dense stand of spruce trees where the engine noise was swallowed by the wind.

"We walk from here, Young Master," Number 7 said quietly.

Chen Xuan screwed the cap back onto his thermos, zipped his dark jacket to the chin, and stepped out into the freezing Changbai night. Above the valley, the winter wind howled across the ridge, covering the sound of their boots in the fresh snow.
