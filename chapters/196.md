# Chapter 196: The Snow Valley of Changbai

The pearl-white private jet descended through a thick bank of winter clouds and touched down smoothly on the cleared asphalt runway of an old military airstrip nestled deep inside the Changbai foothills.

Outside the cabin windows, endless rows of dark green pines stretched toward the white peaks of the northern range. A light flurry of dry snow swirled across the tarmac, caught in the cold mountain wind.

Chen Xuan unbuckled his seatbelt, picked up his scratched green metal thermos, and walked down the cabin aisle. At the doorway, the sharp, freezing northern air hit his face like a slap of ice water, clean and fragrant with the sharp scent of cedar needles.

At the bottom of the airstrip stairs, two figures in clean white down jackets stood waiting beside a heavy dark SUV. Number 12 and Number 44 stepped forward together, their gloved hands at their sides, bowing with quiet precision.

"Welcome to the northern border, sir," Number 12 said respectfully, his breath puffing into white mist. "We have kept a 24-hour watch over the valley since Thursday night. All four perimeter roads are monitored, and no vehicles have left the compound since dawn."

Chen Xuan stepped onto the snow-dusted concrete, pulled the zipper of his dark winter coat up to his chin, and smiled comfortably. "Good work, Number 12. How cold is it out here today?"

"Minus sixteen degrees, sir," Number 44 replied, opening the rear door of the SUV. "The heated seats are on, and we prepared two thermoses of boiling water in the back cabin."

"I brought my own tea leaves," Chen Xuan chuckled, tapping the dented green metal in his grip. "My wife packed three small packets of roasted jasmine before I left the house this morning. She told me if I caught a cold in the north, she would make me drink ginger soup for three weeks."

Number 7 stepped down behind Chen Xuan, carrying a slim leather folder under his arm. "Mrs. Chen is very considerate, sir. Shall we review the valley layout on the way to the forward post?"

"Let us get into the warmth first," Chen Xuan said, ducking into the heated leather seat of the SUV.

The heavy vehicle started with a muted hum and rolled through the snow-banked security gates of the airstrip, turning onto a narrow logging road that wound deeper into the dense pine forest.

Number 12 took the front passenger seat and unfolded a laminated topographical map across his knees. He turned slightly so Chen Xuan could see the red markings overlaying the mountain terrain.

"The facility sits in a blind basin four kilometers north of the logging checkpoint," Number 12 explained, pointing to a tight contour ring between two granite ridges. "Officially, it is registered to a local timber trading firm. Beneath the surface, it is a fully fortified underground refinery and staging vault controlled by the Seven-Harbor Consortium."

"Who commands the site?" Chen Xuan asked, unscrewing his thermos lid and taking a slow sip of hot jasmine tea.

"Two commanders, sir," Number 12 replied. "The Chinese operations chief is Kou Zhenshan, a longtime partner of the Hong Kong trading houses. He manages the accounting ledgers, the gold smelting furnaces, and the local shipping bribes. The armed security is led by Viktor Orlov, a former overseas mercenary commander who has fifty armed men stationed in the barracks above the tunnel."

"And the Four National Banks?" Chen Xuan inquired calmly.

"President Fang Zhengming from Construction Bank and President Han Guoliang from ICBC have already mobilized their joint audit teams to the regional branch in Jilin," Number 7 reported from the back seat. "They have three armored convoys and two dozen federal auditors waiting on standby. State Security teams under Captain Zhou Ming are also holding fifty kilometers south, awaiting our signal."

"They are waiting because Orlov has high explosives rigged to the tunnel," Number 44 added quietly. "If external forces try to breach the mountain gate with standard police squads, Orlov has orders from Hong Kong to detonate the entrance and bury all 300 tons of gold under two hundred meters of rock."

Chen Xuan looked out the frosted window at the ancient snow-covered pines passing by. "Victor Leung thinks a few crates of dynamite can hold back the sovereign balance. A clumsy strategy from an old merchant."

"The Black Guard is already in position along the ridge, sir," Number 7 said softly. "They moved into the snow valley at three o'clock this morning."

Chen Xuan nodded gently, recapping his thermos with a soft click. "Keep the banks and State Security where they are for now. If bankers try to climb snowy mountain ridges in their leather shoes, someone will slip and break an ankle. We will clear the compound first, disarm the detonators, and let President Han and President Fang inspect the ledgers in peace."

"Understood, sir," Number 12 said, tapping a radio transmitter to send the coded acknowledgment.

The SUV rounded a steep hairpin bend on the mountain road. Through the snow-dusted spruce branches below, the gray tin roofs of several large timber storage sheds came into view, nestled silently against the base of the sheer granite cliff. High chain-link fences topped with razor wire encircled the perimeter, guarded by hidden motion sensors and two camouflaged watch posts perched in the high trees.

To an ordinary passerby, it looked like nothing more than an isolated northern lumber mill idling during the winter freeze. But beneath the snow and gravel, forty years of illicit wealth and foreign consortium gold lay waiting behind reinforced steel doors.

Chen Xuan leaned back against the headrest, checked his watch, and noticed it was barely ten o'clock in the morning.

"If we wrap this up cleanly by tonight," Chen Xuan said with a relaxed smile, "I can take the noon flight back to Shanghai tomorrow and make it to the fresh seafood market before four."

"We will make sure you are home on time for Sunday dinner, sir," Number 7 replied with complete confidence.
