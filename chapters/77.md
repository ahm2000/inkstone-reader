# Chapter 77: Tears on the Sixth Floor

The sixth-floor metal door had barely clicked shut before Wu Lanying broke down.

She had held her breath all the way up six flights of stairs, terrified an officer would still shout from the yard about a paperwork mistake. The moment the lock snapped behind Chen Xuan, Wu Lanying grabbed both of Chen Mingde's hands and pushed his dark blue sleeves up to his elbows with shaking fingers.

"Let me look! Do not hide them, Mingde!" Wu Lanying sobbed, her eyes racing over his arms and knuckles. "Did Cheng Biao hit you? Did those animals from Hongrui lay a hand on you inside?"

Liu Meifang hurried in from the hallway without even taking off her shoes. She checked Chen Mingde's collar and shoulders, and her own eyes filled with tears when she saw the faint red ring around his left wrist. "Look at that mark! Putting iron cuffs on an honest man who has never missed a market tax in thirty years! Mingde, does your back hurt? Did they make you sit on cold concrete all night?"

"I am not broken, Lanying. Stop crying, you are getting my sleeves wet," Chen Mingde said. His own voice shook as he patted her shoulder. "Just tight cuffs for an hour when Cheng Biao tried to scare me into signing that move-out paper. Nobody hit me. Before he could bring in his night thugs, the whole station shook like thunder hit the roof!"

Two pairs of hurried footsteps pounded out of the kitchen. Chen Hao and Chen Yuan had been watching the stove since eight o'clock, too worried to sit still. Seeing their uncle safe in the doorway, both young men rushed forward at once.

"Second Uncle!" Chen Yuan shouted, bumping right into Chen Hao as they wrapped their arms around Chen Mingde from both sides. "You are really back! Hao swore we were going to camp outside the county gate with a cardboard sign!"

"Speak for yourself, you were sharpening the kitchen knife at six in the morning!" Chen Hao shot back, blinking hard as he gripped Chen Mingde's arm. "Second Uncle, you scared half the life out of us. Are you sure you are not hurt?"

"If you two squeeze any harder, I will be hurt right here in my brother's doorway!" Chen Mingde laughed. The sound cracked between relief and tears as he clapped both nephews on the back. "Let go, let your uncle breathe!"

Beside the shoe rack, Chen Jianguo stood stiffly with his hands clasped behind his back, trying to keep the calm face of the older brother. Sadly, his glasses had completely fogged up from kitchen steam and sudden heat in his eyes. He pulled them off, wiped them hard on the bottom of his sweater, and cleared his throat loud enough to rattle the tea tray.

"Alright, alright, enough crying in the doorway like a market stall in a storm," Chen Jianguo declared. His voice wobbled on the last word as he put his glasses back on crooked. "Did I not tell you last night not to panic? As the old saying goes, a crooked roof beam cannot outlast a straight iron nail, and a greedy weasel always trips over its own tail when the roosters wake up early!"

Silence hung in the entryway for half a second.

Chen Yuan stared at Chen Jianguo, then burst out laughing through his tears. "Dad, what on earth is that saying? Since when do roosters make weasels trip?"

"He made it up on the spot again!" Chen Hao wheezed, slapping his knee. "Last month it was a blind carp jumping over a frying pan!"

"You two keep quiet!" Chen Jianguo snapped. His ears turned bright red even as a huge, relieved grin broke across his face. "The meaning is solid! Good people stand straight, and crooked dogs break their own legs!"

Liu Meifang wiped her cheeks and gave Chen Jianguo a light tap on the arm, laughing softly for the first time since yesterday. "Stop making up nonsense and get your brother to the table! The ginger pork-rib soup has been cooking for two hours. If it boils dry, nobody eats."

Within two minutes, the dining table was crowded with steaming bowls. Chen Mingde sat in the middle chair. His red-stamped *Cleared Name Paper and Wrongful Arrest Dismissal* stood propped against the salt jar where Wu Lanying could keep glancing at it.

Once the first rush of tears settled, Chen Mingde set down his chopsticks and frowned in pure amazement. He looked from Chen Jianguo to Liu Meifang, and finally rested his eyes on Chen Xuan.

"I still cannot believe what happened this morning," Chen Mingde said slowly, shaking his head. "At seven thirty, Cheng Biao was blowing cigarette smoke in my face, bragging that Chairman Deng Yongchang and County Magistrate Sun Weiming had already split up Old Street and nobody in Yunxi County could touch them. At eight thirty sharp, the hallway exploded. Men in provincial windbreakers kicked the door open, cuffed Cheng Biao in front of his own officers, and hauled Chief Liang Guodong out like a sack of spoiled rice!"

Chen Hao's jaw dropped. "Liang Guodong too? In handcuffs?"

"In front of half the courtyard!" Chen Mingde exclaimed, tapping the table. "Then Inspector Han Zhiyuan himself walked into my holding room, unlocked my cuffs, poured me hot water, and drove me straight to Building 2! How did a provincial joint inspection group move that fast? I never sent a single complaint! Who on earth tipped off the province?"

Chen Jianguo stopped mid-chew, his eyes drifting toward his son. Liu Meifang paused with the soup spoon, and Wu Lanying looked across the table in quiet wonder.

"Wait a minute," Chen Yuan murmured, squinting at Chen Xuan. "Xuan, over boiled eggs this morning you told Dad that Second Uncle would walk up to Building 2 at nine o'clock sharp. Not ten, not this afternoon. Nine o'clock. How did you guess it to the exact five minutes?"

Chen Xuan did not blink. He unscrewed the lid of his worn green thermos, leaned forward, and poured steaming chrysanthemum tea into Chen Mingde's cup. Then he moved smoothly around the table, refilling Chen Jianguo's mug, Liu Meifang's glass, and Wu Lanying's cup without spilling a single drop.

"The law caught up with them, Second Uncle," Chen Xuan said calmly. He set the green thermos down and slid a full bowl of hot rib soup closer to Chen Mingde's elbow. "Sit down and drink your hot soup."

The room paused for a beat, caught by the plain, quiet steadiness of his voice.

"Well," Chen Mingde murmured, letting out a long breath that cleared the last shadow of the night from his chest. He took a slow sip of the rich ginger soup and nodded as warmth returned to his cheeks. "You are right, Xuan. However the province found out, Deng Yongchang and Cheng Biao dug their own graves long before they touched my shop. Good soup, Sister-in-law."

"Drink two more bowls," Wu Lanying urged, piling pork ribs into his dish. "At noon I am cooking braised fish and greens. Nobody in this family is going near Old Street Station today."

"Nobody needs to," Chen Xuan said quietly, taking his seat beside Chen Jianguo. "From today on, Old Street will be quiet."

Chen Jianguo glanced sideways at his son, thought better of asking another question, and took a loud sip of hot tea as laughter rose again over the clink of bowls.
