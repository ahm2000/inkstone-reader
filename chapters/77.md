# Chapter 77: Tears on the Sixth Floor

The sixth-floor security door had barely clicked shut before Wu Lanying broke down.

She had held her breath all the way up six flights of stairs, terrified an officer would still call out from the courtyard about a clerical mistake. The instant the latch snapped behind Chen Xuan, Wu Lanying caught both of Chen Mingde's hands and yanked his dark blue sleeves back to his elbows with shaking fingers.

"Let me look! Do not hide them, Mingde!" Wu Lanying sobbed, eyes darting over his forearms and knuckles. "Did Cheng Biao hit you? Did those animals from Hongrui touch you inside?"

Liu Meifang hurried from the hallway without taking off her shoes, inspecting Chen Mingde's collar and shoulders. Her own eyes brimmed over at the faint red ring around his left wrist. "Look at that mark! Putting iron cuffs on an honest man who has never skipped a market tax in thirty years! Mingde, does your back hurt? Did they leave you on cold concrete all night?"

"I am not broken, Lanying, stop crying, you are soaking my sleeves," Chen Mingde said, his own voice shaking as he patted her shoulder. "Just tight cuffs for an hour when Cheng Biao tried to scare me into signing that demolition waiver. Nobody laid a fist on me. Before he could bring in his night thugs, the whole station shook as if thunder hit the roof!"

Two pairs of hurried footsteps pounded out of the kitchen. Chen Hao and Chen Yuan had been guarding the stove since eight o'clock, too anxious to sit. Seeing their uncle whole in the entryway, both young men lunged forward at once.

"Second Uncle!" Chen Yuan shouted, colliding with Chen Hao as they wrapped their arms around Chen Mingde from both sides. "You are really back! Hao swore we were going to camp outside the county gate with a cardboard sign!"

"Speak for yourself, you were sharpening the kitchen cleaver at six in the morning!" Chen Hao shot back, blinking hard as he gripped Chen Mingde's arm. "Second Uncle, you scared half the life out of us. Are you sure you are not hurt?"

"If you two squeeze any harder, I will be hurt right here in my brother's doorway!" Chen Mingde laughed, the sound cracking between relief and tears as he clapped both nephews on the back. "Let go, let your uncle breathe!"

Beside the shoe rack, Chen Jianguo stood stiffly with his hands clasped behind his back, determined to keep the dignity of the eldest brother. Unfortunately, his glasses had completely fogged over from kitchen steam and sudden heat in his eyes. He yanked them off, wiped them furiously on his sweater hem, and cleared his throat loud enough to rattle the tea tray.

"Alright, alright, enough wailing in the doorway like a market stall in a storm," Chen Jianguo declared, his voice wobbling on the last word as he put his glasses back on crooked. "Did I not tell you last night not to panic? As the old saying goes, a crooked roof beam cannot outlast a straight iron nail, and a greedy weasel always trips over its own tail when the roosters wake up early!"

Silence hung in the entryway for half a second.

Chen Yuan stared at Chen Jianguo, then burst out laughing through his tears. "Dad, what on earth is that proverb? Since when do roosters make weasels trip?"

"He made it up on the spot again!" Chen Hao wheezed, slapping his knee. "Last month it was a blind carp jumping over a frying pan!"

"You two keep quiet!" Chen Jianguo snapped, ears turning bright red even as a relieved grin broke across his face. "The meaning is solid! Good people stand straight, and crooked dogs break their own legs!"

Liu Meifang wiped her cheeks and gave Chen Jianguo a light swat on the elbow, laughing softly for the first time since yesterday. "Stop inventing nonsense and get your brother to the table! The ginger pork-rib soup has simmered for two hours. If it boils dry, nobody eats."

Within two minutes, the dining table was crowded with steaming bowls. Chen Mingde sat in the center chair, his red-stamped *Certificate of Exoneration and Wrongful Detention Dismissal* propped against the salt jar where Wu Lanying could keep glancing at it.

Once the first rush of tears settled, Chen Mingde set down his chopsticks and frowned in astonishment, looking from Chen Jianguo to Liu Meifang, and finally resting on Chen Xuan.

"I still cannot believe what happened this morning," Chen Mingde said slowly, shaking his head. "At seven thirty, Cheng Biao was blowing cigarette smoke in my face, bragging that Chairman Deng Yongchang and Deputy County Magistrate Sun Weiming had already carved up Old Street and nobody in Yunxi County could touch them. At eight thirty sharp, the corridor exploded. Men in provincial windbreakers kicked the door open, cuffed Cheng Biao in front of his own officers, and hauled Chief Liang Guodong out like a sack of spoiled rice!"

Chen Hao's jaw dropped. "Liang Guodong too? In handcuffs?"

"In front of half the courtyard!" Chen Mingde exclaimed, tapping the table. "Then Inspector Han Zhiyuan himself walked into my holding room, unlocked my cuffs, poured me hot water, and drove me straight to Building 2! How did a provincial joint inspection group move that fast? I never filed a petition! Who on earth tipped off the province?"

Chen Jianguo stopped mid-chew, eyes drifting toward his son. Liu Meifang paused with the soup ladle, and Wu Lanying looked across the table in quiet wonder.

"Wait a minute," Chen Yuan murmured, squinting at Chen Xuan. "Xuan, over boiled eggs this morning you told Dad that Second Uncle would walk up to Building 2 at nine o'clock sharp. Not ten, not afternoon. Nine o'clock. How did you guess it to the exact five minutes?"

Chen Xuan did not blink. He unscrewed the lid of his worn green insulated thermos, leaned forward, and poured steaming chrysanthemum tea into Chen Mingde's ceramic cup. Then he moved smoothly around the table, refilling Chen Jianguo's mug, Liu Meifang's glass, and Wu Lanying's cup without spilling a drop.

"The law caught up with them, Second Uncle," Chen Xuan said calmly, setting the green thermos down and sliding a brimming bowl of hot rib broth closer to Chen Mingde's elbow. "Sit down and drink hot soup."

The room paused for a beat, caught by the ordinary steadiness of his tone.

"Well," Chen Mingde murmured, letting out a long breath that emptied the last shadow of the night from his chest. He took a slow sip of the rich ginger broth and nodded as warmth returned to his cheeks. "You are right, Xuan. However the province found out, Deng Yongchang and Cheng Biao dug their own graves long before they touched my shop. Good soup, Sister-in-law."

"Drink two more bowls," Wu Lanying urged, piling pork ribs into his dish. "At noon I am cooking braised fish and greens. Nobody in this family is going near Old Street Station today."

"Nobody needs to," Chen Xuan said quietly, taking his seat beside Chen Jianguo. "From today on, Old Street will be quiet."

Chen Jianguo glanced sideways at his son, thought better of asking another question, and took a loud sip of hot tea as laughter rose again over the clink of bowls.
