# Chapter 130: Number 1's Countermove

At half past eleven that night, two miles west of the Grand River Hotel, lights burned inside an ordinary twelfth-floor office suite.

Thick curtains covered the windows.

Number 1 stood by the window in a dark wool coat, a lit cigarette between two fingers as gray smoke drifted upward.

At the center table, Number 11 laid out three thick paper folders. Across the room, Number 34 pulled off a pair of headphones, stood up from the lead desk, and walked over with a warm stack of printouts.

"We have the whole chain," Number 34 reported calmly, setting the pages beside the ashtray. "Every line has been checked twice against the original bank sheets and server bills."

Number 1 turned slowly from the window, took a quiet drag, and looked down. "Start with the servers."

"Wanfeng Freight rented two locked server rooms in the Sha City bonded warehouse three months ago," Number 34 said, sliding a signed contract forward. "On local paper, those machines only store provincial trucking routes. In truth, Song Baisheng paid a private cable crew 600k yuan in cash to run a hidden line straight out of the warehouse through Hong Kong to Zurich."

"And the buyer in Zurich?" Number 1 asked evenly.

"Northern Ledger Limited," Number 34 answered. "Same group behind Li Weiming's port brokers in Shanghai. We pulled the rental bills, hardware serial numbers, and last week's test logs. Every time a sample harbor cargo list was entered on Song Baisheng's screen, a full copy reached Zurich in under four seconds."

Number 1 nodded once and tapped ash into the tray. "Now the money."

Number 11 slid the second folder across the table.

"Song Baisheng was cheap," Number 11 said dryly. "Over fourteen months, Northern Ledger wired 18 million yuan through three Macao trading firms into Song Baisheng's private accounts in Singapore. We have the transfer receipts, bank stamps, and three recorded calls between Song Baisheng and Li Weiming about tomorrow's signing."

Number 11 opened the third folder, marked with a red clip.

"And here is Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang," Number 11 continued, lowering his voice. "He never touched a single yuan inside the province. Northern Ledger opened a family trust in Zurich under the name of Wu Zhenbang's brother-in-law, a quiet tea merchant in Sha City. Three wires went in over eight months: 10 million yuan in March, 12 million yuan in July, and 13 million yuan two weeks ago. Total: 35 million yuan."

Number 1 picked up the top sheet and studied a scanned handwritten note below the transfers.

"A side agreement," Number 1 murmured, his eyes cold.

"Signed by Wu Zhenbang's secretary using Wu Zhenbang's personal seal," Number 34 confirmed. "It promises to skip open bidding, push the Provincial Freight Data Alliance through this month, and cancel port passes for any company refusing Article 14. Tonight's hotel security log also shows Li Weiming, wearing his Marcus Vance badge, meeting that same secretary in the ninth-floor elevator lobby at five fifteen."

Number 1 crushed his cigarette out slowly in the glass tray.

"Is any piece of this guesswork?" Number 1 asked softly.

"None," Number 34 replied. "Original bank receipts, signed server contracts, passport photos matching Marcus Vance to Li Weiming, and clear recordings of both men. Even if they burn Wanfeng Freight's office before sunrise, nothing here can be denied."

"Good," Number 1 said calmly. "Print two clean sets on plain paper. No marks, no return notes, no fingerprints. Copy the audio onto two plain thumb drives. Number 11 handles Sha City. Send the capital set through the midnight express line."

"Who receives them?" Number 11 asked, reaching for two brown folders.

"Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai in Sha City," Number 1 said quietly, buttoning his coat. "And State Security Minister Ding Guoping in the capital. Deliver both to the committee duty rooms by four, sealed. The duty officers will carry them up at dawn. Leave zero trace."

Ten minutes later, the printers stopped, and the twelfth-floor office went dark.

At four forty-five in the morning, gray stillness hung over the guarded courtyard of the Provincial Party Committee in Sha City.

Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai unlocked the heavy door of his private study, carrying a mug of hot green tea.

Placed in the center of his desk by the night duty officer was a thick brown folder tied with red string, a plain black thumb drive resting on top.

Zhao Dehai's eyes narrowed.

Setting his mug down slowly, Zhao Dehai untied the red string and opened the folder.

Page one showed the passport match between Li Weiming and Marcus Vance. Page three showed the 35 million yuan Zurich trust tied directly to Deputy Governor Wu Zhenbang.

Zhao Dehai's hand froze on the paper. His face darkened like a winter storm as he flipped through the server contracts, the 18 million yuan Singapore transfers, and the signed side letter bearing Wu Zhenbang's personal seal.

Several hundred miles north in the capital, at five o'clock sharp, State Security Minister Ding Guoping walked into his tightly guarded office and froze at the exact same sight.

An identical brown folder bound with red string rested beside his red desk phone.

Ding Guoping did not call the guards or touch the alarm.

He lit a cigarette with a steady hand, let out one slow ribbon of smoke, and picked up the red phone.

It rang twice in Sha City before Zhao Dehai answered.

"Old Zhao," Ding Guoping's deep, calm voice came over the line. "You have a brown folder on your desk."

"I am on page nine right now, Minister Ding," Zhao Dehai replied, his voice low with controlled anger. "Wu Zhenbang took 35 million yuan in Zurich and tried to sell every harbor in my province right under my nose."

"Two hundred miles of coastline and 80 billion yuan in annual trade," Ding Guoping said coldly. "And someone cleared the whole trail before sunrise without leaving a single footprint in either building."

For two seconds, neither leader spoke.

"What are your orders, Minister?" Zhao Dehai asked firmly. "I can have the discipline team pull Wu Zhenbang out of bed in twenty minutes."

"No," Ding Guoping said smoothly, tapping ash into his tray. "Move at five in the morning and Li Weiming slips out a side door while Song Baisheng's partners claim innocence. My special team will wait at the convention center from nine."

"Then we wait for the ten o'clock session?" Zhao Dehai asked.

"Let them walk onto the stage with their big smiles at ten o'clock," Ding Guoping ordered coldly. "When the pen goes up, my team moves on my word."

Outside, the first pale gold of sunrise broke softly over the quiet Sha City River. High up on the eighth floor of the Grand River Hotel, behind drawn white curtains, Chen Xuan slept peacefully without a single worry in the world.
