# Chapter 180: Under the Oriental Pearl Tower

By the time the three siblings stepped out of Plaza 66 onto West Nanjing Road, the afternoon sky had deepened into soft indigo, and golden streetlamps were glowing along the sidewalks.

For the first hundred meters, nobody said a word.

Chen Xuan walked at his usual relaxed pace, carrying the three cream garment bags over his left arm while buying a warm paper bag of roasted chestnuts from a street vendor with his right hand.

Finally, Chen Yuan could not hold back another second.

"Ge," Chen Yuan asked, her eyes still wide. "What on earth was that white card? Why did the General Manager of Plaza 66 and a Huaxia Bank director run down the escalator sweating just to bow to you?"

Chen Hao nodded rapidly, bouncing on his heels now that the shock had faded. "Yeah, Ge! And did you see Zhao Kai's face when the bank director mentioned his father's 400 million yuan loan? He turned whiter than chalk! I thought he would pass out on the carpet!"

Chen Xuan laughed softly and handed the warm bag of chestnuts to his sister. "Eat while they are hot. They get hard to peel once they cool down."

"Do not dodge the question, Ge!" Chen Yuan insisted, cracking open a shell anyway. "Are you really just an ordinary clerk at Hengxin Logistics? Ordinary clerks do not carry matte-white cards that make bank directors sprint in dress shoes!"

Chen Xuan smiled easily, keeping his voice warm and steady.

"I really do sit at a desk at Hengxin Logistics," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Earlier this week, I flew to Shenzhen to help a major shipping partner sort out a cargo delay at Pier Nine. It saved them tens of millions of yuan. As thanks, their senior director linked a temporary family shopping card to their corporate vault at Huaxia Bank so I could buy winter coats for you and Mom."

He shrugged lightly, eating a roasted chestnut. "Those managers did not bow to me. They saw the top corporate vault code on the card machine and panicked that their rude store manager had offended a major bank client. That is all."

Chen Yuan blinked, thinking it over.

"So that is why!" Chen Yuan exhaled in relief, then tapped his arm with a small frown. "Still, 144k yuan for three coats is way too much! Tell me you did not go into debt for this!"

"Not a single yuan of debt," Chen Xuan promised warmly. "My savings from two years at Hengxin covered everything with plenty left over. Just let Mom wear her burgundy coat happily this winter, alright?"

Chen Hao grinned wide, chewing a chestnut. "Honestly, Ge, I do not care whose vault the card came from. Watching that snobby manager get fired on the spot after slapping my hand felt amazing! Nobody in Yunxi County will believe this story!"

"Keep it between the three of us," Chen Xuan advised gently. "No need to make Mom worry about rude people in Shanghai."

"My lips are sealed," Chen Hao promised quickly.

Twenty minutes later, after a short ride east, the three of them climbed the stone steps onto the wide walkway of the Bund.

The cool evening breeze off the Huangpu River brushed their faces, fresh and crisp. Almost the exact moment they reached the granite railing, the full night lights of Lujiazui blazed brightly against the dark sky.

Across the wide dark water of the Huangpu River, Pudong shone like a city built of glass and light.

Directly facing them stood the Oriental Pearl Tower, its tall spheres glowing in magenta, rose gold, and silver. River ferries glided slowly across the water, trailing long ripples of gold and red across the waves.

Along the promenade, families laughed, couples took photos, and gentle music drifted from near the historic clock towers behind them.

Chen Xuan leaned comfortably against the stone railing, setting the three garment bags neatly beside his feet. He passed more hot chestnuts to Chen Yuan and Chen Hao.

Chen Yuan smiled softly, resting her chin on her hand as a bright ferry passed below. "Shanghai really is gorgeous at night, Ge. I can see why people from everywhere want to come here."

Beside her, seventeen-year-old Chen Hao stopped chewing.

He stood straight at the railing with both hands gripping the cool stone ledge, staring straight across the Huangpu River at the glowing Oriental Pearl Tower and the towering height of Shanghai Tower. Colored lights danced across his dark eyes. The earlier sting from Plaza 66 was completely gone, replaced by a fierce, bright determination that made him look older than seventeen.

After a long, quiet minute, Chen Hao turned to face Chen Xuan.

"Ge," Chen Hao said clearly above the soft hum of the river wind. "I made up my mind."

Chen Xuan met his younger brother's gaze. "About what?"

"Next year, I am getting into Fudan University," Chen Hao swore firmly, looking back toward the glowing skyline of Lujiazui. "No excuses, no slacking off. I will study until my eyes ache if I have to. I want to come to Shanghai next autumn, learn real skills, and build my own life here. One day, I am walking into Plaza 66 to buy Mom a coat like that with my own bank card."

Chen Yuan felt her eyes warm with pride, nudging her brother's shoulder. "That is my little brother talking!"

Chen Xuan looked at Chen Hao's bright, earnest face, and deep warmth filled his chest. He reached out and lightly ruffled Chen Hao's short dark hair.

"Deal," Chen Xuan said, a genuine smile softening his whole expression. "Remember your promise tonight. The day your Fudan University admission letter arrives, I will book a window table right over there in Lujiazui, under the Oriental Pearl Tower. Order anything on the menu. My treat."

"You better not forget, Ge!" Chen Hao grinned wide, his eyes shining under the Bund lamps. "I am ordering the biggest crab they have!"

"Count me in too!" Chen Yuan laughed, raising a roasted chestnut like a glass. "To Chen Hao getting into Fudan University!"

"To Fudan!" Chen Hao cheered softly, tapping his chestnut against hers and then Chen Xuan's.

They leaned back against the stone railing together, laughing as the river lights shimmered across the Huangpu.

In his faded dark-blue windbreaker and plain canvas sneakers, Chen Xuan looked like any ordinary older brother enjoying Saturday evening with his family. Fifty meters back along the stone walkway, two silent men in plain matte-black jackets stood casually near a lamppost, blending into the evening crowd while watching quietly over the promenade.

Chen Xuan took a slow breath of cool river air, listening to his sister and brother argue happily over which skyscraper was tallest. Whatever storms waited on Monday morning, tonight belonged to his family.
