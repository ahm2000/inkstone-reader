# Chapter 180: Under the Oriental Pearl Tower

Later that evening, golden streetlights flickered to life along the Bund across the dark waters of the Huangpu River. Above Lujiazui, the glowing spheres of the Oriental Pearl Tower shone warm pink and amber against the darkening sky.

On the upper floor of a cozy riverside restaurant, Chen Xuan, Qin Yuhan, Chen Hao, and Chen Yuan settled around a wooden table beside a tall window. From their seats, the tower looked close enough to touch, its reflection shimmering across the slow-moving barges below.

Steam curled upward from a lively spread of dishes: glossy braised river eel, golden crab roe tofu, crisp roasted duck, clear winter melon soup, a small ceramic pitcher of warm osmanthus rice wine, and a pot of sweet pear tea.

Chen Hao had finally traded his old navy county jacket for his new charcoal wool overcoat over his sweater. Sitting under the warm pendant lights, he looked sharp and grown-up, though he kept sneaking awestruck glances at Chen Xuan whenever Qin Yuhan looked out the window.

Halfway through dinner, Qin Yuhan excused herself politely to wash her hands after peeling river shrimp for Chen Yuan. The moment she stepped behind the carved wooden screen, Chen Hao leaned halfway across the table, dropping his voice to an urgent whisper.

"Brother Xuan!" Chen Hao hissed, his eyes wide with disbelief. "Tell me the truth right now! What on earth was that white card back at Plaza 66? That bank director looked like he was about to faint just from reading the screen! And the mall manager fired that rude woman in five seconds flat!"

Chen Yuan leaned in too, resting her chin on her palms with sparkling curiosity. "Brother Xuan, you told Grandma you just check shipping invoices on the 12th floor. Since when do logistics clerks get ninety-degree bows from bank directors?"

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of warm tea with a straight face. "You two watch too many dramas," he said lazily. "That is just a corporate VIP discount card from an old friend at Huaxia Bank. He owed me a favor, so he gave me a spare card to buy gifts for the elders."

Chen Hao blinked, struggling to match that explanation with Zhao Kai's 150 million credit line freezing in ten seconds. "A discount card freezes a real estate company's loans?"

"Maybe Zhao Kai's company already had terrible credit and Director Lin noticed today," Chen Xuan replied smoothly, picking up a piece of roasted duck. "Do not repeat this to your sister-in-law or Auntie. Yuhan hates unnecessary drama, and Auntie will worry I am spending too much."

Chen Yuan giggled softly, clearly not buying his excuse, yet her eyes shone with pride and trust. "Fine, Brother Xuan. Our lips are sealed. Just teach Hao how to be half as cool as you."

"Easy," Chen Xuan grinned. "Step one is carrying a good thermos. Step two is never losing your temper before dinner."

Just then, Qin Yuhan returned, catching their grins. She sat beside Chen Xuan and raised an eyebrow playfully. "What are you three whispering about? Xuan, are you teaching Hao bad habits?"

"Never," Chen Xuan protested, pouring warm pear tea into her cup. "I was only telling him that cooking braised pork ribs is the smartest investment a young man can make."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, her eyes crinkling with warmth as she lifted her small cup of osmanthus rice wine. "Come on, let us make a toast before the soup cools down."

All four of them raised their cups together against the backdrop of the glowing Oriental Pearl Tower.

"To Sister-in-law and Brother Xuan!" Chen Yuan said brightly. "Thank you for the amazing weekend, the new coats, and the shawls for Mom and Grandma. You are the best sister-in-law anyone could wish for."

"To Sister-in-law!" Chen Hao echoed heartily, clinking his cup with theirs. "Next time you two come back to Handong, I will carry all the luggage up the hill myself!"

Qin Yuhan's smile softened as she sipped the sweet rice wine. She set her cup down, looked at the two young cousins, and then turned her gentle gaze toward Chen Xuan.

"Actually, I should be thanking you both," Qin Yuhan said quietly. "Before Xuan and I got married, my Saturday evenings were usually spent alone in a quiet office on the 38th floor of Haiyue Tower, eating cold takeout while staring at the river. Having a full table and loud laughter like this makes the whole city feel warmer."

Chen Xuan reached over under the tablecloth and gave her hand a quiet, reassuring squeeze. She smiled back at him, completely at peace.

After dinner, they walked outside onto the wide Riverside Promenade. The late November breeze off the river was crisp and refreshing, carrying the faint chill of coming winter. Around them, everyday city life flowed peacefully: a pair of joggers trotted past in reflective jackets, an elderly couple walked a small white dog along the railing, and a young man in a wool cap sat on a wooden bench sketching the lit skyline.

Chen Hao pulled out his phone and backed up a few steps, framing the glowing Oriental Pearl Tower soaring behind their shoulders.

"Everyone squeeze together!" Chen Hao called out. "Grandma wants proof that we made it to Shanghai!"

Qin Yuhan leaned happily against Chen Xuan's arm, her ivory coat glowing under the riverside lamps. Chen Yuan popped up on the other side flashing a peace sign, while Chen Xuan held his dented green thermos casually in one hand with a relaxed, content smile.

Click. The photo came out crisp and bright, capturing all four grinning faces beneath the golden lights of Lujiazui.

As they strolled back toward the parking garage, Chen Xuan slipped Qin Yuhan's cool fingers into his warm jacket pocket. His single smartphone buzzed once against his palm. It was a short text from Grandma back in Handong, asking if the kids had eaten well.

Chen Xuan smiled, attached the glowing group photo under the Oriental Pearl Tower, and tapped out a simple reply.

"Everyone is happy and well-fed."

Under the clear November stars, the quiet warmth of family felt far brighter than all the neon lights along the river.
