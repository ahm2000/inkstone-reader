# Chapter 78: The Honest Deputy

By half past ten on Monday morning, the upstairs duty room of Old Street Station hung thick with gray cigarette smoke. Two overflowing glass ashtrays sat on the scratched table between Han Zhiyuan and Provincial Discipline Inspection deputy Shen Wenchao, flanked by stacks of confiscated Hongrui Real Estate case folders.

The door clicked shut behind the fourth local officer they had interviewed in forty minutes. The man had walked out pale, sweating through his collar, clutching a yellow suspension notice with trembling fingers.

Shen Wenchao exhaled a sharp stream of smoke and tapped his burning cigarette against the ashtray rim. "Four senior officers in a row, Han Zhiyuan, and not one clean spine among them! That deputy instructor practically crawled across the table swearing Cheng Biao forged his signature on six demolition detentions! If we suspend the whole station, who patrols Old Street by noon?"

"We do not put a dirty man in charge just to fill a chair, Shen Wenchao," Han Zhiyuan replied flatly, lighting a fresh cigarette and squinting through the haze at the personnel roster. "Provincial headquarters expects Yunxi County stabilized today without excuses. Hand Old Street Station to one of Liang Guodong's leftovers, and we will be digging up the same rot in six months."

"Then give me one name on that roster who has not eaten Chairman Deng Yongchang's banquet food," Shen Wenchao snapped, jaw tight. "Every squad leader took Spring Festival envelopes. Half of them laughed while Yan Tiehu smashed shopfronts!"

Han Zhiyuan flipped to the very back of the register and tapped a faded personnel card clipped behind the night-shift logs.

"One man did not," Han Zhiyuan said quietly. "Wei Kun. Deputy squad leader, twelve years on the force, banished to night-desk archive filing six months ago by Cheng Biao for poor cooperation. Send him in."

Shen Wenchao frowned, crushing his cigarette dead. "Buried in the basement for six months means he is either stubborn as a mule or hiding his own dirt. Let us see which."

A crisp knock sounded. At Han Zhiyuan's call, a square-jawed officer in his late thirties stepped inside, closed the door firmly, and stood at attention. Wei Kun's uniform cuffs were faded from washing, his eyes shadowed from night shifts, smelling faintly of archive dust rather than banquet liquor.

"Sit down, Wei Kun," Han Zhiyuan said evenly, gesturing to the lone metal chair.

Wei Kun sat without fidgeting, hands flat on his knees. Unlike the four officers before him, he forced no eager smile and did not glance nervously at their badges.

Shen Wenchao leaned forward immediately, voice sharp. "Wei Kun, Cheng Biao is in provincial custody. Chief Liang Guodong is in provincial custody. Four of your colleagues have just been suspended. Tell me straight: why did Cheng Biao banish you to night-desk filing half a year ago?"

"Because I refused to sign three blank administrative summonses for Hongrui Real Estate's demolition crew," Wei Kun answered without hesitation. His voice was blunt and steady. "On October fourteenth, Cheng Biao handed me pre-stamped detention forms targeting three Old Street shopkeepers who refused Chairman Deng Yongchang's buyout. There was no legal disturbance on file. I refused to sign. The next morning I was moved to the basement archive."

Shen Wenchao narrowed his eyes, lighting another cigarette through rising smoke. "Convenient story now that Cheng Biao is in handcuffs. Every coward in this building suddenly claims he opposed Cheng Biao in secret. Why did you not report him to the county bureau?"

Wei Kun's gaze did not waver. "I did. Twice. On October nineteenth and November fourth, I submitted written memos to Chief Liang Guodong's office. Both memos vanished inside forty-eight hours, and Cheng Biao docked my quarterly bonus. After that, I stopped wasting paper on Liang Guodong."

Shen Wenchao's jaw tightened. "So you kept your head down and let Hongrui run wild?"

"I could not arrest my own director," Wei Kun replied evenly, a dull flush of quiet anger rising on his cheeks. "What I could do was keep a private carbon-copy ledger of every irregular summons, altered dispatch time, and late-night meeting between Yan Tiehu's men and Cheng Biao behind the duty garage. It is locked in my desk drawer downstairs. If you had arrested me today, I would have handed you the key anyway."

Thick silence settled over the smoky room. Shen Wenchao froze with his cigarette halfway to his lips, staring into Wei Kun's unblinking eyes.

Han Zhiyuan leaned forward, resting his forearms on the table. "One more question, Wei Kun. Last night, when Cheng Biao dragged Chen Mingde from Mingde Hardware into the holding room, where were you?"

"On night-desk filing," Wei Kun answered crisply. "Cheng Biao locked me out of the holding corridor. When I heard Chen Mingde's name on the intake radio, I checked the logbook, saw zero arrest warrant attached, and refused to stamp the intake registry. Cheng Biao threatened to dismiss me this morning."

"And what would you have done if our provincial team had not arrived at eight thirty?" Han Zhiyuan probed.

Wei Kun's mouth set hard. "I would have taken my carbon-copy ledger onto the nine o'clock highway bus and delivered it to the Provincial Department of Public Security myself. Win or lose, a police station is not a real estate developer's collection kennel."

Shen Wenchao stared at him for three seconds, then let out a short, rough breath that almost sounded like a laugh. He exhaled thick smoke toward the ceiling and glanced at Han Zhiyuan. "Stubborn as a mule," Shen Wenchao muttered, tension easing from his shoulders. "And the only real officer left in this building."

Han Zhiyuan stubbed out his cigarette cleanly. He drew an emergency provincial appointment order bearing the red seal of the joint task force from his briefcase, wrote Wei Kun's name in crisp ink, and slid it across the table.

"Wei Kun," Han Zhiyuan declared, voice authoritative. "Effective immediately, by emergency authorization of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group, you are appointed Acting Director of Old Street Station."

Wei Kun stiffened, eyes widening in genuine disbelief as he stared down at the red seal. "Inspector Han Zhiyuan, I am only a deputy squad leader who has sorted archive boxes for six months. I have no connections in the county government."

"That is precisely why you are getting the seal," Han Zhiyuan cut in firmly. "First, hand Shen Wenchao your carbon-copy ledger so we finish cleaning this station before nightfall."

Han Zhiyuan slid a second thick, sealed official dossier across the wood.

"And second," Han Zhiyuan continued gravely, "inside that folder is the official damage-assessment and full compensation paperwork for Mingde Hardware, signed by the provincial audit team. You will personally deliver it to Mr. Chen Mingde with full courtesy. Make one rule clear to every officer left under your command: neither Chen Mingde nor anyone in his family is ever to be summoned to Old Street Station. We bring the law's apology to their door."

Wei Kun stared at the appointment order, then at the Mingde Hardware compensation file. Slowly, six months of basement dust lifted off his shoulders.

He stood sharply, straightened his faded uniform, and snapped a ringing salute across the smoky table.

"Understood, Inspector Han Zhiyuan! Understood, Deputy Shen Wenchao! I will bring up the ledger now, and I will deliver Mr. Chen Mingde's paperwork myself."
