# Chapter 78: The Honest Deputy

Morning sunlight slanted through the tall open windows of the upstairs duty room at Old Street Station. A cool breeze carried the distant hum of street traffic inside, rustling the corners of pale blue case files stacked neatly across a long oak table. Two green enamel mugs of plain green tea sat beside a brass desk lamp, untouched since sunrise.

The wooden door clicked shut behind the fourth local officer they had questioned in less than an hour. The man stumbled down the hallway pale and sweating, clutching a yellow suspension notice against his chest with trembling fingers.

Hou Liangping, the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief, tapped his fountain pen lightly against a thick ledger seized from Hongrui Real Estate's accounting vault. His expression was sharp and unyielding.

"Four senior station officers in a row, Ji Changming, and not one clean backbone among them," Hou Liangping said evenly. "That last deputy practically knelt beside the desk swearing former station director Cheng Biao forged his signature on six unlawful detention orders. With Deputy Provincial Secretary Gao Yuliang under arrest and Public Security Director Qi Tongwei's network unraveling, everyone down here is scrambling to save his own skin."

Across the table, Ji Changming, the Provincial Chief Procurator, adjusted his spectacles and flipped a page of the duty log. "We do not put a dirty man in charge just to keep a chair warm, Hou Liangping. Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin and Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang made the directive crystal clear. Yunxi County gets scrubbed from top to bottom, starting with Old Street."

"I agree completely," Hou Liangping replied, leaning back slightly in his chair. "If we suspend the entire leadership team without appointing someone honest before noon, the local thugs who used to work for Shanshui Group's contractors might try to stir up trouble on Old Street tonight. And we both know we cannot afford a single scratch on that lane or on the hardware shopkeeper's household."

Ji Changming glanced up over the rims of his glasses. Neither man knew the name of the person behind the untouchable Yunxi household, nor did either of them dare speculate aloud. All they knew was that a single quiet warning from the highest levels had triggered the collapse of Shanshui Group and placed Old Street under strict protection.

"Then look at this file," Ji Changming said calmly, sliding a thin, worn personnel folder across the oak table. "I pulled every service record in the station archive an hour ago. One name kept turning up in the complaint drawer, not for taking bribes, but for refusing to obey Cheng Biao."

Hou Liangping opened the folder and scanned the tidy handwritten evaluations. "Wei Kun. Thirty-six years old. Twelve years on the force. Never promoted past deputy officer, reassigned to the basement archive room and overnight patrols for the past three years."

"Read the bottom note from last night's duty log," Ji Changming prompted.

Hou Liangping turned the page and paused. A thin smile touched the corner of his mouth. "Last night, Wei Kun entered a formal written objection against the arrest of the Mingde Hardware shopkeeper, noting zero evidence and no valid warrant. When Cheng Biao tore up the slip, Wei Kun wrote it a second time in the permanent shift book and tried to bring the detainee warm water."

"Bring him in," Ji Changming said.

Two minutes later, a sharp knock sounded on the door. Wei Kun stepped inside, wearing a clean uniform whose cuffs had been mended twice with neat dark thread. He stood straight at attention, his weathered face calm even though two of the highest-ranking officials in Handong Province sat watching him.

"Deputy Wei Kun reporting as ordered," he said clearly.

Hou Liangping studied him for a long moment. "Sit down, Wei Kun. Tell me honestly, why did you write that objection in the shift book last night when you knew Cheng Biao would punish you for it?"

Wei Kun remained standing for a second before taking the edge of the wooden chair. "Chief Hou, Procurator Ji, I have walked Old Street for twelve years. The owner of Mingde Hardware is an honest craftsman who never cheated a neighbor out of a single coin. Locking him up to force a demolition signature violated every regulation I swore to uphold. Even if Cheng Biao sent me to guard the boiler room, someone had to put the truth on paper."

Ji Changming exchanged a satisfied glance with Hou Liangping.

"Every senior officer above you at this station has just been suspended pending prosecution," Hou Liangping said, standing up and picking up a crisp appointment order stamped by the provincial inspection team. "As of right now, you are appointed Acting Director of Old Street Station."

Wei Kun froze, his eyes widening slightly. "Chief Hou, I am only a regular deputy. I have no connections in the county bureau."

"That is precisely why you are getting the job," Hou Liangping said firmly, walking around the table to place the document in Wei Kun's hands. "You answer directly to the provincial clean-up team and Jingzhou City's oversight office. Your first task is to audit every officer left in this building and remove anyone who ever took a red envelope from Hongrui Real Estate."

"And your second task," Ji Changming added steadily, "is to protect Old Street. Walk the lane yourself today. Let every shopkeeper, especially the family at Mingde Hardware, know that the law has returned to Yunxi County. If a single bully threatens them, arrest him on the spot."

Wei Kun stood up straight, his fingers tightening around the appointment order. Twelve years of quiet frustration vanished from his shoulders, replaced by steady resolve. He raised a crisp salute.

"Understood, Chief Hou! Understood, Procurator Ji! As long as I wear this uniform, not one bully will set foot on Old Street!"

Hou Liangping nodded with approval as Wei Kun turned smartly and marched out of the duty room. Outside the open window, the midday sun shone bright and clear over the quiet rooftops of Yunxi County.
