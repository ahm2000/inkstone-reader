# Chapter 78: The Honest Deputy

A few blocks away from that warm kitchen, the upstairs duty room at Old Street Police Station held no laughter at all. Pale blue case files lay stacked neatly across a long oak table beneath the hum of distant street traffic, beside two green enamel mugs of plain tea that had gone cold untouched.

The wooden door clicked shut behind the fourth senior station officer questioned in less than an hour. The man stumbled down the hallway pale and sweating, clutching a yellow suspension notice against his chest with trembling fingers.

Hou Liangping, Chief of the Handong Anti-Corruption Bureau, tapped his fountain pen lightly against a thick ledger seized from Hongrui Real Estate's accounting vault. His expression remained sharp and unyielding.

"Four senior officers in a row, Ji Changming, and every single one of them appears in Hongrui's monthly cash disbursement sheet," Hou Liangping said. "Station Chief Liu took 200k yuan in disguised consulting fees over ten months. His three shift supervisors split another 150k yuan for rubber-stamping detention slips and ignoring demolition complaints."

Chief Procurator Ji Changming flipped to the back of the personnel roster spread before him. "That explains how Hongrui cleared thirteen shops in half a year without a single formal complaint reaching Jingzhou. When the local station acts as a gatekeeper for a land syndicate, small merchants have nowhere left to file a report."

"We still need someone clean to run this station starting today," Hou Liangping said. "We cannot leave Old Street without steady local policing while the provincial task force audits the county bureau."

Ji Changming pulled a thin, dog-eared file from the bottom of the stack and slid it across the oak table. "Then you should look at Deputy Officer Wei Kun. Thirty-seven years old, eight years at Old Street Station, zero disciplinary marks, and not a single yuan of unexplained income on his family's bank records."

Hou Liangping opened the file and spotted a crumpled pink carbon copy paper-clipped to the front page. It was the administrative detention order drafted the previous evening against Chen Mingde, owner of Mingde Hardware on the corner of Old Street.

Across the bottom signature line, written in firm blue ink, stood a formal refusal: "Declined to execute. No independent witness statement attached, no body-camera log submitted, and property dispute falls outside administrative detention authority. Signed, Wei Kun."

Hou Liangping's gaze sharpened. "He put his name on a written refusal while his station chief sat right down the hall?"

"Not only that," Ji Changming added. "Our team found three internal memos Wei Kun filed with the county bureau over the past five months. Each memo documented Hongrui Real Estate's demolition crews cutting water and power to Old Street merchants without court writs. Former Station Chief Liu buried all three memos in his desk and banished Wei Kun to the night records desk."

"Bring him in," Hou Liangping said at once.

Two minutes later, a steady knock sounded at the door. Wei Kun stepped inside, closed the door quietly, and stood at attention beside the table. His dark blue uniform was faded at the cuffs yet pressed spotless. Ink stains marked his right middle finger from writing field reports, and his eyes met both provincial officials without flinching.

"Deputy Officer Wei Kun reporting, Director Hou, Chief Procurator Ji," he said evenly.

"Have a seat, Comrade Wei," Ji Changming said, gesturing to the wooden chair.

Hou Liangping pushed the crumpled detention slip forward. "Tell me plainly why you refused to sign this order against Chen Mingde last night. You knew Station Chief Liu wanted that parcel cleared before noon."

"Because the paperwork was fabricated from start to finish," Wei Kun answered without hesitation. "Chen Mingde has run Mingde Hardware on Old Street for twenty years without a single citation. The complaint claiming he assaulted a site inspector had no hospital record, no neutral witnesses, and a timestamp from an hour when his shop shutters were already locked. Signing an unlawful detention warrant turns a police badge into a private club for developers. I could not sign it."

Hou Liangping studied him for a quiet moment, then glanced at Ji Changming. Both men saw the same steady resolve.

"Old Street Station is being reorganized under provincial supervision effective at 9:00 AM today," Ji Changming said. "Former Station Chief Liu and four senior supervisors have been suspended and placed under investigation for bribery and abuse of authority. With Secretary Sha Ruijin's approval, we are appointing you Acting Director of Old Street Police Station."

Wei Kun blinked once, surprised, then straightened his shoulders. "Director Hou, Chief Procurator Ji, I have only ever managed a patrol squad and the records desk. I will take the post, provided I have the authority to clean up the station's open cases properly."

"You have full backing from the provincial procuratorate and the Anti-Corruption Bureau," Hou Liangping said, sliding a stamped appointment letter across the table. "Tell us your first steps."

Wei Kun answered without delay. "First, I will pull every active administrative case opened against Old Street merchants over the past twelve months and audit them with the procuratorate team. Any fabricated fine or forced relocation citation tied to Hongrui Real Estate gets formally dismissed, and any seized permits or ledgers go back to their owners with signed receipts. Second, I will return our honest junior officers to daytime foot patrols along Old Street. Small merchants do not ask for special treatment. They only ask that when they open their shutters in the morning, the law protects their shop fairly."

"That is exactly what Yunxi County needs," Ji Changming said with a firm nod. "Send your patrol reports directly to our liaison office for the next thirty days. If any county official or broker pressures your station again, call my office directly."

"Yes, Chief Procurator," Wei Kun said. He stood, signed the appointment receipt in crisp blue ink, and offered a clean salute. "I will brief the morning shift right away."

As the door closed behind him, Hou Liangping picked up his mug of green tea and allowed himself a small, satisfied smile. "At least Old Street still has one officer who remembers what the badge is for."

"Now let us make sure the officials who tried to bury him answer to the court," Ji Changming replied, gathering the Hongrui ledgers into his case.
