# Chapter 78: The Honest Deputy

By half past ten on Monday morning, the upstairs duty room of Old Street Station was thick with cigarette smoke. Two full ashtrays sat between Han Zhiyuan and Provincial Discipline Office deputy Shen Wenchao, surrounded by seized Hongrui Real Estate folders.

The door clicked shut behind the fourth local officer they had questioned in forty minutes. He walked out pale and sweating, clutching a yellow suspension paper with shaking fingers.

Shen Wenchao blew out a sharp stream of smoke and tapped his cigarette. "Four senior officers in a row, Han Zhiyuan, and not one clean backbone among them! That deputy practically crawled across the table swearing Cheng Biao faked his signature on six demolition arrests! If we suspend the whole station, who patrols Old Street by noon?"

"We do not put a dirty man in charge just to fill a chair, Shen Wenchao," Han Zhiyuan replied flatly, lighting a fresh cigarette and squinting at the staff list. "Provincial headquarters expects Yunxi County steady today with zero excuses. Hand Old Street Station to one of Liang Guodong's leftovers, and we will dig up the same rot in six months."

"Then give me one name on that list who has not eaten at Chairman Deng Yongchang's table," Shen Wenchao snapped. "Every squad leader took Spring Festival cash envelopes. Half of them laughed while Yan Tiehu smashed shopfronts!"

Han Zhiyuan flipped to the back of the binder and tapped a faded file card clipped behind the night-shift logs.

"One man did not," Han Zhiyuan said quietly. "Wei Kun. Deputy squad leader, twelve years on the force. Cheng Biao pushed him down to night-shift record filing six months ago for poor cooperation. Send him in."

Shen Wenchao crushed out his cigarette. "Buried in the basement for six months means he is either stubborn as a mule or hiding his own dirt. Let us see which."

A crisp knock sounded. At Han Zhiyuan's call, a square-jawed officer in his late thirties stepped inside, closed the door firmly, and stood at attention. Wei Kun's uniform cuffs were faded from washing, dark rings sat under his eyes, and he smelled of paper dust rather than banquet liquor.

"Sit down, Wei Kun," Han Zhiyuan said evenly, pointing to the metal chair.

Wei Kun sat without fidgeting, hands flat on his knees. Unlike the four before him, he did not force an eager smile or glance nervously at their badges.

Shen Wenchao leaned forward sharply. "Wei Kun, Cheng Biao is under provincial arrest. Chief Liang Guodong is under arrest. Four of your coworkers have been suspended. Tell me straight: why did Cheng Biao send you to night-shift record filing half a year ago?"

"Because I refused to sign three blank police orders for Hongrui Real Estate's demolition crew," Wei Kun answered without pause, voice blunt and steady. "On October fourteenth, Cheng Biao handed me pre-stamped arrest forms aimed at three Old Street shopkeepers who refused Chairman Deng Yongchang's buyout. There was no crime on file. I refused to sign. The next morning I was moved to the basement record room."

Shen Wenchao narrowed his eyes and lit another cigarette. "Convenient story now that Cheng Biao is in handcuffs. Every coward in this building suddenly claims he opposed Cheng Biao in secret. Why did you not report him to the county police office?"

Wei Kun did not blink. "I did. Twice. On October nineteenth and November fourth, I turned in written reports to Chief Liang Guodong's office. Both vanished within forty-eight hours, and Cheng Biao cut my quarterly bonus. After that, I stopped wasting paper on Liang Guodong."

Shen Wenchao's jaw tightened. "So you kept your head down and let Hongrui run wild?"

"I could not arrest my own director," Wei Kun replied evenly, a dull flush of quiet anger on his cheeks. "What I could do was keep a private copy book of every fake police order, changed call time, and late-night meeting between Yan Tiehu's men and Cheng Biao behind the station garage. It is locked in my desk drawer downstairs. If you had arrested me today, I would have handed you the key anyway."

Thick silence filled the smoky room. Shen Wenchao froze with his cigarette halfway to his lips, staring into Wei Kun's steady eyes.

Han Zhiyuan leaned forward on the table. "One more question, Wei Kun. On Friday, when Cheng Biao dragged Chen Mingde from Mingde Hardware into the holding room, where were you?"

"On night-shift record filing," Wei Kun answered crisply. "Cheng Biao locked me out of the holding hallway. When I heard Chen Mingde's name on the front-desk radio, I checked the logbook, saw zero arrest warrant attached, and refused to stamp the arrest book. Cheng Biao threatened to fire me this morning."

"And what would you have done if our provincial team had not arrived at eight thirty?" Han Zhiyuan asked.

Wei Kun's mouth set hard. "I would have taken my private copy book onto the nine o'clock highway bus and delivered it to the Provincial Police Department myself. Win or lose, a police station is not a real estate developer's private kennel."

Shen Wenchao stared for three seconds, then let out a short, rough breath that almost sounded like a laugh. He blew smoke toward the ceiling and glanced at Han Zhiyuan. "Stubborn as a mule," he muttered, tension leaving his shoulders. "And the only real officer left in this building."

Han Zhiyuan put out his cigarette. He pulled an emergency provincial appointment order stamped with the joint task force's red seal from his briefcase, wrote Wei Kun's name in crisp ink, and slid it across the table.

"Wei Kun," Han Zhiyuan declared firmly. "Effective immediately, by emergency order of the Provincial Joint Inspection Group, you are named Acting Director of Old Street Station."

Wei Kun stiffened, eyes widening in disbelief. "Inspector Han Zhiyuan, I am only a deputy squad leader who sorted record boxes for six months. I have no connections in the county government."

"That is the exact reason you are getting the stamp," Han Zhiyuan cut in. "First, hand Shen Wenchao your copy book so we finish cleaning out this station before dark."

Han Zhiyuan slid a second sealed official folder across the table.

"And second," Han Zhiyuan continued gravely, "inside that folder are the official damage check and full payout papers for Mingde Hardware, signed by the provincial team. Personally deliver it to Mr. Chen Mingde with full respect. Make one rule clear to every officer under your command: neither Chen Mingde nor anyone in his family is ever called into Old Street Station. We bring the law's apology to their door."

Wei Kun stared at the appointment order, then at the Mingde Hardware folder. Six months of basement dust seemed to lift off his shoulders.

He stood sharply, straightened his faded uniform, and snapped a crisp salute across the smoky table.

"Understood, Inspector Han Zhiyuan! Understood, Deputy Shen Wenchao! I will bring up the copy book right now, and I will deliver Mr. Chen Mingde's papers myself."
