# Chapter 140: Short Squeeze

At 9:30 on Tuesday morning in Shanghai, it was 2:30 in the dark Zurich night, yet Aegis Meridian's trading floor burned with bright fluorescent light.

Julian Ash stood behind his lead trader holding a paper cup of black coffee.

"Look at those scared little retail buyers," Julian Ash said with a cold chuckle. "Even with the provincial customs bureau issuing a rushed clearance notice at eight o'clock, nobody catches a falling knife. Hit the button, Eric. Drop the whole 4 billion yuan in short orders right on the bell."

"Dropping now," Eric replied briskly, his fingers flying across the keyboard.

At 9:30:01, a massive wave of borrowed Haiyue Group shares slammed onto the market.

Across Shanghai, inside Haiyue Tower's 38th-floor boardroom, Chief Financial Officer Lin Shufen gripped the table edge until her knuckles turned white.

"That is it!" Director Xu Minghui groaned, burying his face in both hands. "We are finished!"

Qin Yuhan stood straight beside the glass window with her arms folded, her dark eyes fixed on the screen without blinking.

Then, at 9:31:00, something happened that nobody in Shanghai or Zurich had ever seen.

In Shanghai, Zhou Ming set down his teacup and spoke one calm word into his headset. "Begin."

Across twelve quiet trading desks in Shanghai, Shenzhen, and Hong Kong, twelve walls of clean cash stepped forward at once.

On Aegis Meridian's Zurich screens, the huge 4 billion yuan sell wall did not push Haiyue's stock down another cent.

500 million yuan.

1 billion yuan.

3 billion yuan.

"Wait a minute!" Eric sat bolt upright, his eyes wide as the numbers flashed too fast to read. "Julian! Someone just swallowed 3 billion yuan of our short block in under a minute!"

Julian Ash's smug grin vanished instantly.

"Who is buying?" Julian Ash snapped. "Haiyue Group only had 800 million yuan in cash!"

"It is not Haiyue!" another trader yelled from two rows back, his voice cracking with shock. "The buy orders are coming from twelve different custody accounts in Shanghai, Shenzhen, and Hong Kong! All pure cash! No margin! They are sweeping every single sell order off the board!"

Three minutes in, Haiyue Group's share price crossed from red into bright green.

Six minutes in, the full 4 billion yuan short wave from Aegis Meridian was completely gone, swallowed without a ripple.

Nine minutes in, the green line shot straight up like a rocket climbing through clear sky.

"Buy back! Cover our shorts now!" Julian Ash roared, slamming his paper cup down so hard it split open across the desk. "Get us out before we cross yesterday's open!"

"I cannot!" Eric shouted back, his face draining white as he hammered his keys. "There are no shares left to buy! Every seller vanished! Someone just stacked another 5 billion yuan in buy orders right behind the price!"

Twelve minutes after the opening bell, at 9:42 AM sharp, Haiyue Group's stock slammed straight up against the daily limit and locked solid.

Bright green filled every screen from edge to edge.

Inside Aegis Meridian's Zurich trading floor, pure panic exploded.

Three red broker phones rang at once.

"The broker is calling a margin call, demanding more cash or he sells us out!" one trader screamed.

"Zurich clearing just froze our credit line!" another yelled, clutching his head. "The loss on our borrowed shares just passed 2 billion yuan, and with the stock locked at the daily limit, we cannot buy back a single share to stop the bleeding!"

Julian Ash grabbed a ringing phone with a shaking hand, only to hear the cold voice of his prime broker informing him that Aegis Meridian's accounts were being liquidated immediately.

He stared up at the giant wall board in horror. Ten years of ruthless short-selling, wiped off the earth in twelve minutes flat.

Two floors above, inside Northern Ledger's wood-paneled boardroom, Henrik Lindholm stood frozen beside the rain-streaked window with a dead telephone pressed to his ear. His unlit cigarette snapped cleanly in half between his trembling fingers.

"Who is backing Qin Yuhan?" Henrik Lindholm whispered to the empty room, his voice hollow with sudden dread.

Five thousand miles away in Shanghai, bright sunlight broke through the morning sea mist over the harbor.

Inside Haiyue Tower's 38th-floor boardroom, dead silence hung over the long stone table.

Director Xu Minghui sat with his mouth hanging half open, staring at the soaring green chart locked tight at the daily limit. Director Tang Weiguo took off his glasses, wiped them twice on his shirt, and put them back on in disbelief.

"Locked at the daily limit," Lin Shufen whispered, tears of pure relief shining in her eyes as her phone buzzed nonstop. "In twelve minutes! And look at the order book, Chairwoman! There are still 5 billion yuan of unfilled buy orders waiting behind the limit price!"

Her desk phone rang again. Lin Shufen answered, listened for five seconds, and let out a breathless laugh.

"That was Shanghai Commercial Bank," Lin Shufen said. "The branch manager is apologizing for yesterday's misunderstanding. He says our 6 billion yuan credit line is renewed, and they want to offer an extra 2 billion yuan for our new dock cranes."

Xu Minghui cleared his throat awkwardly, his face bright red with shame as he avoided Qin Yuhan's gaze. "Well, I always said Haiyue Group's fundamentals were strong."

Qin Yuhan did not even look at him.

She stood quietly beside the window, holding her tablet in both hands, staring at the soaring green chart in stunned disbelief.

She knew Haiyue Group's 500 million yuan buyback had not caused this. No local bank in Sha City had 10 billion yuan in clean cash to deploy across twelve desks in twelve minutes. Whoever had stepped into the market this morning had crushed an overseas wolf pack like stepping on a dry twig, without leaving a single name behind.

Her breath paused softly.

Her mind flashed back to the plain young man in the ironed white shirt sitting at the corner table in Sha City, unscrewing his green thermos while three hundred port chairmen held their breath.

At 11:30 AM, just as the morning session closed with Haiyue Group still locked solid in bright green, the phone in her hand buzzed once.

Qin Yuhan looked down at the screen.

It was a plain text message from Chen Xuan. No mention of stocks, Zurich, or Aegis Meridian. Just four ordinary words:

*"Did you eat lunch?"*

For a long second, Qin Yuhan stared at those four words. The heavy weight pressing on her chest since Sunday night melted away all at once.

A soft, bright smile broke across her tired face. Her thumbs moved quickly as she typed back:

*"Not yet. Are you free for a bowl of noodles at Old Street?"*

Three miles away, on the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics, Chen Xuan glanced at the reply on his plain black phone, smiled quietly, and tucked his green thermos under his arm as he walked out for lunch.
