# Chapter 15: The First Crack in the Concrete

Inside the Qinghe Fresh Florals van on Riverside Road, the rain outside faded to background noise.

Number Seven and Number Fourteen stepped behind the center console, leaning over Number Eleven's shoulders as he cleared the local evidence folders and projected a sprawling network diagram of corporate nodes and crimson arrows across the main display.

"Start from the baseline," Number Seven said quietly.

"Hongrui Real Estate Development Limited," Number Eleven began, his voice clipped and steady. "Registered in Yunxi County six years ago under Deng Yongchang. Registered capital: thirty million yuan. Active payroll: forty-two full-time staff. Over the last four years, Hongrui Real Estate completed exactly three residential complexes in the county seat, six-story walk-ups selling at four thousand eight hundred yuan per square meter. Even at full occupancy and generous margins, their legitimate four-year gross revenue cannot exceed three hundred and twenty million yuan."

Number Fourteen folded his arms. "A standard county developer. Big enough to bully Old Street shopkeepers and buy off Cheng Biao, small enough to stay beneath provincial notice."

"That is what their tax filings want you to see," Number Eleven replied.

He tapped two keys. A translucent amber overlay snapped across the diagram.

"This is the four-year capital flow reconstructed from the encrypted basement ledger and cross-matched against the interbank clearing mirror we froze twenty minutes ago," Number Eleven said. "Between January four years ago and last Tuesday, exactly four point eight billion yuan cycled through Hongrui Real Estate's Yunxi demolition escrow accounts."

Dead silence dropped over the van. Number Thirty-four's hands halted on the signals keyboard, and he turned slowly toward the screen.

"Four point eight billion?" Number Fourteen repeated, his voice dropping. "In Yunxi County? The entire county government's annual fiscal revenue is barely a fraction of that."

"Could it be pooled municipal financing routed through a local platform?" Number Thirty-four asked, squinting at the routing codes.

"No," Number Eleven said immediately. "Look at the direction of the arrows."

He zoomed in on the central cluster of escrow accounts. "When a local developer runs a corrupt demolition project, money flows inward from municipal banks and out to contractors, kickbacks, and land-transfer fees. Here, the demolition escrow accounts for Old Street and the south riverside parcel are not construction pools at all. They are a wash basin."

Number Eleven traced a bright crimson thread stretching outward from Yunxi County across three tiers.

"Large tranches arrive inside Yunxi County disguised as fragmented municipal infrastructure compensation, rural land-reserve reclamation subsidies, and distressed asset buyouts," Number Eleven explained. "Within seventy-two hours of hitting Hongrui Real Estate's escrow accounts, the balances split into clean batches under ten million yuan each and push outward. First hop: seven logistics and building-materials trading firms in Lanshui City, none owning a warehouse or freight truck. Second hop: four equity investment partnerships in Qingyan with nominee legal representatives. Third hop: three offshore trade-settlement shells registered in Hong Kong."

Number Thirty-four let out a low, sharp breath. "Layered smurfing on a sovereign-scale clearing rhythm. Deng Yongchang does not have the brains or banking reach to build that in three lifetimes."

"He did not build it," Number Eleven confirmed, pulling up a shareholder timeline beside the flow chart. "Look at the settlement dates. Every major outbound pulse through Hongrui Real Estate's escrow accounts lands within forty-eight hours of an uncontested land-reserve auction or a rushed Old Street demolition push. Deng Yongchang is not the owner of this pipeline. He is the county janitor keeping the floor swept so nobody opens the basement door."

Number Fourteen slowly lowered his arms, his gaze shifting from the crimson offshore nodes to Number Seven. "That explains why Deng Yongchang panicked over a frozen clearing account instead of caring about Yan Tiehu's wrist."

"It explains more than that," Number Seven said.

He stood still, eyes flicking once to the auxiliary monitor where three green icons blinked calmly for Number Nineteen, Number Twenty-seven, and Number Forty-two. Chen Mingde's wrongful detention case was already solved. The bakery hard drive, the blank DICOM cranial scan, the pre-typed diagnosis, the backdated detention warrant, and Ma Er's recorded payoff call from Yan Tiehu were all sealed and hash-verified. By sunrise, they could hand that spotless file to provincial inspectors, walk Chen Mingde out of Holding Cell Two, and put Cheng Biao, Deputy Director Fang Zhihua, and Yan Tiehu in cuffs before lunch.

If they had stopped ten minutes earlier, they would have cleaned up a county bully and walked away blind to the sinkhole opening beneath their feet.

"A county land-grab does not move four point eight billion yuan," Number Seven said, his voice low and hard. "And nobody panics like Deng Yongchang just did over a two-room hardware store on Old Street unless the ground underneath Old Street is hiding something worth killing for."

Number Seven turned to Number Eleven and Number Thirty-four.

"Do not close the Hongrui file," Number Seven ordered. "Trace every account upstream and downstream. Find out where that four point eight billion originated before it ever touched Yunxi County, and find out which beneficial owners sit behind those Hong Kong shells. Pull the county land-reserve archives going back twenty years, every mineral and geological survey filed for the riverside belt, and every official who signed off on Yunxi's demolition approvals."

"That pulls us into provincial registry mirrors and offshore clearing logs," Number Thirty-four said, already turning back to his console. "Deep queries from one van will light up regional nodes."

"We will not run them from one van," Number Seven replied evenly.

Number Fourteen stepped closer, glancing at the quiet green dot over Fuxing New Village on the left monitor. "Should I wake Chen Xuan tonight?"

Number Seven checked the clock. Half past midnight. Inside that modest third-floor apartment, Chen Xuan was sitting quietly with his parents after a long flight, letting them believe their son was an ordinary office clerk who came home to help his uncle.

"Not yet," Number Seven said. "We do not bring half a picture to him. Notify Number One on the encrypted relay to put the regional financial and archival teams on silent standby. By the time Chen Xuan finishes breakfast with Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang tomorrow morning, I want to know whether this four point eight billion is the whole hole or merely the first crack in the concrete."

"Understood," Number Eleven said, jaw set above his mask as his fingers flew back across the keys. "Opening the upstream trace now."

Beside him, Number Thirty-four began pulling archived county land-reserve deeds and riverside geological logs from the provincial registry mirror, forgotten seals and signatures scrolling silently upward.

"Pinging Number One now," Number Fourteen said, tapping his earpiece. "Regional financial cells will be live in ten minutes."

Inside the quiet flower-delivery van, nobody looked toward the door. The local rescue was over, and the real hunt had just begun.
