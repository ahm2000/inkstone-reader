# Chapter 15: Oh, Nice

Yunxi County, Old Street. Sunday, 7:00 a.m.

Good news travels twice as fast as a bus down a narrow lane.

By seven o'clock, every iron shutter along Old Street had rattled open, and shopkeepers stood clustered on the curb with steamed buns in one hand and buzzing phones in the other.

"They let him out!" Meng Xiaofang cried, hurrying across the pavement with her sleepy four-year-old daughter balanced on her hip. "Old Chen is free! They walked him out at dawn, apologized to his face, and suspended Director Cheng right away! The baker saw the whole thing!"

"Are you serious?!" Old Yu stood frozen in his tailor-shop doorway, still holding a brass thimble on his thumb. He hadn't slept a wink all night. Tucked inside his trouser pocket sat a folded slip of paper he'd written in despair at two in the morning: *I agree to sign.* It had weighed on his chest like a brick.

"It's true! And city anti-corruption investigators are arriving at nine this morning! Nobody's forcing anyone to sell at half price anymore!"

"Thank heavens!" shouted the grocer two doors down. "I heard even the eleven families who already signed might get their contracts reviewed!"

"Wait, how did Old Chen beat Hongrui Real Estate?! Who helped him?!"

Old Yu didn't join the guessing. Slowly reaching into his pocket, he pulled out the folded surrender note, stared at his own shaky handwriting for one quiet second, and tore it neatly in half.

Then into quarters. Then into eighths.

Looking down at the fluttering scraps, he realized his hands had stopped shaking. A man who hadn't signed could still look himself in the mirror, and that was worth more than any buyout check in Yunxi County!

Fuxing New Village, Building 2, Room 601. 7:30 a.m.

"Xuan! Wake up, everybody! Your Second Uncle is home!"

Chen Jianguo burst through the apartment door moving faster than his battered electric scooter had ever gone. Right behind him stood Chen Mingde, looking worn out, clutching a neatly folded thick blanket over his arm and blinking dizzily like a traveler who'd just stepped off a train from the moon.

"Mingde!" Second Aunt threw her arms straight around his shoulders, bursting into loud, unstoppable tears as the entire household erupted into joyful noise!

Chen Xuan was already sitting at the dining table eating a warm red-bean bun. Seeing his uncle standing safely in the doorway, a genuine, relaxed smile lit up his mild face. He finished chewing, swallowed, and nodded happily.

"Oh, nice," he said. "Just in time for breakfast, Second Uncle."

"That's it?! Just 'Oh, nice'?" Chen Yuan stared at her brother in frustration while dabbing her own damp eyes. "Brother, Second Uncle was locked in a cell for two days! Now they let him out at sunrise, the big bad police chief got suspended, and you act like the corner bakery just reopened!"

"They actually apologized to me," Chen Mingde murmured, still gazing around the bright living room in disbelief. "County Police Director Mai shook my hand himself! And Director Cheng, the tough one who threatened me yesterday, got sent packing right in front of everybody. They even poured me hot tea on my way out!"

"Someone up in heaven must've answered our prayers!" Chen Jianguo declared, beaming from ear to ear.

"Or maybe it was that 'friend who knows legal forms' Brother texted yesterday," Chen Yuan said, narrowing her eyes playfully at Chen Xuan.

"Nah, I bet it was my brother's magic 'Oh, nice,'" Chen Hao grinned from his bedroom doorway, hair sticking straight up. "Say it again, Brother, maybe my math exam will pass itself!"

Everyone burst out laughing, even Second Aunt, who was laughing and wiping fresh tears at the exact same time.

"Oh, and the noodles!" Chen Mingde suddenly remembered, lifting the folded blanket. "Last night around ten, somebody sent a steaming bowl of beef noodles with an extra fried egg and this thick blanket through the detention window! Best bowl of noodles I've eaten in my life! Sister-in-law, was that you?"

"Me?!" Liu Meifang put both hands on her hips. "I was pacing this kitchen all night! How would I sneak beef noodles into a police station?"

"Maybe the station has a nicer night kitchen than people think," Chen Xuan said calmly, taking another bite of his bun.

To be honest, watching his uncle sit safely at the table while his mother clattered bowls in the kitchen made his chest feel warmer than any big speech ever could. He'd asked politely, sent clean proof through the proper door, and the answer had come out fair. That was all he'd ever wanted!

"Alright, no more sitting around!" Liu Meifang announced briskly, tying on her cleanest apron. "We're heading out to the village early! Grandpa and Grandma have been burning incense and worrying themselves sick for two days. They need to see Mingde with their own eyes before lunch!"

Hongshi Village, Dongqiao Town. 9:00 a.m.

Tucked at the foot of a green ridge twenty minutes outside the county seat, Hongshi Village had one paved lane, two tiny grocery stalls, and an old irrigation reservoir older than anyone in the family could remember.

"Too thin!" Grandmother Zhou Guilan declared the exact second Chen Xuan stepped through the courtyard gate.

Barely reaching his shoulder, the eighty-year-old grandmother pressed a warm pickled egg into his palm before he could even say hello. Beside the gate, Grandfather Chen Fugen stood tall and straight under a faded straw hat, looking over his younger son Chen Mingde once from head to toe, then giving one satisfied nod as if the world had finally remembered its manners.

"Good, everybody's safe," Grandfather said gruffly. "Let the women talk in the yard. Xuan, grab the rods. The carp are biting early today."

By 9:30 a.m., they had settled along the quiet sandy edge of Qingshan Reservoir, where green water lapped gently against tall reeds. Chen Xuan unfolded a tiny canvas stool, cast his line lazily into the water, and leaned back with his eyes half closed in the warm autumn sunshine.

Ten feet down the bank, another fisherman was already sitting on a folding stool.

He looked to be in his mid-seventies, dressed in a faded gray cap and an old canvas vest with far too many pockets, yet his spine stayed straight as a wooden ruler. He gripped a plain bamboo rod and stared down at his red-and-white float with fierce, steady focus.

"Young man, your float is dead," the old gentleman remarked without turning his head.

"It's resting," Chen Xuan replied mildly.

"A fishing float doesn't rest. You're resting. Your float is dead."

"Mm," Chen Xuan agreed politely, and left his float right where it was.

The old man let out an amused snort. A moment later, he patted his vest pockets one by one, frowned, and glanced sideways at Chen Xuan.

"Young man, do you happen to have ten yuan in cash? The village auntie at the gate collects ten yuan for a day fishing ticket, and I walked out without my wallet again."

"Sure." Without even opening his eyes fully, Chen Xuan fished a crisp ten-yuan bill from his jacket and handed it over.

"Thank you. I'll pay you back," the old man said, tucking the bill neatly into his vest. Gazing out across the green reservoir, he added casually, "Anhe County upstream caught a heavy downpour last night. Water level here is going to climb nicely by Tuesday."

"You follow upstream rain reports?" Chen Xuan asked idly.

"Old habit. I read."

Beside Chen Xuan, Grandfather Chen Fugen leaned over and lowered his voice. "That's Old Zhao. Been staying at the village guesthouse for three weeks on a quiet vacation. Used to work inspecting reservoirs and flood-control ditches across Lanshui Province before he retired, something like that. Never remembers his wallet and never catches a single carp, yet every auntie in the village brings him boiled peanuts!"

"Mm," Chen Xuan murmured contentedly.

He thought nothing more of it. The autumn sun warmed his face, the reeds smelled sweet, and Grandmother had insisted on tucking a thick wool shawl over his knees before they left the yard. His float didn't twitch once for over an hour, and he couldn't have been happier!

Ten feet away, Old Zhao's float didn't move either. Yet the old gentleman wasn't watching the water anymore. Through narrowed, thoughtful eyes, he studied the completely calm young man dozing beside him in the sun. For one brief second, a look of sharp, genuine liking crossed his lined face, before he quietly reached into his vest pocket for his phone.
