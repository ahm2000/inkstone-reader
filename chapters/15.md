# Chapter 15: The First Crack in the Concrete

Inside the Qinghe Fresh Florals van on Riverside Road, the rain outside faded into quiet background noise.

Number 7 and Number 14 stepped up behind the middle desk. They leaned over Number 11's shoulders as he cleared the local evidence folders and pulled up a wide chart of company names and bright red arrows across the main screen.

"Start from the basics," Number 7 said quietly.

"Hongrui Real Estate Development Limited," Number 11 began, his voice crisp and steady. "Started in Yunxi County six years ago under Deng Yongchang. Starting money on paper: 30 million yuan. Full-time workers: forty-two people. Over the last four years, Hongrui Real Estate built exactly three apartment blocks in town, six-story walk-ups selling for four thousand eight hundred yuan per square meter. Even if they sold every single unit at high profit, their real four-year income could not pass 320 million yuan."

Number 14 folded his arms. "A normal county builder. Big enough to bully Old Street shopkeepers and pay off Cheng Biao, small enough to stay off the province's radar."

"That is what their tax papers want you to think," Number 11 replied.

He tapped two keys. A glowing yellow layer snapped over the chart.

"This is the real four-year money trail from the locked basement computer, matched against the bank records we froze twenty minutes ago," Number 11 said. "Between January four years ago and last Tuesday, exactly 4.8 billion yuan moved through Hongrui Real Estate's Yunxi buyout accounts."

Dead silence fell inside the van. Number 34's hands froze on his keyboard, and he turned slowly toward the screen.

"4.8 billion?" Number 14 repeated, his voice dropping. "In Yunxi County? The whole county government does not even collect a fraction of that in a year."

"Could it be city building money routed through a local office?" Number 34 asked, squinting at the bank codes.

"No," Number 11 said at once. "Look at which way the arrows point."

He zoomed in on the middle group of buyout accounts. "When a local builder runs a crooked tear-down project, money comes in from city banks and goes out to builders, bribes, and land fees. Here, the buyout accounts for Old Street and the south riverfront lot are not building funds at all. They are a giant washing machine."

Number 11 traced a bright red line stretching out of Yunxi County across three steps.

"Huge piles of cash arrive in Yunxi County dressed up as city road payments, rural land funds, and cheap debt buyouts," Number 11 explained. "Within three days of landing in Hongrui Real Estate's accounts, the money breaks into neat chunks under 10 million yuan each and shoots back out. First stop: seven shipping and building-supply companies in Lanshui City that do not own a single warehouse or truck. Second stop: four investment shell companies in Qingyan run by stand-in owners on paper. Third stop: three overseas shell companies in Hong Kong."

Number 34 let out a low, sharp breath. "Splitting dirty cash into neat batches on a national scale. Deng Yongchang does not have the brains or the bank pull to build that in three lifetimes."

"He did not build it," Number 11 confirmed, pulling up a date chart beside the arrows. "Look at the payment dates. Every big outgoing wave through Hongrui Real Estate's accounts lands within two days of a fixed land sale or a rushed Old Street buyout push. Deng Yongchang is not the owner of this pipeline. He is just the county janitor keeping the floor swept so nobody opens the basement door."

Number 14 slowly lowered his arms. He looked from the red overseas dots to Number 7. "That explains why Deng Yongchang panicked over frozen bank accounts instead of caring about Yan Tiehu's wrist."

"It explains even more than that," Number 7 said.

He stood still, glancing once at the side screen where three green dots blinked calmly for Number 19, Number 27, and Number 42. Chen Mingde's false arrest case was already solved. The bakery hard drive, the clean head scan, the pre-typed medical sheet, the backdated arrest paper, and Ma Er's recorded phone call from Yan Tiehu were all sealed and checked. By sunrise, they could hand that spotless file to provincial inspectors, walk Chen Mingde out of Holding Cell Two, and put Cheng Biao, Deputy Director Fang Zhihua, and Yan Tiehu in handcuffs before lunch.

If they had stopped ten minutes earlier, they would have cleaned up a county bully and walked away blind to the giant pit opening under their feet.

"A county land grab does not move 4.8 billion yuan," Number 7 said, his voice low and hard. "And nobody panics the way Deng Yongchang just did over a two-room hardware store on Old Street unless the ground under Old Street is hiding something worth killing for."

Number 7 turned to Number 11 and Number 34.

"Do not close the Hongrui file," Number 7 ordered. "Follow every account both ways. Find out where that 4.8 billion came from before it ever touched Yunxi County, and find out who really owns those Hong Kong shell companies. Pull the county land records going back twenty years, every earth and rock survey filed along the riverfront, and every official who signed off on Yunxi's tear-down permits."

"That takes us into provincial archive computers and overseas bank records," Number 34 said, already turning back to his desk. "Digging that deep from one van will set off alarms."

"We will not do it from one van," Number 7 replied evenly.

Number 14 stepped closer, glancing at the quiet green dot over Fuxing New Village on the left screen. "Should I wake Chen Xuan tonight?"

Number 7 checked the clock. Half past midnight. Inside that small third-floor apartment, Chen Xuan was sitting quietly with his parents after a long flight, letting them believe their son was an ordinary office worker who came home to help his uncle.

"Not yet," Number 7 said. "We do not bring him half a story. Send word to Number 1 on the private line to put the regional money and record teams on silent standby. By the time Chen Xuan finishes breakfast with Chen Jianguo and Liu Meifang tomorrow morning, I want to know if this 4.8 billion is the whole hole or just the first crack in the concrete."

"Understood," Number 11 said, jaw set above his mask as his fingers flew back across the keys. "Starting the trace now."

Beside him, Number 34 began pulling old county land deeds and riverside rock surveys from the provincial record room. Forgotten stamps and signatures scrolled silently up the screen.

"Calling Number 1 now," Number 14 said, tapping his earpiece. "The regional teams will be ready in ten minutes."

Inside the quiet flower-delivery van, nobody looked toward the door. The local rescue was over, and the real hunt had just begun.
