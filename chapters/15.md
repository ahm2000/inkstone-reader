# Chapter 15: Seating Order

Eleven chairs stood around the long oak table. Each chair showed how much its owner mattered.

The Standing Committee was the small group of men who really ran the county. The closer a man sat to the Party Secretary, the more power he had.

Seat One faced the door. It was Party Secretary Fan Weiguo, in a plain white shirt, slowly turning his teacup.

Seat Two was County Magistrate Tao Shu. He ran the county government. He had waited eight years for Fan Weiguo's chair.

Tao Shu smiled at him like a kind nephew. In Tao Shu's briefcase lay a gray folder with one word on it: *Fan.* Tao Shu had spent years filling it with Fan Weiguo's mistakes.

Seat Three was Kang Guangming of the Discipline Commission. His job was to investigate officials, even Seat One. Fan Weiguo could never guess what he was thinking.

Seat Four was Shao Wenhua, head of the Organization Department. His office chose and promoted every official in the county. He had not spoken in nine meetings.

Seat Five was Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu Wenzhe. He controlled the county's money and was Tao Shu's closest friend at the table.

"Secretary." Tao Shu spoke softly. "The provincial team comes Wednesday at nine. I propose a county self-check group, led by me. Every Old Street file will be in order. The inspectors will read clean paper."

He laid one finger on his briefcase. "I have kept a folder on you for six years, Secretary. It holds the Riverside land lease your brother-in-law won. If my group runs the check, the inspectors will never see it."

If Fan Weiguo said no, Tao Shu would tell the city that the Secretary did not trust his own county. If he said yes, Tao Shu would hold every book in Yunxi by Wednesday morning.

"Secretary Fan." Kang Guangming had not spoken for an hour. The room turned toward him. "Before any vote, I must read something."

He took a sheet from his folder.

"This morning the official car of Executive Deputy Magistrate Lu stood for forty minutes behind the private office of Hongrui Real Estate. At the back door. My officers watched it."

Lu Wenzhe laughed a little too loudly. "I went to tell Boss Dong to cooperate!"

"After the city opened a file on Boss Dong at four this morning?" Kang Guangming asked mildly. "Through the back door?"

Lu Wenzhe squeezed his cup so hard that it cracked.

Under the table, Tao Shu's hand opened and closed. His self-check group now had a man caught at a developer's back door.

Fan Weiguo felt his chest loosen. He made his move.

"Comrade Lu Wenzhe. From this minute you have no part in the Old Street project. Secretary Kang will seal your office records. Magistrate Tao, your offer is kind. The inspectors will decide when they come."

"Of course, Secretary." Tao Shu was still smiling. So softly that only Fan Weiguo heard, he added, "You sealed a deputy magistrate's office without a committee vote. I took the minutes myself. If the inspectors ask, your name is the only one on that order."

In Seat Four, Shao Wenhua capped his pen and gave Fan Weiguo one small nod. He still said nothing. In that room it was the loudest thing anyone did.

* * *

At twenty past five, Fan Weiguo was still signing the order that sealed Lu Wenzhe's office. His secretary stood close with the stamp.

"Faster."

Then the phone buzzed with a number Fan Weiguo did not know.

"Secretary Fan." The voice was young and polite and in no hurry. "A retired senior leader fished at Qingshan Reservoir today. Do not go near him."

"Who is this? Which leader?"

The line clicked. The caller was one of An Zhiyuan's men.

Ten minutes later the province's General Office called. The inspection team would come tomorrow, Monday, at nine. Not Wednesday. It gave no reason.

Fan Weiguo put the phone down gently. A retired leader at the reservoir, then a team two days early. For the first time in thirty years his own county felt like another man's house.

* * *

Chen Xuan sat on the step with a bun in one hand and his phone in the other.

*Mom: Your uncle slept nine hours and asked for more soup. Wear the thick jacket tomorrow. Did you eat vegetables, you boy? Do not lie.*

His face went soft.

He typed, "Tell Second Uncle I'm so happy he's eating." Then he typed, "I ate cabbage today," and bit the bun. It was a cabbage bun, so it was true.

His grandmother, Zhou Guilan, came out with a quilt. "Thanks, Grandma," he mumbled. Then he was asleep sitting up, with crumbs on his chin.

She laid the quilt over him. "Too thin, my Xuan," she whispered. The cat ate the crumbs.

* * *

Dong Haisheng, head of Hongrui Real Estate, sat with his collar open. He had a cold cup of tea he kept forgetting to drink.

The phone whispered. "Boss Dong, it's Gu Min. Executive Deputy Lu says he's never been in your office."

"Never been in my office? He sat at my table last Thursday and ate my crab!"

The line went dead.

Before he could throw the phone, it rang again. His whole back went wet.

"Boss Dong." Huang Boren, the lender everyone called Chairman Huang, always spoke softly, like a man talking to a sleeping child. "The inspection has moved to tomorrow at nine. Is the Old Street trouble over?"

"The old man is out, Chairman. He went home this morning."

"Which papers hurt most?"

"The Ankang ledgers. The clinic's injury slips, and every payment we made on them."

"Then they shouldn't exist tomorrow. Tonight, Boss Dong. A fire in a storage room is only a fire. And take the debt book your man carries. It has my name in it."

"Abacus called me from a noodle shop. He's shaking."

"Scared men do what they are told. Send him with the bucket. Go and watch."

* * *

The night cleaner at Ankang had worked there for four months. He hummed and swung a mop. He was the Detail's man.

Two men came down the corridor with a red plastic bucket. It sloshed and smelled of kerosene.

One was the clinic's night manager. The other was Xie Boyuan, the bookkeeper called Abacus. He wore a little brass abacus on his belt and held a black debt book against his chest.

Xie Boyuan pushed the door open with his shoulder.

On a metal stool in the middle of the room sat County Police Director Mai Zhenhua. Four city inspectors stood behind him, and a camera light was on.

Mai Zhenhua's men had taken the ledgers at ten, through the door the night cleaner left open. Then they had waited for whoever came to burn them.

Mai Zhenhua nodded. "Good evening. You're late. The ledgers have been waiting."

The bucket fell. Kerosene ran across the floor.

Mai Zhenhua opened the first ledger and read with his finger.

"*Ankang to Hongrui: eleven injury slips, three thousand yuan each.*" He turned a page. "*Lu Wenzhe, consulting fee, eight hundred thousand a quarter, six quarters.*"

A young inspector tapped his phone and whispered, "Four point eight million. That's eighty-six years of a duty officer's pay."

In the doorway, Dong Haisheng stood with his cigarette gone out. He had come to watch. Mai Zhenhua looked up at him.

"It was Boss Dong!" Xie Boyuan squealed at once, pointing with the debt book. "He said fire! I said the bucket was too heavy!"

"You dirty rat!" Dong Haisheng hissed. His open hand cracked across Xie Boyuan's cheek so hard that the brass abacus rang. He leaned in and said it low. "Your mother lives alone on Willow Lane, third floor, no lift. She goes out at seven every morning. One more word to them, and my men go to her door."

Every inspector heard it, and every inspector understood.

"Fed me? You paid me eight hundred yuan a night! You never once said thank you!"

"Boss Dong," said Mai Zhenhua. "You're not under arrest tonight. The city commission wants you to stay in Yunxi. And I'll keep your passport."

Dong Haisheng handed over the passport and the Singapore ticket folded behind it, slowly, like a man giving up a tooth.

In the car, he dialed with fingers that would not close.

"Chairman, they have the books. And the passport."

"I see."

There was a pause. Something clinked, like a cup on a saucer.

"Then I will send a man to your house tonight," said Huang Boren. "He will sit in the room with you. He will stay there until you can never again speak to an inspector. How long that takes is up to you."

Dong Haisheng understood. Either he kept quiet, or the man would make him quiet.

The phone slid from his wet hand.
