# Chapter 15: Oh, Nice

Good news ran down Old Street faster than any bus.

By seven o'clock every iron shutter on Old Street was up, and shopkeepers stood on the curb with a steamed bun in one hand and a buzzing phone in the other.

"They let him out," Meng Xiaofang cried, hurrying across the sidewalk with her sleepy four-year-old on her hip, "and the police apologized to his face! Director Cheng was suspended on the spot, and the baker saw all of it!"

"Is that true?" Old Yu froze in his tailor-shop doorway with a brass thimble still on his thumb.

He had not slept, and in his pocket lay a slip he had written at two in the morning out of despair. It said *I agree to sign*, which meant selling his shop to Hongrui Real Estate for half its worth, and all night it had sat on his chest like a brick.

"It's true, and city investigators arrive at nine today! Nobody has to sell at half price now!"

"Thank heavens!" the grocer shouted from two doors down. "They say even the eleven families who already signed may get their papers checked!"

"Must be nice," a voice muttered at the back, not kindly and not unkindly. "The rest of us just sold."

Nobody answered.

"But how did Old Chen beat Hongrui?"

"Who helped him, and who on this street has a backer that big?"

Old Yu did not join the guessing, and he took out the slip and looked at his own shaky writing for one quiet moment before he tore it in half.

Then into quarters. Then into eighths.

His hands had stopped shaking, because a man who had not signed could still face his own mirror, and that was worth more than any buyout check in the county.

---

Back at Fuxing New Village, the front door flew open.

"Xuan, wake up, everybody, your Second Uncle is home!"

Chen Jianguo burst through the door faster than his old electric scooter had ever gone, and behind him came Chen Mingde, worn out, with a thick folded blanket over his arm, blinking like a man who had just woken up.

"Mingde!" Second Aunt threw both arms around his shoulders and wept out loud while the whole apartment filled with happy noise.

Chen Xuan sat at the dining table with a warm red-bean bun, and when he saw his uncle safe in the doorway he smiled, finished chewing and swallowed.

"Oh, nice," he said. "You're just in time for breakfast, Second Uncle."

Silence.

"That is all you say, just 'Oh, nice'?" His sister Chen Yuan stared at him while she dabbed her own eyes. "Brother, Second Uncle sat in a cell for two days, they let him out at sunrise, the big police boss was suspended, and you talk as if the corner bakery reopened!"

"They apologized to me," Chen Mingde said, still gazing around the bright room, "and Director Mai shook my hand himself! Cheng, the hard one who threatened me yesterday, was sent off in front of everybody, and they even poured me hot tea on my way out."

"Heaven must have heard our prayers!" Chen Jianguo declared, beaming.

"Or it was that friend who knows legal forms," Chen Yuan said, narrowing her eyes at Chen Xuan. "The one Brother mentioned yesterday."

"No, it was Brother's magic 'Oh, nice,'" Chen Hao grinned from his bedroom door, his hair standing straight up. "Say it again, Brother, and maybe my math exam will pass itself!"

Everyone laughed, even Second Aunt, who laughed and wiped away fresh tears at the same time.

"Oh, and the noodles!" Chen Mingde remembered, and he lifted the blanket. "Around ten last night somebody passed a bowl of beef noodles with an extra fried egg through the detention window, along with this blanket, and it was the best bowl of my life! Sister-in-law, was that you?"

"Me?" Liu Meifang planted both hands on her hips. "I paced this kitchen all night, so how would I sneak noodles into a police station?"

Every eye turned to Chen Xuan.

"Maybe the station has a better night kitchen than people think," he said calmly, and took another bite.

Seeing his uncle at the table while his mother clattered bowls in the kitchen warmed his chest more than any speech could. He had asked politely and sent clean proof through the proper door, and the answer had come out fair, which was all he had ever wanted.

"Enough sitting around!" Liu Meifang tied on her cleanest apron. "We leave for the village now, because your grandparents have burned incense and worried for two days, and they must see Mingde with their own eyes before lunch!"

"Mom, Second Uncle needs sleep first," Chen Xuan tried.

"Sleep?" Liu Meifang pressed a hand to her chest. "Your grandmother has been cooking since five, and my own son wants her to wait longer?"

Chen Xuan stood up at once.

He knew that tune, and nobody in the family had ever beaten it.

---

Hongshi Village sat at the foot of a green ridge, twenty minutes from the county seat, and it had one paved lane, two tiny grocery stalls and a reservoir older than anyone in the family could remember.

"Too thin!" Grandmother Zhou Guilan declared the second Chen Xuan stepped through the courtyard gate.

She was eighty and barely reached his shoulder, and before he could say hello she pressed a warm pickled egg into his palm.

Beside the gate, Grandfather Chen Fugen stood straight under a faded straw hat, looked his younger son over from head to toe and gave one satisfied nod, as if the world had finally remembered its manners.

"Good, everybody is safe," Grandfather said gruffly. "Let the women talk in the yard, and Xuan, get the rods, because the carp bite early today."

Soon they sat on the sandy bank of Qingshan Reservoir, where green water lapped at the tall reeds, and Chen Xuan unfolded a small canvas stool, cast his line and leaned back with his eyes half closed in the autumn sun.

Ten feet down the bank, another fisherman sat on a folding stool, a man of about seventy-five in a gray cap and a canvas vest with far too many pockets, with a back as straight as a ruler.

"Young man, your float is dead," he said without turning his head.

Dead?

"It's resting," Chen Xuan said mildly.

"A float does not rest. You rest. Your float is dead."

"Mm," Chen Xuan agreed, and left it where it was.

The old man snorted, amused, patted his vest pockets one by one, frowned and glanced sideways.

"Young man, do you have ten yuan in cash? The auntie at the gate sells day tickets for ten, and I walked out without my wallet again."

"Sure." Chen Xuan did not even open his eyes all the way as he pulled a crisp bill from his jacket and held it out.

"Thank you, I will pay you back." The old man tucked it into his vest and looked out over the water. "It rained hard last night in Anhe County, upstream, and the water here will climb nicely by Tuesday."

"You follow rain reports from upstream?" Chen Xuan asked idly.

"Old habit. I read."

Hmm?

Grandfather leaned over and lowered his voice. "That's Old Zhao, and he has stayed at the village guesthouse three weeks on a quiet vacation. He used to hold a big post in Lanshui Province, and nobody in the village asks which. He never remembers his wallet, he never catches a carp, and every auntie in the village brings him boiled peanuts!"

"Mm," Chen Xuan murmured, content.

He thought nothing more of it, because the sun was warm, the reeds smelled sweet, and Grandmother had tucked a wool shawl over his knees before they left the yard.

His float did not twitch for an hour, and he could not have been happier.

Ten feet away, Old Zhao's float did not move either, but he was no longer watching the water, and as he studied the young man dozing in the sun, a sharp and honest liking crossed his lined face for one second.

Then he reached into his vest for his phone.

Whom was he calling, and why did he look so pleased?
