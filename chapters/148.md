# Chapter 148: Dinner at the Qin Courtyard

On Wednesday evening, Chen Xuan left Hengxin Logistics at 4:55, caught the number twelve bus across Shanghai, and walked the last three blocks to the quiet old lane behind West Lake Park.

Low gray brick walls lined the lane beneath bare winter plane trees.

Chen Xuan carried his canvas bag over one shoulder, with his green thermos in the side pocket.

Before he could knock, the side door opened from inside.

Qin Yuhan stood in the doorway in a soft cream sweater and dark trousers, her hair tied back loosely. Without her business suit, she looked far more at ease.

"Right on time," Qin Yuhan smiled brightly. "I told my father you are never a minute late or early."

"Good evening, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan said, holding out the pears and dried dates. "Fresh from my hometown."

Qin Yuhan took them with both hands, her eyes warming. "Thank you, Chen Xuan. My father loves dried dates in his winter tea. Come in out of the cold."

She led him past a quiet courtyard pond and two winter plum trees into the warm dining hall, where the smell of steamed fish and chicken soup filled the air.

Inside, Qin Zhengming rose from a round mahogany table in a dark cardigan. He looked tired from a long month of business battles, yet his welcome was warm and open.

"Chen Xuan, welcome," Qin Zhengming said, shaking his hand. "Yuhan told me twice how steady you were at the Sha Provincial Forum. Sit down and warm up."

"Thank you for having me, Uncle Qin," Chen Xuan replied easily.

He set his canvas bag on a chair by the wall, pulled out his green thermos out of habit, and set it neatly beside his rice bowl.

Qin Zhengming paused half a second at the worn green thermos sitting next to the fine white porcelain, then chuckled. "Good habit. Hot tea in winter beats cold wine. Let us eat."

The housekeeper brought out steamed sea bass, braised lotus root, winter bamboo shoots, and a clay pot of clear chicken soup.

"At the Sha Provincial Forum," Qin Zhengming said as he served Chen Xuan a piece of fish, "several big heads from the province asked where Hengxin Logistics found such a calm young man. Sun Haoran walked around with his chest puffed out like he had won a gold medal."

"General Manager Sun just likes a smooth meeting," Chen Xuan said mildly. "I only answered what was asked."

Across the table, Qin Yuhan poured him a bowl of soup, studying his face with quiet curiosity.

"Chen Xuan," she asked softly, "can I ask you something honest?"

"Of course."

"The morning after the forum, when Haiyue Group's stock was hammered from three sides," Qin Yuhan said slowly, "my father and I stayed up all night thinking we might lose half the company by noon. Then, inside ten minutes, every buyer attacking our shares pulled out. Two of the funds behind the attack were frozen before the afternoon bell."

Qin Zhengming sighed and shook his head. "Thirty years in business, and I have never seen a storm clear that fast. Those men simply vanished like smoke. Even my old friends in the provincial capital had no idea who stepped in."

Qin Yuhan kept her eyes on Chen Xuan. "You stood outside the hall on the phone for two minutes that morning, right before the market opened. I kept wondering if that was just a strange coincidence."

The dining hall went quiet for a beat.

Chen Xuan unscrewed his green thermos, took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea, and set the lid down with a light click.

"I was calling my mother in Yunxi County," Chen Xuan said calmly, his face completely straight. "She wanted to know if I had packed my wool socks."

Qin Zhengming burst out laughing and slapped his knee. "Look at you, Yuhan! You read too many business thrillers. Chen Xuan is a sensible young man who calls his mother and carries his own thermos. How could a twelve-thousand-yuan clerk move the whole stock market with one call?"

Qin Yuhan bit her lip, smiling despite the thoughtful flicker still in her eyes. "Fair enough. I will stop playing detective. Eat more fish, Chen Xuan."

"And think about my offer," Qin Zhengming added kindly. "If Hengxin Logistics ever gets dull, Haiyue Group always needs honest people in our supply office. I cannot promise 4:55 every Friday, though I can double your pay."

"Thank you, Uncle Qin, that is very generous," Chen Xuan said politely. "I am comfortable at my current desk."

Before Qin Zhengming could tease him further, the outer courtyard gate creaked open.

Steady footsteps crossed the stone path, followed by the housekeeper's joyful voice. "Old Master! You did not tell us you were flying back tonight!"

Qin Zhengming's face lit up as he pushed back his chair. "My father is back from the capital! He was supposed to stay until Friday."

Qin Yuhan stood up smiling too. "Grandpa always skips the last banquet when he gets tired of talking."

The wooden doors slid open, bringing a brief breath of winter air.

General Qin Zhengguo stepped across the threshold in a heavy dark wool overcoat, his silver hair neat, his back straight as a pine tree. Forty years of high command showed in every quiet step.

"Zhengming, Yuhan, do not make a fuss," General Qin Zhengguo said warmly as he reached for his coat buttons. "The meetings ended early, and I wanted a bowl of home soup instead of hotel food. I heard you had a guest for dinner, so I came straight in to say hello."

The words had barely left his mouth when his eyes reached the table.

General Qin Zhengguo's gaze landed on the young man sitting calmly in a plain gray jacket, holding a white rice bowl, with a green thermos resting right beside his elbow.

In a single heartbeat, the old general froze dead in his tracks.

His weathered hand stopped halfway off his coat button. Every trace of casual warmth drained from his face, replaced by sudden, stunned stillness. Without thinking, his heels drew closer together and his shoulders snapped straight, as if an invisible trumpet had sounded inside the quiet room.

Silence stretched for three heavy seconds.

Qin Zhengming blinked, staring from his father's stiff face to Chen Xuan's calm smile and back again.

"Dad?" Qin Zhengming asked in total confusion. "What is the matter? Are you feeling unwell from the flight?"

Qin Yuhan stood frozen beside her chair, her eyes wide. She had never once seen General Qin Zhengguo lose his composure in front of anyone, not even provincial governors or senior ministers.

Yet right now, the old general was staring at an ordinary logistics clerk in a gray jacket as if the sky itself had just walked through the front door and sat down for dinner.
