# Chapter 203: Home Before Dinner

A sharp early December wind swept down Century Avenue in Pudong as Chen Xuan stepped out of the Line 2 subway exit at 3:15 PM. The plane trees lining the sidewalks had shed the last of their dry leaves, leaving bare gray branches rattling against a pale winter sky. Commuters walked briskly with scarves pulled high around their chins, their breath turning into thin white puffs in the cold air.

Chen Xuan zipped his plain charcoal winter coat up to his collar. Over his left shoulder hung a sturdy canvas tote bag packed with roasted Changbai pine nuts, sun-dried wild hazel mushrooms, and mountain honey. Tucked snugly into the outer mesh pocket was his dented green metal thermos, its painted lid worn smooth from years of daily use.

On the crowded subway train, nobody had given him a second glance. Even now, walking toward the neighborhood wet market two blocks from the station, he blended effortlessly into the Sunday afternoon crowd.

A few paces ahead, an uncle in a faded navy padded jacket carried a mesh bag of winter leeks and white radishes. Seeing Chen Xuan come up beside him at the crosswalk, the uncle grinned warmly and shook his head at the wind.

"Cold one today, young man," the uncle said, sounding just like any retired neighbor out for groceries. "If you are heading to Old Zhang's meat stall, hurry up. The winter beef brisket goes fast on Sunday afternoons."

"Thanks, Uncle," Chen Xuan answered with an easy smile. "I am heading straight there. Are the radishes sweet this week?"

"Crisp as pears," the uncle chuckled, patting his bag. "Good for clearing winter heat. Go on, do not let the aunties grab the best cuts before you."

They parted naturally at the market archway. Inside the warm, brightly lit market hall, the air smelled of fresh cilantro, ginger, and simmering broth from a noodle stand near the door. A college-age girl in a cream knit beanie stood aside by the vegetable crates to let Chen Xuan pass with a friendly nod, and a delivery rider resting on a plastic stool near the tofu counter joked with the stall owner about the chilly December wind.

They looked and acted completely like regular Pudong neighbors. Not a single stiff posture, not a single sharp stare. Yet Chen Xuan knew with quiet amusement that every one of them belonged to the Zero Guard, keeping watch over his corner of the city with zero fuss and complete natural warmth.

At Old Zhang's butcher stall, Chen Xuan picked out two pounds of nicely marbled winter beef brisket, a neat tray of paper-thin lamb slices, and a handful of fresh beef marrow bones for the soup base. On his way out, he stopped by the vegetable stand for crisp chrysanthemum greens, baby bok choy, hand-cut firm tofu, and a bunch of fragrant winter scallions.

With two grocery bags in one hand and the canvas tote on his shoulder, he walked the quiet tree-lined lane toward Emerald Riverside Villa. By 3:45 PM, the low winter sun was already turning the river water a pale copper.

Chen Xuan pushed open the low courtyard gate, walked up the stone steps, and unlocked the front door.

The moment the latch clicked, a wave of indoor warmth wrapped around him, carrying the sweet, comforting scent of stewed pear and ginger tea.

Soft footsteps hurried across the wooden hallway floor. Qin Yuhan appeared from the living room wearing a loose cream knit sweater, soft gray lounge pants, and fluffy fleece slippers. Her dark hair was gathered loosely with a claw clip, and she had clearly just set down her work tablet on the sofa.

"You are back!" she said, her eyes lighting up.

Before Chen Xuan could even set the grocery bags down, she stepped right into his arms and wrapped her hands around his waist, pressing her cheek against the cold wool of his coat.

"Careful, I am freezing," Chen Xuan laughed softly, shifting the bags so he could free one hand to pat her back. "You will catch a chill hugging a block of ice."

"I do not care," Qin Yuhan murmured, refusing to let go for a few seconds. She pulled back just enough to look up at his face and frowned lightly. "Two days on a northern warehouse check and your cheeks look windburned. Did Hengxin not even book you a decent heater in the guest house?"

"The heater worked fine," Chen Xuan said, kicking off his winter shoes and slipping into the warm house slippers she had left neatly by the door. "The wind up north is just stubborn in December. Here, open your hand."

He unzipped the canvas tote, pulled out a small handful of the roasted Changbai pine nuts, and dropped them into her palm. The warm, nutty aroma rose immediately.

Qin Yuhan's eyes widened in delight. "Changbai pine nuts! And the shells are already cracked open!"

"Picked the thin-shelled batch so you would not have to wrestle them," Chen Xuan teased, lifting the insulated box and the grocery bags toward the kitchen. "I also brought back wild hazel mushrooms from the mountain and bought fresh beef brisket and lamb at the market. If I get the bones into the pot right now, the broth will be rich and golden by the time Mom, Dad, and Grandpa arrive."

Qin Yuhan popped a pine nut into her mouth, smiling happily as she followed him down the hall in her fleece slippers. "You spoil me too much, Chen Xuan. Let me wash the chrysanthemum greens and slice the tofu while you handle the broth."

"Deal," Chen Xuan said, hanging his winter coat on the wooden rack and rolling up his sweater sleeves.

Outside, the December chill deepened across Pudong. Inside the bright kitchen of Emerald Riverside Villa, the kettle hissed softly, the chopping board clicked in a steady rhythm, and the whole world felt wonderfully small and warm.
