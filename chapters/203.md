# Chapter 203: Home Before Dinner

By three-thirty on Sunday afternoon, Shanghai was bathed in gentle pale gold sunlight.

A mild autumn breeze rustled the yellowing plane tree leaves along the quiet residential avenues of the Pudong riverfront. The afternoon traffic flowed smoothly, and families walked their dogs along the riverside park trails.

Chen Xuan stepped off the subway train at the Lujiazui station, blending effortlessly into the bustling stream of weekend shoppers and office workers. He wore his ordinary dark blue windbreaker, faded jeans, and comfortable canvas shoes. In his left hand he held his scratched green thermos, while over his right shoulder he carried a large cloth shopping bag filled with fresh northern pine nuts, wild mushrooms, and two packages of prime marbled beef brisket he had picked up at the specialty market.

To anyone walking past on the sidewalk, he looked like nothing more than a relaxed twenty-something clerk who had spent his Sunday running grocery errands for his family.

Ten minutes later, Chen Xuan turned through the secure wrought-iron gates of Emerald Riverside Villa.

The private courtyard garden was immaculate. Golden ginkgo leaves had fallen across the stone pavers, and the water in the ornamental koi pond rippled softly under the afternoon sun.

Chen Xuan walked up the front steps, pulled out his brass house key, and turned the lock with a familiar click.

The moment he pushed the heavy wooden door open, the fragrant, comforting scent of simmering chicken broth and spiced star anise greeted him from the entrance hall.

"Yuhan?" Chen Xuan called out cheerfully, kicking off his canvas shoes and stepping into his soft house slippers. "Your traveling warehouse clerk is back."

Footsteps padded quickly across the polished marble floor.

Around the corner of the living room came Qin Yuhan. She was wearing an oversized cream cashmere sweater, loose gray lounge pants, and fuzzy pink fleece slippers. Her long black hair was tied loosely behind her head with a simple wooden hairpin, and a delicate white kitchen apron protected her front.

Her large, bright eyes lit up the moment she saw Chen Xuan standing in the foyer with his grocery bags.

"You really made it back before four!" Qin Yuhan exclaimed, stepping right up to him. She did not hesitate for a second, wrapping her slender arms around his waist and burying her face against the collar of his windbreaker with an affectionate sigh. "You smell like cold mountain air and cedar trees."

Chen Xuan chuckled, resting his chin lightly on top of her head as his arms wrapped around her back. "Did you miss me that much, Madam President? I was only gone for thirty hours."

"Thirty hours is a very long time when I have to eat lunch alone in that giant corner office," Qin Yuhan pouted playfully, pulling back slightly to look up into his face. She reached out two warm hands and patted both of his cheeks, checking his temperature like a mother hen. "Your face is cold! Did you wear enough layers in the north? I told you Changbai is freezing this time of year!"

"I had my trusty green thermos with me the whole time," Chen Xuan smiled, holding up the dented metal cylinder to show her. "Not a single snowflake touched me."

"Hmph, you and that fifteen-yuan antique," Qin Yuhan laughed, swatting his arm fondly before her eyes dropped to the heavy cloth bag in his hand. "More importantly, where are the pine nuts you promised me? If you brought back roasted peanuts from the subway stall, you are banned from the living room tonight."

"Authentic wild Changbai red pine nuts," Chen Xuan said proudly, lifting the bag and setting it on the entrance console. "Freshly harvested from the mountain cooperatives. I also brought back wild hazel mushrooms for the broth and two trays of marbled brisket from the cold-chain depot."

Qin Yuhan opened the cloth bag, picked up a handful of the plump, glossy pine nuts, and took a deep sniff. A radiant smile bloomed across her pretty face.

"Oh, these are amazing!" she beamed, cracking one open with her teeth and nibbling the fragrant white kernel. "So sweet and rich! You actually did a good job for once, Xiao Xuan."

"I always do a good job," Chen Xuan said with mock indignation, taking off his windbreaker and hanging it on the wooden coat rack. "Who was it that spent half an hour picking out the freshest brisket while other husbands were playing video games on the couch?"

"All right, all right, credit where credit is due," Qin Yuhan giggled, grabbing his arm and pulling him toward the bright, open kitchen. "Come on, Chef Chen. Grandpa called twenty minutes ago and said he is already halfway across the bridge. If the hot pot is not bubbling when he walks through the door, he will complain that the young generation has no sense of military punctuality!"

Chen Xuan tied his dark blue cooking apron around his waist, washed his hands at the stainless steel sink, and smiled with pure, unhurried contentment.

After flying two thousand kilometers and dismantling a four-decade criminal syndicate in the frozen northern mountains, there was no place on earth he would rather be than standing beside his wife in their warm kitchen, cutting winter vegetables for Sunday dinner.
