# Chapter 158: Business Trip to Shenzhen

The next morning, the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics hummed with its usual rhythm of clicking keyboards, ringing desk phones, and the faint hiss of the water dispenser near the pantry. While wheels turned behind closed doors in Handong, the open-plan office looked as ordinary as ever.

Chen Xuan stepped out of the elevator right on time, wearing a neat navy windbreaker over a light grey sweater. In his left hand hung his faithful dented 15-yuan green metal thermos filled with freshly steeped jasmine tea, and over his right shoulder sat a compact canvas backpack.

"Morning, Chen Xuan," called Little Lin from the dispatch desk as she sorted a stack of waybills. "Going camping after work?"

"Just staying prepared," Chen Xuan replied with an easy smile, setting his thermos down at his corner desk. He booted up his desktop computer, cleared three routine freight confirmations in five minutes, and leaned back comfortably in his swivel chair.

Before he could take a second sip of tea, a dramatic groan echoed from the glass-walled manager's office at the far end of the floor.

Every head on the 12th floor turned. Manager Hu Dewei stumbled out of his office, one hand clutching his lower back while the other waved a red-stamped dispatch notice from headquarters. His face was twisted into an expression of heroic suffering.

"Oh, my waist," Manager Hu moaned loudly, limping three steps toward the cubicles. "My old lumbar disc slipped again when I bent down to tie my shoelaces this morning. The doctor warned me not to sit on a plane for two hours, or I might end up in a wheelchair."

Several veteran clerks immediately lowered their heads and stared intensely at their monitors, suddenly becoming the busiest employees in Shanghai. Everyone knew what that red-stamped notice meant. Headquarters wanted someone from the 12th floor to fly to Shenzhen before noon to inspect a delayed southern container handover at Pier Nine and reconcile billing discrepancies with the local port operator.

Shenzhen Pier Nine was famous across the entire logistics industry for being a miserable errand. The local dock supervisors were arrogant, the yard clerks loved losing stamped forms, and visiting company reps routinely stood in the sun for half a day just to get a single signature.

Manager Hu scanned the silent floor with narrowed eyes. His gaze passed over the senior clerks who were furiously typing at blank spreadsheets and landed directly on young Sun Haoran.

"Haoran," Manager Hu said, adopting a heavy, regretful tone. "You are young and energetic. Headquarters needs someone at Shenzhen Pier Nine by this afternoon. Since my back is completely useless today, I am trusting you with this important assignment."

Sun Haoran froze, his face draining of color. He stood up awkwardly, clutching a ballpoint pen. "Manager Hu, I would love to help, really, but my girlfriend's parents are flying into Shanghai tonight for the first time. I booked a private room at a restaurant a month ago. If I miss dinner tonight, her father will never let me marry her."

"Work comes first, Haoran!" Manager Hu frowned, conveniently forgetting his own sudden backache as he straightened up a full two inches. "A young man should focus on his career. If you do not go, who else is going to catch the midday flight?"

The office fell awkwardly silent. Sun Haoran looked desperate, his shoulders slumping as he realized he had no way to refuse a direct order without risking his quarterly review.

At the corner desk, Chen Xuan took an unhurried sip of jasmine tea, screwed the lid back onto his dented green thermos, and raised one hand casually.

"Manager Hu, let me go," Chen Xuan said smoothly.

Every eye on the 12th floor snapped toward him. Sun Haoran stared as if he had just seen a miracle.

Manager Hu blinked twice, momentarily stunned that someone had actually volunteered for Pier Nine. "You, Chen Xuan? Are you sure? The flight leaves before noon, and you have to deal with the Pier Nine yard office directly."

"No problem at all," Chen Xuan said with a relaxed smile, patting his canvas backpack. "My weekly manifests are already reconciled, I packed a clean shirt just in case, and I have been meaning to try authentic Shenzhen wonton noodles anyway. Let Haoran go meet his future in-laws."

Manager Hu Dewei's agonizing backache vanished almost instantaneously. A radiant smile broke across his round face as he hurried over to Chen Xuan's desk without a hint of a limp.

"Now that is the spirit of the 12th floor!" Manager Hu declared, clapping Chen Xuan warmly on the shoulder. "Reliable, selfless, and always ready to step up for the team. I will sign your travel approval right now and put you down for the full daily meal allowance. Take your time down there and handle the paperwork cleanly."

Two minutes later, a freshly signed travel slip and an e-ticket confirmation voucher were sitting on Chen Xuan's desk. Manager Hu retreated happily into his office before Chen Xuan could possibly reconsider.

The moment the glass door clicked shut, Sun Haoran rushed over and grabbed Chen Xuan's arm with genuine emotion. "Brother Xuan, you literally just saved my marriage! When you get back from Shenzhen, I am treating you to the best beef hotpot in Pudong. No arguing!"

"Deal," Chen Xuan laughed, sliding his thermos into the side mesh pocket of his backpack. "Just make sure you remember to pour tea for your future father-in-law before you pour your own."

"I will remember!" Sun Haoran grinned.

Chen Xuan's single smartphone buzzed lightly on the desk. He picked it up and read a short message from Qin Yuhan: *Driver is parked by the south entrance. Flight boards at 11:30 AM. Did Manager Hu let you off?*

Chen Xuan typed back with one thumb: *He practically carried my suitcase to the elevator. Coming down now.*

He locked his computer, waved goodbye to Little Lin and the rest of the 12th floor, and strolled out toward the elevators. Behind him, his coworkers shook their heads in mild amazement, convinced that good-natured Chen Xuan had simply taken the worst errand of the week just to help out a younger colleague.
