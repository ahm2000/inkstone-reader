# Chapter 4: Runway Three

Nineteen minutes to the second after leaving Qingyan South Park, the three matte-black sedans exited the expressway onto the unmarked perimeter road leading to Executive and Diplomatic Gate Four.

No one rolled down a window or showed a badge. Thirty yards before the lead sedan touched the sensor strip, the steel gate parted smoothly. The two uniformed airport security officers beside the guardhouse snapped straight, their expressions turning solemn as they stepped back in silent salute without looking into the tinted windows.

On the far apron bordering Runway Three waited a matte slate-gray private jet with no corporate crest or bright livery, its twin engines turning at a quiet idle. Two motionless figures in plain dark coats flanked the lowered airstairs while the airport ground crew waited behind the yellow perimeter line fifty yards away.

The middle sedan stopped ten paces from the stairs. Number 7 stepped out first and opened the rear door.

Chen Xuan stepped onto the tarmac carrying his canvas bag, his inexpensive gray jacket fluttering in the runway breeze. He looked like an ordinary clerk who had wandered onto the wrong side of an international airfield.

The closer figure inclined his head slightly. "Number 9," he said in an even, clipped tone. "Perimeter and customs logs are sealed. No departure record will appear on civil registries."

Across the stairs, the second figure bowed by the exact same angle. "Number 11. Lanshui-Yunxi Regional Airfield has been notified under priority routing. Ground transport at the destination is staged."

Chen Xuan paused at the bottom step, looking calmly from one man to the other. "Thank you both," he said. "Number 9, remain in Qingyan. Monitor any unusual inquiries touching Hengxin Logistics, my personnel file, or my apartment lease while I am away. If anyone starts digging, do not block them at first. Trace who sent them."

"Understood," Number 9 replied cleanly. "I will log every query at the source and forward the chain to Number 1."

Chen Xuan shifted his gaze to the second man. "Number 11, hold the ground desk relay until we touch down in Lanshui-Yunxi. Flag any movement around Old Street Police Station before our wheels hit the runway."

"Understood," Number 11 answered promptly. "The relay stays live until your convoy clears the western apron."

Neither man glanced at Number 7, and Number 7 did not look at either of them. They operated like sealed gears inside a precision watch, each knowing his own duty and the single person he answered to, and nothing beyond that.

High above the apron inside Qingyan Air Traffic Control, senior shift controller Gao Jianming froze with his hand halfway to his coffee mug as a single amber flight strip dropped onto his priority rail from the central regional routing bureau.

Beside him, junior controller Lin Wei leaned over and squinted at the printout. "Section Chief Gao, what is this?" Lin Wei asked, frowning. "No charter company, and the tail number is not even in our civil index. Did the routing bureau send an incomplete military transfer?"

Gao Jianming did not touch his coffee. His eyes fixed on the seven-character clearance code in the upper right corner, and a quiet tension tightened across his shoulders. In fifteen years on the Qingyan tower floor, he had seen that code only twice.

"Put your pen down, Lin Wei," Gao Jianming said softly, his voice dropping an octave. "Do not enter that tail number into the local log, and do not ask the pilot a single question over the radio."

Lin Wei blinked, reading the sudden stillness in his supervisor's face. "Hold on, we have two commercial widebodies on final approach over the bay. Are we really bumping both of them?"

"We are holding both inbound widebodies in a ninety-second spacing pattern over the bay," Gao Jianming said firmly, pressing his transmit switch with steady fingers. "And we are clearing Runway Three for immediate departure. Do it now, and forget you ever saw the amber strip."

Lin Wei swallowed hard, nodded once without another word, and turned back to his radar screen.

Below, Chen Xuan climbed the carpeted airstairs into the hushed, walnut-lined cabin. Number 7 followed three paces behind, sealed the entry hatch, and took a seat by the forward bulkhead.

Chen Xuan settled into the wide armchair beside the left mid-cabin window. On the polished table beside his elbow sat a heavy crystal glass of spring water and a folded linen napkin. Without self-consciousness, he unzipped his canvas bag, lifted out his scratched green metal thermos, and set it squarely beside the crystal glass, placing the plastic-wrapped half of his cold red-bean bun right next to it.

The contrast between the dented thermos and the 50-million-dollar aircraft never crossed his mind. The thermos kept jasmine tea hot for ten hours, and the bun kept him from wasting food. Everything else was merely transport.

The twin engines spooled upward with a smooth surge. Within thirty seconds, the nose lifted off Runway Three, the landing gear folded away with a muted thump, and the lights of Qingyan tilted beneath the wing.

Once the jet leveled into a steady climb, Number 7 rose, stepped quietly to the table, and placed a thick manila folder bound with a plain black clip beside the green thermos.

"The first compilation from our Lanshui-Yunxi data desk," Number 7 said quietly. "Four years of Yunxi County's Old Street redevelopment project, the municipal tender awarded to Hongrui Real Estate, and every land-transfer record tied to Deng Yongchang and Yan Tiehu."

Chen Xuan unscrewed his thermos cap, pouring a small measure of steaming jasmine tea into the cup lid before resting his palm on the folder.

"Did the desk pull the county banking records yet?" Chen Xuan asked calmly.

"Not in full," Number 7 replied, posture straight beside the table. "The public deeds and tender minutes are here. The deeper clearing-house ledgers require Number 1's authorization, which is already in motion. Number 1 will call once we reach cruising altitude."

Chen Xuan nodded. "How long until we descend into Lanshui-Yunxi Regional Airfield?"

"Fifty-two minutes at current speed," Number 7 answered. "Weather over Yunxi is clear, light ground fog in the valleys. Our convoy will wait directly on the western apron when the hatch opens."

"Good," Chen Xuan said softly, taking a slow sip of warm tea. "Do not stand over me for the next hour, Number 7. Go sit up front and rest while I read."

"Yes, Sir," Number 7 said with a brief nod, withdrawing silently to the forward cabin.

Left alone, Chen Xuan removed the black clip and turned to the first page of the Old Street dossier. The quiet clerk from desk fourteen was gone; in his place sat a man reading land deeds, police intake logs, and shell-company transfers like a map of a battlefield he intended to clear before dawn.
