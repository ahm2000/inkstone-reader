# Chapter 4: Runway Three

Nineteen minutes to the second after leaving Qingyan South Park, the three flat-black sedans left the highway and turned onto the unmarked side road leading to VIP Gate Four.

No one rolled down a window or showed a pass. Thirty yards before the lead car reached the sensor strip, the steel gate slid open smoothly. Two airport guards beside the booth snapped to attention. Their faces turned serious as they stepped back in a silent salute without looking at the dark windows.

On the far tarmac beside Runway Three waited a flat gray private jet. It had no company logo and no bright paint, and its twin engines ran at a quiet idle. Two still men in plain dark coats stood on either side of the lowered stairs while the airport ground crew waited behind the yellow line fifty yards away.

The middle sedan stopped ten steps from the stairs. Number 7 stepped out first and opened the back door.

Chen Xuan stepped onto the tarmac carrying his canvas bag, his cheap gray jacket fluttering in the wind. He looked like an ordinary office clerk who had wandered onto the wrong side of an international airport.

The closer man bowed his head slightly. "Number 9," he said in an even, clipped voice. "Gate and customs records are sealed. No flight record will show up on public lists."

Across the stairs, the second man bowed at the exact same angle. "Number 12. Lanshui-Yunxi Regional Airfield has been given priority notice. Cars at the other end are already waiting."

Chen Xuan paused at the bottom step, looking calmly from one man to the other. "Thank you both," he said. "Number 9, stay in Qingyan. Watch for any strange checks on Hengxin Logistics, my work file, or my apartment lease while I am gone. If anyone starts digging, do not block them at first. Trace who sent them."

"Understood," Number 9 replied cleanly. "I will log every check at the source and send the trail to Number 1."

Chen Xuan shifted his gaze to the second man. "Number 12, keep the ground line open until we land in Lanshui-Yunxi. Report any movement around Old Street Police Station before our wheels touch down."

"Understood," Number 12 answered right away. "The line stays live until your cars leave the west tarmac."

Neither man looked at Number 7, and Number 7 did not look at either of them. Each man knew his own duty and the single person he answered to, and nothing beyond that.

High above the tarmac inside Qingyan Air Traffic Control, senior shift controller Gao Jianming froze with his hand halfway to his coffee mug. A single amber flight strip had just dropped onto his priority rail from the central air office.

Beside him, junior controller Lin Wei leaned over and squinted at the printout. "Section Chief Gao, what is this?" Lin Wei asked, frowning. "There is no charter company, and the tail number is not even in our civil list. Did the main office send a half-finished army flight?"

Gao Jianming did not touch his coffee. His eyes locked onto the seven-letter clearance code in the top right corner, and quiet tension stiffened his shoulders. In fifteen years on the Qingyan tower floor, he had seen that code only twice.

"Put your pen down, Lin Wei," Gao Jianming said softly, his voice dropping low. "Do not type that tail number into the local log, and do not ask the pilot a single question over the radio."

Lin Wei blinked, reading the sudden stillness on his supervisor's face. "Hold on, we have two big passenger planes coming in to land over the bay. Are we really holding both of them?"

"We are putting both inbound planes in a ninety-second holding loop over the bay," Gao Jianming said firmly, pressing his radio switch with steady fingers. "And we are clearing Runway Three for takeoff right now. Do it, and forget you ever saw that amber strip."

Lin Wei swallowed hard, nodded once without another word, and turned back to his radar screen.

Down below, Chen Xuan climbed the carpeted stairs into the quiet, wood-lined cabin. Number 7 followed three steps behind, shut the hatch, and took a seat near the front wall.

Chen Xuan sat down in the wide armchair beside the left window. On the polished table next to his elbow sat a heavy crystal glass of spring water and a folded cloth napkin. Without thinking twice, he unzipped his canvas bag, pulled out his scratched green metal thermos, and placed it right beside the crystal glass. Next to it, he set the plastic-wrapped half of his cold red-bean bun.

The sharp contrast between the dented thermos and the 50 million dollar jet never crossed his mind. The thermos kept jasmine tea hot for ten hours, and the bun kept him from wasting food. Everything else was just a ride.

The twin engines roared up smoothly. Within thirty seconds, the nose lifted off Runway Three, the landing gear folded away with a soft thump, and the city lights of Qingyan tilted under the wing.

Once the jet leveled into a steady climb, Number 7 stood up, walked quietly to the table, and placed a thick manila folder held by a plain black clip beside the green thermos.

"The first report from our Lanshui-Yunxi data team," Number 7 said quietly. "Four years of Yunxi County's Old Street rebuild project, the town bid won by Hongrui Real Estate, and every land record tied to Deng Yongchang and Yan Tiehu."

Chen Xuan unscrewed his thermos cap and poured a small cup of steaming jasmine tea into the lid before resting his hand on the folder.

"Did the team pull the county bank records yet?" Chen Xuan asked calmly.

"Not all of them," Number 7 replied, standing straight beside the table. "The public land papers and meeting notes are here. The deeper bank transfer records need Number 1's clearance, which is already moving. Number 1 will call once we reach cruising height."

Chen Xuan nodded. "How long until we land at Lanshui-Yunxi Regional Airfield?"

"Fifty-two minutes at this speed," Number 7 answered. "Weather over Yunxi is clear, with light ground fog in the valleys. Our cars will wait right on the west tarmac when the door opens."

"Good," Chen Xuan said softly, taking a slow sip of warm tea. "Do not stand over me for the next hour, Number 7. Go sit up front and rest while I read."

"Yes, Sir," Number 7 said with a brief nod, stepping quietly back to the front cabin.

Left alone, Chen Xuan pulled off the black clip and turned to the first page of the Old Street file. The quiet clerk from desk fourteen was gone. In his place sat a man reading land papers, police logs, and shell-company transfers like a map of a battlefield he planned to clear before dawn.
