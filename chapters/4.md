# Chapter 4: Watching and Waiting

In the private banquet room at Fuyuan Garden, the round table had six chairs, and one of them stayed empty.

Nobody looked at it.

It belonged to Tao Shu, the County Magistrate. He ran the county government, and only the Party Secretary ranked above him.

An hour ago his secretary had phoned to say Tao Shu had a bad cold.

"A cold." Fan Weiguo turned his teacup slowly. "What a pity."

Fan Weiguo, the County Party Secretary, sat facing the door. It was the seat everyone left for the top man.

He spoke so softly that people leaned toward him without knowing it.

On his left sat Lu Wenzhe, the Executive Deputy Magistrate. He was round-faced and comfortable, and he held the county's money.

Dong Haisheng, head of Hongrui Real Estate, sat below him.

At the far end, in the chair where the cold air from the door blew, sat Cheng Biao. He had come in last and said sorry three times.

Dong Haisheng stood first, holding his small cup in both hands, lower than Fan Weiguo's.

"Secretary Fan, a toast to you. Eleven shop owners on Old Street have signed. Only a few are left."

Fan Weiguo said only, "Haisheng. Sit."

Dong Haisheng sat so fast that his chair squeaked.

Fan Weiguo looked down the table at the man by the door.

"Director Cheng. I hear one of those eleven signed the day he left your station. A coincidence?"

Cheng Biao's hands found each other under the table.

"Secretary, the citizens understood the big picture."

"In my county, a signature is a signature. A signature taken in a cell is something else. It has another name. A crime."

In thirty years nobody had heard him shout. That was why men confessed to him before he asked.

"On Wednesday at nine, a provincial inspection team arrives. They are senior investigators from the provincial capital, and they will stay five days. They will read our files and talk to our citizens. If one old woman cries in front of them, I won't be the only one who answers."

"Five days," Dong Haisheng said thinly.

"From tomorrow, no more arrests on Old Street. Owners sign Hongrui's form or they don't. Is that clear, Director Cheng?"

"Clear, Secretary!" Cheng Biao jumped halfway out of his chair.

A phone buzzed on the tablecloth. Fan Weiguo read the screen, and his face showed nothing at all.

Then he rose, and every chair scraped back.

"Gentlemen, enjoy your dinner. The tea has gone cold."

Fan Weiguo left alone, and the door clicked shut. Then, as if someone had opened a window, the whole table breathed out.

A Hongrui manager wiped his forehead. "Magistrate Lu, you stayed so calm!"

"Calm like a mountain!" Cheng Biao said at once. He moved two seats closer to Lu Wenzhe.

Lu Wenzhe laughed and poured himself a full cup. "Old Fan is a good man. Good men are bad for business." He winked. "Now, Haisheng, shall we talk numbers?"

Dong Haisheng smiled with relief.

"Director Cheng, Hongrui pays thirty thousand yuan, in an envelope, for each signature you help us get. Eleven signatures. That's 330,000." He let the number sit. "Four years of your pay, earned in two months."

Cheng Biao put his toothpick in his mouth, which he did when he was happy. "Boss Dong is too kind."

"Your brother-in-law's demolition firm got the 38-million contract on Tuesday. And your wife's villa will be ready before spring."

"I will remember this all my life!"

"So will we." Dong Haisheng smiled. "Hongrui keeps good books, Director. Every envelope has a date."

That was a warning. If Cheng Biao ever turned on Hongrui, Hongrui could prove he had taken the money. The toothpick stopped, and his smile stayed on his face a moment too long.

"Magistrate Lu's service fee is five percent of the relocation fund." Dong Haisheng lowered his voice. "That is the county's money for moving families out. Six hundred forty million, for Old Street and the three blocks behind it, about 1,100 households and shops. So, 32 million."

"I prefer the words 'service fee.'" Lu Wenzhe grinned. Everyone knew it was a polite name for a bribe, and the whole table laughed.

Lu Wenzhe patted Cheng Biao's sleeve. "And Brother Cheng! Hong Bin, the county police chief, retires in spring. His chair costs one and a half million, I hear." In this room, a "chair" was a job that money could buy. "If Hongrui lends you the money, who says it isn't a loan?"

Cheng Biao's eyes lit up. Dong Haisheng filled his cup and clinked it.

"Scare the owner, and he sells cheap. The cheap shops make the money. The money buys the next chair."

Gu Min, Lu Wenzhe's aide, murmured from his place against the wall. "Magistrate Lu, someone sent a complaint to the province. That is why the team is coming. I bet it was Magistrate Tao."

"Or the clerk!" Cheng Biao slapped the table. "The hardware owner's nephew, from Qingyan. He came to my station tonight with a blanket and said, 'I want to see my uncle.'"

"A clerk with a blanket?" Dong Haisheng laughed until his gold chain jingled.

"A clerk with a lunch box. He slapped Brother Tie in the middle of Old Street."

"Then he is a brave fool," Lu Wenzhe said. "Watch him, Brother Cheng. But no arrests. Anything else is not my business." He smiled warmly and lowered his voice. "The chair you sit in came through my desk. One page from me sends it back. Until Wednesday ends, you see nothing on Old Street."

Cheng Biao's hand froze halfway to his cup.

"Understood, Magistrate!"

Lu Wenzhe raised his cup. "To Wednesday. May the inspectors go home with nothing."

Cheng Biao drank too fast and began to cough.

Nobody noticed that the most frightened man had already left. Fan Weiguo had sat in the highest chair, and he would answer for them all.

* * *

Later that night the shutter of the Yu Tailor Shop was half down. Four people sat inside around a sewing table.

Yu Changshun, the old tailor everyone called Old Yu, sat at the head.

Meng Xiaofang from the bun stall sat beside him. So did Han Guifu, who had taken over old Master Sun's shoe stall forty years ago, so everyone now called him Cobbler Han.

And to everyone's surprise so did Kuang Guihua, the vegetable seller everyone called Old Lady Kuang. On Thursday she had told Chen Mingde to sign.

She scratched at the tablecloth with one nail. "I was wrong. I said no shop is worth jail. Then Brother Tie came to my stall at noon. He said my grandson leaves the river school at four and walks the path home alone. He said, 'Sign by Monday.' That was all." Her hand shook. "Tonight I saw a man slapped for holding a brush. And a boy picked up his father's glasses."

"Brother Tie's van is still at the corner," Han Guifu muttered. "Big Tooth is smoking."

"Let him smoke." Yu Changshun pulled an ink pad out of a drawer. "I saw it at 2:03. The driver looked at Brother Tie and threw himself at the paint cans. Mingde never left his counter. The police came at five past. I will say so to anyone who asks."

He laid a printed form on the table. "This is Hongrui's offer, the paper every owner must sign to sell. Three thousand eight hundred yuan a square meter. The market price is eight thousand. Less than half, for forty years of this shop."

Meng Xiaofang pressed her apron against her mouth. "My stall is eleven square meters." "Forty-one thousand eight hundred, for the place where I raised two children. I was going to sign on Monday."

Meng Xiaofang wiped her face. "I'll say it too. My steamer is my life, but I saw that driver."

One by one they pressed their thumbs onto the ink and then onto a clean sheet. A thumbprint meant: I stand by what I say.

"Names can come later," Yu Changshun said. "Thumbs first."

Outside, across the lane, a delivery rider sat on his scooter with his helmet on. He was not going anywhere. He was watching, too.

"But who do we tell?" Han Guifu whispered. "The station is Cheng's."

Nobody answered.

Yu Changshun spoke slowly. "That boy slapped Brother Tie to his face. In two months, nobody else has dared."

"And Brother Tie let him," said Meng Xiaofang. "Because six men came out of Gong's Noodles with their bowls."

"Lucky timing," said Old Lady Kuang. For a moment even Han Guifu smiled.

"A clerk who slaps Brother Tie with his father on the ground," Yu Changshun said, "is either a fool or a dangerous man."

"Which one?" Meng Xiaofang asked.

Yu Changshun looked at the black thumbprints, and he did not know.
