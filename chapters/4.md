# Chapter 4: Watching and Waiting

In the private banquet room at Fuyuan Garden, the round table had six chairs, and one of them stayed empty.

Nobody looked at it.

It belonged to Tao Shu, the County Magistrate. He ran the county government, and only the Party Secretary ranked above him.

An hour earlier, his secretary had called to say Tao Shu had come down with a bad cold.

"A cold." Fan Weiguo turned his teacup slowly. "What a pity."

Fan Weiguo, the County Party Secretary, sat facing the door, the seat everyone left for the top man. He spoke so softly that people leaned in without realizing it.

On his left sat Lu Wenzhe, the Executive Deputy Magistrate. He was round-faced, easygoing, and in charge of the county's finances.

Dong Haisheng, head of Hongrui Real Estate, sat below him.

At the far end, where the draft from the doorway hit, sat Cheng Biao. He had arrived last and apologized three times.

Dong Haisheng rose first, holding his small cup in both hands, carefully lower than Fan Weiguo's.

"Secretary Fan, a toast to you. Eleven shop owners on Old Street have signed. Only a handful are left."

Fan Weiguo said only, "Dong Haisheng. Sit."

Dong Haisheng sat so fast his chair squeaked.

Fan Weiguo looked down the table at the man by the door.

"Director Cheng. I hear one of those eleven signed the day he left your station. A coincidence?"

Cheng Biao's hands found each other beneath the tablecloth. "Secretary, the citizens understood the bigger picture."

"In my county, a signature is a signature. A signature taken in a holding cell is something else. It has another name. A crime."

In thirty years, nobody had ever heard Fan Weiguo raise his voice. That was why men confessed before he even asked.

"On Wednesday at nine, a provincial inspection team arrives. Senior investigators from the provincial capital, staying five days. They will review our files and speak with our citizens. If even one old woman cries in front of them, I won't be the only one answering for it."

"Five days," Dong Haisheng said thinly.

"Starting tomorrow, no more arrests on Old Street. Owners sign Hongrui's form or they don't. Is that clear, Director Cheng?"

"Clear, Secretary!" Cheng Biao jumped halfway out of his chair.

A phone buzzed on the tablecloth. Fan Weiguo glanced at the screen, his expression unchanged, then stood. Every chair scraped back at once.

"Gentlemen, enjoy your dinner. The tea has gone cold."

Fan Weiguo left alone, and the door clicked shut behind him. As if someone had cracked open a window, the entire table exhaled.

A Hongrui manager wiped his forehead. "Magistrate Lu, you stayed so calm!"

"Steady as a mountain!" Cheng Biao chimed in at once, sliding two seats closer to Lu Wenzhe.

Lu Wenzhe laughed and poured himself a full cup. "Fan Weiguo is a good man. Good men are bad for business." He winked. "Now, Dong Haisheng, shall we talk numbers?"

Dong Haisheng smiled in relief.

"Director Cheng, Hongrui pays thirty thousand yuan in an envelope for every signature you help us secure. Eleven signatures. That comes to 330,000." He let the figure hang in the air. "Four years of your salary, earned in two months."

Cheng Biao slipped a toothpick into his mouth, a habit whenever he was pleased. "You're too kind, Dong Haisheng."

"Your brother-in-law's demolition firm got the 38-million contract on Tuesday. And your wife's villa will be ready before spring."

"I will remember this for the rest of my life!"

"So will we." Dong Haisheng smiled. "Hongrui keeps neat books, Director. Every envelope has a date."

It was a quiet warning. If Cheng Biao ever turned on Hongrui, Hongrui could prove every yuan he had taken. The toothpick went still, and Cheng Biao's smile held a fraction too long.

"Magistrate Lu's service fee is five percent of the relocation fund." Dong Haisheng lowered his voice. "That is the county's budget for moving families out. Six hundred forty million for Old Street and the three blocks behind it, roughly 1,100 households and shops. So, 32 million."

"I do like the phrase 'service fee,'" Lu Wenzhe grinned. Everyone in the room knew what it meant, and the whole table laughed.

Lu Wenzhe patted Cheng Biao's sleeve. "And Director Cheng, Hong Bin, the county police chief, retires in the spring. I hear his chair costs one and a half million." In this room, a chair was a post money could buy. "If Hongrui lends you the money, who is to say it isn't a loan?"

Cheng Biao's eyes lit up. Dong Haisheng filled his cup and clinked it against his.

"Scare the owner, and he sells cheap. Cheap shops make the profit. The profit buys the next chair."

Gu Min, Lu Wenzhe's aide, murmured from his seat against the wall. "Magistrate Lu, someone sent a complaint up to the province. That is why the inspection team is coming. I'd bet it was Magistrate Tao."

"Or that clerk!" Cheng Biao slapped the table. "The hardware owner's nephew from Qingyan. He showed up at my station tonight carrying a blanket and said, 'I want to see my uncle.'"

"A clerk with a blanket?" Dong Haisheng laughed until his gold chain jingled.

"A clerk with a lunch box. He slapped Yan Tiehu right in the middle of Old Street."

"Then he is a brave fool," Lu Wenzhe said. "Keep an eye on him, Director Cheng, but no arrests. Anything outside your jurisdiction is none of my concern." His smile stayed warm as his voice dropped. "The chair you sit in came across my desk. One memo from me sends it right back. Until Wednesday is over, you see nothing on Old Street."

Cheng Biao's hand froze halfway to his cup.

"Understood, Magistrate!"

Lu Wenzhe raised his glass. "To Wednesday. May the inspectors go home empty-handed."

Cheng Biao drank too fast and broke into a cough.

Nobody gave a second thought to the most worried man in the room, who had already left. Fan Weiguo sat in the highest chair, and he would be the one answering for them all.

* * *

Later that night, the shutter of Yu Tailor Shop was pulled halfway down. Four people sat inside around a sewing table.

Yu Changshun, the old tailor, sat at the head.

Meng Xiaofang from the bun stall sat beside him, along with Han Guifu, who had taken over Master Sun's shoe stall forty years earlier.

To everyone's surprise, Kuang Guihua from the vegetable stall was there as well. Only on Thursday, she had urged Chen Mingde to sign.

She scraped a fingernail across the tablecloth. "I was wrong. I said no shop was worth jail. Then Yan Tiehu came by my stall at noon. He mentioned that my grandson leaves the river school at four and walks the path home alone. He said, 'Sign by Monday.' That was all." Her hand trembled. "Tonight I watched a man get slapped for holding a paintbrush, and a son bend down to pick up his father's glasses."

"Yan Tiehu's van is still parked at the corner," Han Guifu muttered. "Niu Dali is out there smoking."

"Let him smoke." Yu Changshun pulled an ink pad from a drawer. "I saw it at 2:03. That driver looked straight at Yan Tiehu and threw himself into the paint cans. Chen Mingde never stepped out from behind his counter. The police pulled up at 2:05. I will tell that to anyone who asks."

He laid a printed form on the table. "This is Hongrui's offer, the paper they want every owner to sign. Three thousand eight hundred yuan a square meter. Market value is eight thousand. Less than half, for forty years of this shop."

Meng Xiaofang pressed her apron to her mouth. "My stall is eleven square meters. Forty-one thousand eight hundred yuan, for the place where I raised two children. I was going to sign on Monday."

She wiped her cheeks. "I'll speak up too. My steamer is my whole livelihood, but I saw what that driver did."

One by one, they pressed their thumbs into the ink and stamped a clean sheet of paper. A thumbprint meant only one thing: I stand by my word.

"Names can come later," Yu Changshun said. "Thumbs first."

Outside, across the lane, a delivery rider sat astride his scooter with his helmet still on. He wasn't going anywhere. He was watching too.

"But who do we even tell?" Han Guifu whispered. "The station belongs to Cheng Biao."

Nobody had an answer.

Yu Changshun spoke slowly. "That young man slapped Yan Tiehu right to his face. In two months, nobody else has dared."

"And Yan Tiehu didn't hit back," Meng Xiaofang said. "Because six men stepped out of Gong's Noodles holding their bowls."

"Lucky timing," Kuang Guihua said. For a brief moment, even Han Guifu managed a smile.

"A clerk who slaps Yan Tiehu with his father on the ground," Yu Changshun said, "is either a fool or a very dangerous man."

"Which one?" Meng Xiaofang asked.

Yu Changshun stared down at the dark thumbprints, and he did not know.
