# Chapter 4: Watching and Waiting

In the private banquet room at Fuyuan Garden, the round table had six chairs, and one of them stayed empty.

Nobody looked at it.

It belonged to Tao Shu, the County Magistrate. He runs the county government, and only the Party Secretary ranks above him.

An hour ago his secretary had phoned to say the Magistrate had caught a bad cold.

"A cold," Fan Weiguo said, turning his teacup a quarter turn. "What a pity."

Fan Weiguo, the County Party Secretary, sat facing the door, in the seat everyone leaves for the top man.

He wore a plain white shirt, and he spoke so softly that people leaned toward him without knowing it.

On his left sat Lu Wenzhe, the Executive Deputy Magistrate, round-faced and comfortable, who held the county's money.

Dong Haisheng sat below him.

At the far end, in the chair where the draft from the door blew, sat Cheng Biao.

He had come in last and apologized three times with both hands pressed to his chest.

Dong Haisheng stood first, with his small cup held in both hands and its rim lower than the Secretary's.

"Secretary Fan, a toast to you. Eleven shop owners on Old Street have signed. Only three are left."

"Haisheng," Fan Weiguo said. "Sit."

Dong Haisheng sat so fast that his chair squeaked.

Fan Weiguo did not look at him.

He looked down the table at the man by the door.

"Director Cheng. I hear one of those eleven signed the day he left your station. Coincidence?"

Cheng Biao's hands had found each other under the table.

"Secretary, the citizens understood the bigger picture."

"In my county, a signature is a signature. A signature taken in a cell is something else. It has another name."

He did not raise his voice.

In thirty years, nobody had heard him shout. That is why men confessed to him before he asked.

"On Wednesday at nine, a provincial inspection team arrives. These are senior investigators from the capital. They will read our files and listen to our citizens. Five days. If one old woman cries in front of them, I won't be the only one answering."

"Five days?" Dong Haisheng said thinly.

"From tomorrow, no more arrests on Old Street. Paper only. Is that clear, Director Cheng?"

"Clear, Secretary!" Cheng Biao jumped halfway to his feet.

"Sit. You are making the carpet nervous."

A phone buzzed on the tablecloth.

Fan Weiguo read the screen, and his face showed nothing at all.

Then he rose, and every chair scraped back.

"Gentlemen, enjoy your dinner. The tea has gone cold."

Lu Wenzhe hurried to walk him out.

"No need, Wenzhe," Fan Weiguo said, and he left alone.

The door clicked shut.

Then, as if someone had opened a window, the whole table breathed out.

"Wah!" Manager Kuang of Hongrui wiped his forehead. "Magistrate Lu, you stayed so calm. A real leader's courage!"

"Calm like a mountain," Cheng Biao said at once, shifting two seats closer to Lu Wenzhe.

Lu Wenzhe laughed and poured himself a full cup.

"Old Fan is a good man. Good men are bad for business." He winked. "Now, Haisheng, since the Secretary's tea is cold, shall we talk figures?"

Dong Haisheng smiled with relief and counted on his fingers.

"Director Cheng, thirty thousand yuan a signature, eleven signatures. That's 330,000." He let the number sit. "Three years of your pay, earned in two months."

Cheng Biao pulled the toothpick back out of his pocket and put it in his mouth, which was what he did when he was happy.

"Boss Dong is too kind."

"Your brother-in-law's demolition firm got the 38-million contract on Tuesday. And your wife's villa will be ready before spring."

"I will remember this, Boss Dong, all my life!"

"So will we," Dong Haisheng said pleasantly. "Hongrui keeps good books, Director. Every envelope has a date."

The toothpick stopped, and Cheng Biao's smile stayed on his face a moment too long.

"Magistrate Lu's service fee is five percent of the relocation fund." Dong Haisheng lowered his voice, almost politely. "Of 640 million. That's 32 million."

"I prefer the words 'service fee,'" Lu Wenzhe said, and the whole table burst out laughing.

"And Brother Cheng," Lu Wenzhe added, patting his sleeve, "Hong Bin's chair, the executive deputy's, costs one and a half million, I hear. If Hongrui lends you the money, who says it isn't a loan?"

Cheng Biao's eyes lit up like lamps, and Dong Haisheng cheerfully filled his cup.

Dong Haisheng clinked his cup against Cheng's and sang it like a nursery rhyme.

"The arrest makes the shop cheap, the cheap shop makes the money, and the money buys the next chair."

Cheng Biao laughed so hard he had to wipe his eyes.

"Magistrate Lu," Gu Min murmured from his place against the wall, "someone sent a complaint to the province. I'd bet it was Magistrate Tao."

"Or the clerk!" Cheng Biao said, and he slapped the table. "The hardware owner's nephew, from Qingyan. He came to my station tonight with a blanket and said, 'I want to see my uncle.' Flat as a plate."

"A clerk with a blanket?" Dong Haisheng laughed until his chain jingled.

"A clerk with a lunch box. He ate a bun in front of Brother Hu."

"Then he is the saddest enemy in history," Lu Wenzhe said. "Watch him, Brother Cheng. But no arrests. Anything else is not my business." He smiled warmly and lowered his voice. "A man who owes his chair to me should know how to be blind. Chairs can be taken back."

Cheng Biao's hand froze halfway to his cup.

"Understood, Magistrate!"

"To Wednesday," Lu Wenzhe said, raising his cup. "May it rain on it."

Cheng Biao drank too fast and began to cough, and Manager Kuang pounded his back.

Nobody noticed that the only truly afraid man had already gone home. His was the highest chair at the table.

* * *

Later that night, the shutter of the Yu Tailor Shop was half down, and four people sat inside around a sewing table.

Old Yu, the tailor, sat at the head, his gray hair standing up and his glasses on a string.

Meng Xiaofang from the bun stall sat beside him, and so did Cobbler Sun.

And, to everyone's surprise, so did Old Lady Fang of the vegetable stall, who on Thursday had told Chen Mingde to sign.

"I was wrong," she said, and she scratched at the tablecloth with one nail. "I said no shop is worth jail. Then Hu came to my stall at noon and said my grandson walks home by the river alone. Only that." Her hand shook. "Tonight I saw a man slapped for holding a brush. And a boy wiped his father's glasses."

"Brother Hu's van is still at the corner," Cobbler Sun muttered. "Big Tooth is smoking."

"Let him smoke." Old Yu pulled an ink pad out of a drawer. "I saw it at 2:03. The driver looked at Brother Hu and threw himself at the paint cans. Mingde never left his counter. The police came at five past. I will say so to anyone who asks."

"Hongrui's paper came on Tuesday." He smoothed a sheet on the table. "Three thousand eight hundred yuan a square meter. The market price is eight thousand. Less than half, for forty years of this shop."

Meng Xiaofang pressed her apron against her mouth.

"My stall is eleven square meters," she said through the cloth. "Forty-one thousand eight hundred, for the place where I raised two children. I was going to sign on Monday. I had the pen in my hand."

Old Lady Fang laid a hard old palm on her back.

"I'll say it too," Meng Xiaofang said at last, wiping her face. "My steamer is my life, but I saw that driver."

One by one, they pressed their thumbs onto the ink, and then onto the edge of the table.

"No paper yet," Old Yu said. "Paper later. Thumbs first."

Outside, across the lane, a delivery rider sat on his scooter with his helmet on, not eating, not going anywhere.

He was watching, too.

"But who do we say it to?" Cobbler Sun whispered. "The station is Cheng's."

Silence.

"That boy told Hu to his face he would remember the hand," Old Yu said slowly. "In two months, nobody else has dared."

"And he ate his bun," said Meng Xiaofang.

"My grandson says a bun means a calm heart," Old Lady Fang offered, and for a moment even Cobbler Sun smiled.

"A man who eats a bun while his father is slapped is either a fool," Old Yu said, and stopped.

"Or what?" Meng Xiaofang asked.

Old Yu looked at the black thumbprints, and he did not know.
