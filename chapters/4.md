# Chapter 4: Runway Three

Nineteen minutes to the second after leaving Qingyan South Park, the three matte-black sedans exited the airport expressway onto an unmarked perimeter road flanked by high concrete walls and rows of sodium floodlights. Commercial passengers bound for domestic terminals turned left toward the bright glass concourses a mile away. The black convoy stayed right, gliding silently toward the reinforced steel barrier marked Executive and Diplomatic Gate Four.

No one inside the convoy rolled down a window. No identification badge was held out toward the guardhouse.

Thirty yards before the lead sedan reached the sensor strip, the heavy steel gate parted smoothly into its concrete housing, and the two uniformed airport security officers standing beside the booth stepped back in silent, practiced salute. Beyond the gate, the vast expanse of the southern tarmac opened under a clear, star-pricked autumn sky, smelling faintly of kerosene, cool river air, and damp concrete.

The convoy followed a single narrow row of blue taxiway lights straight toward the far apron bordering Runway Three.

Waiting beneath the hushed amber wash of two low-glare apron lamps sat a long-range private jet finished in matte slate gray. Like the sedans, it carried no flashy gold striping, no corporate crest along the fuselage, and no ostentatious tail livery. Its twin high-bypass engines were already turning at a low, whisper-quiet idle, sending faint ripples of heated air shimmering across the dark concrete. Along the rear fuselage, a minimal dark-silver registration code caught the light only when viewed from directly alongside.

Two motionless figures in plain dark coats stood flanking the base of the lowered airstairs. Neither turned to look at the other, and neither spoke a word to the airport ground crew, who had already withdrawn beyond the yellow perimeter line fifty yards away.

The middle sedan came to a gentle stop ten paces from the stairs. Number Seven stepped out first and opened the rear door.

As Chen Xuan stepped onto the tarmac, the crisp wind from the open runway ruffled the hem of his inexpensive gray jacket. He carried his canvas messenger bag lightly in one hand, looking for all the world like a tired office clerk who had wandered onto the wrong side of an international airfield.

The closer figure at the foot of the stairs inclined his head slightly. "The Boss," he said in an even, clipped tone. "Number Nine. Perimeter and customs logs are sealed."

From the opposite side of the airstairs, the second figure bowed his head by the exact same measured angle. "The Boss. Number Eleven. Lanshui-Yunxi Regional Airfield has been notified under priority routing. Ground transport at the destination is staged."

"Thank you both," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Number Nine, remain in Qingyan and monitor any unusual queries touching Hengxin Logistics or my apartment lease while I am away. Number Eleven, coordinate the relay from the ground desk until we touch down."

"Understood, the Boss," Number Nine and Number Eleven answered in quiet, staggered harmony. Neither looked at Number Seven, and Number Seven did not glance at either of them. They operated like interlocking gears inside a sealed Swiss movement: each knew his own tolerance, his own duty, and the single hand that wound the mainspring, and nothing beyond that.

High above the apron, inside the darkened glass cab of Qingyan Air Traffic Control, the senior shift controller stared at a single amber flight strip that had just dropped onto his priority rail from the central regional routing bureau. There was no charter company name printed on the strip, only an unindexed tail number paired with an ironclad clearance code he had seen twice in fifteen years of service. He did not ask a single question over the radio. He simply pressed the transmit switch, held two inbound commercial widebodies in a ninety-second spacing pattern over the bay, and cleared Runway Three for immediate departure.

Chen Xuan climbed the carpeted airstairs with an unhurried step, ducked beneath the oval cabin frame, and entered the aircraft. Number Seven followed three paces behind, securing the main entry hatch before taking a seat near the forward bulkhead.

The cabin was hushed, lined in warm dark walnut paneling and hand-stitched charcoal leather. Soft recessed strips along the ceiling cast an even, gentle light that was easy on the eyes. There were no gilded fixtures, no loud screens, and no unnecessary luxury, only quiet craftsmanship built for twelve hours of uninterrupted thought across continents.

Chen Xuan walked to the single wide armchair beside the left mid-cabin window and sat down. Beside his elbow stood a polished walnut conference table holding a heavy crystal water glass filled with room-temperature spring water and a folded linen napkin.

Without a trace of self-consciousness, Chen Xuan unzipped his canvas bag, lifted out his scratched green metal thermos, and set it squarely beside the crystal glass. Next to the thermos, he placed the plastic-wrapped half of his cold red-bean bun from the corner bakery near Hengxin Tower.

The contrast between the dented discount-store thermos and the fifty-million-dollar long-range cabin did not seem to cross his mind for even a second. To Chen Xuan, the thermos kept jasmine tea hot for ten hours, and the red-bean bun prevented him from wasting food. Everything else was merely transport.

The twin engines spooled upward with a smooth, deep surge that pressed gently against the seatbacks. Outside the oval window, the blue taxiway lamps blurred into streaks, then swung swiftly onto the wide centerline of Runway Three. Within thirty seconds, the nose lifted cleanly off the concrete, the landing gear folded into the belly with a muted hydraulic thump, and the glittering grid of Qingyan tilted away beneath the left wing.

Once the cabin leveled into a steady climb above the coastal haze, Number Seven rose smoothly from the forward bulkhead, stepped to the walnut table, and placed a thick manila folder bound with a plain black clip beside the green thermos.

"The first raw compilation from our Lanshui-Yunxi data desk, the Boss," Number Seven said quietly. "It covers the past four years of Yunxi County's Old Street redevelopment project, the municipal tender awarded to Hongrui Real Estate, and every land-transfer record tied to Deng Yongchang and Yan Tiehu."

Chen Xuan unscrewed the stainless-steel cap of his green thermos, releasing a faint, comforting wisp of jasmine steam into the cedar-scented cabin. He poured a small measure into the cup lid, set it down to cool, and rested his palm flat on the cover of the manila folder.

"How long until we descend into Lanshui-Yunxi Regional Airfield?" Chen Xuan asked.

"Fifty-two minutes at current cruising speed, the Boss," Number Seven replied. "Weather over Yunxi is clear, light ground fog in the valleys, visibility well within minimums. Our convoy will be waiting directly on the western apron when the hatch opens."

"Good," Chen Xuan said softly. "You may sit and rest, Number Seven. Let me read."

Number Seven inclined his head once and withdrew silently to the forward cabin, leaving the walnut table bathed in calm reading light.

Chen Xuan took a slow sip of warm jasmine tea, opened the black metal clip, and turned to the first page of the Old Street dossier. The quiet office clerk from desk fourteen was gone; in his place sat a man whose gaze moved down columns of land deeds, police intake logs, and shell-company transfers with the cold, practiced precision of someone reading a map of a battlefield he intended to clear before dawn.
