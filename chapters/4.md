# Chapter 4: Watching and Waiting

Qingyan City, a quiet walled courtyard in the east of town. Friday, 6:15 p.m.

"Chief, this is Nine."

The low, steady voice came from a small encrypted speaker on a dark wooden desk. Beside it sat a white-haired elder with soldier-straight posture, a cup of tea cooling untouched by his elbow.

His name was An Zhiyuan, Chief An to everyone in the hidden protection force. His serial number was Two, meaning across the entire elite network guarding Chen Xuan from the shadows, only one person ranked higher than him.

"Go ahead, Nine."

"The Boss is on the 6:10 express bus to Yunxi County," reported Nine, the plain man in the gray jacket sitting three rows behind Chen Xuan. "At 5:52, his mother sent a voice message. His second uncle, Chen Mingde, was handcuffed and taken from his hardware shop by local police at 2:05. The Boss listened twice and said one word: 'Hmm.'"

"Only 'hmm'?" Chief An raised an eyebrow.

"Yes, Chief. Then he texted his mother to eat dinner first. He's completely calm, hasn't sent any signal, and ate a red-bean bun."

Silence settled over the study. Chief An looked down at his cooling tea, a mix of deep warmth and helpless frustration softening his eyes.

Nobody at Hengxin Logistics, not even Chen Xuan's own parents, had the faintest idea who Chen Xuan really was. They thought he was just an easygoing operations supervisor riding a county bus home for soup. They didn't know his file sat sealed at the highest secret level in the country, or that a quiet team of veteran security officers, analysts, and lawyers watched over every city he lived in. They certainly didn't know that one real emergency request from him could trigger an immediate top-level review across the province, though even his own force lived by strict rules: never break the law, never skip proper steps without real cause.

Yet his uncle had been hauled out of his shop in handcuffs in front of an entire street, and what had the Boss done? Told his mother to eat and chewed a red-bean bun.

Didn't he know one word from him could have investigators moving on Yunxi County before his bus even pulled into the station?

Of course he knew. He simply refused to pull strings on what might still be an ordinary problem. That was Chen Xuan's iron rule: handle things the normal, lawful way first, like any citizen, and never step in unless someone truly crossed the line.

To be honest, that was why an old veteran like Chief An respected him from the bottom of his heart, and why Chief An kept losing sleep!

"Nine, here are my orders," Chief An said crisply. "Stay on the bus and keep out of his sight. Contact Number Twenty-three in Yunxi, Gong Yi's unit. Tell them to keep quiet eyes on the family apartment and the Old Street shop tonight."

Wherever Chen Xuan's family lived, Chief An always kept trusted operatives blended into the neighborhood, just in case.

"Nobody touches the police," Chief An ordered firmly. "Nobody calls any official, and nobody pulls internal files. We do not lift a finger unless the Boss gives the signal. Until then, we watch from the outside and gather only public records. Clear?"

"Clear, Chief."

"One more thing." Chief An tapped a thin folder his aide had laid on the desk. Across the top page, a title was circled in red ink: Yunxi County, Old Street Rebuilding Project. Clipped to it was a note: Provincial inspection team arrives next Wednesday.

An old street, a hardware shop, a sudden arrest, and provincial anti-corruption inspectors arriving in five days. All in the same week?

Was that really an accident?

Chief An had spent forty years reading men and power, and he didn't believe in accidents.

"Tell Twenty-three I want public filings on that Old Street project," Chief An said quietly. "The approved relocation budget, the developer's loans, and which county office runs the buyout. Put it on my desk by morning."

"Understood, Chief."

The speaker clicked off. Somewhere fifty minutes down the highway, a hardware shop stood dark and the Boss was riding a bus home for soup. Chief An hoped it was a small matter. After forty years on the job, though, he was already bracing for a storm.

Yunxi County Bus Station, 7:05 p.m.

The bus hissed to a stop, and the familiar hometown air rushed in: fried dough, damp earth, and sweet woodsmoke drifting in from the countryside.

"Xuan! Over here!"

At the edge of the lot, his father, Chen Jianguo, waved wildly beside a battered electric scooter, wearing the same faded gray work jacket from his factory foreman days.

"Dad, it's a ten-minute walk. You didn't have to come out."

"Nonsense! Your mother kicked me out at six sharp. Get on!" He shoved a tiny plastic helmet into Xuan's hands. "I'll tell you on the way."

As the scooter hummed into evening traffic, Chen Xuan asked, "Dad, what happened?"

All the cheer vanished from Chen Jianguo's voice, replaced by raw fury.

"Two cops marched straight into your uncle's shop and handcuffed him in front of the whole street! Claimed he beat up Hongrui Real Estate's driver! I ran to the police station at three, and they told me, 'Case is under review. Go home and wait.' I told them I'm his older brother, and the smug clerk behind the glass sneered, 'Who do you think you are?' Who do I think I am?! I'm his brother!"

The handlebars shook with his anger!

"And that real-estate boss with the thick gold chain stood outside the shop smiling. Smiling, Xuan!"

"I hear you, Dad," Chen Xuan said steadily, holding the chin strap of the too-small helmet. "Did anyone see the driver fall?"

"Old Yu the tailor saw everything! Swears your uncle never left his counter. But who's going to listen to an old tailor?"

"We'll listen. Let's eat first, Dad. Then we'll know what to ask."

Chen Jianguo let out half a laugh and half a sigh. "You sound just like your grandfather." He steered into Fuxing New Village, and neither of them said another word.
