# Chapter 144: One Cup of Tea

Two gray water birds skimmed low across the glasslike surface of the Western Hills lake, leaving twin silver ripples that faded slowly into the reeds. Morning sunlight slanted beneath the cedar eaves of the pavilion, warming the stone table between Chen Xuan and Elder Song.

Elder Song set his white porcelain teacup down with quiet care and rested both thin, veined hands flat on the polished stone.

"Before we speak another word, young friend," Elder Song said in his steady, raspy voice, "I want to make you a solemn promise on my ninety years of life. I will never ask for your surname, your given name, your workplace, or your family. As long as I draw breath, not a single person in Beijing, not Premier Gu Chengshan and not even my oldest comrade Elder Qin Zhengguo, will ever learn who sat across from me today or what your face looks like. I will carry this morning straight into the earth with me."

Chen Xuan inclined his head respectfully, genuine warmth in his eyes. "Thank you, Elder Song. That promise means more to me than anything else you could offer."

"It is the very least an old man owes you," Elder Song replied gently. "When you handed over the 300 trillion yuan sovereign reserve, you did not attach a single demand, a single title, or a single seat at the table. You asked only that the money be used cleanly for the country and that your quiet life remain undisturbed. If we could not even guard that small wish for you, we would not be worthy of sitting in those offices down in the city."

Chen Xuan picked up his cup and took an unhurried sip. "As long as the money reaches the right places, I have no complaints."

Elder Song's eyes brightened with deep gratitude. "It already has, young friend. You may not see the reports that cross our desks every morning, so let an old man tell you plainly what your gift has done."

He leaned forward slightly, his voice quiet yet firm with pride. "Within seventy-two hours of the reserve being verified, three foreign financial coalitions that had been probing our currency and energy lifelines pulled back completely. Not a single shot was fired, yet a storm that could have shaken tens of millions of livelihoods vanished overnight."

Elder Song held up three thin fingers, counting softly. "Beyond that, the treasury has already locked in thirty-year funding for rural hospitals, clean drinking water across the western plateaus, new schools in mountain counties, and every core laboratory that used to beg for research grants. Working families will never know whose name stood behind it, yet their children will grow up warmer, healthier, and safer because of it."

Chen Xuan listened quietly, a calm, content smile resting on his lips. That was all he had ever wanted. Money sitting in a vault was only dead paper and cold digits. Money turning into warm classrooms, lighted highways, and steady rice bowls was the only purpose it had.

Elder Song watched the young man's peaceful expression for a long moment. A flicker of honest, grandfatherly curiosity appeared in his eyes.

"May an old man ask one personal question, without crossing my promise?" Elder Song asked softly.

"Of course, Elder Song."

"With resources that could shake half the globe," Elder Song murmured, "why choose a modest desk job, a cheap windbreaker, and a quiet kitchen? Most men who hold even a fraction of that power spend their whole lives climbing higher until they forget how to breathe."

Chen Xuan smiled and turned the small white teacup slowly between his fingers.

"Because high places are cold, Elder Song," Chen Xuan said simply. "I have seen plenty of tall towers and grand rooms. People up there spend every meal calculating who is sitting to their left and who is waiting behind their chair. Nobody actually tastes their food."

He glanced down at his dented green thermos, his eyes softening. "Down at street level, the morning millet porridge is hot, the sweet potato vendor saves the softest ones for my wife, and the biggest argument of the week is whether my grandfather-in-law cheated during our chess game. When you already have everything you could ever want in one small kitchen, why trade it for a cold throne?"

For a heartbeat, the pavilion was completely silent save for the gentle whisper of autumn wind through the pines.

Then Elder Song let out a long, warm sigh, raising the back of his hand to dab lightly at the corner of his eye.

"A true sage hidden in the marketplace," Elder Song whispered, shaking his head with deep admiration. "For ninety years, I have met generals, scholars, and leaders from every corner of the earth. None of them ever put it more clearly than you just did. We fight all our lives for a peaceful country, and you are simply living the exact peaceful life we fought to protect."

"You give me too much credit, Elder Song," Chen Xuan chuckled modestly. "I am just a lazy guy who likes clocking out at 4:55 PM and cooking braised pork ribs."

"Haha! If only more people were lazy in the exact same way," Elder Song laughed warmly. He lifted the purple clay teapot and poured the final two cups of fragrant Wuyi rock tea, dividing the last golden drops evenly between them.

"One final cup, young friend," Elder Song said, raising his porcelain cup with both hands. "On behalf of a grateful nation, and from one old man who can now sleep soundly at night, thank you."

Chen Xuan raised his own cup with both hands, meeting the old man's clear gaze with quiet respect.

"To peaceful days and warm kitchens, Elder Song."

The two white porcelain cups met with a soft, clear chime. Outside the pavilion, a gust of crisp late November wind shook loose a shower of crimson maple leaves, sending them spinning gracefully down onto the sunlit lake.
