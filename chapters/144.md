# Chapter 144: One Cup of Tea

Both men took a slow sip of warm Wuyi tea. Outside the wooden pavilion, two gray water birds skimmed across the Western Hills lake, leaving thin ripples on the quiet water.

Ninety-year-old Elder Song set his cup down gently and folded his thin, spotted hands on the round stone table.

"Before we talk," Elder Song said in his raspy voice, "know one thing. I will not ask for your surname, given name, hometown, or family. If you want to stay a stranger to Beijing, I will carry that silence into the ground with me."

Chen Xuan inclined his head with warm respect. "Thank you, Elder Song."

"Do not thank me," Elder Song replied with a faint smile. "After what you did for this country, keeping my mouth shut is the least an old man can manage."

He glanced briefly toward the autumn hills beyond the lake.

"I was eighteen when I carried a rifle into the northern mountains," Elder Song said quietly. "My cloth shoes had holes in the soles, so we stuffed dry grass inside to keep our toes from freezing. Half the boys in my squad never saw twenty. We buried them in shallow earth and promised to build a house where their younger brothers and sisters would never sleep hungry."

He tapped his fingers once against the stone.

"For fifty years, we poured cement, laid rails, and watched lights come on in every village. I thought the hard part was over." Elder Song sighed slowly. "I was wrong. War is loud, so you see the enemy. Rot is quiet. It wears a clean collar, speaks politely at banquets, and smiles while hollowing out the beams above your head."

Chen Xuan listened calmly, hands resting beside his teacup.

"Three years ago, I knew the house was sick," Elder Song continued. "Too many smooth reports, too many honest county heads pushed aside while greedy talkers climbed two ranks a year. Yet I am ninety. My old comrades are gone, and pulling one thread blindly risked bringing the banks down on top of ordinary families."

Elder Song looked straight into Chen Xuan's steady eyes, his gaze shining with heartfelt gratitude.

"Then you stepped in," the old man said softly. "No speech, no banner, no headline. You handed Gao Zhengyi and Zhou Zhenhua clean ledgers nobody could dispute, cut out the rotten roots in one stroke, and left the healthy timber standing."

He leaned forward, pressing his palm gently onto the stone table.

"More than anything else, young man, I asked you here to thank you from the bottom of my heart for that 300 trillion yuan," Elder Song said, his raspy voice trembling slightly. "When our banks stood on the edge of a cliff facing 100 trillion yuan of hidden rot, you sent 300 trillion yuan of clean, legal money free to the country, with up to 500 trillion yuan ready behind it across China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank. Not one fen of interest. Not one share asked. You held the whole roof steady so millions of ordinary families never lost a night's sleep."

Chen Xuan did not boast. He reached calmly across the stone table, picked up his scratched green thermos, and unscrewed the dented metal lid with a soft squeak.

A wisp of jasmine steam rose into the cool air. Chen Xuan took a slow sip, screwed the lid back on neatly, and set the thermos beside the clay teapot.

"Money sitting in vaults does nothing if the country shakes, Elder Song," Chen Xuan replied plainly. "If the banks panic and the streets go cold, numbers in an account are just ink on paper. I only put it where it could keep the floor steady."

Elder Song stared at him for a quiet second, then exhaled in wonder.

"The Finance Ministry finished its third count yesterday," Elder Song told him. "Across forty cleaned-out departments, recovered stolen state wealth has passed 82 trillion yuan. Land, mines, port shares, overseas accounts, all back in the public treasury. Yesterday morning, Gu Chengshan signed the first three orders using that returned money. Every rural county hospital in the western provinces gets new equipment this winter. Every village school gets heated classrooms and hot lunches. Every retired factory worker and old soldier gets a pension raise before New Year."

Elder Song's voice softened further. "On behalf of the boys we left in the northern hills seventy years ago, thank you."

Chen Xuan lowered his head in sincere respect. "I do not deserve thanks from them, Elder Song. They paid with their lives. I only sent a few folders and moved some spare cash."

"Folders and cash nobody else on earth had the power or heart to send," Elder Song said firmly. He pulled an unfiltered cigarette from his cotton jacket and held it beneath his nose with a boyish grin. "My doctor took my lighter six months ago. Said my lungs are older than the train station. Still, I like smelling tobacco with a good guest."

Chen Xuan chuckled softly. "Your doctor is looking out for you."

"He is a tyrant," Elder Song muttered fondly, tucking the unlit cigarette away. Then his bright eyes turned serious again.

"Now comes the real reason I asked you here," Elder Song said quietly. "In ninety years, I learned one strict rule: never let someone who saved your house walk away empty-handed. Tell me plainly, young man. What do you want? A quiet seat where no official dares touch you? A written seal opening any door in Beijing? A title for your family? Name it. While I still breathe, my word carries weight."

The pavilion went still. Only lake water lapped softly against the stones.

Chen Xuan rested one hand on his scratched green thermos and met the old man's eyes with an honest smile.

"All I want is for ordinary people in Yunxi County and Shanghai to live and run their shops in peace," Chen Xuan said simply. "And for myself, I just want to leave work at 4:55 on Fridays."

For three full seconds, Elder Song did not move.

He stared at the young man in a cheap windbreaker beside a fifteen-yuan green thermos. A young man who had given 300 trillion yuan of clean wealth to the nation for free, helped recover 82 trillion yuan in stolen assets, shaken forty ministries, and could have asked for half the sky.

Suddenly, Elder Song let out a soft, trembling laugh. It rose into a warm chuckle that shook his thin shoulders until clear tears shone in the corners of his old eyes.

He dabbed gently at his eyes with a white handkerchief, still laughing softly.

"Forty years in Beijing," Elder Song whispered in wonder. "I have met ministers, generals, tycoons, and foreign presidents. Every single one wanted a bigger chair, a louder name, or a taller wall. And the young man who gave the country 300 trillion yuan without blinking only wants ordinary people in peace and to go home at 4:55 on Fridays."

"Five o'clock traffic in Shanghai is terrible," Chen Xuan explained mildly. "Leaving five minutes early saves twenty minutes on the bus."

Elder Song laughed all over again, his face glowing with deep relief.

"You have my word," Elder Song said softly, his bright eyes steady. "Yunxi County and Shanghai will stay clean. No greedy hand will touch their streets, and no bully will bother an ordinary shopkeeper while my name stands on the books. And nobody will ever send a file to bother your Friday afternoons."

"Then this cup of tea was well worth the trip," Chen Xuan said warmly.

Elder Song poured the final drops from the clay pot. Together, the ninety-year-old founder and the quiet office clerk raised their porcelain cups in silence and drank to a clean, peaceful morning by the lake.
