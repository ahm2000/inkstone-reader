# Chapter 144: One Cup of Tea

Both men sipped warm Wuyi tea as two gray water birds skimmed across the Western Hills lake outside the pavilion.

Ninety year old Elder Song set his cup down and folded his thin hands on the stone table.

"Before we talk," Elder Song said in his raspy voice, "know one thing. I will not ask for your surname, given name, hometown, or family. If you want to stay a stranger to Beijing, I will carry that silence into the ground."

Chen Xuan inclined his head respectfully. "Thank you, Elder Song."

"Do not thank me," Elder Song replied with a faint smile. "After what you did for this country, keeping quiet is the least an old man can manage."

He glanced toward the autumn hills beyond the lake.

"I was eighteen when I carried a rifle into the northern mountains," Elder Song said quietly. "Our cloth shoes had holes, so we stuffed dry grass inside to keep our toes from freezing. Half the boys in my squad never saw twenty. We buried them in shallow earth and promised to build a house where their younger brothers and sisters would never sleep hungry."

He tapped his fingers against the stone.

"For fifty years, we poured cement, laid rails, and watched lights come on in every village. I thought the hard part was over." Elder Song sighed. "I was wrong. War is loud, so you see the enemy. Rot is quiet. It wears a clean collar, speaks politely at banquets, and smiles while hollowing out the beams above your head."

Chen Xuan listened calmly, hands resting beside his teacup.

"Three years ago, I knew the house was sick," Elder Song continued. "Too many smooth reports, too many honest county heads pushed aside while greedy talkers climbed two ranks a year. Yet I am ninety. My old comrades are gone, and pulling one thread blindly risked bringing the banks down on ordinary families."

Elder Song looked into Chen Xuan's steady eyes with gratitude.

"Then you stepped in," the old man said softly. "No speech, no banner, no headline. You handed Gao Zhengyi and Zhou Zhenhua clean ledgers nobody could dispute, cut out the rotten roots in one stroke, and left the healthy timber standing."

He leaned forward, pressing his palm onto the stone table.

"More than anything, young man, I asked you here to thank you from the bottom of my heart for that 300,000,000,000,000 yuan," Elder Song said, his raspy voice trembling. "When our banks faced 100,000,000,000,000 yuan of hidden rot, you sent 300,000,000,000,000 yuan of clean, legal money free to the country, with up to 500,000,000,000,000 yuan ready behind it across China Construction Bank, ICBC, Bank of China, and Huaxia Bank. Not one fen of interest. Not one share asked. You held the roof steady so millions of ordinary families never lost a night's sleep."

Chen Xuan picked up his scratched green thermos, unscrewed the lid, sipped his hot jasmine tea, and set it beside the teapot.

"Money sitting in vaults does nothing if the country shakes, Elder Song," Chen Xuan replied plainly. "If the banks panic and the streets go cold, numbers in an account are just ink on paper. I only put it where it could keep the floor steady."

Elder Song exhaled in wonder.

"The Finance Ministry finished its third count yesterday," Elder Song told him. "Across forty cleaned out departments, recovered stolen state wealth passed 82,000,000,000,000 yuan. Land, mines, port shares, and overseas accounts are all back in the treasury. Yesterday morning, Gu Chengshan signed the first three orders using that returned money. Every rural county hospital in the western provinces gets new equipment this winter. Every village school gets heated classrooms and hot lunches. Every retired factory worker and old soldier gets a pension raise before New Year."

Elder Song's voice softened. "On behalf of the boys we left in the northern hills seventy years ago, thank you."

Chen Xuan lowered his head. "I do not deserve thanks from them, Elder Song. They paid with their lives. I only sent a few folders and moved spare cash."

"Folders and cash nobody else on earth had the power or heart to send," Elder Song said firmly. He pulled an unfiltered cigarette from his jacket and smelled it with a boyish grin. "My doctor took my lighter six months ago. Said my lungs are older than the train station. Still, I like smelling tobacco with a good guest."

Chen Xuan chuckled softly. "Your doctor is looking out for you."

"He is a tyrant," Elder Song muttered fondly, tucking the unlit cigarette away before turning serious.

"Now comes the real reason I asked you here," Elder Song said quietly. "In ninety years, I learned one rule: never let someone who saved your house walk away empty handed. Tell me plainly, young man. What do you want? A quiet seat where no official dares touch you? A written seal opening any door in Beijing? A title for your family? Name it. While I breathe, my word carries weight."

Chen Xuan rested one hand on his green thermos and met the old man's eyes with an honest smile.

"All I want is for ordinary people in Yunxi County and Shanghai to live and run their shops in peace," Chen Xuan said simply. "And for myself, I just want to leave work at 4:55 on Fridays."

For three seconds, Elder Song stared at the young man in a cheap windbreaker who had given 300,000,000,000,000 yuan to the nation for free, helped recover 82,000,000,000,000 yuan in stolen assets, shaken forty ministries, and could have asked for half the sky.

Suddenly, Elder Song let out a trembling laugh until clear tears shone in his old eyes. He dabbed his eyes with a white handkerchief, still chuckling.

"Forty years in Beijing," Elder Song whispered. "I have met ministers, generals, tycoons, and foreign presidents. Every one wanted a bigger chair or a louder name. And the young man who gave the country 300,000,000,000,000 yuan without blinking only wants ordinary people in peace and to go home at 4:55 on Fridays."

"Five o'clock traffic in Shanghai is terrible," Chen Xuan explained mildly. "Leaving five minutes early saves twenty minutes on the bus."

Elder Song laughed again with relief.

"You have my word," Elder Song said softly. "Yunxi County and Shanghai will stay clean. No greedy hand will touch their streets, and no bully will bother a shopkeeper while my name stands on the books. And nobody will bother your Friday afternoons."

"Then this cup of tea was well worth the trip," Chen Xuan said warmly.

Elder Song poured the final drops from the clay pot, and together they raised their cups in silence by the lake.
