# Chapter 144: One Cup of Tea

Both men took a slow sip of the warm Wuyi tea. Outside the pavilion, two gray water birds skimmed low across the Western Hills lake, leaving thin ripples on the quiet water.

Elder Song set his cup down gently and folded his thin hands on the round stone table.

"Before we talk," Elder Song said in his calm, raspy voice, "I want you to know one thing. I am not going to ask for your surname, your given name, your hometown, or your family. If you want to stay a stranger to the capital, I will carry that silence into the ground with me."

Chen Xuan inclined his head slightly, his eyes warm with respect. "Thank you, Elder Song."

"Do not thank me," Elder Song replied with a faint smile. "After what you did for this country, keeping my mouth shut is the least I can manage."

The old man looked briefly toward the autumn hills beyond the lake.

"I was eighteen when I first carried a rifle into the northern mountains," Elder Song said quietly. "My cloth shoes had holes in the soles, and we stuffed dry grass inside so our toes would not freeze. Half the boys in my squad never saw twenty. We buried them in shallow earth and promised to build a house where their younger brothers and sisters would never go to bed hungry."

He paused, tapping his thin fingers once against the stone.

"For fifty years, we poured cement, laid rails, and watched the lights come on in every village. I thought the hard part was over." Elder Song let out a slow sigh. "I was wrong. War is loud, so you know where the enemy stands. Rot is quiet. It wears a clean collar, speaks polite words at banquets, and smiles while it hollows out the beams above your head."

Chen Xuan listened calmly, his hands resting beside his teacup.

"Three years ago, I knew the house was sick," Elder Song continued, his voice low. "Too many smooth reports, too many honest county heads pushed aside while greedy talkers climbed two ranks a year. Yet I am ninety years old. My old comrades are gone, and pulling one thread blindly risked bringing the roof down on ordinary people."

Elder Song looked straight back into Chen Xuan's steady eyes.

"Then you stepped in," the old man said softly. "Without a speech, without a banner, without asking for a headline. You handed Gao Zhengyi and Zhou Zhenhua clean ledgers nobody could argue with, cut the rotten roots in one stroke, and left the healthy timber standing."

Elder Song pressed his palm gently against the stone table.

"The Finance Ministry finished its third count yesterday evening," Elder Song told him plainly. "Across forty cleaned-out departments, recovered state wealth has already passed 82 trillion yuan. Land, mines, port shares, overseas accounts, and hidden company funds, all brought back into the public treasury where they belong."

Chen Xuan stayed completely calm. "That money belongs to the people who built the roads and worked the shifts. I only helped move it back to the right shelf."

"You say that so lightly," Elder Song murmured with a quiet smile. "Yesterday morning, Gu Chengshan signed the first three orders using that returned money. Every rural county hospital in the western provinces gets new equipment this winter. Every village school gets heated classrooms and hot lunches. And every retired factory worker and old soldier gets a raise in their pension before the New Year."

Elder Song's voice softened further. "You pulled the rot out before it killed the country. On behalf of the boys we left in the northern hills seventy years ago, thank you."

Chen Xuan straightened slightly and lowered his head in sincere respect. "I do not deserve thanks from them, Elder Song. They paid with their lives. I only sent a few folders."

"Folders nobody else on earth had the power or the heart to send," Elder Song said firmly. He pulled a single unfiltered cigarette from his cotton jacket pocket and held it beneath his nose with a boyish grin. "My doctor took my lighter away six months ago. Said my lungs are older than the train station. Still, I like to smell the tobacco when I have a good guest."

Chen Xuan chuckled softly. "Your doctor is looking out for you."

"He is a tyrant," Elder Song muttered fondly, tucking the unlit cigarette back into his pocket. Then his bright eyes grew serious again, and he leaned forward slightly.

"Now comes the real reason I asked you here," Elder Song said quietly. "In ninety years, I have learned one strict rule: never let a person who saved your house walk away empty-handed. Tell me plainly, young man. What do you want in return?"

The pavilion went very still.

"Do you want a quiet seat where no official dares touch your business?" Elder Song asked gently. "A written seal from me that opens any door in the capital? Protection for your friends, or a title for your family? Name it. While I still have breath, my word still carries weight."

Chen Xuan did not rush. He reached across the table, picked up his scratched green thermos, and unscrewed the dented metal lid with a soft squeak.

A wisp of jasmine steam rose into the cool air. Chen Xuan took a slow sip from his thermos, screwed the lid back on cleanly, and set it beside the clay teapot.

Then he looked across at the ninety-year-old elder with a calm, honest smile.

"Keep Yunxi County clean," Chen Xuan said simply. "Let ordinary people run their shops in peace. I just want to leave work at 4:55 on Fridays."

For three full seconds, Elder Song did not move a muscle.

He stared at the young man in a cheap dark windbreaker holding a fifteen-yuan green thermos. A young man who had just returned more than 82 trillion yuan to the nation, who had shaken forty ministries in a week, and who could have asked for half the sky.

Suddenly, Elder Song let out a soft, trembling laugh. It rose into a warm, helpless chuckle that shook his thin shoulders until clear tears shone in the corners of his old eyes.

He pulled a clean white handkerchief from his sleeve and dabbed gently at his eyes, still laughing softly.

"Forty years in the capital," Elder Song whispered, shaking his head in wonder. "I have met ministers, generals, tycoons, and foreign presidents. Every single one wanted a bigger chair, a louder name, or a taller wall. And the one young man who held the roof up only wants to go home at 4:55 on Fridays."

"Five o'clock traffic in Shanghai is terrible," Chen Xuan explained mildly. "Leaving five minutes early saves twenty minutes on the bus."

Elder Song laughed all over again, his face glowing with deep relief.

"You have my word," Elder Song said softly, his bright eyes steady once more. "Yunxi County will stay clean. No greedy hand will touch its streets, and no local bully will bother an ordinary shopkeeper while my name stands on the books. And nobody will ever send a file to bother your Friday afternoons."

"Then this cup of tea was well worth the flight," Chen Xuan said warmly.

Elder Song lifted the clay teapot and poured the final drops into both cups. Together, the ninety-year-old founder and the quiet office clerk raised their porcelain cups in silence and drank to a clean, peaceful morning by the lake.
