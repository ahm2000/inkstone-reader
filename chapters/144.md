# Chapter 144: One Cup of Tea

Two gray water birds skimmed low across the glasslike surface of the Western Hills lake, leaving twin silver ripples that faded slowly into the reeds. Morning sunlight slanted beneath the cedar eaves of the pavilion, warming the stone table between Chen Xuan and Elder Song.

Elder Song set his white porcelain teacup down with quiet care and rested both thin, veined hands flat on the polished stone.

"Before we speak another word, young friend," Elder Song said in his steady, raspy voice, "I want to make you a solemn promise on my ninety years of life. I will never ask for your surname, your given name, your workplace, or your family. As long as I draw breath, not a single person in Beijing, not Premier Gu Chengshan and not even my oldest comrade Elder Qin Zhengguo, will ever learn who sat across from me today or what your face looks like. I will carry this morning to my grave."

Chen Xuan looked into the old man's clear, unwavering eyes and felt a quiet wave of respect. "Thank you, Elder Song. I appreciate your word."

"A promise is the least I owe you," Elder Song said softly. He picked up the purple clay teapot and topped off both of their cups with fresh amber tea before setting the pot back beside the glowing charcoal.

"I asked you to climb this hill for one simple reason," Elder Song continued, his tone sincere and completely free of stiff official airs. "I wanted to thank you properly, person to person, for what you did during the banking crisis."

Chen Xuan sat quietly and listened as the copper kettle hissed softly beside them.

"The cabinet accountants finished the full tally last week," Elder Song said calmly. "When the dirty loans in Handong froze, 1.2 trillion yuan vanished from active circulation overnight. That shock triggered regional credit panic and exposed another 3.8 trillion yuan in shaky bonds and stalled payroll lines. All together, we were staring straight into a 5 trillion yuan hole while foreign short-sellers lined up at the border waiting to tear our currency apart."

Elder Song paused, his gaze steady and grateful.

"Your 8 trillion yuan liquidity wall arrived at the exact hour it mattered most. 1.2 trillion yuan plugged the bank holes before a single branch closed its doors. 3.8 trillion yuan backstopped the regional credit market so factories could pay their workers on Friday. And the final 3 trillion yuan slammed straight into the foreign speculators and sent them running before lunch. Because of that, not a single regular family lost their life savings."

The pavilion grew very quiet, save for the faint crackle of charcoal in the tiny clay stove and the soft lap of water against the lakeside stones. Down along the far shore, a breeze shook loose a bright shower of yellow ginkgo leaves that drifted across the water like tiny paper boats.

"I have lived ninety years," Elder Song murmured. "I have seen hunger, and I have seen families lose everything they built over a lifetime. You spared millions of households from that pain, and you stepped back into the quiet before anyone could even shake your hand. As an old man who loves this country, I just wanted to pour you one cup of tea and say thank you."

Chen Xuan looked down at the warm teacup in his hands, watching a pale wisp of steam curl upward into the crisp morning air, then raised his eyes with a gentle, unhurried smile.

"Elder Song, you give me too much credit," Chen Xuan said simply. "My own parents, my relatives, and my neighbors live in this country. They buy vegetables at the same corner markets, ride the same buses, and keep their hard-earned savings in those same four banks. If the markets crash and honest folks lose their livelihoods, nobody gets to live a peaceful life."

"And yet," Elder Song replied quietly, "having the power to act and choosing to stay calm and decent are two very different things. Most people who hold that kind of weight begin to look down on the streets below. You still walk on them."

"The street food tastes much better down there," Chen Xuan joked lightly. "You cannot get a hot scallion pancake from a five-star conference hall."

Elder Song smiled warmly, lifting his small porcelain cup across the stone table. "Well spoken, young friend. Simple truth beats a thousand pages of theory. Come, let us drink to peaceful mornings and hot pancakes."

Chen Xuan lifted his own cup and clinked it lightly against Elder Song's. They both drank, the roasted warmth of the Wuyi rock tea spreading gently against the crisp late November air.

After setting his cup down, Elder Song chuckled and eyed the worn canvas watch strap on Chen Xuan's wrist. "Now tell me honestly. How did you slip away from home on a Saturday morning without raising eyebrows?"

Chen Xuan coughed lightly, looking slightly sheepish. "I told my wife that my logistics company had a mandatory Saturday morning inventory check at our suburban warehouse. I promised I would be back before 3:00 PM to braise pork ribs and simmer winter melon soup for dinner."

Elder Song burst out laughing so hard he had to steady himself against the stone table. "A Saturday warehouse check! And here I was worried I might be pulling you away from some grand international boardroom!"

"Boardrooms are loud and the chairs are uncomfortable," Chen Xuan said with a grin. "My kitchen smells much better, and my wife gets grumpy if she skips dinner after a long week."

"You have figured out the real secret to a happy life," Elder Song said fondly, wiping a tear of laughter from the corner of his eye. "Good food, a warm home, and a quiet conscience. Do not ever let anyone talk you out of it."

"Not a chance, Elder Song," Chen Xuan promised, smiling out over the sunlit autumn lake.
