# Chapter 29: Beyond the Black Pine

Chen Xuan tapped the corner of the matte-black tablet, closing the red-bordered defense dossier. The blue glow dimmed to standby gray, leaving the rooftop quiet under the moon.

For ten long seconds, neither man spoke. Only faint static whispered inside Chen Xuan's left earpiece.

When Number 1 finally spoke, his voice carried a rare, solemn weight, stripped of routine briefing cadence.

"Black Pine Guard has two hundred and fourteen operatives in this province and twelve thousand across the country," Number 1 said slowly, each word measured like iron laid onto a scale. "We can take down Deng Yongchang, Cheng Biao, and every county official tonight. We can walk Chen Mingde out of Old Street Station with a signed exoneration order before sunrise, seal the Pier Four warehouse, and freeze Hongrui Real Estate's accounts within twenty minutes."

Number 1 paused for a deliberate breath.

"We cannot lock down forty national ministries, three theater commands, and the capital's own security organs without triggering a catastrophe. Their roots sit inside the very agencies meant to police them."

Chen Xuan did not look away from the dark horizon. "Give me the exact exposure," he said calmly.

"Based on the authorization hashes inside Folder Four, the syndicate behind Shengyuan Capital does not merely bribe clerks or provincial governors," Number 1 replied clinically. "They hold active deputy directors inside central financial clearing, senior procurement officers in two energy boards, liaison staff inside three coastal theater commands, and at least four division heads within the capital's internal security apparatus."

"And if Black Pine Guard moves simultaneously across all fourteen provinces before dawn?" Chen Xuan asked evenly.

Number 1 did not soften a detail. "Our models ran three times before I called. If we launch a nationwide sweep using Black Pine channels, their early-warning alarms trip inside twelve minutes."

"Walk me through minute fifteen," Chen Xuan said quietly.

"Within fifteen minutes, compromised offices in the capital issue counter-warrants branding Black Pine Guard cells as armed insurgents," Number 1 answered, his voice tight with the frustration of a commander facing a wall he cannot breach cleanly. "Provincial security bureaus seal highways around Yunxi County and seven other transit hubs. Inside the ministries, shredders and thermite server wipes destroy twenty years of primary paper trails. Worse, foreign intelligence handlers on the offshore relays receive every remaining batch of naval radar and power-grid schematics before we can sever their cables."

"And the financial clearing network?" Chen Xuan prompted softly.

"It freezes across fourteen provinces by Monday morning," Number 1 said, his breath audible on the line. "Ordinary citizens wake up unable to withdraw wages or savings. Runs hit regional banks by noon. Even if Black Pine Guard fought through every ambush, the panic alone would tear the country's markets open."

Silence settled over the channel, heavy with the unspoken admission of a commander who had never failed in seven years, yet now faced a target too vast for twelve thousand operatives to contain cleanly.

Black Pine Guard had been forged by Chen Xuan to watch the shadows, audit rot, and shield ordinary people from local wolves without disturbing daily life. They were a scalpel, precise and silent. What lay beneath Shengyuan Capital was no localized tumor. It was an invasive cancer wrapped tightly around the spine and arteries of the state itself.

Cutting it with a scalpel would make the patient bleed to death on the table.

"Do you want us to isolate Yunxi County first?" Number 1 asked quietly, testing the only cautious path left. "We can pull Chen Mingde out before eight o'clock, bury the demolition order, and stay invisible while we spend the next six months dismantling Shengyuan Capital from the outside."

Chen Xuan did not answer right away.

He lowered the tablet and walked along the southern parapet until he stood above the stairwell entrance of Building 2. Looking straight down six stories, he saw the dim courtyard streetlamp touching the outer concrete sill of his mother's sixth-floor kitchen window.

Even from the roof, he could make out the rim of the worn blue plastic basin sitting on that ledge, where Liu Meifang grew green scallions in two inches of water so she would not have to buy a fresh bunch every time she cooked noodles.

Behind that dark pane, his parents were sleeping. Chen Jianguo had stopped pacing only after Chen Xuan promised that nobody in their family would kneel outside Old Street Station tomorrow morning. In the spare room next door, Wu Lanying slept with one arm around Chen Yuan, trusting her husband would come home Monday afternoon to fix a squeaky bicycle chain.

And half a kilometer west on Old Street, behind a bent corrugated shutter, sat a cramped twenty-square-meter hardware shop where Chen Mingde had spent thirty years sorting washers, threading pipes, and paying every fen of tax before the deadline.

They had dragged an honest man into a cell, smashed his livelihood in broad daylight, and left his wife and children trembling over Sunday dinner, just to clear a few square meters of floor tiles above a ventilation shaft.

"Six months," Chen Xuan repeated softly into the midnight air.

On the line, Number 1 went still, catching the sudden chill inside those two words.

"In six months, how many more batches of naval radar calibration tables leave the coast?" Chen Xuan asked, almost conversational, yet colder than the wind off the river. "How many more honest men get locked away because their front door sits over a tunnel?"

Number 1 could not answer. His throat felt dry.

Chen Xuan kept his gaze on the faint yellow light beneath his mother's kitchen window.

"They smashed my Second Uncle's hardware shop to hide a tunnel that sells the country's bones."

On the encrypted channel, Number 1 made no sound. Nothing remained to argue or offer.

For seven years, Number 1 had watched Chen Xuan live like an unremarkable office clerk in Qingyan, carrying a scratched green thermos, eating cheap noodles, tolerating petty supervisors without lifting a finger. Number 1 knew better than anyone that Chen Xuan had boundless patience for trivial arrogance and ordinary foolishness.

He had zero patience for traitors who hollowed out the country's foundation and then reached down to crush the ordinary families living on top of it.

When Chen Xuan linked those two ends in one quiet sentence, from a broken hardware shop on Old Street to stolen naval radar codes and 100-trillion-yuan bloodletting of the state, every ordinary mercy vanished from the night.

"We do not wait six months," Chen Xuan said calmly.

He raised his left hand and glanced at the pale digital clock in the corner of the dimmed tablet screen.

It read 23:58.

In two minutes, Sunday would end, and Monday would begin. Yunxi County slept below, unaware that the weight about to descend over the entire country had already left the scales.
