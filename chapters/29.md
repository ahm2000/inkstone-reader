# Chapter 29: Hotpot Saturday

At Red Pot Alley Hotpot in Qingyan City, Chen Xuan was taking the Saturday afternoon bus home instead of his usual Friday one, and the only reason was that Ma Chao had booked this table two weeks ago.

His plan was simple: eat hotpot until half past two, catch the 3:40 bus to Yunxi, and sit at his mother's table by dinner.

There was only one problem, and it was that forty people were already waiting outside the shop!

"A-thirty-one, now serving!" a girl shouted through a loudspeaker, while customers on little plastic stools held paper numbers like lottery tickets and stared at the door as if it were a bank.

"Relax, I am a Gold Member here, and I have eaten in this shop more than two hundred times!" Ma Chao said, patting his round stomach. He was twenty-nine and wore a bright orange jacket, and he wanted the whole street to know it.

Then he pulled out a new folding phone and flipped it open with a snap. "Ninth generation, and it folds in half, for nine thousand, nine hundred and ninety-nine yuan!"

Wang Lei, a broad, practical man who ran a freight depot, leaned in and frowned. "I pay my best driver seven thousand a month, so that phone is a month and a half of his sweat."

"But it folds!" Ma Chao said proudly.

"So does my wallet after hotpot with you," said Xu Jia, Wang Lei's wife, holding her scarf over her mouth so nobody would see her laughing. "At least spend some of it on a girlfriend, because a folding phone will not hold your hand at New Year's."

Everyone at the table knew what Ma Chao wanted, which was for one person to say something nice about the phone.

Chen Xuan looked at it for a moment. "Oh, nice," he said, and he meant it, because it looked very shiny.

Before Ma Chao could start on the camera, a tall young waiter with a badge that read Tan stepped out of the door and smiled.

"Mr. Ma, your table is ready. Please come this way, to the corner table by the window."

Forty people on plastic stools turned their heads at the same time.

"Gold Member?"

"Since when does this place have members?"

"See?!" Ma Chao cried, puffing out his chest. "A Gold Member never waits!"

"Your booking says eleven-thirty, so we are on time," Wang Lei said slowly, "but these forty people were here before us."

"They love me!"

Behind them, nobody noticed that the waiter's hands, holding the menus, were slightly damp.

To put it simply, the badge said Tan, but the man was Number Twenty-Nine of the Detail, the hidden protectors who watch over Chen Xuan, where a low number means a high rank. In his other life he ran night operations for a unit that nobody in this shop could have pronounced, and today his hardest mission was not to spill the broth!

He had been awake since five, worrying about this one table.

A big party had canceled it that morning, so it was free, and that was true. Number Twenty-Nine had cleaned it very fast and done nothing more, and that was also true.

Was it queue-jumping? No. Was it rigging? No, no, no! The stay-ordinary order was clear: the Detail changes nothing around the Boss unless he signals, and the Boss had not signaled, so a clean table was only a clean table. Right?

He wiped his forehead with the back of his wrist and hoped that he was right.

At the table, the pot arrived, bubbling red on one side and golden on the other.

"The usual, sir?" asked Manager Peng, a stout man in a pressed shirt, who had hurried over to take the order himself.

"Mm, just the usual," Chen Xuan said. "Half tomato broth, half mild, with thin lamb, tofu skin, bean sprouts, and barley tea, please."

Manager Peng bowed.

Hmm?

It was only an inch too deep, but Ma Chao had been bowed to by Manager Peng two hundred times, and he knew exactly how deep a normal bow went.

"Hey, why did he bow to you like that when I am the Gold Member?" Ma Chao said, frowning.

"You are his best customer by weight," Wang Lei said.

The truth was that Manager Peng could not have said why he bowed so deep either. Last month his owner's secretary had phoned with one odd order, "If a young man ever says 'just the usual,' ask no questions," and the owner had passed it down without telling anyone why.

The tomato broth was sweet and a little sour, so Chen Xuan drank a spoonful, closed his eyes, and thought that it was nearly as good as his mother's soup on a day when she was in a hurry.

That was all he thought about, and he ate the lamb slowly.

"So, Chen Xuan," Xu Jia said, dipping a bean sprout, "does your company work with Haiyue Hospitality?"

"Hmm? Yes, I think we have a contract."

"My friend Fang Ting works at Haiyue's head office, and she says everybody there is cleaning their desks. Someone from the chairman's family is coming to visit partner companies in Qingyan, and they are even practicing smiling in the break room!"

"Practicing smiling?!" Ma Chao laughed so hard that he had to put his phone down.

"Our General Manager has been nervous about Haiyue for a week," Chen Xuan said mildly, "so maybe that is why."

"Then maybe your company gets a big order," Wang Lei said. "I could use one, because I have forty trucks and half of them are only lucky when it rains."

Before long, the pot was empty and every plate was clean.

"I will pay, because I invited you!" Ma Chao said.

"I am the oldest, so I pay," said Wang Lei.

"I carry the family card!" said Xu Jia.

Chen Xuan had nothing to add, so he stood up, said, "I'll wash my hands," and walked to the counter.

"Hello, could I settle table nine?"

The cashier shook her head. "It is already paid, sir, three times. Mr. Ma paid through the app, Mr. Wang paid at the register, and Mrs. Xu paid with a red envelope of cash, a traditional gift of money. We will refund two of them."

Chen Xuan paused. He had lost.

Three friends, three payments, one loser!

"Well played," he said, and for once he meant it with his whole heart.

Back at the table, Wang Lei squinted at the receipt. "One thousand, two hundred and seventy-six yuan. When my father started at the textile factory, that was his pay for a whole year."

"And we ate it all afternoon," said Xu Jia.

"It was very good," said Chen Xuan.

They stepped out into the cold, where the line was now fifty-two people long and a girl was shouting "A-forty-four!" at the sky.

"Wait! Please wait, sirs!"

Manager Peng came running after them, out of breath, carrying a large tray of mango pudding, sweet soup and fruit, and his hands were shaking!

"I am so sorry for the wait, so please accept this with our deepest apologies!"

The four friends looked at each other.

"What wait?" said Ma Chao. "We sat down right away."

"Yes, yes, of course," said Manager Peng, bowing again, "it is just that, to us, any wait at all..."

"Oh, thank you," said Chen Xuan, and he took the tray, because refusing would have been very rude.

In his pocket, his phone buzzed.

It was his grandmother, which was strange, because she hated typing.
