# Chapter 29: Beyond the Black Pine

Chen Xuan tapped the top corner of the matte-black tablet, closing the red-bordered defense dossier. The pale blue light dimmed to a soft gray standby screen, leaving the rooftop of Building 2 once more to the cold silver wash of the moon.

For ten long seconds, nobody spoke. Across the neighboring apartment roofs of Fuxing New Village, a gust of night wind rattled a loose aluminum television aerial, producing a thin, metallic clatter before fading back into the quiet hum of the sleeping county.

On the encrypted audio channel, Number One waited without interrupting. Only the faintest static whispered inside Chen Xuan's left earpiece.

When Number One finally spoke, his voice carried a rare, solemn gravity, stripped of all routine briefing cadence.

"Boss, Black Pine Guard has two hundred and fourteen operatives in this province and twelve thousand across the country," Number One said slowly, each word measured like iron weights placed onto a scale. "We can take down Deng Yongchang, Cheng Biao, and every county official tonight. We can pull Chen Mingde out of Old Street Station with a signed exoneration order before sunrise, seal the Pier Four warehouse, and freeze Hongrui Real Estate's local accounts within twenty minutes."

Number One paused for a single, deliberate breath.

"We cannot lock down forty national ministries, three theater commands, and the capital's own security organs without triggering a catastrophe. Their roots sit inside the very agencies meant to police them."

Chen Xuan did not look away from the dark horizon of Yunxi County. "Give me the exact exposure," he said calmly.

"Based on the authorization hashes inside Folder Four, the syndicate behind Shengyuan Capital does not merely bribe clerks or provincial governors," Number One replied, his tone clinical and unvarnished. "They hold active deputy directors inside central financial clearing, senior procurement officers in two energy boards, liaison staff inside three coastal theater commands, and at least four division heads within the capital's internal security apparatus. If Black Pine Guard initiates a nationwide sweep using our own channels, their early-warning alarms will trip inside twelve minutes."

Number One laid out the fallout without softening a single detail.

"Within fifteen minutes, compromised offices in the capital will issue counter-warrants branding Black Pine Guard cells as armed insurgents. Provincial security bureaus will seal the highways around Yunxi County and seven other transit hubs. Inside the ministries, shredders and thermite server wipes will destroy twenty years of primary paper trails. Worse, the foreign intelligence handlers waiting on the offshore relays will receive every remaining batch of naval radar and power-grid schematics before we can cut their cables. Even if we fought through it, the financial clearing network would freeze across fourteen provinces by Monday morning, wiping out ordinary citizens' savings and sparking nationwide panic."

Number One stopped. The silence on the channel was heavy with the unspoken admission of a commander who had never once failed an assignment in seven years, yet now stood staring at a wall too vast for his twelve thousand men to breach cleanly.

Black Pine Guard had been forged by Chen Xuan to watch the shadows, audit rot, shield ordinary people from local wolves, and keep regional power brokers in line without ever disturbing the surface of daily life. They were a scalpel, precise and silent. What lay buried beneath Shengyuan Capital and the fourteen-province drain was no longer a localized tumor. It was an invasive cancer wrapped tightly around the spine and arteries of the state itself.

Cutting it with a scalpel would only make the patient bleed to death on the table.

Chen Xuan lowered the matte-black tablet to his side and walked slowly along the southern edge of the rooftop. His footsteps were quiet against the coarse concrete. Near the corner above the stairwell entrance, he stopped and leaned lightly against the low parapet, looking straight down six stories into the narrow courtyard of Fuxing New Village.

Directly below him, an aging concrete lamp post cast a dim circle of warm yellow light over the damp asphalt, illuminating a row of chained bicycles and a stack of faded red bricks beside the flowerbed. The upper edge of that same yellow glow brushed softly against the concrete sill outside his mother's sixth-floor kitchen window.

Even from the roof, Chen Xuan could make out the rim of the worn blue plastic basin sitting on the outer ledge, where Liu Meifang grew green scallions in two inches of water so she would not have to buy a fresh bunch every time she made noodles.

Behind that dark pane of glass, his parents were sleeping. Chen Jianguo had finally stopped pacing the hallway only after Chen Xuan promised him that nobody in their family would kneel at the police station gate tomorrow morning. In the spare bedroom next door, Wu Lanying was sleeping with one arm around Chen Yuan, trusting that her husband would come home on Monday afternoon to tighten a squeaky bicycle chain.

And down on Old Street, behind a bent corrugated shutter, sat a cramped twenty-square-meter hardware shop where Chen Mingde had spent thirty years sorting iron washers, threading brass pipes, and paying every single fen of municipal tax before the deadline.

They had dragged an honest man into a concrete cell, smashed his livelihood in broad daylight, and left his wife and children trembling over Sunday dinner. All of it, just to clear a few square meters of old concrete floor tiles above a ventilation shaft.

Chen Xuan watched the quiet yellow pool of streetlamp light below his mother's window. His voice, when he spoke into the midnight air, was soft, almost conversational, yet colder than the wind blowing off the dark river.

"They smashed my Second Uncle's hardware shop to hide a tunnel that sells the country's bones."

On the encrypted line, Number One did not make a sound. There was nothing left to argue, and nothing left to add.

For seven years, Number One had watched the Boss live like an unremarkable office worker in Qingyan, carrying a scratched green thermos, eating cheap stir-fried noodles, tolerating petty supervisors without ever lifting a finger against them. Number One knew better than anyone alive that the Boss had boundless patience for trivial arrogance and ordinary human foolishness.

He had zero patience for traitors who hollowed out the foundation of the country and then reached down to crush the ordinary families living on top of it.

When Chen Xuan connected those two ends of the chain in a single quiet sentence, from a broken hardware shop on Old Street to the stolen naval radar codes and hundred-trillion-yuan bloodletting of the state, every ordinary mercy vanished from the night.

Chen Xuan raised his left wrist slightly and glanced at the pale digital clock in the corner of the dimmed tablet screen.

It read 23:58.

In two minutes, Sunday would end, and Monday would begin.

Down in the courtyard of Fuxing New Village, a stray cat slipped silently across the yellow ring of streetlamp light and vanished into the bushes. Nothing else stirred. Yunxi County slept peacefully beneath the cold moon, completely unaware that the weight about to descend over the entire country had already left the scales.
