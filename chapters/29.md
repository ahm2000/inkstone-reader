# Chapter 29: Hotpot Saturday

Qingyan City, Red Pot Alley Hotpot. Saturday, 11:35 a.m.

Chen Xuan had moved his bus home from Friday night to Saturday afternoon for one reason: Ma Chao had booked this table two weeks ago, and a promise to Ma Chao was a promise. After Friday's dinner he had gone back to his Qingyan apartment for the work week, and he was taking the Saturday 3:40 bus only because of this booking.

His plan was simple. Eat hotpot until 2:30, catch the 3:40 bus to Yunxi, and be at his mother's table by dinner.

There was just one problem. The line outside the shop was forty people long.

"A-thirty-one, now serving!" a girl shouted through a loudspeaker. Customers sat on little plastic stools in the cold, holding paper numbers like lottery tickets and staring at the door as if it were a bank.

"Relax! I'm a Gold Member!" Ma Chao declared, patting his round stomach. He was twenty-nine, wore a bright orange jacket, and had eaten here more than two hundred times. "Look."

He pulled out a new phone, a folding one, and flipped it open with a snap. "Ninth-generation! It folds in half. Nine thousand, nine hundred and ninety-nine yuan!"

Wang Lei, a broad, practical man who ran a freight depot, leaned in and frowned. "I pay my best driver 7,000 a month. That phone is almost a month and a half of his sweat."

"It folds!" Ma Chao said proudly.

"So does my wallet after hotpot with you," said Xu Jia, Wang Lei's wife, and she held her scarf over her mouth so nobody would see her laughing.

Chen Xuan looked at the phone. "Oh, nice," he said. He meant it. It looked very shiny.

Before Ma Chao could explain the camera, a tall young waiter with a name badge that read Tan stepped out of the door and smiled.

"Mr. Ma, your table is ready. Corner table, by the window, this way please."

Forty people on plastic stools turned their heads at the same time.

"See?!" Ma Chao cried, puffing out his chest. "Gold Member!"

"Your booking says eleven-thirty," Wang Lei said slowly. "So we are on time. But these forty people were here first."

"They love me!"

Behind them, nobody noticed that the waiter's hands, holding the menus, were very slightly damp.

His badge said Tan. His real rank was Number Twenty-Nine. The Detail is the hidden team of protectors who work for Chen Xuan and act only when he gives the signal, and a low number means a high rank; the Inner Ring are its hundred highest-ranked members. In his other life, he ran night operations for a unit that nobody in this shop could have pronounced. Today his hardest mission was not to spill broth.

He had also been up since five worrying about this corner table.

It was true that a party of six had canceled the corner table that morning, so it was free. It was also true that Number Twenty-Nine had cleaned it in four minutes instead of ten, and done nothing more. He had checked twice, then three times, that this was fast cleaning and not queue-jumping. The stay-ordinary order was clear: the Detail changed nothing around the Boss unless he signaled. A clean table was not rigging. He wiped his forehead with the back of his wrist and hoped he was right.

At the table, the pot arrived, bubbling red on one side and golden on the other.

"The usual, sir?" asked Manager Peng, a stout man in a pressed shirt, who had hurried over to take the order himself.

"Mm, just the usual," Chen Xuan said. "Half tomato broth, half mild. Thin lamb, tofu skin, bean sprouts, and barley tea. Please."

Manager Peng bowed.

It was only an inch too deep. But Ma Chao had been bowed to by Manager Peng two hundred times, and he knew exactly how deep a normal bow went.

"Hey," Ma Chao said, frowning. "Why did he bow to you like that? I'm the Gold Member!"

"You're his best customer by weight," Wang Lei said.

Even Manager Peng could not have said why he had bowed so deep. All he knew was that last month, his owner's secretary had phoned with one odd order: "If a young man ever says 'just the usual,' ask no questions." The owner passed word down. Nobody told the secretary why.

The tomato broth was sweet and a little sour. Chen Xuan drank a spoonful, closed his eyes, and thought: this is nearly as good as Mom's, on a day when she is in a hurry. It was a happy thought. He ate the lamb slowly.

"So, Chen Xuan," Xu Jia said, dipping a bean sprout. "Does your company work with Haiyue Hospitality?"

"Hmm? Yes, I think we have a contract."

"My friend Fang Ting works at Haiyue's head office. She says everybody there is cleaning their desks, because someone from the chairman's family is coming to visit partner companies in Qingyan. They are even practicing smiling in the break room!"

"Practicing smiling?!" Ma Chao laughed so hard he had to put his phone down.

"Our General Manager has been nervous about Haiyue for a week," Chen Xuan said mildly. "Maybe that's why."

"Maybe your company will get a big order," Wang Lei said. "I could use a big order. Forty trucks, and half of them are only lucky when it rains."

At 1:50, the pot was empty and the table was a field of plates.

"I'll pay!" Ma Chao said. "I invited."

"I'm the oldest. I pay," said Wang Lei.

"I carry the family card!" said Xu Jia.

Chen Xuan, who had nothing to add, quietly stood up and said, "I'll wash my hands." Then he walked to the counter.

"Hello. Could I settle table nine?"

The cashier shook her head. "It's already paid, sir. Three times. By Mr. Ma through the app, by Mr. Wang at the register, and by Mrs. Xu with a red envelope of cash, a traditional gift of money. We will refund two of them."

Chen Xuan paused. He had lost.

"Oh, nice," he said, and for once he actually meant it with his whole heart. Three friends who each insisted on paying; he could not win that fight.

Back at the table, Wang Lei squinted at the receipt. "One thousand, two hundred and seventy-six yuan. When my father started at the textile factory, that was his pay for a whole year."

"And now we ate it in an hour and a half," said Xu Jia.

"It was very good," said Chen Xuan.

They stepped out into the cold. The line was now fifty-two people. A girl was shouting "A-forty-four!" at the sky.

"Wait! Please wait, sirs!"

Manager Peng came running after them, a little out of breath, carrying a large tray with mango pudding, sweet soup, and fruit. His hands were shaking.

"I am so sorry for the wait! Please accept this, with our deepest apologies!"

The four friends looked at each other.

"What wait?" said Ma Chao. "We sat down right away."

"Yes, yes, of course," said Manager Peng, bowing again. "It is just that, to us, any wait at all..."

"Oh, nice. Thank you," said Chen Xuan, and he took the tray, because refusing would have been very rude.

In his pocket, his phone buzzed. It was his grandmother, which was strange, because she hated typing.

*Tomorrow 7:30. Vegetable plots. Bring the girl. There is a fence quarrel. Wear boots.*
