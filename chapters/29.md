# Chapter 29: Beyond the White Guard

Chen Xuan tapped the corner of the matte-white tablet, closing the red-bordered defense folder. The blue glow dimmed to standby gray, leaving the rooftop quiet under the moon.

For ten long seconds, neither man spoke. Only faint static whispered inside Chen Xuan's left earpiece.

When Number 1 finally spoke, his voice carried a rare, solemn weight, stripped of his usual briefing tone.

"White Guard has two hundred and fourteen operatives in this province and twelve thousand across the country," Number 1 said slowly, each word measured like iron laid on a scale. "We can take down Deng Yongchang, Cheng Biao, and every county official tonight. We can walk Chen Mingde out of Old Street Station with a signed cleared-name order before sunrise, seal the Pier Four warehouse, and freeze Hongrui Real Estate's accounts within twenty minutes."

Number 1 paused for a deliberate breath.

"We cannot lock down forty national ministries, three military commands, and the capital's own security offices without causing a disaster. Their roots sit inside the very offices meant to police them."

Chen Xuan did not look away from the dark skyline. "Give me the exact reach," he said calmly.

"Based on the signature records inside Folder Four, the group behind Shengyuan Capital does not just bribe clerks or provincial governors," Number 1 replied plainly. "They hold active deputy directors inside central bank clearing, senior buying officers in two energy boards, liaison staff inside three coastal military commands, and at least four division heads inside the capital's internal security office."

"And if White Guard strikes all fourteen provinces at once before dawn?" Chen Xuan asked evenly.

Number 1 did not soften a single detail. "Our models ran three times before I called. If we launch a nationwide sweep using White Guard channels, their warning alarms trip inside twelve minutes."

"Walk me through minute fifteen," Chen Xuan said quietly.

"Within fifteen minutes, crooked offices in the capital issue arrest orders branding White Guard teams as armed rebels," Number 1 answered, his voice tight with the frustration of a commander facing a wall he cannot break cleanly. "Provincial police seal highways around Yunxi County and seven other hubs. Inside the ministries, shredders and burning gear destroy twenty years of primary paper trails. Worse, foreign intelligence handlers on the overseas lines receive every remaining batch of warship radar and power-grid plans before we can cut their cables."

"And the banking network?" Chen Xuan prompted softly.

"It freezes across fourteen provinces by Monday morning," Number 1 said, his breath audible on the line. "Ordinary citizens wake up unable to withdraw wages or savings. Bank runs hit regional branches by noon. Even if White Guard fought through every ambush, the panic alone would tear the country's markets open."

Silence settled over the line, heavy with the unspoken truth of a commander who had never failed in seven years, yet now faced a target too huge for twelve thousand operatives to contain cleanly.

White Guard had been built by Chen Xuan to watch the shadows, audit rot, and shield ordinary people from local wolves without disturbing daily life. They were a sharp scalpel, neat and silent. What lay beneath Shengyuan Capital was no small local sore. It was a deep cancer wrapped tightly around the spine and arteries of the state itself.

Cutting it with a scalpel would make the patient bleed to death on the table.

"Do you want us to handle Yunxi County first?" Number 1 asked quietly, testing the only cautious path left. "We can pull Chen Mingde out before eight o'clock, bury the demolition order, and stay invisible while we spend the next six months taking Shengyuan Capital apart from the outside."

Chen Xuan did not answer right away.

He lowered the tablet and walked along the low concrete wall until he stood above the stairwell entrance of Building 2. Looking straight down six stories, he saw the dim courtyard streetlamp touching the outer concrete sill of his mother's sixth-floor kitchen window.

Even from the roof, he could make out the rim of the worn blue plastic basin sitting on that ledge, where Liu Meifang grew green scallions in two inches of water so she would not have to buy a fresh bunch every time she cooked noodles.

Behind that dark pane, his parents were sleeping. Chen Jianguo had stopped pacing only after Chen Xuan promised that nobody in their family would kneel outside Old Street Station tomorrow morning. In the spare room next door, Wu Lanying slept with one arm around Chen Yuan, trusting her husband would come home Monday afternoon to fix a squeaky bicycle chain.

And half a kilometer west on Old Street, behind a bent metal shutter, sat a cramped twenty-square-meter hardware shop where Chen Mingde had spent thirty years sorting washers, threading pipes, and paying every cent of tax before the deadline.

They had dragged an honest man into a cell, smashed his shop in broad daylight, and left his wife and children trembling over Sunday dinner, just to clear a few square meters of floor tiles above an air shaft.

"Six months," Chen Xuan repeated softly into the midnight air.

On the line, Number 1 went still, catching the sudden chill inside those two words.

"In six months, how many more batches of warship radar charts leave the coast?" Chen Xuan asked, almost conversational, yet colder than the wind off the river. "How many more honest men get locked away because their front door sits over a tunnel?"

Number 1 could not answer. His throat felt dry.

Chen Xuan kept his gaze on the faint yellow light beneath his mother's kitchen window.

"They smashed my Second Uncle's hardware shop to hide a tunnel that sells the country's bones."

On the secure channel, Number 1 made no sound. Nothing remained to argue or offer.

For seven years, Number 1 had watched Chen Xuan live like an ordinary office worker in Shanghai, carrying a scratched green thermos, eating cheap noodles, and ignoring petty supervisors without lifting a finger. Number 1 knew better than anyone that Chen Xuan had endless patience for small arrogance and ordinary foolishness.

He had zero patience for traitors who hollowed out the country's foundation and then reached down to crush the ordinary families living on top of it.

When Chen Xuan linked those two ends in one quiet sentence, from a broken hardware shop on Old Street to stolen warship radar codes and 100 trillion yuan bled out of the state, every ordinary mercy vanished from the night.

"We do not wait six months," Chen Xuan said calmly.

He raised his left hand and glanced at the pale digital clock in the corner of the dimmed tablet screen.

It read 23:58.

In two minutes, Sunday would end, and Monday would begin. Yunxi County slept below, unaware that the weight about to drop over the entire country had already left the scales.
