# Chapter 111: Taking the Wife Home to Handong

The rest of the workweek at Hengxin Logistics passed in the same unhurried rhythm of freight manifests, afternoon tea, and quiet chatter around the cubicles. On Friday evening, after dinner at Emerald Riverside Villa, Chen Xuan finally called home to Yunxi County to tell his parents that he had gotten married and sent a few photos of Qin Yuhan over WeChat, setting off twenty minutes of excited questions from his mother.

By Saturday morning, the white high-speed train bound for Jingzhou City and Yunxi County was gliding smoothly away from the platform beneath the high glass roof of Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station.

In the quiet second row of the first-class carriage, Chen Xuan sat by the window in a comfortable gray cotton jacket, his dented green thermos resting on the small folding tray table in front of him. Beside him sat Qin Yuhan, dressed not in a sharp designer suit from Lujiazui, but in a soft cream knit sweater, simple blue jeans, and clean white sneakers.

On the overhead luggage rack directly above their seats sat four neatly wrapped wooden gift boxes tied with red silk ribbons.

In the adjacent second-class carriage, two college students wearing backpacks shared a bag of sunflower seeds while a middle-aged uncle dozed lightly by the window, blending effortlessly into the weekend crowd while keeping an easy, unhurried eye on the carriage.

Qin Yuhan smoothed the sleeves of her sweater for the third time in five minutes. She looked out at the receding skyline of Shanghai, then turned back to her husband with a rare flicker of nerves in her bright eyes.

"Chen Xuan, tell me honestly," she said softly. "Is this sweater too plain? When I meet your parents for the first time, should I have worn a nicer coat? What if Auntie thinks I did not dress formally enough?"

Chen Xuan chuckled and unscrewed the cap of his green thermos. Steam rose into the cool cabin air, carrying the light scent of autumn chrysanthemum tea. He poured a small cup into the lid and handed it to her.

"You sign 300 million yuan contracts at Haiyue Tower without blinking," Chen Xuan teased gently. "Why are you nervous about meeting my mom and dad?"

"That is business," Qin Yuhan protested, taking a small sip of the warm tea. "This is your family. Your mother raised you, and your grandmother is turning 81 tomorrow. I want them to like me."

"My mom already likes you," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile. "When I called her last night to tell her we got married and were coming home for Grandma's birthday, she spent twenty minutes asking what kind of dumplings you prefer and whether you eat spicy food. Once she saw your photos, she was mostly relieved that I finally stopped living on instant noodles."

Qin Yuhan could not help laughing. The tension in her shoulders eased a little as she rested her elbow on the shared armrest. "Did you tell them what I do for a living?"

"I told them you work in management at a big company in Shanghai and that you are very capable," Chen Xuan replied. "If I tell my dad you run the whole Haiyue Group, he will spend all afternoon worrying that our sofa is too old for you to sit on. Just be yourself. They are simple, warm people."

"Good," Qin Yuhan murmured, her smile turning warm. "I brought West Lake tea for Uncle Chen, soft silk scarves for Auntie and Second Aunt, and a burgundy silk jacket with soft ginseng honey cake for Grandma. I also packed fruit candies and fountain pens for your two cousins."

"You thought of everyone," Chen Xuan said, reaching over to pat her hand. "By the time we leave tomorrow evening, my mom is going to try to stuff half of Yunxi County into our luggage."

As the high-speed train picked up speed, the glass towers of Shanghai gave way to wide autumn plains. Golden rice paddies stretched toward distant low hills, and rows of yellow ginkgo trees flashed past small country stations in the clear morning light.

Three hundred kilometers ahead in Jingzhou City, the autumn breeze rustled the courtyard trees outside the Handong Provincial Party Committee compound. Inside a quiet meeting room, Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin and Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang sat across from each other with plain porcelain teacups.

On the table between them lay the autumn economic and urban renewal report for the province. Ever since Gao Yuliang, Zhao Ruilong, and their corrupt faction had been arrested and swept out of Handong, the heavy fog over the province had finally lifted. Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping was wrapping up the final asset recoveries from Shanshui Group's illicit land grabs, and honest officials could finally focus on practical work.

Li Dakang tapped the Yunxi County section of the report with a satisfied nod. "Secretary Sha, the four national banks, China Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China, have all released the autumn batch of low-interest heritage and rural infrastructure loans. Old Street in Yunxi County is lively again, and the new asphalt road out to Hongshi Village was paved well ahead of winter."

Sha Ruijin looked out the window at the bright autumn sky and smiled faintly. "Once the distorted approval chains were cleared out and the bank credit reached the counties directly, local towns recovered faster than anyone expected."

"What still impresses me is how cleanly the emergency credit line was structured," Li Dakang said thoughtfully, setting his teacup down. "The private reserve that stabilized the national banks never once sent a representative to lobby for contracts, push a favored bidder, or interfere in a single municipal appointment."

"That is how healthy governance should work," Sha Ruijin replied calmly. "Our job now is to keep the oversight tight, see every rural road and small-business loan through to completion, and never let corrupt hands stain Handong again."

Neither leader had any idea that the very man who had quietly set that cleanup in motion was currently sitting on a high-speed train heading straight into their province, peeling a sweet tangerine and arguing playfully with his wife over who got the bigger half.

When the cabin speaker chimed softly to announce the arrival at Yunxi County Station, Chen Xuan wiped his fingers with a tissue and stood up to lift the four wooden gift boxes down from the overhead rack.

"Ready to meet the Chen family?" he asked, his eyes crinkling with amusement.

Qin Yuhan stood up, smoothed her cream sweater one last time, and slipped her slender hand firmly into his. "Ready."

Holding his wife's hand in one palm and his green thermos in the other, Chen Xuan stepped out of the carriage into the crisp autumn air of his hometown.
