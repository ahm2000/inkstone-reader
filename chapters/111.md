# Chapter 111: Marcus Vance Lands in Shanghai

The flight from Zurich touched down in Shanghai just past two in the afternoon.

Li Weiming walked through the arrival hall in a dark gray suit, pulling a small leather suitcase behind him. On his passport, the name printed under his photo was Marcus Vance. Few people in Shanghai knew his real name, and fewer still knew who paid for his plane tickets.

He did not stop for coffee or look around the terminal. A black town car waited at the curb. Forty minutes later, he stepped out in front of the Harbor View Hotel and rode the private elevator straight to the top floor.

A quiet smoking room sat at the back of the executive lounge. Heavy curtains blocked the bright afternoon sun, leaving the room cool and dim. At a corner table near the window, a middle-aged man in a rumpled navy suit sat staring at an ashtray already holding three cigarette butts.

Feng Guoqiang looked up when the door opened. His face was pale, and dark rings sat under his eyes.

"You are late," Feng Guoqiang said, his voice tight.

"Customs took ten extra minutes," Li Weiming replied. He set his suitcase beside a chair, sat down, and poured himself a cup of hot black tea from the pot on the table. "You look like you have not slept since Tuesday."

"Nobody in this city has slept since Tuesday," Feng Guoqiang muttered. He reached into his coat pocket, pulled out a fresh cigarette, and lit it with a cheap plastic lighter. His fingers shook slightly around the flame.

Li Weiming watched the trembling hand for a second before taking a slow sip of tea. "Tell me what happened. Zurich got three different stories in twelve hours, and none of them made sense."

Feng Guoqiang exhaled a long stream of gray smoke toward the ceiling. "Because none of it makes sense to us either. You remember Xu Wenbin, the Customs Director at Shanghai Port?"

"I met him twice last year," Li Weiming said calmly. "Careful man. Never signed his own name to anything dirty, and always kept two backup lines open inside the city."

"He is gone," Feng Guoqiang said flatly. "Not moved to another desk. Not asked to step down quietly. Gone. At midnight on Tuesday, four black cars pulled up outside his house. The officers did not knock on the local police station first. They did not call the city hall. They walked straight through his front gate with signed arrest papers already stamped from the top."

Li Weiming set his cup down gently. "No warning at all?"

"Not a whisper," Feng Guoqiang said, tapping ash into the glass tray. "Not one phone call from the port office. Not one hint from the courts. Even his driver did not know until the cuffs were already on his wrists."

"And Sha City?" Li Weiming asked. "Shengyuan Capital?"

Feng Guoqiang gave a bitter, dry laugh. "Exact same hour. Down to the minute. By one in the morning, every glass door at Shengyuan Capital was sealed with white paper tape. Every bank account tied to the company was locked solid. Over 800 million vanished behind a court freeze before sunrise. All four senior partners were loaded onto a bus before the morning cleaners even showed up for work."

Li Weiming leaned back in his chair. For the first time since leaving Zurich, a thin crease appeared between his eyebrows.

"That does not happen by accident," he said quietly. "To freeze 800 million and pull a port customs director out of bed on the exact same night, someone had to plan it for months. Yet none of our people in Shanghai or Sha City heard a single rumor."

"It is worse than you think," Feng Guoqiang said. He leaned forward, lowering his voice until it barely carried across the table. "Xu Wenbin and Shengyuan Capital were not the whole catch. By Wednesday night, 318 officials across the province and nearby cities had been pulled in for questioning. Three hundred and eighteen, Marcus. City chiefs, tax heads, bank managers, port clerks. Half the people I used to drink tea with are sitting in windowless rooms right now."

Silence hung in the small room for several seconds. Only the faint hum of the air unit filled the space.

"Zurich wants to know who leaked the files," Li Weiming said, keeping his voice level. "The Northern Ledger spent twelve years building those lines. A network that deep does not fall over in one night unless somebody handed the whole map to the central offices."

"I thought the same thing," Feng Guoqiang said. He stubbed out his cigarette, though half of it was still unburned, and pulled a thin paper folder from his briefcase. "I spent two days calling every clerk and lawyer who still answers my phone. Most of them hung up on me. One old friend in the records office gave me a look at the case list before they locked the room."

He slid a single printed sheet across the polished wood.

Li Weiming picked it up and scanned the neat rows of names and case numbers. At the top sat Shanghai Port Customs. Below it sat Sha City Shengyuan Capital, followed by dozens of shipping firms, loan offices, and city departments.

Then his eyes stopped at the very bottom line.

"Yunxi County?" Li Weiming asked, frowning. "Old Street and Pier Four demolition dispute?"

"Look at the time stamp," Feng Guoqiang said, pointing a finger at the right side of the page. "Same night. Same sweep. Same central team signing the orders."

Li Weiming stared at the line for a long moment. "Why would a tiny county demolition project end up in the same file as a port director and 318 senior officials? How much money was even in that county job?"

"Peanuts," Feng Guoqiang said. "A few million in local land work. Barely worth a footnote. Yet the county officials who pushed that demolition were dragged out of bed at the exact same hour as Xu Wenbin."

"That makes no sense," Li Weiming murmured.

"That is why I am telling you to fly back tonight," Feng Guoqiang said, his eyes wide and urgent. He gripped the edge of the table. "Whatever hit us on Tuesday is not a normal business fight. Whoever sits behind that file reaches straight from the top down to a dirt road in Yunxi County. If you poke at it, you will not walk out."

Li Weiming folded the paper neatly and tucked it inside his jacket. His face stayed calm and cold.

"Zurich paid me 500k to land here and bring back an answer," Li Weiming said. "If I fly home tonight with nothing but a scared lawyer's warning, I will not have a job by Monday."

"Better no job than a cell," Feng Guoqiang snapped. "I gave you the list. That is the last favor I owe Zurich. Do not call my office again."

Feng Guoqiang grabbed his briefcase and walked out without looking back.

Li Weiming finished his tea alone by the window, looking down at the gray river and distant port cranes. A small county street did not topple 318 officials on its own. Somebody in Yunxi had pulled a string, or somebody powerful stood behind that street. Either way, local lawyers were too terrified to dig.

He pulled out a plain black phone and dialed an overseas number.

"Carl," Li Weiming said softly when the line opened. "Are you and Anton in the city? Meet me in the lobby coffee bar at seven. I have a quiet drive for you tomorrow."
