# Chapter 111: Marcus Vance Lands in Shanghai

The flight from Zurich touched down in Shanghai just past two in the afternoon.

Li Weiming walked through arrivals in a dark gray suit, pulling a small leather suitcase. His passport listed his name as Marcus Vance. Few in Shanghai knew his real name, and fewer knew who paid for his tickets.

A black town car waited at the curb. Forty minutes later, he rode the private elevator to the top floor of the Harbor View Hotel.

At the back of the executive lounge sat a quiet, dim smoking room. In the corner sat a middle-aged man in a rumpled navy suit beside an ashtray holding three cigarette butts. Feng Guoqiang looked up when the door opened, pale, with dark rings under his eyes.

"You are late," Feng Guoqiang said tightly.

"Customs took ten extra minutes," Li Weiming replied, setting his suitcase down and pouring himself hot black tea. "You look like you have not slept since Tuesday."

"Nobody in this city has slept since Tuesday," Feng Guoqiang muttered, lighting a fresh cigarette with slightly shaking fingers.

Li Weiming watched the trembling hand before sipping his tea. "Tell me what happened. Zurich got three different stories in twelve hours, and none made sense."

Feng Guoqiang exhaled gray smoke. "Because none of it makes sense to us either. Remember Qi Tongwei, the Customs Director at Shanghai Port?"

"I met him twice last year," Li Weiming said calmly. "Careful man. Never signed his own name to anything dirty, and always kept two backup lines open inside the city."

"He is gone," Feng Guoqiang said flatly. "Not moved to another desk. Not asked to step down quietly. Gone. At midnight Tuesday, four black cars pulled up outside his house. The officers did not notify local police or city hall. They walked straight through his front gate with signed arrest papers already stamped from the top."

Li Weiming set his cup down gently. "No warning at all?"

"Not a whisper," Feng Guoqiang said, tapping ash. "Not one call from the port office. Not one hint from the courts. Even his driver did not know until the cuffs were on his wrists."

"And Jingzhou City?" Li Weiming asked. "Shanshui Group?"

Feng Guoqiang gave a bitter laugh. "Exact same hour, down to the minute. By one in the morning, every glass door at Shanshui Group was sealed with white paper tape. Every bank account tied to the company was locked solid. Over 800 million was frozen behind a court order before sunrise. All four senior partners were loaded onto a bus before the cleaners even arrived."

Li Weiming leaned back, a thin crease forming between his eyebrows.

"That does not happen by accident," he said quietly. "To freeze 800 million and pull a port customs director out of bed the same night, someone had to plan it for months. Yet none of our people in Shanghai or Jingzhou City heard a single rumor."

"It is worse than you think," Feng Guoqiang said, leaning forward and lowering his voice. "Qi Tongwei and Shanshui Group were not the whole catch. By Wednesday night, 318 officials across the province and nearby cities had been pulled in for questioning. Three hundred and eighteen, Marcus. City chiefs, tax heads, bank managers, port clerks. Half the people I drank tea with are sitting in windowless rooms right now."

Silence hung in the dim room, broken only by the hum of the air unit.

"Zurich wants to know who leaked the files," Li Weiming said evenly. "The Northern Ledger spent twelve years building those lines. A network that deep does not fall in one night unless somebody handed the whole map to the central offices."

"I thought the same thing," Feng Guoqiang said, stubbing out his half-smoked cigarette and pulling a thin folder from his briefcase. "I spent two days calling every clerk and lawyer who still answers. Most hung up on me. One old friend in the records office let me look at the case list before they locked the room."

He slid a single printed sheet across the table.

Li Weiming scanned the neat rows of names and case numbers. At the top sat Shanghai Port Customs. Below it sat Jingzhou City Shanshui Group, followed by dozens of shipping firms, loan offices, and city departments.

Then his eyes stopped at the bottom line.

"Yunxi County?" Li Weiming frowned. "Old Street and Pier Four demolition dispute?"

"Look at the time stamp," Feng Guoqiang said, tapping the right side of the page. "Same night. Same sweep. Same central team signing the orders."

Li Weiming stared at the line. "Why would a tiny county demolition project end up in the same file as a port director and 318 senior officials? How much money was even in that county job?"

"Peanuts," Feng Guoqiang said. "A few million in local land work. Barely worth a footnote. Yet the county officials pushing that demolition were dragged out of bed at the exact same hour as Qi Tongwei."

"That makes no sense," Li Weiming murmured.

"That is why I am telling you to fly back tonight," Feng Guoqiang urged, gripping the table edge. "Whatever hit us Tuesday is not a normal business fight. Whoever sits behind that file reaches straight from the top down to a dirt road in Yunxi County. If you poke at it, you will not walk out."

Li Weiming folded the paper neatly into his jacket, his face calm and cold.

"Zurich paid me 500k to land here and bring back an answer," Li Weiming said. "If I fly home tonight with nothing but a scared lawyer's warning, I will not have a job by Monday."

"Better no job than a cell," Feng Guoqiang snapped. "I gave you the list. That is the last favor I owe Zurich. Do not call my office again."

Feng Guoqiang grabbed his briefcase and walked out without looking back.

Li Weiming finished his tea alone by the window, looking down at the gray river and port cranes. A small county street did not topple 318 officials on its own. Somebody in Yunxi had pulled a string, or somebody powerful stood behind that street. Local lawyers were too terrified to dig.

He pulled out a plain black phone and dialed an overseas number.

"Carl," Li Weiming said softly when the line opened. "Are you and Anton in the city? Meet me in the lobby coffee bar at seven. I have a quiet drive for you tomorrow."
