# Chapter 111: Taking the Wife Home to Handong

At seven-thirty on Saturday morning, the spring sunshine poured through the high glass roof of Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station as the white high-speed train bound for Jingzhou City and Yunxi County in Handong Province glided away from the platform.

In the quiet second-row seats of the first-class carriage, Chen Xuan sat by the window wearing a comfortable gray cotton jacket, with his scratched green enamel thermos resting on the small folding tray table in front of him. Beside him sat Qin Yuhan, the famous president of Haiyue Group, dressed today not in a sharp designer suit from Lujiazui, but in a soft cream-colored knit sweater, simple blue jeans, and white sneakers.

On the overhead luggage rack directly above their seats sat four neatly wrapped wooden gift boxes tied with red silk ribbons.

Fifty meters back in the adjacent second-class carriage, just behind the glass connecting door, two quiet men from the Black Order sat in plain dark jackets, reading morning newspapers while keeping their silent watch over Chen Xuan.

Qin Yuhan did not look out the window at the green fields rolling past outside Shanghai. Instead, she kept looking up at the overhead luggage rack, her slender fingers fiddling with the strap of her small leather handbag on her lap.

"Chen Xuan," Qin Yuhan whispered for the tenth time since they left Emerald Riverside Villa at six-thirty that morning, "are you completely sure Aunt Liu Meifang likes red-date walnut pastries more than lotus-paste cakes?"

Chen Xuan unscrewed the lid of his green thermos, blew gently on the hot green tea inside, and turned to look at his wife with a teasing grin.

"Wife," Chen Xuan chuckled, covering her restless fingers with his warm hand, "that is the tenth time you have asked me since we left the Bund. In the garage, you made me open the trunk twice to check the pastry boxes from Nanjing Road. In the waiting lounge, you asked me if the Tieguanyin tea leaves for my dad Chen Jianguo were in the red tin or the gold tin. When you signed a 500 million yuan port contract in Jin Mao Tower last Wednesday, you did not even blink."

Qin Yuhan's fair cheeks turned pink, and she gave his hand a light pinch. "A 500 million yuan contract in Jin Mao Tower only has lawyers and bankers across the table. If they do not like my terms, I can tell Assistant Su Wan to show them the door. How can I show your mom and dad the door in Yunxi County?"

"My mom and dad are the easiest people in Handong Province to please," Chen Xuan laughed softly. "My mom Liu Meifang taught primary school Chinese for thirty years, and my dad Chen Jianguo taught middle school math before retiring. Ever since we got our marriage certificate last winter and were too busy to visit Yunxi County together, my mom has called me three times a week just to ask what you like to eat."

"That makes it even more stressful," Qin Yuhan sighed softly, leaning toward his shoulder so the passengers across the aisle would not hear. "Look at what you told me on Thursday night. You said your mom already washed all the curtains in the sixth-floor apartment in Fuxing New Village, and your Second Uncle Chen Mingde even closed his hardware shop on Old Street early yesterday just to bring over fresh free-range eggs."

"Second Uncle Chen Mingde has the warmest heart on Old Street," Chen Xuan smiled fondly. "Wait until you meet my Second Aunt Wu Lanying and my twin cousins Chen Yuan and Chen Hao. Chen Yuan and Chen Hao are in their final year at Yunxi First High School right now. Both of them have their hearts set on testing into Fudan University in Shanghai this June. When my mom told Second Aunt that my new wife graduated from Fudan University, Second Uncle nearly came straight to the train station with a notebook."

Qin Yuhan's eyes brightened a little at that. "Your twin cousins want to apply to Fudan University? What subjects are they planning to study?"

"Chen Yuan wants to study economics, and her brother Chen Hao loves computer science," Chen Xuan said, reaching into his canvas bag and pulling out a small paper box of peeled green grapes he had washed that morning at Emerald Riverside Villa. He popped one sweet grape into Qin Yuhan's mouth. "Once you start talking to those two kids about Fudan University and the study rooms on Handan Road, you will forget all about being nervous."

Qin Yuhan chewed the cool, sweet grape and looked down at her cream-colored sweater. "What about my clothes? When my mother Lin Shuyin called from Beijing last night, she wanted me to bring that 120k yuan custom cashmere coat from Plaza 66. I told her if I wore a 120k yuan coat up six flights of stairs in Fuxing New Village, your parents would feel uncomfortable the whole weekend."

"You made the smartest choice in Shanghai," Chen Xuan said warmly, smoothing a loose strand of dark hair behind her ear. "My dad still rides a twenty-year-old bicycle to the morning vegetable market, and my mom thinks a three-hundred-yuan winter coat from the county department store is already high fashion. In this cream sweater and jeans, you look like a twenty-two-year-old college graduate coming home for spring break."

"Smooth talker," Qin Yuhan murmured, though the tension in her shoulders finally melted away and a genuine smile curved her lips. "How did a twelve-thousand-yuan logistics clerk from Hengxin Logistics learn how to coax people so well?"

"Practice," Chen Xuan grinned, feeding her a second green grape. "Every day at four fifty-five when I pack up my thermos to come home and cook dinner for President Qin, I practice thinking of nice things to say so you do not deduct my grocery allowance."

Qin Yuhan laughed softly, resting her cheek against his cotton sleeve as the announcements over the train speaker chimed that they were crossing the border into Handong Province.

"How much longer until we reach Yunxi County Station?" she asked, her voice warm and relaxed.

"Forty minutes," Chen Xuan said, pulling his light scarf over her lap like a small blanket. "Close your eyes and rest for half an hour. Once we get off the train, my mom's braised pork ribs and handmade dumplings will keep us eating until dinner time."

"Wake me up ten minutes before we arrive so I can comb my hair," Qin Yuhan whispered, closing her eyes with her fingers still linked lightly through his.

"Deal, Wife," Chen Xuan said softly, watching the sunlit hills of Handong Province roll past the window.
