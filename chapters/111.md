# Chapter 111: Taking the Wife Home to Handong

When the weekend arrived, the white high-speed train bound for Jingzhou City and Yunxi County glided smoothly away from the platform beneath the high glass roof of Shanghai Hongqiao Railway Station.

In the quiet second row of the first-class carriage, Chen Xuan sat by the window in a comfortable gray cotton jacket, his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos resting on the small folding tray table in front of him. Beside him sat Qin Yuhan, dressed not in a sharp designer suit from Lujiazui, but in a soft cream knit sweater, simple blue jeans, and clean white sneakers.

On the overhead luggage rack directly above their seats sat four neatly wrapped wooden gift boxes tied with red silk ribbons.

down the street in the adjacent second-class carriage, just behind the glass connecting door, two members of the Black Order sat quietly in plain matte-black jackets. Neither man looked toward the front carriage, yet their gaze swept every aisle and boarding door with silent precision.

Qin Yuhan smoothed the sleeves of her sweater for the third time in five minutes. She looked out at the receding skyline of Shanghai, then turned back to her husband with a rare flicker of nerves in her bright eyes.

"Chen Xuan, tell me honestly," she said softly. "Is this sweater too plain? When I meet your parents, should I have worn a nicer coat? What if Auntie thinks I did not dress formally enough?"

Chen Xuan chuckled and unscrewed the dented cap of his green thermos. Steam rose into the cool cabin air, carrying the light scent of autumn chrysanthemum tea. He poured a small cup into the lid and handed it to her.

"You sign 300 million yuan contracts on the 38th floor of Haiyue Tower without blinking," Chen Xuan teased gently. "Why are you nervous about meeting my mom and dad?"

"That is business," Qin Yuhan protested, taking a small sip of the warm tea. "This is your family. Your mother raised you, and your grandmother is turning eighty-one tomorrow. I want them to like me."

"My mom already likes you," Chen Xuan said with an easy smile. "When I called her last night, she spent twenty minutes asking what kind of dumplings you prefer and whether you eat spicy food. She was mostly relieved that I finally stopped living on instant noodles."

Qin Yuhan could not help laughing. The tension in her shoulders eased a little as she rested her elbow on the shared armrest. "Did you tell them what I do for a living?"

"I told them you work in management at a big company in Shanghai and that you are very capable," Chen Xuan replied. "If I tell my dad you run the whole Haiyue Group, he will spend all afternoon worrying that our sofa is too old for you to sit on. Just be yourself. They are simple, warm people."

"Good," Qin Yuhan murmured, her smile turning warm. "I brought West Lake tea for Uncle Chen, soft silk scarves for Auntie and Second Aunt, and a burgundy silk jacket with soft ginseng honey cake for Grandma. I also packed fruit candies and fountain pens for your two cousins."

"You thought of everyone," Chen Xuan said, reaching over to pat her hand. "By the time we leave tomorrow evening, my mom is going to try to stuff half of Yunxi County into our luggage."

As the high-speed train picked up speed, the glass towers of Shanghai gave way to wide autumn plains. Golden rice paddies stretched toward distant low hills, and rows of yellow ginkgo trees flashed past small country stations in the clear morning light.

Three hundred kilometers ahead in Jingzhou City, the autumn breeze rustled the courtyard trees outside the Handong Provincial Party Committee compound. Inside a quiet meeting room, Provincial Party Secretary Sha Ruijin and Jingzhou Municipal Party Secretary Li Dakang sat across from each other with plain porcelain teacups.

On the table between them lay the autumn economic and urban renewal report for the province. Ever since Gao Yuliang, Zhao Ruilong, and their corrupt faction had been arrested and swept out of Handong, the heavy fog over the province had finally lifted. Anti-Corruption Bureau Chief Hou Liangping was wrapping up the final asset recoveries cleanly, and honest officials could finally focus on simple people's lives.

Li Dakang tapped the Yunxi County section of the report with a satisfied nod. "Secretary Sha, the four national banks, China Construction Bank, Huaxia Bank, ICBC, and Bank of China, have all released the autumn batch of low-interest heritage and rural road funds. Old Street in Yunxi County is lively again, and the new asphalt road out to Hongshi Village was paved ahead of schedule."

Sha Ruijin looked out the window at the bright autumn sky and smiled faintly. "When the right people hold the reins and the dark clouds are cleared away, simple towns recover fast."

"It is still remarkable," Li Dakang said quietly, lowering his voice out of genuine respect. "That anonymous benefactor who stood behind the cleanup and brought the national banks into Handong never once asked for a plaque, a banquet, or a single favor in return. We do not even know his name."

"And we do not need to," Sha Ruijin replied calmly. "A person like that only wants to see the people live in peace. Our job is to guard that peace and never let corrupt hands stain Handong again."

Neither leader had any idea that the very man they spoke of with such deep respect was currently sitting on a high-speed train heading straight into their province, peeling a sweet tangerine and arguing playfully with his wife over who got the bigger half.

When the cabin speaker chimed softly to announce the arrival at Yunxi County Station, Chen Xuan wiped his fingers with a tissue and stood up to lift the four wooden gift boxes down from the overhead rack.

"Ready to meet the Chen family?" he asked, his eyes crinkling with amusement.

Qin Yuhan stood up, smoothed her cream sweater one last time, and slipped her slender hand firmly into his. "Ready."

Holding his wife's hand in one palm and his dented green thermos in the other, Chen Xuan stepped out of the carriage into the crisp autumn air of his hometown.
