# Chapter 55: The Unnoticed Watchman

At six forty on Monday morning, Chen Xuan pushed open the rusted stairwell door of Building Four and stepped onto the damp brick walkway of Fuxing New Village.

The cool air carried the ordinary scent of coal briquettes, fried dough sticks, and wet cement. Two retired neighbors passed with woven grocery baskets while a rusty bicycle rattled toward the main road.

To anyone else, the street looked unchanged from yesterday.

Chen Xuan paused for half a heartbeat while zipping his dark jacket.

Yesterday evening, three Black Pine Guard shadow teams had maintained layered outer rings around this block, invisible to ordinary eyes yet unmistakable to him by the disciplined rhythm of their rotation. Now they were gone. Not a single Black Pine operative remained within two kilometers. Under Zero Guard's stand-down order, even Number 1 had withdrawn his people and stepped back into silent monitoring.

Yet as Chen Xuan walked through the compound archway onto the narrow county street, his gaze swept casually across the asphalt toward the faded green bus stop thirty meters away.

Two quiet, unremarkable figures in plain dark coats stood near the rusted timetable post. One held a folded newspaper without turning its pages; the other leaned lightly against the rail with a paper cup of soy milk, eyes resting calmly on the entrance to Fuxing New Village. Neither wore an earpiece or stiff military posture. They blended effortlessly into the sleepy Monday commuters, yet their spacing covered both ends of the lane, the alley mouth, and every approaching vehicle inside a clean fifty-meter perimeter around Chen Xuan.

Zero Guard did not leave gaps when it took over a watch.

Chen Xuan did not slow his stride. He crossed to the corner where white steam poured from stacked bamboo baskets beside a blue awning.

"Uncle Wang," Chen Xuan called warmly at the wooden cart. "Six pork-and-scallion buns and two red-bean buns, please."

The vendor, Wang Deshun, lifted a bamboo lid, sending savory steam into the cool air. A stocky man in his late fifties with flour across his apron, he dropped his jovial expression into a sympathetic frown the moment he saw Chen Xuan.

"Xiao Xuan," Wang Deshun said, lowering his voice as he grabbed bamboo tongs. "You are up early. Did your family get any sleep?"

"Enough to get by, Uncle Wang," Chen Xuan replied easily, pulling a crisp twenty-yuan note from his pocket. "Chen Yuan and Chen Hao still need hot food before heading out."

Beside the cart, a middle-aged neighbor named Zhao Shufen clutched her shopping bag and leaned in with wide, anxious eyes.

"Xiao Xuan, I heard everything at the vegetable market at six o'clock," Zhao Shufen whispered rapidly, glancing down the lane. "Everyone says Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu dragged your Second Uncle Chen Mingde into Old Street Station last night, and Officer Zhou Quan already signed the detention sheet! Did Chairman Deng Yongchang really tell them to make an example of your family over that shopfront?"

"People at the market always add three extra chapters to a half-page story, Auntie Zhao," Chen Xuan said with a light chuckle, taking the warm bag of buns.

"Do not joke, child," Zhao Shufen sighed with genuine pity. "Who in Yunxi County dares cross Chairman Deng Yongchang? Last year, when the old hardware repairman on East Street refused Deng Yongchang's transfer paper, Yan Tiehu broke two of his ribs and Director Sun Weiming still fined him ten thousand yuan. Your Second Uncle is honest and stubborn. Once they lock him into a file, your whole family will be bled dry."

Wang Deshun tucked two extra pork buns into the bag, tied the handles, and pushed Chen Xuan's twenty-yuan bill firmly back across the counter.

"Keep your money, Xiao Xuan," Wang Deshun muttered, his rough fingers gripping Chen Xuan's wrist for a second. "It is just eight buns. Save every yuan you have. If your father needs a lawyer or bail money for Chen Mingde, you will need every penny. Ordinary folks cannot win against Deng Yongchang and Deputy County Magistrate Liang Guodong. Better bow your heads early before the whole household gets ruined."

Chen Xuan looked into Wang Deshun's sincere, weathered eyes and felt a real spark of warmth. In a small county terrified of local tyrants, offering free breakfast and honest warning to a marked family took quiet courage.

Still, Chen Xuan slid the twenty-yuan note back onto the board and weighted it with a clean metal spoon.

"I will take the two extra buns with thanks, Uncle Wang, because Chen Hao eats like a growing horse," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Keep the money, though. Business is hard enough in this cold."

"Xiao Xuan, listen to an old man," Wang Deshun urged, eyebrows knotting. "Do not be proud today. By nine o'clock, Old Street Station sends Chen Mingde to the county detention center. Once he passes that iron gate, nobody in Yunxi can pull him back out."

"Uncle Wang," Chen Xuan said calmly, his relaxed tone carrying an unmistakable quiet weight. "Auntie Zhao. Take my word for it. Do not worry about Second Uncle, and do not lose sleep over Cheng Biao or Deng Yongchang. People who shout the loudest in small counties usually fold the fastest when a real morning inspection arrives."

Zhao Shufen blinked. "A morning inspection? Who would inspect Yunxi County on an ordinary Monday?"

"Wait until nine o'clock," Chen Xuan said with a pleasant smile. "The street will be much quieter after breakfast."

He bade both neighbors goodbye and turned back toward Fuxing New Village. Behind him, Zhao Shufen clicked her tongue and whispered to Wang Deshun that poor Chen Xuan was putting on a brave front so his parents would not collapse.

Halfway across the narrow street, a noisy three-wheeled delivery cart rattled past, its horn honking twice.

In the brief gap after the cart rolled by, Chen Xuan cast a half-second glance toward the faded green bus stop.

The two dark-coated figures did not turn toward him, nod, or break cover. Yet the one holding the folded newspaper shifted his gaze cleanly by three degrees to sweep the blind alley corner as the cart passed, while the second figure's fingers rested effortlessly near his coat lapel until the lane cleared.

Completely silent. Completely unseen by the neighbors, the vendors, and the local thugs who still believed they owned Yunxi County.

Chen Xuan's lips curved into a quiet, unbothered smile.

Shifting the warm bag of buns to his left hand, he stepped back through the archway of Fuxing New Village and headed upstairs to eat breakfast with his family while the storm rolling down from the capital closed in on Yunxi County minute by minute.
