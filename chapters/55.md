# Chapter 55: The Unnoticed Watchman

At six forty on Monday morning, Chen Xuan pushed open the rusted stairwell door of Building Four and stepped onto the damp brick walkway of Fuxing New Village.

The cool morning air smelled of coal stoves, fried dough sticks, and wet stone. Two older neighbors walked past with grocery baskets while a rusty bicycle rattled toward the main road.

To anyone else, the street looked just like yesterday.

Chen Xuan paused for half a second while zipping up his dark jacket.

Yesterday evening, three White Guard teams had kept a quiet outer ring around this block. Ordinary people never noticed them, yet Chen Xuan knew they were there from the steady pattern of their shifts. Now they were all gone. Not a single White Guard member was within two kilometers. Under Black Guard's stand-down order, even Number 1 had pulled his men back and gone into quiet monitoring.

Yet as Chen Xuan walked through the neighborhood archway onto the narrow county street, his eyes swept casually across the road toward the faded green bus stop thirty meters away.

Two quiet, ordinary-looking men in plain dark coats stood near the rusted bus sign. One held a folded newspaper without turning a page. The other leaned lightly against the rail with a paper cup of soy milk, his eyes resting calmly on the entrance to Fuxing New Village. Neither wore an earpiece or stood like a stiff soldier. They blended right into the sleepy Monday crowd. Even so, their positions covered both ends of the lane, the alley mouth, and every car coming down the street within fifty meters of Chen Xuan.

Black Guard did not leave gaps when it took over a watch.

Chen Xuan did not slow down. He crossed to the street corner where white steam poured from stacked bamboo steamers beside a blue tarp.

"Uncle Wang," Chen Xuan called warmly at the wooden cart. "Six pork-and-scallion buns and two red-bean buns, please."

The vendor, Wang Deshun, lifted a bamboo lid, sending warm, savory steam into the cool air. He was a stocky man in his late fifties with flour dusted across his apron. The moment he saw Chen Xuan, his cheerful smile dropped into a worried frown.

"Xiao Xuan," Wang Deshun said, lowering his voice as he picked up his bamboo tongs. "You are up early. Did your family get any sleep last night?"

"Enough to get by, Uncle Wang," Chen Xuan answered easily, pulling a crisp twenty-yuan bill from his pocket. "Chen Yuan and Chen Hao still need hot food before they head out."

Beside the cart, a middle-aged neighbor named Zhao Shufen clutched her cloth shopping bag and leaned in with wide, worried eyes.

"Xiao Xuan, I heard everything at the vegetable market at six o'clock," Zhao Shufen whispered fast, glancing nervously down the lane. "Everyone says Cheng Biao and Yan Tiehu dragged your Second Uncle Chen Mingde into Old Street Station on Friday, and Officer Zhou Quan already signed the jail paper! Did Chairman Deng Yongchang really tell them to make an example of your family over that little shop?"

"People at the market always add three extra chapters to a half-page story, Auntie Zhao," Chen Xuan said with a light laugh, taking the warm bag of buns.

"Do not joke, child," Zhao Shufen sighed with real pity. "Who in Yunxi County dares cross Chairman Deng Yongchang? Last year, when the old hardware repairman on East Street refused to sign Deng Yongchang's paper, Yan Tiehu broke two of his ribs, and County Magistrate Sun Weiming still fined him ten thousand yuan. Your Second Uncle is honest and stubborn. Once they lock his name into a police file, your whole family will be bled dry."

Wang Deshun slipped two extra pork buns into the bag, tied the handles, and pushed Chen Xuan's twenty-yuan bill firmly back across the wooden board.

"Keep your money, Xiao Xuan," Wang Deshun muttered, his rough fingers gripping Chen Xuan's wrist for a second. "It is just eight buns. Save every yuan you have. If your father needs a lawyer or bail money for Chen Mingde, you will need every penny. Regular folks cannot beat Deng Yongchang and Public Security Chief Liang Guodong. It is better to bow your heads early before the whole family gets ruined."

Chen Xuan looked into Wang Deshun's honest, tired eyes and felt real warmth in his chest. In a small county terrified of local bullies, giving free breakfast and honest warning to a targeted family took quiet courage.

Still, Chen Xuan slid the twenty-yuan bill back onto the board and set a clean metal spoon on top of it so the wind would not blow it away.

"I will take the two extra buns with thanks, Uncle Wang, because Chen Hao eats like a growing horse," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Keep the money, though. Business is hard enough in this cold weather."

"Xiao Xuan, listen to an old man," Wang Deshun urged, his eyebrows knotting. "Do not be stubborn today. By nine o'clock, Old Street Station sends Chen Mingde to the county jail. Once he passes through that iron gate, nobody in Yunxi can pull him back out."

"Uncle Wang," Chen Xuan said calmly, his easy tone carrying a quiet, solid weight. "Auntie Zhao. Take my word for it. Do not worry about Second Uncle, and do not lose sleep over Cheng Biao or Deng Yongchang. The people who shout the loudest in small counties usually fold the fastest when a real morning inspection arrives."

Zhao Shufen blinked in confusion. "A morning inspection? Who would inspect Yunxi County on a plain Monday?"

"Wait until nine o'clock," Chen Xuan said with a friendly smile. "The street will be much quieter after breakfast."

He said goodbye to both neighbors and walked back toward Fuxing New Village. Behind him, Zhao Shufen clicked her tongue and whispered to Wang Deshun that poor Chen Xuan was only acting brave so his parents would not break down.

Halfway across the narrow street, a noisy three-wheeled delivery cart rattled past, honking its horn twice.

In the brief moment after the cart rolled by, Chen Xuan cast a half-second glance toward the faded green bus stop.

The two men in dark coats did not turn toward him, nod, or give themselves away. Yet the one holding the folded newspaper shifted his eyes smoothly to check the blind alley corner as the cart passed, while the other man's hand rested lightly near his coat jacket until the street was clear.

Completely silent. Completely unseen by the neighbors, the street vendors, and the local thugs who still thought they owned Yunxi County.

Chen Xuan's lips curved into a quiet, relaxed smile.

Switching the warm bag of buns to his left hand, he stepped back through the archway of Fuxing New Village and headed upstairs to eat breakfast with his family, while the storm rolling down from Beijing closed in on Yunxi County minute by minute.
