# Chapter 60: Mingde Hardware Reopens

By eight thirty the next morning, Old Street smelled of red gunpowder, fresh-swept dust, and steamed jujube cakes from the corner bakery.

Outside number fourteen, the rolling iron shutter of Mingde Hardware stood pushed all the way up. The cracked glass pane had been replaced, the wooden counter scrubbed until its grain shone warm amber, and every bin of bolts, washers, brass hinges, and padlocks stood lined up in crisp rows.

Above the doorway hung a wide banner of heavy crimson cloth. Its edges were double-stitched by hand with gold thread, the unmistakable craft of Old Tailor Yu Changshun, who had stayed up past midnight to finish it. Painted across the cloth in bold brushstrokes were four simple words: *Mingde Hardware: Still Here*.

Chen Mingde stood on the threshold in a clean dark jacket, his hands slightly unsteady as he touched the doorframe. Beside him, Wu Lanying wiped her eye with her apron and pretended it was only firecracker smoke.

"Light it, Chen Hao!" Chen Jianguo called out, grinning beside Liu Meifang and Zhou Guilan. "Let the whole street hear!"

Chen Hao touched a glowing incense stick to the fuse of the red firecrackers coiled on the curb. Old Street erupted in a roaring storm of scarlet paper and white smoke.

Neighbors poured out from the noodle shop, barber parlor, and tea stall across the alley, clapping and cheering. Tang Xiaoyu stood beside Chen Yuan with her hands lightly over her ears, laughing as pink confetti landed on her red sweater.

Yu Changshun leaned on his cane at the edge of the crowd, squinting up at his crimson banner with quiet pride. "Those stitches will hold through ten winters," he told Chen Fugen. "Longer than any crooked developer."

"Longer than all of them combined," Chen Fugen laughed, clapping Yu Changshun on the back.

As the smoke drifted upward, a white-and-blue patrol car slowed to a gentle stop at the mouth of the alley. No siren sounded. The driver's door opened, and Director Wei Kun stepped out in a crisp, freshly pressed uniform, his new rank insignia catching the morning light.

The chatter quieted for half a beat, then warmed into welcoming murmurs as shopkeepers recognized the young officer who had refused to bend.

Wei Kun walked straight to the red paper carpet outside Mingde Hardware, removed his peaked cap, and offered Chen Mingde a clean, respectful bow.

"Uncle Chen Mingde, Aunt Wu Lanying," Wei Kun said warmly. "Congratulations on reopening. Old Street feels like itself again today."

Chen Mingde blinked, visibly moved, and hurried forward to clasp Wei Kun's hand with both of his own. "Director Wei! You are busy with the new post, you did not have to come in person."

"This is part of my post," Wei Kun replied evenly. "My first patrol of the morning belongs to Old Street. If anyone ever tries to block a doorway, threaten a clerk, or drop an unlawful notice here again, call the station desk directly. My personal number is already on the committee board."

Genuine applause rose from the neighboring shopkeepers. Wei Kun exchanged a brief, quiet nod with Chen Xuan near the doorway. Neither man needed to mention the seven fifty timestamp or the night everything turned. Both knew the weight of that single honest line in the logbook.

After sharing a cup of hot tea with Chen Mingde and greeting Chen Fugen, Wei Kun put his cap back on and returned to his patrol car, leaving the lane lighter than he had found it.

* * *

Half an hour later, when the first rush of neighbors had thinned into steady customers buying latches, pliers, and electrical tape, a familiar rattling clattered down the brick paving of Old Street.

It was a battered green electric delivery tricycle with patched mudguards and a welded steel rack.

The rider braked carefully ten feet from the doorway so he would not kick dust onto the clean threshold. He was a lean young man of twenty-four with sun-weathered cheeks, a faded blue canvas work jacket stained faintly with axle grease, and rubber-soled shoes worn smooth at the heels.

Wang Lei.

He climbed down from the saddle, lifted a woven bamboo basket of shiny red orchard apples from the cargo bed, and looked slightly hesitant at the lively crowd around the shop.

Chen Xuan spotted him at once. Setting down the box of screws he was sorting for his Second Uncle, he walked straight out through the doorway to meet him.

"Wang Lei," Chen Xuan said, his voice carrying a warmth he reserved for only a handful of people. "You came."

Wang Lei's sunburned face broke into an honest grin. "Chen Xuan. I heard Mingde Hardware was opening back up this morning. I just finished a dawn drop at the wholesale market and brought some apples for Uncle Chen Mingde and Aunt Wu Lanying. From my cousin's hillside orchard, nothing fancy, just crisp."

Chen Xuan took the heavy basket with both hands, his fingers brushing Wang Lei's rough, calloused knuckles.

"They are the best gift we have gotten all morning," Chen Xuan said softly.

He had not forgotten what Wang Lei had done during the darkest week of Old Street, when Shen Jinbao's men watched the alley and regular customers stayed away. Wang Lei had walked into the empty shop with his head down, bought a single steel hinge he did not need, and paid with crumpled five-yuan and one-yuan bills from the bottom of his pocket, simply so Chen Mingde would have a sale in the drawer that day.

And Chen Xuan remembered something older than that.

Seven years ago, in senior year at Yunxi High School, Chen Xuan's father had been laid off from the county machinery plant for six months. Every Tuesday and Thursday, when Chen Xuan's meal card beeped empty at the cafeteria window and he quietly turned away pretending not to be hungry, Wang Lei would catch up to him by the concrete stairwell.

Wang Lei's own family was no richer. His mother already suffered from chronic kidney trouble, and Wang Lei swept the courtyard before dawn for a tiny stipend. Yet without a loud word, Wang Lei would press a folded five-yuan note and three crumpled one-yuan notes into Chen Xuan's palm.

*Eight yuan.* Enough for two scoops of cabbage, one piece of braised pork, and a bowl of hot rice.

*Eat first, Chen Xuan,* Wang Lei used to say, rubbing his neck awkwardly. *You cannot solve physics problems on cold tap water. Pay me back when we are both rich.*

Chen Xuan looked at his old classmate standing beside the rusted green tricycle, shoulders slightly stooped from hauling crates since four in the morning, and felt a quiet, deep steadiness settle inside his chest.

"Come inside," Chen Xuan said, guiding Wang Lei toward the counter. "Second Uncle has been asking about you. Let us have tea first, and tell me how your mother is doing."

"Resting better this week," Wang Lei said, stepping carefully over the red firecracker paper. "Just happy to see your family's shop open again, Chen Xuan. Truly happy."
