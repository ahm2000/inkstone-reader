# Chapter 205: Monday Morning at Hengxin

Thin silver frost coated the windshields of parked cars outside the Pudong business park on Monday morning. Inside the 12th floor of Hengxin Logistics, however, the central heating hummed steadily, keyboards clicked in a lively rhythm, and the smell of roasted coffee mixed with the warm steam of morning tea.

Chen Xuan swiped his employee badge at 8:25 AM, hung his charcoal winter coat neatly over the back of his swivel chair, and set his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos beside his monitor. He dropped a pinch of fresh jasmine tea leaves into the thermos, filled it with hot water from the pantry dispenser, and let the clean floral scent drift across his small cubicle.

Monday mornings on the 12th floor always had a familiar, unhurried buzz. Phones rang at the dispatch desks, printers spat out weekly freight schedules, and young clerks swapped weekend stories while waiting for their computers to boot.

At the adjacent desk, Sister Pan Xiulan leaned over the low gray partition holding her pink ceramic mug in one hand and her smartphone in the other. Her eyes were wide with Monday morning excitement.

"Xiao Chen, did you see the news this morning?" Sister Pan asked in a hushed, eager voice, tilting her phone screen toward him. "That huge Hong Kong shipping giant, Seven-Harbor Consortium, blew up completely over the weekend! Their chairman was led out of his luxury penthouse in handcuffs on Sunday morning, and international banks in Singapore and Zurich froze billions of dollars in dirty accounts!"

Chen Xuan leaned back slightly in his chair, glanced politely at the bright news banner on her screen, and gave a mild nod. "I saw a bit of it on the evening news last night. Sounds like a pretty big mess."

"Big mess? It is an earthquake!" Sister Pan exclaimed, tapping the edge of his desk. "Even our mid-tier freight clients in Shanghai are calling in a panic this morning to rebook their December container space. Do you know how powerful Seven-Harbor used to be? Two weeks ago, our sales director could not even get an appointment with their regional vice president. Now the whole empire is gone in forty-eight hours!"

She took a quick sip from her mug and shook her head in wonder. "Everybody in the breakroom is guessing who took them down. Some say it was a joint crackdown from Beijing, others say a hidden financial giant in Zurich pulled the plug. Imagine the kind of power it takes to flatten a Hong Kong shipping titan before Monday breakfast."

"Sounds complicated," Chen Xuan said evenly, unscrewing the lid of his green thermos to let the jasmine tea cool. "Good thing we just handle regular shipping manifests here. Less stress."

Sister Pan laughed and waved a hand at him. "You really have the calmest temperament in the whole building, Xiao Chen. Oh, by the way, how was your northern warehouse check? You did not freeze up there, did you?"

"It was cold, but the pine nuts were good," Chen Xuan smiled, reaching into his desk drawer and handing her a small paper pouch of the cracked Changbai pine nuts. "Brought a bag back for the office. Try some with your tea."

"Oh, you are so thoughtful!" Sister Pan beamed, taking the pouch warmly. "Wait until the girls in accounting see these."

Before she could open the bag, heavy, impatient footsteps marched down the main aisle.

Department supervisor Hu Dewei strode up to Chen Xuan's cubicle wearing a tight pinstripe suit and a sour, self-important scowl. Cradled in his arms was a bulging gray plastic file crate packed to the brim with thick manila folders.

Without a word of greeting, Hu Dewei hoisted the crate and dumped a towering stack of paper directly onto the right corner of Chen Xuan's desk. The heavy thud rattled Chen Xuan's pen cup and sent a stray sticky note fluttering to the floor.

"Chen Xuan," Hu Dewei barked loudly enough for half the 12th floor to hear. "Stop chatting about weekend snacks and look alive. Because of the Hong Kong shipping shake-up, we have 300 backlogged customs clearance files and container manifests that need full line-by-line verification today."

Sister Pan's smile vanished. She stared at the knee-high stack of folders in disbelief. "Manager Hu, 300 customs files? That is three full days of work for two people, even without regular Monday dispatches!"

Hu Dewei shot her a cold glare, then turned a smug, petty smirk back toward Chen Xuan. He had been waiting for an excuse to make Chen Xuan sweat ever since the junior clerk refused to flatter him at last month's department meeting.

"The regional director wants every single manifest audited, cross-checked against the new port codes, and logged into the system before 4:55 PM," Hu Dewei snapped, tapping the top folder with two fingers. "Since Chen Xuan always clocks out right on the dot and looks like he has plenty of energy, he can handle all 300 files himself. Do not even think about leaving early if a single number is wrong."

Around the nearby cubicles, several clerks exchanged uneasy glances, waiting for Chen Xuan to argue, frown, or beg for help.

Chen Xuan did none of those things.

He bent down calmly, picked up the fallen sticky note, and stuck it neatly back onto the corner of his monitor. Then he rested one hand on the towering stack of 300 customs files, straightened the edges until they were perfectly flush, and looked up at Hu Dewei with a polite, completely unbothered smile.

"No problem, Manager Hu," Chen Xuan said smoothly. "I will get through them before 4:55 PM."

Hu Dewei blinked, momentarily thrown off by the total lack of panic. He let out a sharp snort, adjusted his tie, and strutted back toward his glass office, already pleased with himself for putting the junior clerk in his place.

"Xiao Chen, I can help you sort the first fifty once I finish my morning emails," Sister Pan whispered sympathetically across the partition.

"No need, Sister Pan," Chen Xuan chuckled softly, clicking open the logistics spreadsheet on his screen. "They are mostly standard coastal codes. Once you get into a rhythm, they go fast."

He flipped open the first manila folder, recognized the clean port routing numbers with quiet amusement, and lifted his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos. Taking a slow, warm sip of jasmine tea, Chen Xuan settled comfortably into his chair, ready for another peaceful Monday at his corner desk.
