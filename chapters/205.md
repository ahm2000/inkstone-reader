# Chapter 205: Monday Morning at Hengxin

Monday morning arrived over Shanghai with crisp blue skies and the pleasant, bustling energy of a new workweek.

At eight-fifty-five, the elevator doors of the twelfth floor at Hengxin Logistics opened smoothly.

Chen Xuan strolled out into the reception hallway, wearing his neatly ironed light blue button-down shirt, dark trousers, and comfortable black leather shoes. In his left hand, he held his scratched green thermos, filled to the brim with hot roasted jasmine tea that Qin Yuhan had personally prepared for him before he left the villa.

He walked past the glass display cases showcasing Hengxin's shipping trophies and stepped into the main operations bullpen.

Desks were already covered in paper manifests, computer monitors hummed with freight tracking software, and phones rang in steady succession across the department.

"Morning, Brother Xuan!" a young dispatch clerk called out, waving from the copier.

"Good morning, Xiao Liu," Chen Xuan smiled, walking down the central aisle toward his familiar desk by the row of potted ficus plants.

He sat down in his ergonomic swivel chair, booted up his desktop computer, and placed his green thermos neatly on the corner of his desk blotter.

Two desks away, Sister Pan Xiulan looked over her monitor, her eyes wide with excitement as she held up a copy of the Shanghai Morning Post.

"Xiao Xuan, you are back from your northern warehouse trip!" Pan Xiulan whispered eagerly, rolling her chair over to his desk. "Did you see the front page this morning? The whole business world has gone completely insane!"

Chen Xuan opened his freight intake software and chuckled mildly. "What happened, Sister Pan? Did shipping rates spike again?"

"Much bigger than shipping rates!" Pan Xiulan tapped the front page with a manicured fingernail. "The Seven-Harbor Consortium in Hong Kong collapsed overnight! The Four National Banks seized 300 tons of stolen gold reserves in the north, and Victor Leung was arrested by ICAC in his penthouse yesterday morning! All of their domestic freight accounts have been frozen!"

Chen Xuan looked at the bold black headline, which read:
*STATE COUNCIL AND FOUR NATIONAL BANKS RECOVER 300 TONS OF BULLION; SEVEN-HARBOR ASSETS FROZEN ACROSS CHINA.*

"Three hundred tons of gold is quite a lot of metal," Chen Xuan remarked mildly, clicking his mouse to verify the morning's incoming container logs. "Good thing the national banks recovered it. Our state treasury will be much more secure."

"And that is not all!" Pan Xiulan leaned in closer, dropping her voice to a conspiratorial whisper. "The rumor in the shipping circles is that Haiyue Group is taking over all of Seven-Harbor's frozen coastal container routes! That means our 120 million yuan contract with Haiyue might double again before the end of the year!"

Before Chen Xuan could reply, the heavy glass door of the department manager's corner office swung open.

Department Manager Hu Dewei emerged, wearing his slightly wrinkled gray suit, clutching a thick stack of blue customs audit folders against his stomach. His brow was furrowed with stress, and he looked around the bullpen like a hawk searching for prey.

"Everyone, listen up!" Hu Dewei barked, his voice echoing across the twelfth floor. "The coastal customs bureaus are running emergency audits on all maritime freight following the Hong Kong freeze. General Manager Sun wants every single cargo manifest from the past six months re-verified by Friday!"

A collective groan rippled through the operations staff.

Hu Dewei marched down the aisle, his eyes landing on Chen Xuan's calm, relaxed posture. He slapped two thick audit folders onto Chen Xuan's desk with an authoritative thud.

"Chen Xuan! Since you spent the entire weekend wandering around some quiet northern warehouse while the rest of us were stressed about shipping quotas, you can handle the Jilin and Liaoning container verifications! Three hundred files, every customs seal checked against the port registry. I want the preliminary summary on my desk by four o'clock!"

Pan Xiulan gave Chen Xuan a sympathetic wince behind Hu Dewei's back.

Chen Xuan did not look annoyed in the slightest. He glanced at the two folders, noted the neat serial numbers he had already reviewed days ago, and looked up at Hu Dewei with an unhurried, polite smile.

"Understood, Manager Hu," Chen Xuan said calmly. "I will have the summary prepared before four."

"Hmph. Make sure there are no typos," Hu Dewei grunted, adjusting his collar before strutting back toward his corner office.

Pan Xiulan rolled her chair closer and whispered, "Xiao Xuan, three hundred files in six hours? Hu Dewei is deliberately dumping the tedious work on you because you rejected the Deputy General Manager offer last week!"

Chen Xuan unscrewed his green thermos, took a fragrant, peaceful sip of jasmine tea, and smiled serenely.

"It is only paperwork, Sister Pan," Chen Xuan said softly, cracking his knuckles as he opened the first file. "Checking container stamps is quiet work. No loud meetings, no banquets, and nobody shouting into satellite phones."

He checked the desk clock on his computer screen. It was nine-fifteen in the morning.

With six hours of steady, quiet typing ahead of him, he would finish all three hundred files by four-thirty, stroll out of the office at four-fifty-five sharp, take the subway home to Emerald Riverside, and spend another beautiful autumn evening cooking dinner with his wife.
