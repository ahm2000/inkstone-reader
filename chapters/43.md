# Chapter 43: Shanghai Port Customs Director

At two forty on Monday morning, Shanghai Port Customs Director Xu Wenbin paced his waterfront villa, clutching a whiskey glass and a burning cigarette between shaking fingers. Beyond the glass wall, the harbor lay dark and empty.

"Pier Four is empty, Marcus Vance!" Xu Wenbin snapped, spinning toward the sofa where his guest calmly poured scotch. "I checked the ship tracker! The rare-earth barge left the outer wall forty minutes late, and its signal vanished! No radio check, no tugboat log, nothing!"

Marcus Vance, tall in a grey suit with a consular pin, set the bottle down with a smooth smile. "Calm down, Director Xu Wenbin. Sea fog rolls into Shanghai Port every autumn. The captain likely killed his radio to avoid fishing boats."

"Do not talk to me like a clerk!" Xu Wenbin's jaw twitched as he blew smoke through his nose. "I have run Shanghai Port Customs for eleven years! Barges do not lose satellite tracking, radio, and harbor radar in the same minute! Ten minutes ago, my night-shift deputy stopped answering his desk! Even the Pier Four gate cameras are playing a frozen picture!"

Marcus Vance's smile thinned. He pulled a silver phone from his jacket, tapped twice, and watched the signal bar stay grey. His fingers paused half a second before sliding it back inside.

"A normal cable outage along the dock road," Marcus Vance said smoothly, though his shoulders straightened. "Nerves are ruining a clean night, Xu Wenbin. Your last payment of 1.8 million dollars cleared the Zurich Northern Ledger at midnight. Provincial Vice Governor Lin Zhengde's export permit covers the paperwork. You got paid, and the barge reaches open sea before sunrise."

"I want to cancel the cargo list right now!" Xu Wenbin panicked, ice rattling in his glass. "I am logging onto the customs computer to flag container forty-nine! If Coast Guard boats are sweeping the channel, I need a record showing I stopped the cargo myself!"

Marcus Vance stood in one fast move, all polite charm gone. "Sit down, Xu Wenbin. You are not touching that computer."

"Get out of my way!" Xu Wenbin stepped back, sudden suspicion widening his eyes. "Why do you care if I hold one barge of rare-earth ore for two days? Shengyuan Capital has held up ore before! You have never looked this tense over ore!"

Marcus Vance stared in cold silence. "Because container forty-nine does not only hold ore, Director Xu Wenbin."

A cold chill shot down Xu Wenbin's spine. "What did you put under my customs seal? Answer me, Marcus Vance! What is inside that container?"

"Nothing you need to know to survive retirement in Vancouver," Marcus Vance replied coldly. "Your name is on the signed shipping papers. Flag that container now, and your own anti-smuggling team will cut the lock, open the hidden wall, and hang you before noon. Drink your whiskey, shut your mouth, and let my people handle the water."

"You bastard!" Xu Wenbin's face turned blotchy with rage and fear. He jabbed his smoking hand at the foreigner. "You told me it was strictly rare-earth minerals! You swore there were no secret military items on my piers! I took money for trade passes, not treason!"

"You took 80 million yuan over four years through a Zurich front," Marcus Vance mocked. "In this country, Director Xu Wenbin, the firing squad does not care what you thought was inside the crate."

Before Xu Wenbin could scream again, the sliding glass door facing the water glided open without a sound. No hinge squeaked. No alarm chimed.

Xu Wenbin froze. Marcus Vance whipped around, his hand darting toward his inside pocket.

Six figures stepped silently from the deck and hallway at the same instant, sealing every exit in plain black gear and blank visors reflecting only the dim lamps.

Marcus Vance recovered first. He stopped his hand, slowly drew a dark blue diplomatic passport with a gold crest, and held it high.

"Hold right there!" Marcus Vance barked in crisp Mandarin, snapping into foreign-official pride. "I am Marcus Vance, First Commercial Liaison for the Consulate General! I hold full diplomatic protection under international law! You broke into a private home holding a foreign diplomat without notifying my consulate or your Foreign Ministry! Back off now, or you will trigger a crisis sitting on your Foreign Minister's desk before breakfast!"

Xu Wenbin stared desperately at the gold-crested passport, clinging to wild hope as he clutched his whiskey glass against his stomach.

None of the six black-clad figures glanced at the passport or moved an inch.

The figure by the glass door angled its blank visor toward Marcus Vance and spoke once, freezing the room.

"Diplomatic protection does not cover stolen navy radar plans."

The whiskey glass slipped straight from Xu Wenbin's numb fingers.

It shattered against the stone floor, spraying liquor and glass over his shoes. Xu Wenbin did not flinch, staring at Marcus Vance with eyes hollow with terror.

"Navy radar," Xu Wenbin whispered, voice shaking apart. "You put military radar plans inside my Pier Four container."

Marcus Vance's proud mask cracked at once. Blood drained from his face, and his hand holding the passport dropped two inches, trembling openly.

"That is a lie," Marcus Vance snapped, power gone from his voice. "I demand to call my consulate! I demand a lawyer!"

"Pier Four barge number 11 was boarded and taken twelve minutes ago," the guard said evenly. "The hidden wall inside container forty-nine is open. Your fingerprint lock and Xu Wenbin's customs code are both on the case."

Xu Wenbin's knees gave out. He sank straight into the broken glass, ignoring sharp shards pressing into his pants, and buried his face in shaking hands as a ragged sob tore from his throat.

"Take Marcus Vance and Xu Wenbin," the voice ordered. "Seize the customs computer and all phones. Move both men to central holding."

Four operatives stepped forward in silence. Marcus Vance twisted away, shouting furiously in English about treaties, before one fast grip locked his wrists behind his back. Beside him, Xu Wenbin did not fight at all, staring blankly at the dark harbor as they lifted him from the broken glass into the night.
