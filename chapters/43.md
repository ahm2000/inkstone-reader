# Chapter 43: Qingyan Port Customs Director

At two forty in the morning on Monday, Qingyan Port Customs Director Xu Wenbin paced his waterfront villa. He held a whiskey glass in his right hand and a burning cigarette between his shaking left fingers. Beyond the glass wall, the harbor water lay dark and empty, with no ship lights coming in.

"Pier Four is empty, Marcus Vance!" Xu Wenbin snapped, spinning toward the sofa where his guest calmly poured another glass of scotch. "I checked the port ship tracker! The rare-earth barge left the outer wall forty minutes late, and its signal vanished! No radio check, no tugboat log, nothing!"

Marcus Vance, tall in a dark grey suit with a consular pin on his jacket, set the bottle down with a cool, smooth smile. "Calm down, Director Xu Wenbin. Sea fog rolls into Qingyan Port every autumn. The captain likely turned off his radio to avoid fishing boats."

"Do not talk to me like a clerk!" Xu Wenbin's jaw clenched until his cheeks twitched. He took a hard drag on his cigarette and blew smoke from his nose. "I have run Qingyan Port Customs for eleven years! Barges do not lose satellite tracking, radio, and harbor radar in the exact same minute! Ten minutes ago, my night-shift deputy stopped answering his desk line! Even the Pier Four gate cameras are playing a frozen picture!"

Marcus Vance's smile thinned. He pulled a silver phone from his jacket pocket, tapped the screen twice, and watched the signal bar stay flat grey. His fingers paused half a second before he slid it back inside.

"A normal cable outage along the dock road," Marcus Vance said smoothly, though his shoulders straightened. "You are letting nerves ruin a clean night, Xu Wenbin. Your last payment of 18 million dollars went through the Zurich Northern Ledger at midnight. Provincial Vice Governor Lin Zhengde's export permit covers the paperwork. You got paid, and the barge will reach open sea before sunrise."

"I want to cancel the cargo list right now!" Xu Wenbin's voice rose in panic, ice rattling inside his glass. "I am logging onto the customs computer to flag container forty-nine for a check! If Coast Guard boats are sweeping the channel, I need a record showing I stopped the cargo myself!"

Marcus Vance stood up in one fast move. All polite charm vanished from his face. "Sit down, Xu Wenbin. You are not touching that computer."

"Get out of my way!" Xu Wenbin stepped back, eyes wide with sudden suspicion. "Why do you care if I hold one barge of rare-earth ore for two days? Shengyuan Capital has held up ore shipments before! You have never looked this tense over ore!"

Marcus Vance stared at him in cold silence. "Because container forty-nine does not only hold ore, Director Xu Wenbin."

A cold chill shot down Xu Wenbin's spine. His cigarette burned close to the filter, scorching his skin, and he barely felt it. "What did you put under my customs seal? Answer me, Marcus Vance! What is inside that container?"

"Nothing you need to know to survive your retirement in Vancouver," Marcus Vance replied coldly. "Your name is on the signed shipping papers. If you flag that container now, your own anti-smuggling team will cut the lock, open the hidden wall, and hang you before noon. Drink your whiskey, shut your mouth, and let my people handle the water."

"You bastard!" Xu Wenbin's face turned blotchy with anger and fear. He jabbed his smoking hand at the foreigner's chest. "You told me it was strictly rare-earth minerals! You swore there were no secret military items on my piers! I took money for trade passes, not treason!"

"You took 80 million yuan over four years through a Zurich front," Marcus Vance mocked. "In this country, Director Xu Wenbin, the firing squad does not care what you thought was inside the crate."

Before Xu Wenbin could scream another word, the sliding glass door facing the water glided open without a sound. No hinge squeaked. No alarm chimed.

Xu Wenbin froze with his mouth open. Marcus Vance whipped his head toward the deck, his hand darting toward his inside pocket.

Six figures stepped silently into the room from the deck and hallway at the same instant, sealing every exit. They wore plain black gear from head to toe, their blank visors reflecting only the dim lamps.

Marcus Vance recovered first. He stopped his hand, then slowly pulled out a dark blue diplomatic passport stamped with a gold crest and held it high.

"Hold right there!" Marcus Vance barked in crisp Mandarin, snapping back into his proud foreign-official manner. "I am Marcus Vance, First Commercial Liaison for the Consulate General! I hold full diplomatic protection under international law! You broke into a private home holding a foreign diplomat without telling my consulate or your Foreign Ministry! Back off right now, or you will cause a huge crisis that will sit on your Foreign Minister's desk before breakfast!"

Xu Wenbin stared desperately at the gold-crested passport. He clung to the wild hope that diplomatic protection might make these masked figures pause. His chest heaved as he clutched his whiskey glass against his stomach.

None of the six black-clad figures glanced at the passport or moved an inch.

The figure by the glass door angled its blank visor toward Marcus Vance. The guard spoke once, freezing the room.

"Diplomatic protection does not cover stolen navy radar plans."

The whiskey glass slipped straight out of Xu Wenbin's numb fingers.

It hit the stone floor and shattered with a loud crack, spraying liquor and sharp glass across his shoes. Xu Wenbin did not flinch. He stared at Marcus Vance with eyes wide and hollow with terror.

"Navy radar," Xu Wenbin whispered, his voice shaking so hard the words barely held together. "You put military radar plans inside my Pier Four container."

Marcus Vance's proud mask broke at once. Blood drained from his face, leaving his skin grey around his tight jaw. His hand holding the diplomatic passport dropped two inches, trembling openly for the first time.

"That is a lie," Marcus Vance snapped, though all power had left his voice. "I demand to call my consulate right now! I demand a lawyer!"

"Pier Four barge number 11 was boarded and taken twelve minutes ago," the guard said evenly. "The hidden wall inside container forty-nine is open. Your fingerprint lock and Xu Wenbin's customs code are both on the case."

Xu Wenbin's knees gave out. He sank onto the stone floor right into the broken glass, ignoring the sharp pieces pressing into his pants. He buried his face in his shaking hands as a ragged sob tore from his throat.

"Take Marcus Vance and Xu Wenbin," the voice ordered. "Seize the customs computer and all phones. Move both men to central holding."

Four black-clad operatives stepped forward in silence. Marcus Vance twisted his shoulder away, shouting furiously in English about treaties, before one fast grip locked both wrists behind his back and cut him short. Beside him, Xu Wenbin did not fight at all. He stared blankly at the dark water outside as they lifted him from the broken glass and marched him into the night.
