# Chapter 43: Qingyan Port Customs Director

At two forty in the morning on Monday, Qingyan Port Customs Director Xu Wenbin paced his marina villa with a crystal whiskey glass in his right hand and a burning cigarette between his shaking left fingers. Beyond the glass wall, the harbor channel lay dark and empty, with no running lights approaching the slip.

"Pier Four is empty, Marcus Vance!" Xu Wenbin snapped, spinning toward the sofa where his guest calmly poured another finger of scotch. "I checked the port transponder feed! The rare-earth barge left the outer breakwater forty minutes late, and its signal vanished! No radio check, no tugboat log, nothing!"

Marcus Vance, tall in a charcoal suit with a consular pin on his lapel, set the decanter down with a cool, practiced smile. "Calm down, Director Xu Wenbin. Coastal fog rolls into Qingyan Port every autumn. The captain probably switched off his civilian beacon to avoid trawlers."

"Do not talk to me like a clerk!" Xu Wenbin's jaw clenched until his cheeks twitched. He dragged hard on his cigarette and blew smoke from his nostrils. "I have run Qingyan Port Customs for eleven years! Barges do not lose satellite AIS, VHF voice, and harbor radar in the exact same minute! Ten minutes ago, my night-watch deputy stopped answering his desk line! Even the Pier Four gate cameras are looping a frozen frame!"

Marcus Vance's smile thinned. He drew a silver phone from his breast pocket, tapped the screen twice, and watched the signal bar stay flat grey. His fingers paused half a second before he slid it back into his jacket.

"A routine fiber outage along the dock road," Marcus Vance said smoothly, though his shoulders straightened. "You are letting nerves ruin a clean night, Xu Wenbin. Your final tranche of 18 million dollars cleared through the Zurich Northern Ledger at midnight. Provincial Vice Governor Lin Zhengde's export waiver seals the paperwork. You got paid, and the barge will reach international waters before sunrise."

"I want to cancel the manifest right now!" Xu Wenbin's voice rose to a panicked pitch, ice rattling inside his glass. "I am logging onto the customs terminal to flag container forty-nine for inspection! If Coast Guard cutters are sweeping the channel, I need a record showing I stopped the cargo myself!"

Marcus Vance stood up in one swift motion. All diplomatic polish vanished from his face. "Sit down, Xu Wenbin. You are not touching that terminal."

"Get out of my way!" Xu Wenbin stepped back, eyes wide with sudden suspicion. "Why do you care if I hold one barge of dysprosium for forty-eight hours? Shengyuan Capital has delayed ore shipments before! You have never looked this tense over ore!"

Marcus Vance stared at him in cold silence. "Because container forty-nine does not only hold dysprosium, Director Xu Wenbin."

A cold chill shot down Xu Wenbin's spine. His cigarette burned close to the filter, scorching his skin, and he barely noticed. "What did you put under my customs seal? Answer me, Marcus Vance! What is inside that container?"

"Nothing you need to know to survive your retirement in Vancouver," Marcus Vance replied coldly. "Your signature is on the bonded bill of lading. If you flag that container now, your own anti-smuggling bureau will cut the lock, open the false bulkhead, and hang you before noon. Drink your whiskey, shut your mouth, and let my people handle the channel."

"You bastard!" Xu Wenbin's face turned blotchy with rage and terror. He jabbed his smoking hand at the foreigner's chest. "You told me it was strictly rare-earth oxides! You swore there was no military contraband on my piers! I took money for commercial quotas, not treason!"

"You took 80 million yuan over four years through a Zurich cutout," Marcus Vance mocked. "In this country, Director Xu Wenbin, the firing squad does not care what you thought was inside the crate."

Before Xu Wenbin could scream another word, the motorized sliding glass door facing the marina glided open without a sound. Not a hinge squeaked. Not an alarm chimed.

Xu Wenbin froze with his mouth open. Marcus Vance whipped his head toward the terrace, hand darting toward his inside lapel.

Six figures stepped silently into the room from the terrace and hallway at the same instant, sealing every exit. They wore featureless matte-black gear from head to toe, their blank visors reflecting only the dim lamps.

Marcus Vance recovered first. He halted his hand, then deliberately drew a dark blue diplomatic passport stamped with a gold crest and held it high.

"Hold right there!" Marcus Vance barked in crisp, accented Mandarin, snapping back into arrogant consular authority. "I am Marcus Vance, First Commercial Liaison attached to the Consulate General! I carry full diplomatic accreditation under the Vienna Convention! You have entered a private residence hosting an accredited diplomat without consular notification or Foreign Ministry clearance! Stand down immediately, or you will trigger an international incident that will be on your Foreign Minister's desk before breakfast!"

Xu Wenbin stared desperately at the gold-crested passport, clinging to the wild hope that diplomatic cover might force these masked figures to hesitate. His chest heaved as he clutched his whiskey glass against his stomach.

None of the six matte-black figures glanced at the passport or shifted their stance.

The figure before the terrace glass angled its blank visor toward Marcus Vance. When it spoke, the voice was flat, neutral, and completely genderless, carrying an even chill that froze the room.

"Diplomatic immunity does not cover stolen naval radar schematics."

The crystal whiskey glass slipped straight out of Xu Wenbin's numb fingers.

It hit the limestone floor and shattered with an explosive crack, spraying amber liquor and jagged shards across his shoes. Xu Wenbin did not flinch. He stared at Marcus Vance with eyes wide and hollow with suffocating horror.

"Naval radar," Xu Wenbin whispered, his voice shaking so violently the words barely held together. "You put military radar schematics inside my Pier Four container."

Marcus Vance's arrogant mask collapsed instantly. Blood drained from his face, leaving his skin grey around his tightened jaw. His hand holding the diplomatic passport lowered two inches, trembling visibly for the first time.

"That is a fabrication," Marcus Vance snapped, though all command had bled out of his voice. "I demand immediate access to my consulate! I demand a lawyer!"

"Pier Four barge hull number 11 was boarded and secured twelve minutes ago," the flat, neutral, genderless voice stated evenly. "The false bulkhead inside container forty-nine is open. Your biometric seal and Xu Wenbin's digital customs key are both logged on the casing."

Xu Wenbin's knees buckled. He sank onto the limestone floor among the broken glass, ignoring the sharp shards pressing against his trousers, burying his face in shaking hands as a ragged sob tore from his throat.

"Secure Marcus Vance and Xu Wenbin," the voice ordered. "Seize the customs terminal and all communication devices. Transfer both targets to central holding."

Four matte-black operatives stepped forward in silence. Marcus Vance twisted his shoulder away, shouting furiously in English about consular treaties, before a single swift grip locked both wrists behind his back and cut him short. Beside him, Xu Wenbin offered no resistance, staring blankly at the dark water outside as they lifted him from the shattered glass and marched him into the night.
