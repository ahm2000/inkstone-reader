# Chapter 66: Lanshui Provincial Committee: The Empty Seat

At seven forty-five on Monday morning, the closed-door meeting room of the Lanshui Provincial Party Committee smelled of bitter tea and cigarette smoke. Eleven Standing Committee members sat along the dark table, their folders still shut, as thick blue smoke drifted toward the ceiling vents.

Right to the left of Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai, one leather chair sat glaringly empty.

Nobody sat in it. Every pair of eyes slid toward that empty seat and snapped away as if the leather were hot iron.

Executive Vice Governor Cao Wenbin cleared his throat, tapping his fingers twice on his teacup. "Secretary Zhao Dehai, should we wait five minutes for Vice Governor Lin Zhengde? Morning traffic on the ring road was backed up past the bridge."

Zhao Dehai tapped a cigarette out of a plain white pack, lit it with a sharp metal click, and sat straight up. Twenty years of provincial politics had not bent his spine.

"Vice Governor Lin Zhengde is not stuck in traffic, Cao Wenbin," Zhao Dehai said calmly, blowing out thin gray smoke. "At one fifteen this morning, a central discipline team from Beijing escorted Lin Zhengde out of his home. His office was sealed at two o'clock. His deputy secretary and driver were arrested twenty minutes later."

The room went dead quiet.

Across the table, Provincial Propaganda Minister Tang Guohua's teacup clattered hard against its saucer, spilling hot tea over his knuckles. He did not even wipe his hand as his cheeks turned white as ash.

Provincial Organization Minister Xu Jianmin froze with his cigarette halfway to the ashtray. Gray ash dropped straight onto his dark trousers. "Secretary Zhao Dehai, are you sure? Lin Zhengde ran the port meeting on Friday afternoon. There was no early check, not even a whisper from the General Office."

"When Beijing skips the provincial General Office," Provincial CCDI Secretary Shen Wenchao spoke from Zhao Dehai's right, his voice flat and cold, "it means Beijing does not trust the provincial General Office to keep its mouth shut."

Deputy Party Secretary Lu Baoguo shifted hard in his chair, sweat beading along his hairline. "Hold on. Let us not panic before we know how big this is. Lin Zhengde handled foreign trade and coastal building. If he took bribes on a port deal, that is his own mess. Why call an emergency closed-door meeting before eight?"

Zhao Dehai broke the red wax seal on the folder in front of him and laid two heavy sheets flat on the wood.

"Because this arrived by special courier at six forty," Zhao Dehai said. His eyes swept the table until half the men looked down. "It is a joint central order signed by Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi. Listen closely, because I will only read it once."

Tang Guohua swallowed hard, his throat bobbing above his tie.

Zhao Dehai's voice hit like iron. "Starting right now, Lanshui Province is under a full central check. A joint central inspection team has already landed at Lanshui Airport. Every port customs record, every provincial building bond, every county police file flagged for an unfair arrest, and every shell shipping account tied to Qingyan Port and Yunxi County over the past five years is frozen for direct central review."

An even heavier silence crushed the room. Half the table went pale.

Cao Wenbin's jaw tightened until a muscle jumped under his ear. "Yunxi County?" he repeated, his voice dropping low. "Why is a farming county like Yunxi listed next to Qingyan Port? Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi do not sign a joint order over a county magistrate."

"They do when Yunxi County's Pier Four and Qingyan Port's Pier Seven are two mouths on the same smuggling throat," Shen Wenchao cut in sharply, sliding a money chart onto the table. "And when Chairman Deng Yongchang pushed 4.8 billion yuan through twenty-seven shell companies set up between Yunxi County and Lanshui's trade zone. Three of those tax breaks carry Vice Governor Lin Zhengde's own stamp."

Provincial Politics and Law Secretary Zhou Mingyuan leaned forward, his knuckles white against the edge of the table. "Wait. Who in Yunxi County was stupid enough to draw Beijing's eyes with an unfair arrest?"

"Yunxi County Public Security Bureau Director Cheng Biao," Shen Wenchao replied without blinking. "Working with gang leader Yan Tiehu and County Magistrate Sun Weiming, Cheng Biao made up a serious assault case against an ordinary Yunxi County hardware shopkeeper named Chen Mingde, using a fake hospital X-ray and a backdated arrest order."

Lu Baoguo let out a short, strained laugh that died in his throat. "Beijing locked down a whole province over a hardware shopkeeper in Yunxi County? Come on, Shen Wenchao, nobody here was born yesterday. Who is backing that shopkeeper?"

Zhao Dehai slammed his open palm flat onto the red folder. The loud crack echoed off the walls.

"Do not ask questions you have no business knowing, Lu Baoguo!" Zhao Dehai barked, his eyes blazing with cold fury. "Whether the spark was a hardware shopkeeper or a 5 billion yuan money pipeline, the fire is already inside this building! Look at that empty chair!"

Tang Guohua's breath caught. Desperate to calm his nerves, he jammed a cigarette between his lips and fumbled with his heavy brass lighter. His thumb slipped twice. On the third try, the lighter shot out of his sweaty fingers, hit the wooden table with a loud metallic bang, and spun three times before stopping in front of Cao Wenbin.

Nobody moved to pick it up.

Two seats down, Lu Baoguo tried to light his cigarette under the table edge to hide his shaking hands. His plastic lighter slipped onto the carpet. When he bent quickly to grab it, his forehead hit the table rim with a dull thud. He sat back up red-faced, his eyes darting wildly as sweat ran down his temple.

"Pick up your lighters," Zhao Dehai said softly, disgust in every word. "If your hands are clean, why are they shaking?"

"Secretary Zhao Dehai, I never signed anything for Deng Yongchang!" Tang Guohua blurted, his voice cracking as he snatched his lighter back. "Last fall Lin Zhengde invited me to a dinner where Deng Yongchang was there, and I left after twenty minutes! I never touched a single red envelope!"

"Nobody asked you about dinner, Tang Guohua," Shen Wenchao said smoothly, his eyes narrowing like a hawk. "Yet you remembered the exact time in two seconds flat. We will pass your sharp memory along to the central inspection team."

Tang Guohua sank back into his chair, staring blankly at his tea-stained sleeve.

Xu Jianmin put his cigarette out with shaking care. "Secretary Zhao Dehai, what are our orders right now?"

"First, every mobile phone stays on the security tray outside," Zhao Dehai said, standing up slowly as every man straightened right away. "Second, Shen Wenchao and Provincial PSB Deputy Chief Han Zhiyuan are leading a one-hundred-twenty-person joint task force into Yunxi County within the hour to arrest every dirty official and gang thug involved."

Zhou Mingyuan hesitated, his cigarette burning down toward the filter. "Should we tell Yunxi County Magistrate Sun Weiming to cooperate?"

"Sun Weiming is the second name on the arrest warrant," Zhao Dehai snapped, staring until Zhou Mingyuan flinched. "If even one phone call leaks to Yunxi County or Qingyan City before nine o'clock, I will hand the caller to CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi myself."

Zhao Dehai crushed his cigarette into the glass ashtray until the last spark died.

"Tell every bureau chief under you that the window to confess on their own is forty-eight hours," Zhao Dehai said coldly. "After that, Beijing does not ask for excuses. Beijing only counts the empty chairs. Meeting over. Shen Wenchao, launch your task force."
