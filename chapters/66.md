# Chapter 66: Lanshui Provincial Committee: The Empty Seat

At seven forty-five on Monday morning, the closed-door conference room of the Lanshui Provincial Party Committee smelled of bitter tea and burning tobacco. Eleven Standing Committee members sat along the dark table, folders unopened, thick blue cigarette smoke drifting toward the vents.

Directly to the left of Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai, one leather chair stood glaringly empty.

Nobody sat in it. Every pair of eyes slid toward that empty seat and snapped away as if the leather were hot iron.

Executive Vice Governor Cao Wenbin cleared his throat, fingers drumming twice against his teacup. "Secretary Zhao Dehai, should we wait five minutes for Vice Governor Lin Zhengde? The morning ring road was backed up past the bridge."

Zhao Dehai tapped a cigarette from a plain white pack, lit it with a crisp metallic snap, and sat upright, spine unbent by twenty years of provincial politics.

"Vice Governor Lin Zhengde is not stuck in traffic, Cao Wenbin," Zhao Dehai said calmly, exhaling thin gray smoke. "At one fifteen this morning, a central discipline inspection team from Beijing escorted Lin Zhengde out of his residence. His office was sealed at two o'clock. His deputy secretary and driver were detained twenty minutes later."

The room went dead quiet.

Across the table, Provincial Propaganda Minister Tang Guohua's teacup clattered sharply against its saucer, sloshing hot tea over his knuckles. He did not even wipe his hand as his cheeks turned ash-white.

Provincial Organization Minister Xu Jianmin froze with his cigarette halfway to the ashtray, ash dropping straight onto his dark trousers. "Secretary Zhao Dehai, are you certain? Lin Zhengde chaired the port coordination meeting on Friday afternoon. There was no preliminary inquiry, not even a whisper from the General Office."

"When Beijing bypasses the provincial General Office," Provincial CCDI Secretary Shen Wenchao spoke from Zhao Dehai's right, voice flat and cold, "it means Beijing does not trust the provincial General Office to keep its mouth shut."

Deputy Party Secretary Lu Baoguo shifted sharply, sweat beading along his hairline. "Hold on. Let us not panic before knowing the scope. Lin Zhengde oversaw foreign trade and coastal infrastructure. If he took kickbacks on a port contract, that is his personal failure. Why call an emergency closed-door session before eight?"

Zhao Dehai broke the crimson wax strip on the folder in front of him and laid two heavy sheets flat on the wood.

"Because this arrived by special courier at six forty," Zhao Dehai said. His gaze swept the table until half the men dropped their eyes. "It is a joint central directive signed by Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi. Listen carefully, because I will only read it once."

Tang Guohua swallowed hard, his Adam's apple bobbing above his tie.

Zhao Dehai's voice struck like iron. "Effective immediately, Lanshui Province is placed under comprehensive central audit. A joint central inspection group has already landed at Lanshui Airport. Every port customs ledger, every provincial infrastructure bond, every county public security file flagged for irregular detention, and every shell logistics account linked to Qingyan Port and Yunxi County over the past five years is frozen for direct central review."

A heavier silence crushed the room. Half the table turned ash-white.

Cao Wenbin's jaw tightened until a muscle jumped beneath his ear. "Yunxi County?" he repeated, voice dropping half an octave. "Why is an agricultural county like Yunxi listed beside Qingyan Port? Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi do not sign a joint directive over a county magistrate."

"They do when Yunxi County's Pier Four and Qingyan Port's Pier Seven are two mouths on the same smuggling throat," Shen Wenchao cut in sharply, sliding a financial chart onto the table. "And when Chairman Deng Yongchang routed 4.8 billion yuan through twenty-seven shell firms registered between Yunxi County and Lanshui's trade zone. Three of those tax exemptions carry Vice Governor Lin Zhengde's personal chop."

Provincial Politics and Law Secretary Zhou Mingyuan leaned forward, knuckles white against the table edge. "Wait. Who in Yunxi County was stupid enough to draw Beijing's eye with an irregular detention?"

"Yunxi County Public Security Bureau Director Cheng Biao," Shen Wenchao replied without blinking. "Working with gang figure Yan Tiehu and County Magistrate Sun Weiming, Cheng Biao fabricated an aggravated assault case against an ordinary Yunxi County hardware shopkeeper named Chen Mingde, using a forged hospital scan and a backdated warrant."

Lu Baoguo let out a short, strained laugh that died in his throat. "Beijing locked down an entire province over a hardware shopkeeper in Yunxi County? Come now, Shen Wenchao, nobody here is a rookie. Who is backing that shopkeeper?"

Zhao Dehai slammed his open palm flat onto the crimson folder. The crack echoed off the walls.

"Do not ask questions you have no business knowing, Lu Baoguo!" Zhao Dehai barked, eyes blazing with cold fury. "Whether the spark was a hardware shopkeeper or 5-billion-yuan pipeline, the fire is already inside this building! Look at that empty chair!"

Tang Guohua's breath hitched. Desperate to steady his nerves, he jammed a cigarette between his lips and fumbled with his heavy brass lighter. His thumb slipped twice. On the third try, the lighter shot from his sweat-slick fingers, struck the mahogany tabletop with a loud metallic bang, and spun three times before stopping in front of Cao Wenbin.

Nobody moved to pick it up.

Two seats down, Lu Baoguo tried to light his cigarette beneath the table edge to hide his trembling hands. His plastic lighter slipped onto the carpet. When he bent hastily to grab it, his forehead bumped the table rim with a dull thud. He straightened up red-faced, eyes darting wildly as sweat trickled down his temple.

"Pick up your lighters," Zhao Dehai said softly, disgust in every word. "If your hands are clean, why are they shaking?"

"Secretary Zhao Dehai, I never signed anything for Deng Yongchang!" Tang Guohua blurted, voice cracking as he snatched his lighter back. "Last autumn Lin Zhengde invited me to a dinner where Deng Yongchang was present, and I left after twenty minutes! I never touched a single red envelope!"

"Nobody asked you about dinner, Tang Guohua," Shen Wenchao said smoothly, eyes narrowing like a hawk. "Yet you remembered the exact duration in two seconds flat. We will note your memory for the central audit team."

Tang Guohua sank back into his chair, staring blindly at his tea-stained sleeve.

Xu Jianmin stubbed his cigarette out with shaking precision. "Secretary Zhao Dehai, what are our immediate orders?"

"First, all mobile phones stay on the security tray outside," Zhao Dehai said, rising slowly as every man straightened reflexively. "Second, Shen Wenchao and Provincial PSB Deputy Chief Han Zhiyuan are leading a one-hundred-twenty-person joint task force into Yunxi County within the hour to arrest every corrupt official and gang enforcer involved."

Zhou Mingyuan hesitated, his cigarette burning toward the filter. "Should we notify Yunxi County Magistrate Sun Weiming to cooperate?"

"Sun Weiming is the second name on the arrest warrant," Zhao Dehai snapped, staring until Zhou Mingyuan flinched. "If one phone call leaks toward Yunxi County or Qingyan City before nine o'clock, I will personally hand the caller to CCDI Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi."

Zhao Dehai crushed his cigarette into the glass ashtray until the last ember died.

"Tell every bureau chief under you that the window for voluntary confession is forty-eight hours," Zhao Dehai said coldly. "After that, Beijing does not ask for explanations. Beijing only counts the empty chairs. Meeting adjourned. Shen Wenchao, launch your task force."
