# Chapter 66: Sha Provincial Committee: The Empty Seat

On Monday morning, the closed-door hall of the Sha Provincial Party Committee smelled of bitter tea and thick cigarette smoke.

Around the dark table sat twelve core members of the thirteen-member Sha Provincial Standing Committee. Right to the left of Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai, the thirteenth leather chair sat glaringly empty. Nobody touched it. Every pair of eyes slid toward that empty seat and snapped away as if the leather were hot iron.

Behind the main table, along the outer ring, sat non-standing provincial bureau chiefs with open notebooks and stiff backs, taking notes in total silence without interrupting. At a side desk near the heavy doors, a senior clerk sat over a sealed archive terminal to record the formal provincial minutes.

Provincial Party Secretary Zhao Dehai tapped a cigarette from a plain white pack, lit it with a sharp metal click, and sat straight up. Twenty years of provincial politics had not bent his spine.

"Before anyone asks about the empty seat," Zhao Dehai said calmly, blowing thin gray smoke, "Vice Governor Lin Zhengde is not stuck in traffic. Before dawn, a central discipline team from Beijing escorted Lin Zhengde out of his home. His office is sealed, and his deputy secretary and driver were detained minutes later."

A cold chill swept the table. Provincial Propaganda Minister Tang Guohua's teacup clinked softly against its saucer as his hand shook. Beside him, Deputy Party Secretary Lu Baoguo's cigarette burned down toward the filter, long ash falling unnoticed onto his trousers. Neither man spoke a word.

"All mobile phones stay on the security tray outside," Zhao Dehai added evenly. "Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection Secretary Shen Wenchao, deliver the briefing report."

At the lower side of the table, Shen Wenchao stood crisply and opened a red-sealed folder. His voice was flat and cold as winter rain.

"A joint central order signed by Vice Premier Zhou Zhenhua and Central Commission for Discipline Inspection Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi arrived before sunrise," Shen Wenchao reported cleanly. "Starting right now, Sha Province is under a full central inspection. A joint central inspection team has already landed at Sha City Airport."

Every shoulder in the outer ring stiffened. The clerk's fingers clicked sharply over the keyboard.

"Every port customs record, provincial building bond, flagged county police file, and shell shipping account linking Shanghai Port's Pier Seven and Yunxi County's Pier Four over the past five years is frozen for direct central review," Shen Wenchao continued. "Preliminary checks show Yunxi County developer Deng Yongchang moved 4.8 billion yuan through twenty-seven shell companies between Yunxi County and Sha City's trade zone. Three of those tax breaks carry Vice Governor Lin Zhengde's own stamp."

Shen Wenchao turned the last sheet. "In Yunxi County, Old Street Station Director Cheng Biao, gang leader Yan Tiehu, Police Chief Liang Guodong, and County Magistrate Sun Weiming fabricated a serious assault case against hardware shopkeeper Chen Mingde using a fake hospital scan and a backdated arrest order. Beijing orders an immediate Yunxi County sweep and a province-wide audit. Report complete."

Shen Wenchao closed his folder and sat silently.

Heavy dread pressed down on the hall. Sweat beaded along Lu Baoguo's hairline. Provincial Organization Minister Xu Jianmin stared straight down at his closed notebook, jaw locked tight.

"You heard the order," Zhao Dehai said quietly, eyes sweeping the twelve seated members. "Senior standing leadership speaks now. Governor Cao Wenbin."

Provincial Governor Cao Wenbin set his pen down cleanly, a tight muscle jumping once along his jaw as he weighed a 4.8 billion yuan smuggling pipeline running right through his province.

"Beijing's order leaves zero room for doubt, Secretary Zhao Dehai," Cao Wenbin said steadily. "I support launching the Yunxi County sweep at once and locking down every port bond and trade account today so the central inspection team sees a clean ledger."

Zhao Dehai nodded once and looked down the table. "Politics and Law Secretary Zhou Mingyuan."

Zhou Mingyuan leaned forward with practiced calm. Behind his polite face hid sharp panic: County Magistrate Sun Weiming and Police Chief Liang Guodong had risen through his old county circle, and a sudden raid threatened to expose years of buried favors.

"Secretary Zhao Dehai, I respect Beijing's order," Zhou Mingyuan said smoothly. "Yet Yunxi County is a quiet farming county with fragile projects. If outside officers storm in without warning, local cadres will panic and businesses will freeze. Let us notify Magistrate Sun Weiming to cooperate first, and delay the province-wide audit for five days so county bureaus can prepare orderly reports."

Silence fell like a falling blade. Zhao Dehai stared at Zhou Mingyuan until the color drained from his cheeks.

"Sun Weiming is the second name on the arrest warrant, Zhou Mingyuan," Zhao Dehai said softly, voice hard as iron. "You want to phone a suspect and invite him to burn his files? Do not talk to me about five days of delay. If even one call leaks to Yunxi County or Shanghai before our officers cross the county line, I will hand the caller to Deputy Secretary Gao Zhengyi myself."

Zhou Mingyuan's throat bobbed. His palms pressed flat against the table to hide a tremor.

"Enough debate," Zhao Dehai declared evenly. "Two binding resolutions stand before us. First, Secretary Shen Wenchao and Provincial Public Security Deputy Chief Han Zhiyuan immediately lead a one-hundred-twenty-person joint task force into Yunxi County to arrest Sun Weiming, Liang Guodong, Deng Yongchang, Yan Tiehu, and Cheng Biao, and release Chen Mingde with a cleared name. Second, Sha Province opens a full province-wide audit today, with a forty-eight-hour window for self-confession."

Zhao Dehai sat straight. "We will hold a formal show-of-hands vote of the twelve present Standing Committee members. Clerk, prepare the sealed provincial minutes. All in favor, raise your hands."

Zhao Dehai, Governor Cao Wenbin, Secretary Shen Wenchao, and six other Standing Committee members raised their right hands cleanly. Nine hands stood raised in the smoky air.

Three seats stayed down. A freezing two-second pause gripped the table. Tang Guohua and Lu Baoguo exchanged one fleeting, terrified glance. Both carried quiet debts from Lin Zhengde's private dinners; both kept their palms flat on the wood, choosing hesitant abstentions.

"All against?" Zhao Dehai asked calmly.

In a strained voice, Zhou Mingyuan said, "For local stability, I still urge a brief delay." Stiffly, he raised his hand to vote against.

Zhao Dehai did not shout. He simply turned toward the side desk in a clear, chilling tone.

"Clerk," Zhao Dehai said calmly, "record every single name and exact vote in the sealed provincial minutes: nine in favor, two abstentions, one against. List Tang Guohua and Lu Baoguo as abstaining, and Zhou Mingyuan as voting against. Seal a copy immediately for Beijing and the central inspection team."

The sharp clatter of the clerk's keys echoed like iron nails. Zhou Mingyuan went white as paper. Across the table, Tang Guohua fumbled his heavy brass lighter; it dropped onto the polished wood with a loud metallic crack, spun twice, and stopped near Cao Wenbin's sleeve. Nobody picked it up.

"And log the first wave of provincial personnel measures under the central order," Zhao Dehai added coldly. "Clean grassroots officers who execute the Yunxi County sweep honestly receive immediate commendation and priority promotion. Any bureau chief or county supervisor who drags their feet or hides files has their rank frozen and promotions permanently withheld. And starting tomorrow, any faction-tainted official who cannot be trusted with real power moves upward into powerless honorary advisory posts in the Provincial Literary History Office and Retired Policy Research Council, stripping their real authority in one stroke."

An icy hush crushed the hall. Every hesitating member felt the blade: even if prison spared them, an empty honorary chair waited for anyone dragging their feet.

Zhao Dehai crushed his cigarette out until the last spark died.

"Forty-eight hours to come clean," Zhao Dehai said softly. "After that, Beijing does not ask for excuses. Beijing only counts the empty chairs. Session adjourned. Shen Wenchao, launch your task force into Yunxi County."
