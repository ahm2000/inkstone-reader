# Chapter 126: Vacationing at Shanshui Villa

The late-autumn wind in Jingzhou carried a crisp chill off the hills, shaking loose a steady drift of golden ginkgo leaves across the stone driveway of Shanshui Villa.

Half an hour earlier, the high-speed train from Shanghai had pulled smoothly into Jingzhou Station. While Qin Yuhan and Chen Xuan stepped out of Business Class Car 1 straight into a waiting black sedan sent by the forum's VIP reception, far back at the standard exit, Hu Dewei and Sun Haoran had hurried onto the crowded delegate shuttle bus, still grumbling about how Chen Xuan had vanished from Car 5 before the train even stopped.

By Friday afternoon, black sedans and shuttle buses lined the circular entrance of Shanshui Villa as delegates from Shanghai, Beijing, and every city across Handong Province arrived for the Handong Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum. Built around a wide artificial lake framed by crimson maples, weeping willows, and red-tiled pavilions connected by arched stone bridges, the resort looked more like a quiet painting than a business venue.

Inside the bright marble lobby, General Manager Sun Haoran of Hengxin Logistics stood at the check-in counter clutching his leather briefcase. His shoulders were still stiff from the four-hour train ride. Beside him, Department Manager Hu Dewei squinted at their plastic room keycards and muttered under his breath.

"Standard twin rooms in the East Wing," Hu Dewei grumbled, adjusting his tie. "We came all the way to Jingzhou to represent Hengxin Logistics, and they put us in the farthest building. And honestly, Manager Sun, where did Chen Xuan even run off to? He slept through half the train ride, disappeared before we reached the station, and did not even board the delegate shuttle with us. He is probably wandering around outside looking for cheap street snacks with that green thermos of his."

Sun Haoran sighed and rubbed his temples. "Just let him be for now, Dewei. He is a quiet clerk, not a troublemaker. Maybe he grabbed a local taxi to avoid the crowded bus."

Through the tall glass windows near the lobby entrance, Hu Dewei caught sight of a gleaming black VIP sedan gliding past the main drop-off and turning smoothly toward the private lakeside garden gate. A resort concierge in a tailored dark coat jogged forward to greet the passengers before the car disappeared behind a screen of tall maples.

"Look at that," Hu Dewei sighed enviously as they turned toward the East Wing elevators. "That must be one of the big keynote executives from Shanghai or Beijing. Private lakeside suites while we walk ten minutes down a hallway."

Just as the elevator doors slid shut behind them, Hu Dewei's phone buzzed with a casual text from Chen Xuan: *"Caught a ride over early and got settled in. Do not worry about my room key, I will catch up with you both later."*

Hu Dewei snorted and showed the screen to Sun Haoran. "Unbelievable. He must have a relative in Jingzhou or found some cheap guesthouse down the road so he does not have to pay for resort snacks. Fine by me. Less embarrassment for us."

Over at the quiet VIP lakeside reception pavilion, the cool November breeze smelled of pine needles and damp earth. Chen Xuan slipped his phone back into his charcoal windbreaker pocket, his green thermos tucked comfortably in his left hand while his right hand rolled a sleek silver suitcase.

Walking right beside him was Qin Yuhan. The President of Haiyue Group wore a tailored camel wool coat over a dark knit dress, her long hair pinned back neatly and her calm poise drawing respectful bows from the resort staff. As soon as the smiling concierge stepped ahead to guide them along the wooden boardwalk toward Three Willows Suite, Qin Yuhan linked her arm naturally through Chen Xuan's elbow and leaned in with a soft laugh.

"Did Manager Hu just ask where you vanished to?" she teased quietly.

"I told him I caught a ride and got settled," Chen Xuan grinned. "If I walked through the main lobby holding your arm, poor Manager Hu would drop his briefcase on his own foot. Better to let him enjoy his trip in peace."

As they strolled along the wooden walkway overlooking the shimmering lake, the resort felt relaxed and lively.

Near an arched stone bridge, a cheerful middle-aged groundskeeper uncle in a faded blue jacket paused from sweeping golden ginkgo leaves and grinned at Chen Xuan.

"Young man, you picked a great weekend to visit," the uncle called out warmly, leaning on his bamboo broom. "The lake mist looks best at dusk, though the air turns chilly fast after sunset. Make sure your wife keeps that coat buttoned up."

"Will do, Uncle," Chen Xuan laughed, tossing him a friendly wave. "Thanks for the tip."

Further along the boardwalk, a young bellhop in a knit cardigan rolled a luggage cart past them and winked like a cheeky younger brother. "Skip the fancy room service pastries later and ask the kitchen for fresh roasted chestnuts. They just brought in a sack from the hills this morning."

"Now that is useful advice," Chen Xuan said, nodding in approval. "I owe you one."

At the pavilion before Three Willows Suite, a kindly auntie in a thick wool vest was setting out ceramic pots of hot ginger tea. She smiled brightly and handed Chen Xuan two small cups. "Warm your hands, kids. The autumn wind off the water bites right through your sleeves."

Qin Yuhan took a grateful sip of the sweet ginger tea and laughed softly once the concierge showed them inside the quiet private courtyard of Three Willows Suite and took his leave.

"Honestly, Chen Xuan," she teased, setting her handbag on the rosewood table. "We have been at Shanshui Villa for ten minutes, and you are already chatting with half the staff like you grew up here."

Chen Xuan set his green thermos down beside the tea tray and stretched his arms. He glanced out the wide window at the peaceful lake, where the groundskeeper uncle, the bellhop, and the tea auntie blended effortlessly into the late-autumn scenery without a trace of stiffness.

"Friendly people recognize friendly people," Chen Xuan replied with a relaxed smile, slipping off his windbreaker. "Now come sit down, President Qin. Your husband is officially on vacation."
