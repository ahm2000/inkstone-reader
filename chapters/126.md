# Chapter 126: Arrival in Sha City

The express train slowed with a hiss of brakes and rolled into Sha City Railway Station.

Sun Haoran stood in the aisle before the wheels stopped, gripping his briefcase and suitcase, his face bright with nerves and pride.

"We are here, Chen Xuan!" Sun Haoran said, straightening his navy suit jacket for the fourth time. "Sha City! Keep your lanyard ready once we reach the Grand River Hotel. Every big freight chairman in the province is staying there."

"Take it easy, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said with a quiet smile, lifting his worn canvas bag and tucking his scratched green thermos into the side pocket. "The train doors are not running away."

They stepped onto the platform toward the taxi rank. Fifty meters behind them from Carriage Five, two quiet figures in plain dark coats blended into the crowd, matching Chen Xuan's pace to the second without looking directly at him.

Twenty minutes later, a taxi pulled under the glass canopy of the Grand River Hotel. Sun Haoran paid the driver, stepped out, and stopped in his tracks before the twenty six story tower, where three black sedans sat parked and uniformed doormen bowed at the brass doors.

"Good heavens," Sun Haoran muttered, squinting up at the gold letters. "Look at the marble on those pillars. Even the flower pots are taller than my desk in Shanghai."

"The forum committee booked the partner rate," Chen Xuan said calmly, his canvas bag over his shoulder. "We do not have to buy the marble. We just need a bed and hot water."

Inside the sandalwood scented lobby, men in tailored suits stood in clusters near the lounge sofas while waiters carried silver trays. Spotting a small menu on the lounge counter, Sun Haoran leaned in and nearly coughed.

"Eighty eight yuan for one cup of black coffee?" Sun Haoran whispered, grabbing Chen Xuan's sleeve. "And look at the sign by the desk. A standard suite costs 2,800 yuan a night! Our whole Shanghai warehouse drinks tea for three months on that money."

"Good thing we brought our own tea leaves," Chen Xuan said evenly.

At the front desk, a receptionist checked their invitation letters and handed over two key cards for Room 812 and two forum badges on blue ribbons.

"Welcome to Sha City, General Manager Sun and Mr. Chen," she said politely. "The welcome reception begins at six thirty in the Grand Ballroom on the third floor."

"Thank you very much," Sun Haoran replied, bowing slightly too low as he took the cards with both hands.

As they walked to the elevators, the two figures in plain dark coats stepped to the far end of the desk, spoke two quiet words to the clerk, and took a key card for Room 818 down the same eighth floor corridor, never crossing Sun Haoran's sight.

Inside Room 812, Sun Haoran dropped his suitcase and walked to the river window.

"We really made it, Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran said, rubbing his palms together. "Three months ago, Hengxin Logistics was scraping by and worrying about fuel bills. Today, we are at the Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum."

He hurried to the mirror, adjusted his red striped tie, smoothed his hair, and cleared his throat.

"Good evening, Chairman Song," Sun Haoran practiced with a stiff smile. "I am Sun Haoran of Hengxin Logistics from Shanghai. We have long admired Wanfeng Freight!" He frowned and shook his head. "Too stiff. Let me try again. Honorable Deputy Governor Wu, what a privilege to hear your vision! Wait. Should I shake hands with both hands, or wait for him first?"

Behind him, an electric kettle clicked. Sun Haoran turned and stared.

Chen Xuan had hung up his spare white shirt, rinsed his scratched green thermos with warm water, and dropped a spoonful of jasmine tea leaves inside.

"Chen Xuan!" Sun Haoran groaned, waving both hands. "We are meeting the wealthiest freight chairmen and provincial officials in two hours, and you are brewing tea like we are in the Shanghai break room!"

"You cannot shake hands properly on an empty stomach and a dry throat," Chen Xuan replied smoothly. When the kettle boiled, he filled his thermos and poured a share into a porcelain cup. "Drink this while it is hot. Your voice sounds like you swallowed gravel."

Sun Haoran took a sip. The warm jasmine tea eased the knot in his chest, and he slumped into the armchair.

"I know I am acting like a country cousin," Sun Haoran admitted with a sheepish laugh. "In Shanghai, I know every dock foreman by nickname. Here, everyone wears expensive suits. Song Baisheng's Wanfeng Freight moves 6,000,000,000 yuan in cargo every year. What if those chairmen look at our Hengxin Logistics badge and laugh us out of the room?"

Chen Xuan sipped from his dented thermos and sat across the small table. "General Manager Sun, how many of our Shanghai trucks missed a delivery schedule last month?"

"Not one," Sun Haoran answered at once, chin lifting. "Every shipment arrived on time and intact."

"How many unpaid driver wages or fake customs forms do we have on our books?"

"Zero! You know I keep our books cleaner than my kitchen floor."

"Then you have nothing to bow your head about," Chen Xuan said simply. "A shiny lobby only proves a hotel spent money on marble. It does not make the men inside it any taller than you. Walk in straight, speak plainly, eat a good dinner, and listen more than you talk."

Sun Haoran stared at his clerk for three seconds before grinning. "You know what, Chen Xuan? Sometimes I forget you are half my age. When you talk like that, even a 2,800 yuan room feels ordinary."

"It is ordinary," Chen Xuan said, glancing toward the door before looking at the river. "Just higher off the ground."

Down the hall in Room 818, two silent dark coated guards stood near the door, listening to the quiet corridor.

Sun Haoran checked his watch and stood up with fresh energy. "All right! Let me wash my face, iron my collar, and pack my cards. Six thirty is right around the corner."
