# Chapter 126: Arrival in Lanshui

The express train slowed with a long hiss of brakes and rolled smoothly into Lanshui Railway Station.

Sun Haoran was already standing in the aisle before the wheels stopped turning, gripping his brown leather briefcase in one hand and his rolling suitcase in the other. He craned his neck toward the window, his face bright with nerves and pride.

"We are here, Chen Xuan!" Sun Haoran said, straightening his navy suit jacket for the fourth time since the conductor announced the stop. "Lanshui! The provincial capital! Keep your lanyard ready once we get to the hotel. Every big freight chairman in the province will be staying at the Grand River Hotel."

"Take it easy, General Manager Sun," Chen Xuan said with a quiet smile, lifting his worn dark blue canvas bag from the overhead rack. He tucked his scratched green thermos neatly into the side pocket. "The train doors are not running away."

They stepped out onto the busy platform and joined the stream of travelers heading for the taxi rank. Fifty meters behind them, stepping down from Carriage Five, two quiet figures in plain dark coats blended easily into the crowd. Neither spoke, and neither looked directly at Chen Xuan, yet their pace matched his to the exact second as they moved toward the station exit.

Twenty minutes later, a yellow taxi pulled up beneath the wide glass canopy of the Grand River Hotel.

Sun Haoran paid the driver, stepped out onto the red stone driveway, and stopped dead in his tracks.

The hotel rose twenty-six stories into the pale afternoon sky, its windows shining like polished silver. Three black sedans with provincial plates sat parked near the curved entrance, and two doormen in crisp charcoal uniforms pulled the tall brass-framed doors open with polite bows.

"Good heavens," Sun Haoran muttered under his breath, squinting up at the gold letters above the canopy. "Look at the marble on those pillars. Even the flower pots are taller than my desk back in Qingyan."

"The forum committee booked the partner rate," Chen Xuan said calmly, stepping out of the taxi with his canvas bag slung over one shoulder. "We do not have to buy the marble. We just need a bed and hot water."

Inside the lobby, cool air scented with light sandalwood drifted across a floor so clean it reflected the hanging crystal lights overhead. Men in tailored gray and black suits stood in small clusters near the lounge sofas, speaking in low, confident voices while waiters carried silver trays of coffee.

Sun Haoran slowed his steps as they approached the long reception desk. Out of the corner of his eye, he spotted a small printed menu sitting on the lounge counter near the coffee bar. He leaned in for one second and nearly coughed.

"Eighty-eight yuan for a single cup of black coffee?" Sun Haoran whispered, grabbing Chen Xuan's sleeve. "And look at the display sign by the desk. A standard suite here costs 2,800 yuan a night! Our whole Qingyan warehouse drinks tea for three months on that money."

"Good thing we brought our own tea leaves," Chen Xuan said evenly.

At the front desk, a smiling receptionist checked their forum invitation letters and handed over two plastic key cards for Room 812, along with a pair of white forum badges on blue ribbons.

"Welcome to Lanshui, General Manager Sun and Mr. Chen," the receptionist said politely. "The evening welcome reception begins at six thirty in the Grand Ballroom on the third floor."

"Thank you, thank you very much," Sun Haoran replied, bowing slightly too low as he took the key cards with both hands.

As they walked toward the central elevators, two figures in plain dark coats stepped up to the far end of the same reception desk. One placed a plain payment card on the counter and spoke two flat, quiet words to the clerk. A minute later, they took a key card for Room 818 down the same eighth-floor corridor, never once crossing Sun Haoran's line of sight.

When the door to Room 812 clicked shut behind them, Sun Haoran let out a long breath, dropped his suitcase near the wardrobe, and walked straight to the wide window overlooking the river.

"We really made it, Chen Xuan," Sun Haoran said, rubbing his palms together. "Three months ago, Hengxin Logistics was scraping by on small coastal runs and worrying about fuel bills. Today, we are sitting in the Grand River Hotel with an official invitation to the Provincial Economic and Logistics Forum."

He spun around and hurried to the tall mirror beside the bathroom door. He loosened his red-striped tie, tightened it again, smoothed the thin hair at his temples, and cleared his throat loudly.

"Good evening, Chairman Song," Sun Haoran practiced at his reflection, stretching his mouth into a stiff, overly eager smile. "I am Sun Haoran of Hengxin Logistics from Qingyan. We have long admired Wanfeng Freight's scale and look forward to learning from your leadership!"

He frowned at himself and shook his head. "No, no, that sounds too stiff. Let me try again. Honorable Deputy Governor Wu, what a tremendous privilege to hear your vision for our coastal trade! Wait. Should I shake hands with both hands, or wait for him to offer his hand first?"

Behind him came the soft click of an electric kettle.

Sun Haoran turned around and stared.

Chen Xuan had already hung his spare white shirt neatly on a wooden hanger, placed his worn canvas bag on the luggage stand, and set his scratched green thermos beside the hotel's shiny white kettle. He rinsed the thermos thoroughly with warm water, pinched a measured spoonful of jasmine tea leaves from a small tin bag, and dropped them inside.

"Chen Xuan!" Sun Haoran groaned, waving both hands in the air. "We are going down to meet half the wealthiest freight chairmen and provincial officials in the region in two hours, and you are standing there brewing tea like we are sitting in the Qingyan break room!"

"You cannot shake hands properly on an empty stomach and a dry throat," Chen Xuan replied smoothly. When the kettle clicked off, he poured a steady stream of boiling water into his thermos, let the fragrant steam rise for a moment, and poured a small share into one of the hotel's porcelain cups.

He walked over and held the cup out. "Here. Drink this while it is hot. Your voice sounds like you swallowed gravel."

Sun Haoran hesitated, then took the cup and took a careful sip. The warm jasmine scent immediately eased the tight knot in his chest. He let out a quiet sigh and slumped into the armchair by the window.

"I know I am acting like a country cousin visiting the city for the first time," Sun Haoran admitted with a sheepish laugh. "Back in Qingyan, I know every dock foreman and truck driver by his nickname. Here, everyone wears suits that cost more than my old sedan. Song Baisheng's Wanfeng Freight moves 6 billion yuan in cargo every year. What if those big chairmen look at our little Hengxin Logistics badge and laugh us out of the room?"

Chen Xuan took a calm sip straight from his dented green thermos and pulled up the second chair across the small round table.

"General Manager Sun, how many of our Qingyan trucks missed a delivery schedule last month?" Chen Xuan asked mildly.

"Not one," Sun Haoran answered at once, his chin lifting slightly. "Every single shipment arrived on time and intact."

"How many unpaid driver wages or fake customs forms do we have on our books?"

"Zero! You know I keep our books cleaner than my kitchen floor."

"Then you have nothing to bow your head about," Chen Xuan said simply, setting his thermos on the glass tabletop. "A shiny lobby only proves a hotel spent a lot of money on marble. It does not make the men standing inside it any taller than you. Walk in straight, speak plainly, eat a good dinner, and listen more than you talk."

Sun Haoran stared at his quiet clerk for a full three seconds. Then a genuine, relaxed grin spread across his round face.

"You know what, Chen Xuan?" Sun Haoran said, taking another long sip of jasmine tea. "Sometimes I forget you are half my age. When you talk like that, even a 2,800-yuan room feels ordinary."

"It is ordinary," Chen Xuan said, glancing toward the quiet hallway door before looking back at the river outside. "Just higher off the ground."

Down the eighth-floor corridor, behind the closed door of Room 818, two silent dark-coated guards stood in stillness near the entrance, listening to the faint hum of the hotel hallway.

Sun Haoran checked his watch and stood up with fresh energy. "All right! Let me wash my face, iron the collar of my shirt, and pack my business cards. Six thirty is right around the corner. Let us go see what this big provincial forum is really all about."
