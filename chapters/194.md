# Chapter 194: Ten Seconds in the Alley

A narrow brick alley cut between an old print shop and a closed fabric store just off Nanjing Road, connecting the main street to the quieter back lane near the subway.

Rain poured steadily off rusted gutters, drumming against humming air-conditioning units and pooling across cracked cobblestones. No streetlights reached the middle of the lane. Only a faint orange glow from Nanjing Road spilled across the front entrance.

Kieran Cole reached the alley mouth first, glancing once back down the sidewalk. Chen Xuan was still thirty meters away, walking slowly under his black umbrella.

"Cut through the lane," Kieran Cole murmured into his earpiece, slipping inside the dark brick corridor. "Orlov, Novak, meet me six paces in. Alvarez, follow him off the street on my count. Five seconds before he hits the corner, we pull him inside."

Viktor Orlov and Tomasz Novak stepped quietly into the alley from the opposite end, black boots splashing through shallow puddles. Rico Alvarez peeled off the sidewalk a second later to link up with Cole, his folding knife already open inside his sleeve.

All four foreign killers stood together inside the rain-drenched lane.

Kieran Cole reached beneath his raincoat, his scarred fingers closing smoothly around his silenced pistol. He let out a low, confident chuckle.

"Easiest 8 million yuan we ever made," Kieran Cole muttered in English. "Get ready. Three seconds."

One second passed.

Then the steady hiss of rain at both ends of the alley changed pitch.

Kieran Cole's smile vanished. His combat instincts, sharpened across fifteen years of dirty jobs, screamed a sudden warning.

At the front mouth of the alley, blocking the orange glow from Nanjing Road, stood a tall figure dressed from head to toe in plain matte-black gear and a full black mask. Not a single badge, stripe, or number marked the dark fabric.

Viktor Orlov spun around with a sharp curse.

At the rear exit stood a second masked figure in the exact same plain matte-black gear, sealing the alley completely.

"Who the hell are you?" Kieran Cole snapped, yanking his silenced pistol clear of his raincoat. "Shoot! Drop them now!"

Neither masked figure answered. Neither made a sound.

The clock started.

Second one.

The first guard exploded forward across the wet stones faster than Cole could track. Before Cole's finger tightened on the trigger, a black-gloved left hand clamped down on the pistol slide, locking the gun dead while a sharp right chop struck the nerve inside Cole's wrist.

Cole's fingers sprang open involuntarily. The silenced pistol clattered into a puddle.

Second three.

Cole threw a desperate left hook with his scarred knuckles, only for the masked figure to duck half an inch under the punch, step inside his guard, and drive a brutal elbow into his ribs. Breath burst out of Cole's lungs in a wet gasp. A clean knee strike to the side of his thigh dropped the hardened mercenary flat onto his face in the rain.

At the exact same instant, four meters deeper in the lane, Viktor Orlov roared and lunged forward with a serrated combat knife aimed straight at the second guard's chest.

Second five.

The second guard did not retreat a single step. Moving with effortless balance on the slick stone, the Black Guard member sidestepped the blade by two inches, caught Orlov's thick forearm with both hands, and twisted sharply using the giant mercenary's own rushing weight.

A dull pop echoed beneath the rain as Orlov's shoulder wrenched out of line. The knife spun into the dark. Before Orlov could yell, the second guard whipped him sideways, slamming his heavy frame directly into Tomasz Novak just as Novak raised his silenced pistol.

Second seven.

Both men crashed hard against the wet brick wall. Novak's pistol fired a muffled cough into a metal drainpipe before the second guard stepped in, caught Novak's jaw with a sharp palm strike, and finished Orlov with a clean blow to the side of the neck. Both men slumped limp into the puddles.

Second eight.

Near the front wall, Rico Alvarez stared in sheer terror at his three fallen teammates. In less than eight seconds, three veteran killers had been dismantled without landing a single hit.

Alvarez dropped his knife and bolted blindly toward the alley mouth.

Second nine.

The first guard took two silent strides, hooked one black boot behind Alvarez's running ankle, and sent him skidding face-first across the wet stones. Before Alvarez could scramble up, a knee pinned his shoulders down while a black-gloved hand tapped the pressure point behind his ear.

Alvarez went completely limp.

Second ten.

Silence returned to the brick alley, broken only by steady autumn rain and distant traffic from Nanjing Road.

Outside on the main sidewalk, ten feet past the alley mouth, Chen Xuan walked calmly past under his black umbrella. In one hand he carried the double-wrapped bookstore bag holding Chen Hao's college-prep math books. Under his other arm rested his scratched green thermos.

He did not pause. He did not glance sideways into the dark lane. His footsteps stayed even and relaxed as he walked toward the warm lights of the subway entrance.

Inside the alley, the two Black Guard members moved with quiet precision. Without a single spoken word, they hauled all four unconscious foreign assassins out of the puddles and sat them neatly upright against the brick wall. Heavy black zip ties snapped tight around their wrists and ankles.

Beside Kieran Cole's knees, the first guard stacked four unloaded silenced pistols, three knives, four wireless earpieces, and four foreign passports in a neat row.

Two seconds later, both masked figures stepped into the dark rain at the rear of the alley and vanished completely.

Less than three minutes later, two unmarked black sedans pulled smoothly up to the curb outside the old print shop.

Six plainclothes officers from Shanghai State Security stepped out into the rain, led by Captain Zhou Ming, a stern-faced man in his forties wearing a dark trench coat.

"We just got an anonymous priority ping from downtown monitoring," Captain Zhou Ming said sharply, flicking on a bright flashlight as he led his men into the alley. "Four armed foreign nationals flagged off a Hong Kong flight three hours ago. Stay sharp!"

His flashlight beam swept across the wet brick wall. Captain Zhou Ming froze.

Sitting neatly in a row against the wall were four large foreign men, bound tight at the wrists and ankles, completely unconscious, without a single drop of blood spilled on the stones. Before them lay four unloaded silenced pistols, three knives, and four open passports arranged like a display counter.

A younger officer let out a stunned whistle. "Captain Zhou, look at these names! Kieran Cole, Viktor Orlov! Interpol has hunted them for six years! Who took down all four of them in a dark alley without firing a shot?"

Captain Zhou Ming crouched beside Kieran Cole, checking his steady pulse, then slowly stood and shone his light toward the empty rear exit where rain fell undisturbed.

He thought of the sudden arrests at Shanghai Port, the quiet sweeps in Sha City, and the rumor whispered only among senior directors in Beijing about silent shadows guarding the city.

"Bag the weapons and load them into the vans," Captain Zhou Ming said quietly, clicking off his flashlight as he looked back toward Nanjing Road. "And tell Beijing that whoever sent these four killers from Hong Kong just made the biggest mistake of their lives."
