# Chapter 194: Ten Seconds in the Alley

The footsteps closed in from both ends of the narrow brick alley. Rainwater trickled down old drainpipes and pooled across the uneven stones, catching the pale yellow glow of the single streetlamp.

Halfway down the lane, Chen Xuan stopped under his plain black umbrella. Ten paces ahead of him, Kieran Cole stood squarely in the center of the path, rain dripping off his dark jacket, with Dmitri Sokol holding overwatch a few paces behind him near the far exit. Behind Chen Xuan, Tomasz Novak and Rico Alvarez stepped smoothly into the alley mouth, sealing off the way back to Nanjing Road.

Kieran Cole studied the young man with cool detachment. The target did not run, did not shout, and did not even drop his paper bag of chestnuts.

"You are smart not to scream," Kieran Cole said in clipped English, taking a slow step forward. "Hand over your phone and come quietly. Make it easy, and it will be painless."

Behind Chen Xuan, Rico Alvarez slipped his hand inside his coat toward a carbon-fiber blade while Tomasz Novak cracked his knuckles and stepped forward to block any dash. At the far end behind Cole, Dmitri Sokol shifted sideways for a clean sightline, resting his hand under his jacket on his suppressed pistol.

Chen Xuan did not even shift his umbrella. He tilted his chestnuts away from the dripping eaves and sighed in casual Mandarin.

"It is a freezing December night," Chen Xuan remarked mildly. "Coming all this way just to catch a cold in a Shanghai alley seems like terrible work-life balance."

Cole frowned, sensing something off in his complete lack of fear. "Take him now."

Before Rico's blade cleared his coat or Sokol's thumb reached his safety switch, a cheerful voice rang out from the alley mouth behind Novak and Rico.

"Hey, young man, watch that puddle! Fresh winter radishes were half price at the corner market. Did you grab enough for your wife's bone soup tonight?"

A middle-aged uncle in a faded navy raincoat shuffled into the narrow lane, carrying a worn black umbrella and a bulging plastic bag of white radishes. He looked completely unhurried, like any neighbor heading home before the rain worsened.

At the exact same second, a squeaky electric scooter coasted into the far end of the alley right behind Dmitri Sokol. Its rider wore a bright yellow rain poncho and a scuffed helmet with the visor flipped up, grinning as he tapped his horn twice.

"Beep-beep! Hot noodle delivery coming through, brothers, make some room!"

Cole snapped his head toward the scooter, while Tomasz Novak barked a curse and reached out a massive hand to shove the uncle back onto the street.

Everything that happened next took ten seconds flat.

Uncle Lao Zhou did not tense up or drop his grocery bag. As Novak's thick arm swung toward him, the uncle stepped neatly aside as if dodging a splash. His forearm tapped the inside of Novak's elbow with effortless precision, numbing the huge man's entire right arm. Before Novak could gasp, the uncle's shoulder nudged his ribs, and the heavy breacher folded straight onto his knees in a puddle, eyes rolling back.

Rico Alvarez whipped his carbon-fiber knife forward in a fast slash. Uncle Lao Zhou tilted his worn umbrella, caught the flat of the blade on the metal tip, and flicked his wrist. The knife spun into a gutter. A split second later, the heel of the uncle's palm tapped Rico beneath the sternum, emptying his lungs and dropping him flat on the wet bricks.

At the far end of the lane, Dmitri Sokol drew his suppressed pistol and swung it toward the yellow poncho. Xiao Lin braked beside Sokol's hip, stepped off smoothly, and slapped Sokol's wrist upward before the barrel leveled. In one fluid motion, the rider twisted the pistol away, tossed it into his delivery box, and swept Sokol's feet out from under him. Xiao Lin caught his collar and tapped his neck, putting him cleanly to sleep.

Kieran Cole realized he had walked into a nightmare. Abandoning his team, he lunged forward at Chen Xuan with a sharp throat strike.

He never got within three steps.

Uncle Lao Zhou glided smoothly past Chen Xuan to stand between them, his bag of radishes still swinging lightly in his left hand. His right hand caught Cole's wrist as easily as steadying a tripping child. A soft pop echoed as the uncle rolled Cole's shoulder out of alignment, spun him cleanly, and pressed him face-down onto the wet stone.

Ten seconds. Not a single shot fired, not a loud shout, and not a drop of mud on Chen Xuan's trousers.

Xiao Lin whistled softly, pulling nylon delivery twine from his scooter basket, and bound all four unconscious mercenaries' wrists and ankles in neat loops.

"Man, these foreign fellows picked a lousy night for overtime," Xiao Lin grinned up at Chen Xuan as he tied off Cole's boots. "Your chestnuts still warm? My girlfriend swears by that stall."

"Still piping hot," Chen Xuan smiled easily. "Thanks for saving me the trouble of setting down my umbrella, Xiao Lin."

Uncle Lao Zhou adjusted his faded raincoat, smiling warmly like a real neighborhood elder. "Head on home, kid. The December wind is biting, and your wife is waiting for dinner. We will leave a quiet call for the local bureau to sweep up this mess."

"Appreciate it, Uncle Zhou," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Tell Auntie I will drop off some winter tea next weekend."

"You just focus on cooking well for your wife," Uncle Lao Zhou laughed, waving him off.

Chen Xuan chuckled, shifted his dry canvas tote higher on his shoulder, and strolled out toward the subway. Neither his cream cashmere scarf nor his sesame rice cakes had a single smudge.

Five minutes later, two unmarked black sedans pulled silently to the alley mouth. Captain Zhou Ming of Shanghai State Security stepped out under his umbrella and froze dead in his tracks.

At his feet lay four internationally wanted ex-mercenaries, neatly trussed with yellow delivery twine. Beside them sat a carbon-fiber knife and a suppressed pistol resting on dry cardboard. Tucked inside Kieran Cole's collar was a plain delivery slip noting a Shenzhen charter flight and a Macau escrow code.

"Who took them down?" a junior officer whispered, staring down the empty wet lane. "Not one witness, and the street cameras only caught rain glare."

Captain Zhou Ming stared at the effortless knots on Cole's wrists, a chill running down his spine.

"Leave the speculation for the debrief," Zhou Ming said quietly. "Bag the weapons, secure all four prisoners, and trace that Shenzhen charter back to Hong Kong immediately. Someone just handed us Seven-Harbor's neck on a silver platter."
