# Chapter 200: 300 Tons of Gold

Forty minutes later, the silence of the snowy Changbai valley was broken by the low rumble of heavy diesel engines. Two yellow snowplows led a long column of dark armored trucks and heated SUVs up the narrow mountain road, their bright headlights cutting cleanly through the drifting December snow.

Inside the lead SUV sat two senior directors from State Security alongside the four presidents of China's Four National Banks: Fang Zhengming of China Construction Bank, Han Guoliang of ICBC, Liang Qingshan of Huaxia Bank, and Tang Wenhao of Bank of China. All four banking leaders had flown north on short notice after receiving an urgent encrypted dispatch from Beijing telling them to prepare for an emergency treasury recovery.

When the convoy reached the concrete apron outside the old mining tunnel, armed State Security officers fanned out immediately. They expected a standoff, barricades, or at least locked blast doors.

Instead, the massive steel door stood wide open. Outside on the snow-free concrete, six steel crates of demolition charges sat neatly stacked beside their removed brass primer caps, rendered completely harmless. Inside the warm outer holding bay, Kou Zhenshan, Viktor Orlov, and sixteen foreign mercenaries sat bound in tidy rows against the wall, conscious, with no serious injuries, and waiting silently for formal arrest.

"Every single one of them is here," the lead State Security officer murmured in astonishment as his team confirmed Kou Zhenshan's identity and began reading the arrest warrants. "Not a single shot fired."

Fang Zhengming and the other three bank presidents stepped past the holding bay and walked down the central aisle into the main vault chamber. Halfway through the doorway, all four men stopped dead in their tracks.

Under the bright overhead fluorescent lights, heavy steel racks stretched deep into the carved mountain rock. Row upon row of stamped gold bullion gleamed cleanly across every shelf. The sheer weight and scale of the treasure made the warm room feel suddenly breathless.

"Three hundred tons," Han Guoliang breathed, stepping up to the nearest rack and touching the cold, solid edge of a stamped bar. "I have worked in national finance for thirty-five years, and I have never seen this much smuggled physical gold recovered intact in a single night."

Liang Qingshan let out a long, shaky exhale. "At current market value, this is well over 150 billion yuan in pure physical reserves. Every gram was siphoned out of domestic credit lines and regional infrastructure funds by Seven-Harbor's front companies."

"Look at the desk," Tang Wenhao called out quietly.

The four bank presidents gathered around the heavy steel table in the center of the chamber, joined by the lead State Security director. Sitting neatly in the middle were twelve sealed encrypted hard drives, a thick stack of cloth-bound ledgers detailing a decade of illegal transfers, and a single folded sheet of plain white paper weighted down by a small gold bar.

The State Security director put on white cotton gloves, lifted the plain sheet, and read the brief handwritten memo aloud.

"300 tons of physical gold, full Seven-Harbor ledgers, and offline drives accounted for. Return to the national treasury to settle the remaining regional bank shortfalls and stabilize rural credit. Kou Zhenshan and Viktor Orlov are in the outer bay. Everything else is yours to handle by law."

There was no signature at the bottom. No name, no title, and no phone number.

For a long moment, nobody in the bright underground hall spoke. The four bank presidents exchanged deeply moved, silent glances. They all knew who had made tonight possible, even though none of them knew his face or his name.

"Not even a signature," Fang Zhengming said softly, his voice filled with quiet respect. "Earlier this season, the emergency reserve plugged the 5 trillion yuan hole left by frozen loans and market panic while holding back the overseas short-sellers. Now these 300 tons of physical gold and the original ledgers close the final chapter on Seven-Harbor for good."

"Tell Beijing the recovery is complete," the State Security director instructed his deputy, already motioning for the armored transport crews and treasury auditors to begin cataloging the racks. "Seal the perimeter, transfer Kou Zhenshan and Orlov under maximum escort, and bring the gold home."

Within minutes, teams of treasury auditors in heavy coats filed into the chamber with handheld scanners and numbered seal tags. Han Guoliang and Tang Wenhao stepped aside to watch the first steel cart roll down the central aisle, while Fang Zhengming carefully placed the unsigned memo note inside a clear evidence sleeve for State Security to archive. Outside in the snow, the armored trucks reversed into loading position under the watchful eyes of armed guards. By sunrise, every single bar of gold would be locked inside a national bullion convoy heading straight for the central vaults.

High above the snow-covered ridges of Changbai Mountain, far beyond the glow of the convoy's headlights, the sleek white jet climbed smoothly through the thin winter clouds and banked south toward Shanghai.

Inside the quiet cabin, the temperature was warm and comfortable. Chen Xuan leaned back in his leather seat, unscrewed the lid of his green thermos, and poured himself a fresh cup of hot jasmine tea.

His phone buzzed softly on the small folding table beside him over the cabin link. He picked it up and saw a reply from Qin Yuhan.

"Safe flight back tomorrow. Grandpa asked if you can play two games of chess with him on Sunday, and Mom said she misses your braised beef brisket."

Chen Xuan smiled, set his phone back down on the folding table, and took a relaxed sip of tea as pale moonlight brushed the clouds outside the cabin window. He would be back in Pudong well before dawn, with plenty of time to pick out fresh ingredients at the neighborhood market for tomorrow evening's lamb hot pot, followed by Sunday chess with Grandpa before returning to his corner desk on Monday.
