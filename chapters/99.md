# Chapter 99: Wang Lei Gets a Call

By ten-thirty Tuesday morning, the sun over Yunxi County's wholesale market made sweat bead on Wang Lei's forehead.

He squatted beside the curb with a rusty wrench and a black rubber patch, his battered blue delivery tricycle tilted on two red bricks. The rear tire had picked up a bent nail outside the grain warehouse twenty minutes earlier, right in the middle of a thirty-yuan run.

Wang Lei roughed up the rubber with sandpaper, smeared glue over the puncture, and blew on it while his knees ached.

"Still nursing that old tube, Wang Lei?" called Old Ma, the gray-haired owner of the tiny tire stall beside him. "That rear tire has six patches already. One more nail and it will look like a quilt."

Wang Lei let out a dry chuckle without looking up. "As long as it holds air until month's end, Old Ma, I am not complaining. A new tire costs ninety yuan. That is three trips across town."

"Rough season," Old Ma sighed. "Good thing those thugs from Hongrui Real Estate got swept out yesterday. Maybe the market will pick up."

"Hope so," Wang Lei murmured, pressing the patch firmly onto the tube.

Life had been hard for two years. His father's hospital bills had drained his savings, and when Hongrui Real Estate's crew muscled into the county freight yards, honest drivers like Wang Lei were left with scraps. He woke at five every morning, hauled flour sacks and steel pipe on his rattling tricycle, and counted every ten-yuan bill twice.

Just as he reached for the hand pump, his cracked phone buzzed in his shirt pocket.

Worried it was the grain shop owner yelling about delayed sacks, Wang Lei wiped his greasy fingers on his pants and squinted at a Shanghai landline number.

He answered. "Hello? Wang Lei speaking."

"Good morning, Mr. Wang Lei," a polite, steady voice said. "My name is Lin Jianhua. I am the regional manager for Ancheng Express Freight in Shanghai. Am I catching you busy?"

Wang Lei blinked at the flat tube in his lap. "Ancheng Express? Sorry, friend, wrong number. I do not ship big freight. I just run a three-wheel tricycle around Yunxi County."

"No mistake, Mr. Wang Lei," Lin Jianhua replied warmly. "We are opening a direct county-to-city delivery route between Yunxi County and Shanghai this week. Our office asked three wholesale shopkeepers in Yunxi County to recommend reliable local drivers with clean records and zero damaged goods. Your name came up on all three lists."

Wang Lei froze two seconds, then let out a short, skeptical laugh as he stood up beside his tilted tricycle.

"Look, buddy," Wang Lei said bluntly, "if this is one of those scams where you promise a big truck and then ask me to wire ten thousand yuan for a training deposit, save your breath. I do not even have two thousand yuan on my bank card."

Lin Jianhua chuckled softly without offense. "I understand why you are careful, Mr. Wang Lei. Let me be clear. Ancheng Express charges zero deposit, zero agency fee, and zero vehicle down payment. You do not pay us a single yuan."

Wang Lei paused, rubbing his thumb over the cracked phone edge. "Hold on. Who put you up to this? Is Old Liu from the fruit stall playing a prank on me?"

"Nobody is playing a prank, Mr. Wang Lei," Lin Jianhua said seriously. "Our Yunxi County branch opened yesterday on Central Avenue, Building 14, across from the Agricultural Bank. Walk over yourself and check our business license on the front wall."

Wang Lei glanced toward Central Avenue. He had ridden past Building 14 yesterday and seen workers hanging a clean dark blue sign for Ancheng Express Freight, a real provincial company with clean white vans.

His heart beat faster. "Wait. What kind of work are you talking about?"

"A steady county-to-city delivery contract," Lin Jianhua explained. "You drive one of our company-owned white box vans on a daily loop between the Yunxi County wholesale market and our Shanghai hub. We cover fuel, highway tolls, insurance, and repairs. Base pay is eight thousand five hundred yuan a month, paid to your bank card on the fifth, plus a bonus for every completed round trip and one full day off each week."

Wang Lei stared at his patched inner tube in the dust. His throat went dry.

Eight thousand five hundred yuan a month. An enclosed van with air conditioning and a roof in the rain. Fuel covered. Fair pay on the fifth without begging for scraps.

"Manager Lin Jianhua," Wang Lei said, voice low and rough. "Are you sure you have the right Wang Lei? I only have a C1 license. I have not driven a box van since I left the furniture factory three years ago."

"A C1 license is all our light box vans need," Lin Jianhua replied easily. "We checked your record through the regular transport list. Seven years on the road, zero drunk-driving marks, zero major accidents. Honest drivers who look after cargo are hard to find, Mr. Wang Lei."

Wang Lei had no idea that Ancheng Express Freight's county expansion had been quietly set up through Number 11's normal business network two days earlier, with one quiet rule to hire honest local drivers on fair terms and never mention a name.

All Wang Lei knew was that for the first time in three long years, someone spoke to him with plain, genuine respect.

"Mr. Wang Lei?" Lin Jianhua asked gently after a pause. "Are you still there?"

Wang Lei quickly wiped his wrist across his eyes before Old Ma saw them turning red, taking a deep breath.

"I am here, Manager Lin Jianhua," Wang Lei said, voice catching before turning firm. "Yes. I am here."

"Wonderful," Lin Jianhua said warmly. "If you are free at two o'clock this afternoon, bring your ID card and C1 license to Building 14 on Central Avenue. We will read the standard contract line by line. If you are happy with every term, you get the keys to your van tomorrow morning."

"I will be there at one-fifty," Wang Lei promised, nodding hard. "Thank you, Manager Lin Jianhua. Really, thank you."

"See you at two o'clock, Mr. Wang Lei. Have a safe morning."

The call ended. Wang Lei stood beside the curb, gripping his cracked phone in his grease-stained hand.

"Hey, Wang Lei!" Old Ma called curiously. "You look like you won the lottery. Who called?"

Wang Lei looked at his battered tricycle, then grinned wider than he had in years.

"Not the lottery, Old Ma," Wang Lei said, his chest feeling lighter than air as he picked up his wrench. "Just a real job. Here, take my last pack of cigarettes. After I drop off these flour sacks, I am going home to wash up and put on a clean shirt!"
