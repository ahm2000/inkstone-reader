# Chapter 99: A Deal Over Green Tea

Before Qin Yuhan could pick up the second walnut cookie, a heavy shadow fell across their marble table.

"Yuhan! What a surprise running into you here!"

A man in his late twenties wearing a shiny navy suit and far too much cologne walked over, tilting his wrist deliberately so his gold watch caught the noon light. It was Zhou Mingcheng, the Lujiazui fund manager whom Lin Shuyin had been praising on the phone all week.

Qin Yuhan's warm smile vanished at once. "Mr. Zhou. I told my mother clearly that I had a personal lunch appointment today."

Zhou Mingcheng barely listened. His eyes swept over Chen Xuan's 20-yuan white shirt and landed on the dented green metal thermos resting beside the sugar bowl. A mocking smirk curled his lips.

"So this is your personal lunch appointment?" Zhou Mingcheng chuckled loudly enough for neighboring tables to glance over. "Yuhan, Auntie Lin Shuyin told me this morning that she booked us a private room at IFC Mall tonight. You do not need to waste your noon break sitting with some delivery clerk who brings a battered tin can into Cloud Corner Café."

He stepped closer, resting one manicured hand on the back of Chen Xuan's chair as if claiming the table. "Listen, pal. I manage private portfolios with a 500k minimum entry, and my Porsche is parked downstairs. A cup of coffee here probably costs half your daily wages. Be smart, take your thermos, and find a bench outside."

Chen Xuan did not frown or raise his voice. He calmly picked up his teacup, took a slow sip of hot green tea, and looked up at Zhou Mingcheng with polite curiosity.

"Mr. Zhou, right?" Chen Xuan asked mildly. "Do you always spray half a bottle of cologne before lunch? It is making my walnut cookies taste like cheap department store perfume."

A woman at the next table muffled a laugh behind her menu.

Zhou Mingcheng's face darkened instantly. "Who the hell do you think you are talking to?"

"Just a regular office worker enjoying his tea," Chen Xuan replied smoothly, setting his cup down. "Though if I may offer a small tip, people who are truly comfortable with their bank balance rarely feel the need to wave their wrist watch in a stranger's face."

"You!" Zhou Mingcheng raised a finger, his polished smile cracking completely.

Before he could shout another word, Qin Yuhan stood up smoothly, her expression cool and decisive. She stepped right beside Chen Xuan's chair and looked straight into Zhou Mingcheng's eyes.

"That is quite enough, Mr. Zhou," Qin Yuhan said, her voice calm yet carrying the unmistakable authority of a company president. "Please remove your hand from my fiancé's chair."

The café around Table Six went remarkably quiet. Zhou Mingcheng froze, his jaw dropping slightly. "Your what? Yuhan, do not joke around! Auntie Lin Shuyin would never approve of some nobody in a 20-yuan shirt!"

"My marriage is my own decision, not a business deal for my mother," Qin Yuhan replied evenly. "Chen Xuan and I are registering our marriage at the Pudong Civil Affairs Bureau this afternoon. Please tell my mother whatever you like, and kindly stop interrupting our lunch."

Zhou Mingcheng stared from Qin Yuhan's resolute face to Chen Xuan's completely unbothered smile. Realizing he was only making a fool of himself in front of half of Lujiazui's lunch crowd, he yanked his hand off the chair with a stiff scoff.

"Fine! Go ahead and throw your future away on a broke clerk!" Zhou Mingcheng hissed. "We will see how long you last when Auntie Lin finds out!"

He spun around and stalked out of the café, the glass door swinging shut behind him.

Qin Yuhan sat back down and let out a long, quiet breath, her cheeks faintly pink. "Mr. Chen, I am truly sorry for dragging you into that. I lost my patience with him."

"No need to apologize," Chen Xuan said, refilling her empty teacup with fresh green tea. "Honestly, the free comedy show went quite well with the cookies."

Qin Yuhan looked at him, a soft, genuine laugh escaping her lips. Then her gaze turned serious again. "What I asked you before he walked over was not a joke, Chen Xuan. And what I just told him does not have to be a lie, if you are willing."

She laid out her terms clearly and respectfully over the steaming green tea.

"We register legally as husband and wife so my mother stops forcing arrogant men onto me and my grandfather in Beijing can finally relax," Qin Yuhan said softly. "You move into my villa in Pudong. We have separate bedrooms and complete mutual respect. You keep your day job, your 4:55 PM clock-out, and your own schedule. All I ask is honesty, kindness, and your company when I visit my family."

Chen Xuan looked across the marble table at this bright, composed woman who carried an entire corporate group on her shoulders yet smiled with pure relief over a bakery walnut cookie. She was kind, proud without being snobbish, and genuinely tired of being alone. Besides, his mother had been begging him to bring home a daughter-in-law for two years.

He lifted his green teacup with a warm, lazy grin.

"Deal, Miss Qin," Chen Xuan said easily. "On one condition."

Qin Yuhan raised an eyebrow. "Name it."

"I take over the kitchen at home," Chen Xuan told her seriously. "You look like someone who survives on cold salads and black coffee. If I am going to be your husband, you are eating proper hot dinners."

Qin Yuhan blinked, then broke into the brightest smile she had worn in months. She lifted her porcelain cup and tapped it lightly against his.

"Deal, Chen Xuan."

Ten minutes later, Chen Xuan sent a quick afternoon leave note to Pan Xiulan so she could laugh at Hu Dewei's expense, picked up his household registration booklet from his small flat, and met Qin Yuhan outside the Pudong Civil Affairs Bureau under the golden autumn sky.
