# Chapter 193: Rain on Nanjing Road

By nine-thirty on Friday night, steady autumn rain fell across downtown Shanghai.

Along Nanjing Road, red and gold neon signs shimmered across the slick stone pavement. Shoppers hurried beneath bright umbrellas, and the cool wet air smelled faintly of rain and roasted sweet potatoes.

Half a block off the main street, inside a warm bookstore called East Wind Books, Chen Xuan stood quietly in the high school study aisle.

He wore his usual faded dark-blue windbreaker, plain gray trousers, and worn canvas sneakers. Tucked under his left arm was his scratched green metal thermos, still hot with jasmine tea. With his right hand, he flipped through a thick college-prep math workbook printed by a well-known Shanghai teaching group, nodded with quiet satisfaction, and added a Fudan University entrance exam mock set to go with it.

At the front counter, the cheerful cashier, Zhou Meifen, smiled as she scanned both books.

"Buying college-prep math on a rainy Friday night?" Zhou Meifen asked warmly. "For your younger brother?"

"Yes, Auntie," Chen Xuan smiled easily, tapping his phone to pay the eighty-six yuan. "He is in his last year of high school back in Yunxi County. He swore last weekend he is getting into Fudan University next autumn."

"What a good brother you are!" Zhou Meifen beamed, slipping both heavy books into one plastic bag and tying a second clear bag tightly over the top. "Double-wrapped so the rain will not soak a single corner. Tell your brother an auntie on Nanjing Road is rooting for him!"

"I will definitely tell him," Chen Xuan laughed softly. "Good night, Auntie."

He stepped outside beneath the narrow awning. Cool rain pattered against the canvas overhead. Before he opened his plain black umbrella, his cheap phone buzzed in his pocket.

Chen Xuan smiled at the caller name and answered.

"Hao, why are you still awake? It is past nine-thirty."

"Ge!" Chen Hao's excited voice came clear from Yunxi County. "I just got home from evening self-study! Our homeroom teacher handed back the monthly math mock exam tonight. I scored one hundred and thirty-eight out of one hundred and fifty! Third highest in the whole grade!"

Warmth spread across Chen Xuan's face as he leaned lightly against the doorframe, watching rain fall across the street.

"One hundred and thirty-eight?" Chen Xuan chuckled. "Not bad at all. Looks like that big crab under the Oriental Pearl Tower is going to cost me plenty next summer."

"You promised, Ge! No backing out!" Chen Hao laughed, then lowered his voice curiously. "Wait, is it raining there? Did Hengxin Logistics make you work late again?"

"No overtime tonight," Chen Xuan said calmly, shifting the double-wrapped bag. "I just stopped by a bookstore near Nanjing Road to buy you the newest Shanghai college-prep math book and a Fudan mock set. I will mail them first thing tomorrow morning."

"Seriously? Ge, you are the best!" Chen Hao cheered. "Are you resting this weekend?"

"I have a quick two-day cargo trip up north starting tomorrow," Chen Xuan answered easily. "Just helping a client inspect a winter warehouse. Tell Mom not to worry if my signal gets weak in the mountains. I will call Sunday night."

"Got it! Wear a thick coat, Ge, the news said it is already snowing up north!"

"I will. Go get some sleep, third-place scholar."

Chen Xuan hung up, slipped his phone away, and clicked open his black umbrella. He stepped out into the steady rain, walking at an unhurried pace toward the quieter side street leading to the subway.

Across the intersection near Nanjing Road, the pedestrian light glowed red.

Under the dark awning of a closed watch shop on the northwest corner, Kieran Cole adjusted his black raincoat and pressed two fingers against the tiny wireless earpiece in his left ear.

"Eyes on the target," Kieran Cole murmured in flat English, watching the lone young man with the black umbrella and scratched green thermos cross under a streetlamp. "Dark-blue windbreaker, white bookstore bag, green metal bottle. Exactly like the Shenzhen photo. No bodyguards. Completely alone."

Across the wet asphalt on the opposite crosswalk, two broad-shouldered foreigners in dark raincoats shifted their umbrellas. Viktor Orlov, a burly former soldier from Eastern Europe, cracked his knuckles inside his sleeve. Beside him, lean, sharp-faced Tomasz Novak checked the silenced pistol tucked under his coat.

"Moving to box him from the front," Viktor Orlov replied quietly through his mic. "Block ahead goes empty after the bank branch."

Forty paces behind Chen Xuan near a closed tea shop, the fourth killer, Rico Alvarez, stepped off the curb onto the wet pavement. His dark eyes locked onto Chen Xuan's back as he matched his stride, one hand curled around a black folding knife inside his pocket.

"Tail is set," Rico Alvarez whispered. "No patrol cars nearby. The rain cleared the tourists."

"Good," Kieran Cole said, a cold smile curving his lips as the crosswalk signal turned green. "Close in from both corners. Push him past the narrow lane next to the old print shop. Ten seconds, clean and silent. Let us earn our 8 million yuan."

From opposite crosswalks, all four foreign assassins moved smoothly through the rain.

Ten meters. Twenty meters. Thirty meters.

Without knowing what invisible line they had just crossed, all four armed men stepped straight inside Chen Xuan's 50-meter perimeter.

Ahead of them, Chen Xuan walked calmly along the wet sidewalk, his worn sneakers stepping neatly around puddles. He passed the glowing green sign of a China Construction Bank ATM booth, the steady hiss of rain filling the quiet block.

For half a second, Chen Xuan paused beneath a streetlamp to unscrew the lid of his scratched green thermos.

In the dark glass reflection of a closed tailor shop beside him, he saw two tall foreign men in dark raincoats closing fast from the front crosswalk, their right hands hidden inside their jackets. In the same dark glass, he caught two more men closing smoothly from behind.

Four trained killers, moving in a tight box pattern, timing their steps to trap him beside the dark mouth of a narrow brick alley twenty meters ahead.

Chen Xuan's expression did not change by a single millimeter. His eyes stayed calm and clear, untouched by alarm. He raised his green thermos, took a slow sip of hot jasmine tea, and screwed the lid back on with a soft click.

He did not reach for a phone. He did not speed up his steps. He simply lifted his black umbrella and kept walking toward the corner.

Forty meters behind him, beneath the dark awning of a shuttered pharmacy, two tall figures in plain matte-black rain jackets stood motionless in the shadows. They wore no badges, no stripes, and no numbers.

The exact second Kieran Cole and his three men stepped inside the 50-meter ring with weapons beneath their coats, both figures in plain matte-black gear shifted together, stepping silently off the curb and melting into the rain like smoke.
