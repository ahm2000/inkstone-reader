# Chapter 193: Rain on Nanjing Road

Cold December rain fell steadily over Shanghai at 7:40 PM, turning the wide granite tiles of Nanjing Road into a shimmering river of red, gold, and icy blue neon. A sharp winter breeze swept off the Huangpu River, making shoppers pull their wool collars tighter and hurry beneath the glowing department store awnings.

Chen Xuan walked at an unhurried pace under a plain black folding umbrella. He wore a charcoal winter coat, dark trousers, and worn leather sneakers that handled wet pavement well. Inside the canvas tote bag slung over his shoulder rested his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos, still faintly warm from the jasmine tea he had brewed at his twelfth-floor desk at Hengxin Logistics before clocking out at 4:55 PM.

He had lingered in Puxi after work for one simple reason: the temperature had dropped sharply since morning, and his wife's favorite silk scarf was far too thin for December wind.

His regular smartphone vibrated in his coat pocket. Chen Xuan shifted his umbrella onto his shoulder, pulled out the phone, and smiled when he saw Qin Yuhan's name on the screen.

"Still stuck on the thirty-eighth floor?" he asked as soon as he answered.

"Just walked out of the final budget review," Qin Yuhan's voice came through, soft and a little tired. "The rain looks miserable out there. Are you already home at Emerald Riverside Villa?"

"Not yet," Chen Xuan said, stepping neatly around a deep puddle. "I stopped by Nanjing Road to pick up a couple of things. Have you eaten?"

"I had half a sandwich at 4:00 PM," she admitted sheepishly.

"That does not count as dinner, President Qin," Chen Xuan teased warmly. "Go straight home and soak your feet in hot water. I am bringing back fresh roasted chestnuts and those warm sesame rice cakes from the corner bakery. Give me forty minutes and I will make ginger beef noodles."

A soft laugh drifted through the speaker. "You spoil me too much for a guy who claims he is too lazy to walk past the subway exit. Bundle up, Xuan. The wind is freezing tonight."

"Yes, ma'am," Chen Xuan chuckled. "See you at home."

He tucked his single smartphone back into his pocket and pushed open the glass door of a quiet, old-fashioned wool shop off the main square. Warm cedar-scented air greeted him. He ignored the flashy designer racks near the front and went straight to the inner counter, running his fingers gently over a stack of soft cream-colored cashmere scarves. He checked the stitching and tested the wool against the back of his hand to make sure it would not itch Qin Yuhan's neck.

Satisfied, he paid with his phone, asked the clerk to wrap it in a waterproof paper sleeve, and tucked it carefully into his canvas tote.

Two doors down, a street-side window vented thick clouds of sweet, smoky steam into the December chill. Chen Xuan bought a brown paper bag of hot sugar-roasted chestnuts and a small box of sesame rice cakes, slipping the box inside his coat to keep it warm while holding the paper bag in his left hand.

As he turned back toward the subway entrance, Chen Xuan peeled a hot chestnut with two fingers and popped it into his mouth. It was sweet, soft, and steaming.

Then his relaxed gaze drifted across the rain-streaked glass of a watch boutique beside him.

In the reflection, four foreign men in dark waterproof jackets were moving in a loose, practiced diamond through the evening crowd. One thin, pale man walked on the opposite side of the pedestrian strip, keeping exact pace with Chen Xuan's stride. Two others, one stocky and broad-shouldered, the other compact and nimble, trailed fifteen paces behind on his left. Ahead near the corner intersection, a lean man with a faint scar on his jaw paused under a bus shelter, pretending to check his watch while angling his shoulders to block the bright path toward the subway stairs.

Chen Xuan chewed his chestnut slowly, not a single muscle in his shoulders tensing.

He recognized the spacing immediately. These were not local pickpockets or street thugs hired by an angry competitor. Their footwork was too measured, their eyes too calm, and the thin man across the street kept his right hand resting just inside the lapel of his coat where a shoulder holster would sit.

Seven-Harbor must have felt the squeeze from Shenzhen and Handong faster than expected, Chen Xuan thought with mild amusement. Sending foreign contract killers into downtown Shanghai in December rain was a bold move, though not a very smart one.

He glanced around at the couples sharing umbrellas and a young mother hurrying past with a sleepy child. If he stayed on Nanjing Road and let these four crowd him near the subway entrance, someone panicked might pull a weapon in the open and hurt innocent bystanders. Worse, a stray scuffle could crush Qin Yuhan's sesame rice cakes or ruin her new cashmere scarf.

"Better take this somewhere quiet," Chen Xuan murmured under his breath.

Keeping his stride completely relaxed, as if he were merely taking a shortcut to avoid the wind on the main boulevard, Chen Xuan angled right at the next corner. He stepped off the glowing granite strip and turned into a narrow, rain-slicked brick side alley lined with shuttered tea warehouses and quiet back doors.

The neon glow faded behind him, replaced by the pale yellow wash of a single streetlamp hanging near the mouth of the lane. Rain pattered steadily against corrugated tin awnings and splashed into dark puddles along the curb.

Without looking back, Chen Xuan heard the soft, synchronized shift of three pairs of heavy boots turning off the main street behind him. At the far end of the lane, the scarred leader had already circled through a parallel passage and stepped calmly into the mouth of the alley to cut off his exit.

Chen Xuan took one more warm chestnut from the paper bag, folded the top neatly to keep the rain out, and waited.
