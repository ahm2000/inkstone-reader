# Chapter 165: Dinner at MixC Mall

By 6:15 PM, the evening lights of Shenzhen were glowing brightly against the cool late November sky. Inside MixC Mall, warm golden lighting spilled across polished marble floors, and the lively hum of shoppers and clinking teacups replaced the salty wind of Pier Nine.

Chen Xuan and Qin Yuhan walked hand in hand up to the fourth floor, where a renowned Cantonese restaurant sat tucked behind carved wooden screens. A cheerful middle-aged waiter with a warm smile greeted them right at the entrance and led them to a quiet corner booth beside a tall glass window overlooking the glittering city avenues.

Nearby, a young couple chatted happily over dim sum, and a silver-haired uncle at the next table slowly poured tea while reading a newspaper. Everyone looked completely relaxed and at home, blending effortlessly into the cozy evening atmosphere.

Once they sat down, Qin Yuhan slipped off her camel trench coat and hung it over the chair, letting out a long, contented sigh. Chen Xuan set his dented 15-yuan green metal thermos neatly beside her handbag and studied the menu with total concentration.

"Let us see," Chen Xuan murmured, tapping the glossy page. "Half a Cantonese roast goose with plum sauce, steamed sea bass with ginger and shredded scallions, claypot rice with cured sausage, blanched choi sum with oyster sauce, and a pot of aged pu-erh tea. Does that sound right, Mrs. Chen?"

Qin Yuhan rested her chin in her palm and laughed softly. "You remembered every single dish I mentioned on the flight down from Shanghai."

"Of course," Chen Xuan said as the waiter jotted down the order and walked away. "A good husband might forget where he left his keys, but he never forgets what his wife wants for dinner."

Warm tea arrived first. Chen Xuan rinsed their porcelain cups neatly and poured a steaming amber cup for Qin Yuhan. She wrapped both hands around the warm porcelain, gazing out at the bright Shenzhen skyline for a moment before looking back at him with sparkling eyes.

"Chen Xuan," she said, shaking her head in amused disbelief, "I still cannot quite wrap my head around what happened at Pier Nine today."

"Oh? What part?" Chen Xuan asked innocently, taking a sip of tea.

"All of it!" Qin Yuhan laughed quietly. "At 4:45 PM, Shen Jingzhou was acting like he owned the entire Pearl River Delta. Then out of nowhere, federal customs, the Maritime Safety Administration, and three major banks all hit his family in the exact same ten-minute window. I have been running Haiyue Group for years, and I have never seen karma move that fast."

Chen Xuan smiled faintly, leaning back in the cushioned booth. "Maybe the Shen Family finally pushed their luck too far with the wrong regulators. People who cut corners for years always trip when they least expect it."

Qin Yuhan nodded thoughtfully, her sharp business mind fitting the pieces together in the only logical way she knew. "That makes sense. The port authority must have already been investigating Shen Maritime Holdings for months, and our dispute just happened to land right on the afternoon their audit dropped. Still, it feels unreal."

She leaned across the table slightly, her dark eyes warm and teasing. "Do you realize how ridiculously lucky I always am whenever you are beside me? Back in Shanghai, whenever a deal gets messy, you bring me soup or carry my bag, and somehow everything works out smoothly. Even Grandpa says you brought good luck into Emerald Riverside Villa."

"Speaking of Grandpa," Chen Xuan grinned, "Elder Qin Zhengguo is probably sitting in his study right now practicing a new opening trap for our next chess match. He still refuses to admit I beat him fair and square on Sunday."

Qin Yuhan burst out laughing. "He spent forty minutes telling my father that you distracted him by talking about braised pork ribs during the endgame!"

"That is a valid psychological tactic," Chen Xuan replied solemnly. "Besides, I promised your parents I would cook a big pot of braised pork ribs this weekend once we get back to Pudong. Your mother already sent me a message asking me to buy fresh lotus root too."

Before Qin Yuhan could tease him further, the waiter returned carrying their dishes. The centerpiece was a glistening platter of Cantonese roast goose, its mahogany skin roasted to a delicate sheen, served beside a small bowl of sweet-and-sour plum sauce.

The savory aroma filled their booth immediately.

Qin Yuhan picked up her chopsticks first, selected the crispiest, juiciest piece from the breast, dipped a corner lightly into the plum sauce, and reached across the small table toward Chen Xuan.

"Open up, Mr. Overtime," she teased, her eyes crinkling with affection. "Since you worked eleven extra minutes holding my handbag at Pier Nine, this first piece is your bonus."

Chen Xuan chuckled and leaned forward, happily taking the roast goose right from her chopsticks. The skin crackled softly, rich and fragrant without a trace of heaviness.

"Incredible," Chen Xuan praised, nodding in genuine approval. "Almost as good as my own cooking."

"Listen to you brag," Qin Yuhan giggled, shaking her head.

In return, Chen Xuan reached for the steamed sea bass. With practiced, unhurried care, he lifted a tender portion from the belly, checked carefully to remove every tiny bone, drizzled a spoonful of warm soy sauce over it, and placed the snow-white fish neatly into Qin Yuhan's bowl.

"Eat while it is hot," he told her softly. "You barely touched lunch today."

Qin Yuhan looked down at the neatly deboned fish in her bowl, then back up at her husband's calm, smiling face. The tension of boardrooms, shipping disputes, and arrogant heirs melted away completely. Around them, Shenzhen glittered like a sea of stars beyond the tall glass, yet inside this small booth, everything felt quiet, steady, and wonderfully simple.

She took a bite of the tender sea bass and smiled brightly at him. "Chen Xuan, I am really glad I married you."

"Me too, Mrs. Chen," Chen Xuan replied, raising his teacup gently to clink against hers. "Now eat up. There is still plenty of claypot rice waiting."
