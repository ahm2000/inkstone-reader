# Chapter 165: Dinner at MixC Mall

By six o'clock, the harsh afternoon glare over Shenzhen had softened into a warm golden twilight.

Inside MixC Mall, known to locals as Wanxiang City in Nanshan, the evening crowd was beginning to gather. Office workers in rolled-up sleeves strolled past outdoor cafes, and families paused near the fountain while the bright towers of Nanshan lit up against the purple sky.

On the second floor of a bustling Cantonese restaurant overlooking the main courtyard, Chen Xuan sat at a quiet window table, happily lifting a piece of glistening roast goose with his chopsticks.

The skin was crisp and deep amber, the meat tender and juicy.

"Now this is proper roast goose," Chen Xuan said with genuine approval, pouring a splash of hot pu'er tea into his cup. "Back in Shanghai, the places around Nanjing Road make the sauce too sweet. Down here, they get the skin just right."

Across the small wooden table, Qin Yuhan rested her elbow lightly near her teacup, watching him with an amused, gentle smile.

She had taken off her crisp suit jacket, wearing only a soft cream silk blouse, her dark hair falling loosely over her shoulders.

"Eat slowly, Mr. Chen," Qin Yuhan teased softly, pushing the plate of bright green blanched choy sum closer to him. "You look like a man who just finished a normal day of filing shipping forms, not someone who turned Shenzhen's biggest port upside down an hour ago."

Chen Xuan picked up a crisp stalk of choy sum and nodded calmly. "That is because I did finish a normal day. Six containers cleared, zero overtime. What more can a working man ask for?"

Qin Yuhan let out a soft, bright laugh, shaking her head.

"Zero overtime," she repeated playfully, lifting her teacup. "Mr. Chen, be honest with me for one second. Three national banks in Futian CBD, China Construction Bank, ICBC, and Huaxia Bank, all froze 12 billion yuan of the Shen Family's credit lines at four fifty sharp. Eight minutes later, the head of the Shenzhen Port Authority ran up to Pier Nine drenched in sweat and suspended his own inspector on the spot. Are you really going to sit here eating roast goose rice and tell me that was pure luck?"

Chen Xuan chewed thoughtfully, swallowed, and took a sip of hot tea before answering with a completely straight face.

"Well, banks are very strict about risk reviews these days," Chen Xuan said evenly. "Especially when a company borrows 12 billion yuan and buys three penthouses in Shenzhen Bay No. 1. As for Director Gao Zhenhua, he looked a little out of breath. Running down to the dock was probably good exercise for him."

Qin Yuhan laughed again, her eyes curving into bright crescents. Ever since Shanghai, she had seen enough to realize that the quiet man sitting across from her in his cheap gray windbreaker carried a weight far beyond anything in the business world. Yet he never bragged, never raised his voice, and never asked for praise. All he ever wanted was to clock out on time and eat a good meal.

Her phone chimed softly on the table.

"Good news from Manager Zhou Ming," Qin Yuhan said, setting her phone back down. "All six of Hengxin Logistics' containers are already loaded onto trucks heading north, and our cold-chain fruit and medical shipments cleared customs without a single scratch. My southern buyers just called to confirm they are keeping our full five-year contract."

"That is good to hear," Chen Xuan said warmly. "Saves you a lot of trouble."

"It saved Haiyue Group more than 100 million yuan," Qin Yuhan corrected gently, her gaze softening as she looked at him. "Thank you, Chen Xuan. Truly. If you had not come down with me from Shanghai today, Shen Jingzhou would have bled us dry at Pier Nine."

"I had work to do at the dock anyway," Chen Xuan replied lightly, refilling her teacup. "Eat your dinner before the goose gets cold. Are we flying back to Shanghai Hongqiao Airport tomorrow morning?"

"Yes, the ten o'clock flight," Qin Yuhan said, picking up her chopsticks. "Though I did wonder for a moment if Shen Wanshan might try something reckless tonight. Losing 12 billion yuan in credit lines all at once is enough to drive a proud man crazy."

Chen Xuan smiled faintly, shaking his head.

"Do not worry about Shen Wanshan," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Right now, he is sitting in his office in Futian CBD calling every bank manager in Guangdong and praying his cranes do not get repossessed at sunrise. He has no time left to bother us over dinner."

Qin Yuhan nodded, feeling a deep, rare sense of peace settle over her shoulders. For the first time in weeks, she did not feel the heavy pressure of running Haiyue Group alone. She picked up a piece of roast goose, dipped it in plum sauce, and smiled as they fell into easy conversation about the weather back along the Bund and the best street snacks in Shanghai.

Outside the tall restaurant window, fifty meters away across the wide stone plaza of MixC Mall, evening music drifted softly from the courtyard speakers.

Under the dark shade of a trimmed banyan tree, at a quiet outdoor coffee table overlooking the restaurant entrance, sat two figures in plain dark coats.

Two untouched cups of black coffee rested between them. Both wore simple matte-black gear beneath their coats and plain black masks covering the lower halves of their faces, blending naturally into the cool evening shadows like ordinary city commuters taking a break.

They were Black Guard members, part of the permanent close-protection team that never strayed more than fifty meters from Chen Xuan.

Neither spoke a word. The first guard sat angled toward the main mall escalator, his eyes calm and sharp as he watched the flow of shoppers. The second guard faced the wide vehicle drop-off lane along the outer street, noting every sedan that slowed near the curb.

Two minutes later, a black sedan with a scratched bumper rolled slowly past the plaza entrance. Inside, two burly men in tight suits peered out through the tinted window, scanning the courtyard cafes.

The first guard did not reach for a weapon or even shift his chair. He simply turned his dark, steady gaze directly toward the passing car.

Across thirty meters of open air, the driver of the black sedan suddenly felt a cold chill crawl up the back of his neck. His eyes met the unblinking stare of the masked figure under the banyan tree. Without a word, the driver hit the gas and sped quickly away down the Nanshan boulevard, vanishing into the evening traffic.

The first guard lowered his eyes back to the quiet plaza, silent and motionless.

Fifty meters away behind the warm glowing window of the restaurant, Chen Xuan raised his porcelain teacup, clinked it lightly against Qin Yuhan's, and took another peaceful bite of his roast goose rice.
