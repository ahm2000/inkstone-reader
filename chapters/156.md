# Chapter 156: Qin Yuhan's Midnight Call

Late Monday night settled quietly over the old residential lane in Shanghai. Most of the fourth-floor windows along the brick building had already gone dark, leaving only the pale glow of the streetlamp filtering through the sheer curtain of Chen Xuan's small rented room.

Inside, Chen Xuan stood by the narrow kitchen counter, rinsing his scratched green thermos under the warm tap. He shook out the old jasmine leaves into the small trash bin, dried the dented steel cap with a clean dishcloth, and set the thermos upside down beside his chipped ceramic mug to air dry for the morning.

It had been a steady, ordinary Monday at Hengxin Logistics. He had sorted forty shipping slips, filed two crates of customs receipts, eaten a bowl of beef noodles at the corner shop on Old Street, and walked home before nine. He was just reaching for the light switch when his old phone buzzed loudly against the wooden table.

Chen Xuan paused and picked it up. The screen showed Qin Yuhan's name.

He glanced at the plastic wall clock. It was eleven forty-five. Qin Yuhan was a woman who ran a multi-billion-yuan shipping group with clockwork discipline. She rarely sent messages after nine o'clock, let alone called near midnight.

Chen Xuan pressed the green button and lifted the phone to his ear. "President Qin?"

For two seconds, there was only the quiet hum of an office air conditioner on the other end, followed by the soft rustle of paper. When Qin Yuhan finally spoke, her usual crisp, composed tone was pulled tight with strain.

"Chen Xuan," she said, her voice low and hurried. "I am sorry to wake you this late. Were you already asleep?"

"Not yet," Chen Xuan replied evenly, pulling out a wooden chair and sitting down at the table. "I was just finishing up. What happened?"

Qin Yuhan let out a slow, tired breath. He could almost picture her standing beside the tall glass window of Haiyue Group's top-floor office, one hand rubbing the bridge of her nose while stacks of port reports covered her desk.

"Our flagship container ship, the Haiyue Star, docked at Shenzhen Pier Nine at eight o'clock this morning," Qin Yuhan said, getting straight to the point. "She is carrying six hundred containers of seasonal electronics, medical supplies, and factory parts bound for southern distributors. Two hours after she tied up, the Shenzhen Port Inspection Office locked the pier gate and slapped an emergency safety hold on the whole vessel."

Chen Xuan's eyes narrowed slightly, though his voice stayed calm. "What kind of safety hold?"

"A completely fake one," Qin Yuhan said, a sharp edge of anger breaking through her control. "First they claimed a ballast valve on the lower hull failed a routine pressure reading, even though the ship passed a full national safety inspection in Shanghai four days ago. When our captain offered to run the test in front of their inspectors, the chief inspector packed up his clipboard, said the paperwork needed a seven-day committee review, and ordered the tugboats and dock cranes to shut down."

"And the people behind the inspection office?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"The Shen Family of Shenzhen," Qin Yuhan answered without hesitation. "They own Shenhai Shipping, and their relatives and old classmates sit in half the seats at the Shenzhen Port Authority. Pier Nine has been their private backyard for twenty years. Ever since Haiyue Group won two southern supply contracts last autumn, they have been looking for a way to choke our berth schedule."

She paused, the sound of her heel tapping once against the office floor carrying faintly over the line.

"Every single day the Haiyue Star sits locked behind that gate costs Haiyue Group 8 million yuan," Qin Yuhan said, her voice tight. "Docking fees, cold-chain power charges, late-delivery fines to our buyers, and stalled freight contracts down the whole coast. If the ship does not unload by Thursday, three of our biggest southern clients will have the legal right to cancel their annual orders and hand them straight to Shenhai Shipping."

Chen Xuan tapped his fingers gently against the wooden table. "8 million yuan a day is not a small number. Did you speak with your father or your uncle?"

A bitter, exhausted sigh came through the speaker.

"My father is stuck in provincial board meetings until Wednesday, and he told me to fly down tomorrow and try to talk sense into the Shen Family," Qin Yuhan said. "An hour ago, I called Uncle Zhengguo."

"General Qin Zhengguo," Chen Xuan murmured.

"Yes," Qin Yuhan said quietly. "I did not even ask him to send anyone. I just asked if he knew an honest senior official in Shenzhen Province who could push the inspection office to follow basic rules. Uncle Zhengguo stopped me right away. He was very firm."

"What did he tell you?" Chen Xuan asked.

"He warned me never to use military pull in a commercial port fight," Qin Yuhan replied, her voice dropping even lower. "He said the Shen Family and their friends in the coastal trade chambers are waiting for Haiyue Group to make one wrong move. If a single military phone call touches Shenzhen Port, they will scream about military interference in private business, leak it to the newspapers, and turn a dock dispute into a political mess for the entire Qin Family. He told me this is a business war, and I have to win it on business ground."

Silence stretched for a moment between them. In that quiet, Chen Xuan could hear the weight pressing down on her shoulders. Qin Yuhan was proud and capable, the kind of woman who preferred to carry a mountain alone rather than ask for help. Calling an ordinary logistics clerk at midnight meant she had already looked at every card in her hand and found every door bolted shut.

"Are you flying down tomorrow?" Chen Xuan asked gently.

"Yes. I booked the noon flight to Shenzhen," Qin Yuhan said. "The Shen Family's eldest grandson, Shen Jingzhou, already sent word to my local port manager. He wants to meet me at the gate of Pier Nine at four o'clock tomorrow afternoon. I know exactly what he is going to ask for. He does not want inspection fees. He wants a big piece of Haiyue's southern routes."

"Do not sign anything he puts in front of you," Chen Xuan said simply.

A small, surprised laugh escaped Qin Yuhan's throat, softening the tension in her voice for just a second. "I was not planning to. Even if I lose 8 million yuan a day for a week, I am not handing twenty years of Haiyue's work to a pack of dock bullies. I just needed to talk to someone who never panics. I am sorry, Chen Xuan. You are not even in Haiyue Group, and here I am dumping port troubles on you at midnight."

"Hengxin Logistics has six containers of precision parts on the Haiyue Star," Chen Xuan said mildly. "I checked the loading sheet myself on Friday afternoon. If your ship stays locked up, my General Manager Sun is going to lose half his hair by tomorrow morning."

"Ah," Qin Yuhan murmured, sounding faintly amused despite her exhaustion. "So you have a personal stake in this after all."

"A very serious one," Chen Xuan replied with an easy smile she could hear in his voice. "Now go home and get five hours of sleep, President Qin. Pack a thin jacket, because Shenzhen is humid this time of year. And drink a glass of warm water before bed instead of another cup of black coffee."

Across the city, Qin Yuhan stared at the half-empty mug of cold coffee on her desk and shook her head slowly. "How do you always know when I am drinking coffee?"

"Because your voice speeds up when you have had too much of it," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Good night, President Qin. Have a safe flight tomorrow."

"Good night, Chen Xuan," she said softly. "Thank you for picking up."

The call ended with a quiet click.

Chen Xuan did not go back to bed. He set his phone down, walked to the small sliding glass door, and stepped out onto his narrow fourth-floor balcony. The midnight air was cool and smelled faintly of damp leaves from the courtyard below.

Down on the empty street, fifty meters past the building's iron gate, a plain dark sedan sat parked quietly under a wide plane tree. Its lights were off and its windows were dark, blending completely into the shadows.

Chen Xuan rested one hand on the cool concrete rail, raised his phone, and dialed a short number from memory.
