# Chapter 156: Late Night at Emerald Riverside

Late November wind brushed past the tall glass doors of Emerald Riverside Villa, carrying the crisp chill of the Huangpu River across the dark lawn. Inside the living room, the floor heating kept everything warm, and a soft yellow lamp glowed above the long walnut dining table.

Chen Xuan sat cross-legged on the cream sofa in faded cotton pajamas, slowly peeling a bright orange tangerine while a late-night food show played on low volume. Across the room, Qin Yuhan sat behind her open laptop, a beige cashmere cardigan draped over her silk loungewear. Her brows were knitted slightly as she scrolled through Haiyue Group's fourth-quarter shipping ledgers.

"You have been staring at that screen for two hours," Chen Xuan said without looking away from the television. He tossed a tangerine segment into his mouth. "Even the accounting software needs a break after ten."

"Just checking the European shipment before I close it," Qin Yuhan replied warmly. "Once the cargo clears Shenzhen and transfers to the feeder vessel, I can take Saturday off and visit Grandpa with you."

"Good," Chen Xuan smiled lazily. "Last time we went, Grandpa complained that you brought your tablet to the chessboard. He still owes me a rematch."

Before Qin Yuhan could answer, her smartphone buzzed sharply on the table. The screen showed Zhou Ming, Haiyue Group's southern marine operations director. Qin Yuhan glanced at her laptop clock. It was 10:40 PM. Zhou Ming never called this late unless something had gone wrong.

She answered immediately. "Director Zhou, go ahead."

Chen Xuan paused with a tangerine segment halfway to his mouth. From the sofa, he watched the relaxed line of his wife's shoulders stiffen within seconds.

"Detained?" Qin Yuhan's voice dropped into her crisp boardroom tone. "The Haiyue Star cleared customs this afternoon. We have the stamped release forms and the paid berth receipt. On what grounds did they lock the yard gate?"

She listened for half a minute, her fingers tightening around her pen. "A 12 million yuan coordination surcharge? That is not a port fee, Director Zhou. That is daylight extortion. Who signed the notice?"

Another brief pause followed. Qin Yuhan let out a cool breath. "The Shen Family's Pier Nine office. Understood. Do not pay them a single cent. Gather every stamped customs receipt, the original berth contract, and the port authority logs. Have the legal team prepare an emergency filing, and book me on the late morning flight to Shenzhen tomorrow."

She ended the call and set the phone down, rubbing the bridge of her nose.

Chen Xuan placed the tangerine peel on a tissue, slipped into his slippers, and walked quietly into the kitchen. He sliced three thin pieces of fresh ginger and boiled them in a small pot with dark brown sugar and two split red dates.

Five minutes later, a warm, spicy fragrance drifted into the living room. Chen Xuan carried a steaming ceramic mug over and set it beside her mouse.

"Drink this while it is hot," he said, leaning against the back of her chair. "A cold stomach makes bad business news feel twice as heavy."

Qin Yuhan looked up, the tension in her eyes softening as she wrapped both hands around the mug. "Thank you, Xuan."

"Sounds like someone down south is trying to bully President Qin," Chen Xuan teased lightly, pulling out the chair beside her. "12 million yuan just for locking a gate? That is more expensive than buying a whole street of braised pork shops."

Despite her worry, Qin Yuhan let out a quiet laugh. "It is the Shen Family in Shenzhen. They control private stevedoring and bonded yards around Pier Nine. My ship, the Haiyue Star, carries 68 million yuan in precision medical and electronic components for a European client. We have a strict forty-eight-hour transfer window. If we miss the feeder ship, Haiyue faces a 20 million yuan breach penalty."

She took a slow sip of the ginger tea. "They know our deadline. That is why they invented a compliance dispute tonight. They think I will panic and wire them 12 million yuan to save the schedule."

"Will you fly down alone?" Chen Xuan asked evenly.

"Director Zhou and two lawyers are already there," she said, a flicker of fatigue crossing her face. "The Shen Family has deep roots at that dock. Dealing with local port bullies is never simple, so I have to go in person."

Chen Xuan tapped his chin, a playful grin spreading across his face. "What a coincidence. Hengxin Logistics has a southern route reconciliation trip to Shenzhen Pier Nine on the schedule this week. Manager Hu has been groaning about it since Friday because nobody on the 12th floor wants to deal with port paperwork."

Qin Yuhan blinked at him. "Wait. Your company has business at Pier Nine?"

"We move freight through half the southern ports," Chen Xuan said with a shrug. "If I volunteer tomorrow morning, Manager Hu will approve my flight on the spot. That way I get a company-paid trip south, and President Qin gets a free logistics escort to carry her suitcase."

"You just want to skip desk work and eat Shenzhen roast goose," Qin Yuhan said, though her eyes curved with genuine relief.

"A husband has to keep his priorities clear," Chen Xuan replied solemnly. "First, make sure my wife eats warm meals instead of surviving on black coffee. Second, roast goose."

Qin Yuhan leaned her head briefly against his shoulder, letting out a long breath. Around her husband, the weight of running Haiyue Group always felt lighter. He was only a laid-back logistics clerk who clocked out at 4:55 PM and treasured his dented green thermos, yet having him beside her made even a messy southern dispute feel manageable.

"All right," she murmured, smiling as she closed her laptop. "If your manager approves it in the morning, you can come with me."

"Consider it done," Chen Xuan said, handing her the remaining half of his tangerine. "Now go wash up and get some sleep. Tomorrow is going to be a long day."
