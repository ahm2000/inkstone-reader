# Chapter 156: Qin Yuhan's Midnight Call

Late Monday night settled quietly over the old residential lane in Shanghai. Inside his fourth floor room, Chen Xuan rinsed his scratched green thermos at the kitchen counter, shook out the old jasmine leaves, and set it upside down to dry.

It had been an ordinary Monday at Hengxin Logistics sorting shipping slips and customs receipts before eating beef noodles on Xinhua Road. He was reaching for the light switch when his phone buzzed on the wooden table.

The screen showed Qin Yuhan's name.

He glanced at the wall clock. It was eleven forty five. Qin Yuhan ran a massive shipping group with strict discipline and never called near midnight unless something serious happened.

Chen Xuan answered the call. "President Qin?"

When Qin Yuhan spoke, her usual composed tone was pulled tight with strain.

"Chen Xuan, I am sorry to wake you this late," she said hurriedly. "Were you already asleep?"

"Not yet," Chen Xuan replied evenly, sitting down at the table. "What happened?"

Qin Yuhan let out a tired breath.

"Our flagship container ship, the Haiyue Star, docked at Shenzhen Pier Nine at eight this morning," Qin Yuhan said directly. "She carries six hundred containers of electronics, medical supplies, and factory parts for southern distributors. Two hours after she tied up, the Shenzhen Port Inspection Office locked the pier gate and slapped an emergency safety hold on the vessel."

Chen Xuan's eyes narrowed slightly, though his voice stayed calm. "What kind of safety hold?"

"A completely fake one," Qin Yuhan said angrily. "They claimed a lower hull ballast valve failed a pressure reading, even though the ship passed a full national inspection in Shanghai four days ago. When our captain offered to run the test in front of them, the chief inspector demanded a seven day committee review and shut down the tugboats and dock cranes."

"And the people behind the inspection office?" Chen Xuan asked softly.

"The Shen Family of Shenzhen," Qin Yuhan answered immediately. "They own Shenhai Shipping, and their relatives fill half the seats at the Shenzhen Port Authority. Pier Nine has been their private backyard for twenty years. Ever since Haiyue Group won two southern supply contracts last autumn, they have looked for a way to choke our berth schedule."

She paused, her heel tapping once against her office floor.

"Every day the Haiyue Star sits locked behind that gate costs Haiyue Group 8,000,000 yuan," Qin Yuhan said tightly. "Docking fees, cold chain power, late fines, and stalled contracts down the coast. If the ship does not unload by Thursday, three of our biggest southern clients can legally cancel their annual orders and hand them straight to Shenhai Shipping."

Chen Xuan tapped the table gently. "Losing 8,000,000 yuan a day is not small. Did you speak with your father or your uncle?"

A bitter sigh came through the speaker.

"My father is in provincial board meetings until Wednesday and told me to fly down tomorrow to talk to the Shen Family," Qin Yuhan said. "An hour ago, I called Grandpa."

"General Qin Zhengguo," Chen Xuan murmured.

"Yes," Qin Yuhan said quietly. "I only asked if he knew an honest senior official in Guangdong Province who could push the inspection office to follow basic rules. Grandpa stopped me right away."

"What did he tell you?" Chen Xuan asked.

"He warned me never to use military pull in a commercial port fight," Qin Yuhan replied in a low voice. "He said the Shen Family and the coastal trade chambers are waiting for Haiyue Group to make one wrong move. If a military call touches Shenzhen Port, they will scream about military interference in private business, leak it to the press, and turn a dock dispute into a political mess for the Qin Family. He told me this is a business war, and I must win it on business ground."

Silence stretched for a moment. Qin Yuhan was proud and preferred carrying a mountain alone. Calling an ordinary clerk at midnight meant every door in her hand was bolted shut.

"Are you flying down tomorrow?" Chen Xuan asked gently.

"Yes, on the noon flight to Shenzhen," Qin Yuhan said. "The Shen Family's eldest grandson, Shen Jingzhou, sent word to meet me at Pier Nine at four tomorrow afternoon. He does not want inspection fees. He wants Haiyue's southern routes."

"Do not sign anything he puts in front of you," Chen Xuan said simply.

A small laugh softened Qin Yuhan's tension. "I will not. Even if I lose 8,000,000 yuan a day for a week, I am not handing twenty years of Haiyue's work to dock bullies. I just needed to talk to someone who never panics. I am sorry for dumping port troubles on you at midnight."

"Hengxin Logistics has six containers of precision parts on the Haiyue Star," Chen Xuan said mildly. "I checked the loading sheet on Friday. If your ship stays locked up, my General Manager Sun will lose half his hair tomorrow."

"Ah," Qin Yuhan murmured with faint amusement. "So you have a personal stake in this after all."

"A very serious one," Chen Xuan smiled. "Now go home and get five hours of sleep, President Qin. Pack a thin jacket for Shenzhen, and drink warm water instead of black coffee."

Across the city, Qin Yuhan looked at her half empty mug of cold coffee and shook her head. "How do you always know when I drink coffee?"

"Your voice speeds up," Chen Xuan said calmly. "Good night, President Qin. Safe flight tomorrow."

"Good night, Chen Xuan. Thank you for picking up."

After the call ended, Chen Xuan stepped onto his fourth floor balcony into the cool night air. Down on the empty street fifty meters past the gate, a dark sedan occupied by the Black Guard in unnumbered black gear sat parked quietly under a plane tree.

Chen Xuan rested a hand on the rail, raised his phone, and dialed a short number from memory.
